# Editorial Product Information: Ursodeoxycholic Acid

## Editorial Note
- This editorial information has been developed based on the Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the clinical and regulatory profile of ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI. Clinicians should consult the current, approved Product Information within their specific jurisdiction before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA), a naturally occurring bile acid.
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Ursodeoxycholic Acid [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 250 mg hard gelatin capsules containing a white or almost white powder [Australian]. 
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic cholestatic liver diseases:** [Australian-approved]. This include specific applications in:
    - Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC).
    - Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC).
    - Cystic Fibrosis (CF)-related cholestasis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Elderly)
- **PBC and general chronic cholestatic liver diseases (excluding CF and PSC):** 12–16 mg/kg/day [Australian].
- **Cystic Fibrosis-related cholestasis:** 20 mg/kg/day [Australian].
- **Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC):** 10–15 mg/kg/day. Note that doses of 20 mg/kg/day have demonstrated histological and biochemical improvement in some studies [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules should be swallowed whole with liquid and taken regularly.
- **Frequency:** Initially, UDCA should be taken in 2 to 3 divided doses daily. For PBC patients, once liver function parameters improve, the total daily dose may be transitioned to a single evening dose [Australian].
- **Weight Limitations:** For patients weighing less than 34 kg or those with dysphagia, an oral suspension formulation should be used [Australian].

### Titration and Special Instructions
- **Initial Symptom Deterioration:** In rare cases of PBC, pruritus may worsen at the start of therapy. In such instances, the dose should be reduced to one 250 mg capsule daily and then gradually increased weekly until the target dose is reached [Australian].
- **PSC Management:** Any dominant stenoses of the bile ducts must be dilated prior to and during treatment [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to ursodeoxycholic acid or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Liver Function Monitoring:** Liver parameters (AST, ALT, and GGT) must be monitored every 4 weeks for the first three months of treatment, and every 3 months thereafter. This monitoring identifies responders versus non-responders and facilitates early detection of potential hepatic deterioration [Australian].
- **Bile Duct Stenosis:** UDCA is not recommended for patients with dominant bile duct stenoses unless the obstruction has been dilated [Australian].
- **Diarrhoea:** If diarrhoea occurs, the dose should be reduced. Persistent diarrhoea requires discontinuation of therapy [Australian].
- **High-Dose PSC Therapy:** Long-term use of high doses (28–30 mg/kg/day) in PSC patients is associated with a higher rate of serious adverse events and is not recommended [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Ursodeoxycholic acid is generally well-tolerated. Reported adverse effects include:

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhoea (reported in up to 3% of patients in controlled trials). |
| **Very Rare (≤0.01%)** | Severe right upper abdominal pain (in PBC), decompensation of hepatic cirrhosis (in advanced PBC; partially regressed upon discontinuation), calcification of gallstones, urticaria. |
| **Frequency Not Stated** | Initial increase in pruritus, nausea, vomiting, sleep disturbance, and increased cholestasis. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Bile Acid Sequestrants and Antacids:** Cholestyramine, colestipol, charcoal, and antacids containing aluminium hydroxide or smectite (aluminium oxide) bind UDCA in the gut and inhibit absorption. These must be taken at least 2 hours apart from UDCA [Australian].
- **Cyclosporin:** UDCA may alter cyclosporin absorption. Monitoring of cyclosporin plasma levels and dose adjustments may be necessary [Australian].
- **Ciprofloxacin:** Reduced absorption of ciprofloxacin has been reported in isolated cases [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic and Metabolic Interactions
- **Statins (OATP1B1 Substrates):** UDCA may increase plasma levels of rosuvastatin (up to 60% increase in AUC) by inhibiting OATP1B1 transporters via HNF 1α inhibition. Dose reduction of rosuvastatin may be required. This interaction potentially extends to other OATP1B1 substrates like atorvastatin, fluvastatin, and pravastatin [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Induction:** UDCA may induce CYP3A4, potentially reducing the efficacy of substrates such as nitrendipine or dapsone. Caution and potential dose adjustments are advised for drugs metabolised by this enzyme [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. Animal studies have shown evidence of teratogenicity (tail malformations in rats at high doses) and embryotoxicity. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women during the first trimester; therefore, UDCA should not be used during the first three months of pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception [Australian].
- **Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy (ICP):** UDCA may be used in the second or third trimester to reduce pruritus, though data on neonatal outcomes are insufficient [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts of UDCA are excreted in human milk. While adverse reactions in infants are unconfirmed, clinicians should weigh the risks or consider discontinuing breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data are conflicting, showing some reduction in mating behaviour and embryolethality in Wistar rats at high doses, but no effect in Sprague-Dawley rats. Human data are unavailable [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Data are very limited. Studied doses generally range from 15–20 mg/kg/day [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No specific dose adjustments are required; see standard adult dosing [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** The effect of UDCA in patients with renal impairment has not been studied [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Renal excretion becomes a major elimination route for bile acids in patients with severe liver disease [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** UDCA increases the concentration of ursodeoxycholic acid in the bile while decreasing the levels of hydrophobic, toxic bile acids (cholic and chenodeoxycholic acids). It has a choleretic effect (increasing bile flow) and may have immunomodulatory effects, such as reducing HLA Class I antigen expression on hepatocytes and suppressing certain cytokines [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 60% to 20% depending on dose (absorption percentage decreases as dose increases). Tmax occurs at 60 and 180 minutes [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 96–98% protein bound. First-pass hepatic extraction is 50–70% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Conjugated in the liver with glycine and taurine. In the gut, it is deconjugated and may be converted to lithocholic acid [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in the faeces. The biological half-life is 3.5–5.8 days due to enterohepatic circulation [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PBC:** Eight pivotal trials (at least 2 years duration) showed significant biochemical improvement in liver function. Long-term studies suggested increased survival without transplantation, particularly in patients with less advanced disease [Australian].
- **PSC:** One 2-year study (13–15 mg/kg) showed biochemical improvement (bilirubin, ALP, AST) but no significant effect on symptoms, histology, or survival. A smaller study at 20 mg/kg showed reduced histological progression [Australian].
- **Cystic Fibrosis:** A double-blind study (900 mg/day) showed significant improvement in GGT and 5’-nucleosidase. Higher doses (20 mg/kg) were more effective than 10 mg/kg in improving GGT and ALT [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the packaging or the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) [Australian].
