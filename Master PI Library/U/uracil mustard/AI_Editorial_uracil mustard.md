## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from genetic metabolic disorders [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
Levocarnitine must be administered intravenously. Parenteral products should be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours; intervals must never be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].

### ESRD on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without a history of seizure activity. In those with pre-existing epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation):** The safety of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine has not been established in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD, as it may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Metabolic Patients:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor plasma free carnitine (target: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistry, and vital signs weekly and monthly [US].
- **ESRD Patients:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations (target normal: 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Gastritis and abdominal pain have also been reported [US].
- **General:** Body odor (often described as "fishy") may occur [US].

### Adverse Effects in ESRD Trials (Frequency ≥5%)
The following were reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients, though causality was not always established:
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), arrhythmia [US].
- **Neurological/Nervous:** Headache (3–37%), dizziness (10–18%), paraesthesia (3–12%), insomnia [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough (10–18%), dyspnea [US].
- **Systemic:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), fever (5–12%), chest pain (6–15%), flu syndrome (15–29%) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Jurisdictional Category:** Not explicitly assigned a lettered category in provided source [US]. 
    - **Clinical Data:** Animal reproductive studies (rats and rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks to the infant; consider discontinuation of nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment was observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders and ESRD apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not detailed in the source; follow standard adult dosing with caution [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. However, chronic high-dose *oral* administration is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Corrects carnitine deficiency, which is biochemically defined as plasma concentrations <20 µmol/L at one week post-term [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg doses post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While levocarnitine effectively increases plasma concentrations, its impact on specific clinical outcomes in ESRD remains to be fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL concentration) for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].
