# Editorial Product Information: Umeclidinium and Vilanterol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. For full prescribing details, clinicians should consult the relevant local regulatory documentation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Umeclidinium (as bromide) and Vilanterol (as trifenatate).
- **Chemical Class:** Combination of a long-acting muscarinic receptor antagonist (LAMA) and a long-acting beta2-adrenergic agonist (LABA).
- **Brand Name:** **ANORO ELLIPTA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R03AL03.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Inhalation powder. Each delivered dose (leaving the mouthpiece) contains 55 micrograms of umeclidinium (equivalent to 65 micrograms of umeclidinium bromide) and 22 micrograms of vilanterol (as trifenatate) [Australian]. 
    - *Note on formulation:* Each blister contains 74.2 micrograms umeclidinium bromide and 25 micrograms vilanterol.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral inhalation.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD):** Indicated as a long-term, once-daily maintenance bronchodilator treatment to relieve symptoms in adult patients with COPD [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose:** One inhalation once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** For oral inhalation only. Doses should be administered at the same time each day. 
- **Maximum Dose:** Not to exceed one inhalation in any 24-hour period [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered; however, the next dose should not be taken sooner than 24 hours after the previous dose.
- **Priming:** The Ellipta device does not require priming or special preparation before first use.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to umeclidinium, vilanterol, or any excipients (e.g., magnesium stearate) [Australian].
- **Milk Protein Allergy:** Contraindicated in patients with severe milk-protein allergy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Asthma:** This combination product must not be used in patients with asthma, as it has not been studied in this population [Australian].
- **Acute Symptoms:** Not for use as rescue therapy. Acute episodes of bronchospasm should be treated with a short-acting bronchodilator.
- **Paradoxical Bronchospasm:** As with other inhaled therapies, administration may produce life-threatening paradoxical bronchospasm. Therapy should be discontinued immediately and alternatives initiated [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** May cause cardiac arrhythmias (e.g., atrial fibrillation, tachycardia). Use with caution in patients with severe cardiovascular disease [Australian].
- **Antimuscarinic Activity:** Due to umeclidinium's anticholinergic profile, use with caution in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma or urinary retention [Australian].
- **Disease Deterioration:** Increased use of short-acting bronchodilators indicates worsening COPD; patients should be clinically reassessed.
- **Excipients:** Contains lactose monohydrate. Patients with rare hereditary problems of galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption should avoid this product [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are classified by system organ class and frequency [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Upper respiratory tract infection, pharyngitis, sinusitis, nasopharyngitis, urinary tract infection, cough, oropharyngeal pain, constipation, dry mouth. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Atrial fibrillation, supraventricular tachycardia, tachycardia, palpitations, rash, anxiety, tremor, dysgeusia, muscle spasms. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis, angioedema, urticaria), vision blurred, glaucoma, increased intraocular pressure, eye pain, paradoxical bronchospasm, dysphonia, urinary retention, dysuria, headache. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Beta-blockers:** May antagonise the effect of vilanterol. Avoid concurrent use of non-selective or selective beta-adrenergic blockers unless there are compelling reasons [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Vilanterol is a substrate of CYP3A4. Co-administration with strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) may increase systemic exposure to vilanterol. While clinical trials showed no significant systemic beta-agonist effects at tested doses, caution is advised [Australian].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Inhibitors:** Both components are substrates of P-gp. Interaction studies with verapamil showed a 1.4-fold increase in umeclidinium AUC but no effect on vilanterol. No dosage adjustment is typically required [Australian].
- **Other Anticholinergics/Sympathomimetics:** Co-administration with other long-acting muscarinic antagonists or beta2-adrenergic agonists has not been studied and is generally not recommended due to potential additive effects.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3:** No adequate trials in pregnant women. Animal studies with vilanterol have shown teratogenicity and embryotoxicity at high exposures. Use only if the benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if umeclidinium or vilanterol are excreted in human milk; however, other beta2-agonists are detected. A risk to the newborn/infant cannot be excluded. A decision to discontinue breastfeeding or the drug must be made based on clinical priority [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available. Animal studies showed no effects on male or female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established; should not be used in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required in patients over 65 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required in mild or moderate impairment. Studies have not been conducted in patients with severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Umeclidinium:** A long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA) that competitively inhibits acetylcholine at M3 receptors in the airway smooth muscle, leading to bronchodilation [Australian].
    - **Vilanterol:** A selective long-acting beta2-adrenergic agonist (LABA) that stimulates intracellular adenylate cyclase, increasing cyclic AMP levels and causing relaxation of bronchial smooth muscle [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Improvements in lung function are evident within 15 minutes of administration and are maintained over 24 hours. No evidence of tachyphylaxis was observed over 24 weeks [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 13% for umeclidinium and 27% for vilanterol. Steady state is achieved within 6–10 days [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Umeclidinium is primary metabolised by CYP2D6; vilanterol by CYP3A4. Both are P-gp substrates [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Half-lives following inhaled dosing are approximately 19 hours for umeclidinium and 11 hours for vilanterol [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** In Phase III studies, once-daily administration demonstrated statistically significant improvements in trough FEV1 (167 mL improvement vs. placebo at 24 weeks; p<0.001) [Australian].
- **Symptom Relief:** Significant improvements were noted in breathlessness (TDI focal score) and health-related quality of life (SGRQ) compared to placebo and tiotropium [Australian].
- **Exacerbations:** Treatment resulted in a 50% risk reduction in the time to first COPD exacerbation compared with placebo (Hazard Ratio 0.5) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life After Opening:** Once the foil tray is opened, the inhaler may be stored for a maximum of 6 weeks. 
- **Handling:** If stored in a refrigerator, the inhaler should be allowed to reach room temperature for at least one hour before use [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
