# Editorial Product Information: Ubrogepant

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **ubrogepant** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory instructions into a single, coherent reference.
- **Note:** This editorial is a clinical synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for definitive prescribing requirements. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not available in the provided source material and is noted as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Ubrogepant**, a calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **UBRELVY** [US]. 
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Oral Tablets:** 50 mg and 100 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AbbVie Inc. (formerly Allergan, Inc.) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Migraine Treatment:** Indicated for the acute treatment of migraine with or without aura in adults [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** **Ubrogepant** is not indicated for the preventive treatment of migraine [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial Dose:** 50 mg or 100 mg taken orally, with or without food [US].
- **Repeat Dosing:** If the migraine persists or recurs, a second dose may be administered at least 2 hours after the initial dose [US].
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** The maximum total dose in a 24-hour period is 200 mg [US].
- **Usage Frequency:** The safety of treating more than 8 migraine attacks in a 30-day period has not been established [US].

### Dose Modifications (Drug Interactions and Special Populations)
- **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Initial dose of 50 mg; avoid a second dose within 24 hours [US].
- **Weak CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** 50 mg initial dose; 50 mg second dose (if needed) after 2 hours [US].
- **Weak and Moderate CYP3A4 Inducers:** 100 mg initial dose; 100 mg second dose (if needed) after 2 hours [US].
- **BCRP and/or P-gp Only Inhibitors:** 50 mg initial dose; 50 mg second dose (if needed) after 2 hours [US].
- **Severe Hepatic Impairment (Child-Pugh Class C):** 50 mg initial dose; 50 mg second dose (if needed) after 2 hours [US].
- **Severe Renal Impairment (CLcr 15-29 mL/min):** 50 mg initial dose; 50 mg second dose (if needed) after 2 hours [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin) is contraindicated due to significant increases in **ubrogepant** exposure [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of serious hypersensitivity to **ubrogepant** or any of its excipients. Reported reactions include anaphylaxis, dyspnea, and facial/throat edema [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions (anaphylaxis, dyspnea, angioedema, rash, urticaria) have been reported. These can occur within minutes to days of administration. If a serious reaction occurs, discontinue the medication immediately and initiate appropriate therapy [US].
- **Hypertension:** New-onset or worsening of pre-existing hypertension has been reported post-marketing, often within 7 days of treatment initiation. Some cases required hospitalisation. Monitor blood pressure and consider discontinuation if blood pressure is inadequately controlled [US].
- **Raynaud’s Phenomenon:** Post-marketing reports indicate the development or worsening of Raynaud’s phenomenon. Symptoms typically appear a median of 1.5 days after dosing and can be severe. Discontinue if signs of digital ischemia or debilitating pain occur [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate. Data from placebo-controlled trials [US]:

| Adverse Reaction | Placebo (N=984) | **Ubrogepant** 50 mg (N=954) | **Ubrogepant** 100 mg (N=485) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nausea** | 2% | 2% | 4% |
| **Somnolence*** | 1% | 2% | 3% |
| **Dry Mouth** | 1% | <1% | 2% |

*\*Somnolence includes sedation and fatigue.*

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis), severe hypertension, and Raynaud’s phenomenon [US].
- **Discontinuation Rates:** In long-term safety studies (up to 1 year), 2.5% of patients discontinued due to adverse reactions; nausea was the most common cause [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole) Increase **ubrogepant** AUC by nearly 10-fold. Use is contraindicated [US].
- **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., verapamil, fluconazole, grapefruit juice) Increase exposure. Dose adjustment is required [US].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampin, phenytoin, St. John's Wort) Significantly decrease **ubrogepant** exposure (approx. 80%), likely leading to loss of efficacy. Concomitant use should be avoided [US].
- **BCRP and P-gp Inhibitors:** (e.g., quinidine, carvedilol) May increase exposure as **ubrogepant** is a substrate for these transporters. Dose adjustment is required [US].

### Evaluated Drugs with No Significant Interaction
- No clinically significant interactions were observed with acetaminophen, naproxen, sumatriptan, oral contraceptives, esomeprazole, or other CGRP-pathway drugs (erenumab, galcanezumab, atogepant) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a lettered category under the Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule (PLLR). 
    - **Clinical Data:** No adequate human data on developmental risk. Animal studies (rabbits) showed increased embryofetal mortality and abortions at high doses associated with maternal toxicity.
    - **Registry:** A pregnancy exposure registry is available to monitor outcomes [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Ubrogepant** is excreted in human milk in low amounts (Relative Infant Dose approx. 0.15%). The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be considered alongside the mother’s clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility or reproductive performance were observed in animal studies at exposures 30 times the maximum recommended human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics are similar to younger adults. Dose selection should be cautious, typically starting at the lower end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Severe (CLcr 15-29 mL/min):** Dose adjustment required (max 100 mg/24h) [US].
    - **End-Stage Renal Disease (CLcr <15 mL/min):** Avoid use [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh Class C):** Dose adjustment required (max 100 mg/24h) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Ubrogepant** is an orally active, small-molecule calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) receptor antagonist. It blocks the binding of CGRP—a neuropeptide associated with migraine pathophysiology—to its receptor [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is approximately 1.5 hours. High-fat meals delay Tmax by 2 hours and reduce Cmax by 22% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is 87% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 5 to 7 hours. Excreted mainly via the biliary/fecal route (42% unchanged) and minimally via the renal route (6% unchanged) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** At twice the maximum recommended daily dose, **ubrogepant** does not prolong the QT interval to a clinically relevant extent [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
The efficacy of **ubrogepant** was demonstrated in two Phase 3, randomised, placebo-controlled trials (Study 1 and Study 2) [US]:
- **Primary Endpoints:** Pain freedom at 2 hours post-dose and Most Bothersome Symptom (MBS) freedom at 2 hours post-dose.
- **Results:** In Study 1, both 50 mg and 100 mg doses showed significantly higher rates of pain freedom (19.2% and 21.2% respectively) compared to placebo (11.8%). MBS freedom (photophobia, phonophobia, or nausea) was also significantly improved [US].
- **Sustained Response:** **Ubrogepant** demonstrated superior sustained pain freedom from 2 to 24 hours compared to placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** Supplied in unit-dose packets within boxes of 10, 16, or 30 tablets [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx Only).
