# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial is a harmonised synthesis based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- This document is intended as a clinically accurate overview for healthcare professionals and is an editorial synthesis. It does not replace the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) was not available at the time of drafting and is therefore omitted.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic/Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow IV bolus (2–3 minutes) or infusion.
    - Acute Crisis: A loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
    - Maximum Dose: Up to 300 mg/kg has been administered in severe cases [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow IV bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose Adjustment: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L). Adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL. Store in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) for up to 24 hours [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of injection. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations.
    - Recommended weekly/monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Toxigenic Metabolites:** Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function (or ESRD on dialysis) may result in the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are potentially toxic [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of the indications (ESRD or metabolic errors).

### Very Common (>10%) and Common (1–10%)
Frequencies derived from clinical trials in ESRD patients [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), flu syndrome (25–29%), headache (3–37%), chest pain (6–15%), fever (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), anorexia (3–6%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Body Odour:** Reported with levocarnitine therapy.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concurrently with levocarnitine. Monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or adjusting the dose of levocarnitine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Not formally assigned a letter category in this PI version. Reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established; see Section 3 for dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources; general caution regarding comorbidities and polypharmacy is advised.
- **Renal Impairment:** Oral levocarnitine safety/efficacy has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For ESRD-specific IV dosing, see Section 3 [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic/fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Protein Binding:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients results in increased plasma concentrations of levocarnitine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels. While biochemical correction is demonstrated, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx Only).
