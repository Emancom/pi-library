# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not substitute for the official, current Product Information approved for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered in severe metabolic crisis, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range from 50 mg/kg, or as clinically required. Doses as high as 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Therapy may be initiated if trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels are below normal (40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L). Dose adjustments should be guided by trough levels and can be reduced (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) as early as the third or fourth week of treatment.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate the risks/benefits of re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs weekly or monthly [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>1–10%) to Very Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting. Body odor and gastritis are reported less frequently [US].
- **Hemodialysis Specific (≥5% Incidence):** In clinical trials of ESRD patients, the following events occurred at a rate of 5% or higher, regardless of causality:
    - **General:** Chest pain, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, fever, abdominal pain, asthenia.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea.
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia.
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned in current PI; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.]
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of carnitine following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While levocarnitine increases concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on clinical outcomes has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the opened vial as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
