# Editorial Product Information: Ulipristal Acetate

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information (PI) for **ulipristal acetate**, designed to provide an expert clinical overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local regulatory guidelines for legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ulipristal acetate (a synthetic selective progesterone receptor modulator).
- **Brand Names:** **EllaOne** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G03AD02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 30 mg film-coated tablet [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Perrigo Australia (distributed for Laboratoire HRA Pharma) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Emergency Contraception:** Indicated for use within 120 hours (5 days) of unprotected sexual intercourse or known/suspected contraceptive failure [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** A single 30 mg tablet taken orally as soon as possible, but no later than 120 hours after unprotected intercourse.
- **Timing:** Treatment can be initiated at any point during the menstrual cycle.
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food.
- **Vomiting Management:** If vomiting occurs within 3 hours of administration, a second 30 mg dose should be taken.
- **Pregnancy Exclusion:** Before administration, pregnancy must be excluded if the menstrual period is late or if clinical symptoms of pregnancy are present.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **ulipristal acetate** or any excipients (e.g., lactose).
- **Pregnancy:** **Ulipristal acetate** must not be administered to women who are known or suspected to be pregnant [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Limited Efficacy Period:** **Ulipristal acetate** is intended for emergency use and does not prevent pregnancy in every instance. It is ineffective once ovulation has occurred or if pregnancy is already established.
- **Ectopic Pregnancy:** While **ulipristal acetate** does not cause ectopic pregnancy, clinicians should consider this diagnosis if pregnancy occurs or if abdominal pain/irregular bleeding follows treatment. Uterine bleeding does not definitively rule out ectopic pregnancy.
- **Severe Asthma:** Use is not recommended in women with severe asthma who are currently treated with oral glucocorticoids [Australian].
- **Repeated Use:** Not recommended for use more than once within the same menstrual cycle, as safety and efficacy for frequent use have not been established.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended for use in patients with severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Lactose Intolerance:** Tablets contain lactose; use is contraindicated in patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is based on clinical trials involving over 4,700 women. Most reactions are mild to moderate and resolve spontaneously.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache, nausea, abdominal pain, dysmenorrhea. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, mood disorders, myalgia, back pain, pelvic pain, breast tenderness, fatigue, vomiting. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Influenza, appetite disorders, anxiety, insomnia, somnolence, migraine, visual disturbances, diarrhea, dry mouth, dyspepsia, acne, pruritus, menorrhagia, vaginal discharge, metrorrhagia, vaginitis, hot flushes, pyrexia, chills. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Disorientation, tremor, syncope, photophobia, vertigo, dry throat, urticaria, ruptured ovarian cyst, hypomenorrhea. |

**Menstrual Impacts:** Menstrual periods may occur earlier or later than expected. Approximately 18.5% of women experience a delay of more than 7 days, and 4% experience a delay exceeding 20 days [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions (Effect of other drugs on Ulipristal)
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, St John’s wort) significantly reduce plasma concentrations of **ulipristal acetate** (AUC decreased by up to 90%). Concomitant use is not recommended and should be avoided even if the inducer was stopped in the preceding 2–3 weeks.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir, ketoconazole) increase exposure; however, this is unlikely to be clinically significant for a single-dose emergency treatment.
- **Gastric pH Modifiers:** Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) may reduce Cmax by ~65%, but this is not expected to impact the efficacy of a single 30 mg dose.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Because **ulipristal acetate** binds to progesterone receptors, it may interfere with progestin-containing contraceptives. 
    - **Progestin-only pills:** Should not be started until at least 5 days after **ulipristal acetate** intake. 
    - **Levonorgestrel EC:** Concomitant use with levonorgestrel-based emergency contraception is not recommended.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Classification:** Category D [Australian]. 
    - **Note:** Limited data exist regarding exposure during early pregnancy, but no specific safety concerns have been identified to date. It is not intended for termination of an existing pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** **Ulipristal acetate** is excreted in breast milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended for one week following administration. Mothers should express and discard milk during this period to maintain lactation.
- **Fertility:** A rapid return to fertility is expected. A reliable barrier method of contraception must be used for all subsequent acts of intercourse until the next menstrual period.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No differences in safety or efficacy have been observed in post-pubertal adolescents compared to adults. It is not indicated for prepubertal children.
- **Elderly Use:** No relevant data available; not an intended population.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific studies in mild/moderate impairment. Use is not recommended in severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A selective progesterone receptor modulator with antagonistic and partial agonistic effects. It primarily works by inhibiting or delaying ovulation by suppressing the LH surge, even when taken immediately before ovulation is scheduled to occur.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak plasma concentration (Cmax) at ~1 hour. Absolute bioavailability is ~27%.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (>98%) to albumin and lipoproteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via CYP3A4 to an active monodemethylated metabolite.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via faeces; terminal half-life is approximately 32.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** In a single-blind comparative study (HRA2914-513), **ulipristal acetate** was non-inferior to levonorgestrel 1.5 mg. When taken within 72 hours, the pregnancy rate was 1.9% for **ulipristal acetate** vs 2.6% for levonorgestrel.
- **Extended Window:** In an open-label study (HRA2914-509) focusing on the 48–120 hour window, the pregnancy rate was 2.1%.
- **Meta-analysis:** Pooled data indicated that the risk of pregnancy with **ulipristal acetate** was significantly lower than with levonorgestrel across the entire 0–120 hour timeframe.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Protection:** Keep the tablet in the original blister and outer carton to protect from moisture and light.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 3 (Pharmacist Only Medicine) [Australian].
