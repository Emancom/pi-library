# Editorial Product Information: umeclidinium + vilanterol

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **umeclidinium + vilanterol**
- Chemical class: Combination long-acting muscarinic receptor antagonist (LAMA) + long-acting selective beta2-adrenergic receptor agonist (LABA)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral inhalation only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Long-term once-daily maintenance bronchodilator treatment to relieve symptoms in adult patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).
- Not indicated for treatment of asthma; **do not use in patients with asthma** (not studied).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adults)
- Recommended dose: one inhalation once daily of **umeclidinium + vilanterol 62.5/25 micrograms** (delivered dose) by the orally inhaled route.
- Take at the same time each day.
- Do not administer more frequently than once every 24 hours.

### Children
- **Not for use in children.**

### Special dosing notes
- No titration schedule specified in available sources.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- For oral inhalation use only.
- Not intended for relief of acute bronchospasm (rescue therapy). Use a short‑acting inhaled bronchodilator for acute symptoms.
- Increasing use of short-acting bronchodilators indicates deterioration; review patient therapy.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to umeclidinium, vilanterol, or any excipient in the product.
- Severe milk-protein allergy (product contains lactose monohydrate which contains milk protein).
- Any other contraindication: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed warnings: None specified in available sources.
- General warnings and precautions:
  - **Asthma:** Not studied in asthma; should not be used for asthma.
  - **Not for acute bronchospasm:** Not a rescue inhaler; not for immediate relief of acute symptoms.
  - **Paradoxical bronchospasm:** May occur with inhaled therapies and can be life‑threatening. Discontinue immediately if this occurs and institute alternative therapy.
  - **Cardiovascular effects:** Sympathomimetic agents and antimuscarinic agents can cause cardiac arrhythmias (e.g., atrial fibrillation) and tachycardia. Use with caution in patients with severe cardiovascular disease.
  - **Antimuscarinic effects:** Use with caution in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma and urinary retention. Educate patients to seek medical attention for symptoms suggestive of acute narrow-angle glaucoma (eye pain, blurred vision, visual halos, red eyes) or urinary retention.
  - **Excipients:** Contains lactose (milk protein). Patients with hereditary problems of galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose‑galactose malabsorption should not take this product.
  - **Deterioration of disease:** Increasing need for rescue bronchodilator suggests worsening COPD; evaluate patient.
- Monitoring requirements:
  - Monitor for signs/symptoms of antimuscarinic effects (dry mouth, visual disturbances, urinary retention).
  - Monitor cardiovascular status in patients with known or suspected cardiovascular disease.
  - Monitor COPD control (use of rescue medication, exacerbation frequency).
- Specific clinical precautions:
  - Use with caution when co‑prescribing systemic or topical agents that may affect cardiac conduction or beta‑adrenergic activity.
  - Pregnancy/lactation decisions should weigh maternal benefit vs foetal/infant risk (see Section 9).
  - No specific laboratory test interactions established.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- General statement: Clinically significant interactions at therapeutic inhaled doses are considered unlikely due to low systemic exposure; however, potential interactions have been identified and warrant caution.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Beta-adrenergic blockers (both non-selective and cardioselective): May antagonise the bronchodilatory effect of beta2-agonists such as vilanterol. Concurrent use should be avoided unless compelling reasons justify co‑administration; monitor closely if used.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- CYP3A4 inhibitors:
  - Vilanterol is primarily cleared by CYP3A4 (extensive first-pass metabolism in gastrointestinal tract and liver).
  - Co-administration with a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor (ketoconazole 400 mg) increased mean vilanterol AUC0‑t by ~65% and Cmax by ~22%. Although this increase was not associated with observed increases in beta‑agonist systemic effects (heart rate, potassium, QTcF) in the studied setting, caution is advised when co‑administering with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors due to the potential for increased systemic exposure and adverse reactions.
- P‑glycoprotein (P‑gp) inhibitors / substrates:
  - Both umeclidinium and vilanterol are substrates of P‑gp.
  - Co-administration with verapamil (240 mg once daily; a moderate P‑gp inhibitor) produced no change in Cmax for either agent and an approximate 1.4‑fold increase in umeclidinium AUC with no effect on vilanterol AUC. Based on the magnitude observed, no clinically relevant interaction is expected with P‑gp inhibitors at clinical doses.
- Other interactions: Not specified in available sources.

### Management notes
- When strong CYP3A4 inhibitors are necessary, consider the potential for increased systemic exposure to vilanterol and monitor for beta‑agonist related adverse effects (e.g., tachycardia, hypokalaemia, QT prolongation, tremor).
- Avoid routine co‑prescription of beta-blockers where possible. If unavoidable, use the lowest effective dose and monitor respiratory status.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category: [Australian] Category B3.
- Clinical data: No adequate and well-controlled trials in pregnant women.
- Animal data: Teratogenicity observed in animals (vilanterol in rabbits). Umeclidinium did not produce embryo‑foetal developmental toxicity in rats and rabbits at exposures substantially greater than clinical systemic exposure. Vilanterol produced maternal toxicity, embryotoxicity and an increased incidence of malformations (including rare open eyelid) in rabbits at exposures several-fold above clinical exposure; NOAEL values reported in animal studies (details available in original PI). 
- Recommendation: Use during pregnancy only if expected maternal benefit justifies potential foetal risk.

### Breastfeeding
- It is unknown whether umeclidinium or vilanterol are excreted in human milk. Other beta2‑agonists have been detected in human milk.
- A risk to breastfed infants cannot be excluded. Decide whether to discontinue breastfeeding or to discontinue therapy after weighing benefits and risks.

### Fertility
- Human data: Not available.
- Animal data: No effect on male or female fertility in rats when umeclidinium or vilanterol were administered individually at doses producing large multiples of human systemic exposure.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- **Not for use in children**; safety and efficacy not established.

### Elderly
- No dosage adjustment required for patients over 65 years. No special precautions beyond those noted in Warnings and Precautions.

### Renal impairment
- No dosage adjustment required in patients with renal impairment.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dosage adjustment required in patients with mild or moderate hepatic impairment.
- Not studied in severe hepatic impairment; use with caution and consider risk/benefit in severe hepatic dysfunction.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **umeclidinium**: Long‑acting muscarinic receptor antagonist (anticholinergic) that competitively inhibits acetylcholine at muscarinic receptors on airway smooth muscle — slow reversibility at the M3 receptor and prolonged bronchodilatory effect.
- **vilanterol**: Selective long‑acting beta2‑adrenergic receptor agonist (beta2‑agonist) that stimulates adenylate cyclase, increasing intracellular cAMP and producing bronchial smooth muscle relaxation and reduced mediator release from mast cells.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Onset and duration: Improvement in lung function observed as early as 15 minutes after dosing and maintained for 24 hours.
- Clinical measures: Mean increases in FEV1 versus placebo were observed rapidly (e.g., ~112 mL improvement at 15 minutes after first dose in one study) and sustained with repeated dosing. No evidence of tachyphylaxis over repeated dosing in clinical studies.
- Cardiovascular effects: In a controlled QT study, once-daily doses of umeclidinium/vilanterol 125/25 µg and supratherapeutic dose 500/100 µg produced maximum mean QTcF prolongations from placebo of approximately 4.3 ms and 8.2 ms respectively; these were not considered clinically relevant in the studied population. No clinically significant effects on cardiac rhythm were observed in Holter monitoring studies in patients with COPD treated up to 12 months.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption/distribution/elimination: Detailed numerical parameters (Cmax, Tmax, half-life, volume, clearance) are not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism: Vilanterol is cleared predominantly by CYP3A4-mediated metabolism (first-pass in GI tract and liver). Both umeclidinium and vilanterol are substrates of P‑glycoprotein.
- Systemic exposure after inhalation: Plasma concentrations are low after inhaled dosing; clinically significant systemic drug–drug interactions are considered unlikely at therapeutic inhaled doses, but specific interactions (see Section 8) have been demonstrated under study conditions.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Development program: Efficacy and safety evaluated in multiple Phase III clinical studies in adult patients with COPD including:
  - Five 6‑month efficacy studies (multiple doses including umeclidinium/vilanterol 62.5/25 µg and 125/25 µg).
  - Two 12‑week exercise endurance studies.
  - One 12‑month safety study.
- Key efficacy findings (umeclidinium/vilanterol 62.5/25 µg):
  - Placebo-controlled 6‑month study: Mean improvement in trough FEV1 at Week 24 vs placebo was 167 mL (95% CI 128 to 207 mL; p<0.001).
  - Bronchodilation was evident after the first dose and sustained through 24 weeks (e.g., Day 1 and Week 24 FEV1 improvements).
  - Symptom and quality-of-life outcomes: Statistically and clinically meaningful improvements versus placebo in Transition Dyspnoea Index (TDI) focal score (mean 2.4 vs 1.2; improvement over placebo 1.2 units) and St George’s Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ) total score (mean change −8.07 vs −2.56; improvement over placebo −5.51 units). A greater proportion of patients achieved MCID for TDI and SGRQ versus placebo.
  - Rescue medication: Reduction in daily rescue bronchodilator use and increased percentage of days without rescue medication versus placebo.
  - Exacerbations: Lower risk of COPD exacerbation vs placebo (time to first exacerbation HR 0.5, 95% CI 0.3–0.8; ~50% risk reduction) in at least one study.
- Active comparator studies:
  - Compared with tiotropium (18 µg once daily), umeclidinium/vilanterol 62.5/25 µg demonstrated statistically significant improvements in trough FEV1 at Week 24 in two large studies (improvements over tiotropium approx. 90–112 mL; p<0.001 in major studies) and numerically greater improvement in a third study.
  - Improvements in weighted mean FEV1 over 0–6 hours and reductions in rescue medication use were also observed vs tiotropium in several studies.
- Safety over longer term:
  - A 12‑month safety study reported adverse events including back pain, cough, urinary tract infection, abdominal pain, pleuritic pain, and diabetes mellitus at low incidence rates; overall safety profile across studies was consistent with muscarinic antagonist and beta2‑agonist pharmacology and post‑marketing experience.

## 13. Overdose
- Expected signs and symptoms: Those consistent with antimuscarinic effects (e.g., dry mouth, visual accommodation disturbances, tachycardia) and beta2‑agonist effects (e.g., tremor, headache, tachycardia, hypokalaemia).
- Management: Treat supportively with appropriate monitoring and symptomatic therapy as necessary. For regional poison information contact details: Not specified in available sources (regional contact numbers not provided).
