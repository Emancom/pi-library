# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document has been developed using the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and pharmacological profiles into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource. It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute intravenous bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Acute Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required based on clinical response. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels.

### Monitoring
- Plasma free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations is recommended.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No specific contraindications are listed in the provided source; however, see Section 5 regarding hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral formulations in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of plasma carnitine levels and clinical status is essential to prevent over-supplementation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD trials are often confounded by the underlying pathology.
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, body odor, and gastritis [US].
- **ESRD Specific (≥5% regardless of causality):** 
    - **General:** Chest pain, headache, infection, injection site reaction, fever, flu syndrome.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia.
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea, dyspepsia.
    - **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia.
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnea.
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness, paresthesia.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Seizures, anaphylaxis, and severe hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels frequently after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly lettered]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. A decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the risks to the child against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to the "Metabolic Disorders" dosing section for weight-based administration instructions [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing is specifically tailored for ESRD patients on hemodialysis (see Section 3). Use of oral formulations is cautioned in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities caused by the accumulation of toxic organic acids and corrects carnitine deficiency (defined as <20 µmol/L at one week post-term) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine levels (initially ~20 µmol/L) rose to between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week of therapy. 
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg confirmed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. While efficacy in increasing carnitine levels is proven, the direct effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
