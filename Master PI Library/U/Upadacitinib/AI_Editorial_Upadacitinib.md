# Editorial Product Information: Upadacitinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the approved Product Information for **upadacitinib**. It is intended as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) in any specific jurisdiction. Prescribers must consult the current approved documentation in their local region before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Upadacitinib (as hemihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Janus Kinase (JAK) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **RINVOQ** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AA44.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Modified-release tablets: 15 mg, 30 mg, and 45 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AbbVie Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Upadacitinib is indicated for the following conditions [Australian]:
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):** Moderate to severe active RA in adults with inadequate response or intolerance to one or more DMARDs. Used as monotherapy or with methotrexate/csDMARDs.
- **Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA):** Moderate to severe active PsA in adults with inadequate response or intolerance to one or more DMARDs. Used as monotherapy or with non-biological DMARDs.
- **Non-radiographic Axial Spondyloarthritis (nr-axSpA):** Active nr-axSpA in adults with objective signs of inflammation (elevated CRP or MRI changes) and inadequate response to NSAIDs.
- **Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS):** Active AS in adults.
- **Atopic Dermatitis (AD):** Moderate to severe AD in adults and adolescents (≥12 years, weighing ≥40 kg) who are candidates for systemic therapy and have failed topical therapies.
- **Ulcerative Colitis (UC):** Moderate to severe active UC in adults with inadequate response, loss of response, or intolerance to conventional or biological therapies.
- **Crohn’s Disease (CD):** Moderate to severe active CD in adults with inadequate response, loss of response, or intolerance to conventional or biological therapies.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- Therapy must be initiated and monitored by a specialist experienced in the use of immunomodulatory agents for the specific indication.
- Tablets must be swallowed whole; they should not be crushed, split, or chewed.
- Can be taken with or without food.

### Dosing by Indication [Australian]
- **RA, PsA, AS, and nr-axSpA:** 15 mg once daily.
- **Atopic Dermatitis:**
    - *Adults <65 years:* Initially 15 mg once daily. May be increased to 30 mg once daily after 4 weeks if clinically warranted.
    - *Adults ≥65 years:* 15 mg once daily.
    - *Adolescents (12–17 years, ≥40 kg):* 15 mg once daily.
- **Ulcerative Colitis:**
    - *Induction:* 45 mg once daily for 8 weeks. May be extended for a further 8 weeks (total 16 weeks) if benefit is not reached. Discontinue if response is not achieved by Week 16.
    - *Maintenance:* 15 mg once daily. 30 mg once daily may be considered for patients with severe/extensive disease or those with inadequate response to 15 mg. Use the lowest effective dose.
- **Crohn’s Disease:**
    - *Induction:* 45 mg once daily for 12 weeks. If benefit is not reached, 30 mg once daily may be continued for another 12 weeks. Discontinue if no response by Week 24.
    - *Maintenance:* 15 mg once daily. 30 mg once daily may be considered for patients at low risk of VTE/MACE/Malignancy who have refractory or extensive disease.

### Missed Dose
- If the missed dose is remembered **more than 10 hours** before the next scheduled dose, take the missed dose immediately.
- If **less than 10 hours** remain before the next dose, skip the missed dose and resume the normal schedule.
- Do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to upadacitinib or any inactive ingredients.
- Use in combination with biological DMARDs (bDMARDs) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Major Warnings [Australian]
Based on a post-marketing study of another JAK inhibitor (tofacitinib), upadacitinib should only be used if no suitable treatment alternatives are available in patients:
- Aged 65 years and older.
- Who are current or past long-time smokers.
- With a history of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease or other cardiovascular risk factors.
- With malignancy risk factors (e.g., current or history of malignancy).

### General Warnings
- **Serious Infections:** Increased risk of pneumonia, cellulitis, and opportunistic infections (tuberculosis, multi-dermatomal herpes zoster, candidiasis). Avoid initiation during active serious infections.
- **Thrombosis:** Serious and sometimes fatal events, including deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), and arterial thrombosis, have occurred. Use with caution in patients with high VTE risk.
- **MACE:** Increased risk of cardiovascular death, non-fatal MI, and non-fatal stroke.
- **Malignancy:** Increased risk of lymphoma and non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC). Periodic skin examinations are recommended.
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Use with caution in patients with a history of diverticulitis or those using NSAIDs, corticosteroids, or opioids.
- **Retinal Vein Occlusion:** Reported with JAK inhibitors; patients should seek prompt medical care for sudden vision changes.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** May cause neutropenia, lymphopenia, anaemia, lipid elevations, and transaminase elevations.

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Baseline:** Neutrophils (ANC), Lymphocytes (ALC), Haemoglobin (Hb), LFTs, and viral hepatitis screening (HBV/HCV). Screen for TB.
- **Lipids:** Monitor 12 weeks after initiation.
- **Routine:** Periodic monitoring of ANC, ALC, Hb, and LFTs is required. Interrupt therapy if ANC <1.0 x $10^9$ cells/L, ALC <0.5 x $10^9$ cells/L, or Hb <80 g/L.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies vary by indication and dose (15 mg vs 30 mg vs 45 mg).

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Upper respiratory tract infections (URTI), acne [in AD/UC/CD]. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Bronchitis, herpes zoster, herpes simplex, folliculitis, headache, cough, nausea, pyrexia, increased CPK, increased ALT/AST, weight increase, hypercholesterolemia. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Pneumonia, oral candidiasis, hypertriglyceridemia, NMSC, anaemia [in AD]. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Serious infections, MACE, VTE (DVT/PE), and GI perforation.
**Post-marketing:** Semen discolouration (blue/green) has been reported, primarily with the 45 mg dose; this is typically transient and not clinically meaningful [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, grapefruit):** Increase upadacitinib exposure.
    - *RA/PsA/AS/nr-axSpA:* Use 15 mg once daily with caution.
    - *AD:* 30 mg once daily is not recommended with chronic strong inhibitors.
    - *UC/CD Induction:* Reduce dose to 30 mg once daily.
    - *UC/CD Maintenance:* Use 15 mg once daily.
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin):** Decrease upadacitinib exposure; avoid use as it may reduce efficacy.
- **OATP1B Inhibitors:** No clinically relevant effect.
- **Other Immunosuppressants:** Combination with potent immunosuppressants (e.g., azathioprine, ciclosporin) or other JAK inhibitors has not been evaluated and is not recommended.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category D** [Australian]. Upadacitinib is teratogenic based on animal data (skeletal and cardiac malformations). It should not be used during pregnancy.
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for 4 weeks after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use is contraindicated. Animal data show high excretion in milk (30-fold higher than plasma); risks to the newborn cannot be excluded.
- **Fertility:** No reduction in fertility observed in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety/efficacy not established in children <18 years for RA, PsA, AS, nr-axSpA, UC, or CD. Approved for AD in adolescents ≥12 years weighing ≥40 kg.
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** Increased risk of serious infections and MACE. Recommended maintenance dose is 15 mg once daily.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - *Stage 1-2 (eGFR ≥60):* No dose adjustment.
    - *Stage 3-4 (eGFR 15-60):* RA/PsA/AS/AD (15 mg QD); UC/CD Induction (30 mg QD); UC/CD Maintenance (15 mg QD).
    - *Stage 5 (eGFR <15):* Not recommended (not studied) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - *Mild/Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):* No dose adjustment.
    - *Severe (Child-Pugh C):* Not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective and reversible inhibitor of JAK1. It inhibits JAK1-mediated signaling of pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, IFN-$\gamma$, IL-4, IL-13) while showing significantly less potency against JAK2 (involved in erythropoiesis) and JAK3.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - *Absorption:* Tmax 2–4 hours. High-fat meals increase AUC by 29% but have no clinically relevant effect.
    - *Distribution:* 59% plasma protein bound.
    - *Metabolism:* Primarily CYP3A4, with minor contribution from CYP2D6. No active metabolites.
    - *Elimination:* Terminal half-life of 9–14 hours. 24% excreted unchanged in urine; 38% unchanged in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RA:** The SELECT program demonstrated superiority over placebo and adalimumab (in SELECT-COMPARE) across ACR20/50/70 responses and radiographic progression.
- **AD:** The MEASURE UP and AD UP trials showed significantly higher EASI 75 and vIGA-AD 0/1 responses compared to placebo.
- **UC/CD:** The U-ACHIEVE, U-ACCOMPLISH, and U-EXCEED trials demonstrated significant rates of clinical remission and endoscopic response during induction and maintenance phases.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- Keep in the original blister pack to protect from moisture.
