# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local regulatory documentation before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical class: carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported clinical dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are supra-normal [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If severe, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit analysis [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with renal failure or ESRD on dialysis has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in this population may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### General
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting. 
- **Less frequent:** Gastritis and a "fishy" body odor [US].

### Patients with ESRD on Hemodialysis (Frequency ≥5%)
Data from placebo-controlled trials indicate the following events occurred regardless of causality [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (21%), Asthenia (12%), Chest pain (15%), Fever (12%), Flu syndrome (29%), Headache (37%), Injection site reaction (38%), Infection (24%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (21%), Hypotension (19%), Tachycardia (9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (35%), Vomiting (21%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (15%), Hypervolemia (12%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (18%), Paresthesia (12%).
- **Respiratory:** Cough increased (18%), Pharyngitis (27%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at treatment initiation and following any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category B (contextual)]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women; use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies on levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers are unavailable. While carnitine is a normal component of human milk, the risks of excess intake in the infant versus the benefits to the mother must be weighed [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical documents do not specify specific dose adjustments; however, caution is advised as with all medications in the elderly due to potential comorbidities [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis. Avoid high-dose *oral* formulations in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process facilitates fatty acid oxidation and subsequent energy production in skeletal and cardiac muscle [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine over 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x/week post-dialysis), mean trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While increases in carnitine concentrations are well-documented, the specific effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in ESRD has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
