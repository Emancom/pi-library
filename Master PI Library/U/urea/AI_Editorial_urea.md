# Editorial Product Information: Urea

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Diabact UBT 50mg tablet
    - **US (FDA)**: Uredex 20% Cream (Blue Heron Pharmaceuticals, LLC)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing or dispensing in a specific jurisdiction. The US source provided contains a disclaimer that the product has not been found by the FDA to be safe and effective, and the labeling is not FDA-approved.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Urea (Generic name); Urea [¹³C] (Stable isotope used for diagnostic purposes)
- **Chemical Class:** Diamide of carbonic acid
- **Brand Names:** 
    - **Diabact UBT** [Australian]
    - **Uredex** [US]
- **ATC Code:** V04CX (Other diagnostic agents) [Australian]
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Oral Tablet:** 50 mg urea [¹³C] [Australian]
    - **Topical Cream:** 20% (200 mg urea per gram) [US]
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral (diagnostic); Topical (therapeutic)
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Medical Diagnostics Australasia Pty Ltd [Australian]
    - Blue Heron Pharmaceuticals, LLC [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
Indications vary significantly by route of administration:

- **In vivo diagnosis of gastroduodenal *Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori)* infection** [Australian-approved; not specified in US source].
- **Debridement and promotion of normal healing** of hyperkeratotic surface lesions, particularly where healing is retarded by infection, necrotic tissue, or debris [US-approved; not specified in Australian source].
- **Treatment of hyperkeratotic conditions:** Including dry/rough skin, dermatitis, psoriasis, xerosis, ichthyosis, eczema, keratosis pilaris, keratosis palmaris, keratoderma, corns, calluses, and damaged/ingrown/devitalized nails [US-approved; not specified in Australian source].

## 3. Dosage and Administration

### Oral Administration (Diagnostic) [Australian]
- **Adult Dosage:** A single 50 mg tablet per test occasion.
- **Preparation:** The patient must fast for at least 6 hours prior to the test. If fasting is problematic, a light breakfast (e.g., tea and toast) is permissible, but a heavy meal requires a 6-hour fast.
- **Procedure:**
    1. Collect baseline breath samples (00-minute).
    2. Swallow the 50 mg tablet whole with a glass of water. **Do not chew** (chewing increases the risk of false positives).
    3. Wait 10 minutes.
    4. Collect post-urea breath samples (10-minute).

### Topical Administration (Therapeutic) [US]
- **Dosage:** Apply to affected area(s) twice daily or as directed by a physician.
- **Administration:** Rub in until completely absorbed. For diseased or damaged nails, apply twice daily to the nail surface.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Documented or suspected hypersensitivity to urea or any excipients.
- **Diagnostic Interference:** Use is contraindicated in patients with documented or suspected gastric infections that might interfere with the urea breath test [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **External Use Only:** Topical urea is for external use only and is not for ophthalmic use [US].
- **Gastric Malignancy:** A positive urea breath test does not confirm the need for eradication therapy alone; differential diagnosis via endoscopy may be required to exclude gastric ulcers, autoimmune gastritis, or malignancies [Australian].
- **Atrophic Gastritis:** May result in false-positive breath test results [Australian].
- **Partial Gastrectomy:** Insufficient data exists regarding the reliability of the urea breath test in patients with partial gastrectomy [Australian].
- **Chewing Tablets:** If the diagnostic tablet is chewed, the test must be repeated the following day to avoid false-positive outcomes [Australian].
- **Patient Safety:** Keep out of reach of children [US]. Discontinue topical use if redness, irritation, or rash develops [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects differ by route of administration:

### Oral [¹³C] Urea [Australian]
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>1%)** | Taste disturbance |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, parosmia, fatigue |

*Note: Oral events were generally mild to moderate, transient, and resolved without treatment.*

### Topical Urea [US]
- **Frequency not specified:** Transient stinging, burning, itching, or irritation may occur. These typically disappear upon discontinuation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antibiotics and Bismuth:** Use of systemic antibacterials within four (4) weeks of a urea breath test may lead to false-negative results [Australian].
- **Acid Antisecretory Agents:** Proton-pump inhibitors (PPIs) should be discontinued at least two (2) weeks before the urea breath test to avoid interference with *H. pylori* status or urease activity [Australian].
- **Topical Interactions:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **Australian Category B2:** Limited data in pregnant women; no observed increase in malformations. Endogenous urea production (25–35 g/day) far exceeds the 50 mg diagnostic dose. Use is permitted during pregnancy.
    - **US Category C:** Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. Use only if clearly needed and benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:**
    - It is unknown if urea is excreted in human milk. Caution is advised for both oral [Australian] and topical [US] administration. However, the Australian source notes the 50 mg oral dose is unlikely to cause adverse effects due to high endogenous urea levels.
- **Fertility:** No specific studies have been conducted on the impact of urea on human fertility [Australian][US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - **Australian:** Insufficient data to recommend use in patients under 18 years of age.
    - **US:** Not specified; follow physician directions.
- **Elderly:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Diagnostic (Oral):** *H. pylori* produces the enzyme urease. Upon ingestion, ¹³C-labeled urea is metabolised by bacterial urease into ammonia and ¹³CO₂. The ¹³CO₂ is absorbed, transported to the lungs, and exhaled. A change in the ¹³C/¹²C isotope ratio in the breath indicates infection [Australian].
    - **Therapeutic (Topical):** Urea dissolves the intercellular matrix, leading to the loosening of the horny layer of the skin and shedding of scaly skin. This softens hyperkeratotic areas [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Oral:** Urea is rapidly absorbed from the GI tract and distributed to extracellular and intracellular fluids. It is reported to cross the placenta and penetrate the eye. Excreted unchanged in the urine [Australian].
    - **Topical:** The specific mechanism of action of topically applied urea is not yet known [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Diagnostic Efficacy [Australian]:** Data from six open studies (n=1,413) established a cut-off point of 1.5‰ (parts per thousand) for the ¹³C/¹²C ratio to identify infection. 
    - **Sensitivity:** 96% to 98%.
    - **Specificity:** 100%.
- **Topical Efficacy [US]:** No specific trial data provided in the source document.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Oral Tablets:** Store below 25°C in the original package to protect from light [Australian].
- **Topical Cream:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:**
    - **Australian:** Not scheduled.
    - **US:** Rx-only (Prescription drug).
- **Regulatory Status:** The US product (Uredex) is listed as an "unapproved drug other," meaning it has not been found by the FDA to be safe and effective [US].
