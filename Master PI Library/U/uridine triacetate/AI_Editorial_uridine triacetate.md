# Editorial Product Information: Uridine Triacetate

## Editorial Note
- This harmonised editorial overview has been developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a comprehensive, clinically accurate editorial synthesis for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial summary and does not replace the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the United States PI was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Uridine triacetate (a pyrimidine analog).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Vistogard** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral granules, 10 grams per packet (95% w/w) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral; may be administered via nasogastric (NG) or gastrostomy (G-tube) [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** BTG International Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Uridine triacetate** is indicated for the emergency treatment of adult and paediatric patients [US]:
- Following an overdose of fluorouracil or capecitabine, regardless of the presence of clinical symptoms.
- Who exhibit early-onset, severe, or life-threatening toxicity affecting the central nervous system or cardiac system.
- Who exhibit unusually severe adverse reactions (e.g., gastrointestinal toxicity, neutropenia) within 96 hours of the completion of fluorouracil or capecitabine administration.

**Limitations of Use [US]:**
- Not recommended for the non-emergent management of adverse reactions associated with fluorouracil or capecitabine, as it may diminish the efficacy of these chemotherapeutic agents.
- Safety and efficacy have not been established when initiated more than 96 hours after the end of fluorouracil or capecitabine administration.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Dosage:** 10 grams (one full packet) administered orally every 6 hours for 20 doses [US].
- **Timing:** Treatment should be initiated as soon as possible after overdose or the identification of early-onset toxicity (within 96 hours). The full course of 20 doses must be completed [US].
- **Relation to Meals:** May be administered without regard to meals [US].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Dosage:** 6.2 grams/m² of body surface area (BSA) administered orally every 6 hours for 20 doses [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** Not to exceed 10 grams per dose [US].
- **Measurement:** Doses should be measured using a scale accurate to 0.1 g or a graduated teaspoon accurate to 1/4 teaspoon [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Oral Administration:** Mix each dose with 3 to 4 ounces of soft food (e.g., applesauce, pudding, yogurt). Ingest within 30 minutes of mixing. **Do not chew** the granules. Follow with at least 4 ounces of water [US].
- **Enteral Tube (NG/G-tube) Administration:** 
    1. Prepare 100 mL of a food starch-based thickening product in water.
    2. Crush the 10-gram packet of granules into a fine powder.
    3. Add powder to the thickened mixture (for paediatric doses <10g, use a ratio no greater than 1g per 10mL). 
    4. Mix thoroughly and flush the tube with water after administration [US].

### Missed Dose and Vomiting
- **Vomiting:** If the patient vomits within 2 hours of a dose, a complete replacement dose should be administered as soon as possible. The next dose should still be taken at the regularly scheduled time [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, administer it as soon as possible and resume the original schedule for the subsequent dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Efficacy Interference:** Use is restricted to emergency settings; non-emergent use may reduce the anti-tumour efficacy of fluorouracil or capecitabine [US].
- **Monitoring:** While no specific laboratory monitoring is mandated for the antidote itself, patients should be monitored for the progression of fluorouracil/capecitabine-induced toxicities (cardiac, neurologic, and haematologic).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions were assessed in 135 patients. The discontinuation rate due to adverse events was 1.4% [US].

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (N=135) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | |
| Vomiting | 10% |
| Nausea | 5% |
| Diarrhea | 3% |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** One patient (0.7%) experienced Grade 3 nausea and vomiting [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **In Vitro Data:** Uridine triacetate and its metabolite uridine do not inhibit major CYP enzymes (3A4, 1A2, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1) nor do they induce CYP1A2, 2B6, or 3A4 [US].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** Uridine triacetate is a weak substrate for P-gp and inhibits the transport of digoxin (a P-gp substrate) in vitro (IC50 = 344 µM). Interactions with orally administered P-gp substrates cannot be ruled out due to high local concentrations in the gut [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Available case reports are insufficient to determine a drug-associated risk. Animal studies in rats using doses equivalent to one-half the Maximum Recommended Human Dose (MRHD) showed no evidence of teratogenicity or embryo-fetal developmental toxicity.
- **Breastfeeding:** [US]: There are no data regarding the presence of uridine triacetate in human milk, effects on the breastfed infant, or effects on milk production. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother’s clinical need for the drug.
- **Fertility:** Orally administered uridine triacetate did not affect fertility or general reproductive performance in rats at doses up to one-half the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness are established. Data from 6 paediatric patients (ages 1 to 16 years) showed safety and clinical response profiles similar to adults [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** In clinical trials, 30% of patients were ≥65 years and 11% were ≥75 years. No specific differences in safety or efficacy were identified compared to younger patients, though data are limited [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, uridine is metabolized via normal pyrimidine catabolic pathways present in most tissues [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Uridine triacetate is an acetylated prodrug of uridine. Following oral administration, it is deacetylated by non-specific esterases. The resulting uridine in the circulation competitively inhibits the cell damage and cell death caused by fluorouracil. Fluorouracil toxicity occurs via incorporation of its metabolite (FUTP) into RNA and inhibition of thymidylate synthase; supplemental uridine is converted to UTP, which competes with FUTP for RNA incorporation [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Delivers 4- to 6-fold more uridine into systemic circulation than equimolar doses of uridine. Tmax is 2 to 3 hours [US].
- **Food Effect:** No difference in the rate or extent of exposure between fed and fasted states [US].
- **Distribution:** Uridine is taken up by mammalian cells via specific nucleoside transporters and crosses the blood-brain barrier [US].
- **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 2 to 2.5 hours. Excreted via the kidneys and catabolised by pyrimidine pathways [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Efficacy was demonstrated in two open-label trials (n=135) involving patients with fluorouracil/capecitabine overdose or early-onset toxicity. 
- **Survival Outcome:** Overall survival was 96% (130/135). In the overdose subgroup (n=117), survival was 97%. In the early-onset toxicity subgroup (n=18), survival was 89% [US].
- **Historical Comparison:** In a retrospective cohort of patients who received supportive care only for fluorouracil overdose, the mortality rate was 84% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
