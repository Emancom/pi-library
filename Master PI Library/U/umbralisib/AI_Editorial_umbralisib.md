# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was developed based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for specific prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic), Carnitor (Reference product).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with a confirmed inborn error of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours [US].
    - Subsequent daily maintenance doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range but may be adjusted based on clinical response, up to a maximum reported dose of 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- For ESRD patients, therapy may be prompted if trough plasma concentrations fall below the normal range of 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L [US].

### Monitoring
- It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before therapy [US].
- Routine monitoring should include weekly and monthly blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and treat appropriately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions in Renal Impairment:** While this document covers the injection, it is noted that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect profiles can be confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Very Common (>10%) [US]
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Abdominal pain (21% at 10mg dose), Fever (12%), Asthenia (12%), Accidental injury (12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14-19%), Chest pain (15%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16-35%), Vomiting (15-21%), Nausea (12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23-27%), Cough increase (18%), Dyspnea (11%), Rhinitis (11%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (10-18%), Paresthesia (12%).

### Common (1–10%) [US]
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6-15%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia (5-9%), Palpitations (8%), Atrial fibrillation (2-6%).
- **Other:** Anemia (12%), Dyspepsia (6-9%), Weight decrease (8%), Leg cramps (8%), Pruritus (8%), Eye disorder (6%), Taste perversion (9%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress (bronchospasm/laryngeal edema) [US].
- Convulsions/Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered in provided source, but states "should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed." 
    - **Animal Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While carnitine is a normal component of human milk, the risks of excess intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) for metabolic disorders; specific safety profiles in neonates and premature infants focus on defining deficiency as plasma levels below age-related normals [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **ESRD on Dialysis:** Indicated for use; requires post-dialysis dosing due to dialytic losses [US].
    - **Note:** Accumulation of metabolites TMA and TMAO is a concern with high-dose *oral* therapy in renal failure [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations and may alleviate metabolic abnormalities related to the accumulation of toxic organic acids [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis, mean trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations rose from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed a dose-proportional increase in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL concentration) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only; Classified as a Human Prescription Drug (ANDA 217896) [US].
