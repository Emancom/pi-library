# Editorial Product Information: Ublituximab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis has been compiled using official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**
    - **US (Food and Drug Administration - FDA)**
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs) by integrating regulatory data from multiple regions. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for full prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ublituximab (known as ublituximab-xiiy in the [US])
- **Chemical Class:** CD20-directed chimeric monoclonal antibody (IgG1)
- **Brand Name:** **Briumvi**
- **ATC Code:** L04AG14
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrate for solution for infusion; 150 mg/6 mL (25 mg/mL) in a single-dose vial.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - [Australian]: Neuraxpharm Australia Pty Ltd.
    - [US]: TG Therapeutics, Inc.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsing Forms of Multiple Sclerosis (RMS):** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with active disease defined by clinical or imaging features.
- **Specific Clinical Classifications [US]:** Explicitly includes clinically isolated syndrome (CIS), relapsing-remitting disease (RRMS), and active secondary progressive disease (SPMS) in adults.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Schedule (Adults)
The treatment consists of an initial loading phase followed by maintenance infusions every 24 weeks.
- **First Infusion:** 150 mg IV infusion.
- **Second Infusion:** 450 mg IV infusion, administered 2 weeks after the first infusion.
- **Subsequent Infusions:** 450 mg IV infusion administered 24 weeks after the first infusion and every 24 weeks thereafter.
- **Minimum Interval:** A minimum interval of 5 months should be maintained between maintenance doses.

### Premedication
To reduce the frequency and severity of infusion-related reactions (IRRs), the following must be administered 30–60 minutes prior to every infusion:
- **Corticosteroid:** 100 mg methylprednisolone IV (or an equivalent). [Australian: specifies 10–20 mg dexamethasone as an alternative].
- **Antihistamine:** Diphenhydramine (or equivalent) orally or IV.
- **Antipyretic:** Acetaminophen/paracetamol may also be considered.

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride to a final concentration of ~0.6 mg/mL (first dose) or ~1.8 mg/mL (subsequent doses).
- **Rate:** 
    - **First Infusion (4 hours):** Start at 10 mL/hr, titrating up to 100 mL/hr.
    - **Subsequent Infusions (1 hour):** Start at 100 mL/hr for 30 minutes, then increase to 400 mL/hr.
- **Observation:** Monitor patients for at least one hour after the completion of the first two infusions. Subsequent monitoring is at physician discretion unless IRRs occurred previously.

### Infusion Adjustments for IRRs
- **Life-threatening/Disabling:** Stop immediately and permanently discontinue.
- **Severe:** Interrupt infusion; restart at half the previous rate only after symptoms resolve.
- **Mild to Moderate:** Reduce rate to half the onset rate for at least 30 minutes; if tolerated, resume titration.

### Missed Doses
Administer the missed dose as soon as possible. Do not wait for the next scheduled dose. Reset the 24-week schedule from the date of the administered missed dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Active Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) infection.
- History of life-threatening infusion reaction to ublituximab [US].
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients [Australian].
- Patients in a severely immunocompromised state [Australian].
- Severe active infections [Australian].
- Known active malignancies [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs):** Highly common, especially with the first dose. Symptoms include pyrexia, chills, headache, tachycardia, and anaphylaxis. Reactions can occur up to 24 hours post-infusion.
- **Infections:** Increased risk of serious, life-threatening, or fatal bacterial and viral infections. Delay administration if an active infection is present.
- **Hepatitis B Reactivation:** Fulminant hepatitis and death have occurred with anti-CD20 antibodies. Screen all patients for HBV before initiation.
- **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML):** JCV infection leading to PML has been observed with anti-CD20 therapies. Monitor for new or worsening neurological symptoms. If suspected, withhold ublituximab; if confirmed, discontinue permanently.
- **Reduction in Immunoglobulins:** Decreases in total immunoglobulins (primarily IgM) are expected. Monitor levels before, during, and after treatment. Consider discontinuation if serious opportunistic or recurrent infections develop.
- **Liver Injury:** Clinically significant liver injury has been reported post-marketing. Obtain liver function tests (ALT, AST, bilirubin) at baseline and monitor during treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Infusion-related reactions (45–48%), upper respiratory tract infections (45%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Nasopharyngitis, headache, pyrexia, nausea, lymphopenia, insomnia, fatigue, pain in extremity.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Serious infections (5% vs 3% for teriflunomide), serious IRRs (0.4–0.6%), and rare cases of PML.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Transient lymphopenia (91% at week 1), neutropenia (15%), and decreased IgM levels (21% at 96 weeks).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Immunosuppressants:** Concomitant use with other immune-modulating or immunosuppressive therapies (including corticosteroids for relapses) may increase infection risk. 
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted. Ublituximab is a protein cleared by catabolism; cytochrome P450 interactions are not expected.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** [Australian: Category C]. 
    - **Risk:** May cause fetal B-cell depletion based on animal data and the known transport of IgG across the placenta. Avoid use unless benefits outweigh risks. 
    - **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for a period after the last dose: **[Australian: 4 months; US: 6 months].**
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if ublituximab is excreted in human milk. Maternal IgG is excreted in milk; potential risk to the infant cannot be excluded.
- **Infant Vaccinations:** Do not administer live or live-attenuated vaccines to infants exposed in utero until B-cell recovery is confirmed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established.
- **Geriatric Use:** Limited data in patients >55 years [Australian] or >65 years [US]. No specific dose adjustments are currently recommended, but caution is advised.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is expected for mild to moderate impairment. Not studied in severe impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is expected for mild impairment. Not studied in moderate to severe impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Chimeric monoclonal antibody targeting CD20 on pre-B and mature B lymphocytes. It induces B-cell lysis through antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Results in rapid depletion of CD19+ B-cells (median reduction of 97% after the first infusion).
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Elimination Half-life:** Approximately 22 days.
    - **Metabolism:** Degradation into small peptides and amino acids by proteolytic enzymes.
    - **B-cell Repletion:** Median time to repletion is ~70 weeks after the last infusion.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** ULTIMATE I and ULTIMATE II (Phase III, randomised, double-blind, active-controlled trials).
- **Comparator:** Teriflunomide 14 mg orally once daily.
- **Efficacy Results:** 
    - **Annualised Relapse Rate (ARR):** Ublituximab significantly reduced ARR by 59% (Study 1) and 49% (Study 2) compared to teriflunomide.
    - **MRI Endpoints:** ~97% reduction in T1 Gd-enhancing lesions and ~90% reduction in new/enlarging T2 lesions compared to teriflunomide.
    - **Disability:** No statistically significant difference was observed in 12-week confirmed disability progression between groups in the pooled analysis (5.2% vs 5.9%).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Do not shake. Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Diluted Solution:** 
    - [Australian]: Stable for 24 hours at 2°C–8°C, and 8 hours at room temperature.
    - [US]: If not used immediately, store refrigerated at 2°C–8°C for up to 24 hours. Allow to equilibrate to room temperature for ~2 hours before infusion.

- **Legal Classification [US]:** Rx-only.
