# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)** for Levocarnitine Injection.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly specified in the source document).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - Acute Management: A loading dose is often administered in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Dosing Frequency: Every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h).
    - Maximum administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose Adjustments: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard unused portions as the formulation contains no preservatives.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. Most cases occurred in ESRD patients on dialysis. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs and monitor if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine Caution:** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine in renal impairment has not been evaluated. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic patients, monitoring of plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L), blood chemistry, and vital signs is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Headache (22%), Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Infection (15%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (13%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), Dyspnea (7–19% [US]).

### Common (1–10%)
- **General:** Abdominal pain, Asthenia, Back pain, Fever, Allergic reaction.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, Palpitations, Arrhythmia, Vascular disorder.
- **Digestive:** Nausea, Gastritis, Dyspepsia, Anorexia, Melena.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Hypervolemia, Peripheral edema, Weight changes.
- **Nervous:** Insomnia, Paresthesia, Anxiety, Depression, Vertigo.
- **Skin:** Pruritus, Rash, Body odor.
- **Special Senses:** Amblyopia, Eye disorder, Taste perversion.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity [US].
- Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not explicitly stated, but clinical data provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefits. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight; see Section 3 for specific metabolic disorder dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Use caution with high-dose *oral* formulations due to metabolite accumulation (see Section 5) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. In cases of organic acidemias, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours.
- **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations from a baseline trough of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine concentrations increased, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of opened vials; no preservative present [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only [US].
