# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- At the time of preparation, official PI documents from other major jurisdictions (Australian (TGA), European (EMA), UK (MHRA), Canadian (Health Canada), and Japanese (PMDA)) were not provided for comparison. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, legally approved PI in a specific jurisdiction when prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects (e.g., glutaric aciduria II, methylmalonic aciduria, propionic acidemia, and medium-chain fatty acyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency) that result in carnitine depletion [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients requiring chronic maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Doses should be given every 3 to 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours.
    - **Max Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered in clinical practice [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at 25°C when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None [US].

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit assessment [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulations):** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Plasma Concentrations:** Monitor trough levocarnitine levels (normal range 40–50 µmol/L). For patients with metabolic disorders, recommended plasma free carnitine levels are between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** Periodic blood chemistry, vital signs, and overall clinical status should be assessed [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% Frequency)
Data from clinical trials in ESRD patients on hemodialysis [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), abdominal pain (9%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), chest pain (12%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), peripheral edema (5%).

### Serious and Post-marketing Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C equivalent]. Animal reproductive studies (rats and rabbits) using up to 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders; 10–20 mg/kg for ESRD). See Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustments or age-related concerns are noted in the source document [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Note that the IV route is preferred over the oral route in this population to avoid metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also aids in the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In ESRD patients, levocarnitine injection increases plasma carnitine concentrations, which are often depleted due to dialysis losses, reduced renal synthesis, and limited dietary intake [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not protein or albumin bound. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma carnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** A study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, clinical outcomes related to signs and symptoms of deficiency in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Vials are single-dose only; discard any unused portion [US].
