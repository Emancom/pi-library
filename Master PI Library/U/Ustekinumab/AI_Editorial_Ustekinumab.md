# Editorial Product Information: Ustekinumab

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesises product information derived from official regulatory documentation provided by the **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **ustekinumab** (specifically the biosimilar **EPYZTEK**) intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulatory guidelines and the current approved PI for their region before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ustekinumab
- **Chemical Class:** Human IgG1κ monoclonal antibody; Interleukin-12 (IL-12) and Interleukin-23 (IL-23) antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **EPYZTEK** (Biosimilar to Stelara) [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AC05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Solution for Subcutaneous (SC) Injection:** 45 mg in 0.5 mL and 90 mg in 1 mL in a single-use pre-filled syringe [Australian].
    - **Concentrate for Intravenous (IV) Infusion:** 130 mg in 26 mL (5 mg/mL) single-use vial [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection; Intravenous infusion (induction only for specific indications).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Samsung Bioepis AU Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Plaque Psoriasis (Adults):** Treatment of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis in patients (≥18 years) who are candidates for phototherapy or systemic therapy [Australian].
- **Plaque Psoriasis (Paediatric):** Treatment of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis in children and adolescents from 6 years of age who are inadequately controlled by, or intolerant to, other systemic therapies or phototherapies [Australian].
- **Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA):** Treatment of signs and symptoms of active psoriatic arthritis in adults, used alone or in combination with methotrexate, where response to previous non-biological DMARD therapy was inadequate [Australian].
- **Crohn’s Disease (CD):** Treatment of moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease in adults who have had an inadequate response, lost response, or were intolerant to conventional therapy or TNFα antagonists, or have medical contraindications to such therapies [Australian].
- **Ulcerative Colitis (UC):** Treatment of moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis in adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration

### Plaque Psoriasis and Psoriatic Arthritis (Adults)
- **General Dosing:** 45 mg administered at Weeks 0 and 4, followed by every 12 weeks.
- **Weight-Based Adjustment:** For patients weighing >100 kg, the dose may be increased to 90 mg at the same intervals.
- **Inadequate Response:** Consideration may be given to dosing every 8 weeks in those with a sub-optimal response to 12-weekly dosing. Discontinue if no response is observed by Week 28.

### Plaque Psoriasis (Paediatric, 6–17 years)
- **Weight ≥60 kg to ≤100 kg:** 45 mg at Weeks 0 and 4, then every 12 weeks.
- **Weight >100 kg:** 90 mg at Weeks 0 and 4, then every 12 weeks.
- **Note:** EPYZTEK pre-filled syringes do not allow for weight-based dosing for patients <60 kg; alternative ustekinumab products in vials must be used for these patients [Australian].

### Crohn’s Disease and Ulcerative Colitis (Adults)
- **Initial IV Induction (Single Tiered Dose):**
    - **≤55 kg:** 260 mg (2 vials)
    - **>55 kg to ≤85 kg:** 390 mg (3 vials)
    - **>85 kg:** 520 mg (4 vials)
- **SC Maintenance:** 90 mg SC administered 8 weeks after the IV induction, then every 8 weeks thereafter. Some patients may transition to 12-weekly SC dosing based on clinical judgment.

### Administration Instructions
- **SC Injection:** Avoid areas of tender, bruised, erythematous, or indurated skin. Patients may self-inject after proper training and physician approval.
- **IV Infusion:** Must be diluted in 0.9% sodium chloride or 0.45% sodium chloride and administered over at least one hour using an in-line, sterile, non-pyrogenic, low protein-binding filter (0.2 micrometer).

## 4. Contraindications
- Severe hypersensitivity to ustekinumab or any excipient (e.g., histidine, sucrose, polysorbate 80).
- Clinically important, active infection [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serious Infections:** Ustekinumab may increase risk of bacterial, fungal, and viral infections. Evaluation for tuberculosis (TB) is mandatory before treatment; do not initiate in patients with active TB.
- **Malignancies:** Potential increased risk of malignancy. Monitor all patients, particularly those >60 years or with history of immunosuppressant/PUVA therapy, for non-melanoma skin cancer.
- **Non-infectious Pneumonia:** Rare cases of interstitial, eosinophilic, and cryptogenic organizing pneumonia reported. Discontinue if respiratory failure or interstitial infiltrates occur.
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Rare neurological condition. Monitor for headache, seizures, or confusion; discontinue immediately if suspected.
- **Serious Skin Conditions:** Risk of exfoliative dermatitis and erythrodermic psoriasis. Discontinue if a drug reaction is suspected.
- **Immunisations:** Do not administer live vaccines (e.g., BCG) concurrently. Withhold live vaccines for at least 15 weeks after the last dose of ustekinumab and resume no earlier than 2 weeks post-vaccination.

## 6. Adverse Effects

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nasopharyngitis, headache. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Upper respiratory tract infection, dizziness, oropharyngeal pain, diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, pruritus, back pain, myalgia, arthralgia, fatigue, injection site erythema/pain. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Herpes zoster, cellulitis, dental infections, depression, nasal congestion, acne, injection site reactions (haematoma, induration, swelling). |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Allergic alveolitis, serious hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis, angioedema), exfoliative dermatitis. |

- **Serious Cardiovascular Events:** Reported in 0.3% of psoriasis patients in clinical trials; however, rates were comparable to the general population.
- **Immunogenicity:** Antibodies to ustekinumab developed in up to 12.4% of psoriasis/PsA patients [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Live Vaccines:** Contraindicated for concurrent use.
- **CYP450 Substrates:** Chronic inflammation can suppress CYP450 enzymes. As an IL-12/23 antagonist, ustekinumab may normalize these levels. Monitor drugs with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., warfarin, cyclosporine) upon initiation.
- **Concomitant Immunosuppressants:** Safety in combination with other biologics or phototherapy in psoriasis has not been fully evaluated. Concomitant methotrexate did not appear to impact safety/efficacy in PsA.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **B1** [Australian]. Limited human data; over 450 first-trimester exposures did not show increased malformation risk. Use only if benefits clearly outweigh potential fetal risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Ustekinumab is excreted in human milk in very small amounts. Decision to continue breastfeeding should consider the importance of the drug to the mother and the potential risk to the infant.
- **Infant Exposure In Utero:** It is recommended to wait 6 months after birth before administering live vaccines to infants exposed to ustekinumab in utero [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for plaque psoriasis in patients ≥6 years. Not studied in paediatric PsA, CD, or UC.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy observed in patients ≥65 years, though data in this cohort are limited.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Ustekinumab has not been studied in these populations; no dosage recommendations can be made.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Binds to the shared **p40 subunit** of IL-12 and IL-23, preventing their binding to the IL-12Rβ1 receptor on immune cells. This disrupts Th1 and Th17 cytokine pathways (IFNγ, IL-17A, IL-21, IL-22) central to the pathology of psoriasis and inflammatory bowel disease.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median Tmax is ~8.5 days (SC); absolute bioavailability is ~57.2%.
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is ~4.4 to 4.6 L in IBD patients.
    - **Elimination:** Median half-life is ~3 weeks (range 15–32 days). Clearance is unaffected by age, sex, or race, but is increased in patients with higher body weight and those with diabetes.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PHOENIX 1 & 2:** Demonstrated significant PASI 75 response in adults with psoriasis (~67% vs 3-4% for placebo) at Week 12.
- **ACCEPT:** Showed superiority of ustekinumab over etanercept in achieving PASI 75 at Week 12.
- **UNITI/IM-UNITI:** Demonstrated efficacy in Crohn's disease for both induction (IV) and maintenance (SC) across TNF-α antagonist-naive and experienced populations.
- **UNIFI:** Confirmed efficacy in ulcerative colitis for clinical remission and mucosal healing.
- **SB17-3001:** Pivotal study for the biosimilar **EPYZTEK** showing equivalent efficacy and comparable safety/immunogenicity to the reference product (Stelara) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze.
- **Light:** Store in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Room Temperature Window:** Pre-filled syringes (not vials) may be stored at up to 30°C for a single period of up to 30 days. Once stored at room temperature, they must not be returned to refrigeration and must be discarded if not used within the 30-day window [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring in Australia for new safety information [Australian].
