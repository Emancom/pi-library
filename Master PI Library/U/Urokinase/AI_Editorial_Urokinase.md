# Editorial Product Information: Urokinase

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview developed by an expert clinical pharmacist for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a coherent clinical reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, legally approved PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Urokinase (Low molecular weight form).
- **Chemical Class:** Thrombolytic agent (Enzyme).
- **Brand Names:** **Kinlytic** [US].
- **ATC Code:** B01AD04.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile lyophilized white powder for injection; 250,000 International Units (IU) per vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ImaRx Therapeutics, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pulmonary Embolism (Adults):** Lysis of acute massive pulmonary emboli, defined as obstruction of blood flow to a lobe or multiple segments [US-approved].
- **Hemodynamically Unstable Pulmonary Embolism (Adults):** Lysis of pulmonary emboli accompanied by unstable hemodynamics (e.g., failure to maintain blood pressure without supportive measures) [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Urokinase therapy should be initiated as soon as possible after the onset of pulmonary embolism; delays may reduce clinical efficacy.

- **Loading Dose:** 4,400 IU/kg of body weight administered at a rate of 90 mL/hour over 10 minutes.
- **Continuous Infusion:** 4,400 IU/kg/hour administered at a rate of 15 mL/hour for a total duration of 12 hours.
- **Repeat Administration:** Treatment may be repeated as clinically necessary [US].

### Preparation and Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Aseptically add 5 mL of Sterile Water for Injection, USP. **Do not use** Bacteriostatic Water for Injection.
- **Handling:** To minimize the formation of filaments, roll and tilt the vial; do not shake. Reconstituted solution should be pale and straw-colored. Thin translucent filaments may occasionally occur but do not indicate a loss of potency.
- **Dilution:** Reconstituted urokinase must be diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Dextrose Injection prior to infusion.
- **Filtration:** The solution may be filtered through a 0.45 micron or smaller cellulose membrane filter.
- **Administration:** Deliver via a constant infusion pump. At the end of the cycle, perform a flush procedure using an equal volume of the primary diluent to ensure the total dose is delivered.
- **Compatibility:** No other medications should be added to the urokinase solution or administered through the same line.

## 4. Contraindications
Urokinase is contraindicated in the following clinical scenarios [US]:
- Active internal bleeding.
- Recent (within two months) cerebrovascular accident (CVA).
- Recent (within two months) intracranial or intraspinal surgery.
- Recent trauma, including cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR).
- Intracranial neoplasm, arteriovenous malformation, or aneurysm.
- Known bleeding diatheses.
- Severe uncontrolled arterial hypertension.
- Known hypersensitivity to urokinase or any component of the formulation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Bleeding
The most significant risk associated with urokinase is serious, potentially fatal hemorrhage (including intracranial and retroperitoneal).
- **Monitoring:** Monitor all potential bleeding sites (catheter insertion sites, arterial/venous punctures, etc.).
- **Clinical Management:** Avoid intramuscular injections and nonessential handling of the patient. If arterial puncture is required, use upper extremity vessels and apply pressure for at least 30 minutes.
- **High-Risk Conditions:** Weigh benefits against risks in patients with recent (within 10 days) major surgery, obstetric delivery, organ biopsy, serious GI bleeding, pregnancy, or diabetic hemorrhagic retinopathy.

### Anaphylaxis and Infusion Reactions
Hypersensitivity reactions, including fatal anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and orolingual edema, have been reported. Infusion reactions (fever, chills, rigors) typically occur within one hour of starting the infusion. If a reaction occurs, the infusion should be discontinued and appropriate therapy (antihistamines, corticosteroids) instituted.

### Cholesterol Embolization
Though rare, cholesterol embolism can be serious or lethal. Clinical features may include livedo reticularis, "purple toe" syndrome, acute renal failure, and myocardial or cerebral infarction.

### Product Source Material
Urokinase is derived from human neonatal kidney cells. While donor screening and validated inactivation processes (including heat treatment) are employed to reduce the risk of viral transmission, the risk of transmitting infectious agents, including those causing Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD), cannot be totally eliminated [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Bleeding (reported in 37% of patients in controlled trials, defined as a ≥5% decrease in hematocrit) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Not specifically categorized by this frequency in available sources.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Anaphylaxis [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Fatal hemorrhage (intracranial, retroperitoneal, gastrointestinal).
    - Myocardial infarction.
    - Recurrent pulmonary embolism.
    - Stroke or hemiplegia.
    - Cholesterol embolization.
    - Cardiac arrest.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets:** Concurrent use of heparin, oral anticoagulants, or agents inhibiting platelet function (e.g., aspirin, NSAIDs, dipyridamole, GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors) increases the risk of serious bleeding [US].
- **Thrombolytics:** Administration of other thrombolytic agents prior to, during, or after urokinase may further increase bleeding risk.
- **Specific Interaction Studies:** Formal drug interaction studies with urokinase have not been performed [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category B]. Animal reproduction studies at high doses showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Urokinase should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if urokinase is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in mice or rats at doses up to 1,000 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine differential responses. Use with caution in this population.
- **Renal Impairment:** Hemostatic defects secondary to severe renal disease may increase bleeding risk [US]. Specific dose adjustments are not provided in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Reduced urokinase clearance is expected in patients with hepatic impairment (e.g., 2- to 4-fold elevation of endogenous levels in cirrhosis). Use with caution due to increased bleeding risk associated with hemostatic defects in liver disease [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Urokinase is an enzyme that acts directly on the endogenous fibrinolytic system by converting plasminogen to the active enzyme plasmin. Plasmin subsequently degrades fibrin clots, fibrinogen, and other plasma proteins.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Intravenous infusion leads to increased circulating fibrinolytic activity. While fibrinogen levels decrease, effect on embolus resolution does not always correlate linearly with laboratory assay results. Fibrinogen levels may remain depressed for 12–24 hours post-infusion [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly cleared by the liver.
    - **Elimination Half-life:** 12.6 ± 6.2 minutes (for biologic activity).
    - **Excretion:** Small fractions are excreted via bile and urine.
    - **Distribution Volume:** 11.5 L [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** The Urokinase Pulmonary Embolism Trial (UPET) and the Urokinase-Streptokinase Pulmonary Embolism Trial (USPET).
- **Outcomes:** Treatment with urokinase demonstrated significantly greater improvement in pulmonary angiography, lung perfusion scans, and hemodynamic measurements within 24 hours compared to heparin. However, by day 7, lung perfusion scanning showed no significant difference between urokinase and heparin groups [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Storage:** Refrigerate lyophilized powder at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Contains no preservatives; do not reconstitute until immediately before use. Discard any unused portion.
