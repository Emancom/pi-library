# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single, cohesive reference. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing or dispensing in any particular region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN). 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in provided source (Generic: Levocarnitine Injection, USP).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - [US]: Sterile injection solution, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (q3h/q4h) and never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h). 
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations.

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended.
- **ESRD:** Therapy is often initiated when trough plasma carnitine levels fall below the normal range (40–50 µmol/L).

## 4. Contraindications
- [US]: None known.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred—primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated. 
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.
- **Renal Impairment (Oral formulation precaution):** While this PI covers the injection, it is noted that safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>5% in ESRD trials):**
    - **General:** Abdominal pain, headache, injection site reaction, fever, chest pain, flu syndrome.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea.
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnea.
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcaemia, hypervolaemia.
- **Uncommon/Rare:** Body odor, gastritis.
- **Post-marketing Experience:** Seizures, anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [US]: Category not explicitly lettered, but stated that reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg) and applicable to paediatric patients.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. 
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, carnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces). 
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, levocarnitine injection (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg) significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels remained stable or increased over 24 weeks of therapy. While biochemical efficacy is established, the impact on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.
- **Stability in Admixture:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL).
