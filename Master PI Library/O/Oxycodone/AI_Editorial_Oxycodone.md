# Editorial Product Information: Oxycodone Hydrochloride and Naloxone Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (TGA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the fixed-dose combination of oxycodone hydrochloride and naloxone hydrochloride modified-release tablets, intended for use by healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis of available regulatory information and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Oxycodone hydrochloride (opioid agonist) and naloxone hydrochloride (opioid antagonist).
- **Chemical Class:** Oxycodone is a semi-synthetic opioid; naloxone is a synthetic congener of oxymorphone.
- **Brand Names:** ARX-Oxycodone/Naloxone [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N02AA55
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Modified-release (prolonged-release) tablets. Available strengths (oxycodone/naloxone mg): 2.5/1.25, 5/2.5, 10/5, 15/7.5, 20/10, 30/15, 40/20, 60/30, and 80/40 [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mundipharma Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Pain:** Management of severe pain where opioid responsiveness is established, daily long-term treatment is required, and alternative non-opioid or immediate-release treatments have failed or are inappropriate [Australian].
- **Opioid-Induced Constipation (OIC):** Treatment or prophylaxis of constipation specifically associated with opioid therapy [Australian].
- **Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS):** Second-line symptomatic treatment for patients with severe to very severe idiopathic RLS following the failure of dopaminergic therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole. They must not be broken, chewed, or crushed, as this causes rapid release of oxycodone and may lead to a fatal overdose.
- **Analgesia (Adults/Elderly):**
    - **Opioid-naïve:** Usually starts at 10/5 mg every 12 hours.
    - **Opioid-tolerant:** Patients may be converted from other formulations using established equivalence tables.
    - **Titration:** Adjust every 1–2 days to achieve adequate analgesia.
    - **Maximum Dose:** 160/80 mg daily [Australian].
- **Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS):**
    - **Starting Dose:** 5/2.5 mg every 12 hours.
    - **Titration:** Adjusted on a weekly basis if required.
- **Hepatic/Renal Impairment:** In mild impairment, initiate at 1/3 to 1/2 of the standard dose with careful titration.
- **Missed Dose:** If the next dose is more than 8 hours away, take the missed dose immediately. If less than 8 hours remain, skip the missed dose and resume the normal schedule. Do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to oxycodone, naloxone, or any excipients.
- Severe respiratory depression or acute/severe respiratory disease.
- Elevated carbon dioxide levels in the blood.
- Moderate to severe hepatic impairment (naloxone plasma levels increase significantly).
- Cor pulmonale or cardiac arrhythmias.
- Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (severe) or uncontrolled bronchial asthma.
- Non-opioid induced paralytic ileus.
- Suspected surgical abdomen or delayed gastric emptying.
- Alcoholism or delirium tremens.
- Concurrent use of MAOIs (or within 14 days of cessation).
- Pregnancy and lactation [Australian].
- History of opioid abuse in the context of RLS treatment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Hazardous/Harmful Use):** Poses risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, which can lead to fatal overdose. Patients should be assessed and monitored for these risks [Australian].
- **Boxed Warning (Respiratory Depression):** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur. Risk is highest during initiation or following a dose increase [Australian].
- **Boxed Warning (CNS Depressants):** Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other CNS depressants (e.g., gabapentinoids) may result in profound sedation, coma, or death [Australian].
- **Sleep-Related Breathing Disorders:** Can cause dose-dependent central sleep apnoea (CSA).
- **Hyperalgesia:** Opioid-induced hyperalgesia may occur, requiring dose reduction or opioid rotation.
- **Tapering:** Abrupt discontinuation in dependent patients may cause withdrawal. Taper doses gradually (e.g., 10–25% every 2–4 weeks).
- **Gastrointestinal Effects:** While naloxone reduces OIC, the beneficial effect may be impaired if supplemental single-entity oxycodone is used.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Fatigue, headache, somnolence, nausea, constipation, hyperhidrosis.
- **Common (1–10%):** Vertigo, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, dry mouth, vomiting, decreased appetite, dizziness, insomnia, pruritus, rash, hot flush, asthenic conditions.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Palpitations (withdrawal-related), visual impairment, flatulence, peripheral oedema, chills, malaise, hepatic enzyme increases, hypersensitivity, muscle spasms/twitching, dysgeusia, tremor, convulsion, syncope, anxiety, confusion, depression, libido decrease, urinary urgency, dyspnoea, erectile dysfunction.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory depression, profound sedation, coma, and physical dependence leading to withdrawal syndrome.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Alcohol, benzodiazepines, and other opioids significantly increase the risk of respiratory depression and death.
- **Anticholinergics:** Concurrent use may increase risks of urinary retention and severe constipation.
- **Serotonergic Agents:** Increased risk of serotonin toxicity (agitation, tachycardia, hyperreflexia) when used with SSRIs or SNRIs.
- **CYP3A4/2D6 Interactions:**
    - **Inhibitors (e.g., clarithromycin, ketoconazole, paroxetine):** May increase oxycodone plasma concentrations.
    - **Inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, St. John’s wort):** May decrease oxycodone efficacy.
- **Coumarin Derivatives:** Clinically relevant changes in INR have been observed.
- **MAOIs:** Interaction can cause anxiety, confusion, and fatal respiratory depression.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category C**. Not recommended for use during pregnancy or childbirth. Long-term maternal use can lead to neonatal opioid withdrawal syndrome (NOWS).
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Oxycodone is excreted in breast milk (milk:plasma ratio 3.4:1). It is unknown if naloxone is excreted in significant amounts [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show no direct impairment of fertility, but prolonged human opioid use may result in sexual dysfunction and irregular menses.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 18 years of age due to lack of safety/efficacy data.
- **Elderly Use:** Plasma concentrations of oxycodone are approximately 18% higher. Initial doses should be 1/3 to 1/2 of the standard adult dose.
- **Renal Impairment:** Plasma concentrations of both components increase. Use with caution in mild-to-severe impairment; starting dose reduction (1/3 to 1/2) is required. Alternative products without naloxone may be preferred in severe impairment to avoid withdrawal risk [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in moderate to severe impairment. In mild impairment, initiate at 1/3 to 1/2 of the standard dose with intensive monitoring.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Oxycodone is a full opioid agonist at mu, kappa, and delta receptors. Naloxone is a competitive opioid antagonist.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Oral naloxone has extremely low bioavailability (<3%) and acts locally in the gastrointestinal tract to antagonise the constipating effects of oxycodone without compromising systemic analgesia.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability of oxycodone is ~50% (up to 87% in cancer patients).
    - **Distribution:** Oxycodone is ~45% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Oxycodone is metabolised via CYP3A4 (noroxycodone) and CYP2D6 (oxymorphone). Naloxone undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Oxycodone half-life is ~4.8 hours. Both drugs are excreted primarily in the urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Analgesia and OIC (Studies 3001, 3006):** 12-week trials in patients with non-malignant pain demonstrated that the oxycodone/naloxone combination provided analgesic efficacy non-inferior to oxycodone alone, while significantly improving bowel function as measured by the Bowel Function Index (BFI).
- **Restless Legs Syndrome (Study OXN3502):** A 12-week randomized study showed a significant decrease in mean IRLS (International Restless Legs Syndrome) scores (8.15 point improvement over placebo) in patients who had failed dopaminergic therapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Shelf life is specified on the external packaging.
