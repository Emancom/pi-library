# Editorial Product Information: Omadacycline

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information has been harmonised based on the official Product Information (PI) from the **United States (FDA)**. 
- This document provides a clinical synthesis for use by healthcare professionals and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 
- At the time of this synthesis, only the United States PI was available for review; therefore, all regulatory and clinical positions reflect that jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Omadacycline (as omadacycline tosylate).
- **Chemical Class:** Aminomethylcycline antibacterial (a semisynthetic derivative of the tetracycline class).
- **Brand Name:** **NUZYRA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01AA12.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Injection:** 100 mg of omadacycline (equivalent to 131 mg omadacycline tosylate) as a sterile lyophilised powder in a single-dose vial [US].
    - **Tablets:** 150 mg of omadacycline (equivalent to 196 mg omadacycline tosylate) film-coated tablets [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion or Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Paratek Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Community-Acquired Bacterial Pneumonia (CABP):** Treatment of adults with CABP caused by susceptible isolates of *S. pneumoniae*, *S. aureus* (MSSA), *H. influenzae*, *H. parainfluenzae*, *K. pneumoniae*, *L. pneumophila*, *M. pneumoniae*, and *C. pneumoniae* [US].
- **Acute Bacterial Skin and Skin Structure Infections (ABSSSI):** Treatment of adults with ABSSSI caused by susceptible isolates of *S. aureus* (MSSA and MRSA), *S. lugdunensis*, *S. pyogenes*, *S. anginosus* group, *E. faecalis*, *E. cloacae*, and *K. pneumoniae* [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
The treatment duration for both indications is 7 to 14 days [US].

| Infection | Loading Regimen | Maintenance Regimen |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CABP** | **IV:** 200 mg once OR 100 mg twice on Day 1 **OR** <br> **Oral:** 300 mg twice on Day 1 | **IV:** 100 mg once daily **OR** <br> **Oral:** 300 mg once daily |
| **ABSSSI** | **IV:** 200 mg once OR 100 mg twice on Day 1 **OR** <br> **Oral:** 450 mg once daily on Day 1 and Day 2 | **IV:** 100 mg once daily **OR** <br> **Oral:** 300 mg once daily |

### Administration Instructions
- **Oral Fasting Requirement:** Patients must fast for at least 4 hours prior to oral dosing. After administration with water, no food or drink (except water) should be consumed for 2 hours.
- **Interaction Management:** Dairy products, antacids, or multivitamins containing multivalent cations must be avoided for 4 hours after oral dosing.
- **IV Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with Sterile Water, 0.9% NaCl, or 5% Dextrose. The final concentration should be 1 mg/mL (for 100 mg dose) or 2 mg/mL (for 200 mg dose). 
- **Infusion Rate:** Administer 100 mg over 30 minutes or 200 mg over 60 minutes. Do not co-infuse with solutions containing multivalent cations (e.g., calcium, magnesium) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to omadacycline, other tetracycline-class antibacterials, or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Tooth Discoloration and Enamel Hypoplasia:** Use during tooth development (second/third trimester of pregnancy, infancy, and childhood up to age 8) may cause permanent yellow-gray-brown discoloration and enamel defects [US].
- **Inhibition of Bone Growth:** Reversible inhibition of fibula growth has been observed in premature infants exposed to tetracyclines.
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all systemic antibacterials; severity can range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis [US].
- **Tetracycline Class Effects:** Potential for photosensitivity, pseudotumor cerebri (idiopathic intracranial hypertension), and anti-anabolic actions (leading to increased BUN, azotemia, and acidosis) [US].
- **Mortality Imbalance in CABP:** In clinical trials, a higher mortality rate was observed in omadacycline-treated CABP patients (2%) compared to moxifloxacin (1%), though the cause remains unclear [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions vary by route and indication. 

**Common Adverse Effects (≥2%):**
- **ABSSSI:** Nausea (21.9%—higher with oral loading), vomiting (11.4%), infusion site reactions (5.2%), increased ALT/AST, headache, and diarrhea [US].
- **CABP:** Increased ALT (2.8%), headache (2.8%), hypertension (2.2%), and increased AST (2.1%) [US].

**Infrequent/Serious Effects (<2%):**
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, atrial fibrillation.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, constipation.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Urticaria, rash, and potential anaphylaxis (class effect).
- **Other:** Reversible dose-dependent increase in heart rate was noted in Phase 1 studies [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants:** Tetracyclines may depress plasma prothrombin activity; patients on anticoagulant therapy (e.g., warfarin) may require downward dose adjustments [US].
- **Multivalent Cations:** Absorption of oral omadacycline is significantly impaired by antacids (Al, Ca, Mg), bismuth subsalicylate, and iron preparations [US].
- **P-gp Inhibitors:** Co-administration with oral verapamil (a P-gp inhibitor) increased omadacycline AUC by approximately 25% [US].
- **Cytochrome P450:** Omadacycline is not a substrate, inhibitor, or inducer of major CYP450 enzymes [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not Recommended]. Based on animal data and tetracycline class effects, use during the 2nd and 3rd trimesters may cause fetal harm (bone/tooth issues). Available human data are insufficient to evaluate risk of major birth defects or miscarriage [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Tetracyclines are excreted in human milk. Due to potential risks of tooth discoloration and bone growth inhibition, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 4 days after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, omadacycline reduced sperm motility/counts in males and increased embryo loss/reduced ovulation in females at clinically relevant exposures [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in patients <18 years. Use in children <8 years is specifically avoided due to tooth/bone risks [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger adults, though greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required, including in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) receiving hemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for patients with mild, moderate, or severe hepatic insufficiency (Child-Pugh Classes A, B, or C) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Binds to the 30S ribosomal subunit of susceptible bacteria, inhibiting protein synthesis. It is generally bacteriostatic but shows bactericidal activity against some isolates of *S. pneumoniae* and *H. influenzae* [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** 34.5% (fasted).
    - **Plasma Protein Binding:** ~20%.
    - **Distribution:** High volume of distribution (190 L); significant lung penetration (concentrations in alveolar cells are ~26-fold higher than plasma).
    - **Metabolism:** Not metabolized.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (77.5%–84%) and secondarily urinary (14.4%–27%).
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 16 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CABP (Trials 1 & 4):** Omadacycline demonstrated non-inferiority to moxifloxacin. In Trial 1, early clinical response (ECR) was 81.1% for omadacycline vs 82.7% for moxifloxacin [US].
- **ABSSSI (Trials 2 & 3):** Omadacycline demonstrated non-inferiority to linezolid. In Trial 2 (IV-to-oral), ECR was 84.8% vs 85.5% for linezolid. In Trial 3 (oral only), ECR was 87.3% vs 88.2% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets and Vials:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Diluted IV Solution:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (≤25°C) or 7 days if refrigerated (2°C to 8°C). Do not freeze [US].
