# Editorial Product Information: Osilodrostat

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs) to support clinical decision-making. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI. Clinicians must consult the current approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Osilodrostat (as phosphate).
- **Chemical Class:** Cortisol synthesis inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** Isturisa [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H02CA02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Film-coated tablets: 1 mg, 5 mg, and 10 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Recordati Rare Diseases Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Endogenous Cushing’s syndrome:** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial Dose:** 2 mg taken twice daily.
- **Ancestry-Based Adjustment:** For patients of Asian ancestry, a reduced starting dose of 1 mg twice daily is recommended [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** Typically ranges between 2 mg and 7 mg twice daily.
- **Maximum Dose:** 30 mg twice daily [Australian].

### Titration and Monitoring
- Titrate in increments of 1 mg or 2 mg based on individual clinical response and tolerability.
- **Monitoring:** Assess cortisol levels (e.g., 24-hour urinary free cortisol, serum/plasma cortisol) every 1–2 weeks until clinical response is stable, then at regular intervals.
- **Dose Increases:** Should occur no more frequently than once every 1–2 weeks.

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food.
- **Missed Dose:** Patients should skip the missed dose and take the next dose at the scheduled time; do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to osilodrostat or any of the product excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypocortisolism:** Treatment may lead to life-threatening adrenal insufficiency or cortisol withdrawal syndrome. Patients should be monitored for signs such as nausea, vomiting, fatigue, hypotension, and electrolyte imbalances. If suspected, reduce the dose or interrupt treatment.
- **QTc Prolongation:** Osilodrostat is associated with dose-dependent QT interval prolongation. 
  - **Requirement:** Perform an ECG prior to initiation and within one week of starting treatment.
  - **Threshold:** If QTc exceeds 480 ms, cardiology consultation and dose reduction/interruption are recommended.
  - **Corrective Action:** Correct hypokalaemia, hypocalcaemia, or hypomagnesaemia before starting therapy.
- **Corticotroph Tumour Growth:** Treatment discontinuation should be considered if MRI evidence shows increasing tumour invasiveness.
- **Adrenal Precursors and Androgens:** Inhibition of CYP11B1 leads to accumulation of precursors (e.g., 11-deoxycortisol) and androgens. This may cause hypokalaemia, hypertension, oedema, and in females, hirsutism and acne.
- **Enzyme Interactions:** Use caution when introducing or stopping strong enzyme inhibitors/inducers, as they may significantly alter osilodrostat exposure.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on pivotal Phase III studies (N=210) in Cushing’s disease.

| Frequency | Adverse Drug Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Adrenal insufficiency, fatigue, oedema, vomiting, nausea, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, decreased appetite, dizziness, headache, hypotension, arthralgia, myalgia, tachycardia, rash, hirsutism (females), acne (females), increased blood testosterone (females), increased blood corticotrophin. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Syncope, malaise, QTc prolongation, increased transaminases. |
| **Not Known** | Neutropenia (identified via post-market data). |

- **Serious Effects:** Adrenal insufficiency and QTc prolongation require immediate clinical attention and potential dose modification.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Co-administration with drugs known to affect the QT interval (e.g., pasireotide, ketoconazole) may have additive effects. A washout period is recommended when switching between these agents.

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Effect of Osilodrostat on Other Drugs:** Osilodrostat is a moderate inhibitor of CYP1A2 and a mild-to-moderate inhibitor of CYP2C19. It is a mild inhibitor of CYP2D6 and CYP3A4/5. 
  - **Caution:** Use with sensitive substrates of these enzymes, especially those with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., theophylline, tizanidine).
- **Effect of Other Drugs on Osilodrostat:** The potential for single CYP or UGT enzyme inhibitors to significantly affect osilodrostat is considered low because multiple enzymes contribute to its clearance. However, caution is advised with strong multi-enzyme inhibitors/inducers.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category D [Australian]:** Osilodrostat may cause foetal harm. Animal studies have shown reproductive toxicity at high doses (embryofetal death and malformations). 
  - **Recommendation:** Do not use during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception. Verify pregnancy status before initiation.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if osilodrostat is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment and for at least one week after the final dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed impaired fertility and impaired embryo viability at high exposures (92-fold the MRHD). Human data are not available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** Limited data available; use with caution. No specific dose adjustment is currently required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. However, urinary free cortisol (UFC) levels should be interpreted with caution in moderate-to-severe impairment due to reduced excretion; consider alternative monitoring [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
  - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment.
  - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Starting dose 1 mg twice daily.
  - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Starting dose 1 mg once daily in the evening.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent inhibitor of 11β-hydroxylase (CYP11B1), the enzyme responsible for the final step of cortisol biosynthesis in the adrenal gland.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (Tmax ~1h). Steady state reached within 2 days. Food does not clinically significantly affect absorption.
  - **Distribution:** Median apparent volume of distribution is ~100 L. Protein binding is low (<40%).
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily cleared via metabolism. Multiple enzymes involved (CYP3A4, 2B6, 2D6, and UGTs); no single enzyme contributes >25% to total clearance.
  - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~4 hours. 91% of dose eliminated in urine (mostly as metabolites).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study C2301:** A 48-week multicenter study including a 26-week open-label period followed by an 8-week randomized withdrawal phase. 86.1% of patients continuing osilodrostat maintained complete response (mUFC ≤ULN) compared to 29.4% in the placebo group at week 34.
- **Study C2302:** A 12-week double-blind, placebo-controlled study. Complete response rate was 77.1% for osilodrostat vs. 8.0% for placebo. Improvements were also noted in cardiovascular and metabolic parameters (blood pressure, weight, HbA1c).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C. 
- **Packaging:** Keep in the original container (Alu/Alu blisters) to protect from moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
