# Editorial Product Information: Oxybutynin Hydrochloride (Topical)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of topical oxybutynin (specifically the 20% lotion formulation) for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Oxybutynin hydrochloride (Anticholinergic/Antimuscarinic).
- **Brand Name(s):** APOHIDE [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G04BD04 (Oxybutynin).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Topical lotion containing 200 mg/g (20%) of oxybutynin hydrochloride. Each actuation (pump) delivers 100 μL of lotion [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical (percutaneous).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Accelagen Pty Ltd (Sponsor); Hisamitsu Pharmaceutical Co., Inc. (Trademark owner) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Primary Palmar Hyperhidrosis:** Indicated for the treatment of excessive sweating of the palms [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Recommended Dose:** 5 pumps (approximately 500 μL, equivalent to ~96 mg of oxybutynin hydrochloride) applied once daily.
- **Frequency:** Once daily before bedtime.

### Administration Instructions
- Apply the lotion to the entire palms of both hands.
- **Pre-application:** Palms should be clean and dry.
- **Post-application:** 
    - Refrain from wetting hands after application until the following morning to prevent removal.
    - Wash hands thoroughly upon waking to avoid unintentional transfer to non-application sites (especially the eyes).
    - Do **not** cover treated palms with occlusive dressings or airtight gloves [Australian].
- **Accidental Eye Contact:** If contact occurs, rinse eyes immediately with water.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
Oxybutynin is contraindicated in patients with:
- **Angle-closure glaucoma:** Anticholinergic effects may increase intraocular pressure.
- **Lower urinary tract obstruction:** Including prostatic hypertrophy, as it may inhibit bladder contraction and worsen symptoms.
- **Severe cardiac disease:** Risk of increased cardiac workload due to tachycardia.
- **Gastrointestinal obstruction:** Including intestinal obstruction or paralytic ileus, as it inhibits GI motility.
- **Myasthenia gravis:** Anticholinergic effects may further decrease muscle tone.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to oxybutynin or any excipients (e.g., ethanol, sodium lactate, diisopropyl adipate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Heat Prostration:** Oxybutynin decreases sweating. In high environmental temperatures or during excessive exercise, this can lead to fever and heat stroke.
- **CNS Effects:** Anticholinergic CNS effects (hallucinations, agitation, confusion, sleep disturbances) have been reported. Monitor patients closely, particularly in the first few months of therapy [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Hypomotility:** Monitor for symptoms of decreased GI movement; discontinue if symptoms occur.
- **Skin Integrity:** Do not apply to areas with wounds, eczema, or dermatitis, as this may increase systemic absorption and toxicity.

### Monitoring & Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Urinary Retention:** Exercise caution in patients with non-obstructive lower urinary tract disease.
- **Cardiovascular:** Caution in patients with congestive cardiac failure, tachyarrhythmia, or hyperthyroidism (potential for worsened tachycardia).
- **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** Caution in patients with ulcerative colitis (risk of toxic megacolon) or hiatus hernia/GERD (risk of exacerbated oesophagitis).
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Caution in patients with Parkinson’s disease, dementia, or cerebrovascular disorders, as neuropsychiatric symptoms may worsen [Australian].
- **Oral Health:** Reduced salivary secretion may lead to dental caries, periodontitis, or oral candidiasis.
- **Excipients:** This product contains 49% v/v alcohol; it is flammable and must be kept away from fire/heat.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Safety data is derived from clinical trials involving patients with primary palmar hyperhidrosis [Australian].

### Common (≥ 1/100 to < 1/10)
- **Application Site:** Dermatitis (4.2%), pruritus (2.1%), eczema.
- **Systemic Anticholinergic:** Thirst (3.5%).
- **Infections:** Nasopharyngitis (3.5%).
- **Investigations:** Increased blood uric acid, increased ALT, asteatosis (skin dryness).

### Uncommon (≥ 1/1,000 to < 1/100)
- **Nervous System:** Headache.
- **Eye Disorders:** Dry eye, blepharitis, blepharospasm, photophobia, conjunctivitis.
- **Cardiac:** Left bundle branch block.
- **Respiratory:** Dry throat, oropharyngeal discomfort.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Angular cheilitis.
- **Skin:** Dyshidrotic eczema, skin fissures, xeroderma, skin exfoliation, compensatory sweating.
- **Investigations:** Glucose/blood in urine, increased AST, increased creatinine, abnormal ECG T-wave, increased eosinophils.

### Not Known (Frequency cannot be estimated)
- **Serious/Significant:** Paralytic ileus, urinary retention, decreased platelets.
- **Other:** Dizziness, cystitis, ventricular extrasystoles, constipation, stomatitis, dysuria, oedema, urticaria.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Oxybutynin is metabolised by CYP3A4. Concomitant use with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors can significantly increase systemic oxybutynin exposure [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Anticholinergic Agents:** Concurrent use with other anticholinergics (e.g., tricyclic antidepressants, phenothiazines, MAOIs) enhances the antimuscarinic effect.
- **Cholinesterase Inhibitors:** Oxybutynin may reduce the efficacy of cholinesterase inhibitors.
- **Sedatives/Alcohol:** May enhance oxybutynin-induced drowsiness.
- **Gastroprokinetic Agents:** Anticholinergic effects may antagonise the effects of prokinetics.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Australian Category B1:** Animal studies with other oxybutynin products showed no evidence of teratogenicity. However, an increased incidence of abortion was noted in rabbits at high doses (100 mg/kg/day).
- **Recommendation:** Safety in human pregnancy has not been established. Use only if potential benefits outweigh potential hazards [Australian].

### Breastfeeding
- **Data:** Animal studies show oxybutynin/metabolites are excreted in milk. Human data is unavailable.
- **Recommendation:** Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [Australian].

### Fertility
- No definite evidence of impaired fertility was observed in rat studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy demonstrated in children **12 years of age and older**. No data available for children under 12 [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited efficacy evidence for patients > 65 years. Use with caution and monitor closely [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Because oxybutynin is primarily metabolised in the liver, adverse effects are more likely in patients with severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Oxybutynin is a competitive muscarinic receptor antagonist. It antagonises muscarinic receptors (specifically M3 and M4) expressed in the eccrine sweat glands, thereby acting as an antiperspirant when applied topically [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Absorbed via passive diffusion across the stratum corneum. Following repeated daily application to the palms, steady state is reached within 72 hours for oxybutynin.
- **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4 (liver and gut wall). The active metabolite is N-desethyloxybutynin (DEO). 
- **Bypass Effect:** Topical administration bypasses significant first-pass gastrointestinal and hepatic metabolism, resulting in a lower ratio of DEO to parent drug compared to oral administration.
- **Elimination:** Extensively metabolised; < 0.1% of a dose is excreted unchanged in the urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Phase III (Japan):** A double-blind, placebo-controlled study in patients (12–77 years) with primary palmar hyperhidrosis. 52.8% of patients using the 20% lotion achieved at least a 50% decrease in sweat volume at week 4, compared to 24.3% in the placebo group (p < 0.001) [Australian].
- **Long-term Study:** In a 52-week study, 72.6% of participants maintained at least a 50% decrease from baseline sweat volume at week 52 [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Safety:** Keep out of reach of children.
- **Handling:** Contains alcohol; keep away from open flames.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
