# Editorial Product Information: Omeprazole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Omeprazole magnesium (a substituted benzimidazole).
- **Chemical Class:** Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI).
- **Brand Names:** **ACIMAX** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A02BC01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Enteric-coated tablets (Multiple Unit Pellet System):** 20 mg (containing 20.6 mg omeprazole magnesium) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmaco (Australia) Ltd under license from CHEPLAPHARM Arzneimittel GmbH [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Omeprazole** is indicated for the following conditions [Australian]:
- **Gastro-Oesophageal Reflux Disease (GORD):** Relief of symptomatic GORD (heartburn and associated symptoms) and the treatment and prevention of relapse in erosive oesophagitis.
- **Peptic Ulcer Disease:** Treatment of gastric and duodenal ulcers; combination therapy for *Helicobacter pylori* eradication; and long-term prevention of relapse in patients who are *H. pylori* negative or where eradication is inappropriate/ineffective.
- **NSAID-Associated Lesions:** Treatment and prevention of gastric and duodenal ulcers or erosions associated with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs).
- **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome:** Management of pathological hypersecretory conditions.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
Tablets should be swallowed whole with liquid and must not be crushed or chewed. For patients with swallowing difficulties, tablets may be dispersed in non-carbonated water or non-carbonated fruit juice (mineral water is unsuitable). The dispersion should be consumed immediately or within 30 minutes.

### Adult Dosing
- **Symptomatic GORD:** 10 mg to 20 mg once daily for up to 4 weeks. If symptom control is not achieved after 4 weeks at 20 mg, further investigation is warranted.
- **Erosive Oesophagitis:** 20 mg once daily for 4 to 8 weeks. For refractory cases, 40 mg once daily is recommended.
- **Maintenance (GORD):** 10 mg once daily, increasing to 20 mg if required.
- **Duodenal Ulcer:** 20 mg once daily for 4 to 8 weeks. Refractory cases: 40 mg once daily. 
- **Gastric Ulcer:** 20 mg once daily for 4 to 8 weeks. Refractory cases: 40 mg once daily.
- **NSAID-Associated Lesions:** 20 mg to 40 mg once daily for treatment; 20 mg once daily for prevention.
- ***H. pylori* Eradication:** **Omeprazole** 40 mg once daily or 20 mg twice daily in combination with dual antibiotics (e.g., amoxicillin/metronidazole, amoxicillin/clarithromycin, or clarithromycin/metronidazole) for 1–2 weeks.
- **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome:** Initial dose is 60 mg once daily. Maintenance range is 20 mg to 120 mg daily. Doses exceeding 80 mg daily should be divided and administered twice daily.

### Missed Dose
Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **omeprazole**, substituted benzimidazoles, or any excipients.
- Concomitant use with **cilostazol** (due to **omeprazole** being a CYP2C19 inhibitor) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Undiagnosed Malignancy:** Antisecretory treatment may mask symptoms of gastric malignancy. Malignancy should be excluded if "alarm symptoms" occur (e.g., significant weight loss, recurrent vomiting, dysphagia).
- **Clopidogrel Interaction:** Concomitant use should be avoided as **omeprazole** reduces the plasma concentration of clopidogrel's active metabolite [Australian].
- **Acute Interstitial Nephritis (AIN):** Reported with all PPIs; may occur at any time and progress to renal failure. Discontinue if suspected.
- **Cyanocobalamin (B12) Deficiency:** Long-term use (>3 years) may lead to malabsorption due to hypo- or achlorhydria.
- **Fracture Risk:** Increased risk of osteoporosis-related fractures (hip, wrist, spine) with long-term, high-dose therapy.
- **Infection Risk:** Increased risk of gastrointestinal infections (e.g., *Salmonella*, *Campylobacter*, and potentially *Clostridium difficile* in hospitalised patients).
- **Subacute Cutaneous Lupus Erythematosus (SCLE):** PPIs are associated with rare cases of SCLE.
- **Hypomagnesaemia:** Reported rarely, typically after >3 months (mostly >1 year) of therapy. May lead to tetany, arrhythmias, or seizures and may cause secondary hypocalcaemia or hypokalaemia.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue immediately at the first sign of a skin reaction.
- **Laboratory Interference:** Stop PPIs 5–14 days before Chromogranin A (CgA) measurements to avoid false results in neuroendocrine tumour investigations.

## 6. Adverse Effects
**Omeprazole** is generally well-tolerated.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Headache, abdominal pain, constipation, diarrhoea, flatulence, nausea/vomiting. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Insomnia, dizziness, paraesthesia, somnolence, vertigo, increased liver enzymes, dermatitis, pruritus, rash, urticaria, malaise. |
| **Rare** (0.01–0.1%) | Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, hypersensitivity (fever, angioedema, anaphylaxis), hyponatraemia, agitation, confusion, depression, taste disturbance, blurred vision, bronchospasm, dry mouth, stomatitis, GI candidiasis, hepatitis, alopecia, photosensitivity, arthralgia, myalgia, AIN, gynaecomastia, sweating, peripheral oedema. |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, hypomagnesaemia (may cause hypokalaemia/hypocalcaemia), aggression, hallucinations, dyspnoea, microscopic colitis, hepatic failure, encephalopathy, SCARs (SJS, TEN, DRESS), muscular weakness, impaired renal function/nephrosis, impotence. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP450)
**Omeprazole** inhibits CYP2C19.
- **Diazepam, Phenytoin, Warfarin:** Clearance may be decreased; monitoring and dose reduction may be required.
- **Cilostazol:** Increased levels; co-administration is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Clopidogrel:** Reduced clopidogrel active metabolite levels; avoid concomitant use.
- **Voriconazole:** More than doubles **omeprazole** exposure.
- **CYP Inducers (Rifampicin, St John’s Wort):** May decrease **omeprazole** levels.

### pH-Dependent Absorption
- **Increased Absorption:** Digoxin (bioavailability increases by 10-30%).
- **Decreased Absorption:** Ketoconazole, itraconazole, erlotinib, and mycophenolate mofetil. Use with caution in transplant patients.

### Other Interactions
- **Antiretrovirals:** Nelfinavir and atazanavir levels are significantly decreased (avoid use). Saquinavir levels may be increased.
- **Methotrexate:** Levels may increase; consider temporary **omeprazole** withdrawal during high-dose methotrexate therapy.
- **Tacrolimus:** Serum levels may be increased.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Epidemiological studies show no increase in overall malformation rates, though some data suggest a potential increase in cardiac defects. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Omeprazole is excreted in animal milk; human data is insufficient. Use in nursing mothers is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies at clinical doses.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children ≥1 year.
    - **10–20 kg:** 10 mg once daily.
    - **>20 kg:** 20 mg once daily.
    - Dose may be increased to 20 mg and 40 mg respectively if required [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No dosage adjustment is necessary.
- **Renal Impairment:** Bioavailability is not significantly altered; no dose adjustment required.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is reduced and half-life is prolonged (up to four-fold). However, 20 mg daily is generally tolerated without accumulation; no adjustment to the normal regime is required for standard dosing [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Irreversibly inhibits the $H^+/K^+$-ATPase enzyme system (the proton pump) at the secretory surface of the gastric parietal cell. This blocks the final step of acid production, inhibiting both basal and stimulated acid secretion.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Acid-labile; administered as enteric-coated pellets. Bioavailability is ~35% (single dose) to ~60% (repeated dose). Peak plasma levels occur within 4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 95% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver via CYP2C19 (forming hydroxyomeprazole) and CYP3A4 (forming omeprazole sulphone). 
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~40 minutes (IV) to <1 hour (oral). 80% excreted in urine, remainder in faeces.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Antisecretory effect begins within 1 hour, is maximal at 2 hours, and lasts up to 72 hours. Regular daily dosing leads to maximum acid reduction within 4 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Symptomatic GORD:** Randomised trials (n=1710) showed **omeprazole** 20 mg was superior to 10 mg and placebo, and comparable or superior to ranitidine 150 mg BD for heartburn relief over 4 weeks.
- **Erosive Oesophagitis Relapse:** Meta-analysis (n=1154) demonstrated 6-month remission rates of 72% (10 mg) and 82% (20 mg) compared to placebo.
- **Paediatric Data:** Trials in children (0.5–17 years) showed healing of erosive oesophagitis at doses of 0.5–2.1 mg/kg/day.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Store in the original container (blister packs).
