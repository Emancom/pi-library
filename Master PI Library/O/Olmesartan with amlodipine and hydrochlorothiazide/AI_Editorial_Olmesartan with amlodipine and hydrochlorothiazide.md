# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for **levocarnitine** injection. It is designed as a professional reference for healthcare providers to assist in clinical decision-making.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a synthesis of available regulatory data and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) source was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic name); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic treatment of patients with metabolic disorders resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion.
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is typically followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (minimum every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses should remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Dose adjustments may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- For metabolic disorders: Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- For ESRD: Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks vs. benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **TMA/TMAO Accumulation:** While this applies primarily to high-dose oral administration, chronic use in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in the urine [US].

- **Common clinical observations:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter in provided text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for inborn errors of metabolism is weight-based (50 mg/kg) and applicable to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects. Dose selection should be cautious, starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **ESRD:** Specifically indicated for use post-dialysis.
    - **Caution:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; however, the injection is used to manage dialytic losses [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it replenishes carnitine stores lost to dialysis and meat-restricted diets [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean plasma levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels. While carnitine concentrations increased, the specific effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in ESRD has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
