# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- At the time of writing, official PI documents for other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and must not be substituted for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), a naturally occurring carrier molecule.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic; Sponsor: Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for intravenous injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This should be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administration should occur as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose may be utilized, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and no less frequent than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are currently no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate therapy initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in both patients with and without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Serious Adverse Effects
| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect (Frequency ≥5% in ESRD Trials) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain (9%), Asthenia (9%), Fever (7%), Headache (22%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Arrhythmia (2%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Anorexia (5%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm (rare/post-marketing) [US].
- **Post-marketing Experience:** Body odor and gastritis have been noted. Seizures have been reported regardless of administration route [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustment in patients receiving oral anticoagulants [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
    - There are no adequate/well-controlled human studies; use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. In nursing mothers, the benefits to the mother should be weighed against the risks of excess carnitine intake by the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in provided sources; standard adult dosing applies unless renal function is a concern.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine clears acylCoA compounds, preventing the disruption of intermediary metabolism and life-threatening acidosis [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are TMA and TMAO (primarily relevant to oral administration) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg thrice weekly), mean trough (pre-dialysis) levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses thrice weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine levels in ESRD patients, the effects on clinical outcomes (e.g., signs and symptoms of deficiency) have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of an opened vial, as the formulation is preservative-free [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Schedules:** Not specified in available sources.
