# Editorial Product Information: Oseltamivir

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: This editorial synthesis is based on provided documentation from the **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory information into a single reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for information purposes only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current official PI approved in their region before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Oseltamivir phosphate (a pro-drug of oseltamivir carboxylate).
- **Chemical Class:** Neuraminidase inhibitor; antiviral.
- **Brand Names:** **Oseltamivir Lupin** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AH02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Hard gelatin capsules: 30 mg, 45 mg, and 75 mg [Australian].
    - Powder for oral suspension (available in other brands) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lupin Australia Pty Ltd; Distributed by Generic Health Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Treatment of Influenza:** Indicated for infections due to influenza A and B viruses in adults and children, including full-term neonates. Treatment must commence within 48 hours of symptom onset [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis of Influenza:** Indicated for adults and children aged 1 year and older. This includes post-exposure prophylaxis (following contact with an infected individual) and seasonal prophylaxis during community outbreaks [Australian]. 
- **Note:** Vaccination remains the preferred method for routine prophylaxis [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Treatment of Influenza (5-Day Course)
- **Adults and Adolescents (≥13 years):** 75 mg twice daily.
- **Children (≥1 to <13 years):** Weight-adjusted dosing:
    - ≤15 kg: 30 mg twice daily.
    - >15–23 kg: 45 mg twice daily.
    - >23–40 kg: 60 mg twice daily.
    - >40 kg: 75 mg twice daily.
- **Children (<1 year):** 3 mg/kg twice daily [Australian].

### Prophylaxis of Influenza
- **Adults and Adolescents (≥13 years):** 75 mg once daily for 10 days (post-exposure) or up to 6 weeks (community outbreak). Protection lasts as long as dosing continues [Australian].
- **Children (≥1 to <13 years):** Weight-adjusted dosing once daily for 10 days:
    - ≤15 kg: 30 mg OD.
    - >15–23 kg: 45 mg OD.
    - >23–40 kg: 60 mg OD.
    - >40 kg: 75 mg OD.
- **Immunocompromised Patients:** 75 mg once daily for 12 weeks for seasonal prophylaxis [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food; however, administration with food may improve tolerability.
- If capsules cannot be swallowed, the powder for oral suspension should be utilized (where available) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to oseltamivir phosphate or any components of the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Neuropsychiatric Events:** Convulsions and delirium-like events (including self-injury) have been reported, primarily in children and adolescents. Patients should be monitored for abnormal behaviour [Australian].
- **Severe Conditions:** No evidence of efficacy in patients with viral or bacterial pneumonia or other complications of acute influenza. Efficacy has not been established in patients with chronic cardiac or respiratory disease [Australian].
- **Pharmaceutical Precaution:** Direct contact with the powder (within capsules) should be avoided, as oseltamivir is a potential skin sensitiser and eye irritant [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Elevated liver enzymes have been reported in patients with influenza-like illness receiving oseltamivir [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequently reported adverse drug reactions (ADRs) are nausea, vomiting, and headache. These typically occur on the first or second day of treatment and resolve spontaneously.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (10%), Headache (up to 17% in prophylaxis) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vomiting (8% in adults; 16% in children), Pain (4% in prophylaxis) |
| **Post-Marketing/Rare** | Anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), Hepatitis, GI bleeding, Haemorrhagic colitis, Thrombocytopenia, Visual disturbances |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Oseltamivir is converted by esterases (not CYP450) to its active metabolite. Interactions involving CYP450 or glucuronyl transferase are unlikely [Australian].
- **Renal Interactions:** Competition for renal tubular secretion (anionic pathway) is possible. 
- **Probenecid:** Co-administration results in an approximate 2-fold increase in exposure to the active metabolite; however, no dose adjustment is required in patients with normal renal function [Australian].
- **Clinically Insignificant:** No interactions observed with paracetamol, aspirin, cimetidine, antacids, warfarin, or oral contraceptives [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Human data from >1,000 outcomes indicate no direct or indirect harmful effects on the foetus. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Oseltamivir and its active metabolite are excreted in breast milk at very low levels. Use only if the benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on male or female fertility was observed in rat studies [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Recommended for treatment in full-term neonates and children; prophylaxis recommended for children ≥1 year. Data are not available for infants <2 weeks of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required unless there is co-existent renal impairment [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Adjustments required based on Creatinine Clearance (CrCl):
    - **CrCl >60 mL/min:** No adjustment.
    - **CrCl >30–60 mL/min:** Treatment: 30 mg BD; Prophylaxis: 30 mg OD.
    - **CrCl 10–30 mL/min:** Treatment: 30 mg OD; Prophylaxis: 30 mg every other day.
    - **Haemodialysis:** Treatment: 30 mg after every session; Prophylaxis: 30 mg after every alternate session.
    - **Peritoneal Dialysis:** Treatment: 30 mg every 5 days; Prophylaxis: 30 mg every 7 days.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. Not studied in severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Oseltamivir phosphate is a pro-drug. The active metabolite (**oseltamivir carboxylate**) is a selective inhibitor of influenza A and B neuraminidase enzymes, preventing the release of viral particles from infected cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; >75% reaches systemic circulation as active metabolite.
    - **Distribution:** Mean volume of distribution (Vss) is ~23 L; plasma protein binding is negligible (~3%).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively converted by hepatic esterases.
    - **Elimination:** >99% renal excretion (glomerular filtration and tubular secretion). Half-life is 6–10 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Treatment:** Two pivotal Phase III trials (WV15670/WV15671) showed oseltamivir 75 mg BD reduced the median time to symptom alleviation by up to 30 hours compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Paediatric Treatment:** In children (1–12 years), oseltamivir reduced the time to freedom from illness by 1.5 days compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis:** Community outbreak studies showed a reduction in the incidence of laboratory-confirmed influenza from 4.8% (placebo) to 1.2% (oseltamivir) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Consult the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be disposed of at a local pharmacy; avoid environmental release [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
