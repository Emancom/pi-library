# Editorial Product Information: Olopatadine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a single, cohesive reference. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Olopatadine hydrochloride (a dibenzoxepine derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective histamine H₁-antagonist and mast cell stabiliser.
- **Brand Names:** **Patanol** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** S01GX09.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Ophthalmic solution (eye drops) containing olopatadine hydrochloride 1.11 mg/mL (equivalent to 1.0 mg/mL olopatadine base) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ocular.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Seasonal Allergic Conjunctivitis:** Indicated for the treatment of signs and symptoms associated with seasonal allergic conjunctivitis. Use may be maintained for up to 14 weeks if clinically necessary [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** One to two drops instilled into the conjunctival sac of the affected eye(s) twice daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - For topical ocular use only. 
    - To prevent contamination, avoid touching the dropper tip to the eyelids, surrounding areas, or any other surfaces. 
    - The bottle must be kept tightly closed when not in use.
- **Duration of Therapy:** Clinical data supports use for up to 14 weeks [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specifically addressed in available sources; however, standard clinical practice suggests applying the dose when remembered, unless it is nearly time for the next scheduled dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to olopatadine hydrochloride or any of the excipients (including benzalkonium chloride, sodium chloride, and dibasic sodium phosphate dodecahydrate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Strictly for topical ocular use; not for injection or oral ingestion.
- **Contact Lens Care:** The preparation contains **benzalkonium chloride** as a preservative, which may be absorbed by and discolour soft (hydrophilic) contact lenses. Lenses should be removed prior to instillation and may be reinserted 15 minutes after application [Australian].
- **Contamination Prevention:** Patients should be instructed to avoid contact between the dropper tip and any surface to maintain sterility.
- **Visual Disturbances:** Temporary blurring of vision or other visual disturbances may occur immediately following instillation. Patients should wait until vision is clear before driving or operating machinery [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild and localized to the eye.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Headache (reported at 7%) [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | **Ocular:** Punctate keratitis, keratitis, eye pain, dry eye, eyelid oedema, pruritus, eye discharge, ocular hyperaemia, eyelid margin crusting, ocular discomfort. <br> **Systemic:** Dysgeusia, fatigue, nasal dryness [Australian]. |
| **Rare** (0.01–0.1%) | **Ocular:** Photophobia, blurred vision, erythema of the eyelid. <br> **Systemic:** Dizziness, dry mouth, contact dermatitis [Australian]. |
| **Not Known** (Post-marketing) | Increased lacrimation, nausea, hypersensitivity reactions [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Olopatadine is not an inhibitor of cytochrome P-450 isozymes (1A2, 2C8/9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, or 3A1). 
- **Systemic Potential:** The potential for drug-drug interactions is considered very low, as systemic levels following ocular administration are negligible [Australian].
- **Management:** No specific drug interactions were investigated in vivo [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1.** 
    - Animal studies using high systemic doses showed reproductive toxicity but no teratogenic effects in rats or rabbits. 
    - No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use only if the potential benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Olopatadine has been detected in the milk of lactating rats following oral administration. It is unknown if topical ocular administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable levels in human breast milk. Antihistamines may also affect milk production. Use with caution in breastfeeding women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available. In animal studies, extremely high oral doses (7,800 times the human ocular level) showed no effect on fertility, though higher doses (400 mg/kg/day) reduced fertility indices [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children under 3 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data or dose adjustments provided in available sources [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, 60–70% of the drug is excreted unchanged in urine [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Olopatadine is a potent, selective histamine H₁-antagonist. It stabilises human conjunctival mast cells, inhibiting the release of pro-inflammatory mediators (e.g., histamine) and preventing Type I immediate hypersensitivity reactions [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Olopatadine does not significantly affect alpha-adrenergic, dopamine, or muscarinic (Type 1 and 2) receptors.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Systemic exposure is low. Plasma concentrations are typically <0.5 ng/mL.
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is approximately 8–12 hours. 
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal (60–70% as parent drug). Two minor metabolites (mono-desmethyl and N-oxide) are excreted in urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Safety Data:** Evaluated in 15 clinical studies (n=1,208). No clinically significant changes were observed in visual acuity, intraocular pressure, or systemic parameters (e.g., blood chemistry, pulse, or blood pressure).
- **Efficacy:** 
    - **Conjunctival Antigen Challenge (CAC):** In three pivotal studies, olopatadine was significantly more effective than placebo in preventing ocular itching, redness, and chemosis for up to 8 hours post-dose.
    - **Environmental Studies:** A study during ragweed season showed a significant reduction in itching, redness, and tearing compared to placebo.
    - **Comfort:** Studies showed olopatadine 0.1% was significantly more comfortable upon instillation than ketorolac 0.5% and levocabastine 0.05% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard the container 28 days after opening [Australian].
- **Handling:** Ensure the bottle is tightly closed after each use.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** 
    - **Australian:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4).
