# Editorial Product Information: OnabotulinumtoxinA

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate editorial overview for healthcare professionals.
- **Editorial Note:** As only the United States PI was provided for this synthesis, regional variations from other jurisdictions (e.g., Australia, Europe, Canada) are not reflected in this version. This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** OnabotulinumtoxinA; a sterile, vacuum-dried purified botulinum toxin type A produced from the Hall strain of *Clostridium botulinum*.
- **Chemical Class:** Acetylcholine release inhibitor; neuromuscular blocking agent.
- **Brand Name(s):** **BOTOX**, **BOTOX Cosmetic** [US].
- **ATC Code:** M03AX01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Vacuum-dried powder for solution for injection in single-dose vials containing 100 Units or 200 Units [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular, intradetrusor, or intradermal injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AbbVie Inc. (Manufacturer); Sportpharm LLC (Distributor) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Bladder Dysfunction:** 
    - Overactive bladder (OAB) in adults with symptoms of urge urinary incontinence, urgency, and frequency who have had an inadequate response to or are intolerant of anticholinergic medication [US-approved].
    - Urinary incontinence due to detrusor overactivity associated with a neurologic condition (e.g., SCI, MS) in adults with inadequate response/intolerance to anticholinergics [US-approved].
    - Neurogenic detrusor overactivity (NDO) in paediatric patients 5 years of age and older with inadequate response/intolerance to anticholinergics [US-approved].
- **Chronic Migraine:** Prophylaxis of headaches in adult patients with $\ge$ 15 headache days per month (lasting $\ge$ 4 hours/day) [US-approved].
- **Spasticity:** Treatment of spasticity in patients 2 years of age and older [US-approved].
- **Cervical Dystonia:** To reduce the severity of abnormal head position and neck pain in adults [US-approved].
- **Primary Axillary Hyperhidrosis:** Severe cases in adults inadequately managed by topical agents [US-approved].
- **Blepharospasm and Strabismus:** In patients 12 years of age and older [US-approved].
- **Aesthetic Use:** Temporary improvement in the appearance of moderate to severe glabellar lines, crow’s feet, forehead lines, and platysma bands in adults [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing Principles:** 
    - Potency Units are **not interchangeable** with other botulinum toxin products [US]. 
    - Use the lowest recommended dose when initiating treatment. 
    - In a 3-month interval, do not exceed a cumulative dose of 400 Units for adults, or the lesser of 10 Units/kg or 340 Units for paediatric patients [US].
- **Selected Indication Dosing [US]:**
    - **OAB:** 100 Units (as 0.5 mL injections across 20 sites into the detrusor).
    - **Adult Neurologic Detrusor Overactivity:** 200 Units (as 1 mL injections across 30 sites).
    - **Chronic Migraine:** 155 Units divided across 7 head/neck muscles every 12 weeks.
    - **Axillary Hyperhidrosis:** 50 Units per axilla injected intradermally.
    - **Adult Upper Limb Spasticity:** Up to 400 Units divided among affected muscles.
- **Administration:** Must be reconstituted with sterile, preservative-free 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, USP. Administration of bladder injections requires a cystoscope.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to any botulinum toxin preparation or any component in the formulation [US].
- Infection at the proposed injection site [US].
- **Intradetrusor Injections:** Presence of a urinary tract infection (UTI) or acute urinary retention (in patients not routinely performing clean intermittent catheterization) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Distant Spread of Toxin Effect [US]:** Postmarketing reports indicate that the effects of all botulinum toxin products may spread from the area of injection, producing symptoms consistent with botulism (e.g., asthenia, muscle weakness, diplopia, ptosis, dysphagia, dysphonia, dysarthria, and life-threatening breathing difficulties). Symptoms can occur hours to weeks after injection. The risk is highest in children treated for spasticity but can occur in adults, especially those with underlying conditions predisposing them to these symptoms.
- **Interchangeability:** OnabotulinumtoxinA Units are specific to the assay method and cannot be compared to or converted into Units of other botulinum toxin products [US].
- **Pre-existing Neuromuscular Disorders:** Patients with peripheral motor neuropathic diseases, ALS, or neuromuscular junction disorders (e.g., myasthenia gravis) may be at increased risk of clinically significant effects, including severe dysphagia and respiratory compromise [US].
- **Pulmonary Effects:** Use with caution in patients with compromised respiratory function. Reduced lung function has been observed in patients treated for spasticity who have pre-existing respiratory disease [US].
- **Ocular Risks:** Reduced blinking following blepharospasm treatment may lead to corneal exposure and ulceration. Retrobulbar haemorrhage has occurred during strabismus treatment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General:** Localized pain, infection, inflammation, and bruising at the injection site [US].
- **Frequency Data (Indication-Specific) [US]:**
    - **OAB:** UTI (18%), dysuria (9%), urinary retention (6%).
    - **Adult Detrusor Overactivity (Neurologic):** UTI (24%), urinary retention (17%).
    - **Chronic Migraine:** Neck pain (9%), headache (5%), eyelid ptosis (4%), muscular weakness (4%).
    - **Cervical Dystonia:** Dysphagia (19%), upper respiratory infection (12%), neck pain (11%).
    - **Axillary Hyperhidrosis:** Injection site pain/haemorrhage, non-axillary sweating, pharyngitis.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Dysphagia, aspiration pneumonia, and systemic allergic reactions (anaphylaxis) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Aminoglycosides/Neuromuscular Blockers:** Co-administration may potentiate the effect of the toxin; use with caution [US].
- **Anticholinergic Drugs:** May increase systemic anticholinergic effects [US].
- **Muscle Relaxants:** May exaggerate muscle weakness when used before or after toxin administration [US].
- **Other Botulinum Toxins:** The effect of administering different products within several months of each other is unknown; excessive weakness may occur [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned under current labelling; based on animal data, may cause fetal harm]. Animal studies at clinically relevant doses resulted in reduced fetal weight and decreased skeletal ossification associated with maternal toxicity [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of the drug in human or animal milk. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need and any potential adverse effects on the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Reduced fertility was observed in male rats and female rats at high doses in pre-mating studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Approved for NDO ($\ge$ 5 years), spasticity ($\ge$ 2 years), and blepharospasm/strabismus ($\ge$ 12 years) [US].
    - Safety and effectiveness not established for OAB, Chronic Migraine, or Hyperhidrosis in paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Generally, no overall differences in safety were observed compared to younger adults, though patients over 65 treated for OAB showed a higher incidence of UTI and urinary retention compared to younger cohorts [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Blocks neuromuscular transmission by binding to acceptor sites on motor or autonomic nerve terminals. It enters the terminals and cleaves SNAP-25, a protein required for the successful docking and release of acetylcholine. This results in chemical denervation and localized reduction in muscle or sweat gland activity [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Using current technology, the toxin is not detectable in peripheral blood following intramuscular injection at recommended doses [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reinnervation of the muscle may occur through axonal sprouting, slowly reversing the denervation effect [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Chronic Migraine:** Two randomized trials demonstrated a significant decrease in headache day frequency (approx. 8–9 days from baseline) compared to placebo at 24 weeks [US].
- **OAB:** Randomized trials showed a significant reduction from baseline in daily urinary incontinence episodes at week 12 (approx. 2.5–3 episodes) compared to placebo [US].
- **Adult Detrusor Overactivity:** Two studies showed significant improvements in weekly incontinence episodes (decrease of approx. 18–20 episodes) and increased maximum cystometric capacity [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store in a refrigerator between 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) for up to 36 months [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Should be administered within 24 hours. During this period, store in a refrigerator (2°C to 8°C) [US].
- **Handling:** Solution should be clear, colourless, and free of particulate matter. Vials are for single-dose use only [US].
