# Editorial Product Information: olmesartan + amlodipine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **olmesartan + amlodipine**
- Chemical class:
  - Olmesartan medoxomil: angiotensin II receptor (AT1) antagonist (angiotensin receptor blocker)
  - Amlodipine (as besilate): dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of source PI(s): Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of hypertension.
- Treatment should not be initiated with this fixed-dose combination; the combination is intended for patients already established on the component agents or when titration from monotherapy is appropriate.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Available fixed-dose strengths included in the source: olmesartan medoxomil 20 mg or 40 mg combined with amlodipine (as besilate) 5 mg or 10 mg (20/5, 20/10, 40/5, 40/10). 
- Recommended dose: one tablet once daily, with or without food.

### Dosing by clinical situation / titration
- Replacement therapy: patients taking olmesartan and amlodipine separately at the same component doses may be switched to an equivalent fixed-dose tablet.
- Add‑on / titration strategy for inadequate control:
  - If blood pressure not controlled on either olmesartan or amlodipine monotherapy, consider switching to combination therapy.
  - Titrate in steps as needed:
    - AK olmesartan + amlodipine 20/5 → 40/5
    - 40/5 → 40/10 if still uncontrolled
  - If blood pressure remains uncontrolled on 40/10, addition of a thiazide diuretic may be considered with appropriate monitoring and adherence to the thiazide product prescribing information.

### Administration instructions
- One tablet once daily, with or without food.
- Do not initiate therapy with this fixed-dose combination (start treatment with monotherapy as clinically appropriate, then switch or titrate to combination).

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
(All contraindications listed in the source are included below.)
- Hypersensitivity to any component of the tablet or to dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers.
- Pregnancy. **Do not use during pregnancy.**
- Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 20 mL/min).
- Severe hepatic impairment or biliary obstruction.
- Patients with diabetes who are receiving aliskiren.
- Conditions related to the amlodipine component:
  - Cardiogenic shock.
  - Acute myocardial infarction (within the first 4 weeks).
  - Unstable angina pectoris.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources (no regionally labelled boxed/black‑box warning text provided in the source).

### General warnings and precautions
- Intravascular volume depletion: symptomatic hypotension can occur in volume- or sodium-depleted patients (e.g., vigorous diuretic therapy, dietary salt restriction, diarrhoea, vomiting); correct depletion or provide close supervision when initiating therapy.
- Conditions dependent on renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAS): patients whose vascular tone and renal function depend on RAS activity (e.g., severe congestive heart failure, renal disease including renal artery stenosis) are at increased risk of hypotension, azotemia, oliguria, acute renal failure and rarely death when treated with RAS blockers.
- Renovascular hypertension: increased risk of severe hypotension and renal insufficiency in bilateral renal artery stenosis or stenosis of a single functioning kidney.
- Sprue‑like enteropathy: severe, chronic diarrhoea with substantial weight loss reported months to years after initiation of olmesartan; intestinal villous atrophy reported on biopsy in many cases. Exclude other causes and consider discontinuation if no alternative etiology is identified.
- Hyperkalaemia: may occur with olmesartan (as with other ARBs/ACE inhibitors), especially in renal impairment and heart failure; concomitant use with potassium-sparing diuretics, potassium supplements, salt substitutes containing potassium or other potassium‑increasing agents (e.g., trimethoprim) may increase risk.
- Aortic/mitral valve stenosis and obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy: use vasodilators with caution.
- Primary aldosteronism: antihypertensives acting via RAS inhibition are generally ineffective; use not recommended.
- Angioedema: angioedema, including laryngeal/glottic oedema and airway obstruction, has been reported with olmesartan. Discontinue immediately and do not re‑administer if angioedema occurs. Some affected patients had prior angioedema with ACE inhibitors.
- Increased angina and/or myocardial infarction: rare reports of increased frequency, duration and/or severity of angina or acute myocardial infarction on initiation or dose increase of calcium channel blockers, particularly in patients with severe obstructive coronary artery disease.
- Congestive heart failure:
  - Changes in renal function may occur in susceptible individuals due to RAS inhibition; in severe heart failure, treatment has been associated with oliguria and/or progressive azotemia and (rarely) acute renal failure and/or death.
  - Amlodipine: in a placebo‑controlled trial of patients with NYHA Class III or IV heart failure (n≈1153) on stable background therapy, doses 5–10 mg/day produced no overall adverse effect on survival or cardiac morbidity. Shorter trials in NYHA II/III (total n≈697) showed no evidence of worsened heart failure.
- Beta‑blocker withdrawal: amlodipine is not a beta‑blocker and offers no protection from abrupt beta‑blocker withdrawal; taper beta‑blockers gradually.
- Peripheral oedema: mild to moderate peripheral oedema is the most common adverse event related to amlodipine; incidence is dose‑dependent.
- Ethnic differences: blood pressure lowering with olmesartan may be somewhat less in black patients than non‑black patients, possibly due to higher prevalence of low‑renin hypertension.
- Concomitant use of ACE inhibitors or ARBs, NSAIDs (including COX‑2 inhibitors), and thiazide diuretics: combination of these three classes increases the risk of renal impairment; use with caution and monitor renal function. Elderly and patients with pre‑existing renal impairment are at higher risk.
- Lithium: reversible increases in serum lithium concentration and toxicity reported with ACE inhibitors and ARBs; concurrent use not recommended unless serum lithium levels are closely monitored.
- Hepatic impairment: because amlodipine is extensively metabolized by the liver, exposure is increased in hepatic impairment. Use caution in mild–moderate hepatic impairment; not recommended in severe hepatic impairment or biliary obstruction. Consider lower starting dose and monitor liver function tests.
- Renal impairment: periodic monitoring of serum potassium and creatinine recommended. Not recommended in severe renal impairment (CrCl < 20 mL/min). No experience in recent kidney transplant recipients or end‑stage renal impairment (CrCl < 12 mL/min).
- Use in elderly: no specific dose adjustment generally required, but monitor renal function and haemodynamic status as clinically indicated.
- Paediatric use: not recommended in children and adolescents < 18 years due to lack of safety and efficacy data.
- General: exercise caution in patients with prior hypersensitivity to other ARBs.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic monitoring of:
  - Blood pressure.
  - Serum electrolytes (particularly potassium).
  - Renal function (serum creatinine, urea).
  - Signs/symptoms of angioedema, severe diarrhoea and weight loss (sprue‑like enteropathy).
  - Liver function tests in patients with hepatic impairment or when clinically indicated.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The source lists multiple adverse reactions associated with olmesartan and amlodipine. Where frequency data are reported in the source, they are shown; if not reported, frequency is noted as "not specified in available sources" or "post‑marketing reports" as appropriate.
- Serious adverse effects are highlighted.

### Key adverse effects (narrative)
- Peripheral oedema: most common adverse event with amlodipine; incidence dose-dependent (3.0–10.8% in the 5–10 mg dose range).
- Angioedema: reported with olmesartan; can involve larynx/glottis and airway obstruction; some patients had prior ACE inhibitor‑related angioedema.
- Sprue‑like enteropathy: severe chronic diarrhoea with substantial weight loss and villous atrophy reported months to years after starting olmesartan.
- Renal events: symptomatic hypotension, azotemia, oliguria, acute renal failure have been reported in susceptible patients treated with RAS blockers.
- Hyperkalaemia: reported, especially in patients with renal impairment and/or heart failure; also reported post‑marketing.
- Worsening liver function tests: may occur with amlodipine in hepatic impairment (no frequencies stated).
- Increased blood creatinine: reported post‑marketing.
- Increased angina and/or myocardial infarction: rare reports on initiation or dose increase of calcium channel blockers.
- Other adverse effects: the source indicates that amlodipine therapy has not been associated with clinically significant changes in routine laboratory tests and that no clinically relevant changes were observed in many measured lab parameters in trials; however, specific additional adverse event frequencies are not provided in the available text.

## 8. Drug Interactions
(Interactions described below derive from component data in the source. No dedicated interaction studies with the fixed‑dose combination were conducted according to the source; reported interactions are for olmesartan and amlodipine individual components.)

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant antihypertensive drugs: additive blood pressure lowering effect.
- Dual RAS blockade (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, aliskiren): increased risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia and renal function changes including acute renal failure. Monitor blood pressure, renal function and electrolytes if combined. Do not co‑administer aliskiren with olmesartan + amlodipine in patients with diabetes. Avoid aliskiren with olmesartan + amlodipine in patients with renal impairment (GFR < 60 mL/min).
- NSAIDs (including high‑dose aspirin >3 g/day and COX‑2 inhibitors) plus ARB: may reduce renal function and decrease antihypertensive effect; monitor renal function and ensure adequate hydration.
- Potassium supplements, potassium‑sparing diuretics, potassium‑containing salt substitutes, heparin, trimethoprim: may increase serum potassium when used with olmesartan; concomitant use not recommended or requires close monitoring.
- Lithium: combination with olmesartan not recommended; if necessary, monitor lithium concentrations closely.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions and notable coadministrations
- Colesevelam hydrochloride: coadministration with olmesartan medoxomil (40 mg) and colesevelam (3750 mg) reduced olmesartan Cmax by 28% and AUC by 39%; administering olmesartan 4 hours before colesevelam reduced the effect (Cmax ↓4%, AUC ↓15%); consider dosing separation (olmesartan 4 hours before colesevelam).
- Antacids (aluminium/magnesium hydroxide): modest reduction in olmesartan bioavailability after antacid; for amlodipine, no significant effect.
- Warfarin, digoxin, hydrochlorothiazide, pravastatin: olmesartan had no clinically relevant interactions; coadministration did not affect pharmacokinetics or pharmacodynamics significantly in specific studies.
- Cytochrome P450 (CYP) interactions:
  - Olmesartan: in vitro studies indicate no clinically relevant inhibition of multiple CYP enzymes; no in vivo interactions expected.
  - Amlodipine: metabolized by CYP3A4; plasma concentrations increased with CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, diltiazem); strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir) may increase amlodipine exposure — use caution.
  - CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampicin, Hypericum perforatum/St John’s Wort) may reduce amlodipine concentrations — use caution.
- Grapefruit juice: may increase bioavailability of calcium channel blockers including amlodipine; coadministration not recommended.
- Simvastatin: coadministration with multiple doses of amlodipine 10 mg and simvastatin 80 mg increased simvastatin exposure by 77%; limit simvastatin dose to 20 mg daily when coadministered with amlodipine.
- Atorvastatin: coadministration with multiple 10 mg amlodipine and 80 mg atorvastatin produced no significant change in atorvastatin steady‑state pharmacokinetics.
- Sildenafil: single‑dose sildenafil (100 mg) had no effect on amlodipine pharmacokinetics; combined use produced independent BP‑lowering effects.
- Ciclosporin: in renal transplant patients, amlodipine coadministration was associated with an average 40% increase in trough ciclosporin levels — monitor ciclosporin concentrations and adjust dosing as needed.
- Aluminium/magnesium antacid and amlodipine: no significant pharmacokinetic interaction.

### Management notes
- Monitor BP, renal function and electrolytes when combining with other RAS‑affecting agents, NSAIDs, or diuretics.
- Avoid concurrent potassium supplements/potassium‑sparing diuretics unless necessary and with monitoring.
- Separate dosing of olmesartan and colesevelam by approximately 4 hours when coadministration is required.
- Use caution and consider dose adjustments (or alternative agents) when combining amlodipine with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or with simvastatin at higher doses.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Not recommended for children and adolescents < 18 years of age due to lack of safety and efficacy data.

### Elderly
- No general dose adjustment is required solely on the basis of age; use clinical judgement and monitor renal function and haemodynamic status.

### Renal impairment
- Mild to moderate renal impairment: no adjustment of the recommended dose is required.
- Severe renal impairment: use not recommended; contraindicated in patients with creatinine clearance < 20 mL/min. No experience in end‑stage renal impairment (CrCl < 12 mL/min) or recent kidney transplant recipients.

### Hepatic impairment
- Mild to moderate hepatic impairment: use with caution; exposure to amlodipine and olmesartan may be increased; a lower starting dose may be necessary and monitoring of liver function is advised.
- Severe hepatic impairment and biliary obstruction: use is not recommended / contraindicated.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Olmesartan medoxomil: prodrug converted to active olmesartan; selectively blocks angiotensin II AT1 receptors, inhibiting effects of angiotensin II (vasoconstriction, aldosterone secretion), thereby lowering blood pressure.
- Amlodipine (besilate): dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker that inhibits transmembrane influx of calcium ions into vascular smooth muscle and cardiac muscle, producing vasodilation and reduced peripheral resistance.

### Pharmacokinetics (selected points reported)
- Olmesartan:
  - Antacid coadministration produced modest reduction in bioavailability.
  - Colesevelam significantly reduces olmesartan absorption if given concurrently; separation of dosing mitigates this reduction.
  - No clinically relevant inhibition of several CYP450 isoenzymes in vitro; significant CYP‑mediated interactions not expected.
- Amlodipine:
  - Extensively metabolized by the liver (CYP3A4 involvement).
  - Exposure (half‑life) increased in hepatic impairment; caution advised.
  - Plasma concentrations increased by some CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, diltiazem); strong inhibitors may increase exposure further.
  - Grapefruit juice may increase bioavailability and is not recommended.
  - Co‑administration with simvastatin significantly increased simvastatin exposure; limit simvastatin dosing when used concomitantly.
  - Ciclosporin trough levels increased with amlodipine concomitant use (observed average 40% increase).

### Pharmacodynamics
- Combined effect: additive blood pressure lowering via RAS blockade (olmesartan) and peripheral arterial vasodilation (amlodipine). Monitor for hypotension, particularly in volume‑depleted patients or those on multiple antihypertensives.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Amlodipine in heart failure:
  - A randomized, placebo‑controlled trial in patients with NYHA Class III or IV heart failure (n ≈ 1153) receiving stable background therapy (ACE inhibitor, digoxin, diuretics) evaluated amlodipine 5–10 mg/day. Mean follow‑up about 14 months. No overall adverse effect on survival or on cardiac morbidity was observed.
  - Four short‑term (8–12 week) trials in patients with NYHA Class II/III heart failure (total n ≈ 697) found no evidence of worsened heart failure based on exercise tolerance, NYHA class, symptoms or left ventricular ejection fraction.
- Other pivotal hypertension efficacy trials for the fixed‑dose combination are not described in the available source text. The source indicates that no specific drug interaction studies were conducted with the fixed‑dose combination itself; component-level studies form the basis of interaction guidance.
