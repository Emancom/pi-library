# Editorial Product Information: omeprazole + sodium

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **omeprazole + sodium**
- Chemical class: Proton pump inhibitor (PPI) combination with an alkaline buffer (sodium bicarbonate)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral (suspension); administration also described via nasogastric (NG) or orogastric (OG) tube

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2004 [US]
- Last revision/update of provided product information: December 2023 [US]
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Registration/approval dates for other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Short‑term treatment of active duodenal ulcer in adults (most patients heal within 4 weeks; some patients may require an additional 4 weeks).
- Short‑term treatment of active benign gastric ulcer in adults (4–8 weeks).
- Treatment of symptomatic gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD; heartburn and related symptoms) in adults for up to 4 weeks.
- Treatment of erosive esophagitis (EE) due to acid‑mediated GERD in adults (4–8 weeks). If no response by 8 weeks, an additional 4 weeks may be given; recurrence may be treated with additional 4–8 week courses.
- Maintenance of healing of EE due to acid‑mediated GERD in adults (controlled studies up to 12 months).
- Reduction of risk of upper gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding in critically ill adult patients (specified regimen) [US].

(Where regional approvals differ: Not specified in available sources beyond the U.S. indication set above.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adults)
- Recommended omeprazole‑based dosing (by indication):
  - Active duodenal ulcer: 20 mg once daily for 4 weeks; some patients may require an additional 4 weeks.
  - Active benign gastric ulcer: 40 mg once daily for 4–8 weeks.
  - Symptomatic GERD: 20 mg once daily for up to 4 weeks.
  - EE due to acid‑mediated GERD: 20 mg once daily for 4–8 weeks; if no response at 8 weeks, may give additional 4 weeks.
  - Maintenance of healing of EE due to acid‑mediated GERD: 20 mg once daily (controlled studies do not extend beyond 12 months).
  - Reduction of risk of upper GI bleeding in critically ill adult patients: 40 mg initial dose, followed by 40 mg 6–8 hours later, then 40 mg once daily thereafter for 14 days.

- Available omeprazole strengths described in source: 20 mg and 40 mg omeprazole per unit‑dose packet; each packet also contains sodium bicarbonate (see Warnings/Administration).

- Important sodium content note: each packet contains 1,680 mg (20 mEq) sodium bicarbonate and a total sodium content of 460 mg. Because sodium bicarbonate content is the same per packet, do not substitute two 20 mg packets for one 40 mg packet (sodium load differs) — consider sodium burden when prescribing.

### Administration instructions
- Intended to be mixed with water and administered orally or via NG/OG tube.
- Oral (by mouth):
  - Empty packet into a small cup containing 5–10 mL water. Do not mix with liquids or foods other than water. Stir and drink immediately. Refill cup with water and drink immediately.
  - Administer on an empty stomach at least 1 hour before a meal.
- NG/OG tube:
  - Add 20 mL water to a catheter‑tipped syringe, then add packet contents. Use appropriately sized catheter‑tipped syringe. Shake to dissolve and administer immediately into the stomach. Refill syringe with an equal amount of water, shake and flush any remaining contents through the tube into the stomach.
  - If administered via NG/OG tube while on enteral feeding, suspend enteral feeding approximately 3 hours before and 1 hour after administration.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to substituted benzimidazoles or to any component of the formulation (hypersensitivity reactions reported include anaphylaxis, anaphylactic shock, angioedema, bronchospasm, acute tubulointerstitial nephritis, and urticaria).
- Concomitant use with rilpivirine‑containing products is contraindicated (interaction may reduce rilpivirine efficacy or alter exposure).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Presence of gastric malignancy: Symptomatic response to therapy does not exclude gastric malignancy. Consider additional follow‑up and diagnostic testing (including endoscopy in older patients) in patients with suboptimal response or early symptomatic relapse.
- Acute tubulointerstitial nephritis (TIN): TIN has been reported with PPIs and may occur at any time during therapy. Discontinue drug and evaluate patients with suspected TIN.
- Sodium bicarbonate buffer content:
  - Each packet contains a substantial bicarbonate and sodium load (1,680 mg sodium bicarbonate; total sodium 460 mg per packet). Take sodium intake into consideration for patients on sodium‑restricted diets or at risk for fluid overload/heart failure or edema.
  - Chronic administration of bicarbonate with calcium or milk can cause milk‑alkali syndrome.
  - Chronic sodium bicarbonate may cause systemic alkalosis.
  - Avoid in patients with Bartter's syndrome, hypokalemia, hypocalcemia, or acid‑base balance problems.
- Clostridium difficile‑associated diarrhea: PPI therapy may be associated with increased risk, especially in hospitalized patients. Consider C. difficile in cases of persistent diarrhea.
- Bone fracture: Observational studies suggest an association between long‑term or high‑dose PPI therapy and increased risk of osteoporosis‑related fractures (hip, wrist, spine). Use lowest effective dose and shortest duration appropriate to the condition.
- Severe cutaneous adverse reactions: Reports include Stevens–Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS), and acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP). Discontinue at first signs of severe cutaneous reactions.
- Cutaneous and systemic lupus erythematosus: New onset and exacerbations of cutaneous lupus erythematosus (CLE) and systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) have been reported. Most cases are cutaneous (subacute CLE). Discontinue if suspected and refer for specialist evaluation.
- Interaction with clopidogrel: Avoid concomitant use. Omeprazole can inhibit CYP2C19 and reduce conversion of clopidogrel to its active metabolite; consider alternative antiplatelet therapy.
- Cyanocobalamin (vitamin B‑12) deficiency: Long‑term daily use (e.g., >3 years) of acid‑suppressing medication may lead to malabsorption of vitamin B‑12; consider this diagnosis if clinically suspected.
- Hypomagnesemia and mineral metabolism:
  - Hypomagnesemia (symptomatic and asymptomatic) has been reported, typically in patients treated for ≥3 months, most commonly after a year of therapy.
  - Serious events include tetany, arrhythmias and seizures. Hypomagnesemia may cause or exacerbate hypocalcemia and hypokalemia.
  - For patients expected to be on prolonged treatment or taking medications which may cause hypomagnesemia (e.g., diuretics) or digoxin, consider baseline and periodic monitoring of magnesium and calcium; supplement as necessary. Discontinue PPI if hypomagnesemia is refractory to replacement.
- Interaction with St. John’s wort or rifampin: These agents induce CYP2C19 and/or CYP3A4 and can substantially decrease omeprazole concentrations; avoid concomitant use.
- Interference with investigations for neuroendocrine tumors: Omeprazole increases chromogranin A (CgA) levels via reduced gastric acidity; stop omeprazole for at least 14 days before assessing CgA and consider repeating testing if elevated.
- Interaction with methotrexate: Concomitant PPI use may elevate and prolong methotrexate levels (primarily at high methotrexate doses); consider temporary withdrawal of the PPI during high‑dose methotrexate administration.
- Fundic gland polyps: PPI use is associated with increased risk of fundic gland polyps, particularly with long‑term use beyond one year. Use the shortest duration appropriate.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor renal function if TIN is suspected.
- Consider monitoring magnesium and calcium prior to initiation and periodically during therapy in patients with risk factors for hypomagnesemia or hypocalcemia.
- Monitor for signs of severe cutaneous reactions, lupus‑like syndromes, vitamin B‑12 deficiency, and possible C. difficile infection as clinically indicated.
- Consider monitoring sodium/electrolytes and fluid status in patients on sodium‑restricted diets or with heart failure/edema risk due to sodium content.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid use for maintenance of healing of erosive esophagitis in patients with hepatic impairment and in Asian patients [US] (avoidance for that specific use is noted).
- Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest necessary duration, particularly in at‑risk populations (osteoporosis risk, elderly patients, long‑term therapy).

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions (clinically significant)
- Clopidogrel: Concomitant use should be avoided. Omeprazole inhibits CYP2C19 and can reduce conversion of clopidogrel to its active metabolite, decreasing antiplatelet effect; this interaction occurs even when administered 12 hours apart. Consider alternative antiplatelet therapy [US].
- St. John’s wort or rifampin: Inducers of CYP2C19 and/or CYP3A4 can substantially decrease omeprazole concentrations; avoid concomitant use.
- Methotrexate: Concomitant PPI use (primarily reported with high‑dose methotrexate) may elevate and prolong serum methotrexate and/or metabolite concentrations, potentially leading to toxicity; consider temporary PPI withdrawal when administering high‑dose methotrexate.
- Rilpivirine‑containing products: Co‑administration is contraindicated.
- Digoxin and drugs that can cause hypomagnesemia (e.g., certain diuretics): While not necessarily direct PK interactions, patients on digoxin or drugs that predispose to hypomagnesemia may be at higher risk of complications if hypomagnesemia develops; consider monitoring magnesium and clinical status.

### Interactions affecting diagnostic testing
- Chromogranin A (CgA): Omeprazole elevates CgA levels via decreased gastric acidity and may cause false positives in investigations for neuroendocrine tumors; stop omeprazole at least 14 days before CgA testing and consider repeating if levels are elevated.

### Management notes
- For high‑dose methotrexate administration, consider temporary withdrawal of PPI.
- Avoid concomitant use with clopidogrel; select alternative antiplatelet therapy as clinically appropriate.
- Avoid concurrent use with strong CYP2C19/CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampin, St. John’s wort) which may reduce omeprazole exposure.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Clinical trial experience cited in source:
  - In the U.S. clinical trial population of 465 adult patients, adverse reactions reported in ≥1% included headache, diarrhea, abdominal pain, nausea, upper respiratory infection, dizziness, vomiting, rash, constipation, cough, asthenia, and back pain (see Adverse Effects).
  - For active duodenal ulcer, most patients healed within 4 weeks; some required an additional 4 weeks.
  - Efficacy for maintenance of healing of EE is based on controlled studies up to 12 months.
  - Reduction of risk of upper GI bleeding in critically ill patients: regimen studied consisted of 40 mg initially, 40 mg 6–8 hours later, then 40 mg once daily for 14 days.
