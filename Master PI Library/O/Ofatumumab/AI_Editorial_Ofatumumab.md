# Editorial Product Information: Ofatumumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ofatumumab for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ofatumumab, a recombinant fully human monoclonal immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) antibody against the human CD20 molecule. It is produced in a murine cell line (NS0) using recombinant DNA technology.
- **Brand Name:** **Kesimpta** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AG12.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 20 mg/0.4 mL solution for injection in a single-use pre-filled syringe or pre-filled pen [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsing Forms of Multiple Sclerosis (RMS):** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with RMS to delay the progression of physical disability and reduce the frequency of relapse [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial Dosing:** 20 mg by subcutaneous injection at weeks 0, 1, and 2.
- **Maintenance Dosing:** 20 mg by subcutaneous injection monthly, commencing at week 4.

### Administration Instructions
- **Self-Administration:** Intended for patient self-administration after proper instruction.
- **Injection Sites:** Abdomen, thigh, or the upper outer arm.
- **Initial Guidance:** The first injection should be performed under the guidance of a healthcare professional.
- **Preparation:** The solution should be clear to slightly opalescent and colourless to slightly brownish-yellow.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a dose is missed, it should be administered as soon as possible. Subsequent doses should not be delayed to wait for the next scheduled dose; rather, the recommended monthly intervals should be resumed from that point.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ofatumumab or any of the product excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infections:** Ofatumumab can increase the risk of serious, life-threatening, or fatal bacterial, fungal, and viral infections. Delay treatment in patients with severe active infections. Screen for **Hepatitis B Virus (HBV)** prior to initiation; patients with active HBV should not be treated.
- **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML):** While no cases were reported in RMS trials, PML has occurred with other anti-CD20 antibodies. Monitor for new or worsening neurological symptoms.
- **Injection-Related Reactions:** Systemic injection-related reactions (SIRRs) are most common with the first injection (e.g., fever, headache, myalgia). Local injection site reactions (erythema, pain, swelling) are also common.
- **Liver Injury:** Clinically significant liver injury has been reported. Obtain liver function tests (LFTs) at baseline and monitor during treatment. Discontinue if liver injury is suspected and no alternative etiology is found.
- **Malignancies:** An increased risk of breast cancer has been noted with other anti-CD20 therapies; patients with active malignancies should not be treated.
- **Immunosuppression:** Do not initiate in severely immunocompromised patients.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data (from RMS Clinical Trials)
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection-related reactions (systemic), Nasopharyngitis, Injection site reactions (local), Urinary tract infection |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Back pain, Decreased Blood Immunoglobulin M (IgM), Anxiety, Pyrexia, Constipation, Muscular weakness, Influenza-like illness |
| **Unknown (Post-marketing)** | Anaphylaxis, Liver injury |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis:** Rare cases of anaphylaxis have been reported post-marketing.
- **Severe Infections:** Including potential reactivation of latent viruses (e.g., HBV).
- **Liver Injury:** Markedly elevated serum hepatic enzymes and total bilirubin.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450:** Ofatumumab is not metabolized by CYP450 enzymes; pharmacokinetic interactions with chemical drugs are not expected.
- **Vaccinations:** Live or live-attenuated vaccines should be administered at least 4 weeks prior to starting ofatumumab. Inactivated vaccines should be given at least 2 weeks prior. Vaccination during treatment is not recommended due to potential impaired response from B-cell depletion.
- **Immunosuppressants:** Co-administration with other immunosuppressive or immune-modulating therapies (except corticosteroids for relapse) may increase the risk of additive immunosuppressive effects.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C [Australian]:** Monoclonal antibodies are known to cross the placental barrier. There is a risk of transient peripheral B-cell depletion and lymphocytopenia in neonates. 
    - **Contraception:** Females of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Data are limited. Ofatumumab is excreted in breast milk at low levels. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in monkeys showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility parameters.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Ofatumumab was studied in patients aged 18 to 55. Population PK data suggests no dose adjustment is required for patients over 55/65, though clinical experience is limited.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. Ofatumumab is not excreted via urine.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. Hepatic metabolism of monoclonal antibodies is negligible.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ofatumumab binds to a distinct epitope on both the small and large extracellular loops of the CD20 molecule on B-cells. This binding triggers B-cell lysis primarily through complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) and antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment leads to a rapid and sustained reduction in B-cells. Median time to B-cell recovery after discontinuation is approximately 23 to 25 weeks.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Predominantly via the lymphatic system; $T_{max}$ is reached within the first week.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution at steady state is approximately 5.42 L.
    - **Elimination:** Follows non-linear target-mediated and target-independent routes. Half-life at steady state is approximately 16 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** ASCLEPIOS I and II (G2301 and G2302) were randomized, double-blind, active-controlled Phase 3 studies.
- **Comparison:** Compared ofatumumab 20 mg SC monthly to teriflunomide 14 mg orally daily.
- **Outcomes:** Ofatumumab demonstrated a significant reduction in the Annualized Relapse Rate (ARR) by 50.5% and 58.4% in the two studies, respectively. It also significantly reduced the risk of 3-month and 6-month confirmed disability worsening (CDW) and the number of Gd-enhancing T1 lesions on MRI.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store between 2°C and 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Light Protection:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Room Temperature Excursion:** If necessary, the product may be stored unrefrigerated (up to 30°C) for a single period of up to 7 days. If not used, it can be returned to the refrigerator for a maximum of 7 days before it must be discarded.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
