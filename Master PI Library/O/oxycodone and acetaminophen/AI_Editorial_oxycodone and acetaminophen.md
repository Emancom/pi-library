# Editorial Product Information: Oxycodone and Acetaminophen

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of the clinical properties and regulatory requirements for the combination product of oxycodone and acetaminophen. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a consolidated reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredients:** Oxycodone hydrochloride (a semisynthetic mu-opioid agonist) and Acetaminophen (a non-opiate, non-salicylate analgesic and antipyretic).
*   **Brand Names:** **Prolate** [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths [US]:**
    *   5 mg oxycodone HCl / 300 mg acetaminophen tablets.
    *   7.5 mg oxycodone HCl / 300 mg acetaminophen tablets.
    *   10 mg oxycodone HCl / 300 mg acetaminophen tablets.
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Forte Bio-Pharma LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Severe Pain Management:** Indicated for the management of pain severe enough to require an opioid analgesic and for which alternative treatments (e.g., non-opioid analgesics) are inadequate or not tolerated [US].
*   **Limitations of Use:** Due to risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, this product should be reserved for patients where alternative options are insufficient [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The dosage must be individualised based on pain severity, patient response, and prior analgesic experience. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration [US].
*   **5 mg/300 mg:** 1 tablet every 6 hours as needed. Maximum 12 tablets/day.
*   **7.5 mg/300 mg:** 1 tablet every 6 hours as needed. Maximum 8 tablets/day.
*   **10 mg/300 mg:** 1 tablet every 6 hours as needed. Maximum 6 tablets/day.

### Administration Instructions
*   Do not exceed a total daily dose of 4 grams of acetaminophen [US].
*   When converting from other opioids, avoid overestimating the dose to prevent fatal respiratory depression [US].

### Titration and Maintenance
*   Titrate upward until adequate analgesia is achieved, while monitoring for adverse effects.
*   Continually re-evaluate the need for continued opioid therapy [US].

### Discontinuation and Tapering
*   Do **not** abruptly discontinue in physically dependent patients.
*   Taper the dose gradually (e.g., 10% to 25% reduction of total daily dose every 2 to 4 weeks) to avoid withdrawal symptoms and psychological distress [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   Significant respiratory depression [US].
*   Acute or severe bronchial asthma in an unmonitored setting or without resuscitative equipment [US].
*   Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
*   Hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis) to oxycodone, acetaminophen, or any excipient [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
*   **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Risk of opioid use disorder, which can lead to overdose and death. Assess risk before prescribing and monitor regularly.
*   **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal cases may occur; highest risk is during initiation and dose increases.
*   **Accidental Ingestion:** Even one dose, especially if taken by a child, can be fatal.
*   **Concomitant CNS Depressants:** Use with benzodiazepines or alcohol may cause profound sedation, coma, and death.
*   **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Prolonged use during pregnancy can result in life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate.
*   **Cytochrome P450 3A4 Interaction:** Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors can increase oxycodone levels and lead to fatal respiratory depression.
*   **Hepatotoxicity:** Acetaminophen is linked to acute liver failure, often at doses exceeding 4,000 mg/day.

### General Warnings
*   **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH) and Allodynia:** Paradoxical increase in pain despite stable or increased dosing.
*   **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, typically after >1 month of therapy.
*   **Severe Hypotension:** Risk is increased in patients with compromised blood pressure or those taking CNS depressants.
*   **Serious Skin Reactions:** Acetaminophen may rarely cause Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) or Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN).
*   **Gastrointestinal Effects:** May cause spasm of the sphincter of Oddi and increase serum amylase.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are reported from post-approval use; frequencies are not always precisely defined in the available sources [US].

| System | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common/Frequent** | Lightheadedness, dizziness, drowsiness/sedation, nausea, vomiting. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Constipation, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, dry mouth, ileus. |
| **Central Nervous System** | Euphoria, dysphoria, seizures, headache, agitation, confusion. |
| **Dermatological** | Pruritus, rash, urticaria, flushing. |
| **Serious/Life-Threatening** | Respiratory depression, apnea, circulatory depression, shock, cardiac arrest, hepatotoxicity (dose-dependent). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, ketoconazole) increase oxycodone levels; inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine) decrease them [US].
*   **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with benzodiazepines, alcohol, gabapentinoids, and muscle relaxants [US].
*   **Serotonergic Drugs:** Use with SSRIs, SNRIs, or TCAs may result in **Serotonin Syndrome** [US].
*   **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Not recommended for use within 14 days of MAOI therapy [US].
*   **Mixed Agonist/Antagonists (e.g., Buprenorphine):** May reduce analgesic effect and precipitate withdrawal [US].
*   **Anticholinergics:** Increased risk of urinary retention and severe constipation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy Category [US]:** Category C. Prolonged use can lead to Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS). Use during labour may cause respiratory depression in the neonate.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Oxycodone and acetaminophen are excreted in breast milk. Infants should be monitored for excess sedation and respiratory depression. Doses <60 mg/day of oxycodone are unlikely to cause clinical effects in the infant, but withdrawal can occur when maternal dosing stops [US].
*   **Fertility:** Long-term opioid use may cause reduced fertility in both sexes via effects on the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
*   **Geriatric Use:** Start at the low end of the dosing range. Elderly patients are more sensitive to respiratory depression and likely to have renal/hepatic impairment [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance of both oxycodone and acetaminophen is reduced. Initiate with lower doses and monitor closely for toxicity [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Oxycodone elimination is prolonged. Initiate with lower doses and titrate carefully [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Oxycodone is a full mu-opioid receptor agonist. Acetaminophen’s analgesic mechanism is thought to involve central actions, though the exact mechanism is not fully established [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability of oxycodone is ~87%. Acetaminophen is rapidly and almost completely absorbed.
    *   **Distribution:** Oxycodone is 45% protein-bound. Acetaminophen is 10–25% protein-bound at therapeutic levels.
    *   **Metabolism:** Oxycodone is metabolised via CYP3A4 (to noroxycodone) and CYP2D6 (to oxymorphone). Acetaminophen is primarily metabolised in the liver via glucuronidation and sulfation, with a minor CYP2E1 pathway forming NAPQI.
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily renal [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Source documents cite observational cohort studies (2017–2021) estimating the risks of addiction and misuse. Over 12 months, 1% to 6% of participants met criteria for addiction, and 9% to 22% met criteria for abuse/misuse when initiating long-term Schedule II opioids [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   Protect from moisture and light.
*   Dispense in a tight, light-resistant, child-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Schedule II (CII) controlled substance. Requires a Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) [US].
*   **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
