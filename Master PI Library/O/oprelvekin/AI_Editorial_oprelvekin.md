# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations below 40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments should be guided by trough concentrations; downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may occur as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with known seizure activity, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations (target: 35–60 µmol/L) before and during therapy, alongside blood chemistries and vital signs [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Commonly Observed:** Transient nausea and vomiting. Less frequent effects include body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Clinical Trial Data (ESRD Patients):** In placebo-controlled trials, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, dyspepsia, nausea, vomiting |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, depression, paresthesia, insomnia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Other** | Pruritus, eye disorder, urinary tract infection |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Not formally assigned a letter category in the provided source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased levocarnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg) and applicable to the pediatric population [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral formulations in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients showed that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While carnitine concentrations were successfully increased, the specific effects on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL. Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].
