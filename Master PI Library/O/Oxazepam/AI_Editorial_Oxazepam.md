# Editorial Product Information: Oxazepam

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- At the time of compilation, only the Australian PI was available for review. Information from other major regulatory authorities (e.g., US FDA, European EMA) was not provided for this specific harmonisation task.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Oxazepam (a benzodiazepine derivative).
- **Brand Names:** **Alepam** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05BA04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Tablets:** 15 mg and 30 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris (Alphapharm Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anxiety Disorders:** Management of anxiety disorders or short-term relief of anxiety symptoms. This includes anxiety associated with depression [Australian].
- **Note on Stress:** Anxiety or tension associated with the routine stress of daily life generally does not require anxiolytic treatment [Australian].
- **Alcohol Withdrawal:** Relief of acute tremulousness, confusional states, or anxiety associated with alcohol withdrawal [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Dosage must be individualised based on clinical response and severity of symptoms.
- **Mild to Moderate Anxiety:** 7.5 mg to 15 mg, 3 or 4 times daily [Australian].
- **Severe Anxiety/Anxiety with Depression:** 15 mg to 30 mg, 3 or 4 times daily [Australian].
- **Alcohol Withdrawal:** 15 mg to 30 mg, 3 or 4 times daily [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Assessment:** Usefulness of therapy should be reassessed periodically. Long-term use is not recommended; treatment is generally limited to 2 to 4 weeks [Australian].
- **Alcoholics:** Do not administer to patients in a state of acute inebriation [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to benzodiazepines [Australian].
- Severe respiratory insufficiency (including COPD with incipient respiratory failure) [Australian].
- Severe pulmonary insufficiency [Australian].
- Sleep apnoea [Australian].
- Myasthenia gravis [Australian].
- Spinal or cerebellar ataxia [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Concomitant Opioid Use:** Concurrent use of **oxazepam** and opioids increases the risk of profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death. Reserve concomitant prescribing for cases where alternative treatments are inadequate. Limit dosage and duration to the minimum required [Australian].
- **Withdrawal and Tapering:** Abrupt discontinuation may cause withdrawal symptoms (e.g., tremors, convulsions, psychosis, rebound insomnia). After extended therapy, a gradual tapering schedule is required, ranging from four weeks to four months depending on the patient [Australian].
- **Duration of Treatment:** Long-term use is not recommended due to the risk of dependence. Efficacy for sleep (hypnotic effects) may decrease after several weeks [Australian].
- **Amnesia:** Anterograde amnesia may occur. Patients should ensure 7–8 hours of uninterrupted sleep after ingestion [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Reactions:** Paradoxical reactions (agitation, rage, hallucinations) may occur, particularly in children and the elderly. If these occur, discontinue the drug [Australian].
- **Suicide Risk:** Benzodiazepines may unmask suicidal tendencies in depressed patients and should not be used as primary therapy for depression without adequate antidepressant coverage [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic blood counts and liver function tests are recommended during long-term therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often dose-dependent and typically diminish with continued use or dose reduction.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Sedation, somnolence, fatigue [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Ataxia, confusion, dizziness, muscular weakness, depression, asthenia [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Libido disorder, erectile dysfunction, abnormal orgasm [Australian]. |
| **Less Common** | Hypotension, oedema, skin rashes (morbilliform, urticarial), nausea, hepatic dysfunction, abdominal pain, leucopenia, tremor, paraesthesia, headache, vertigo, syncope, hallucinations [Australian]. |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis, SIADH, hyponatraemia, respiratory depression, apnea, coma, extrapyramidal disorder, convulsion, amnesia, dysarthria, jaundice, alopecia [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects occur with alcohol, barbiturates, antipsychotics, sedatives, tricyclic antidepressants, MAO inhibitors, and anaesthetics [Australian].
- **Opioids:** Increased risk of fatal respiratory depression and sedation [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Unlike many benzodiazepines, **oxazepam** is not significantly involved with the Cytochrome P450 system; therefore, typical P450-mediated drug interactions are not observed [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** Effects of atropine and similar agents may be potentiated [Australian].
- **Sodium Oxybate:** May cause increased respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Theophylline/Aminophylline:** May reduce the sedative effects of **oxazepam** [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Benzodiazepines cross the placenta and may increase the risk of congenital malformations if used in the first trimester. Use in late pregnancy may lead to "floppy infant syndrome" (hypotonia, hypothermia, respiratory depression) and neonatal withdrawal symptoms [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Oxazepam** is excreted in breast milk. It may cause drowsiness and feeding difficulties in the infant. Use is generally not recommended unless benefits outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed decreased vaginal oestrus in mice; human data are not available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for children under 16 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly/Debilitated:** Increased sensitivity to CNS effects. Initial dose should be limited to 7.5 mg, 2 to 3 times daily. Monitor closely for ataxia and confusion to prevent falls and fractures [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution and consider dose reduction. May worsen hepatic encephalopathy in severe cases [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics may change; use with caution and consider dose reduction [Australian].
- **Thyroid Disease:** Hyperthyroidism may increase clearance; severe hypothyroidism may decrease glucuronidation [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potentiates the inhibitory effects of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) by binding to specific benzodiazepine receptors in the CNS [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed; peak plasma levels in 2–3 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 95% to 98% protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Conjugated directly to an inactive glucuronide. There are **no active metabolites** [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life ranges from 4 to 15 hours. At least 95% is excreted in urine as the glucuronide conjugate [Australian].
