# Editorial Product Information: Ospemifene

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States (FDA)**: OSPHENA (ospemifene) tablets, for oral use.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ospemifene for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, current Product Information in any specific jurisdiction where the drug is prescribed.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Ospemifene (Z-2-[4-(4-chloro-1,2-diphenylbut-1-enyl)phenoxy]ethanol).
- **Chemical Class**: Estrogen receptor agonist/antagonist (also known as a Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulator, or SERM).
- **Brand Name**: **Osphena** [US].
- **ATC Code**: G03XC05.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: 60 mg film-coated, biconvex, oval tablets, white to off-white, engraved with "60" on one side [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Duchesnay USA, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Dyspareunia**: Treatment of moderate to severe dyspareunia, a symptom of vulvar and vaginal atrophy (VVA) due to menopause [US].
- **Vaginal Dryness**: Treatment of moderate to severe vaginal dryness, a symptom of vulvar and vaginal atrophy (VVA) due to menopause [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: One 60 mg tablet daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions**: The tablet must be taken orally with food to ensure optimal absorption [US].
- **Treatment Duration**: Should be prescribed for the shortest duration consistent with individual treatment goals and risks. Therapy should be re-evaluated periodically to determine if treatment remains necessary [US].
- **Missed Dose**: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding** [US].
- **Known, suspected, or history of estrogen-dependent neoplasia** [US].
- **Active or history of venous thromboembolism (VTE)**, including deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE) [US].
- **Active or history of arterial thromboembolic disease**, such as stroke or myocardial infarction (MI) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity** to ospemifene or any of the inactive ingredients (e.g., angioedema, urticaria, rash) [US].
- **Known or suspected pregnancy** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]**: 
    - **Endometrial Cancer**: Ospemifene has estrogenic effects on the endometrium. In women with a uterus, unopposed estrogens increase the risk of endometrial cancer. Persistent or recurring abnormal genital bleeding must be investigated to rule out malignancy.
    - **Cardiovascular Disorders**: There is an increased risk of stroke and DVT. Ospemifene should be prescribed for the shortest duration possible.
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE)**: If a VTE occurs or is suspected, discontinue ospemifene immediately. Discontinuation should be considered at least 4 to 6 weeks before surgeries associated with an increased risk of VTE or during periods of prolonged immobilisation [US].
- **Malignant Neoplasms**:
    - **Breast Cancer**: Ospemifene 60 mg has not been adequately studied in women with breast cancer and should not be used in women with a known, suspected, or history of breast cancer [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Do not use in patients with severe hepatic impairment [US].
- **Monitoring**: Women should undergo yearly breast examinations and mammography as appropriate based on age and risk factors [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in 12-week and 52-week clinical trials [US]:

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (12-week trials) | Frequency (up to 52 weeks) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hot flush** | 6.5% | 12.2% |
| **Vaginal discharge** | 3.8% | 6.0% |
| **Muscle spasms** | 1.8% | 4.5% |
| **Hyperhidrosis** | 1.1% | 2.5% |
| **Headache** | <1.0% | 2.8% |
| **Vaginal hemorrhage** | <1.0% | 1.3% |
| **Night sweats** | <1.0% | 1.2% |

- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Thromboembolic and haemorrhagic stroke, deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism, and endometrial hyperplasia/cancer [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience**: Allergic conditions (angioedema, urticaria, rash, pruritus) have been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Estrogens and SERMs**: Concomitant use with estrogens or other estrogen agonist/antagonists has not been studied and should be avoided [US].
- **CYP3A4/2C9/2C19 Inhibitors**:
    - **Fluconazole**: Increases ospemifene systemic exposure by 2.7-fold. Concomitant use is prohibited [US].
    - **Ketoconazole**: Increases exposure by 1.4-fold; use with caution as it may increase the risk of adverse reactions [US].
- **CYP3A4/2C9/2C19 Inducers**:
    - **Rifampin**: Decreases ospemifene exposure by 58%. Concomitant use is expected to reduce clinical efficacy and should be avoided [US].
- **Protein-Bound Drugs**: Ospemifene is >99% serum protein-bound. Concomitant use with other highly protein-bound drugs may increase the exposure of either drug [US].
- **Warfarin**: No significant effect on the pharmacokinetics of a single 10 mg dose of warfarin was observed, but effects on clotting time (INR/PT) were not studied [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Contraindicated** [US]. 
    - **US Category**: Not specified by letter, but label states ospemifene may cause fetal harm. Animal data show embryo-fetal lethality and increased pup deaths at doses near or below clinical exposure [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if ospemifene is excreted in human milk. In rats, it is excreted in milk at higher concentrations than in maternal plasma. A decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother [US].
- **Fertility**: Animal studies showed decreased fertility and reproductive organ atrophy, consistent with estrogen receptor activity [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Not indicated and not studied in the paediatric population [US].
- **Elderly Use**: No clinically meaningful differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between women ≥65 years and younger women [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose adjustment is required in patients with renal impairment, including severe impairment (CrCL <30 mL/min) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**:
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B)**: No dose adjustment is required [US].
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C)**: Not studied; use is contraindicated/not recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: A Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulator (SERM). It binds to estrogen receptors, activating estrogenic pathways in some tissues (agonism) and blocking them in others (antagonism) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Serum estrogen concentrations do not predict therapeutic response or risk [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Reaches peak median concentration at ~2 hours (fasted). Bioavailability increases 2-3 fold when taken with food [US].
    - **Distribution**: Highly protein-bound (>99%). Apparent volume of distribution is 448 L [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4 and CYP2C9; CYP2C19 also contributes. The major metabolite is 4-hydroxyospemifene [US].
    - **Elimination**: Apparent terminal half-life is ~26 hours. Excretion is primarily faecal (75%), with approximately 7% in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy**: Effectiveness was established in three 12-week, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials [US].
- **Findings (Dyspareunia)**: Trials 1 and 2 showed a statistically significant improvement in the severity of dyspareunia (the most bothersome symptom) compared to placebo at week 12.
- **Findings (Vaginal Dryness)**: Trials 1 and 3 showed a statistically significant improvement in moderate to severe vaginal dryness compared to placebo at week 12.
- **Physiological Outcomes**: Significant increases in superficial cells and decreases in parabasal cells on vaginal smears, along with a significant reduction in vaginal pH, were observed across efficacy trials [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life**: Not specified in available source.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification**: **Prescription Only (Rx-only)** [US].
