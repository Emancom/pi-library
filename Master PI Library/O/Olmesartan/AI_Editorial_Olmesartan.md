# Editorial Product Information: Olmesartan Medoxomil and Hydrochlorothiazide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the fixed-dose combination of olmesartan medoxomil and hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ) for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference by clinicians. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Prescribers must consult the local approved PI for full legal and clinical compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Olmesartan medoxomil (Angiotensin II receptor antagonist) and Hydrochlorothiazide (Thiazide diuretic).
- **Brand Names:** AKM-OLMESARTAN HCTZ [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets in the following combinations: 20 mg/12.5 mg, 40 mg/12.5 mg, and 40 mg/25 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** For the treatment of hypertension. This fixed-dose combination is not indicated for the initiation of therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Administered once daily, with or without food.
- **Dosing Strategy:** Dosage must be individualised. If blood pressure is inadequately controlled on monotherapy (olmesartan or HCTZ), patients may be switched to the combination.
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments may be performed after 4 weeks of therapy. 
- **Maximum Dose:** Doses exceeding 40 mg olmesartan / 25 mg HCTZ are not recommended [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** If a specific strength (e.g., 20/25 mg) is required but not available under the current brand, an alternative brand should be sought [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to olmesartan medoxomil, sulfonamide-derived drugs (thiazides), or any excipients.
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated [Australian: Category D].
- **Renal/Urinary:** Anuria or severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min).
- **Hepatic:** Severe hepatic impairment, cholestasis, or biliary obstruction.
- **Lactation:** Breastfeeding is contraindicated.
- **Electrolyte/Metabolic:** Refractory hypokalaemia, hypercalcaemia, hyponatraemia, and symptomatic hyperuricaemia.
- **Dual Blockade:** Concomitant use with aliskiren in patients with diabetes mellitus.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Intravascular Volume Depletion:** Symptomatic hypotension may occur, particularly after the first dose, in patients who are volume or sodium-depleted.
- **Renin-Angiotensin System (RAS) Stimulation:** In patients with severe congestive heart failure or renal artery stenosis, treatment may be associated with acute hypotension, azotaemia, or acute renal failure.
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Risk is increased in patients with renal impairment or heart failure. Close monitoring of serum potassium is recommended, especially when used with potassium-sparing agents or supplements.
- **Non-Melanoma Skin Cancer (NMSC):** Cumulative exposure to HCTZ is associated with an increased risk of basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma. Patients should be advised to limit UV exposure and report new skin lesions [Australian].
- **Sprue-like Enteropathy:** Severe chronic diarrhoea and weight loss have been reported (months to years after initiation). If other etiologies are excluded, discontinuation should be considered.
- **Acute Respiratory Toxicity:** Very rare but severe cases of Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) have been reported following HCTZ intake. Withdrawal is mandatory if ARDS is suspected.
- **Ocular Effects:** HCTZ can cause idiosyncratic reactions resulting in choroidal effusion, acute myopia, and secondary angle-closure glaucoma.
- **Metabolic Effects:** Thiazides may impair glucose tolerance, increase cholesterol/triglyceride levels, and precipitate gout.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Treatment is generally well-tolerated. Dizziness was the only adverse effect reported significantly more frequently than placebo in clinical trials.

| Body System | Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous System** | Common (1–10%) | Dizziness, Headache |
| **Infections** | Common (1–10%) | Bronchitis, Nasopharyngitis, Upper respiratory tract infection, UTI |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Common (1–10%) | Back pain, Arthralgia |
| **General** | Common (1–10%) | Fatigue, Influenza-like illness |
| **Metabolism** | Common (1–10%) | Hyperuricaemia, Hypertriglyceridaemia |
| **Cardiac** | Uncommon (0.1–1%) | Palpitations |
| **Vascular** | Uncommon (0.1–1%) | Hypotension, Orthostatic hypotension |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Rare/Post-marketing | Abdominal pain, Nausea, Vomiting, Diarrhoea |
| **Immune** | Rare/Post-marketing | Anaphylactic reactions, Angioedema |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Sprue-like enteropathy, ARDS, acute renal failure, and rhabdomyolysis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Lithium:** Co-administration is not recommended due to risk of lithium toxicity.
- **NSAIDs/COX-2 Inhibitors:** May act synergistically to decrease glomerular filtration and reduce the antihypertensive effect. Risk of acute renal failure, especially in elderly or dehydrated patients.
- **Dual Blockade of RAS:** Combining ARBs, ACE inhibitors, or aliskiren increases risks of hypotension and hyperkalaemia.
- **Potassium-affecting agents:** Potassium-sparing diuretics, supplements, or salt substitutes may lead to hyperkalaemia. Conversely, HCTZ-induced potassium loss may be potentiated by corticosteroids or ACTH.
- **Colesevelam HCl:** Reduces exposure to olmesartan. Administer olmesartan at least 4 hours before colesevelam.
- **Alcohol/Barbiturates:** May potentiate orthostatic hypotension.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **[Australian Category D]** Contraindicated. Use of drugs acting on the RAS during the second and third trimesters can cause foetal injury (renal failure, skull hypoplasia) and death. If pregnancy is detected, discontinue immediately.
- **Breastfeeding:** Thiazides appear in human milk. Due to potential adverse effects on the infant, a decision must be made to discontinue nursing or the drug.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No initial dosage adjustment is generally necessary; however, caution is advised during up-titration to maximum doses [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild to Moderate (CrCl 30–80 mL/min): No dose adjustment required; monitor renal function and electrolytes periodically.
    - Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min): Contraindicated.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh 5–9): No dose adjustment required.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh 10–15): Contraindicated.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Olmesartan medoxomil:** A selective AT1 receptor antagonist that blocks the vasoconstrictor and aldosterone-secreting effects of Angiotensin II.
    - **HCTZ:** A thiazide diuretic affecting renal tubular electrolyte reabsorption, increasing excretion of sodium and chloride.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Olmesartan:** Rapidly converted from the prodrug; absolute bioavailability is 25.6%. Peak plasma levels (Cmax) reached in 2 hours. Excreted in urine (30-50%) and faeces.
    - **HCTZ:** Cmax reached in 1.5 to 2 hours. Not metabolised; excreted unchanged in urine.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Combined therapy provides additive blood pressure reduction. Onset of action for HCTZ occurs within 2 hours, persisting for 6–12 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Factorial Trials:** In a factorial trial (n=502), the 40/25 mg dose produced a placebo-adjusted blood pressure reduction of 24/14 mmHg [Australian].
- **Long-term Efficacy:** Antihypertensive effects are maintained for at least 1 year without evidence of tachyphylaxis or rebound hypertension upon withdrawal.
- **ROADMAP/ORIENT Studies:** Investigated olmesartan in diabetic patients. Note: These studies showed increased cardiovascular mortality in the olmesartan arm compared to placebo in specific diabetic cohorts, though non-fatal MI risk was lower [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** 24 months [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Al/Al blister packs or HDPE bottles with child-resistant closures.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (S4) [Australian].
