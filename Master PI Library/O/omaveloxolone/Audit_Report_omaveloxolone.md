## Originality Strengths
*   **Numerical Synthesis:** The editorial successfully harmonizes disparate data points. For example, in the "Elevation of Aminotransferases" section, it synthesizes the TGA’s "29.4%" and the FDA’s "31%" into a range ("29–31%").
*   **Pharmacokinetic Merging:** The editorial merges the half-life data (FDA cites 57 hours, TGA cites 58 hours) into "approximately 57–58 hours," demonstrating an active effort to reconcile sources rather than choosing one.
*   **Structural Reorganization:** The editorial abandons the rigid 17-section FDA structure and the 10-section TGA structure in favor of a new, logical 11-section "Unified" format.
*   **Dosing Harmonization:** Section 5 (Hepatic Impairment) successfully combines the Child-Pugh B instructions from both agencies, noting that both suggest a 100 mg reduction but framing it in an original "Standard/Missed/Impairment" layout.

## Differences from Source PIs
*   **Harmonized Storage:** The editorial merges the FDA's specific excursion temperatures (15°C to 30°C) with the TGA’s "Store below 30°C" limit.
*   **Omission of Minor Data:** The editorial omits granular data found in the TGA PI regarding specific study numbers (e.g., Study 1402) in favor of a high-level summary of findings.
*   **Metric Conversion:** The editorial uses TGA metric measurements for weight gain, which is more precise for an international "unified" document than the imperial units used in the FDA source.
*   **Consolidated Excipients:** The editorial groups the capsule shell components and internal contents into a single list in Section 3, whereas the TGA splits them across different sections.
