# Editorial Product Information: Opicapone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Opicapone (a peripheral, selective, and reversible catechol-O-methyltransferase [COMT] inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** Ongentys [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N04BX04.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Hard capsules, 50 mg.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Sponsor: Maxx Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].
    - Manufacturer: BIAL – Portela & Cª, S.A. [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Parkinson’s Disease:** Indicated as adjunctive therapy to preparations of levodopa/DOPA decarboxylase inhibitors (DDCI) in adult patients with Parkinson’s disease and end-of-dose motor fluctuations who cannot be stabilised on those combinations [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg taken once daily at bedtime.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Should be taken without food (preferably on an empty stomach).
    - Avoid food intake for at least one hour before and one hour after administration of levodopa combinations.
    - Specifically, it should be taken two hours before or two hours after a meal.
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole with water.
- **Dose Adjustments (Antiparkinsonian therapy):** As opicapone enhances levodopa effects, it is often necessary to adjust the levodopa dose (by extending dosing intervals or reducing the dose per administration) within the first days to weeks of starting treatment to mitigate dopaminergic adverse reactions.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the next dose should be taken at the scheduled time. An extra dose should not be taken to compensate.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to opicapone or any listed excipients (including lactose and sulfites).
- Phaeochromocytoma, paraganglioma, or other catecholamine-secreting neoplasms.
- History of neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS) and/or non-traumatic rhabdomyolysis.
- Concomitant use with non-selective monoamine oxidase (MAO-A and MAO-B) inhibitors (e.g., phenelzine, tranylcypromine, moclobemide) other than those specifically indicated for Parkinson’s disease.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Dopaminergic Effects:** Opicapone potentiates levodopa. Monitor for dyskinesia, hallucinations, nausea, vomiting, and orthostatic hypotension.
- **Psychiatric/Impulse Control Disorders:** Patients should be monitored for the development of pathological gambling, increased libido, hypersexuality, compulsive spending, and binge eating.
- **Liver Function:** While opicapone itself has not been specifically linked to severe hepatotoxicity in the provided source, the COMT-inhibitor class has reports of increased liver enzymes. Evaluation of liver function should be considered if patients experience progressive anorexia, asthenia, or weight decrease.
- **Ischemic Heart Disease:** Observed frequently with other COMT inhibitors; caution is advised.
- **Excipients:** Contains lactose; contraindicated in patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption. Contains trace sulfites (from gelatin).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally related to increased dopaminergic stimulation.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dyskinesia (18.3%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Constipation, insomnia, dry mouth, dizziness, headache, orthostatic hypotension, hallucinations, abnormal dreams, muscle spasms, vomiting, increased blood creatine phosphokinase |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypertriglyceridemia, decreased appetite, auditory hallucinations, anxiety, depression, syncope, dysgeusia, dry eye, ear congestion, dyspnoea, abdominal distension, chromaturia, nocturia |

- **Post-marketing reports (Frequency unknown):** Agitation, psychotic disorder, confusional state, balance disorder, diarrhea, fatigue, and malaise.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAO Inhibitors:** 
    - Non-selective MAOIs: Contraindicated. 
    - MAO-B inhibitors for PD (e.g., rasagiline up to 1 mg/day, selegiline up to 10 mg/day): Permissible. 
    - Safinamide: No experience; use with caution.
- **COMT-Metabolised Drugs:** Potential to potentiate effects of adrenaline, noradrenaline, dopamine, dobutamine, and isoprenaline. Careful monitoring is required.
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants/SNRIs:** Limited experience; use with caution.
- **Quinidine:** Systemic exposure of opicapone decreases by approximately 37% when co-administered; this combination should be avoided.
- **Warfarin:** Slight reduction in AUC of S- and R-warfarin (13-14%) observed; no change in Cmax.
- **Transporters:** Inhibition of OATP1B1 cannot be ruled out; opicapone is a substrate of OATP1B3, P-gp, and BCRP.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B2. There is no or limited data in pregnant women. Not recommended during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using contraception.
- **Breastfeeding:** Animal studies show excretion in milk. A risk to newborns cannot be excluded; breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment.
- **Fertility:** No human data. No effects on rat fertility at 16–17 times human therapeutic exposure.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No relevant use or data in children with Parkinson's disease.
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** No dose adjustment required. Caution is advised in patients ≥75 years due to limited experience.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required; opicapone is not primarily excreted by the kidneys.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** **Not recommended** for patients with any degree of hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class A, B, or C) [Australian]. Bioavailability is significantly increased in moderate impairment (Class B).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective, reversible, and peripheral COMT inhibitor. By inhibiting the conversion of levodopa to 3-O-methyldopa (3-OMD), it increases levodopa plasma levels, extending the clinical response.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits long-lasting COMT inhibition (>24 hours) with >90% inhibition at a 50 mg dose.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (Tmax 0.5–3.0h). High-fat meals significantly decrease Cmax and AUC.
    - **Distribution:** High protein binding (99.9%).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via sulfation (yielding inactive opicapone sulfate).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life of parent drug is 0.7–2.3h; however, the sulfate metabolite has a long half-life (94–114h). Elimination is 70% faecal.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Two Phase 3 double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (Study 1 also included an entacapone arm).
- **Outcomes:** Opicapone 50 mg demonstrated superior reduction in absolute "OFF" time compared to placebo (LS mean change: -103.6 min vs -48.3 min in Study 1; -107.0 min vs -54.6 min in Study 2). Non-inferiority to entacapone was demonstrated in Study 1.
- **Long-term Data:** One-year open-label extensions showed maintenance of effect.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C [Australian].
- Keep in the original package to protect from moisture.
- Shelf life is specified on the secondary packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **Additional Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring in Australia to allow quick identification of new safety information.
