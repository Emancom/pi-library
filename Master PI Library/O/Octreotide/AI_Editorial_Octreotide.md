# Editorial Product Information: Octreotide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, professional format.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Octreotide (as acetate), a synthetic octapeptide analogue of naturally occurring somatostatin.
- **Brand Names:** Octreotide GH [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Solution for injection (clear and colourless).
    - Available in 1 mL ampoules containing 0.05 mg, 0.1 mg, or 0.5 mg of octreotide [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection; intravenous (IV) infusion/bolus (referenced in overdose/specific settings).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Generic Health Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acromegaly:** For the reduction of growth hormone (GH) and IGF-1 plasma levels and symptomatic control in patients inadequately managed by surgery, radiotherapy, or dopamine agonists. Also indicated for those unfit for surgery or during the interim period before radiotherapy takes full effect [Australian].
- **Gastro-entero-pancreatic (GEP) Endocrine Tumours:** For the relief of symptoms associated with functional tumours, specifically:
    - Carcinoid tumours (carcinoid syndrome).
    - Vasoactive intestinal peptide secreting tumours (VIPomas) [Australian].
- **Pancreatic Surgery:** For the reduction of complications following surgery on the pancreas [Australian].
- **Note:** **Octreotide** is not curative in patients with functional GEP tumours [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Acromegaly:** Initially 0.05 mg to 0.1 mg via SC injection every 8 or 12 hours. Dosage should be titrated based on monthly GH/IGF-1 levels and clinical tolerance. Most patients require 0.2 mg to 0.3 mg daily. The maximum dose is 1.5 mg per day [Australian].
- **GEP Endocrine Tumours:** Initially 0.05 mg SC once or twice daily. Depending on response, dose may be increased to 0.2 mg three times daily. Doses above 0.75 mg/day are rarely required [Australian].
- **Complications of Pancreatic Surgery:** 0.1 mg SC three times daily for seven consecutive days. The first dose should be administered at least one hour prior to laparotomy [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- To minimize local discomfort, allow the solution to reach room temperature before injection.
- Avoid multiple injections at the same site within short intervals.
- Ampoules should be opened immediately before use; any unused portion must be discarded [Australian].

### Titration and Monitoring
- Treatment for acromegaly should be discontinued if no relevant reduction in GH levels or symptomatic improvement occurs within three months [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **octreotide** or any excipient in the formulation (glycine, mannitol, hydrochloric acid) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Pancreatic Function:** Pancreatic exocrine insufficiency (PEI) has been observed. Symptoms include steatorrhoea, loose stools, bloating, and weight loss. Symptomatic patients should be screened and treated accordingly [Australian].
- **Gallbladder Events:** Cholelithiasis is very common (15-30% of long-term users). Gallbladder ultrasound is recommended at baseline and at 6-12 month intervals. Cases of cholecystitis and cholangitis have occurred [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Events:** Bradycardia is common. Adjustments to concurrent medications such as beta-blockers or calcium channel blockers may be necessary [Australian].
- **Glucose Regulation:** **Octreotide** inhibits the release of insulin, glucagon, and GH. It may reduce insulin requirements in Type I diabetics or cause prandial hyperglycaemia in non-diabetics or Type II diabetics with partial insulin reserves. Close monitoring of glucose tolerance is required [Australian].
- **Pituitary Tumours:** Growth hormone-secreting tumours may expand; patients should be monitored for signs of expansion (e.g., visual field defects) [Australian].
- **Nutrition:** May alter absorption of dietary fats and Vitamin B12. Monitoring of B12 levels is recommended in patients with a history of B12 deprivation [Australian].
- **Thyroid Function:** Prolonged treatment warrants monitoring of thyroid function [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Diarrhoea, abdominal pain, nausea, constipation, flatulence, headache, cholelithiasis, hyperglycaemia, injection site reactions. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Dyspepsia, vomiting, abdominal distension, steatorrhoea, loose stools, dizziness, hypothyroidism, biliary sludge, hyperbilirubinaemia, hypoglycaemia, anorexia, pruritus, rash, alopecia, dyspnoea, bradycardia, asthenia. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Dehydration, tachycardia. |
| **Not Known** | Thrombocytopenia, anaphylaxis, urticaria, acute pancreatitis, acute hepatitis, jaundice, arrhythmias. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** Typically occurs within the first hours/days of treatment or upon withdrawal.
- **Anaphylaxis:** Isolated cases of anaphylactic shock have been reported [Australian].
- **Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Including QT prolongation and ECG changes [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cyclosporin:** **Octreotide** reduces intestinal absorption of cyclosporin, necessitating monitoring of blood levels.
- **Cimetidine:** Absorption of cimetidine may be delayed.
- **Bromocriptine:** Bioavailability of bromocriptine is increased when co-administered.
- **CYP450 Metabolism:** Somatostatin analogues may decrease metabolic clearance of drugs handled by CYP3A4 (e.g., quinidine) due to GH suppression. Use caution with CYP3A4 substrates that have a narrow therapeutic index [Australian].
- **Antidiabetics:** Adjustments to insulin or oral hypoglycaemic agents may be required [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category C**. Data are limited; spontaneous abortions have been reported in acromegalic patients, though no congenital malformations were noted. Use only under compelling circumstances [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if **octreotide** is excreted in human milk; however, animal studies show excretion. Breastfeeding should be avoided during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Normalisation of IGF-1 levels in acromegalic women may restore fertility. Advise patients of childbearing potential to use adequate contraception [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Experience is very limited. In cases of overdose, mild hyperglycaemia was reported [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No evidence of reduced tolerability or requirement for altered dosages [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Total exposure (AUC) after SC administration is not affected; no dose adjustment is necessary for SC dosing. However, clearance is reduced by approximately 50% in patients with severe renal failure requiring dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In patients with liver cirrhosis, the elimination half-life may be increased, requiring consideration of maintenance dose adjustments [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Synthetic octapeptide analogue of somatostatin. It more potently inhibits the secretion of growth hormone, glucagon, and insulin than natural somatostatin, with greater selectivity for GH and glucagon. It also inhibits the release of serotonin and other GEP peptides (gastrin, VIP, secretin, motilin) and reduces splanchnic blood flow [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and complete via SC; peak plasma levels reached in 0.4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 0.27 L/kg; 65% protein bound (mainly to lipoproteins).
    - **Metabolism:** Clearance is non-linear at higher doses (above 600 µg/day).
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is 1.5 hours. Approximately 32% is excreted unchanged in urine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Short-term Storage:** Unopened ampoules may be stored at or below 25°C for up to two weeks; discard if not used within this period.
- **Protection:** Keep in the original package to protect from light [Australian].
