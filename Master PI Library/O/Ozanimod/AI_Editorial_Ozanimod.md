# Editorial Product Information: Ozanimod

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions Contributed:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information (PI) for **ozanimod**. It is designed as a structured clinical reference for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information. Clinicians must consult the current PI approved in their specific region before prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ozanimod (as hydrochloride)
- **Chemical Class:** Sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P) receptor modulator
- **Brand Name:** **ZEPOSIA** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L04AE02
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 230 microgram capsules (imprinted "OZA" / "0.23 mg")
    - 460 microgram capsules (imprinted "OZA" / "0.46 mg")
    - 920 microgram capsules (imprinted "OZA" / "0.92 mg")
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bristol-Myers Squibb [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis (RMS):** Treatment of adult patients with relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis [Australian].
- **Ulcerative Colitis (UC):** Treatment of adult patients with moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis who have had an inadequate response, lost response, or were intolerant to conventional or biological therapies [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Maintenance Dose:** 920 microgram once daily.
- **Administration:** Swallow whole; with or without food.

### Titration Schedule
To mitigate the risk of bradycardia and heart rate reduction, a 7-day titration is mandatory:
- **Days 1–4:** 230 microgram once daily.
- **Days 5–7:** 460 microgram once daily.
- **Day 8 and thereafter:** 920 microgram once daily (maintenance).

### Prior to Initiation Requirements
- **Cardiac:** Obtain an ECG to screen for pre-existing conduction abnormalities.
- **Hepatic:** Obtain Liver Function Tests (LFTs) within the last 6 months.
- **Haematologic:** Obtain a Complete Blood Count (CBC), including lymphocyte count, within 6 months.
- **Ophthalmic:** Assess for macular oedema risk (e.g., diabetes, uveitis).
- **Infectious Disease:** Test for varicella zoster virus (VZV) antibodies; vaccinate antibody-negative patients at least 1 month before starting treatment.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- **During the first 2 weeks:** If a single dose is missed, re-initiate the 7-day titration regimen.
- **After the first 2 weeks:** Continue with the planned dose.
- **Prolonged Interruption:** Re-initiate titration if more than 7 consecutive days are missed (Days 15–28) or more than 14 consecutive days are missed (after Day 28) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Recent Cardiovascular Events:** Initiation is contraindicated if the patient experienced myocardial infarction, unstable angina, stroke, TIA, decompensated heart failure (requiring hospitalisation), or Class III/IV heart failure within the previous 6 months.
- **Cardiac Conduction Disorders:** History or presence of second-degree AV block Type II, third-degree AV block, or sick sinus syndrome (unless a functional pacemaker is present).
- **Respiratory:** Severe untreated sleep apnoea.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **ozanimod** or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bradycardia and Heart Rate Reduction:** Transient decreases in heart rate occur upon initiation. Dose titration is essential. Consult a cardiologist for patients with significant QT prolongation or those taking Class Ia/III antiarrhythmics.
- **Infections:** **Ozanimod** reduces peripheral lymphocyte counts (to ~45% of baseline). Delay initiation in patients with active infections.
    - **PML:** Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy has been reported. Monitor for new/worsening neurological symptoms.
    - **IRIS:** Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome can occur after discontinuing S1P modulators in the setting of PML.
- **Liver Injury:** Clinically significant liver injury can occur as early as 10 days post-initiation. Discontinue if significant injury is confirmed.
- **Macular Oedema:** Patients with a history of diabetes or uveitis are at increased risk. 
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Reported rarely; discontinue if suspected.
- **Increased Blood Pressure:** Monitor BP regularly during treatment.
- **MS Disease Rebound:** Severe exacerbation is possible after discontinuation.
- **Respiratory Effects:** Minor dose-dependent reductions in FEV1 and FVC have been observed.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Serious Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nasopharyngitis, Lymphopenia |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Pharyngitis, UTI, Herpes zoster, ALT/GGT increase, Hypertension, Orthostatic hypotension, Headache, Back pain, Peripheral oedema |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Macular oedema, Hypersensitivity (rash/urticaria) |

- **Serious:** Liver injury, Bradycardia (usually transient and asymptomatic on Day 1), and serious opportunistic infections.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP2C8 Inhibitors:** (e.g., gemfibrozil, clopidogrel) Increase exposure of active metabolites; use with caution [Australian].
- **CYP2C8 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin) Co-administration is not recommended.
- **MAO Inhibitors:** (e.g., selegiline, phenelzine) Potential for clinical interaction has not been studied; co-administration is not recommended.
- **Heart Rate-Slowing Drugs:** Caution with beta-blockers or calcium channel blockers due to additive effects on heart rate.
- **Vaccinations:** Avoid live attenuated vaccines during and for 3 months after treatment. Efficacy of vaccines may be reduced.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D**. **Ozanimod** may cause fetal harm (teratogenicity and embryo-fetal death in animal studies). Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for **3 months** after stopping [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Ozanimod/metabolites are present in rat milk; risks to nursing infants cannot be excluded.
- **Fertility:** No apparent effect on sperm count/motility in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Safety and effectiveness in patients <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly:** No dose adjustment required for patients >65 years; however, use caution due to increased risk of serious infections.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild/Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** Titrate normally, then use 920 microgram **every other day** for maintenance.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment necessary.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A selective S1P receptor modulator (subtypes 1 and 5). It induces the retention of lymphocytes in lymph nodes, preventing their migration into the CNS (in MS) and the intestine (in UC).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is 6–8 hours. High-fat meals do not affect exposure.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised. The major active metabolites (CC112273 and CC1084037) account for ~90% of the total activity.
    - **Elimination:** Effective half-life of CC112273 is approximately 11 days. Steady state is reached in ~45 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Multiple Sclerosis:** Evaluated in SUNBEAM (1 year) and RADIANCE (2 years). **Ozanimod** 920 mcg significantly reduced the Annualised Relapse Rate (ARR) compared to intramuscular interferon beta-1a (0.181 vs 0.350 in SUNBEAM; 0.172 vs 0.276 in RADIANCE).
- **Ulcerative Colitis:** Evaluated in TRUENORTH. Significant improvements were seen in clinical remission (18% vs 6% placebo at Week 10; 37% vs 19% placebo at Week 52) and mucosal healing.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Store in the original package.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
