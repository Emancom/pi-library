# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - United States (US Food and Drug Administration)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (not less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - Maintenance daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Dosage adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations fall outside the desired range [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before considering re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients receiving levocarnitine, including those without a prior history of seizure activity. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting. Less frequent effects include body odor and gastritis [US].
- **ESRD Clinical Trial Data (≥5% frequency):**
    - **Body as a whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), and pain (21–35%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%) [US].
    - **Neurologic:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Seizures and serious hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis) have been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned, but follows former Category C-style labelling]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows indicate levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients. Specific age-range safety profiles beyond the indicated uses are not detailed [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See *Section 5: Warnings and Precautions* regarding oral levocarnitine and metabolite accumulation. Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine helps clear accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Trials:** Studies in healthy volunteers showed the absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15% [US].
- **ESRD Trials:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean trough levocarnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L by week 8 [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
