# Editorial Product Information: Oxcarbazepine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing and dispensing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Oxcarbazepine (a dibenzazepine derivative).
- **Chemical Name:** 10,11-Dihydro-10-oxo-5H-dibenz [b,f]azepine-5-carboxamide.
- **Brand Name:** **Trileptal** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated Tablets:** 150 mg (pale grey-green, scored), 300 mg (yellow, scored), and 600 mg (light pink, scored) [Australian].
    - **Oral Suspension:** 60 mg/mL (off-white to reddish-brown) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Epilepsy:** Indicated for use as monotherapy or adjunctive therapy in the treatment of partial seizures and generalised tonic-clonic seizures in both adults and children [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Adults:**
    - **Initial Dose:** 600 mg/day (8–10 mg/kg/day) administered in two divided doses.
    - **Maintenance:** 600 mg to 2400 mg/day. Increases should not exceed 600 mg/day at weekly intervals.
    - **Maximum Dose:** 2400 mg/day.
- **Paediatrics (1 month to 18 years):**
    - **Initial Dose:** 8–10 mg/kg/day in two divided doses.
    - **Maintenance:** Target doses vary by age; children aged 1 month to <4 years may require twice the adult dose per body weight due to higher clearance.
    - **Maximum Dose:** 60 mg/kg/day.

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food.
- **Tablets:** The 300 mg tablet score line is for equal dosing; 150 mg and 600 mg score lines are for ease of swallowing only [Australian].
- **Oral Suspension:** Must be shaken well. Doses should be prepared using the provided oral syringe and can be mixed in water or swallowed directly.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **oxcarbazepine**, **eslicarbazepine**, or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Class I (immediate) reactions (angioedema, anaphylaxis) can occur. Discontinue immediately if suspected.
- **Multi-organ Hypersensitivity:** Median onset is 13 days. Manifestations include rash, fever, lymphadenopathy, and liver function abnormalities.
- **Serious Dermatological Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) have been reported. 
    - **Pharmacogenomics:** Testing for **HLA-B\*1502** is recommended in patients of Han Chinese or Thai ancestry prior to initiation [Australian]. **HLA-A\*3101** may also be a risk factor in European and Japanese populations.
- **Hyponatraemia:** Serum sodium <125 mmol/L occurs in up to 2.7% of patients. Monitor sodium levels in patients with pre-existing renal conditions or those on sodium-lowering medications (e.g., diuretics).
- **Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation:** All antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) carry an increased risk. Monitor for changes in mood or behaviour.
- **Withdrawal:** Must be withdrawn gradually to minimise the risk of increased seizure frequency.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Somnolence, headache, dizziness, diplopia, nausea, vomiting, fatigue.
- **Common (1–10%):** Hyponatraemia, agitation, depression, ataxia, tremor, nystagmus, blurred vision, vertigo, diarrhoea, constipation, rash, alopecia, acne, asthenia.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Leucopenia, urticaria, increased hepatic enzymes/alkaline phosphatase.
- **Very Rare (<0.1%):** Bone marrow depression, aplastic anaemia, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, arrhythmia, atrioventricular block, pancreatitis, hepatitis, SJS/TEN, Systemic Lupus Erythematosus.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN, multi-organ hypersensitivity, and clinically significant hyponatraemia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzyme Inhibition:** Oxcarbazepine and its active metabolite (MHD) inhibit **CYP2C19**. Increased levels of phenytoin (up to 40% at doses >1200 mg/day) and phenobarbitone (15%) may occur.
- **Enzyme Induction:** Induces **CYP3A4/5**, significantly reducing plasma levels of hormonal contraceptives (EE and LNG AUC decreased by ~50%), calcium antagonists (e.g., felodipine), and some AEDs (e.g., carbamazepine).
- **Alcohol:** May have an additive sedative effect.
- **St John’s Wort:** Use with caution due to potential induction of metabolism.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Data suggest a potential for serious birth defects (e.g., VSD, cleft palate). MHD levels may decrease during pregnancy; monitoring is required. Folic acid supplementation (5 mg) is recommended prior to and during the first trimester.
- **Breastfeeding:** Oxcarbazepine and MHD are excreted in breast milk (milk-to-plasma ratio 0.5). Use is not recommended during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data show reduced fertility in females at high doses; no human data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not studied in infants <1 month. Weight-adjusted clearance is higher in younger children.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose recommendations unless renal impairment is present. Higher risk of hyponatraemia.
- **Renal Impairment:** In patients with CrCl <30 mL/min, initiate at 300 mg/day and titrate slowly [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment. Not studied in severe impairment; use with caution.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primary activity is via the metabolite **MHD**. Blockade of voltage-sensitive sodium channels stabilises hyperexcited neural membranes and inhibits repetitive neuronal firing.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Completely absorbed; unaffected by food.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly reduced by cytosolic enzymes in the liver to MHD.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (>95%); MHD half-life is ~9.3 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** MHD is a potent anticonvulsant. It does not significantly interact with brain neurotransmitter or modulator receptor sites.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Demonstrated in 10 double-blind controlled trials (8 monotherapy, 2 adjunctive).
- **Comparative Efficacy:** Shown to have similar efficacy to valproate, phenytoin, and carbamazepine, with a better tolerability profile than phenytoin [Australian].
- **Paediatric Adjunctive Trial:** Significant reduction in seizure frequency at 60 mg/kg/day compared to 10 mg/kg/day in children aged 1 month to <4 years.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store in the original package below 30°C.
- **Oral Suspension:** Must be used within 7 weeks of first opening the bottle [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
