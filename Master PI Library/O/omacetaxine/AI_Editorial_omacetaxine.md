# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. At the time of writing, only the United States documentation was available for review. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection, USP; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Dosing Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These can occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients [US]. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately.

### Precautions and Monitoring
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring (Metabolic):** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Form):** While this editorial covers the injection, it is noted that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (ESRD Clinical Trials, ≥5%)
The following effects were reported regardless of causality in ESRD patients on hemodialysis:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%) [US].

### Other Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis occur less frequently [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Recommendation:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned via letter, but listed as "use only if clearly needed"]. Animal reproductive studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women exist [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, levocarnitine concentration in milk increases following exogenous administration. Clinicians should weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders and ESRD apply to the paediatric population [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; toxic metabolite accumulation is a risk. For the *injection*, dose adjustments in ESRD are based on pre-dialysis trough concentrations [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for oxidation and energy production [US]. 
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, it promotes the excretion of excess organic acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are quickly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Efficacy was demonstrated in two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials. Results showed that levocarnitine injection (10, 20, or 40 mg/kg) significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. However, the impact of this increase on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of an opened vial as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
