# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial was developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory guidance.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing and dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Clear, colourless sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- Administer by intravenous injection only.
- Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required. The maximum reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Therapy may be initiated if pre-dialysis (trough) plasma carnitine levels are below normal (40 to 50 µmol/L). Dose adjustments (as low as 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough concentrations [US].

### Monitoring
- It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before therapy.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is advised [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Hypersensitivity Reactions
- Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. 
- Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration. 
- **Management:** Discontinue immediately and initiate appropriate medical treatment. Re-administration should only be considered after weighing risks and benefits, followed by close monitoring [US].

### Seizures
- Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with established seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### General Precautions
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD or severe renal compromise may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally cleared by the kidneys [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).

### Other Reported Effects
- Transient nausea and vomiting.
- Gastritis.
- Body odor (less frequent).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Risk Summary:** No evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm was observed in rat and rabbit reproductive studies at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area). However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].

### Breastfeeding
- **Risk Summary:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].

### Fertility
- No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See dosage and administration section for weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg in metabolic disorders) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Extensively studied in ESRD patients undergoing hemodialysis (see Section 3). Levocarnitine is dialyzable [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US]. 
- It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are quickly eliminated.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following a single 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is low, approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x/week after dialysis), trough carnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** A study of ~30 patients per group receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks. 
- **Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the impact of supplemental carnitine on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Packaging:** Supplied in 1 g/5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials, 10 vials per carton [US].
