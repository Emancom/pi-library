# Unified Editorial Product Information: Olodaterol and Tiotropium Inhalation Solution

**Editorial Note:** This document represents a unified Product Information (PI) summary for the combination of olodaterol and tiotropium. It has been synthesized from the official regulatory documents of the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Where clinical thresholds or specific safety instructions differ between the two agencies, the more comprehensive or conservative guidance has been selected and annotated accordingly.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Names:** Spiolto® Respimat® [TGA] / Stiolto® Respimat® [FDA]

**Active Ingredients:**
*   **Tiotropium bromide monohydrate:** A long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA/anticholinergic).
*   **Olodaterol hydrochloride:** A long-acting beta₂-adrenergic agonist (LABA).

Each actuation delivers 2.5 micrograms of tiotropium and 2.5 micrograms of olodaterol from the mouthpiece. A single medicinal dose consists of two actuations, delivering a total of 5 micrograms of tiotropium and 5 micrograms of olodaterol.

---

## 2. Approved Indications
This combination therapy is indicated for the long-term, once-daily maintenance treatment of airflow obstruction in patients with **chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)**, including chronic bronchitis and emphysema.

**Important Limitations of Use:**
*   **Not for Acute Use:** This medication is not a rescue therapy and must not be used to treat acute episodes of bronchospasm or acutely deteriorating COPD.
*   **Not for Asthma:** The safety and efficacy of this product have not been established in patients with asthma; it is not indicated for the treatment of asthma [FDA/TGA].

---

## 3. Contraindications
Administration is contraindicated in:
*   Patients with a history of hypersensitivity to tiotropium, olodaterol, ipratropium, or any component of the formulation.
*   Patients with a history of hypersensitivity to atropine or its derivatives (e.g., oxitropium) [TGA].
*   **Asthma without ICS:** The use of a LABA without a concomitant inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) is contraindicated in patients with asthma [FDA].

---

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Asthma-Related Events
While indicated for COPD, the LABA component (olodaterol) belongs to a class associated with an increased risk of asthma-related death when used as monotherapy. Data suggest this risk is not significantly increased when LABAs are used in fixed-dose combination with an inhaled corticosteroid.

### Deterioration and Acute Episodes
Treatment should not be initiated during acutely deteriorating COPD. Increasing the frequency of dose beyond once daily is inappropriate. Patients must have a short-acting, inhaled beta₂-agonist (SABA) available for acute symptoms.

### Paradoxical Bronchospasm
As with other inhaled medicines, life-threatening paradoxical bronchospasm may occur. If this occurs, the medication should be discontinued immediately and alternative therapy instituted.

### Cardiovascular Effects
Olodaterol may produce clinically significant cardiovascular effects, including increases in pulse rate, blood pressure, and ECG changes (e.g., T-wave flattening, ST-segment depression, or QTc prolongation). Use with caution in patients with:
*   Cardiac arrhythmias or coronary insufficiency.
*   Hypertension or hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy.
*   Known or suspected QTc interval prolongation.

### Coexisting Conditions
Use with caution in patients with convulsive disorders, thyrotoxicosis, or those unusually responsive to sympathomimetic amines. High doses of beta₂-agonists may also aggravate pre-existing diabetes mellitus or ketoacidosis.

### Anticholinergic Effects
Consistent with its anticholinergic activity, the tiotropium component requires cautious use in:
*   **Narrow-angle glaucoma:** Patients should be alert for eye pain, blurred vision, or visual halos.
*   **Urinary retention:** Use with caution in patients with prostatic hyperplasia or bladder-neck obstruction.

### Renal Impairment
As tiotropium is primarily excreted renally, patients with moderate to severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 60 mL/min [FDA] or $\le$ 50 mL/min [TGA]) should be monitored closely for anticholinergic side effects.

---

## 5. Dosage and Administration
**Adult Dosage:**
The recommended dose is **two inhalations (puffs) once daily** at the same time of day.

**Administration Guidelines:**
*   For oral inhalation only.
*   Do not use more than two inhalations every 24 hours.
*   **Elderly/Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for geriatric patients or those with mild-to-moderate hepatic impairment. Data are not available for severe hepatic impairment.
*   **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required, though monitoring is essential (see Section 4).

---

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following adverse reactions have been identified in clinical trials:
*   **Most Common (>3%):** Nasopharyngitis, cough, and back pain.
*   **Infections:** Pneumonia, bronchitis, sinusitis, and urinary tract infection.
*   **Nervous System:** Dizziness, insomnia, and headache.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Dry mouth (common anticholinergic effect), constipation, and oropharyngeal candidiasis.
*   **Cardiac:** Palpitations, tachycardia, and atrial fibrillation (rare).
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Rash, pruritus, angioedema, and urticaria.

---

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Adrenergic Agents:** Concomitant use of other sympathomimetic agents may increase the risk of cardiovascular side effects.
*   **Xanthine Derivatives, Steroids, or Diuretics:** These may potentiate the hypokalemic effect of beta₂-agonists. Non-potassium sparing diuretics (loop or thiazide) should be used with caution.
*   **Beta-Blockers:** Beta-adrenergic antagonists may interfere with the effect of olodaterol and may induce severe bronchospasm in COPD patients. If required, cardioselective beta-blockers should be used with extreme caution.
*   **MAOIs and TCAs:** Drugs that prolong the QTc interval or inhibit monoamine oxidase may potentiate cardiovascular effects.
*   **Anticholinergics:** Chronic co-administration with other anticholinergic-containing drugs (e.g., ipratropium) is not recommended due to the potential for additive effects.

---

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies showed fetal toxicity only at very high systemic exposures. Due to the inhibitory effect of beta-agonists on uterine contractions, use during labor should be restricted to cases where the benefits clearly outweigh the risks.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown whether tiotropium or olodaterol are excreted in human milk. Though detected in the milk of lactating rats, use in breastfeeding women should only be considered if the expected benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the infant.

---

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Following inhalation, approximately 33% of tiotropium and 30% of olodaterol reach the systemic circulation. Olodaterol reaches peak plasma levels within 10 to 20 minutes.
*   **Distribution:** Tiotropium has a plasma protein binding of 72%; olodaterol binding is approximately 60%.
*   **Metabolism:** Tiotropium undergoes minimal non-enzymatic cleavage. Olodaterol is substantially metabolized via direct glucuronidation and O-demethylation (CYP2C9 and CYP2C8).
*   **Elimination:** The terminal half-life for both components following inhalation is approximately 45 hours. Tiotropium is primarily excreted unchanged in the urine, whereas olodaterol is mainly excreted via the feces (84%) following oral intake, with a small percentage (5–7%) excreted unchanged in the urine after inhalation.

---

## 10. Mechanism of Action
This product provides additive bronchodilation through two distinct pathways:
1.  **Tiotropium:** Competitively and reversibly inhibits M₃-receptors in the airway smooth muscle, resulting in long-acting relaxation of the airways.
2.  **Olodaterol:** Acts as a long-acting beta₂-adrenoceptor agonist that stimulates intracellular adenyl cyclase. This increases cyclic-AMP levels, leading to relaxation of the bronchial smooth muscle.

---

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [TGA] or at controlled room temperature 20°C to 25°C [FDA].
*   **Handling:** Do not freeze.
*   **Shelf Life:** The cartridge must be discarded **3 months** after insertion into the inhaler or when the device locks, whichever occurs first.
