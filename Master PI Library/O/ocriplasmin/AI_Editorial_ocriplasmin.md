# Unified Product Information: Ocriplasmin

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** JETREA  
**Active Ingredient:** Ocriplasmin  

Ocriplasmin is a recombinant, truncated version of human plasmin, produced via recombinant DNA technology in a *Pichia pastoris* expression system. It has a molecular weight of 27.2 kDa and is provided as a sterile, preservative-free solution for intravitreal injection.

## 2. Approved Indications
Ocriplasmin is indicated for the treatment of adults with:
*   **Vitreomacular Traction (VMT):** This includes cases where VMT is associated with a macular hole with a diameter of 400 microns or less [TGA].
*   **Symptomatic Vitreomacular Adhesion (VMA):** This terminology is utilized to describe the clinical condition of tractional forces at the vitreoretinal interface [FDA].

The diagnosis should be confirmed through a comprehensive clinical assessment, including the use of optical coherence tomography (OCT).

## 3. Contraindications
Treatment with ocriplasmin is contraindicated in the following groups:
*   Patients with confirmed or suspected ocular or periocular infections.
*   Patients with a known hypersensitivity to ocriplasmin or any of the inactive ingredients (citric acid, mannitol, sodium chloride, sodium hydroxide, and hydrochloric acid).

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Decreased Visual Acuity
A significant, though often temporary, reduction in visual acuity may occur within the first week following treatment. In clinical trials, approximately 5.6% of patients experienced a decrease of 3 lines or more in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) compared to 3.2% in the control group [TGA]. While most cases resolve as the underlying traction clears or following vitrectomy, a small percentage of patients may experience long-term vision loss.

### Lens Subluxation and Phacodonesis
Because ocriplasmin is a proteolytic enzyme, there is a theoretical and observed risk of weakening the lens zonules. Cases of lens subluxation or phacodonesis have been reported. Animal studies demonstrated that doses higher than the recommended human dose resulted in 100% subluxation in some species [TGA].

### Retinal Abnormalities
*   **Retinal Breaks:** Ocriplasmin can increase tractional forces during the induction of posterior vitreous detachment (PVD), leading to retinal tears or detachment.
*   **Panretinal Disease:** Treatment is not recommended in patients with pre-existing panretinal diseases (e.g., retinitis pigmentosa) that are associated with abnormal electroretinogram (ERG) findings [TGA].
*   **Dyschromatopsia:** Approximately 2% of patients report changes in color vision, often described as "yellowish vision." This may be accompanied by abnormal ERG findings (decreased a- and b-wave amplitudes).

### Potential for Reduced Efficacy
Clinical data suggest that the efficacy of ocriplasmin is reduced in patients with a diameter of vitreomacular adhesion greater than 1,500 µm or in those with an epiretinal membrane (ERM).

## 5. Dosage and Administration
**Dose:** The recommended dose is 0.125 mg (0.1 mL) administered as a single intravitreal injection into the affected eye.

**Administration Protocols:**
*   **Single Use Only:** Each vial should be used to treat only one eye.
*   **No Repeat Dosing:** Repeated administration in the same eye is not recommended [TGA/FDA].
*   **Bilateral Treatment:** Administration to both eyes during the same procedure or within 7 days of the first injection is not recommended to allow for adequate monitoring of the post-injection course [TGA].
*   **Preparation:** The solution must be thawed (approx. 2 minutes) and inspected for particulates. Use aseptic technique to withdraw the contents and switch to an appropriate injection needle (e.g., 30-gauge).

## 6. Adverse Effects
The majority of adverse reactions are ocular, mild to moderate in severity, and typically resolve within 2 to 3 weeks.
*   **Very Common (≥10%):** Vitreous floaters, eye pain, conjunctival hemorrhage, and photopsia.
*   **Common (1% to 10%):** Blurred vision, reduced visual acuity, retinal hemorrhage, vitreous detachment, increased intraocular pressure (IOP), and macular edema.
*   **Uncommon (<1%):** Transient blindness, lens subluxation, retinal tears/detachment, night blindness, and miosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted. As ocriplasmin is a proteolytic enzyme, it should not be administered in close temporal proximity to other intravitreal medications in the same eye, as this may impact the activity of both agents [TGA]. There are no data regarding the concurrent use of ocriplasmin with VEGF inhibitors.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**Category B2 [TGA]:** There are no adequate or well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Ocriplasmin should be used during pregnancy only if the potential clinical benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Systemic exposure following a single intravitreal injection is expected to be negligible.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown whether ocriplasmin is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised when the drug is administered to nursing women, weighing the benefits of treatment against the potential risks.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
Ocriplasmin levels in the vitreous decline rapidly following administration. In clinical studies, activity was measured at only 9% of the theoretical starting concentration 2 to 4 hours post-injection and was undetectable by day 7 [TGA]. 
Once in the systemic circulation, ocriplasmin enters the endogenous protein catabolism pathway. It is rapidly inactivated by protease inhibitors, specifically α2-antiplasmin and α2-macroglobulin.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Ocriplasmin is a proteolytic enzyme with serine protease activity. It targets and dissolves the protein components of the vitreoretinal interface, specifically laminin, fibronectin, and collagen. By breaking down this protein matrix, the drug aims to resolve the abnormal vitreomacular adhesion or traction responsible for visual impairment or the formation of macular holes.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Frozen Storage:** Store in a deep freeze at -20°C ± 5°C.
*   **Light Protection:** Keep the vial in the original carton to protect from light until the time of use.
*   **After Thawing:** Unopened vials can be stored in a refrigerator (2°C to 8°C) for up to one week. Once removed from the freezer or refrigerator, the vial may be kept below 25°C for up to 8 hours but must not be refrozen [TGA].
*   **Single Use:** Discard any unused portion immediately after the procedure.
