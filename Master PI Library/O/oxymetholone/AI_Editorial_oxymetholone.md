# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for regional legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with an inborn error of metabolism which results in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
*   **General Dose:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
*   **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection or by infusion [US]. 
*   **Initial/Loading Dose:** A loading dose is often given in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
*   **Frequency:** Should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
*   **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
*   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
*   **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
*   **Titration:** Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### General Administration Notes
*   Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
*   **Monitoring:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to starting therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug and initiate appropriate medical treatment [US].
*   **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
*   **Renal Impairment (Oral formulation precaution):** While the injection is used in ESRD, note that chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% frequency) regardless of causality:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, abdominal pain, asthenia. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations. |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia. |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes. |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression. |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnea, rhinitis. |
| **Other** | Body odor (less frequent), anemia, pruritus, eye disorders. |

*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly lettered in current PI]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Studies in dairy cows indicate levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. In humans, the risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). Specific safety/efficacy profiles for other paediatric uses are not detailed beyond standard dosing [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine clears toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitines, which are rapidly excreted.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels rose from a baseline of 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
*   **Stability:** When mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
