# Editorial Product Information: Oteseconazole

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of oteseconazole for healthcare professionals, synthesising information from the provided regulatory source.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Oteseconazole (azole antifungal).
- **Brand Name:** **VIVJOA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lavender hard gelatin capsules containing 150 mg of oteseconazole [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mycovia Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Recurrent Vulvovaginal Candidiasis (RVVC):** Indicated to reduce the incidence of RVVC in females with a history of the condition who are **NOT of reproductive potential** [US]. 
- *Note:* Females not of reproductive potential are defined as biological females who are postmenopausal or have other reasons for permanent infertility (e.g., tubal ligation, hysterectomy) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration Instructions
- Administer orally with food [US].
- Capsules must be swallowed whole; do not chew, crush, dissolve, or open [US].

### Dosage Regimens [US]
There are two approved regimens. Clinicians should select one of the following:

**1. VIVJOA-only Regimen**
- **Day 1:** 600 mg (4 x 150 mg capsules) as a single dose.
- **Day 2:** 450 mg (3 x 150 mg capsules) as a single dose.
- **Day 14 onwards:** 150 mg once weekly (every 7 days) for 11 weeks (Weeks 2 through 12).

**2. Fluconazole/VIVJOA Regimen**
- **Days 1, 4, and 7:** Administer fluconazole 150 mg orally.
- **Days 14 through 20:** Administer **VIVJOA** 150 mg once daily for 7 days.
- **Day 28 onwards:** Administer **VIVJOA** 150 mg once weekly (every 7 days) for 11 weeks (Weeks 4 through 14).

### Missed Doses
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Females of reproductive potential [US].
- Pregnant and lactating women [US].
- Known hypersensitivity to oteseconazole [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Embryo-Fetal Toxicity
- **Boxed Warning:** Not present, but identified as a primary safety concern.
- **Risk:** Based on animal studies, oteseconazole may cause fetal harm, specifically ocular abnormalities (e.g., cataracts, retinal atrophy) [US].
- **Exposure Window:** Oteseconazole has a very long half-life (~138 days). The drug exposure window is approximately **690 days** (5 times the half-life), which precludes adequate mitigation of embryo-fetal risks in women of reproductive potential [US].

### General Precautions
- **Creatine Phosphokinase (CPK) Elevations:** Transient elevations in CPK (≥10x ULN) were observed in 1.9% of patients in clinical trials [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (Incidence > 2%)
| Adverse Reaction | Frequency [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| Headache (includes migraine, sinus headache) | 7.4% |
| Nausea | 3.6% |

### Other Adverse Reactions (Incidence < 2%)
- **Laboratory:** Increased blood CPK [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Dyspepsia [US].
- **Vascular:** Hot flush [US].
- **Renal/Urinary:** Dysuria [US].
- **Reproductive:** Menorrhagia (including metrorrhagia/menometrorrhagia) and vulvovaginal irritation/discomfort [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **BCRP (Breast Cancer Resistance Protein) Substrates:** Oteseconazole is a BCRP inhibitor. Concomitant use increases the exposure of BCRP substrates (e.g., **rosuvastatin**).
    - **Management:** Use the lowest possible starting dose of the BCRP substrate, consider dose reduction, and monitor for substrate-related adverse reactions [US].
- **CYP Enzymes:** No clinically significant differences in the PK of midazolam (CYP3A4 substrate), ethinyl estradiol, or norethindrone were observed when co-administered with oteseconazole [US].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** No clinically significant effect on digoxin (P-gp substrate) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Jurisdictional Classification:** Not specified by letter category in source, but explicitly **Contraindicated** [US].
- **Risk:** Potential for fetal ocular abnormalities. Human data are insufficient to exclude these risks [US].

### Breastfeeding
- **Contraindicated:** Due to potential risks to the breastfed infant and the prolonged exposure window [US].
- **Data:** There are no data on the presence of oteseconazole in human or animal milk [US].

### Fertility
- **Animal Data:** In rats, high doses were associated with abnormal sperm and reduced sperm counts (7x MRHD). Female fertility was affected only at doses causing maternal toxicity (11x MRHD) [US].

## 9. Use in Specific Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in females of reproductive potential. Safety/effectiveness not established in pre-menarchal pediatric females [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Data are insufficient to determine if patients ≥65 years respond differently from younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild-to-moderate: No dose adjustment required [US].
    - Severe or ESRD: **Not recommended** [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild (Child-Pugh A): No dose adjustment required [US].
    - Moderate-to-severe (Child-Pugh B-C): **Not recommended** [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Oteseconazole is an azole metalloenzyme inhibitor that targets fungal sterol 14α-demethylase (CYP51). This inhibition disrupts ergosterol biosynthesis, leading to the accumulation of toxic 14-methylated sterols. Oteseconazole is designed to have a lower affinity for human CYP enzymes compared to fungal CYP51 [US].

### Pharmacokinetics [US]
- **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is 5 to 10 hours. Administration with a high-fat, high-calorie meal increases $C_{max}$ and $AUC_{0-72h}$ by 45% and 36% respectively.
- **Distribution:** Central $V_d$ is ~423 L; 99.5–99.7% protein bound.
- **Metabolism:** Does not undergo significant metabolism.
- **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~138 days.
- **Excretion:** 56% in feces (biliary) and 26% in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Trials 1 & 2 (CL-011 and CL-012):** Randomized, placebo-controlled trials in patients with RVVC. Following fluconazole induction, **VIVJOA** was superior to placebo in preventing culture-verified acute VVC episodes through Week 48 (Trial 1: 6.7% vs 42.8%; Trial 2: 3.9% vs 39.4%) [US].
- **Trial 3 (CL-017):** Compared **VIVJOA** to fluconazole/placebo. **VIVJOA** was superior in preventing recurring acute VVC episodes through Week 50 (10.3% vs 42.9%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light when removed from the outer carton [US].
