# Editorial Product Information: onasemnogene abeparvovec-xioi

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): onasemnogene abeparvovec-xioi
- Chemical class: Recombinant adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) vector-based gene therapy delivering a functional survival motor neuron (SMN) transgene
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous infusion only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2019 (as stated in the source document)
- Date of last revision/update of source PI available: Revised 6/2026 (as stated in the source document)
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- If registration/approval dates for other jurisdictions are required: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated for the treatment of pediatric patients less than 2 years of age with spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) with bi-allelic mutations in the survival motor neuron 1 (SMN1) gene.
- Limitations of use:
  - Safety and effectiveness of repeat administration have not been evaluated.
  - Use in patients with advanced SMA (for example, complete paralysis of limbs or permanent ventilator dependence) has not been evaluated.
- Use in older children has been studied in post‑authorization clinical studies, but the primary approved indication presented in available sources is limited to patients <2 years of age. [Where jurisdictional approvals differ: Not specified in available sources]

### Administration instructions
- Administer as a single-dose intravenous infusion over 60 minutes.
- Do not administer as an intravenous push or bolus.
- Flush the intravenous line with saline after completion of infusion.
- Administer only when the patient is clinically stable in baseline health status (adequate hydration and nutrition, absence of infection). Postpone infusion if infection is present until the infection has resolved and the patient is clinically stable.
- Begin systemic corticosteroid therapy one day prior to infusion (see corticosteroid prophylaxis below).
- Monitor the patient during and after the infusion for infusion-related reactions; interrupt infusion and provide supportive therapy if reactions occur. Resumption of infusion may be considered based on clinical assessment.

### Corticosteroid prophylaxis and management of liver enzyme elevations
- Begin systemic corticosteroid therapy equivalent to oral prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day starting one day prior to infusion and continue for a total of 30 days.
- At the end of the 30-day period, assess liver function clinically and by laboratory tests (ALT, AST, total bilirubin, prothrombin time, INR).
  - If findings are unremarkable (normal clinical exam; total bilirubin and prothrombin time/INR within normal; ALT and AST < 2 × upper limit of normal [ULN]), taper corticosteroids gradually over the following 28 days. Do not stop corticosteroids abruptly.
  - If liver function abnormalities persist, continue systemic corticosteroids (equivalent to oral prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day) until ALT and AST are both below 2 × ULN and other assessments return to normal; then taper corticosteroids gradually over at least 28 days (longer if needed). Do not stop corticosteroids abruptly.
  - If liver function abnormalities continue to persist ≥ 2 × ULN after the initial 30‑day corticosteroid period, promptly consult a pediatric gastroenterologist or hepatologist.
  - If oral corticosteroids cannot be tolerated, consider intravenous corticosteroid administration as clinically indicated.

### Laboratory testing and monitoring to assess safety (baseline and follow-up)
- Baseline:
  - Liver function: clinical exam, AST, ALT, total bilirubin, albumin, prothrombin time, partial thromboplastin time (PTT), INR.
  - Complete blood count including hemoglobin and platelet count.
  - Creatinine.
  - Baseline testing for anti-AAV9 antibodies.
- Post-infusion monitoring:
  - Liver function (AST, ALT, total bilirubin, prothrombin time, INR): weekly for the first month after infusion and during corticosteroid treatment/taper period. If clinically stable and unremarkable at the end of taper, continue liver function monitoring every other week for an additional month. Continue monitoring liver function for at least 3 months after infusion and at other times as clinically indicated.
  - Platelet count: at least weekly for the first month; then every other week for the second and third months or until platelet counts return to baseline.
  - Monitor for clinical signs of systemic immune activation, thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA), infusion reactions, and other adverse events.
  - Consider cardiac evaluation after infusion if indicated (see cardiac monitoring below).
- Anti‑AAV9 antibody testing: perform baseline testing prior to infusion. Retesting may be performed if anti‑AAV9 antibody titers are reported as > 1:50.

### Missed dose and re-dosing
- Dosage is a single intravenous administration; safety and effectiveness of repeat dosing have not been evaluated.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning / Critical warning
- **Serious liver injury and acute liver failure:** Cases of acute liver failure with fatal outcomes have been reported. Prior to infusion, assess liver function in all patients and administer systemic corticosteroids before and after infusion. Monitor liver function for at least 3 months post-infusion and as clinically indicated. Patients with preexisting liver impairment may be at higher risk.

### General warnings and precautions
- Systemic immune response: Activation of humoral and cellular immunity can occur after infusion. Administer only to patients who are clinically stable (e.g., no active infection). Postpone infusion in the presence of infection until resolution. Increased vigilance for prevention, monitoring, and management of infection is recommended. Consider seasonal prophylaxis for influenza and RSV and ensure vaccination status is up-to-date prior to administration; where possible, adjust vaccination timing to accommodate corticosteroid administration.
- Hepatotoxicity: Immune‑mediated hepatotoxicity generally manifests as elevated ALT/AST; may be severe, prolonged, or lead to acute liver failure. Some patients have had ALT/AST > 20 × ULN and prolonged prothrombin time with symptoms (vomiting, jaundice). Prompt assessment and specialist consultation (pediatric gastroenterologist/hepatologist) are advised if serious hepatic injury is suspected.
- Thrombocytopenia: Transient decreases in platelet counts, some meeting thrombocytopenia criteria, typically occur within first two weeks after infusion.
- Thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA): Post‑marketing cases of TMA have occurred generally within the first two weeks after infusion. TMA features include thrombocytopenia, microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, and acute kidney injury; concurrent immune activation (e.g., infection, vaccination) identified in some cases. Promptly evaluate and consult pediatric hematology/nephrology if TMA is suspected.
- Elevated troponin I and potential cardiac effects: Increases in cardiac troponin I have been observed following infusion. Cardiac toxicity was observed in animal studies. Consider cardiac evaluation and consult a cardiologist as needed.
- AAV vector DNA integration and theoretical tumorigenicity risk: The vector DNA persists largely in episomal form, but random integration into the host genome has been reported. Cases of tumors in patients who received the vector have been reported post-approval; causal relationship not established. Report tumors per local reporting procedures.
- Infusion-related reactions: Hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, have occurred. Signs may include rash, urticaria, vomiting, dyspnea, respiratory symptoms, and alterations in heart rate or blood pressure. Interrupt infusion and provide supportive care as appropriate; infusion may be resumed based on clinical assessment.
- Use in patients with preexisting liver impairment or acute hepatic viral infection: These patients may be at higher risk of serious liver injury/acute liver failure; risks and benefits should be carefully considered. Patients with ALT, AST, or total bilirubin > 2 × ULN (except neonatal jaundice) were not studied in clinical trials.

### Monitoring requirements (summary)
- Baseline: anti-AAV9 antibody, liver function panel (AST/ALT, total bilirubin, albumin, PT, PTT, INR), CBC with platelet count, creatinine.
- After infusion: liver tests weekly for first month and during corticosteroid treatment/taper; then every other week for another month if clinically stable; total monitoring for at least 3 months. Platelets weekly first month, then every other week during months 2–3 or until baseline. Monitor for TMA signs (hypertension, bruising, seizures, oliguria), systemic immune activation, infusion reactions, and cardiotoxicity (consider troponin/ECG/cardiac consult).

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Concomitant corticosteroid administration:
  - Systemic corticosteroids are required prophylactically (see Dosage and Administration). Where feasible, adjust a patient’s vaccination schedule to accommodate corticosteroid administration prior to and following infusion.
- Vaccinations and immune activation:
  - Concomitant immune activation (e.g., infection, recent vaccination) may increase risk of systemic immune response or TMA. Consider timing of vaccinations relative to infusion and corticosteroid therapy.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions: Not specified in available sources beyond immune-related interactions described above.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Indication and age: Indicated for pediatric patients less than 2 years of age with bi‑allelic SMN1 mutations.
- Premature neonates: Use in premature neonates before reaching full‑term gestational age is not recommended because concomitant corticosteroid treatment may adversely affect neurological development. Delay infusion until full‑term gestational age is reached.
- Older children: Safety has been evaluated in a post‑authorization study in children aged 1.5 to 9.1 years (safety data available), but the approved indication in the available source text is limited to patients <2 years.

### Elderly use
- Not applicable; indications and clinical studies involve pediatric populations. Dose adjustments or safety data for elderly patients: Not specified in available sources.

### Renal impairment
- Dose adjustments by degree of renal impairment (eGFR/stage): Not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Patients with preexisting liver impairment may be at higher risk for acute serious liver injury or liver failure. Patients with ALT, AST, or total bilirubin > 2 × ULN (except due to neonatal jaundice) were not studied in clinical trials. Carefully weigh risks and benefits in patients with preexisting hepatic impairment.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- onasemnogene abeparvovec-xioi is a recombinant, non‑replicating AAV9 vector that delivers a functional copy of the SMN transgene to motor neuron cells to increase SMN protein expression.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Distribution/persistence: Vector DNA persists largely in episomal (non‑integrated) form, though random integration events have been reported.
- Metabolism / elimination: Not specified in available sources (detailed ADME parameters not provided).
- Immunogenicity: Administration induces humoral and cellular immune responses to AAV9 vector components and/or transgene product; baseline anti‑AAV9 antibodies should be assessed prior to infusion.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Functional increase in SMN protein with clinical benefit in SMA has been observed in clinical studies (see Clinical Trials). Detailed pharmacodynamic markers and timelines: Not specified in available sources.
