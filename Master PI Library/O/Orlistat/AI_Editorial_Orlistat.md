# Editorial Product Information: Orlistat

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Orlistat product information, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for the most up-to-date regulatory requirements. At the time of compilation, only Australian regulatory documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Orlistat, a peripherally acting anti-obesity agent.
- **Brand Names:** **ORLISTAT VIATRIS** (formerly Orlizen) [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A08AB01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 120 mg hard gelatin capsules [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Accord Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Obesity Management:** Indicated for the treatment of obese patients (BMI $\ge$ 30 kg/m²) or overweight patients (BMI $\ge$ 27 kg/m²) in the presence of associated risk factors.
- **Co-therapy Requirement:** Must be used in conjunction with a mildly hypocaloric diet [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 120 mg (one capsule) taken with each main meal.
- **Administration Timing:** Dose should be taken during the meal or up to one hour following the meal.
- **Meal Omission:** If a meal is missed or contains no fat, the dose may be omitted.
- **Dietary Considerations:** Patients should follow a nutritionally balanced, mildly hypocaloric diet where approximately 30% of calories are derived from fat. Daily intake of fat, protein, and carbohydrates should be distributed across three main meals.
- **Nutritional Supplementation:** Due to potential reductions in the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) and beta-carotene, a multivitamin supplement is recommended. This should be taken at least 2 hours before or after orlistat (e.g., at bedtime) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to orlistat or any capsule excipients.
- Chronic malabsorption syndrome.
- Cholestasis.
- Pancreatic enzyme deficiency or chronic pancreatitis.
- Major gastrointestinal surgery.
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal Events:** Common GI side effects are related to the mechanism of action. The likelihood of these events increases with a high-fat diet (> 30% calories from fat).
- **Cyclosporin Interaction:** Significant reductions in cyclosporin plasma levels have been observed. Frequent monitoring is required during co-administration.
- **Diabetes Management:** Weight loss may improve metabolic control in Type 2 diabetes, necessitating a reduction in the dose of hypoglycaemic medications (e.g., sulfonylureas).
- **Coagulation Monitoring:** Patients on oral anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin) require monitoring of INR and other coagulation parameters.
- **Rectal Bleeding:** Cases have been reported; prescribers should investigate severe or persistent symptoms.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** While no direct interaction exists, severe diarrhoea induced by orlistat may reduce the efficacy of oral contraceptives. Additional barrier methods are recommended in such cases.
- **Specific Clinical Cautions:** Use with caution in patients with active peptic ulcer disease, symptomatic cholelithiasis, postsurgical adhesions, bulimia, or laxative abuse [Australian].

Adverse reactions are primarily gastrointestinal and often transient, occurring early in treatment.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Ciclosporin:** Reduced plasma levels; co-administration is generally not recommended [Australian].
- **Amiodarone:** 25–30% reduction in systemic exposure; reinforcement of clinical and ECG monitoring is required.
- **Warfarin/Anticoagulants:** Potential for decreased Vitamin K absorption. Monitor INR closely.
- **Levothyroxine/Iodine Salts:** Rare cases of hypothyroidism or reduced control of hypothyroidism.
- **Anti-epileptics:** Potential for decreased absorption leading to convulsions; monitor for changes in seizure frequency/severity.
- **Antiretrovirals (HIV):** Potential for reduced absorption and efficacy.
- **Acarbose:** Concomitant use is not recommended due to a lack of interaction studies.
- **Fat-soluble Vitamins:** Significant reduction in absorption of supplementary vitamins A, D, E, and beta-carotene [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Animal studies showed no teratogenic effects, but human safety is not established. Contraindicated during pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Secretion into human milk is uninvestigated. Contraindicated during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impact on fertility at doses significantly higher than the human therapeutic range.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 18 years of age. Safety and efficacy are not established in children.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustments are required; however, clinical investigations in geriatric populations have not been specifically undertaken.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required. Caution is advised in patients with a history of nephrolithiasis due to the risk of hyperoxaluria.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A potent, specific, and reversible inhibitor of gastrointestinal lipases. It forms a covalent bond with the active serine site of gastric and pancreatic lipases in the GI lumen, preventing the hydrolysis of dietary fat (triglycerides) into absorbable free fatty acids and monoglycerides. Approximately 30% of dietary fat absorption is inhibited.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Effect is observed 24–48 hours after dosing. Faecal fat levels return to baseline within 48–72 hours of discontinuation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Systemic absorption is minimal (< 5 ng/mL).
    - **Distribution:** > 99% bound to plasma proteins (lipoproteins and albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily occurs within the gastrointestinal wall. Two minor metabolites (M1 and M3) account for ~42% of the total plasma concentration but are pharmacologically inconsequential.
    - **Elimination:** 97% of the dose is excreted in faeces (83% as unchanged drug). Renal excretion is < 2% [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** The **XENDOS** study (4-year) and various 1-2 year phase III trials demonstrated that Orlistat plus diet is significantly more effective for weight loss than diet alone.
- **Key Outcomes:**
    - At one year, ~68.5% of patients achieved > 5% weight loss (vs 49.1% with diet alone) [Australian].
    - Long-term treatment (4 years) resulted in sustained weight loss and a 37.3% relative reduction in the risk of developing Type 2 diabetes.
    - Improvements were noted in LDL-cholesterol, blood pressure, waist circumference, and glycaemic control [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Keep blister packs in the original carton to protect from moisture and light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** **Schedule 3** (Pharmacist Only Medicine).
- **Dosage Form Approval:** Hard gelatin capsules, 120 mg [Australian].
