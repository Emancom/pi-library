# Product Information: Octocog alfa (Recombinant Coagulation Factor VIII)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** ADVATE  
**Active Ingredient:** Octocog alfa  

Octocog alfa is a recombinant antihaemophilic factor VIII (rAHF) produced using a plasma/albumin-free method (rAHF-PFM). It is synthesized in a genetically engineered Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cell line. The manufacturing process is entirely free from human or animal-derived proteins, significantly reducing the risk of blood-borne pathogen transmission. The resulting glycoprotein is structurally and functionally similar to endogenous Factor VIII, consisting of 2,332 amino acids with a molecular mass of approximately 280 kDa.

## 2. Approved Indications
Octocog alfa is indicated for use in patients with haemophilia A (congenital Factor VIII deficiency) for:
*   **Control and prevention of bleeding episodes:** Including the management of minor, moderate, and major haemorrhagic events.
*   **Routine Prophylaxis:** To prevent or reduce the frequency of bleeding episodes.
*   **Perioperative Management:** To maintain haemostasis during and after surgical procedures.

**Note:** This product is not indicated for the treatment of von Willebrand’s disease.

## 3. Contraindications
Octocog alfa is contraindicated in patients with:
*   A known history of life-threatening hypersensitivity to octocog alfa or any of the excipients (including trehalose, histidine, and mannitol).
*   Known hypersensitivity to mouse or hamster proteins, which are present in trace amounts as a byproduct of the manufacturing process.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
Allergic-type reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported. Initial symptoms may include dizziness, rash, hives, flushing, chest tightness, and dyspnea. Patients should be advised to discontinue use immediately and seek emergency care if these symptoms occur. In pediatric patients, the smaller injection volumes (e.g., 2 mL) may lead to a faster onset of reactions during infusion; clinicians may consider using a 5 mL diluent to extend the administration timeframe and improve the window for reaction management [TGA].

### Inhibitor Formation
The development of neutralizing antibodies (inhibitors) to Factor VIII is a known complication. This is most common in previously untreated patients (PUPs) or during the first 20 exposure days. Patients should be regularly monitored for inhibitors using the modified Bethesda assay. If expected plasma Factor VIII activity levels are not attained or bleeding is not controlled with an appropriate dose, the presence of an inhibitor should be suspected.

### Cardiovascular Risks
Factor VIII replacement therapy may increase the risk of thromboembolic events, particularly in patients with existing cardiovascular risk factors. 

### Catheter-Related Complications
If octocog alfa is administered via a central venous access device (CVAD), there is a risk of catheter-related complications, including local infections and thrombosis.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Treatment should be initiated and supervised by a physician experienced in the management of haemophilia A.

### General Dosing Formula
The dosage is calculated based on the desired peak Factor VIII rise, using the following formula:
**Required Units (IU) = Body Weight (kg) x Desired Factor VIII Rise (%) x 0.5**

### Guidance for Bleeding and Surgery
*   **Minor Bleeding (e.g., early haemarthrosis):** Target 20–40% peak activity. Repeat every 12–24 hours for 1–3 days.
*   **Moderate Bleeding (e.g., muscle bleed, definite joint bleed):** Target 30–60% peak activity. Repeat every 12–24 hours for 3 or more days until healing is achieved.
*   **Major/Life-Threatening Bleeding (e.g., intracranial, GI, fractures):** Target 60–100% peak activity. Initial dose 30–50 IU/kg, followed by maintenance every 8–24 hours.
*   **Major Surgery:** Target 80–120% (pre- and post-operative). Maintenance doses should be adjusted based on Factor VIII assays and wound healing status.

### Prophylaxis
The standard regimen is 20–40 IU/kg every other day (3 to 4 times weekly). Alternatively, a pharmacokinetic (PK)-guided regimen (20–80 IU/kg every 72 hours) may be utilized to maintain trough levels ≥ 1%.

### Administration
Octocog alfa is administered via intravenous bolus at a maximum rate of 10 mL/min. It must be administered within 3 hours of reconstitution and should not be refrigerated after mixing. For continuous infusion, specific pump rates (e.g., ≥ 0.4 mL/hr for syringe pumps) and sterility protocols must be strictly followed [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most significant adverse reaction is the development of Factor VIII inhibitors. In clinical trials, inhibitors occurred in approximately 4% of all subjects, though the rate was significantly higher in PUPs (approximately 29%).

**Common Adverse Reactions (≥1%):**
*   Headache
*   Pyrexia (fever)
*   Injection site reactions
*   Dizziness

**Uncommon/Rare Reactions (<1%):**
*   Dysgeusia (taste perversion)
*   Palpitations and hot flushes
*   Pruritus and rash
*   Anaphylactic reactions (post-marketing)

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted. No interactions with other medicinal products are currently known.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**Category B2 [TGA]:** Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted with octocog alfa. It is unknown whether the drug can cause fetal harm or affect reproductive capacity. Octocog alfa should be administered to pregnant women only if clearly indicated.

### Breastfeeding
It is not known whether octocog alfa is excreted in human milk. Because many drugs are excreted in human milk, caution should be exercised, and the benefits of treatment should be weighed against the potential risks to the infant.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
Octocog alfa demonstrates an incremental recovery of approximately 2 IU/dL per IU/kg infused. The terminal half-life is age-dependent:
*   **Adults:** ~12–13 hours.
*   **Adolescents (12–16 years):** ~12 hours.
*   **Children (2–12 years):** ~10 hours.
*   **Infants (1 month to 2 years):** ~8.5 hours.

Clearance is higher in younger children, which may necessitate more frequent dosing or higher doses per kg of body weight in pediatric populations.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Octocog alfa replaces the missing clotting Factor VIII required for effective haemostasis. In the coagulation cascade, Factor VIII acts as a vital cofactor for Factor IXa. This complex converts Factor X into activated Factor X (Xa), which eventually leads to the conversion of prothrombin to thrombin and the formation of a stable fibrin clot.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
*   **Room Temperature:** If necessary, the product may be stored at or below 25°C for a single period of up to 6 months. Once moved to room temperature, the product must not be returned to the refrigerator and should be discarded if not used within 6 months or by the expiration date.
*   **Protection:** Store in the original package to protect the lyophilized powder from light.
