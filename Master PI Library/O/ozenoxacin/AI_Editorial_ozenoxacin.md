# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is intended as a clinical reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in a particular region.
- **Information Gap:** At the time of compilation, PI documents from other major jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided. Consequently, all clinical guidance herein reflects United States regulatory labeling unless otherwise specified.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism; chemically defined as (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administered every 3 or 4 hours; never less frequently than every 6 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2–3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are supra-normal.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL).

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended.
- **Target Range:** Maintain plasma free carnitine concentrations between 35 and 60 µmol/L [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** While the source document states "None known" in the dedicated section, subsequent warnings detail serious hypersensitivity reactions; clinical discretion is advised in patients with known sensitivity to levocarnitine components [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of injection. Discontinue immediately if severe reactions occur.
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, there may be an increase in seizure frequency or severity.
- **Metabolite Accumulation (Oral vs. IV):** Chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are potentially toxic. The IV formulation bypasses initial gut metabolism but monitoring remains prudent in ESRD.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Frequency Summary (ESRD Clinical Trials)
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), headache (3–37%), pharyngitis (15–27%), hypertension (18–21%), nausea/vomiting (8–21%), diarrhea (9–35%), dizziness (10–18%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, infection, abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, tachycardia, hypercalcemia, anemia. |
| **Postmarketing/Rare** | Seizures, body odor, gastritis, anaphylaxis. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and severe bronchospasm; increased seizure activity.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered, but the PI notes that reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against maternal benefits.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment in animal models.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for both inborn errors and ESRD. Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic; 10–20 mg/kg for ESRD).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in the provided source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specially indicated for ESRD on dialysis. However, safety and efficacy of the *oral* formulation have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency due to metabolite accumulation.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is essential for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases plasma carnitine levels. In ESRD patients, it restores the ratio of acylcarnitine to free carnitine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma carnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the effects on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the vial as the formulation contains no preservative.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Human Prescription Drug).
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
