# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The primary source document contributing to this editorial is the **United States (FDA)** Product Information for Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation). 
- **Note on Jurisdictions:** While this document follows a harmonised format, only the US-approved Product Information was provided for analysis. Therefore, all clinical recommendations and regulatory classifications are based on [US] standards.
- **Purpose:** This is a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with a documented inborn error of metabolism which results in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### General Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before administration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** None known at the time of publication, though hypersensitivity reactions have been reported post-marketing [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Accumulation of Metabolites:** The safety of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal impairment or ESRD has not been established; chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may result in the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% regardless of causality:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, and pain.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia, and palpitations.
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia, and dyspepsia.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), and hypervolemia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), and rhinitis (9%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia, and depression.

### Other Reported Effects
- **Transient Effects:** Nausea and vomiting have been observed.
- **Less Frequent:** Body odor and gastritis.
- **Serious Post-marketing:** Seizures, anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; however, specific supplementation in nursing mothers has not been studied. Weigh the benefits of supplementation to the mother against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg). See Section 3 for specific age-related dosing guidance where applicable [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources; generally, dose selection should be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose adjustments are required for ESRD patients on dialysis based on trough plasma concentrations. Oral levocarnitine is not recommended for chronic use in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine alleviates metabolic abnormalities by promoting the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids through acylcarnitine formation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), trough concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 24 weeks showed a dose-proportional increase in trough levocarnitine concentrations (148 ± 50, 240 ± 60, and 456 ± 162 µmol/L, respectively) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only.
