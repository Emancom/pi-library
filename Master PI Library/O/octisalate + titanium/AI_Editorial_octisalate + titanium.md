# Editorial Product Information: octisalate + titanium

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **octisalate + titanium**
  - Octisalate (ethylhexyl salicylate) 3.5% (35 mg per 1 mL)
  - Titanium dioxide 0.94% (9.4 mg per 1 mL)
- Chemical class: Not specified in available sources.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Topical (skin).

- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.
- Other registration/approval dates by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Topical sunscreen to help prevent sunburn; provides Broad Spectrum protection with SPF 15. [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
General dosing (adult)
- Apply liberally to exposed skin 15 minutes before sun exposure.
- Reapply at least every 2 hours.
- Reapply more frequently when swimming or sweating; use a water-resistant sunscreen if swimming or sweating.

Dosing by indication
- No indication-specific dose adjustments specified in available sources.

Administration instructions
- Apply liberally and evenly to all exposed skin.
- Keep out of eyes; if product enters the eyes, rinse with water to remove.
- Children under 6 months: ask a doctor before use.

Titration schedules
- Not applicable / Not specified in available sources.

Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Do not use on damaged or broken skin.
- No other contraindications are specified in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **For external use only.**
- Keep out of eyes. If product gets into eyes, rinse with water to remove.
- Stop use and ask a doctor if rash occurs.
- Keep out of reach of children. If swallowed, get medical help or contact a Poison Control Center right away.
- Use a water-resistant sunscreen if swimming or sweating.
- Spending time in the sun increases the risk of skin cancer and early skin aging. To help decrease this risk, regularly use a broad spectrum sunscreen with SPF 15 or higher and adopt other sun protection measures (limit time in the sun, especially between 10 a.m. and 2 p.m.; wear long-sleeved shirts, pants, hats, and sunglasses).
- Children under 6 months: ask a doctor before use.

### Monitoring requirements
- Specific laboratory or clinical monitoring requirements are not specified in available sources.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid application to damaged or broken skin.
- Avoid intentional ingestion; if ingestion occurs, seek medical help.
- Observe for signs of cutaneous reaction (e.g., rash); discontinue on rash and consult a clinician.
