# Originality Audit Report: orserdu

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 88/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 2
- **Overall Assessment:** Pass

## Verbatim Copied Passages
The following passages are identical or near-identical to the source text. Note that in regulatory writing, certain clinical instructions are often kept near-verbatim for safety and accuracy.

1. **Vomiting Instructions**
    - **Editorial Text:** "If vomiting occurs after taking a dose, do not take an additional dose on that day; resume the regular schedule the following day."
    - **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 4.2: "If the patient vomits after taking the ORSERDU dose, the patient should not take an additional dose on that day and should resume the usual dosing schedule the next day at the usual time."
    - **Location:** Section 3, Missed Doses and Vomiting.

2. **Chemical Name/Description**
    - **Editorial Text:** "Elacestrant (as dihydrochloride), a tetrahydronaphthalene compound."
    - **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 5.1: "Elacestrant, a tetrahydronaphthalene compound..."
    - **Location:** Section 1, Product Identification.

## Close Paraphrases
1. **Missed Dose Protocol:** The editorial's description of the 6-hour window for missed doses ("If a dose is missed by less than 6 hours... if more than 6 hours have passed...") closely mirrors the structure of TGA Section 4.2 and FDA Section 2.2, though it simplifies the language for better readability.
2. **Indications Statement:** The definition of the indication in Section 2 uses the exact sequence of modifiers found in the "Indications and Usage" sections of both PIs (ER-positive, HER2-negative, locally advanced or metastatic, etc.). This is unavoidable for regulatory compliance.
3. **Pharmacokinetic Values:** The data points for half-life (30–50 hours), fecal excretion (82%), and Tmax (1–4 hours) are direct extractions from the PK tables.

## Originality Strengths
The editorial excels in **comparative synthesis**. Rather than choosing one jurisdiction, it actively monitors and highlights discrepancies between TGA and FDA regulations, which constitutes original editorial analysis.
- **Section 3 (Dose Modifications):** The writer correctly identifies and presents the difference in the "floor" for dose reductions (TGA allows only one reduction to 258 mg; FDA allows a second reduction to 172 mg). This side-by-side comparison is not present in either source document.
- **Section 7 (Drug Interactions):** The writer synthesizes the specific TGA-suggested dose reductions (86 mg/172 mg) when inhibitors are used, which is presented as a narrative in the source but converted here into a structured overview.
- **Section 10 (Pharmacology):** The writer condenses several pages of technical PK/PD data into a concise, high-level summary using original phrasing ("inhibits estradiol-dependent and independent growth").

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Jurisdictional Tagging:** The editorial adds metadata tags (e.g., `[TGA only]`, `[FDA]`) to distinguish regional requirements, creating a "harmonised" view that does not exist in the source files.
- **ATC Integration:** The editorial includes the ATC code (L02BA04), which is present in the TGA document but not the FDA document.
- **Structure:** The editorial uses a custom hierarchical structure (Sections 1-14) that deviates from the standard 17-section FDA format and the 10-section TGA format.
- **Simplified Language:** It converts clinical trial descriptions (Section 11) from dense regulatory reports into a streamlined summary of the "Key Outcome."

## Recommendation
**PASS.**

The editorial is a genuine original synthesis. While it uses some standard clinical phrases and verbatim data points (essential for medical accuracy), it provides significant value-add through jurisdictional comparison and structural reorganisation. No revision is required.
