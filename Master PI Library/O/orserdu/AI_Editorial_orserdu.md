# Editorial Product Information: Orserdu (elacestrant)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) documents provided by the **Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA)** of Australia and the **Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** of the United States.
- **Purpose:** To provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical data and regulatory requirements from multiple jurisdictions.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Elacestrant (as dihydrochloride), a tetrahydronaphthalene compound.
- **Chemical Class:** Estrogen receptor antagonist and degrader (SERD).
- **Brand Name:** **ORSERDU**.
- **ATC Code:** L02BA04 [TGA].
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 345 mg film-coated tablets (equivalent to 400 mg elacestrant dihydrochloride).
    - 86 mg film-coated tablets (equivalent to 100 mg elacestrant dihydrochloride).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer:** 
    - **Australia:** A. Menarini Australia Pty Ltd.
    - **United States:** Stemline Therapeutics, Inc. (a Menarini Group Company).

## 2. Approved Indications
**ORSERDU** is indicated as monotherapy for the treatment of postmenopausal women and men with estrogen receptor (ER)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer. 

Clinical use is restricted to patients:
- With an activating **ESR1** mutation (detected via an FDA-authorized or adequately validated companion diagnostic test).
- Whose disease has progressed following at least one line of endocrine therapy, including a CDK 4/6 inhibitor.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Standard Dose:** 345 mg (one 345 mg tablet) taken once daily.
- **Administration:** Tablets must be swallowed whole (not chewed, crushed, or split). They should be taken with a light meal at approximately the same time each day to reduce gastrointestinal side effects.

### Missed Doses and Vomiting
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed by less than 6 hours, it should be taken immediately. If more than 6 hours have passed, skip the dose and resume the next day at the usual time.
- **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after taking a dose, do not take an additional dose on that day; resume the regular schedule the following day.

### Dose Modifications for Adverse Reactions
There is a significant jurisdictional difference regarding the extent of allowable dose reductions:
- **First reduction:** 258 mg once daily (three 86 mg tablets).
- **Second reduction:** 172 mg once daily (two 86 mg tablets) **[FDA only]**.
- **Discontinuation:** 
    - **[TGA]:** Permanently discontinue if further reduction below 258 mg is required.
    - **[FDA]:** Permanently discontinue if further reduction below 172 mg is required.

### Modification by Severity (CTCAE Grade)
- **Grade 1 or 2:** Generally maintain or temporarily interrupt until recovery to $\le$ Grade 1.
- **Grade 3 or 4:** Interrupt until recovery to $\le$ Grade 1, then resume at a reduced dose level. Permanently discontinue if Grade 3 or 4 toxicities recur despite dose reduction.

## 4. Contraindications
- **[TGA]:** Hypersensitivity to elacestrant dihydrochloride or any of the product excipients.
- **[FDA]:** None listed in the official PI highlights.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Dyslipidemia:** Hypercholesterolemia (30%) and hypertriglyceridemia (27%) are common. Lipid profiles should be monitored at baseline and periodically during treatment.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Based on animal data, **ORSERDU** can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before initiation. Advise patients of reproductive potential to use effective contraception (females and males with female partners) during treatment and for **one week** after the final dose.
- **Thromboembolic Events:** Commonly observed in advanced breast cancer patients; clinicians should consider this risk when prescribing to patients with pre-existing risk factors [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Safety data is primarily based on the **EMERALD** trial (N=237 for elacestrant).

### Frequency Table (Common Reactions)
| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction | Frequency (All Grades) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea (35%), Vomiting (19%), Diarrhoea (13%), Constipation (12%), Abdominal pain (11%), Dyspepsia (10%) | Very Common |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Musculoskeletal pain (41%), Arthralgia/Back pain | Very Common |
| **Metabolism** | Decreased appetite (15%), Dyslipidemia (Hypercholesterolemia/Hypertriglyceridemia) | Very Common |
| **General** | Fatigue (26%), Asthenia | Very Common |
| **Vascular** | Hot flush (11%) | Very Common |
| **Nervous System** | Headache (12%) | Very Common |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Serious reactions occurred in 12% of patients. 
- Most frequent serious reactions (>1%): Musculoskeletal pain and nausea.
- Fatalities (1.7%) in trials included cardiac arrest, septic shock, and diverticulitis (one patient each) [FDA].
- **Laboratory abnormalities:** Decreased haemoglobin, increased AST/ALT, increased creatinine, and decreased sodium were very common (>10%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Impact of Other Drugs on Elacestrant
- **Strong and Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Increase elacestrant exposure (Strong: ~5.3-fold; Moderate: ~2.3-fold). Avoid concomitant use.
    - **[TGA specific guidance]:** If a strong inhibitor must be used, reduce elacestrant to 86 mg daily. If a moderate inhibitor must be used, reduce to 172 mg daily.
- **Strong and Moderate CYP3A4 Inducers:** Decrease elacestrant exposure (Strong: ~86% reduction; Moderate: ~55–73% reduction). Avoid concomitant use.
- **OATP2B1 Inhibitors:** Caution is recommended as elacestrant is a substrate of OATP2B1.

### Impact of Elacestrant on Other Drugs
- **P-gp Substrates:** Elacestrant increases exposure (e.g., digoxin Cmax increased by 27%). Monitor and consider dose reductions of P-gp substrates.
- **BCRP Substrates:** Elacestrant increases exposure (e.g., rosuvastatin Cmax increased by 45%). Monitor and consider dose reductions of BCRP substrates.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **[TGA]:** Category D.
    - **[FDA]:** Advise of potential fetal risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if elacestrant is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for **one week** after the last dose.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential impairment of both female and male fertility (atrophy of reproductive organs and decreased cellularity of Leydig cells/seminiferous epithelium).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients aged 0 to 18 years.
- **Elderly:** No dose adjustment is required based on age. Patients $\ge$ 75 years may experience a higher frequency of gastrointestinal disorders [TGA].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild/Moderate:** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe:** No dose recommendation can be made due to lack of data [TGA].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Child-Pugh A (Mild):** No adjustment required.
    - **Child-Pugh B (Moderate):** Reduce dose to 258 mg once daily.
    - **Child-Pugh C (Severe):** Avoid use [FDA] / Not recommended [TGA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Elacestrant binds to estrogen receptor-alpha (ER$\alpha$) as an antagonist and induces its degradation through the proteasomal pathway. It inhibits estradiol-dependent and independent growth, including in models with **ESR1** mutations.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 10% bioavailability; Tmax is 1–4 hours.
    - **Food Effect:** High-fat meals increase Cmax by 42% and AUC by 22%.
    - **Distribution:** >99% plasma protein bound; penetrates the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily CYP3A4; minor contributions from CYP2A6 and CYP2C9.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life of 30–50 hours. Excretion is primarily faecal (82%), with 7.5% in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
The pivotal **EMERALD** trial (NCT03778931) was a randomised, open-label, active-controlled trial comparing elacestrant to standard of care (SOC: fulvestrant or an aromatase inhibitor). 
- **Key Outcome (ESR1 Mutation Subgroup):**
    - Median Progression-Free Survival (PFS): 3.8 months vs. 1.9 months (HR 0.55; 95% CI: 0.39, 0.77; p=0.0005).
- Overall survival (OS) did not reach statistical significance at the time of the primary analysis.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **[TGA]:** Store below 30°C.
- **[FDA]:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine). Subject to additional monitoring ($\blacktriangledown$).
- **United States:** Rx-only.
