# Editorial Product Information: Oxacillin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: United States (Food and Drug Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Oxacillin sodium monohydrate (a semisynthetic, penicillinase-resistant penicillin).
- **Brand Names:** Oxacillin for Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01CF04 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for oxacillin).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Powder for solution for injection: 1 gram and 2 grams per vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) and Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Onesource Specialty Pharma Limited [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Staphylococcal Infections:** Treatment of infections caused by penicillinase-producing staphylococci that have demonstrated susceptibility to the drug [US].
- **Empiric Therapy:** May be used to initiate therapy in suspected cases of resistant staphylococcal infections pending susceptibility results [US].
- **Note on Limitations:** Should not be used for infections caused by organisms susceptible to penicillin G. To prevent drug-resistant bacteria, usage should be restricted to proven or strongly suspected bacterial infections [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Mild-to-moderate infections:** 250 mg to 500 mg IM or IV every 4 to 6 hours [US].
- **Severe infections:** 1 gram IM or IV every 4 to 6 hours [US].

### Dosing by Population
- **Infants and Children (< 40 kg):** 
    - Mild-to-moderate: 50 mg/kg/day in equally divided doses every 6 hours [US].
    - Severe: 100 mg/kg/day in equally divided doses every 4 to 6 hours [US].
- **Premature Infants and Neonates:** 25 mg/kg/day IM or IV [US].

### Administration and Duration
- **Duration:** At least 14 days for severe staphylococcal infections. Therapy should continue for at least 48 hours after the patient is afebrile and asymptomatic with negative cultures. Osteomyelitis and endocarditis may require longer courses [US].
- **Intravenous Injection:** Reconstitute with Sterile Water for Injection or Sodium Chloride Injection. Administer slowly over approximately 10 minutes [US].
- **Intramuscular Injection:** Reconstitute with Sterile Water for Injection to a concentration of 250 mg/1.5 mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Penicillin Hypersensitivity:** A history of hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reaction to any penicillin is a strict contraindication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal anaphylactic shock has been reported (incidence 0.015% to 0.04%). Requires immediate emergency treatment if it occurs [US].
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents, ranging from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis. May occur over two months after finishing treatment [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Perform periodic assessment of renal, hepatic, and hematopoietic function during prolonged therapy [US].
    - Obtain blood cultures, WBC, and differential counts prior to initiation and at least weekly during therapy [US].
    - Monitor AST (SGOT) and ALT (SGPT) periodically for liver function abnormalities [US].
    - Perform periodic urinalysis, BUN, and creatinine determinations [US].
- **Neurotoxicity:** Large IV doses may cause neurotoxic reactions similar to penicillin G, particularly in patients with renal insufficiency [US].
- **Fungal/Bacterial Overgrowth:** Prolonged use may result in overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity (0.7% to 10%):** Urticaria, pruritus, angioneurotic edema, laryngospasm, bronchospasm, and serum sickness-like reactions (fever, malaise, myalgia) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, stomatitis, black/hairy tongue, and pseudomembranous colitis [US].
- **Renal:** Renal tubular damage and interstitial nephritis (rash, fever, eosinophilia, hematuria, proteinuria). Usually reversible upon discontinuation [US].
- **Hematologic:** Agranulocytosis, neutropenia, and bone marrow depression [US].
- **Hepatic:** Hepatotoxicity (fever, nausea, vomiting, elevated SGOT/AST levels) [US].
- **Local Reactions:** Thrombophlebitis may occur with IV administration, particularly in elderly patients [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Tetracyclines:** These bacteriostatic antibiotics may antagonize the bactericidal effect of penicillin; concurrent use should be avoided [US].
- **Probenecid:** Blocks renal tubular secretion of penicillins, increasing and prolonging oxacillin serum concentrations. This combination should be limited to infections requiring very high serum levels [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category B:** Reproduction studies in mice, rats, and rabbits showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women exist; use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Penicillins are excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to a nursing woman [US].
- **Fertility:** No long-term animal studies have been conducted on carcinogenesis or mutagenesis [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Incompletely developed renal function may lead to abnormally high blood levels. Frequent blood level monitoring and dosage adjustments are advisable [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Studies did not identify different responses compared to younger patients, but dose selection should be cautious (usually starting at the low end). Monitor renal function as the drug is substantially excreted by the kidney [US].
- **Sodium Content:** Contains 57.4 mg (2.5 mEq) of sodium per gram of oxacillin. This may be clinically significant for patients with congestive heart failure or those requiring salt restriction [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Consider reduction in total dosage if renal impairment is suspected or known to exist to avoid neurotoxic reactions [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall biosynthesis during the stage of active multiplication. It is resistant to inactivation by staphylococcal penicillinase [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak serum levels occur ~5 minutes after IV and ~30 minutes after IM injection [US].
    - **Distribution:** 94.2% protein-bound (mainly albumin). Found in therapeutic levels in pleural, bile, and amniotic fluids. Insignificant levels in CSF and ascitic fluid under normal conditions [US].
    - **Elimination:** Rapidly excreted unchanged in urine via glomerular filtration and active tubular secretion. Elimination half-life is approximately 0.5 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Formal clinical trial summaries for this generic penicillin are not provided in the primary source document [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Dry Powder:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Reconstituted Stability (IM):** Stable for 3 days at room temperature (70°F) or 7 days under refrigeration (40°F) [US].
- **IV Stability:** Varies by concentration and diluent. Generally, concentrations of 10–100 mg/mL are stable for 4 days at room temperature and 7 days refrigerated in Sterile Water or 0.9% NaCl [US].
