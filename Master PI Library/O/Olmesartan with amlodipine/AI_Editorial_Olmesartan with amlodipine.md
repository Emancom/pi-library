# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)** for Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation). 
- **Note:** Only one jurisdictional document (US) was available for this synthesis. Consequently, regional comparisons are not available for this specific entry.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP), a carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Clear, sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Errors:** Acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and management of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and must not be less frequent than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though adjustments may be made based on clinical response. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Therapy initiation may be based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels (<40 to 50 µmol/L). Adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) can be made as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- There are no known contraindications listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These may occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon a severe reaction [US].
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with established seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before and during (weekly/monthly) treatment. Monitoring should include vital signs, blood chemistry, and clinical status [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event frequency is derived from clinical trials in ESRD patients on hemodialysis.

| System Organ Class | Common (1–10%) | Very Common (>10%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General/Injection Site** | Fever, flu syndrome, allergic reaction, asthenia | Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), headache, chest pain, abdominal pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, palpitations, tachycardia, arrhythmia | Hypotension (up to 19%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, anorexia | Diarrhea (up to 35%) |
| **Respiratory** | Cough, rhinitis, dyspnea | Pharyngitis (up to 27%) |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia | — |

- **Other Noted Effects:** Gastritis and a transient "body odor" have been reported.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and increased seizure activity [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility. Use during pregnancy only if clearly required [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if levocarnitine is excreted in human milk; however, studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); safety and efficacy follow adult guidelines [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** While the IV form is used in ESRD, high doses of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for lipid metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean trough carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed a dose-proportional increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine serum concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population remains undetermined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
