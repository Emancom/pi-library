# Editorial Product Information: Ofloxacin (Ophthalmic)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ofloxacin
- **Chemical Class:** Third-generation fluorinated 4-quinolone; fluoroquinolone antibacterial.
- **Brand Names:** OCUFLOX [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Ophthalmic solution (eye drops) containing 3 mg/mL of ofloxacin.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical Ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AbbVie Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Corneal Ulcers (Bacterial Keratitis):** Treatment of ulcers caused by ofloxacin-sensitive organisms in adults [Australian].
- **Severe Bacterial Conjunctivitis:** Treatment of severe conjunctivitis caused by ofloxacin-sensitive organisms in adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
To minimise systemic absorption, patients should be instructed to apply pressure to the tear duct immediately following administration.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Corneal Ulcers (Bacterial Keratitis):**
  - **Days 1 and 2:** Instill 1–2 drops into the affected eye(s) every 30 minutes while awake. Additional doses (1–2 drops) should be instilled 4 hours and 6 hours after retiring.
  - **Days 3 to 7:** Instill 1–2 drops every hour while awake.
  - **Day 7 to Completion:** Instill 1–2 drops four times daily until the ulcer is completely healed (typically within 21 days) [Australian].
- **Bacterial Conjunctivitis:**
  - One drop every four hours for the first two days.
  - One drop every six hours for the subsequent six days (up to 8 days total).
  - Treatment should not exceed 10 days without ophthalmic review [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Avoid contacting the dispensing tip with the eye or surrounding structures to prevent injury and contamination.
- **Contact Lenses:** The preservative (benzalkonium chloride) may discolour soft contact lenses. Lenses must be removed prior to instillation; patients should wait at least 15 minutes before reinsertion [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to ofloxacin or any excipients in the formulation.
- History of hypersensitivity to other quinolone anti-infectives (e.g., nalidixic acid) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided sources.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious and occasionally fatal anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions have occurred with systemic quinolones, sometimes after the first dose. If an allergic reaction occurs, discontinue use immediately. Rare reports of Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis have been associated with topical ofloxacin [Australian].
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi.
- **Corneal Effects:** Corneal precipitates and perforations have been reported in patients with pre-existing corneal epithelial defects/ulcers, though a causal relationship is not established [Australian].
- **Phototoxicity:** Systemic quinolones may induce phototoxicity. Patients should avoid direct exposure to sunlight or UV light; therapy should be ceased if photosensitivity occurs [Australian].
- **Not for Injection:** This product is for topical ophthalmic use only and must not be injected [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency where data is available:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>10%)** | Burning or stinging (10-14%), tearing (6-11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Eye pain (1-5%), itching, foreign body sensation, photophobia, blurred vision, hyperemia |
| **Uncommon/Rare** | Conjunctivitis, chemical keratitis, periocular/facial oedema, dry eyes, dizziness, nausea |

**Post-marketing Experience (Frequency Unknown):**
- **Immune System:** Anaphylactic reaction/shock, angioedema (including laryngeal/pharyngeal/tongue swelling), allergic dermatitis.
- **Ocular:** Lacrimation increased, eyelid oedema.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Caffeine and Theophylline:** While systemic quinolones can inhibit the clearance of these substances, studies with systemic ofloxacin show no significant effect on their metabolic clearance [Australian].
- **NSAIDs:** Increased prevalence of CNS toxicity has been reported with the combination of systemic fluoroquinolones and systemic NSAIDs; however, this has not been reported specifically with ofloxacin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies at high oral doses (810 mg/kg/day) showed decreased fetal weight and increased mortality. Because systemic quinolones cause arthropathy in immature animals, use is not recommended during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Systemic ofloxacin is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for harm to nursing infants, a decision should be made to either temporarily discontinue breastfeeding or withhold the drug, considering the mother’s clinical need [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety has not been established in children. Use should be avoided in children who have not attained joint maturity, as oral quinolones have caused arthropathy and cartilage lesions in immature animals [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No comparative data available for topical dosing in the elderly versus other age groups [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources for topical ophthalmic administration.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A bactericidal agent that inhibits bacterial DNA gyrase, the enzyme required for DNA replication, transcription, and repair. Ofloxacin also possesses a secondary bactericidal mechanism independent of protein or RNA synthesis, making it active in both replicating and non-replicating stages [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Following topical dosing, tear film concentrations range from 5.67 to 31.0 µg/g. Mean serum concentrations (0.4 to 1.9 ng/mL) are more than 1000 times lower than those seen after standard oral doses.
  - **Distribution:** Effective concentrations in tears are maintained for 4 to 6 hours.
  - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in the urine in unmodified form [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Corneal Ulcer Study:** In a randomised, double-blind trial (n=134) of patients with positive bacterial cultures, ofloxacin eye drops achieved an 86% clinical success rate. The median time to success was 11 days (range 8–14 days) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Protect from light and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** 2 years. Discard contents 4 weeks after opening the bottle [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
