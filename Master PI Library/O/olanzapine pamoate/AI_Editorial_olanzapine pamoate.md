# Editorial Product Information: olanzapine pamoate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **olanzapine pamoate**
- Chemical class: Long-acting injectable atypical antipsychotic (pamoate salt)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular (deep gluteal) injection only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Revision date shown in available source: May 2026 [US]. Other jurisdiction revision dates: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: A restricted distribution/safety program is described in the available sources for the United States. No other withdrawal/suspension history specified.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of schizophrenia (efficacy established in adults in clinical trials).
- Use in other indications: Not specified in available sources.
- Jurisdictional note:
  - [US] Approval and prescribing information describe use for schizophrenia.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General adult dosing (recommended ranges and equivalents)
- Efficacy demonstrated with doses corresponding to approximately:
  - 150 mg every 2 weeks, 210 mg every 2 weeks, 300 mg every 2 weeks, 300 mg every 4 weeks, or 405 mg every 4 weeks depending on prior oral olanzapine dose and clinical judgment.
- Doses greater than 405 mg every 4 weeks or 300 mg every 2 weeks have not been evaluated in clinical trials.

### Recommended initiation and maintenance (correspondence to oral olanzapine)
- Establish tolerability with oral olanzapine prior to initiating the long‑acting injectable formulation.
- Maintenance dosing regimens demonstrated in trials include options equivalent to common oral dosages; examples used in clinical studies:
  - For patients previously stabilized on oral olanzapine 10 mg/day: options during initiation included 210 mg every 2 weeks or 150 mg every 2 weeks or 405 mg every 4 weeks; maintenance dosing after 8 weeks commonly 300 mg every 4 weeks.
  - For patients previously stabilized on oral olanzapine 15 mg/day: initiation example 300 mg every 2 weeks; maintenance options included 210 mg every 2 weeks or 405 mg every 4 weeks.
  - For patients previously stabilized on oral olanzapine 20 mg/day: initiation and maintenance example 300 mg every 2 weeks.
- For debilitated patients, those with predisposition to hypotension, or patients likely to have slower olanzapine metabolism (e.g., nonsmoking females ≥65 years), a conservative starting dose is recommended (example: 150 mg every 4 weeks) with cautious escalation.

### Administration instructions (preparation and injection)
- Indication: deep intramuscular injection into the gluteal muscle only.
- Do not administer intravenously or subcutaneously.
- Needle: a 19‑gauge, 1.5‑inch (38 mm) needle is recommended for typical adults; for obese patients a 2‑inch (50 mm) 19‑gauge or larger needle may be used.
- Aspiration: after needle insertion into muscle, aspirate for several seconds; if blood is aspirated, discard syringe and dose and prepare a new dose.
- Injection technique: inject with steady, continuous pressure; do not massage the injection site after administration.
- Observe the patient after each injection per local safety program requirements (see Warnings and Precautions).

### Reconstitution (clinical convenience kit instructions summarized)
- Suspension concentration after reconstitution: 150 mg/mL.
- Vial strengths (single-dose): 210 mg, 300 mg, 405 mg (corresponding to final concentrations when reconstituted as specified).
- Diluent volumes to add (to powder vial) for reconstitution:
  - For 210 mg vial: add 1.3 mL diluent
  - For 300 mg vial: add 1.8 mL diluent
  - For 405 mg vial: add 2.3 mL diluent
- Final volumes to withdraw and inject (after reconstitution):
  - 150 mg dose → inject 1.0 mL
  - 210 mg dose → inject 1.4 mL
  - 300 mg dose → inject 2.0 mL
  - 405 mg dose → inject 2.7 mL
- Reconstitution procedure highlights:
  - Use only the supplied diluent.
  - Loosen powder by tapping; inject diluent into vial; vigorously shake until a smooth, yellow opaque suspension is obtained; if foam forms, allow to dissipate.
  - Reconstituted product may remain stable up to 24 hours at room temperature in the vial; if not used immediately, re‑shake before use.
  - Withdraw required volume using a new syringe and attach a new safety needle for injection. Some excess will remain in vial.
  - Use gloves when reconstituting; flush exposed skin with water if contact occurs.
  - Dispose of vials and needles appropriately; vial is single‑dose only.

### Observation, accompaniment, and activity restrictions
- [US] After each injection, the patient must be observed continuously in a certified healthcare setting with ready access to emergency response services for at least 3 hours for signs of post‑injection delirium/sedation syndrome (see Warnings). Before release, confirm the patient is alert, oriented, and without relevant signs or symptoms. All patients must be accompanied by another individual upon leaving the healthcare setting and should avoid driving or operating heavy machinery for the remainder of the day.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed/major warnings:
  - **Post‑injection delirium/sedation syndrome (PIDS):** Reports of severe sedation (including coma) and/or delirium have occurred following injections. A risk mitigation program and prolonged post‑injection observation are described in the United States. [US]
  - **Increased mortality in elderly patients with dementia‑related psychosis:** Treatment with antipsychotics is associated with increased risk of death in this population; olanzapine pamoate is not approved for dementia‑related psychosis.

- General warnings and precautions (clinical points):
  - Post‑injection delirium/sedation syndrome (PIDS): events consistent with olanzapine overdose have been reported after injections, including cognitive impairment, reduced consciousness, extrapyramidal signs, dysarthria, ataxia, aggressiveness, or convulsions. Onset is most likely within the first hour but can occur after 3 hours. Most affected patients recovered by 72 hours. The risk per injection is low but cumulative per patient.
  - Psychiatric risks: The possibility of suicide inherent in schizophrenia requires monitoring of high‑risk patients.
  - Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS): may occur; manage with immediate discontinuation and close monitoring.
  - Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS): discontinue if suspected.
  - Metabolic changes: hyperglycemia, dyslipidemia, and clinically significant weight gain have been associated with treatment; hyperglycemia can be severe (including ketoacidosis, hyperosmolar coma, death).
  - Tardive dyskinesia: may be irreversible; discontinue if clinically appropriate.
  - Orthostatic hypotension and related syncope: especially during initial titration; use caution in patients with cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease.
  - Falls: may be associated with somnolence, postural instability, syncope, or orthostatic hypotension.
  - Hematologic effects: leukopenia, neutropenia, and agranulocytosis have been reported; monitor CBC in patients with prior history and consider discontinuation at first sign of clinically significant WBC decline absent other causes.
  - Seizures: use cautiously in patients with history of seizures or conditions that lower seizure threshold.
  - Anticholinergic effects: use with caution with other anticholinergic agents and in patients susceptible to urinary retention, prostatic hypertrophy, severe constipation, paralytic ileus.
  - Cognitive and motor impairment: may impair judgment, thinking, and motor skills; advise patients accordingly.
  - Hyperprolactinemia: treatment may elevate prolactin levels.
  - Dysphagia and aspiration risk: exercise caution.
  - Body temperature regulation: disturbances may occur; use caution in circumstances that might predispose to hyper-/hypothermia.

- Monitoring requirements:
  - Baseline and periodic fasting blood glucose measurement.
  - Baseline and periodic fasting lipid profile measurement.
  - Regular weight monitoring.
  - CBC monitoring for patients with prior history of low WBC/drug‑induced leukopenia or neutropenia and as clinically indicated.
  - Observe for signs/symptoms of PIDS during mandated post‑injection observation period where applicable.

  - Common: 1–10%
  - Uncommon: 0.1–1%
  - Rare: <0.1%
  - Very rare: <0.01%

### Summary of adverse reactions with frequency where available
- Most common adverse reactions (reported as ≥5% in at least one treatment group and greater than placebo) — categorized as Common (1–10%):
  - Headache
  - Sedation
  - Weight gain
  - Cough
  - Diarrhea
  - Back pain
  - Nausea
  - Somnolence
  - Dry mouth
  - Nasopharyngitis
  - Increased appetite
  - Vomiting

- Post‑Injection Delirium/Sedation Syndrome (PIDS):
  - Reported frequency in clinical development:
    - Per injection: occurred in <0.1% of injections (Rare)

  - Clinical features include varying degrees of sedation (up to coma), delirium, confusion, disorientation, slurred speech, ataxia, extrapyramidal symptoms, dysarthria, weakness, hypertension, convulsions.

- Serious adverse events reported (frequency not specified in available sources unless stated above):
  - Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS)
  - Tardive dyskinesia
  - Leukopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis
  - Seizures
  - Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS)
  - Orthostatic hypotension, syncope, falls
  - Hyperglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperosmolar coma
  - Dysphagia and aspiration
  - Increased mortality in elderly patients with dementia‑related psychosis (see Warnings)

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacodynamic and pharmacokinetic interactions reported in available sources include:

  - CNS depressants and alcohol:
    - Concomitant use may potentiate sedation and orthostatic hypotension; exercise caution and consider dose adjustments or monitoring.

  - Antihypertensive agents:
    - Olanzapine pamoate may enhance the antihypertensive effect of these agents; monitor blood pressure and adjust therapy as needed.

  - Levodopa and dopamine agonists:
    - Antipsychotics may antagonize therapeutic effects of levodopa/dopamine agonists; monitor and manage dopaminergic therapy accordingly.

  - Benzodiazepines (example: diazepam):
    - Concomitant use may potentiate orthostatic hypotension and sedation.

  - Carbamazepine:
    - Inducer that increases clearance of olanzapine; may reduce olanzapine exposure and efficacy; consider alternative anticonvulsant or adjust dosing with clinical monitoring.

  - Fluvoxamine:
    - Inhibitor that may increase olanzapine concentrations; monitor for increased adverse effects and consider dose reduction when coadministered.

- Management notes:
  - Use caution when combining olanzapine pamoate with other centrally acting drugs, alcohol, or medications that affect blood pressure.
  - Monitor for additive sedation and orthostatic effects.
  - Adjust concomitant medication dosing based on clinical response and tolerability where necessary.
  - Specific interaction magnitude and recommendations for dose adjustment are not specified in available sources beyond the agents listed.

- Jurisdictional notes:
  - Interaction cautions above are presented as general clinical guidance; no additional region‑specific interaction differences are specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Safety and effectiveness in children and adolescents (<18 years) have not been established. Use is not recommended in these age groups unless supported by specific local approvals or data (not provided in available sources).

### Elderly use
- Increased mortality observed in elderly patients with dementia‑related psychosis treated with antipsychotics; olanzapine pamoate is not approved for this indication.
- Caution in elderly patients generally, particularly those who are debilitated or have conditions predisposing to hypotension; consider lower starting dose (e.g., 150 mg every 4 weeks) and slow titration.

### Renal impairment
- Use in patients with renal impairment has not been studied; specific dosing adjustments by level of renal dysfunction are not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Use in hepatic impairment has not been studied; specific dosing adjustments by severity of hepatic dysfunction are not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- Described as a long‑acting atypical antipsychotic. The detailed molecular mechanism (receptor binding profile and primary mechanism) is not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacokinetics
- The formulation is an injectable pamoate salt intended to provide extended release of olanzapine. Rapid, unintentional increases in serum olanzapine concentration have been observed in some cases associated with PIDS; the precise mechanism for these rapid increases is not known.
- Detailed absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination parameters (e.g., Tmax, half‑life, metabolic pathways) are not specified in the available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Efficacy in schizophrenia was established in two pivotal clinical trials in adults:
  - One 8‑week randomized trial in adults with schizophrenia.
  - One maintenance trial demonstrating efficacy over periods including up to 24 weeks in stabilized patients.
- Safety findings of note from clinical development:
  - Post‑injection delirium/sedation syndrome events occurred in <0.1% of injections and in approximately 2% of patients who received injections for up to 46 months; most cases required hospitalization and some required supportive care including intubation; most patients largely recovered by 72 hours.
  - Analyses of placebo‑controlled trials in elderly patients with dementia‑related psychosis (across atypical antipsychotics) indicated increased mortality in drug‑treated patients compared to placebo over trials with modal duration of 10 weeks; causes of death were varied, often cardiovascular or infectious.

- Trial design and primary outcome measures:
