# Editorial Product Information: Ocrelizumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating the most current regulatory and clinical data available from the source.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information or Summary of Product Characteristics used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ocrelizumab (recombinant humanised anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody; IgG1 subtype).
- **Brand Name:** **Ocrevus** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AG08.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Intravenous (IV) Formulation:** Concentrate for solution for infusion; 300 mg/10 mL (30 mg/mL) in a single-use vial [Australian].
    - **Subcutaneous (SC) Formulation:** Solution for injection; 920 mg/23 mL (40 mg/mL) in a single-use vial. Contains the excipient **vorhyaluronidase alfa** to facilitate absorption [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous infusion or Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsing Forms of Multiple Sclerosis (RMS):** Indicated for the treatment of patients with RMS to delay the progression of physical disability and reduce relapse frequency [Australian-approved].
- **Primary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis (PPMS):** Indicated for the treatment of patients with PPMS to delay the progression of physical disability [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment may be initiated with either the IV or SC formulation. Patients currently on IV therapy may switch to the SC formulation [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication (RMS and PPMS)
- **Intravenous (IV) Dosing:**
    - **Initial Dose:** 600 mg administered as two separate 300 mg infusions; the first at Day 1 and the second two weeks later.
    - **Subsequent Doses:** A single 600 mg infusion every 6 months. A minimum interval of 5 months must be maintained between doses.
- **Subcutaneous (SC) Dosing:**
    - **Initial and Subsequent Doses:** 920 mg administered as a single injection every 6 months. No dose division is required for the first dose. A minimum interval of 5 months must be maintained between doses.

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Administration:** Must be diluted in 0.9% sodium chloride to a concentration of approximately 1.2 mg/mL. Administer via a dedicated line with a 0.2 or 0.22 micron in-line filter. Do not administer as a bolus or push.
- **SC Administration:** Administer 23 mL (920 mg) into the abdomen over approximately 10 minutes. Use of a winged/butterfly infusion set is recommended. Do not inject into areas where skin is red, bruised, tender, or scarred.
- **Premedication:** 
    - **IV:** 100 mg IV methylprednisolone (or equivalent) 30 minutes prior to infusion, plus an antihistamine. An antipyretic may also be considered.
    - **SC:** 20 mg oral dexamethasone (or equivalent) and an antihistamine prior to injection.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a dose is missed, it should be administered as soon as possible. Do not wait until the next planned dose. Maintain the 6-month interval (minimum 5 months) between subsequent doses [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **ocrelizumab** or any excipients (including vorhyaluronidase alfa for the SC formulation) [Australian].
- Current active infection.
- Severely immunocompromised state.
- Known active malignancies [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs) and Injection Reactions (IRs):** Very common. Symptoms include pruritus, rash, urticaria, erythema, throat irritation, dyspnoea, and hypotension. Patients must be monitored for at least one hour post-administration.
- **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML):** JC virus infection resulting in PML has been observed rarely in patients treated with anti-CD20 antibodies. Vigilance for new or worsening neurological symptoms is required.
- **Hepatitis B Reactivation:** Screening for HBV is required before treatment initiation. Active HBV is a contraindication.
- **Infections:** Serious, sometimes fatal, infections (bacterial, fungal, viral) have occurred. Treatment should be delayed in patients with active infections.
- **Immune-mediated Colitis:** Post-marketing reports of severe, acute-onset colitis. Monitor for persistent diarrhoea or gastrointestinal symptoms.
- **Liver Injury:** Clinically significant liver injury has been reported. Monitor hepatic enzymes and bilirubin before and during treatment. Discontinue if liver injury is confirmed.
- **Late Neutropenia:** May occur at least 4 weeks after the latest dose.
- **Malignancy:** A potential increased risk of malignancy, including breast cancer, exists. Patients should follow standard cancer screening guidelines.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data from Clinical Trials (IV Formulation)
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Infusion-related reactions (34-40%), Upper respiratory tract infection, Nasopharyngitis, Influenza. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Sinusitis, Bronchitis, Gastroenteritis, Oral herpes, Herpes zoster, Conjunctivitis, Cellulitis, Cough, Catarrh. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Serious infections (respiratory, skin, and opportunistic).
- Severe IRRs/IRs, including anaphylaxis.
- Immune-mediated colitis [Australian].
- Clinically significant liver injury [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal studies performed; interactions via CYP enzymes or transporters are not expected [Australian].
- **Vaccinations:** The safety of live or live-attenuated vaccines has not been studied; they are not recommended during treatment or until B-cell repletion. All other vaccinations should be completed at least 6 weeks prior to starting **ocrelizumab**.
- **Immunosuppressants:** Co-administration with other MS disease-modifying therapies is not recommended. Concomitant corticosteroids for relapse treatment are acceptable.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Ocrelizumab is an IgG1 monoclonal antibody known to cross the placental barrier. Potential B-cell depletion in the foetus may occur. Avoid during pregnancy unless benefits outweigh risks.
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for 4 months after the last dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Ocrelizumab is excreted in human milk in very low concentrations (relative infant dose ~0.1%). The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in cynomolgus monkeys showed no impact on male or female fertility indices [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients >65 years have not been established [Australian]. (Note: Some sections of the source also mention data is limited for those >55 years).
- **Renal Impairment:** No formal studies. No dose adjustment is expected for patients with renal impairment as monoclonal antibodies are cleared via catabolism rather than renal excretion [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No formal studies. No dose adjustment is expected for patients with hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A recombinant humanised monoclonal antibody that selectively targets and depletes CD20-expressing B-cells. Depletion occurs via antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis, antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, complement-dependent cytotoxicity, and apoptosis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Central volume of distribution is estimated at 2.78 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Cleared principally by catabolism into peptides and amino acids.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 26 days.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment leads to rapid depletion of CD19+ B-cells in blood within 14 days. B-cell repletion median time is 72 weeks (range 27–175 weeks) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RMS (OPERA I & II):** Two 96-week, randomised, double-blind trials comparing **ocrelizumab** 600 mg IV to interferon beta-1a. **Ocrelizumab** showed a 46-47% relative reduction in annualised relapse rate and a 40% reduction in 12-week confirmed disability progression (pooled analysis) [Australian].
- **PPMS (ORATORIO):** A placebo-controlled trial where **ocrelizumab** 600 mg IV (given as two 300 mg doses) showed a 24% risk reduction in 12-week confirmed disability progression compared to placebo.
- **SC Formulation (OCARINA II):** A Phase III trial demonstrated that 920 mg SC was non-inferior to 600 mg IV based on ocrelizumab exposure (AUC) over 12 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. Keep in the outer carton to protect from light. Do not freeze or shake [Australian].
- **IV Diluted Solution:** Use immediately. If necessary, can be stored at 2°C to 8°C for up to 24 hours, and then for 8 hours at room temperature.
- **SC Prepared Syringe:** Use immediately. If storage is required, the syringe (without the infusion set attached) can be stored at 2°C to 8°C for up to 72 hours, followed by 8 hours at room temperature (up to 25°C).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
