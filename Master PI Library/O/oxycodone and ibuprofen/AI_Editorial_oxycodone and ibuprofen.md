# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a coherent, professional reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - Maximum recorded dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments (downward, e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Plasma Monitoring:** Free carnitine levels should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations is recommended for patients with metabolic disorders [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients [US]. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may trigger seizures in patients with or without a history of seizure activity. In patients with pre-existing epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **ESRD Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic high doses of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US]. *Note: The safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated specifically in renal insufficiency.*
- **Monitoring:** In ESRD patients, therapy initiation should be prompted by pre-dialysis plasma concentrations below the normal range (40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (regardless of causality) [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (21%), headache (37% at 20 mg/kg dose), injection site reaction (38%), fever (12%), chest pain (15%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (21%), hypotension (19%), tachycardia (9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (35% at 40 mg/kg dose), vomiting (21%), nausea (12%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (18%), paresthesia (12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (27%), cough increase (18%), dyspnea (11%).

### Post-Marketing Experience
- **Serious Effects:** Seizures, anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].
- **Other:** Transient nausea and vomiting, body odor, and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US]. 
    - Recommendation: Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration [US]. The risks of excess intake by the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were observed in standard tests (*S. typhimurium*, *S. cerevisiae*, *S. pombe*). Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders). See Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources; general caution regarding hepatic/renal function in the elderly is standard clinical practice.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safely used intravenously in ESRD patients on dialysis. Avoid chronic high-dose **oral** therapy in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Bioavailability:** Oral formulations have a low absolute bioavailability (~15%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg dose 3x weekly), mean pre-dialysis plasma carnitine levels rose from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Trough concentrations remained stable or increased over 12–24 weeks of therapy across 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg dose groups [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (signs/symptoms of deficiency) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation is preservative-free [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only).
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local jurisdiction for specific scheduling, e.g., Australian S4).
