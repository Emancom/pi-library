# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinically relevant reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines mandated for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport. 
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic/Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This should be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    - *Loading Dose:* In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - *Maintenance:* Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours (q3h/q4h) and never less frequent than every 6 hours (q6h). Daily doses generally range from 50 mg/kg upwards as clinically required; the maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - *Starting Dose:* 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - *Administration:* Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - *Dose Adjustment:* Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- For ESRD, monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration.
- **Management:** Discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical intervention. Risk-benefit must be carefully assessed before re-challenge; if re-administered, patients require intensive monitoring for recurrence [US].

### General Precautions
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulations):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been established in renal impairment. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD or severe renal impairment may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally renally excreted [US].

| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |

### Serious and Significant Reactions
- **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with established seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and transient nausea/vomiting.
- **Other:** Body odor has been reported at low frequencies.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely upon initiation of levocarnitine or following any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified in contemporary format; however, reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8x human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation has not been specifically studied in nursing mothers. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from potential excess carnitine intake should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are applicable to paediatric patients. Specific age-range safety profiles for ESRD indications are not explicitly detailed beyond general dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See *Dosage and Administration* for ESRD-specific protocols. Note that toxic metabolites (TMA/TMAO) may accumulate with oral administration in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not protein-bound (including albumin).
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (found in feces).
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).
- **Half-life:** Mean distribution $t_{1/2}$ is 0.585 hours; mean apparent terminal elimination $t_{1/2}$ is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma carnitine from ~20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
