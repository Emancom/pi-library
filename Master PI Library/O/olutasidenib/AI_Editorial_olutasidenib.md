# Editorial Product Information: Olutasidenib

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis designed for use by healthcare professionals. It provides a clinically accurate overview of the drug's profile across the contributing jurisdiction.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Olutasidenib (a small-molecule inhibitor of mutated isocitrate dehydrogenase-1 [IDH1]).
- **Brand Name(s):** **REZLIDHIA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 150 mg opaque white hard gelatin capsules [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Rigel Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsed or Refractory Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML):** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory AML who have a susceptible IDH1 mutation as confirmed by an approved companion diagnostic test [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 150 mg taken orally twice daily [US].
- **Duration:** Continue treatment until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. For patients without disease progression or toxicity, treatment for a minimum of 6 months is recommended to allow for a clinical response [US].

### 3.2 Administration Instructions
- **Food Requirements:** Administer on an empty stomach, at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal [US].
- **Method:** Swallow capsules whole. Do not break, open, or chew [US].
- **Timing:** Administer at approximately the same time each day. Do not administer two doses within an 8-hour window [US].

### 3.3 Missed or Vomited Doses
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed or not taken at the usual time, administer it as soon as possible, provided it is at least 8 hours before the next scheduled dose. Resume the normal schedule the following day [US].
- **Vomited Dose:** If a dose is vomited, do not take a replacement dose; wait until the next scheduled dose [US].

### 3.4 Dosage Modifications for Adverse Reactions
Reductions and interruptions are required for specific toxicities. When dose reduction is necessary, the suggested level is 150 mg once daily [US].
- **Differentiation Syndrome:** Withhold if suspected and initiate corticosteroids. Resume at 150 mg BID once resolved. If it recurs, resume at 150 mg QD for 7 days, then increase to 150 mg BID [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity (Grade 3):** Withhold until resolved to < Grade 1 or baseline. Resume at 150 mg QD; may increase to 150 mg BID if stable for 28 days. Discontinue if Grade 3 recurs at the lower dose [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity (Grade 4):** Permanently discontinue [US].
- **Other Grade 3+ Toxicities:** Interrupt until resolved to ≤ Grade 2. Resume at 150 mg QD, may increase to 150 mg BID if stable for 1 week. Discontinue if Grade 3+ recurs at the lower dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None listed in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### 5.1 Boxed Warning: Differentiation Syndrome [US]
- **Summary:** Differentiation syndrome can be fatal. It is associated with the rapid proliferation and differentiation of myeloid cells.
- **Symptoms:** Dyspnea, pulmonary infiltrates, pleuropericardial effusion, kidney injury, hypotension, fever, and weight gain.
- **Management:** If suspected, withhold **olutasidenib** and initiate systemic corticosteroids (e.g., dexamethasone 10 mg IV every 12 hours) and hemodynamic monitoring until resolution.

### 5.2 Hepatotoxicity
- **Olutasidenib** can cause significant increases in ALT, AST, bilirubin, and alkaline phosphatase.
- **Monitoring:** Perform liver function tests (LFTs) prior to initiation, weekly for the first two months, bi-weekly for the third month, and then monthly thereafter [US].
- **Clinical Note:** In clinical trials, the median time to onset was 1.2 months [US].

### 5.3 Monitoring Requirements
- Assess blood counts and blood chemistries (including LFTs) according to the schedule outlined in Section 5.2.
- Monitor for signs of Differentiation Syndrome.
- Monitor for QT prolongation, especially in patients with pre-existing conditions or those taking concomitant QT-prolonging medications [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Very Common (>10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (38%), constipation (26%), mucositis (23%), diarrhea (20%), abdominal pain (18%), vomiting (17%).
- **General:** Fatigue/malaise (36%), pyrexia (24%), edema (18%).
- **Musculoskeletal:** Arthralgia (28%).
- **Respiratory:** Dyspnea (24%), cough (17%).
- **Dermatologic:** Rash (24%).
- **Neurological:** Headache (13%).
- **Vascular:** Hypertension (10%).
- **Laboratory Abnormalities (All Grades):** AST increased (47%), ALT increased (46%), potassium decreased (46%), sodium decreased (42%), alkaline phosphatase increased (42%), creatinine increased (38%), bilirubin increased (26%), uric acid increased (25%).

### 6.2 Serious Adverse Effects
- **Differentiation Syndrome:** Occurred in 16% of patients; 8% were Grade 3 or 4 [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity/Transaminitis:** Serious transaminitis occurred in 6% of patients [US].
- **QT Prolongation:** Observed as a concentration-dependent increase in QTc interval [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### 7.1 Effect of Other Drugs on Olutasidenib
- **Strong/Moderate CYP3A Inducers:** Concomitant use decreases **olutasidenib** exposure (AUC decreased by 80% with rifampin), which may reduce efficacy. Avoid concomitant use [US].
- **Strong CYP3A/P-gp Inhibitors:** No clinically significant effect observed with itraconazole [US].

### 7.2 Effect of Olutasidenib on Other Drugs
- **Sensitive CYP3A Substrates:** **Olutasidenib** is a CYP3A inducer. Concomitant use may decrease the plasma concentration and efficacy of sensitive CYP3A substrates. Avoid concomitant use unless otherwise specified in the substrate's PI; monitor for loss of therapeutic effect [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified as a letter grade; however, based on animal data, it may cause fetal harm. Advise pregnant women of potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of the drug in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child, advise patients not to breastfeed during treatment and for 2 weeks after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal fertility studies have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in effectiveness were observed between patients ≥65 and younger patients. However, those ≥65 years had a higher incidence of hepatotoxicity and hypertension [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild to moderate (CLcr 30 to <90 mL/min): No dose adjustment required.
    - Severe or ESRD (CLcr <30 mL/min): Not studied; safety has not been established [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - Mild to moderate (Bilirubin ≤3x ULN): No dose adjustment required. Monitor closely for Differentiation Syndrome.
    - Severe (Bilirubin >3x ULN): Not studied; safety has not been established [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### 10.1 Mechanism of Action
- **Olutasidenib** is a small-molecule inhibitor of mutated IDH1. Mutated IDH1 produces 2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG), which inhibits cellular differentiation. **Olutasidenib** reduces 2-HG levels and restores normal myeloid differentiation [US].

### 10.2 Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Median $T_{max}$ is 4 hours. A high-fat meal increases $C_{max}$ by 191% and AUC by 83% [US].
- **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is 319 L; 93% plasma protein bound [US].
- **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized (90%) by CYP3A4, with minor contributions from CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP1A2, and CYP2C19 [US].
- **Elimination:** Mean half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is approximately 67 hours. Recovery is 75% in feces and 17% in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 2102-HEM-101:** An open-label, single-arm trial of 147 adult patients with R/R AML and IDH1 mutations. 
    - **Primary Outcome:** The Combined Complete Remission (CR) + Complete Remission with partial hematologic recovery (CRh) rate was 35% (95% CI: 27, 43). 
    - **Duration:** The median duration of CR+CRh was 25.9 months [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** Dispense in original container (high-density polyethylene bottle) [US].

- **United States:** Rx-only.
