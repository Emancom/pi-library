# Editorial Product Information: Ondansetron

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. It does not replace the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ondansetron (as hydrochloride dihydrate or base depending on formulation).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective 5-HT$_3$ receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Ondansetron ODT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A04AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Orally Disintegrating Tablets (ODT): 4 mg and 8 mg [Australian].
    - *Note: Other formulations such as injections, standard tablets, and suppositories are referenced in clinical data but may not be available under this specific brand.*
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral (specifically orodispersible).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prevention and treatment of nausea and vomiting induced by cytotoxic chemotherapy** [Australian].
- **Prevention and treatment of nausea and vomiting induced by radiotherapy** [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
The selection of dose is based on the emetogenic potential of the specific treatment regimen. The daily dose range is typically 8 mg to 32 mg [Australian].

### Emetogenic Chemotherapy and Radiotherapy
- **Initial Dose:** 8 mg orally, administered 2 hours prior to the commencement of treatment [Australian].
- **Follow-up:** 8 mg orally every 12 hours for up to 5 days following the course of treatment to prevent delayed emesis [Australian].

### Highly Emetogenic Chemotherapy
- **Single Dose Regimen:** A single oral dose of up to 24 mg administered 1 to 2 hours before chemotherapy, ideally in combination with 12 mg of oral dexamethasone [Australian].
- **Follow-up:** 8 mg orally every 12 hours for up to 5 days [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Orally Disintegrating Tablets:** Place the tablet on the tongue. It will dissolve within seconds and should then be swallowed with saliva [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Concomitant Apomorphine Use:** Use with apomorphine hydrochloride is strictly contraindicated due to reports of profound hypotension and loss of consciousness [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ondansetron or any components of the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Prolongation:** Ondansetron causes dose-dependent prolongation of the QT interval. Post-marketing cases of Torsade de Pointes have been reported. Avoid in patients with congenital long QT syndrome. Use with extreme caution in patients with electrolyte imbalances (hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia), congestive heart failure, or bradyarrhythmias [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Cases have been reported, particularly when used alongside other serotonergic agents (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs). Symptoms may include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular abnormalities [Australian].
- **Masking of Intestinal Obstruction:** As ondansetron increases large bowel transit time, patients with signs of subacute obstruction should be monitored [Australian].
- **Phenylketonuria:** ODT formulations containing aspartame should be used with caution in patients with phenylketonuria [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity to other 5-HT$_3$ Antagonists:** Cross-reactivity has been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Apomorphine:** Potentially fatal hypotension; concomitant use is contraindicated [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Potent inducers (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampicin) significantly increase oral clearance and reduce blood concentrations of ondansetron. Dose adjustments may be required, though specific clinical guidance in cancer patients is limited [Australian].
- **Tramadol:** Ondansetron may reduce the analgesic efficacy of tramadol [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of Serotonin Syndrome when co-administered with SSRIs or SNRIs [Australian].
- **QT-Prolonging Agents:** Caution is required when co-administering drugs that prolong the QT interval or cause electrolyte disturbances [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1.**
    - **First Trimester:** Ondansetron is suspected to cause orofacial malformations (e.g., oral clefts) when used in the first trimester. Epidemiological data suggests a small but significant increased risk (approx. 3 additional cases per 10,000 women). Use in the first trimester is not recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Animal studies show excretion in milk. It is recommended that mothers receiving ondansetron do not breastfeed [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in rat studies at doses up to 15 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children over 4 years of age for CINV/RINV. Recommended intravenous dose is 5 mg/m$^2$ followed by oral doses of 4 mg twice daily for 5 days [Australian]. Safety in children under 2 years is not established.
- **Elderly Use:** No adjustment of dose or frequency is required for patients over 65 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No alteration of daily dosage, frequency, or route of administration is required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate:** Clearance is reduced by approximately 50%.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh score $\geq$ 10):** Clearance is reduced to 20% of normal. **Total daily dose must not exceed 8 mg** [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ondansetron is a potent, highly selective 5-HT$_3$ receptor antagonist. It blocks the initiation of the vomiting reflex by antagonising 5-HT$_3$ receptors on vagal afferents in the gastrointestinal tract and neurons in the area postrema (chemoreceptor trigger zone) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels reached in ~1.5 hours. Bioavailability is approximately 60% [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding 70–76%; Volume of distribution is 1.8 L/kg [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver via CYP3A4, CYP2D6, and CYP1A2 [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 4.1 to 11.6 hours. Less than 5% is excreted unchanged in the urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Cisplatin Regimens:** Studies comparing 8 mg and 32 mg IV doses prior to cisplatin ($\geq$ 50 mg/m$^2$) showed no significant difference in efficacy, though some studies in very high-dose cisplatin ($\geq$ 100 mg/m$^2$) suggested 32 mg might be superior [Australian].
- **Oral vs. IV:** In patients receiving cisplatin, 24 mg oral ondansetron was found to be as effective as 8 mg IV ondansetron in controlling acute emesis [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** The addition of dexamethasone significantly enhances the antiemetic effect of ondansetron [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
