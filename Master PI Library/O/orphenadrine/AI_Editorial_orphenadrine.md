# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    *   **United States**: Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
*   **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
*   **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Chemical Class**: Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Name(s)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
*   **ATC Code**: A16AA01 (Not specified in available source, provided for clinical context).
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    *   **Injection**: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration**: Intravenous (IV). 
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency**: For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
*   **Route**: For intravenous use only. Administer by bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
*   **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg.
*   **Loading Dose**: Often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
*   **Frequency**: Administer every 3 or 4 hours (q3h/q4h); frequency should never be less than every 6 hours (q6h).
*   **Maintenance**: Subsequent daily doses are recommended in the range of 50 mg/kg or as clinically required.
*   **Maximum Dose**: The highest recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
*   **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
*   **Administration**: Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
*   **Dose Adjustments**: Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
*   **Metabolic Disorders**: Weekly and monthly monitoring recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L).
*   **Preparation**: Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.

## 4. Contraindications
*   **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
*   **Seizures**: Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency and/or severity has been observed [US].
*   **TMA and TMAO Accumulation**: Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis may result in the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)

| **General** | Injection site reaction | 33% |

| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension | 20% |

### Other Adverse Effects
*   **Gastrointestinal**: Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent: gastritis and body odor [US].
*   **Neurologic**: Seizures [US].
*   **Post-marketing**: Reports of drug dependence (2%), hypertonia (1%), and various cardiovascular disturbances [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly. 
*   **Management**: Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **US Category**: Not assigned a lettered category in the provided source.
*   **Animal Data**: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
*   **Recommendation**: Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].

### Breastfeeding
*   **Safety Profile**: Levocarnitine supplementation in lactating women has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration.
*   **Recommendation**: Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of either nursing or the drug may be considered [US].

### Fertility
*   No impairment of fertility observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use**: Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg) [US].
*   **Elderly Use**: Not specifically detailed in the source.
*   **Renal Impairment**: Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. IV dosing is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3) [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
*   **Metabolism**: Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
*   **Excretion**: Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
*   **Bioavailability**: Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations (tablets/solution) is approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies**: A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed that trough concentrations reached between 54 and 180 µmol/L (normal: 40–50 µmol/L) within one week.
*   **Dose-Ranging**: Studies (N≈30 per group) comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Conditions**: Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
*   **Handling**: Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
*   **Compatibility**: Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
