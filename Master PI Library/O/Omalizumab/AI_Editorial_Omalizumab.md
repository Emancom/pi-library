# Editorial Product Information: Omalizumab

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority for the biosimilar medicine **Omlyclo**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, regulatory requirements, and administration guidelines.
- **Prescribing Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Omalizumab (rch)
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant DNA-derived humanised monoclonal antibody (IgG1 kappa)
- **Brand Name(s):** **Omlyclo** (Biosimilar to **Xolair**) [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** R03DX05 (Monoclonal antibodies for respiratory diseases)
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Solution for injection in a pre-filled syringe (with needle guard) or pre-filled pen:
        - 75 mg in 0.5 mL
        - 150 mg in 1.0 mL
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Celltrion Healthcare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Allergic Asthma:** 
    - **Paediatric (6 to <12 years):** Add-on therapy for severe allergic asthma in patients with documented exacerbations despite daily high-dose inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) and matching IgE levels.
    - **Adults and Adolescents (≥12 years):** Management of moderate to severe allergic asthma in patients already treated with inhaled steroids and matching IgE levels.
- **Chronic Rhinosinusitis with Nasal Polyps (CRSwNP):** Add-on treatment in adults (≥18 years) with severe CRSwNP and inadequate response to intranasal corticosteroids [Australian].
- **Chronic Spontaneous Urticaria (CSU):** For adults and adolescents (≥12 years) who remain symptomatic despite H1 antihistamine treatment.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles (Asthma and CRSwNP)
- **Dose Determination:** Based on baseline serum total Immunoglobulin E (IgE) (IU/mL) measured before starting treatment and body weight (kg).
- **Dose Range:** 75 mg to 600 mg per administration, delivered in 1 to 4 injections.
- **Frequency:** Administered every 2 weeks or every 4 weeks depending on the IgE/weight calculation.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Doses should be adjusted for significant changes in body weight but do not need adjustment for variations in total IgE over time.

### Dosing for Chronic Spontaneous Urticaria (CSU)
- **Standard Dose:** 300 mg every four weeks.
- **Alternative Dose:** Some patients may achieve control with 150 mg every four weeks.

### Administration Instructions
- **Sites:** Subcutaneous injection in the front of the thighs or lower abdomen (avoiding the 5 cm area around the navel). Caregivers/HCPs may also use the outer upper arm.
- **Volume:** Doses >150 mg should be divided across two or more injection sites.
- **Training:** The first 3 doses should be administered by or under the supervision of an HCP. Patients/caregivers may self-inject after proper training if deemed appropriate.
- **Missed Dose:** Not explicitly detailed; standard practice involves administering as soon as remembered and adjusting the subsequent schedule.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to omalizumab or any excipient (arginine hydrochloride, histidine hydrochloride monohydrate, histidine, polysorbate 20).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Allergic Reactions:** 
    - **Anaphylaxis:** Rare in clinical trials but reported in post-marketing. May occur after the first or subsequent doses, usually within 2 hours but sometimes beyond 24 hours.
    - **Serum Sickness:** Delayed Type III reactions (arthritis, fever, rash, lymphadenopathy) have been reported, typically 1–5 days post-administration.
- **Corticosteroid Tapering:** Do not abruptly discontinue systemic or inhaled corticosteroids upon starting omalizumab. Reduction should be gradual and under medical supervision.
- **Churg-Strauss and Hypereosinophilic Syndrome:** Rarely, patients may present with systemic eosinophilia and vasculitis, often associated with the reduction of oral steroids.
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Pre-clinical data showed dose-related thrombocytopenia in non-human primates. While not observed in clinical trials, it has been reported post-marketing. Monitor platelet counts before and during therapy [Australian].
- **Parasitic Infestations:** IgE is involved in the immune response to helminth infections. Use caution in patients at high risk of parasitic infection.
- **Laboratory Interference:** Total IgE levels increase up to 4-fold during treatment and remain elevated for up to one year after discontinuation; therefore, total IgE cannot be used for dose titration during treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Allergic Asthma
- **Very Common (>10%):** Pyrexia (in children 6–12 years) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Injection site reactions (pain, swelling, erythema, pruritus), headache, upper abdominal pain (in children).
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Dizziness, somnolence, paresthesia, syncope, postural hypotension, flushing, pharyngitis, coughing, allergic bronchospasm, nausea, diarrhoea, weight increase, fatigue.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Anaphylactic reactions, laryngoedema, angioedema.

### CSU and CRSwNP
- **CSU:** Nasopharyngitis, sinusitis, viral upper respiratory tract infection, and headache (very common at 300 mg dose: 12%).
- **CRSwNP:** Headache, dizziness, arthralgia, and injection site reactions.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Arteriothrombolic Events (ATE):** A numerical imbalance was observed in observational studies (stroke, MI, unstable angina), though a causal relationship is not established.
- **Malignancies:** Observed in 0.5% of active vs 0.2% of control patients in initial trials, though later 5-year observational studies did not indicate increased risk.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Formal Studies:** None performed.
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Omalizumab has been used safely with ICS, LABAs, oral/intranasal corticosteroids, H1/H2 antihistamines, and leukotriene receptor antagonists (LTRAs).
- **Mechanism Interaction:** No relevant effect of H2 antihistamines or LTRAs on omalizumab pharmacokinetics was observed.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category:** B1 (Drugs taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the human fetus having been observed).
    - **Data:** Human IgG crosses the placental barrier. Registry data (EXPECT study) showed no increase in major congenital anomalies but noted an increased rate of low birth weight.
- **Breastfeeding:** IgG is present in human milk. While the EXPECT study showed no adverse effects on breastfed infants, use should be considered only if clinically needed.
- **Fertility:** No human data. Animal studies showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Asthma:** Safety/efficacy not established in children <6 years.
    - **CSU:** Safety/efficacy not established in children <12 years.
    - **CRSwNP:** Safety/efficacy not established in patients <18 years.
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** Limited data; no evidence that different dosing is required.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific studies have been conducted. Use with caution due to the lack of pharmacokinetic data.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Omalizumab binds to free serum IgE at the same site where IgE binds to the high-affinity receptor (FcεRI). This reduces the amount of free IgE available to trigger the allergic cascade and down-regulates FcεRI receptors on mast cells and basophils.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Slowly absorbed (peak serum concentration in 6–10 days). Absolute bioavailability is approximately 53–71%.
    - **Distribution:** Typical of large macromolecules (110 ± 14 mL/kg).
    - **Elimination:** Half-life averages 22–24 days. Clearance involves IgG clearance processes and liver reticuloendothelial system degradation.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Serum free IgE levels are reduced within 2 hours of SC dosing.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Allergic Asthma (Adults/Adolescents):** Study 2306 showed a 74% treatment effect ratio favouring omalizumab in reducing exacerbations requiring systemic steroids.
- **Allergic Asthma (Paediatrics):** Study IA05 (52 weeks) demonstrated a 31–43% relative decrease in exacerbation rates.
- **CRSwNP:** Two studies (N=138, N=127) showed statistically significant improvements in Nasal Polyp Score (NPS) and Nasal Congestion Score (NCS) at 24 weeks compared to placebo.
- **CSU:** Studies Q4881g, Q4882g, and Q4883g demonstrated significant dose-dependent reductions in the weekly Itch Severity Score (ISS) and Urticaria Activity Score (UAS7).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Protect from light by keeping in the original carton.
- **Ex-refrigerator Stability:** May be kept at room temperature (up to 25°C) for a total of 7 days. If not used within 7 days, it must be discarded [Australian].
- **Preparation:** Allow the pre-filled syringe (20 mins) or pen (30–45 mins) to reach room temperature before injection. Do not warm using external heat sources.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [General].
