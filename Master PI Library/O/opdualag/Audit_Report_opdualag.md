## Close Paraphrases

    - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 4.4: "Where immune-related colitis appears to be corticosteroid-refractory, consider repeating investigations for... (CMV) infection/reactivation."

    - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 4.4.

3. **Storage Stability:** "Room temperature/room light: Use within 8 hours of preparation. Refrigerated (2°C to 8°C): Use within 24 hours of preparation."

## Originality Strengths
- **Section 1 (Product Identification):** Successfully merges different nomenclature standards (the FDA's 4-letter suffix "-rmbw" vs. the TGA's "(rch)" designation) into a single identification block.
- **Section 3.3 (Dosage Modifications):** The author creates a "Conservative Harmonisation" section. Instead of just listing one table, it identifies where the FDA and TGA thresholds differ (e.g., AST/ALT >5x vs >8x ULN) and presents them side-by-side. This is an original editorial synthesis.
- **Section 6 (Adverse Effects):** The data is presented in a custom table format that prioritizes "Very Common" vs "Serious" categories, which is not how the data is structured in the source PI (which uses "All Grades" vs "Grade 3-4").

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Synthesis of Jurisdictions:** The editorial adds brackets like `[TGA]` and `[FDA]` to highlight specific regional differences in manufacturers and naming conventions.
- **Harmonised Terminology:** Uses "Glucose 5%" (TGA) alongside "Dextrose 5%" (FDA) to ensure the document is readable in both jurisdictions.
- **Exclusion of Fine Detail:** Omitted extensive animal data, specific excipient weights (e.g., 85.6 mg sucrose), and complex PK subgroup ranges to maintain an "editorial synthesis" focus.
- **Patient Safety Additions:** Explicitly notes the Australian requirement for a "Patient Card," a detail found in the TGA document but not the FDA highlights.

## Recommendation
The editorial **PASSES** the originality review.

While there are three instances of verbatim technical language (primarily in the Indications and Mechanism of Action sections), these are standard regulatory phrases where deviation could potentially alter the legal or clinical meaning of the approval. The remainder of the document—specifically the dosage modification synthesis and the side-by-side comparison of TGA/FDA lab thresholds—demonstrates significant original effort to harmonise and rewrite the information for a clinical audience. No further revisions are required.
