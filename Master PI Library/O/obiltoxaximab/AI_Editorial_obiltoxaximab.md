# Editorial Product Information: Obiltoxaximab

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising available product information for **obiltoxaximab**. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the United States (FDA) PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Obiltoxaximab (chimeric IgG1 kappa monoclonal antibody).
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Anthim** [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (typically classified under J06BB: Specific Immunoglobulins/Antibodies).
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 600 mg/6 mL (100 mg/mL) solution in a single-dose vial [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Elusys Therapeutics, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Treatment of Inhalational Anthrax:** Indicated in adult and paediatric patients for the treatment of inhalational anthrax due to *Bacillus anthracis*, used in combination with appropriate antibacterial drugs [US].
*   **Prophylaxis of Inhalational Anthrax:** Indicated for the prevention of inhalational anthrax when alternative therapies are not available or appropriate [US].
    *   *Note:* Effectiveness is based solely on efficacy studies in animal models; human clinical trials with intentional exposure are not ethical or feasible [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
*   **Adult Patients:** A single dose of 16 mg/kg [US].
*   **Pre-medication:** Administer diphenhydramine prior to the infusion to reduce the risk of infusion-related reactions [US].

### Paediatric Dosing (Weight-Based)
The following single-dose regimens are recommended for paediatric patients based on population pharmacokinetic (PK) modelling [US]:
*   **>40 kg:** 16 mg/kg.
*   **>15 kg to 40 kg:** 24 mg/kg.
*   **≤15 kg:** 32 mg/kg.

### Administration Instructions
*   **Preparation:** Must be diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, USP. Do not shake the vial. Inspect for particulate matter; a few translucent-to-white proteinaceous particles are acceptable [US].
*   **Infusion Rate:** Administer intravenously over 90 minutes (1 hour and 30 minutes) [US].
*   **Filtration:** Use a 0.22-micron inline filter during administration [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Administer in a monitored setting equipped to manage anaphylaxis. Monitor the patient throughout the infusion and for a period after completion [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis [US]
*   **Anaphylaxis and hypersensitivity reactions** have been reported during **obiltoxaximab** infusion.
*   **Requirement:** Administer only in monitored settings by personnel trained and equipped to manage anaphylaxis.
*   **Action:** If hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis occurs, stop the infusion immediately and initiate appropriate medical treatment.

### General Warnings
*   **Limitations of Prophylactic Use:** Should only be used for prophylaxis when the benefits outweigh the risks of hypersensitivity [US].
*   **Antibacterial Co-administration:** **Obiltoxaximab** does not possess direct antibacterial activity and must be used in combination with appropriate antibiotics for the treatment of anthrax [US].
*   **Blood-Brain Barrier:** The drug is not expected to cross the blood-brain barrier and is not effective for the prevention or treatment of anthrax meningitis [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions
In clinical trials involving healthy adult subjects, the following reactions occurred at a frequency of ≥1.5% [US]:
*   **Very Common (>10%):** Hypersensitivity reactions (pooled) (10.6%).
*   **Common (1–10%):** Headache (8%), pruritus (4%), upper respiratory tract infections (5%), cough (3%), vessel puncture site bruise (3%), infusion site swelling (3%), urticaria (2%), nasal congestion (2%), infusion site pain (2%), and pain in extremity (2%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   **Anaphylaxis:** Reported in 0.9% (3/320) of healthy subjects during or immediately after infusion [US]. Symptoms included rash, urticaria, cough, dyspnoea, cyanosis, postural dizziness, and chest discomfort.

### Immunogenicity
*   Treatment-emergent anti-therapeutic antibodies (ATA) were detected in 2.5% (8/320) of subjects. Titers were generally low (1:20 to 1:320) with no observed effect on PK or safety [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Ciprofloxacin:** Co-administration of IV **obiltoxaximab** (16 mg/kg) with IV or oral ciprofloxacin did not alter the pharmacokinetics of either agent [US].
*   **Other Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category:** Not assigned (Current US labelling uses Risk Summary).
    *   **Data:** No human data are available. Animal studies (rabbits) using up to 32 mg/kg showed no adverse developmental outcomes at exposures approximately 0.62 times the human AUC at 16 mg/kg [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if **obiltoxaximab** is excreted in human milk. Maternal IgG is known to be present in human milk. Consideration should be given to the maternal clinical need and potential risk to the infant [US].
*   **Fertility:** Specific fertility studies have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** No safety or PK studies have been conducted in children. Dosing was derived from simulations using a population PK approach [US].
*   **Geriatric Use:** Limited data (9.4% of trial subjects were ≥65 years). No dose adjustment is recommended for patients ≥65 years [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** **Obiltoxaximab** is not cleared renally; therefore, renal impairment is not expected to affect exposure [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
**Obiltoxaximab** is a monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity (Kd = 0.33 nM) to the protective antigen (PA) of *B. anthracis*. By binding to PA, it prevents the assembly of anthrax toxin (lethal toxin and edema toxin) and its entry into host cells, thereby neutralising the toxic effects [US].

### Pharmacokinetics [US]
*   **Absorption:** Immediate via IV route; PK is linear between 4 mg/kg and 16 mg/kg.
*   **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is slightly larger than plasma volume, suggesting some tissue distribution.
*   **Metabolism/Elimination:** Clearance is significantly lower than the glomerular filtration rate, indicating minimal renal clearance. 
*   **Half-life:** Not explicitly stated as a single value, but repeat dosing at 2 weeks resulted in a 2-fold increase in AUC.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Efficacy:** Based on the "Animal Rule." In New Zealand White (NZW) rabbits and cynomolgus macaques, a single 16 mg/kg IV dose significantly improved survival compared to placebo when administered after the onset of bacteremia or toxemia [US].
*   **Combination Therapy:** Studies showed higher survival rates when **obiltoxaximab** was used in combination with antibiotics (levofloxacin, ciprofloxacin, or doxycycline) compared to antibiotics alone [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Protect from light by keeping the vial in the original carton. Do not freeze. Do not shake [US].
*   **Diluted Stability:** Once diluted, the solution is stable for 8 hours at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) or 8 hours if refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Rx-only.
