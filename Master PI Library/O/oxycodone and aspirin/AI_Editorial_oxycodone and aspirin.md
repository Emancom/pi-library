# Editorial Product Information: Oxycodone and Aspirin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) source was provided; therefore, all information reflects [US] regulatory positions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Oxycodone Hydrochloride (opioid agonist) and Aspirin (salicylate).
- **Chemical Class:** Opioid analgesic and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID).
- **Brand Names:** Distributed as a generic product by Epic Pharma, LLC [US].
- **ATC Code:** N02BA51 (Oxycodone and salicylic acid derivatives) [Not explicitly in source; inferred for classification].
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Tablet:** 4.8355 mg oxycodone hydrochloride (equivalent to 4.3346 mg oxycodone free base) and 325 mg aspirin [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Epic Pharma, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pain Management:** Indicated for the management of pain severe enough to require an opioid analgesic and for which alternative treatments (e.g., non-opioid analgesics) are inadequate [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Due to risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, this product should be reserved for patients where alternative options have not been tolerated or have failed to provide adequate analgesia. Long-term use should only continue if pain remains severe and alternative options remain inadequate [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 
    - **Initiation:** One tablet every 6 hours as needed for pain [US].
    - **Titration:** Dosing must be individualised based on pain severity, patient response, and prior analgesic experience [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The maximum daily dose of aspirin must not exceed 4 grams (12 tablets) [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets are for oral use only [US].
- **Discontinuation:** Do not stop therapy abruptly in physically dependent patients. Taper the dose gradually (e.g., by 10% to 25% of the total daily dose every 2 to 4 weeks) to avoid withdrawal symptoms [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the next dose should be taken at the usual time; do not double the dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Respiratory:** Significant respiratory depression; acute or severe bronchial asthma in unmonitored settings or without resuscitative equipment [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to oxycodone, aspirin, or other salicylates [US].
- **Coagulation Disorders:** Patients with hemophilia [US].
- **Pediatric/Viral Infections:** Children or teenagers with viral infections (with or without fever) due to the risk of Reye syndrome [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [US]:**
    - **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Risk of opioid addiction which can lead to overdose and death.
    - **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal depression may occur, especially during initiation or dose escalation.
    - **Accidental Ingestion:** Even one dose can be fatal, particularly in children.
    - **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Prolonged use during pregnancy can result in life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate.
    - **Cytochrome P450 3A4 Interaction:** Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors (or discontinuation of inducers) can increase oxycodone levels and lead to fatal respiratory depression.
    - **CNS Depressants:** Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other CNS depressants may result in profound sedation, coma, and death.
- **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH) and Allodynia:** Paradoxical increase in pain despite stable or increased opioid dosing [US].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, typically following >1 month of therapy [US].
- **Severe Hypotension:** Risk of orthostatic hypotension and syncope, especially in patients with compromised blood volume or concurrent CNS depressant use [US].
- **Seizure Disorders:** Oxycodone may increase seizure frequency in patients with known seizure disorders [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Effects:** Risk of gastric mucosal irritation and bleeding due to the aspirin component; oxycodone may cause spasm of the sphincter of Oddi [US].
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Aspirin may cause exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (Frequency not specified but listed as "most frequent"):** Lightheadedness, dizziness, sedation, nausea, vomiting. These are more prominent in ambulatory patients [US].
- **Other Reported Effects:** Euphoria, dysphoria, constipation, pruritus [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - **Respiratory:** Apnea, respiratory arrest, circulatory depression [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension, shock, tachycardia, bradycardia [US].
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Hemorrhagic ulcers, intestinal perforation, pancreatitis, Reye Syndrome [US].
    - **Hematologic:** Prolonged prothrombin time, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia [US].
    - **General:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., macrolides, azole antifungals, protease inhibitors):** May increase oxycodone plasma concentrations; monitor frequently and consider dose reduction [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine):** May decrease oxycodone efficacy or precipitate withdrawal; monitor for reduced effect [US].
- **Benzodiazepines and CNS Depressants:** Additive effect on respiratory depression and sedation; reserve for cases with no alternative and use lowest effective dose [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Risk of serotonin syndrome when used with SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, or triptans [US].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Avoid use within 14 days of MAOI therapy due to risk of serotonin syndrome or opioid toxicity [US].
- **Mixed Agonist/Antagonist Analgesics (e.g., buprenorphine, pentazocine):** May reduce analgesic effect or precipitate withdrawal [US].
- **Anticoagulants (Heparin/Warfarin):** Aspirin increases bleeding risk by displacing warfarin from binding sites and inhibiting platelet function [US].
- **NSAIDs:** Concurrent use with other NSAIDs should be avoided due to increased risk of bleeding and renal impairment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **NOWS:** Risk of Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome with prolonged use [US].
    - **Fetal Toxicity:** Use of NSAIDs (aspirin) at ~20 weeks gestation or later may cause fetal renal dysfunction leading to oligohydramnios. Avoid use at ~30 weeks gestation or later due to the risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Oxycodone and salicylic acid are present in breast milk. Monitor infants for excess sedation, respiratory depression, and adverse effects on platelet function. The risk of Reye syndrome from salicylate in breast milk is unknown [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term opioid use may cause reduced fertility in both sexes (androgen deficiency) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Pediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established. Contraindicated in children/teenagers for viral illnesses due to Reye syndrome [US].
- **Geriatric Use (≥65 years):** Increased sensitivity to oxycodone. Start at the low end of the dosing range and titrate slowly; monitor for respiratory depression and renal function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Mean elimination half-life of oxycodone is prolonged. Use with caution. Avoid aspirin in severe renal impairment (GFR <10 mL/min) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Oxycodone clearance is decreased in end-stage liver disease. Use with caution. Avoid aspirin in severe hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Oxycodone:** Full mu-opioid receptor agonist; produces analgesia and CNS effects [US].
    - **Aspirin:** Irreversibly inhibits cyclo-oxygenase, preventing the formation of thromboxane A2 and prostaglandins [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Mean oral bioavailability of oxycodone is ~87% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Oxycodone is 45% plasma protein bound; aspirin (as salicylate) is variably bound to albumin [US]. Both cross the placenta and enter breast milk.
    - **Metabolism:** Oxycodone is metabolized via CYP3A4 (N-demethylation to noroxycodone) and CYP2D6 (O-demethylation to oxymorphone) [US]. Aspirin is rapidly hydrolyzed to salicylic acid.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in urine (8%–14% of oxycodone as free drug; 80%–100% of aspirin as salicylate/metabolites) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].
- **Disposal:** If a take-back program is unavailable, unused tablets should be disposed of by flushing down the toilet to prevent accidental ingestion [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **US:** Schedule II (CII) Controlled Substance. Rx-only.
