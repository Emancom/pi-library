# Editorial Product Information: Oritavancin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of oritavancin (specifically as **KIMYRSA**) for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and administration guidelines into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for the most current and region-specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Oritavancin (as oritavancin diphosphate).
- **Chemical Class:** Semisynthetic lipoglycopeptide antibacterial.
- **Brand Names:** **KIMYRSA**, **ORBACTIV** [US]. 
    - *Editorial Note: This document focuses primarily on the **KIMYRSA** formulation as per the provided source.*
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **KIMYRSA**: 1,200 mg of oritavancin (equivalent to 1331.16 mg oritavancin diphosphate) as a sterile, lyophilized powder in a single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Melinta Therapeutics, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Bacterial Skin and Skin Structure Infections (ABSSSI):** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with ABSSSI caused or suspected to be caused by susceptible isolates of the following Gram-positive microorganisms [US]:
    - *Staphylococcus aureus* (including methicillin-susceptible and methicillin-resistant [MRSA] isolates).
    - *Streptococcus pyogenes*.
    - *Streptococcus agalactiae*.
    - *Streptococcus dysgalactiae*.
    - *Streptococcus anginosus* group (includes *S. anginosus*, *S. intermedius*, and *S. constellatus*).
    - *Enterococcus faecalis* (vancomycin-susceptible isolates only).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** A single 1,200 mg dose administered as an intravenous infusion [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Infusion Duration:** Administer over 1 hour [US]. 
    - *Clinical Note:* This differs from other oritavancin products (e.g., **ORBACTIV**), which may require a 3-hour infusion [US].
- **Preparation (KIMYRSA):**
    - **Reconstitution:** Aseptically add 40 mL of Sterile Water for Injection (SWFI) to the 1,200 mg vial to achieve a 30 mg/mL solution. Gently swirl to avoid foaming [US].
    - **Dilution:** Withdraw and discard 40 mL from a 250 mL IV bag containing either 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% dextrose in sterile water (D5W). Add 40 mL of the reconstituted oritavancin solution to the bag to reach a final volume of 250 mL (concentration: 4.8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible with 0.9% sodium chloride injection and 5% dextrose in sterile water (D5W) [US].
- **Incompatibilities:** **KIMYRSA** may be incompatible with drugs formulated at basic or neutral pH. Do not administer simultaneously with other medications through the same IV port. If using a common line for sequential infusion, flush the line with 0.9% sodium chloride or D5W before and after oritavancin administration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Heparin Use:** Use of intravenous unfractionated heparin sodium is contraindicated for 120 hours (5 days) following oritavancin administration [US]. This is due to artificial prolongation of the activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to oritavancin products [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Coagulation Test Interference:** Oritavancin binds to and prevents the action of phospholipid reagents used in laboratory tests. It artificially prolongs:
    - aPTT: up to 120 hours (5 days).
    - PT and INR: up to 12 hours.
    - ACT: up to 24 hours.
    - Silica clot time (SCT): up to 18 hours.
    - D-dimer: up to 72 hours [US].
    - *Monitoring:* For patients requiring anticoagulation monitoring within these windows, use non-phospholipid-dependent tests (e.g., Chromogenic Factor Xa assay) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis:** Serious reactions have been reported. Discontinue infusion immediately if acute hypersensitivity occurs. Use with caution in patients with known glycopeptide allergy (e.g., vancomycin) due to potential cross-sensitivity [US].
- **Infusion-Related Reactions:** Symptoms such as flushing of the upper body, urticaria, pruritus, rash, chest pain, and back pain may occur. Slowing or stopping the infusion may alleviate these symptoms [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** May range in severity from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis. Consider CDAD in all patients presenting with diarrhea post-antibiotic use [US].
- **Osteomyelitis:** In clinical trials, more cases of osteomyelitis were reported in the oritavancin arm compared to vancomycin. Monitor patients for signs/symptoms of osteomyelitis and initiate alternate therapy if suspected [US].
- **Warfarin Use:** Oritavancin may artificially prolong PT/INR for up to 12 hours, making warfarin monitoring unreliable during this window. Monitor for bleeding regardless of lab results if used concomitantly [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials (≥1.5% frequency) for oritavancin products compared to vancomycin:

| Adverse Reaction | Oritavancin (N=976) | Vancomycin (N=983) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nausea** | 9.9% | 10.5% |
| **Headache** | 7.1% | 6.7% |
| **Vomiting** | 4.6% | 4.7% |
| **Diarrhea** | 3.7% | 3.4% |
| **Abscess (limb/subcutaneous)** | 3.8% | 2.3% |
| **Dizziness** | 2.7% | 2.6% |
| **ALT Increased** | 2.8% | 1.5% |
| **Tachycardia** | 2.5% | 1.1% |
| **Infusion site phlebitis** | 2.5% | 1.5% |

- **Other Reactions (<1.5%):** Anemia, eosinophilia, bronchospasm, wheezing, angioedema, erythema multiforme, and hypoglycemia [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Cellulitis was the most common serious adverse reaction reported in trials (1.1%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Metabolism:** Oritavancin is a non-specific, weak inhibitor of CYP2C9 and CYP2C19, and a weak inducer of CYP3A4 and CYP2D6. Concomitant use with drugs of narrow therapeutic indices metabolized by these enzymes (e.g., warfarin) requires close monitoring for toxicity or reduced efficacy [US].
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** See Section 5. Oritavancin does NOT interfere with coagulation in vivo, nor does it affect tests for Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT) [US].
- **Positive Antiglobulin Test (IAT/DAT):** Positive indirect/direct antiglobulin tests were noted; this may interfere with cross-matching before blood transfusions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified as a letter grade; however, data is insufficient to evaluate drug-associated risks for major birth defects or miscarriage. Animal studies in rats and rabbits showed no embryo-fetal harm at doses equivalent to 25% of the human 1,200 mg dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Oritavancin is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. It is unknown if it is present in human milk. Consider the developmental benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother’s clinical need and potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility or reproductive performance were observed in male or female rats at doses equivalent to 25% of the clinical dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not identify overall differences in safety or efficacy between elderly and younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate:** No dosage adjustment required [US].
    - **Severe:** Not evaluated [US].
    - **Hemodialysis:** Oritavancin is not removed from the blood by hemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh Class B):** No dosage adjustment required [US].
    - **Severe:** Not studied [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal activity via three mechanisms:
    1. Inhibition of the transglycosylation (polymerization) step of cell wall biosynthesis.
    2. Inhibition of the transpeptidation (crosslinking) step of cell wall biosynthesis.
    3. Disruption of bacterial membrane integrity, leading to depolarization and cell death [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 85% protein bound; extensively distributed into tissues (Volume of distribution ~87.6 L) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Not metabolized [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted slowly and unchanged in feces (<1%) and urine (<5%) over two weeks [US].
    - **Half-life:** Terminal plasma half-life is approximately 245 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trials (Trial 1 and Trial 2):** Two identically designed, double-blind, non-inferiority trials (n=1,987) compared a single 1,200 mg dose of oritavancin to twice-daily vancomycin for 7–10 days.
- **Primary Endpoint:** Early clinical response (cessation of spread or reduction in lesion size, absence of fever, and no rescue antibiotics) at 48–72 hours.
- **Results:** Oritavancin was non-inferior to vancomycin. In Trial 1, response rates were 82.3% vs 78.9%; in Trial 2, response rates were 80.1% vs 82.9% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials (Unopened):** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Diluted Solution:** 
    - **Room Temperature:** Use within 4 hours.
    - **Refrigerated (2°C to 8°C):** Use within 12 hours.
    - *Note:* The total combined time (reconstituted vial + diluted bag + 1-hour infusion) must not exceed 4 hours at room temperature or 12 hours if refrigerated [US].
