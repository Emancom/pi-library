# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **US (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN); chemical class: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Brand Names**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd for Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors)**: Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in ESRD**: Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders**: Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis**: Starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.

### Administration Instructions
- **Metabolic Disorders**: Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **ESRD**: Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Visual Inspection**: Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Metabolic Crisis**: A loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **ESRD Adjustments**: Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].
- **Monitoring Goals**: Target plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known**: No contraindications are identified in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity**: Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been reported to cause seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Note)**: While this document concerns the injection, the source notes that high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine/TMA and trimethylamine-N-oxide/TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
Based on placebo-controlled trials in ESRD patients, the following effects were reported regardless of causality [US]:
- **Body as a Whole**: Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), chest pain (6–15%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%).
- **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive**: Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Metabolic**: Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
- **Nervous**: Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Neurologic**: Seizures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity**: Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management**: Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Category**: Not assigned a letter grade in the current PI, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
    - **Clinical Recommendation**: Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: 
    - Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. 
    - **Clinical Recommendation**: Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Refer to the Dosage and Administration section; metabolic dosing is weight-based [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specifically addressed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment**: Levocarnitine is easily removed by dialysis. In ESRD, accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) is a concern with chronic *oral* administration; the injection is specifically indicated for use in this population post-dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific organic acidopathies by forming acylcarnitine esters that are quickly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination**: Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis) increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies**: Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcomes Note**: While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations, its effect on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, arrhythmias) in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling**: Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing. 
- **Stability after Dilution**: Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL, stored at room temperature [US].
- **Vial Integrity**: Vials are single-dose; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
