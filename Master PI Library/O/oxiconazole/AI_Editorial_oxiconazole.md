# Editorial Product Information: Oxiconazole Nitrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of oxiconazole nitrate for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States PI was available for review; therefore, all clinical data and regulatory classifications reflect US standards.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Oxiconazole nitrate (an imidazole derivative antifungal).
- **Chemical Name:** 2',4'-dichloro-2-imidazol-1-ylacetophenone (Z)-[0-(2,4-dichlorobenzyl)oxime], mononitrate.
- **Brand Names:** Oxiconazole Nitrate Cream (E. Fougera & Co.) [US].
- **ATC Code:** D01AC11 (Imidazole and triazole derivatives).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Topical cream, 1% (containing 10 mg of oxiconazole per gram).
- **Route of Administration:** Topical (dermatologic) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** E. Fougera & Co. (a division of Fougera Pharmaceuticals, LLC) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Oxiconazole nitrate cream is indicated for the topical treatment of the following dermal infections [US]:
- **Tinea pedis** (athlete's foot) caused by *Trichophyton rubrum*, *Trichophyton mentagrophytes*, or *Epidermophyton floccosum*.
- **Tinea cruris** (jock itch) caused by *T. rubrum*, *T. mentagrophytes*, or *E. floccosum*.
- **Tinea corporis** (ringworm) caused by *T. rubrum*, *T. mentagrophytes*, or *E. floccosum*.
- **Tinea (pityriasis) versicolor** caused by *Malassezia furfur*.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Tinea pedis, tinea corporis, or tinea cruris:** Apply to the affected and immediately surrounding skin areas once or twice daily [US].
- **Tinea (pityriasis) versicolor:** Apply to the affected and surrounding areas once daily [US].

### Duration of Treatment
To reduce the risk of recurrence, the following treatment durations are recommended [US]:
- **Tinea corporis, tinea cruris, and tinea versicolor:** 2 weeks.
- **Tinea pedis:** 1 month.

### Administration Instructions
- For external dermatologic use only. Not for ophthalmic or intravaginal use.
- Hands should be washed immediately after application.
- Patients should avoid occlusive dressings unless specifically directed by a physician.
- If no clinical improvement is observed after the designated treatment period, the diagnosis should be re-evaluated.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to oxiconazole nitrate or any component of the cream formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Route Restrictions:** Strictly for external dermal use. Avoid contact with the eyes, nose, mouth, and other mucous membranes [US].
- **Sensitivity Reactions:** If a reaction suggesting sensitivity or chemical irritation occurs, treatment must be discontinued, and appropriate therapy instituted.
- **Epidermal Irritation:** Discontinue use if signs of significant epidermal irritation occur.
- **Tinea Versicolor Pigmentation:** Patients should be advised that treatment for tinea versicolor may not immediately restore pigment to the affected sites. Normalisation of skin pigment is variable and may take several months depending on skin type and sun exposure [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
In clinical trials involving 955 patients, the overall incidence of adverse reactions thought to be related to drug therapy was 4.3% [US].

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Pruritus (1.6%), Burning (1.4%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Irritation (0.4%), Allergic contact dermatitis (0.4%), Folliculitis (0.3%), Erythema (0.2%) |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Papules, fissure, maceration, rash, stinging, nodules |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Systemic Interactions:** Potential drug interactions between oxiconazole nitrate and other medications have not been systematically evaluated. Given the low systemic absorption of the topical formulation, significant systemic interactions are not expected [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B** [US]. Reproduction studies in rabbits, rats, and mice at oral doses up to 27–57 times the human dose revealed no evidence of foetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Oxiconazole is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised when the drug is administered to a nursing woman [US].
- **Fertility:** In rat studies, oral doses above 3 mg/kg/day (females) and 15 mg/kg/day (males) resulted in reduced fertility parameters, including decreased sperm count and extended estrous cycles [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for use in paediatric patients for tinea corporis, tinea cruris, tinea pedis, and tinea versicolor. While clinical data in children aged 10 and under indicate safety and efficacy, these conditions rarely occur in children under the age of 12 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials included approximately 396 patients aged 60 or older. No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed compared to younger patients. No dosage adjustment is required for geriatric patients [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments are not specified in available sources, likely due to low systemic absorption.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Oxiconazole is an imidazole antifungal that primarily inhibits the biosynthesis of ergosterol, a critical component for maintaining fungal cell membrane integrity [US].
- **Microbiology:** Demonstrates activity against *Epidermophyton floccosum*, *Trichophyton mentagrophytes*, *Trichophyton rubrum*, and *Malassezia furfur*. It also shows *in vitro* activity against *Candida albicans* and various *Microsporum* and *Trichophyton* species, though clinical significance for these specific organisms has not been established.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Systemic absorption is very low. Less than 0.3% of the applied dose was recovered in the urine of volunteers up to 5 days post-application [US].
    - **Penetration:** Five hours after application (2.5 mg/cm²), concentrations were measured at 16.2 μmol in the epidermis, 3.64 μmol in the upper corium, and 1.29 μmol in the deeper corium.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Tinea Pedis:** Two pivotal trials (n=281) compared once-daily (q.d.) and twice-daily (b.i.d.) application. Mycological cure rates were 77% (b.i.d.) and 79% (q.d.) at two weeks post-treatment, significantly superior to vehicle (33%).
- **Tinea Versicolor:** Two pivotal trials (n=219) for once-daily application for 2 weeks showed a mycological cure rate of 88% compared to 67% for the vehicle at the two-week follow-up [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the tube tightly closed. Keep out of reach of children.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as **Rx-only** (Prescription Only Medicine).
