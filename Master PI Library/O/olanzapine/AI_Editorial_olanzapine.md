# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documentation from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in a particular region. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Levocarnitine** (USP); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often utilized in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Adjustments:** Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Pre-therapy plasma carnitine levels should be obtained [US].
- Regular weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** No specific contraindications are listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Accumulation of Metabolites:** In patients with severely compromised renal function (including ESRD), chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]); however, the implications for the intravenous formulation are not fully defined [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology of metabolic disorders or ESRD.

### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% Frequency in Dialysis Trials)
| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain (12%), Fever (7%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Nausea (8%), Vomiting (15%), Abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Depression (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase (9%), Pharyngitis (23%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hypervolemia (5%) |

### Serious/Other Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and body odor have been reported [US].
- **Neurological:** Seizures (post-marketing reports) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when **levocarnitine** is co-administered with warfarin.
    - **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of treatment or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter grade in the current PI, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Although bovine studies show increased carnitine levels in milk, the risks to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of treatment to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders apply to pediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3 for dosing). Use caution with high-dose therapy due to potential metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
**Levocarnitine** is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It acts as a carrier molecule that facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with ESRD, intravenous administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. However, the direct effect on specific clinical symptoms of deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dialysis Studies:** In two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, patients receiving **levocarnitine** (10, 20, or 40 mg/kg) showed significant increases in trough carnitine levels compared to baseline. A 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg thrice weekly) showed trough levels increasing from ~21 µmol/L at baseline to ~190 µmol/L by week 8 [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature when mixed in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
