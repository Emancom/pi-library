# Editorial Product Information: olmesartan + hydrochlorothiazide

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **olmesartan + hydrochlorothiazide**
- Chemical class:
  - Olmesartan medoxomil: angiotensin II receptor (AT1) antagonist (prodrug olmesartan medoxomil).
  - Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ): thiazide diuretic (sulfonamide-derived).
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral.
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form descriptions, tablet physical descriptions, or PBS/schedule listings.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of hypertension.
- Treatment should not be initiated with the fixed-dose combination product; it is intended for patients whose blood pressure is not adequately controlled by olmesartan medoxomil or hydrochlorothiazide alone.  
- Jurisdictional differences in approved indications: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Available combination strengths documented: 20/12.5 mg, 40/12.5 mg and 40/25 mg (olmesartan medoxomil / hydrochlorothiazide).
- Usual regimen: once daily, with or without food.
- Do not initiate antihypertensive therapy with the fixed-dose combination.

### Dosing by clinical situation / titration
- When adding HCTZ to olmesartan medoxomil:
  - Start HCTZ 12.5 mg. If blood pressure remains inadequately controlled, consider up-titration of HCTZ to 25 mg or increasing olmesartan medoxomil to 40 mg.
- When adding olmesartan medoxomil to HCTZ:
  - Start olmesartan medoxomil 20 mg; if blood pressure remains inadequately controlled, consider up-titration to 40 mg.
- Dose titration may be adjusted after approximately 4 weeks depending on response.
- Doses above olmesartan 40 mg / HCTZ 25 mg are not recommended.

### Administration instructions
- Administer once daily, with or without food.
- Do not initiate therapy with the fixed-dose combination product; instead, initiate with a single component and add the second component as clinically indicated.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List of contraindications identified in the available sources:
- Hypersensitivity to olmesartan medoxomil, hydrochlorothiazide, sulfonamide-derived drugs (e.g. thiazides), or any component of the product.
- Pregnancy (use in pregnancy contraindicated).
- Patients with anuria.
- Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min).
- Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh score 10–15), cholestasis or biliary obstruction.
- Breastfeeding (contraindicated).
- Refractory hypokalaemia, hypercalcaemia, hyponatraemia, and symptomatic hyperuricaemia.
- Patients with diabetes who are taking aliskiren.

(If a region-specific contraindication differs, that variation is not specified in available sources.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No jurisdiction-specific boxed warning text is specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Intravascular volume depletion: symptomatic hypotension (including first-dose hypotension) can occur in volume- and/or sodium-depleted patients (e.g. vigorous diuretic therapy, salt restriction, diarrhoea, vomiting). Correct volume depletion before initiating therapy where possible.
- Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAS) dependency: patients whose vascular tone and renal function depend on RAS (e.g. severe congestive heart failure, underlying renal disease including renal artery stenosis) may experience acute hypotension, azotaemia, oliguria, acute renal failure, or death with RAS blockade. Similar effects cannot be excluded with olmesartan.
- Renovascular hypertension: increased risk of severe hypotension and renal insufficiency in patients with bilateral renal artery stenosis or stenosis of the artery to a single functioning kidney.
- Hyperkalaemia: may occur with RAS inhibitors, particularly in renal impairment and/or heart failure. Concomitant potassium-sparing drugs, potassium supplements, salt substitutes containing potassium, or drugs that increase potassium (e.g. trimethoprim-containing medicines) may precipitate hyperkalaemia.
- Non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC): epidemiological studies have observed an increased risk of basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma with increasing cumulative exposure to hydrochlorothiazide; photosensitising action of HCTZ is a possible mechanism. Patients should be advised to monitor skin and minimise UV exposure.
- Sprue-like enteropathy: severe, chronic diarrhoea with substantial weight loss reported months to years after olmesartan initiation; intestinal biopsies often show villous atrophy. Exclude other causes; consider discontinuation if no other cause identified.
- Aortic and mitral valve stenosis / obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy: use vasodilators with caution.
- Primary aldosteronism: antihypertensive agents acting via RAS inhibition are generally ineffective; use not recommended.
- Metabolic and endocrine effects:
  - Thiazide therapy may impair glucose tolerance; diabetic patients may need insulin/oral hypoglycaemic dose adjustment.
  - Thiazides associated with increases in cholesterol and triglycerides.
  - Hyperuricaemia or gout may be precipitated.
- Electrolyte imbalance:
  - Thiazides can cause hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hypochloraemic alkalosis; also decrease urinary magnesium (possible hypomagnesaemia) and decrease urinary calcium excretion (can elevate serum calcium slightly).
  - Olmesartan component may reduce diuretic-induced hypokalaemia but, via RAS inhibition, may also cause hyperkalaemia.
  - Monitor serum electrolytes periodically during therapy.
- Angioedema: angioedema including laryngeal/glottic swelling has been reported; discontinue immediately and do not rechallenge.
- Acute respiratory toxicity: very rare reports of severe acute respiratory toxicity including acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) following hydrochlorothiazide intake; pulmonary oedema can develop rapidly. Discontinue if ARDS suspected; do not rechallenge if ARDS previously occurred with HCTZ.
- Photosensitivity: photosensitivity reactions reported with thiazides; discontinue if photosensitivity occurs. If re-challenge considered, ensure sun/UVA protection.
- Ethnic differences: antihypertensive effect of olmesartan may be somewhat less in black patients compared with non-black patients.
- Lithium: co-administration with lithium is not recommended.
- Choroidal effusion / acute myopia / secondary angle-closure glaucoma: idiosyncratic reaction to sulfonamides (including HCTZ) reported; symptoms may occur hours to weeks after initiation. Discontinue HCTZ immediately if suspected and consider urgent ophthalmological management.
- Concomitant use of ACE inhibitors or ARBs with NSAIDs and thiazide diuretics: triple combination increases risk of renal impairment; monitor renal function closely, especially at treatment initiation and in elderly or pre-existing renal impairment.
- Hepatic impairment: use with caution in impaired hepatic function or progressive liver disease as electrolyte/fluid changes may precipitate hepatic coma. Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh 10–15), cholestasis, biliary obstruction.
- Renal impairment: contraindicated in severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min). No dosage adjustment necessary for mild (CrCl 50–80 mL/min) to moderate (CrCl 30–<50 mL/min), but periodic monitoring of renal function, serum potassium and uric acid is advised. Thiazide-associated azotaemia may occur.
- Elderly: no dosage adjustment required in general, but initiate cautiously and monitor due to likelihood of decreased organ function and concomitant disease/drugs.
- Paediatric use: safety and efficacy not established.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic monitoring advised: blood pressure, renal function (serum creatinine, creatinine clearance), serum electrolytes (potassium, sodium, magnesium, calcium), blood glucose (if diabetic or at risk), lipids (cholesterol, triglycerides), uric acid.
- Monitor for skin lesions (NMSC risk) and advise dermatological evaluation for suspicious lesions.
- Monitor for signs of sprue-like enteropathy (chronic severe diarrhoea and weight loss) and ophthalmological symptoms (for angle-closure glaucoma).

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Dual blockade of the RAS (ACE inhibitors, angiotensin II receptor blockers, aliskiren):
  - Increased risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia, and renal function changes (including acute renal failure) compared to monotherapy. Close monitoring of blood pressure, renal function and electrolytes recommended when combining agents that affect the RAS.
  - Do not co-administer aliskiren with olmesartan + hydrochlorothiazide in patients with diabetes (contraindicated).
  - Avoid using aliskiren with olmesartan + hydrochlorothiazide in patients with renal impairment (GFR <60 mL/min).
- Potassium-modifying agents:
  - Potassium-depleting effect of HCTZ may be potentiated by other kaliuretic agents, laxatives, corticosteroids, ACTH, amphotericin, penicillin sodium, or salicylic acid derivatives.
  - Concomitant use with potassium-sparing diuretics, potassium supplements, potassium-containing salt substitutes, heparin, or trimethoprim-containing medicines may increase serum potassium; monitor potassium levels.
- NSAIDs (including high-dose aspirin and COX-2 inhibitors):
  - NSAIDs and angiotensin II receptor antagonists may act synergistically to decrease glomerular filtration and increase the risk of acute renal failure. NSAIDs can also blunt the diuretic and antihypertensive effects of thiazides; observe patients closely for loss of diuretic efficacy and monitor renal function at treatment initiation.
- Lithium:
  - Co-administration is not recommended (risk of increased lithium levels and toxicity).
- Colesevelam (specific pharmacokinetic interaction):
  - Concomitant administration of olmesartan medoxomil (40 mg) with colesevelam hydrochloride (3750 mg) resulted in ~28% reduction in olmesartan Cmax and ~39% reduction in AUC. When olmesartan medoxomil was given 4 hours prior to colesevelam the reductions were ~4% (Cmax) and ~15% (AUC). Consider administering olmesartan medoxomil 4 hours before colesevelam hydrochloride.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Antacids: text truncated in available sources; details not specified.
- Other specific pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources beyond colesevelam data.

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- Avoid combined use of aliskiren in diabetic patients and avoid in patients with significant renal impairment (GFR <60 mL/min) [see Contraindications and Dual RAS blockade].
- Monitor renal function and electrolytes when combining with NSAIDs, other RAS agents, potassium-modifying drugs, or in patients with pre-existing renal impairment or elderly.
- Consider spacing administration when co-administered with colesevelam (administer olmesartan medoxomil 4 hours before colesevelam where possible).
- Use caution and monitoring if combining with lithium; co-administration not recommended.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents have not been established.

### Elderly use
- No routine dosage adjustment required based solely on age; however, use caution, start at lower end of dosing range and monitor closely due to increased likelihood of decreased organ function, comorbidities and concomitant medications.

### Renal impairment
- Contraindicated in severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min).
- No dosage adjustment required for mild (CrCl 50–80 mL/min) to moderate (CrCl 30–<50 mL/min) renal impairment, but periodic monitoring of renal function, serum potassium and uric acid is recommended.
- Intravascular volume depletion: use under close medical supervision; monitor BP and renal function.
- Recent kidney transplantation: no experience documented in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dosage adjustment specified for mild (Child-Pugh 5–6) to moderate (Child-Pugh 7–9) hepatic impairment; exercise caution and monitor blood pressure and renal function, especially in patients already on diuretics/other antihypertensives.
- Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh 10–15), cholestasis and biliary obstruction.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Olmesartan medoxomil: prodrug converted to olmesartan, an angiotensin II receptor (AT1) antagonist that inhibits the renin–angiotensin system (RAS).
- Hydrochlorothiazide: a thiazide diuretic (sulfonamide-derived) that produces diuresis and natriuresis; associated with alterations in electrolyte and metabolic parameters (see Warnings and Adverse Effects).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Physicochemical properties (as reported):
  - Olmesartan medoxomil: white to off-white crystalline powder; practically insoluble in water and heptane, slightly soluble in ethanol (96%), sparingly soluble in methanol.
  - Hydrochlorothiazide: white or almost white crystalline powder; very slightly soluble in water, soluble in acetone, sparingly soluble in ethanol (96%); dissolves in dilute alkali hydroxide solutions.
- Absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination: Not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacokinetic data in renal or hepatic impairment: Not specified in available sources (pharmacokinetics not studied in these populations as per the available text).

### Pharmacodynamics
- Blood pressure lowering via AT1 receptor antagonism (olmesartan) combined with diuretic effect (HCTZ) leading to decreased plasma volume and reduced peripheral resistance.
