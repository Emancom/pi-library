# Editorial Product Information: Olanzapine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from the following regulatory authorities: 
    - Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **olanzapine** for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Olanzapine
- **Chemical Class:** Thienobenzodiazepine; atypical antipsychotic, antimanic, and mood-stabilising agent.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Olanzapine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05AH03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Tablets (Uncoated):** 2.5 mg, 5 mg, 7.5 mg, and 10 mg [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia:** Treatment of schizophrenia and related psychoses.
- **Bipolar I Disorder (Acute):** Short-term treatment of acute manic episodes, either as monotherapy or in combination with lithium or valproate.
- **Bipolar I Disorder (Maintenance):** Prevention of recurrence in patients with manic, mixed, or depressive episodes.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
**Olanzapine** may be administered once daily without regard to food intake.

### Schizophrenia and Related Psychoses
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 5 mg to 10 mg daily.
- **Therapeutic Range:** 5 mg to 20 mg daily.
- **Titration:** Increases beyond the routine 10 mg daily dose should only occur after clinical reassessment.

### Acute Mania (Bipolar I Disorder)
- **Monotherapy Starting Dose:** 10 mg or 15 mg daily.
- **Combination Therapy (with Lithium or Valproate):** 10 mg daily.
- **Titration:** Adjustments should occur at intervals of no less than 24 hours in increments/decrements of 5 mg. Efficacy is established between 5 mg and 20 mg daily.

### Maintenance in Bipolar Disorder
- **Patients already on Olanzapine:** Continue at the same dose used for the acute episode.
- **New Patients (Remission):** Suggested starting dose is 10 mg daily.
- **Adjustments:** Range of 5 mg to 20 mg daily based on clinical status.

### Missed Doses
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **olanzapine** or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Elderly Patients with Dementia-Related Psychosis:** **Olanzapine** is NOT approved for this population. Clinical trials demonstrated a significantly higher incidence of death (3.5% vs 1.5% placebo) and cerebrovascular adverse events, including stroke and TIA, in these patients [Australian].
- **Metabolic Alterations:**
    - **Hyperglycemia and Diabetes:** Significant risk of treatment-emergent hyperglycemia, sometimes leading to ketoacidosis, coma, or death. Monitor glucose at baseline and periodically.
    - **Lipid Changes:** Increases in fasting total cholesterol, LDL, and triglycerides are common. Regular lipid monitoring is recommended.
    - **Weight Gain:** Significant weight gain is common across all BMI categories. Monitor weight regularly.
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal; requires immediate discontinuation if symptoms (hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, autonomic instability) occur.
- **Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS):** Discontinue if suspected (rash, fever, eosinophilia, and systemic complications).
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Risk increases with long-term exposure; consider dose reduction or discontinuation if signs appear.
- **Cardiac Effects:**
    - **Postural Hypotension:** Particularly in the elderly.
    - **QT Interval:** Caution when used with other QTc-prolonging medications.
    - **Sudden Cardiac Death:** Very rare reports; observational data suggest a dose-related increase in risk compared to non-users of antipsychotics.
- **Seizures:** Use cautiously in patients with a history of seizures or lowered seizure threshold.
- **Anticholinergic Activity:** Use with caution in prostatic hypertrophy, narrow-angle glaucoma, or paralytic ileus.
- **Blood Abnormalities:** Exercise caution in patients with low leukocyte/neutrophil counts or drug-induced bone marrow depression.
- **Sleep Apnea:** Reported in patients with or without prior history; caution is advised, especially with concomitant CNS depressants.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data (Clinical Trials)
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Somnolence, weight gain (≥7% of baseline), orthostatic hypotension, increased prolactin, increases in fasting cholesterol/triglycerides/glucose (borderline to high). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, akathisia, asthenia, fatigue, pyrexia, constipation, dry mouth, increased appetite, peripheral oedema, arthralgia, ALT/AST elevations, eosinophilia, leucopenia. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Bradycardia, photosensitivity, abdominal distension, amnesia, restless legs syndrome, epistaxis, stuttering, salivary hypersecretion. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Seizures, hepatitis, rash, elevated creatinine phosphokinase. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | NMS, DRESS, pancreatitis, jaundice, thrombocytopenia, rhabdomyolysis, priapism, venous thromboembolism, alopecia. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Cerebrovascular events** in elderly patients with dementia.
- **Diabetic ketoacidosis/coma**.
- **Venous thromboembolism** (including pulmonary embolism).
- **Sudden unexplained death**.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Charcoal:** Reduces oral bioavailability by 50–60%.
    - **CYP1A2 Inducers:** Smoking and carbamazepine increase **olanzapine** clearance (by 33% and 44% respectively).
    - **CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** Fluvoxamine and ciprofloxacin decrease clearance, significantly increasing **olanzapine** Cmax and AUC. Consider lower doses.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **CNS Depressants:** Additive sedation with alcohol and other centrally acting drugs.
    - **Dopamine Agonists:** **Olanzapine** may antagonize the effects of direct and indirect dopamine agonists.
    - **Antihypertensives:** Potential for increased orthostatic hypotension.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C:** Neonates exposed during the third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal symptoms and/or withdrawal (agitation, hypertonia, tremor, somnolence, respiratory distress). Use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** **Olanzapine** is excreted in breast milk (approx. 1.8% of maternal dose). Breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show impaired mating performance (sedation) and disrupted oestrous cycles.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients under 18 years. 
    - **Note on Adolescents (13-17):** Clinical trials showed a higher magnitude of weight gain and higher mean increases in prolactin and lipids compared to adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Consider a lower starting dose (5 mg) for patients ≥65 years if clinical factors warrant.
- **Renal Impairment:** Consider a lower starting dose (5 mg). **Olanzapine** is not removed by dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Consider a lower starting dose (5 mg). Exercise caution in patients with pre-existing limited functional reserve.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Atypical antipsychotic with broad receptor affinity. Antagonist at serotonin (5HT2A/2C, 5HT3, 5HT6), dopamine (D1-D5), muscarinic (m1-m5), histamine (H1), and alpha1-adrenergic receptors. It shows higher affinity for 5HT2 than D2 receptors.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed; peak plasma levels in 5–8 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 93% protein bound (albumin and alpha1-acid glycoprotein).
    - **Metabolism:** Hepatic via conjugation and oxidation (primarily CYP1A2 and CYP2D6).
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is 33 hours. 57% excreted in urine (principally as metabolites), 30% in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Schizophrenia:** Established in trials (n > 2,900) showing superiority to placebo and haloperidol on BPRS and PANSS scales, particularly for negative symptoms.
- **Bipolar Disorder:** Efficacy established in three-week and four-week trials for acute mania. Recurrence prevention was demonstrated in a 12-month study where **olanzapine** significantly delayed time to symptomatic relapse compared to placebo (174 days vs 22 days).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Keep in the original container.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
