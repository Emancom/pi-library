# Editorial Product Information: Olanzapine and Fluoxetine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: **United States (FDA)**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for the combination of **olanzapine** and **fluoxetine**. It is intended as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** **Olanzapine** (atypical antipsychotic) and **Fluoxetine** (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor/SSRI).
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Editorial Note: Commercially known as Symbyax in the US; document provided refers to Teva generic).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths [US]:** Oral capsules containing olanzapine/equivalent fluoxetine base in the following ratios:
    - 3 mg/25 mg
    - 6 mg/25 mg
    - 6 mg/50 mg
    - 12 mg/25 mg
    - 12 mg/50 mg
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor [US]:** Teva Pharmaceuticals USA, Inc.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Depressive Episodes Associated with Bipolar I Disorder:**
    - Adults: For the acute treatment of depressive episodes [US].
    - Pediatrics (10–17 years): For the acute treatment of depressive episodes [US].
- **Treatment-Resistant Depression (TRD):**
    - Adults: Indicated for Major Depressive Disorder in patients who have failed to respond to two separate trials of different antidepressants of adequate dose and duration in the current episode [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Administration:** Administer once daily in the evening, with or without food [US].
- **Adult Dosing (Bipolar I Depression & TRD):**
    - **Starting Dose:** Generally 6 mg/25 mg once daily [US].
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Adjust based on clinical efficacy and tolerability.
    - **Maximum Dose:**
        - Bipolar Depression: 12 mg/50 mg daily [US].
        - Treatment-Resistant Depression: 18 mg/75 mg daily (Note: safety of doses >18 mg/75 mg has not been evaluated) [US].
- **Pediatric Dosing (Bipolar I Depression, 10–17 years):**
    - **Starting Dose:** 3 mg/25 mg once daily [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** 12 mg/50 mg once daily [US].
- **Dosing in Special Populations:**
    - **Predisposition to Hypotension/Slow Metabolism:** Start with 3 mg/25 mg to 6 mg/25 mg and escalate cautiously [US].
    - **Hepatic Impairment:** Use a lower or less frequent dose in patients with cirrhosis [US].
- **Switching/Washout Periods:**
    - **From MAOI to Olanzapine/Fluoxetine:** Allow at least 14 days after stopping an MAOI [US].
    - **From Olanzapine/Fluoxetine to MAOI:** Allow at least 5 weeks after stopping the combination before initiating an MAOI [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):**
    - Concurrent use or use within 5 weeks of stopping **olanzapine/fluoxetine** is contraindicated due to the risk of Serotonin Syndrome [US].
    - Initiation of **olanzapine/fluoxetine** within 14 days of stopping an MAOI is contraindicated [US].
    - Use with **linezolid** or **intravenous methylene blue** is contraindicated [US].
- **Pimozide:** Concurrent use is contraindicated due to the risk of QT interval prolongation [US].
- **Thioridazine:** Concurrent use or use within 5 weeks of stopping **olanzapine/fluoxetine** is contraindicated due to the risk of QT interval prolongation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]:** 
    - **Suicidality:** Increased risk of suicidal thinking and behavior in children, adolescents, and young adults (up to age 24) taking antidepressants. Monitor all patients closely for clinical worsening and emergence of suicidal thoughts.
    - **Dementia-Related Psychosis:** Increased mortality in elderly patients treated with antipsychotic drugs. This combination is **not approved** for the treatment of patients with dementia-related psychosis.
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal; manage with immediate discontinuation and intensive monitoring [US].
- **Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS):** Discontinue treatment if DRESS is suspected [US].
- **Metabolic Changes:** Atypical antipsychotics are associated with:
    - **Hyperglycemia/Diabetes:** Monitor fasting blood glucose before and during treatment [US].
    - **Dyslipidemia:** Monitor lipid profiles at baseline and periodically [US].
    - **Weight Gain:** Significant weight gain occurs; monitor weight regularly [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Risk increases with concomitant serotonergic agents. Discontinue if symptoms (autonomic instability, neuromuscular changes) occur [US].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Risk increases with duration of treatment; consider discontinuation if signs appear [US].
- **Orthostatic Hypotension:** Risk of syncope and bradycardia, particularly during initial titration [US].
- **Leukopenia, Neutropenia, and Agranulocytosis:** Monitor CBC in patients with a history of low WBC or drug-induced leukopenia [US].
- **QT Prolongation:** Avoid use with other QT-prolonging drugs. Use with caution in patients with risk factors for arrhythmia [US].
- **Other Precautions:** Angle-closure glaucoma, seizures (use cautiously in patients with a history), increased bleeding risk (with NSAIDs/warfarin), and hyponatremia/SIADH [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (Incidence ≥5% and ≥2x Placebo)
| Population | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Adults** | Sedation, weight increased, appetite increased, dry mouth, fatigue, edema, tremor, disturbance in attention, blurred vision [US]. |
| **Pediatrics** | Sedation, weight increased, appetite increased, tremor, triglyceride increased, hepatic enzymes increased [US]. |

### Frequency Categorization [US]
- **Very Common (>10%):** Somnolence (Adult 27%, Ped 24%), Weight increased (Adult 25%, Ped 20%), Increased appetite (Adult 20%, Ped 17%), Edema (Adult 15%), Dry mouth (Adult 15%), Fatigue (Adult 12%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Tremor, blurred vision, disturbance in attention, restlessness, hepatic enzyme elevation, blood triglycerides increased (Ped 7%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Rhabdomyolysis, venous thromboembolic events (VTE), pulmonary embolism, and cerebrovascular accidents (CVAE) in elderly patients [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP2D6 Inhibition:** Fluoxetine is a potent inhibitor; use caution with drugs like tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs), vinblastine, and certain antiarrhythmics [US].
    - **CYP1A2 Inducers:** Carbamazepine may increase olanzapine clearance by 50% [US].
    - **CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** Fluvoxamine increases olanzapine levels; consider lower olanzapine doses [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **CNS Acting Drugs:** Caution with alcohol and other CNS depressants [US].
    - **Antihypertensives:** Olanzapine may enhance hypotensive effects [US].
    - **Levodopa/Dopamine Agonists:** Olanzapine may antagonize these agents [US].
    - **Hemostasis:** NSAIDs, aspirin, and warfarin increase bleeding risk when used with fluoxetine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy [US]:**
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter code in source, but data provided:
    - **Risks:** Neonates exposed to antipsychotics in the 3rd trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms. SSRI use late in pregnancy increases the risk of Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN) and neonatal poor adaptation.
    - **Management:** Weigh risks of untreated depression against drug risks.
- **Breastfeeding [US]:**
    - Both **olanzapine** and **fluoxetine** are excreted in human milk.
    - **Infant Monitoring:** Monitor for excess sedation, irritability, poor feeding, and extrapyramidal symptoms.
- **Fertility:** Prolactin elevation may lead to reversible reduction in female fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Pediatric Use:**
    - Approved for Bipolar I Depression in ages 10–17 [US].
    - **Not approved** for TRD in patients <18 years or Bipolar Depression <10 years [US].
- **Geriatric Use:**
    - Start at the low end of the dosing range [US].
    - High risk of orthostatic hypotension and hyponatremia [US].
    - Increased mortality and stroke risk in dementia-related psychosis [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing adjustment is not routinely required, though metabolites of fluoxetine may accumulate in severe impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Lower or less frequent dosing is required in patients with cirrhosis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Olanzapine:** Antagonist at serotonin (5HT2A/2C, 5HT6), dopamine (D1-4), histamine (H1), and adrenergic (α1) receptors [US].
    - **Fluoxetine:** Inhibits the serotonin transporter (SERT) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Olanzapine peak ~6 hours; Fluoxetine peak ~6–8 hours. Food does not significantly affect absorption [US].
    - **Distribution:** Both are highly protein-bound (>93%) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Olanzapine via CYP1A2 and CYP2D6; Fluoxetine via CYP2D6 to active metabolite norfluoxetine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Olanzapine half-life ~30 hours; Fluoxetine half-life ~1–3 days (acute) to 4–6 days (chronic); Norfluoxetine half-life ~4–16 days [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bipolar I Depression:** Efficacy established in two 8-week adult trials (N=788) and one 8-week pediatric trial (N=255). The combination was superior to placebo and olanzapine monotherapy in reducing MADRS/CDRS-R scores [US].
- **Treatment-Resistant Depression:** Efficacy demonstrated in three 8-week acute trials and one 47-week maintenance study. The combination significantly increased time to relapse compared to fluoxetine monotherapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light and moisture. Keep the container tightly closed and dispense in a light-resistant container [US].
