# Editorial Product Information: Odevixibat

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates Product Information (PI) from the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA, Australia) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA, United States).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised, clinically accurate overview of odevixibat for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing. Clinicians must consult local regulatory documents for the most current legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Odevixibat (as sesquihydrate)
- **Chemical Class:** Ileal bile acid transporter (IBAT) inhibitor
- **Brand Name:** **Bylvay**
- **ATC Code:** A05AX05 [TGA]
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Hard Capsules / Oral Pellets:** 200 mcg, 400 mcg, 600 mcg, and 1200 mcg.
    - *Note:* The FDA distinguishes between "Oral Pellets" (200 mcg, 600 mcg) and "Capsules" (400 mcg, 1200 mcg), whereas the TGA classifies all strengths as "Hard Capsules."
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Albireo Pharma, Inc. [FDA]; Ipsen Pty Ltd [TGA]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Progressive Familial Intrahepatic Cholestasis (PFIC):** 
    - Treatment of PFIC in patients aged 6 months or older [TGA].
    - Treatment of pruritus in patients aged 3 months or older with PFIC [FDA].
    - *Limitation of Use:* Odevixibat may be ineffective and is not recommended in PFIC Type 2 patients with specific ABCB11 variants resulting in complete absence of the Bile Salt Export Pump (BSEP) protein (e.g., BSEP3 subtype).
- **Alagille Syndrome (ALGS):** 
    - Treatment of cholestatic pruritus in patients aged 6 months or older [TGA].
    - Treatment of cholestatic pruritus in patients aged 12 months or older [FDA].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Should be taken in the morning. The FDA recommends administration with a meal, while the TGA allows intake with or without food.
- **Formulation Use:** 
    - Pellets (200 mcg and 600 mcg) are intended for patients weighing <19.5 kg or those unable to swallow capsules [FDA].
    - Capsules (400 mcg and 1200 mcg) are generally for patients weighing ≥19.5 kg [FDA]. 

### Dosage by Indication
- **PFIC:** 
    - Initial dose: 40 mcg/kg once daily. 
    - Titration: If no improvement in pruritus is observed after 3 months, the dose may be increased in 40 mcg/kg increments up to a maximum of 120 mcg/kg daily.
    - Maximum total daily dose: 6 mg (6000 mcg) [FDA] or 7.2 mg (7200 mcg) [TGA].
- **ALGS:** 
    - Recommended dose: 120 mcg/kg once daily.
    - Dose Reduction: A reduction to 40 mcg/kg daily may be considered if tolerability issues occur.

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules may be swallowed whole with a glass of water.
- **Sprinkling Method:** For patients unable to swallow whole, capsules/pellet shells may be opened and the contents sprinkled onto a small amount (approx. 30 mL) of soft food (e.g., apple sauce, oatmeal, yogurt) at or below room temperature. Alternatively, contents may be mixed in 5 mL of liquid (e.g., water, formula, breast milk) and administered via an oral syringe. 
- **Important:** Do not crush or chew the pellets or capsules. Do not administer liquid mixtures via a "sippy cup" or bottle as pellets may block the opening.

### Missed Dose
- Take the missed dose as soon as possible, but do not exceed one dose per day.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to odevixibat or any of the excipients [TGA].
- Patients with prior or active hepatic decompensation events, such as variceal hemorrhage, ascites, or hepatic encephalopathy [FDA].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Odevixibat is associated with potential drug-induced liver injury (DILI). Obtain baseline liver function tests (LFTs) (ALT, AST, Bilirubin, INR) prior to initiation. Monitor frequently for the first 6–8 months, then as clinically indicated.
    - *Management:* Consider dose reduction or interruption if LFT abnormalities occur. Permanently discontinue if hepatic decompensation occurs or if LFT abnormalities are persistent or recurrent.
- **Diarrhoea:** Common adverse reaction; may lead to dehydration. Monitor hydration status. Treatment interruption or discontinuation may be required for persistent diarrhoea.
- **Fat-Soluble Vitamin (FSV) Deficiency:** Odevixibat may interfere with the absorption of Vitamins A, D, E, and K (monitored via INR). Obtain baseline levels and monitor during treatment. Supplement as necessary. Discontinue if deficiency worsens despite supplementation.
- **Bone Fractures:** Observed in post-marketing studies (approx. 4–5% of patients). Monitor FSV levels and consider treatment interruption if a fracture occurs [FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally similar between PFIC and ALGS populations.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea, abdominal pain, vomiting, pyrexia, increased blood bilirubin, Vitamin D deficiency |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Increased ALT/AST, hepatomegaly, Vitamin E deficiency, hematoma [FDA], decreased weight [FDA] |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Drug-induced liver injury (DILI), including cases requiring liver transplantation [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Bile Acid Binding Resins:** Cholestyramine, colesevelam, or colestipol may bind odevixibat in the gut, reducing efficacy. Administer odevixibat at least 4 hours before or 4 hours after these agents.
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Inhibitors:** Odevixibat is a P-gp substrate. Co-administration with strong P-gp inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole) increases odevixibat exposure by 50–60%, though this is not considered clinically relevant.
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Odevixibat may slightly decrease midazolam exposure (approx. 30%), but this is not considered clinically significant.
- **Lipophilic Oral Contraceptives:** While an interaction study with ethinyl estradiol/levonorgestrel showed no clinically relevant impact, an effect on other lipophilic medicinal products cannot be excluded [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **TGA Category D:** There is a risk of cardiac malformations (ventricular diverticulum, small ventricle, dilated aortic arch) based on animal studies in rabbits at clinically relevant exposures. Use is not recommended during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception.
    - **FDA Recommendation:** Monitor for FSV deficiency in pregnant patients as it may impact fetal growth and development.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if odevixibat is excreted in human milk. Systemic absorption is minimal (<1%), making significant infant exposure unlikely, but the risk of FSV malabsorption in the infant cannot be excluded.
- **Fertility:** No human data; no impact on fertility observed in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established from 3 months [FDA] or 6 months [TGA] in PFIC, and from 6 months [TGA] or 12 months [FDA] in ALGS.
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data; PFIC and ALGS are primarily paediatric/young adult diseases.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required due to minimal systemic absorption and negligible renal excretion.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - No adjustment for mild or moderate impairment (Child-Pugh A or B).
    - Avoid in patients with hepatic decompensation [FDA]. 
    - Not studied in severe impairment (Child-Pugh C); if used, frequent liver monitoring is warranted [TGA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Odevixibat is a reversible, selective inhibitor of the IBAT. It acts locally in the distal ileum to inhibit the reuptake of bile acids, increasing their clearance via the colon and reducing serum bile acid levels.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Minimal (<1%). Peak plasma concentrations reached in 1–5 hours.
    - **Distribution:** >99% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Minimally metabolised in humans via mono-hydroxylation.
    - **Elimination:** Primary excretion is via faeces (82.9%), with >97% as unchanged drug. Less than 0.002% is excreted in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PFIC (Trial 1/A4250-005):** A 24-week, double-blind trial in 62 patients. Odevixibat demonstrated a significant reduction in serum bile acids and a higher proportion of positive pruritus assessments (43.5% at 40 mcg/kg and 21.1% at 120 mcg/kg) compared to placebo (0%).
- **ALGS (Trial 3/A4250-012):** A 24-week trial in 52 patients. Odevixibat 120 mcg/kg showed a statistically significant reduction in scratching scores (LS mean change -1.69) versus placebo (-0.80) and a significant reduction in serum bile acids.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [TGA]. FDA specifies 20°C to 25°C with excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
- **Protection:** Keep in original container to protect from light.
- **Container:** HDPE bottle with child-resistant closure.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Poison Schedule 4 (S4) – Prescription Only Medicine. Subject to additional monitoring (Black Triangle).
- **United States:** Prescription Only (Rx-only).
