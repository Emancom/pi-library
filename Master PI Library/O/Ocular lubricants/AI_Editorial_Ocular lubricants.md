# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **US (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any specific region.
- **Note**: While the context provided by the user mentioned an Australian source for ocular lubricants, the primary source material provided for analysis is exclusively the US PI for **Levocarnitine Injection**. Consequently, this editorial reflects data from the US regulatory perspective. Information for other jurisdictions is stated as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine, USP (INN: Levocarnitine). 
- **Chemical Class**: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. A naturally occurring carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s)**: Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources (Typically A16AA01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - **Injection**: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders**:
    - **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose**: Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency**: Every 3 or 4 hours; should not be administered less frequently than every 6 hours.
    - **Dose Range**: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg or as clinically required.
    - **Maximum Dose**: 300 mg/kg has been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
    - **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration Timing**: Administer as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustments**: Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions manifest within minutes of intravenous administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before any attempt to re-administer [US].
- **Seizure Activity**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring**: 
    - **Metabolic Patients**: Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended.
    - **ESRD Patients**: Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels. Normal range is approximately 40–50 µmol/L.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Commonly Reported (>5% in ESRD trials)**:
    - **General**: Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), headache (3–37%), chest pain (6–15%), flu syndrome (15–29%), fever (5–12%).
    - **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
    - **Gastrointestinal**: Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%).
    - **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Anaphylaxis and other severe hypersensitivity reactions; seizures [US].
- **Other**: Body odor and gastritis have been reported less frequently.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Management**: Monitor INR levels regularly after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Classification**: Category Not explicitly lettered, but documentation states reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant of excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3.
- **Elderly Use**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment**: 
    - **ESRD**: Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). 
    - **Dialysis**: Levocarnitine is easily removed by hemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: In ESRD, it increases plasma carnitine concentrations and may alleviate metabolic abnormalities associated with carnitine loss during dialysis.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination**: Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine over 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis showed that trough levels increased from ~20 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging**: Studies at 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection**: Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling**: Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
- **Stability**: Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification**:
    - **US**: Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **Poison Schedule**: Not specified for Australia/other regions in available sources.
