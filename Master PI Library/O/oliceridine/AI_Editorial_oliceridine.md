# Editorial Product Information: Oliceridine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of oliceridine, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Oliceridine (as oliceridine fumarate).
- **Chemical Class:** Opioid agonist; Relatively selective for the mu-opioid receptor.
- **Brand Name:** Olinvyk [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Injection (clear, colorless, sterile, preservative-free solution) [US]:
        - 1 mg/mL (single-dose 2 mL vial) [US].
        - 2 mg/2 mL (single-dose 2 mL vial) [US].
        - 30 mg/30 mL (single-patient-use vial, for Patient-Controlled Analgesia [PCA] only) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Trevena, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Pain Management:** Indicated in adults for the management of acute pain severe enough to require an intravenous opioid analgesic and for whom alternative treatment options (e.g., non-opioid analgesics) are inadequate or not tolerated [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Due to risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, oliceridine should be reserved for patients where alternative treatments are insufficient. Use beyond 48 hours has not been studied in controlled clinical trials [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Instructions
- **Cumulative Daily Limit:** The cumulative total daily dose must not exceed **27 mg** [US]. 
- **Prescribing Requirements:** Should only be prescribed by clinicians knowledgeable in the use of opioids and the mitigation of associated risks [US].
- **Dosing Individualisation:** Titrate to the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration required [US].

### Adult Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** 1.5 mg administered by a healthcare provider [US].
- **Supplemental Doses:** 0.75 mg may be administered beginning 1 hour after the initial dose, and hourly thereafter as needed [US].
- **Patient-Controlled Analgesia (PCA):**
    - Initial dose: 1.5 mg [US].
    - Demand dose: 0.35 mg (recommended) or 0.5 mg (if the benefit outweighs risks) [US].
    - Lockout interval: 6 minutes [US].

### Discontinuation and Tapering
- In physically dependent patients, do not abruptly discontinue oliceridine. Gradually taper the dose to prevent withdrawal symptoms [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Significant respiratory depression [US].
- Acute or severe bronchial asthma in an unmonitored setting or in the absence of resuscitative equipment [US].
- Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
- Known hypersensitivity to oliceridine (e.g., anaphylaxis) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Risk of opioid use disorder, which can lead to overdose and death. Assess risk prior to prescribing and monitor regularly.
- **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal cases may occur; monitoring is essential, particularly during initiation and dose escalation.
- **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Prolonged use during pregnancy can result in life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate.
- **Cytochrome P450 Interaction:** Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers (or discontinuation of inducers) may result in fatal overdose or withdrawal.
- **Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or Other CNS Depressants:** May result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

### General Warnings
- **QT Prolongation:** Daily doses exceeding 27 mg increase the risk of QTc interval prolongation. Do not exceed this cumulative limit [US].
- **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH) and Allodynia:** May occur; if suspected, consider dose reduction or opioid rotation [US].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, typically after >1 month. Monitor for non-specific symptoms (nausea, fatigue, hypotension) [US].
- **Severe Hypotension:** Use with caution in patients with compromised blood volume or concurrent CNS depressant use. Avoid in patients with circulatory shock [US].
- **Increased Intracranial Pressure (ICP):** Monitor for sedation and respiratory depression in patients with head injury or brain tumors [US].
- **Seizure Disorders:** May increase seizure frequency [US].
- **Sphincter of Oddi Spasm:** May increase serum amylase; monitor patients with biliary tract disease [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data [US]
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea, vomiting, dizziness, headache, constipation, pruritus, and hypoxia.
- **Common (1–10%):** Somnolence, sedation, oxygen saturation decreased, hypoventilation, hot flush, and hyperhidrosis.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Discontinuation due to QT prolongation, hypotension, or alanine aminotransferase (ALT) increased.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Life-threatening respiratory depression.
- Serotonin syndrome (when used with serotonergic drugs).
- Anaphylaxis.
- Severe hypotension and syncope.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions [US]
- **CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** May increase oliceridine plasma concentrations, prolonging opioid effects and increasing respiratory depression risk. Less frequent dosing may be required.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** May decrease oliceridine levels, leading to reduced efficacy or withdrawal.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions [US]
- **CNS Depressants (including Alcohol/Benzodiazepines):** Additive risk of respiratory depression and profound sedation.
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Risk of serotonin syndrome. Monitor closely if combined with SSRIs, SNRIs, or MAOIs.
- **Mixed Agonist/Antagonist and Partial Agonist Opioids:** (e.g., buprenorphine, nalbuphine) Avoid use as they may reduce analgesic effect or precipitate withdrawal.
- **Muscle Relaxants:** Oliceridine may enhance neuromuscular blocking action.
- **Anticholinergic Drugs:** Increased risk of urinary retention and severe constipation/paralytic ileus.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No category assigned; narrative risk provided]. Prolonged use may cause NOWS. Animal data suggests oliceridine may reduce litter size and pup survival at clinically relevant exposures [US].
- **Labor and Delivery:** Not recommended immediately prior to or during labor. Monitor neonates for respiratory depression if exposed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk is unknown. Monitor infants for excess sedation and respiratory depression [US].
- **Fertility:** Chronic opioid use may cause reduced fertility in both sexes. Animal data showed prolonged estrous cycles and decreased implantations [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in patients <18 years [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Start at the low end of the dosing range; titrate slowly. Increased risk of respiratory depression [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required even in end-stage renal disease [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - Mild/Moderate: No initial dose adjustment; however, less frequent dosing may be needed [US].
    - Severe: Consider reducing initial dose and monitor closely [US].
- **Poor CYP2D6 Metabolizers:** May require less frequent dosing due to potentially 2-fold higher plasma concentrations [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Mu-opioid receptor (MOR) agonist. While it is a full agonist, the precise mechanism of analgesia remains unknown [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - Onset of action: Median 1–3 minutes [US].
    - CNS: Respiratory depression and miosis [US].
    - GI: Reduced motility, sphincter of Oddi spasm [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution 90–120 L; 77% protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily CYP3A4 and CYP2D6, with minor contributions from CYP2C9 and CYP2C19 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Majority (70%) excreted in urine as inactive metabolites. Parent drug half-life is 1.3–3 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Orthopedic Surgery (Bunionectomy):** A 48-hour study showed statistically significant pain relief (SPID-48) with 0.35 mg and 0.5 mg demand doses compared to placebo [US].
- **Plastic Surgery (Abdominoplasty):** A 24-hour study confirmed efficacy (SPID-24) for 0.35 mg and 0.5 mg demand doses compared to placebo [US].
- **Open-Label Safety Study:** Included 768 patients; confirmed the safety profile across various surgical procedures, including colorectal and cardiothoracic surgeries [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light and freezing [US].
