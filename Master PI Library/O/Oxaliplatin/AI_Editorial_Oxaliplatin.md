# Editorial Product Information: Oxaliplatin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Oxaliplatin for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s latest PI for specific prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Oxaliplatin, a platinum-based antineoplastic agent.
- **Chemical Class:** Diethylenetriamine platinum derivative (specifically a 1,2-diaminocyclohexane [DACH] complex).
- **Brand Names:** DBL Oxaliplatin Concentrate [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XA03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Concentrate for intravenous infusion: 50 mg/10 mL and 100 mg/20 mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Adjuvant Treatment:** Indicated for stage III (Duke’s C) colon cancer in combination with fluorouracil (FU) and folinic acid (FA) following complete resection of the primary tumour [Australian].
- **Advanced Colorectal Cancer:** Treatment of advanced colorectal cancer in combination with FU and FA [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended dose for both adjuvant and advanced settings is **85 mg/m²** intravenously, repeated every two weeks [Australian].
- **Duration (Adjuvant):** 12 cycles (6 months) is the recommended duration [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Do NOT** use materials containing aluminium during preparation or administration.
    - **Do NOT** mix with sodium chloride or other chloride-containing solutions.
    - Dilute only with 5% glucose (250–500 mL).
    - Infuse over 2 to 6 hours.
    - **Oxaliplatin must precede the administration of fluorouracil.**
    - Prehydration is not required [Australian].
- **Dosage Modification:**
    - **Neurological Toxicity:** If acute dysaesthesia occurs, increase infusion time to 6 hours. For Grade 2 persistent sensory loss, reduce dose by 25%. For Grade 3, discontinue treatment [Australian].
    - **Haematological Toxicity:** Delay next cycle if neutrophils < 1.5 x 10⁹/L or platelets < 75 x 10⁹/L. Reduce dose by 20% in subsequent cycles [Australian].
    - **Gastrointestinal Toxicity:** For Grade 3–4 reactions, delay treatment until resolution and reduce dose by 20% [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known history of hypersensitivity to oxaliplatin, other platinum compounds, or excipients [Australian].
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding [Australian].
- Baseline myelosuppression (neutrophils < 1.5 x 10⁹/L or platelets < 75 x 10⁹/L) prior to the first course [Australian].
- Peripheral sensory neuropathy with functional impairment prior to the first course [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Warning:** Must be administered under the supervision of an experienced clinical oncologist [Australian].
- **Neurological Toxicity:** Dose-limiting. Includes acute pharyngolaryngeal dysaesthesia (triggered by cold) and persistent peripheral sensory neuropathy. Patients should avoid cold food, drinks, and exposure to cold air during or within 48 hours of infusion [Australian].
- **Allergic Reactions:** Can occur during any cycle and may be fatal. Rechallenge is contraindicated after an anaphylactic-type reaction [Australian].
- **Reversible Posterior Leukoencephalopathy Syndrome (RPLS/PRES):** Rare neurological condition requiring MRI confirmation; treatment must be discontinued if diagnosed [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** Associated with fatal pulmonary fibrosis. Discontinue for unexplained respiratory symptoms until interstitial lung disease is ruled out [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Risk of liver sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (VOD). Monitor for portal hypertension or abnormal liver function tests [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** Caution in patients with a history of QT prolongation or those taking medications that prolong the QT interval [Australian].
- **Extravasation:** Not a potent vesicant, but may cause severe local pain and inflammation [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **Haematological:** Anaemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, leucopenia.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, stomatitis, abdominal pain, constipation, anorexia.
    - **Neurological:** Peripheral sensory neuropathy, dysgeusia, headache.
    - **General:** Fatigue, fever, asthenia, rigors, injection site reactions.
    - **Metabolic:** Hyperglycaemia, hypokalaemia, increased transaminases.
- **Common (1–10%):** Dyspepsia, gastrointestinal haemorrhage, hiccups, deep vein thrombosis, hypertension, conjunctivitis, exfoliative rash.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Sepsis and neutropenic sepsis (potentially fatal).
    - Haemolytic-Uraemic Syndrome (HUS).
    - Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC).
    - Acute Coronary Syndrome and cardiac arrhythmias.
    - Rhabdomyolysis.
    - Intestinal ischaemia and duodenal ulceration.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Fluorouracil (FU):** No interaction observed at 85 mg/m² oxaliplatin; however, high-dose oxaliplatin (130 mg/m²) may increase FU plasma levels by 20% [Australian].
- **Plasma Protein Binding:** No significant displacement by erythromycin, salicylates, granisetron, or paclitaxel *in vitro*.
- **Cytochrome P450:** Oxaliplatin does not appear to interact with the P450 system.
- **Pharmacodynamic:** Caution when co-administered with drugs known to cause QT prolongation or rhabdomyolysis [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Probable fetal toxicity. Females of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for 9 months after treatment. Males with female partners must use contraception for 6 months after the last dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated during therapy and for 3 months following the final dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show testicular degeneration and hypoplasia. Potential for irreversible anti-fertility effects in males; sperm conservation should be discussed [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and efficacy not established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment required for patients > 65 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild:** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate:** Monitor closely; initial treatment at the recommended dose.
    - **Severe (CrCl < 30 mL/min):** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in severe impairment; no dose adjustment required for mild-to-moderate dysfunction [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Cytotoxic agent that undergoes non-enzymatic biotransformation to form active aqua-derivatives. These interact with DNA to create inter- and intra-strand cross-links, disrupting DNA synthesis.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits synergistic activity with fluorouracil both *in vitro* and *in vivo*.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributed into tissues; 85% of platinum is removed from systemic circulation by the end of a 2-hour infusion. Irreversibly binds to red blood cells and albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive non-enzymatic biotransformation.
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal (54% recovered in urine by day 5). Clearance is significantly reduced in renal impairment [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MOSAIC Study (Adjuvant):** FOLFOX4 (oxaliplatin/FU/FA) demonstrated significantly better 4-year disease-free survival (75.9%) compared to FU/FA alone (69.1%) in stage III colon cancer patients [Australian].
- **EFC7462/N9741 (Advanced):** In previously untreated advanced colorectal cancer, FOLFOX4 showed a significantly longer time to progression (8.7 vs 6.9 months) and median survival (19.4 vs 14.6 months) compared to IFL (irinotecan/FU/FA) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Concentrate:** Store below 25°C and protect from light.
- **Diluted Infusion:** Microbiological stability indicates use as soon as possible. If storage is required, hold at 2°C to 8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
