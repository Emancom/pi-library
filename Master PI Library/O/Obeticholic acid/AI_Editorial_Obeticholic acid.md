# Editorial Product Information: Obeticholic acid

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesised from official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines and the full, current PI before making prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Obeticholic acid; a 6-ethyl derivative of the human bile acid chenodeoxycholic acid.
- **Chemical Class:** Selective farnesoid X receptor (FXR) agonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **OCALIVA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A05AA04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 5 mg film-coated tablets (yellow, round).
    - 10 mg film-coated tablets (yellow, triangular).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amdipharm Mercury (Australia) Pty Ltd, an ADVANZ PHARMA company [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC):** Indicated for the treatment of PBC in adults:
    - In combination with ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) for patients with an inadequate response to UDCA.
    - As monotherapy for patients unable to tolerate UDCA [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):**
    - **Initial Dosage:** 5 mg once daily for the first 6 months.
    - **Titration:** After 6 months, if the patient has not achieved adequate reduction in alkaline phosphatase (ALP) and/or total bilirubin, and is tolerating the drug, increase to a maximum of 10 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Pre-treatment Assessment:** Prior to initiation, healthcare professionals must confirm the patient does not have decompensated cirrhosis, a prior decompensation event, or compensated cirrhosis with evidence of portal hypertension, as the drug is contraindicated in these populations [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be taken orally with or without food [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Management of Severe Pruritus:**
    - Addition of antihistamines or bile acid binding resins.
    - **Dose Reduction:** 5 mg every other day (if intolerant to 5 mg daily) or 5 mg once daily (if intolerant to 10 mg daily).
    - **Dose Interruption:** Temporary cessation for up to 2 weeks, followed by restarting at a lower dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to obeticholic acid or any excipients.
- Decompensated cirrhosis (e.g., Child-Pugh Class B or C) or a prior hepatic decompensation event.
- Compensated cirrhosis with evidence of portal hypertension (e.g., ascites, gastroesophageal varices, persistent thrombocytopenia).
- Complete biliary obstruction [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [Australian]: Hepatic Decompensation and Failure.** Hepatic failure, sometimes fatal, has been reported in PBC patients with compensated or decompensated cirrhosis. The drug must be permanently discontinued if there is laboratory or clinical evidence of hepatic decompensation, new evidence of portal hypertension, or clinically significant hepatic adverse reactions.
- **Hepatic Monitoring:** Liver tests (ALT, AST, ALP, total bilirubin, INR, albumin, and platelets) should be performed before initiation, within the first 6 months of treatment, and as clinically indicated thereafter.
- **Suspected Liver Injury:** Treatment should be promptly interrupted if liver injury is suspected (anorexia, jaundice, right upper abdominal discomfort). Treatment may only resume if an alternative cause is identified.
- **Severe Pruritus:** Clinical evaluation is required for new-onset or worsening severe pruritus.
- **Reduction in HDL-C:** Dose-dependent reductions in high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) have been observed. Lipid levels should be monitored; if response is inadequate after one year at the maximum tolerated dose, the risks of continuing treatment against the benefit should be weighed [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on a phase III clinical study [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Pruritus (63%), Fatigue (22%), Abdominal pain and discomfort |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Thyroid function abnormality, Dizziness, Palpitations, Oropharyngeal pain, Constipation, Eczema, Rash, Arthralgia, Oedema peripheral, Pyrexia |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Hepatic failure, cirrhosis, and jaundice have been reported, particularly in patients with progressive liver disease or when dosed more frequently than recommended [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Pruritus was the most common cause for treatment discontinuation (11% in 10 mg arm vs 1% in titration arm) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Obeticholic acid may decrease the International Normalised Ratio (INR). Monitor INR and adjust warfarin dose as necessary.
- **CYP1A2 Substrates:** May increase exposure to CYP1A2 substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., theophylline, tizanidine). Monitoring is recommended.
- **Bile Acid Binding Resins:** These agents (e.g., cholestyramine, colestipol) adsorb obeticholic acid, reducing its efficacy. Administer obeticholic acid at least 4–6 hours before or after a resin.
- **BSEP Inhibitors:** Avoid concomitant use with inhibitors of the bile salt efflux pump (e.g., cyclosporine), as this may exacerbate the accumulation of conjugated bile salts in the liver [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B1 [Australian].**
    - Human data are insufficient to inform drug-associated risks. Animal studies showed no developmental abnormalities at exposures significantly higher than the human maximum recommended dose (MRHD).
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. A risk-benefit assessment considering the benefits of breastfeeding for the child and the mother's clinical need for therapy is required [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies did not indicate alterations in male or female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No relevant use established for PBC in this population. No pharmacokinetic studies have been performed in patients under 18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** Limited data; no dose adjustment currently required, though data are insufficient for patients aged 75 and older [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Modest increases (1.6 to 2.5-fold) in exposure observed across all stages of renal impairment; however, this is not considered clinically meaningful. No dose adjustment required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh Class A):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate to Severe (Child-Pugh Class B or C):** Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A selective and potent agonist for the farnesoid X receptor (FXR). FXR activation suppresses *de novo* synthesis of bile acids from cholesterol and increases bile acid transport out of hepatocytes, reducing hepatic exposure to toxic bile acid levels.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces markers of inflammation and apoptosis (CRP, IgM, CK18) in PBC patients.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ of ~2 hours. A high-fat meal increases AUC by ~20% but does not significantly affect $C_{max}$.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is >99%. Volume of distribution is 618 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Conjugated with glycine or taurine in the liver and secreted into bile. These conjugates are the primary active forms and undergo enterohepatic recirculation.
    - **Elimination:** >87% excreted in faeces; <3% excreted in urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **POISE Trial:** A 12-month phase III study (N=216) evaluated Ocaliva (10 mg or titration from 5 to 10 mg) versus placebo in PBC patients.
- **Primary Endpoint:** Achievement of ALP <1.67x upper limit of normal (ULN) with a decrease of at least 15%, and total bilirubin within the normal range.
- **Outcomes:** 
    - 10 mg arm: 48% achieved the endpoint at 12 months.
    - Titration arm: 46% achieved the endpoint at 12 months.
    - Placebo arm: 10% achieved the endpoint at 12 months.
    - Significant reductions in ALP were noted as early as week 2 [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Packaging:** High-density polyethylene (HDPE) bottles with child-resistant closures [Australian].
