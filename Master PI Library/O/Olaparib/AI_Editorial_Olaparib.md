# Editorial Product Information: Olaparib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based solely on the official Product Information provided by the **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulations and the most current official PI for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Olaparib (a poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **Lynparza** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XG01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 100 mg (yellow to dark yellow, oval, debossed ‘OP100’) and 150 mg (green to green/grey, oval, debossed ‘OP150’) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AstraZeneca Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Lynparza** is indicated for use in adult patients for the following:

- **Ovarian Cancer (Monotherapy):** 
    - First-line maintenance for advanced, high-grade epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer with deleterious/suspected deleterious *BRCA* mutations (germline/somatic) in response to first-line platinum chemotherapy.
    - Maintenance for platinum-sensitive relapsed high-grade epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer in response to platinum-based chemotherapy (after ≥2 courses).
- **Ovarian Cancer (Combination Therapy):**
    - In combination with bevacizumab for maintenance of advanced epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer in response to first-line platinum-based chemotherapy plus bevacizumab, provided the cancer is homologous recombination deficiency (HRD)-positive (defined by *BRCA* mutation and/or genomic instability).
- **Breast Cancer (Monotherapy):**
    - Adjuvant treatment of HER2-negative, high-risk early breast cancer with germline *BRCA* mutations (g*BRCA*m) following neoadjuvant or adjuvant chemotherapy.
    - Treatment of HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer with g*BRCA*m previously treated with chemotherapy (neoadjuvant, adjuvant, or metastatic).
- **Pancreatic Cancer (Monotherapy):**
    - Maintenance of metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma with g*BRCA*m that has not progressed after ≥16 weeks of first-line platinum chemotherapy.
- **Prostate Cancer:**
    - Monotherapy for metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) with *BRCA* mutations (germline/somatic) progressed after a new hormonal agent (NHA).
    - In combination with abiraterone and prednisone/prednisolone for mCRPC with *BRCA* mutations (germline/somatic).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 300 mg (two 150 mg tablets) taken twice daily (total daily dose 600 mg).
- **Administration:** Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not chew, crush, dissolve, or divide. May be taken with or without food.
- **Duration of Treatment:** 
    - **First-line Ovarian/HRD+ Ovarian:** Until disease progression or up to 2 years (treatment beyond 2 years may be considered if disease remains and clinical benefit is likely).
    - **Adjuvant Breast:** Up to 1 year or until disease recurrence/toxicity.
    - **Other indications:** Until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the patient should take the next normal dose at its scheduled time.
- **Dose Adjustments for Adverse Reactions:**
    - First reduction: 250 mg twice daily.
    - Second reduction: 200 mg twice daily.
- **Dose Adjustments for CYP3A Inhibitors:**
    - Strong inhibitors: Reduce olaparib to 100 mg twice daily.
    - Moderate inhibitors: Reduce olaparib to 150 mg twice daily.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of the excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haematological Toxicity:** Very common, including anaemia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia. Do not initiate until recovery from prior chemotherapy (haemoglobin, platelets, and neutrophils ≤CTCAE Grade 1). Monitor CBC at baseline and monthly for the first 12 months.
- **Myelodysplastic Syndrome/Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (MDS/AML):** Reported in ~1.5% of patients; most cases were fatal and associated with prior platinum chemotherapy or other DNA-damaging agents. Permanently discontinue olaparib if MDS/AML is confirmed.
- **Pneumonitis:** Rare but potentially fatal. Investigate new or worsening respiratory symptoms (dyspnoea, cough, fever). Discontinue if pneumonitis is confirmed.
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Reported in 8% of mCRPC patients (including 6% pulmonary embolism) vs 2.5% in control arms. Monitor for clinical signs of thrombosis and treat as medically appropriate.
- **Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI):** Post-marketing cases have been reported. Monitor hepatic function; interrupt if DILI is suspected and discontinue if confirmed.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are based on monotherapy pooled data:
- **Very Common (>10%):** Anaemia, neutropenia, leukopenia, decreased appetite, headache, dysgeusia, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, fatigue (including asthenia), cough, dyspnoea.
- **Common (1–10%):** Thrombocytopenia, lymphopenia, stomatitis, upper abdominal pain, rash, VTE, increased blood creatinine.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** MDS/AML, hypersensitivity, dermatitis, pneumonitis, increase in mean cell volume.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Angioedema, erythema nodosum, DILI.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4/5 Inhibitors:** Olaparib is primarily metabolised by CYP3A. Strong inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole, clarithromycin) and moderate inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, fluconazole, diltiazem) increase olaparib exposure and are not recommended unless the dose is reduced.
- **CYP3A4/5 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., phenytoin, rifampicin, St John’s Wort) and moderate inducers (e.g., bosentan, efavirenz) significantly decrease olaparib AUC and may diminish efficacy. Co-administration is not recommended.
- **Effect of Olaparib on Other Drugs:** 
    - **CYP3A4:** Olaparib is a weak inhibitor. Exercise caution with narrow therapeutic index substrates (e.g., simvastatin, cyclosporin, fentanyl).
    - **Transporters:** Olaparib inhibits P-gp, OATP1B1, OCT1, OCT2, OAT3, MATE1, and MATE2K. Monitor for increased toxicity of substrates (e.g., digoxin, metformin, statins).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. Olaparib is teratogenic and genotoxic. It can cause foetal harm or lethality. Advise against pregnancy during treatment.
- **Contraception:** Females of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for 6 months after the final dose. Male patients (with pregnant or fertile partners) must use condoms during and for 3 months after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established; breastfeeding is contraindicated during treatment and for 1 month following the last dose.
- **Fertility:** Human effects are unknown; subclinical effects were noted in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in children/adolescents.
- **Elderly Use:** No adjustment required in starting dose; limited data for patients ≥75 years.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild (CrCl 51–80 mL/min): No adjustment.
    - Moderate (CrCl 31–50 mL/min): Reduce dose to 200 mg twice daily.
    - Severe/ESRD (CrCl ≤30 mL/min): Not recommended (safety/PK not studied).
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B): No adjustment.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Not recommended (safety/PK not studied).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Olaparib inhibits PARP-1, PARP-2, and PARP-3. It traps PARP on DNA single-strand breaks, preventing repair and leading to double-strand breaks. In cells with HRD (e.g., *BRCA* mutations), these breaks cannot be repaired, resulting in "synthetic lethality."
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak plasma concentrations in ~1.5 hours. Food slows rate but not extent of absorption.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding ~91%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4/5.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life ~15 hours. ~44% excreted in urine, ~42% in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SOLO-1:** Demonstrated significant PFS benefit in first-line maintenance *BRCA*m ovarian cancer (HR 0.30).
- **SOLO-2:** Maintenance in relapsed g*BRCA*m ovarian cancer; PFS 19.1 months (olaparib) vs 5.5 months (placebo).
- **PAOLA-1:** Benefit of olaparib + bevacizumab in HRD+ ovarian cancer.
- **OlympiA:** 1-year adjuvant treatment in g*BRCA*m HER2-negative breast cancer showed significant IDFS benefit.
- **PROfound/PROpel:** Significant improvements in rPFS for mCRPC patients with *BRCA* mutations.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C in the original container to protect from moisture.
