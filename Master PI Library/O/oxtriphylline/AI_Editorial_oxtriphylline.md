# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following information is derived from official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary intended for clinical reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States regulatory documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (L-carnitine); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acids.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic treatment of patients with genetic metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Dialysis:** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

### Monitoring and General Instructions
- **Target Levels:** Plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration before administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known based on provided sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if severe reactions occur [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Significant Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in ESRD clinical trials (incidence ≥5% regardless of causality) include:
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), abdominal pain (5–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
- **Neurological/Nervous System:** Headache (3–37%), dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%), insomnia (3–6%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), asthenia (8–12%) [US].

### Other Reported Effects
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia [US].
- **Specific Reactions:** Transient nausea/vomiting and "fishy" body odor have been reported with carnitine supplementation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels at treatment initiation and following any dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not lettered but stated as "use only if clearly needed"]. Animal reproduction studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation for the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to pediatric patients. Safety and efficacy in ESRD have not been specifically detailed for the pediatric population in provided sources [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not provided in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** **Levocarnitine injection** is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, the safety/efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency, and chronic high doses of oral formulations in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), mean pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels rose from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While levocarnitine effectively increases plasma carnitine concentrations, the definitive effect on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
