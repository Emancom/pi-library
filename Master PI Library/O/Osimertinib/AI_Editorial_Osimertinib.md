# Editorial Product Information: Osimertinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **osimertinib** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and administration guidelines into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Osimertinib (as mesilate).
- **Chemical Class:** Tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI); epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Tagrisso** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EB04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Film-coated tablets in 40 mg (round, biconvex, beige, debossed ‘AZ 40’) and 80 mg (oval, biconvex, beige, debossed ‘AZ 80’) strengths [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AstraZeneca Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Osimertinib** is indicated for the following [Australian]:

**Monotherapy:**
- **Adjuvant Therapy:** Treatment following complete tumour resection in patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) whose tumours harbour activating EGFR mutations (detected by a validated test).
- **Stage III NSCLC:** Treatment of patients with locally advanced, unresectable (Stage III) NSCLC whose tumours have activating EGFR mutations and whose disease has not progressed following platinum-based chemoradiation therapy.
- **First-line Metastatic NSCLC:** Treatment of patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC with activating EGFR mutations.
- **T790M Mutation-positive NSCLC:** Treatment of patients with locally advanced or metastatic EGFR T790M mutation-positive NSCLC.

**Combination Therapy:**
- **First-line Metastatic NSCLC:** In combination with pemetrexed and platinum-based chemotherapy for patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC whose tumours have EGFR exon 19 deletions or exon 21 L858R mutations.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 80 mg once daily.
- **Duration (Adjuvant):** Treat until disease recurrence, unacceptable toxicity, or for a total of three years.
- **Duration (Advanced/Metastatic):** Treat until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed whole with water, with or without food, at approximately the same time each day. 
- **Alternative Administration:** If the patient cannot swallow, the tablet may be dispersed in 50 mL of non-carbonated water (without crushing). Stir until dispersed and swallow immediately; rinse with an additional half-glass of water to ensure no residue remains.
- **Nasogastric (NG) Tube:** Disperse in 15 mL of water, followed by 15 mL rinses. Administer within 30 minutes of preparation [Australian].

### Dose Adjustments
If dose reduction is required due to adverse drug reactions, reduce to 40 mg once daily. Specific modifications for toxicities:
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** Permanently discontinue.
- **QTc Interval >500 msec:** Withhold until QTc <481 msec or baseline, then resume at 40 mg.
- **QTc Prolongation with Serious Arrhythmia:** Permanently discontinue.
- **Grade 3 or Higher Adverse Reactions:** Withhold for up to 3 weeks. If improved to Grade 0-2, resume at 80 mg or 40 mg. If not improved within 3 weeks, permanently discontinue.

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, it should be taken unless the next scheduled dose is due within 12 hours.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **osimertinib** or any of the excipients (e.g., mannitol, microcrystalline cellulose, sodium stearyl fumarate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Reported in 4% of monotherapy patients; can be fatal. Incidence is significantly higher in patients of Japanese ethnicity (11.2%). Withhold treatment for any new or worsening respiratory symptoms (dyspnoea, cough, fever) [Australian].
- **Radiation Pneumonitis:** High incidence (48%) observed in patients treated after definitive platinum-based chemoradiation. Monitor closely [Australian].
- **QTc Interval Prolongation:** Avoid in patients with congenital long QT syndrome. Monitor ECGs and electrolytes in patients with heart failure or those taking QTc-prolonging medications.
- **Cardiac Contractility:** Decreases in Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF) ≥10 percentage points to <50% have occurred. Assess LVEF at baseline and every 12 weeks during treatment in at risk patients.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Cases of Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) have been reported. Discontinue permanently if confirmed.
- **Aplastic Anaemia:** Rare but potentially fatal. Monitor for signs of bone marrow failure (fever, bruising, pallor).
- **Keratitis:** Patients with acute or worsening eye inflammation should be referred to an ophthalmologist.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally Grade 1 or 2. Frequencies from pooled monotherapy data (n=1813) include [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea (47%), Rash (46%), Paronychia (34%), Dry skin (32%), Stomatitis (24%), Fatigue (21%), Pruritus (17%), Decreased Leukocytes/Lymphocytes/Platelets/Neutrophils |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Epistaxis, Interstitial Lung Disease (4%), Alopecia, Urticaria, Palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia, Increased Blood Creatinine, QTc prolongation |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Keratitis (0.6%), Erythema multiforme |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Aplastic anaemia (0.06%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Pneumonia, ILD, and PE.
- **Post-marketing:** Cutaneous vasculitis, myositis, rhabdomyolysis, and eosinophilic pneumonia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St John’s Wort). Avoid concomitant use. If unavoidable, increase **osimertinib** dose to 160 mg; resume 80 mg three weeks after stopping the inducer [Australian].
- **BCRP and P-gp Substrates:** **Osimertinib** may increase exposure of substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran, rosuvastatin). Monitor for toxicity.
- **Gastric Acid Reducing Agents:** No clinically significant interaction with omeprazole or pH-modifying agents; no restrictions required.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Based on mechanism of action, **osimertinib** may cause foetal harm. Animal studies showed embryolethality and reduced foetal weight.
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during and for at least 6 weeks after the final dose. Males should use contraception for 4 months after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment due to potential excretion in milk and serious adverse reactions in infants.
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggest potential impairment of male and female fertility (testicular degeneration, anoestrus); findings in ovaries and testes were reversible [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required. Patients ≥65 years reported higher rates of Grade 3+ adverse events and dose modifications compared to younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild, moderate, or severe impairment. Use with caution in patients with CrCl <15 mL/min or those on dialysis (limited data).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B) impairment. Not recommended for use in severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Irreversible inhibitor of EGFRs harbouring sensitising mutations (L858R, del19) and the T790M resistance mutation. It spares wild-type EGFR activity significantly more than earlier TKIs.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability 70%; median $T_{max}$ 6 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Extensive tissue distribution (Vss 918 L). 95% protein bound. Penetrates the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Predominantly via CYP3A4 and CYP3A5. Active metabolites include AZ7550 and AZ5104.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life ~44 hours. Primarily excreted in faeces (68%) and urine (14%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ADAURA (Adjuvant):** Demonstrated significant DFS benefit (HR 0.17 in Stage II-IIIA) and OS benefit (HR 0.49) vs. placebo.
- **LAURA (Stage III):** Significant PFS improvement (HR 0.16; median 39.1 vs 5.6 months) in patients following chemoradiation.
- **FLAURA (First-line):** Significant PFS (HR 0.46) and OS (HR 0.80) benefit vs. gefitinib or erlotinib.
- **AURA3 (T790M+):** Significant PFS improvement (HR 0.30) vs. platinum-pemetrexed chemotherapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C [Australian].
- Shelf life is specified on the secondary packaging.
