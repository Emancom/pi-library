# Editorial Product Information: Oxybuprocaine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**. No documents from other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian) were provided for this synthesis.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Oxybuprocaine hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Local anaesthetic of the ester type.
- **Brand Name(s):** Minims Oxybuprocaine Hydrochloride [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** S01BA02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Single-use, preservative-free eye drops, solution containing 0.4% (4 mg/mL) oxybuprocaine hydrochloride [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Ophthalmic (topical).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bausch & Lomb (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Indicated for the production of local anaesthesia of the eye during short ophthalmological procedures [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Tonometry:** One drop of 0.4% solution is sufficient; anaesthesia is achieved after approximately one minute. 
- **Contact Lens Fitting:** One drop followed by a second drop after 90 seconds.
- **Removal of Foreign Bodies or Minor Surgery:** Three to six drops are suggested.
- **Pterygium Surgery:** One drop per minute for 10 minutes.

### Administration Instructions
- **Clinician Administration Only:** This medication must be administered by a healthcare professional and is not for patient self-instillation [Australian].
- **Deep Anaesthesia:** To achieve deeper effects, additional drops may be instilled at intervals of no less than 90 seconds.
- **Systemic Absorption Reduction:** To minimise systemic exposure, clinicians should compress the lacrimal sac at the medial canthus for one minute during and immediately following instillation.
- **Single Use:** Each unit is for single-use only and must be discarded immediately after use.

### Recovery
- Corneal sensitivity typically returns to normal after approximately one hour.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to ester-type local anaesthetics or any component of the formulation [Australian].
- Presence of a concomitant infection in the eye [Australian].
- Patient self-administration [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Duration of Use:** Oxybuprocaine must not be used for prolonged periods. Chronic or frequent use may result in severe corneal damage, keratitis, and the development of acquired tolerance [Australian].
- **Corneal Protection:** The anaesthetised eye must be protected from dust and bacterial contamination until sensitivity returns.
- **Vision Impairment:** Transient blurring of vision may occur. Patients should be cautioned against driving or operating machinery until vision is clear.
- **Systemic Effects:** Although rare with topical use, systemic absorption can occur. A decline in mean arterial oxygen pressure (paO2) has been reported in rare instances involving respiratory anaesthesia.
- **Route Warning:** **NOT FOR INJECTION.** For topical ophthalmic use only.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Ocular Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Transient stinging or burning sensation upon instillation.
- **Frequency Unknown:** Eye allergy, allergic blepharitis, reductions in tear film stability, and inhibition of corneal epithelial healing.
- **Associated with Frequent/Chronic Use:** Stromal infiltration, oedema, candida keratitis, disciform keratitis, peripheral corneal ring formation, and irreversible apical cell damage to corneal endothelial cells.

### Systemic Effects
- **Immunological:** Allergic reactions (rare); anaphylactic shock (rare); hypersensitivity reactions including lung oedema [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular:** Sinus bradycardia (rare/isolated case).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Occasional nausea, vomiting, and dysphagia.
- **Central Nervous System:** Generally associated with overdose or abuse; may include sedation, confusion, agitation, euphoria, seizures, and respiratory depression.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticholinesterases:** May inhibit the metabolism of ester-type local anaesthetics, potentially prolonging the effects of oxybuprocaine [Australian].
- **Suxamethonium:** Ester-type local anaesthetics may competitively enhance the neuromuscular blocking action of suxamethonium [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category D. Safety during pregnancy has not been established. It should be used only if the physician deems it essential [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no studies establishing safety during lactation. Use only when considered essential by a physician [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No specific data provided; however, lacrimal sac compression is especially advised in children to reduce systemic absorption [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical evidence suggests that patients over 40 years of age may achieve sufficient anaesthesia for tonometry with lower concentrations (0.2%) [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data available in the provided source.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A surface/topical anaesthetic that blocks conduction in sensory, motor, and autonomic nerve fibres by reducing the excitability of nociceptors. It also affects the conducting system of the heart if absorbed systemically.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Anaesthetic activity is approximately ten times that of cocaine and twice that of tetracaine. Onset occurs in ~1 minute, with peak response between 1 and 15 minutes. Anaesthesia persists for 20 to 30 minutes.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed through mucous membranes.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by plasma esterases and, to a lesser extent, by the liver. The main metabolite is 3-butoxy-4-aminobenzoic acid (70–90%).
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 90% of an oral dose (drug and metabolites) is excreted in the urine within 9 hours.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Light:** Do not expose to strong light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
