# Editorial Product Information: Olsalazine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian** (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current documentation before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Olsalazine sodium (a salicylate derivative).
- **Brand Names:** **Dipentum** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Capsules: 250 mg [Australian].
    - Tablets: 500 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Atnahs Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Ulcerative Colitis:** Indicated for the treatment of ulcerative colitis specifically in patients who are intolerant to sulfasalazine [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Administration:** **Olsalazine** should be taken at regular intervals throughout the day, strictly after meals [Australian].
- **Dosing for Long-Term Maintenance of Remission:**
    - **Adults (including elderly):** 1 g daily, administered as 250 mg four times daily or 500 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Dosing for Acute Ulcerative Colitis:**
    - **Standard Dose:** 2 g daily in divided doses [Australian].
    - **Maximum Dose:** If required and well-tolerated, the dose may be increased to 3 g daily. Note: A single dose should not exceed 1 g [Australian].
- **Titration Schedule:** 
    - To improve compliance and gastrointestinal tolerance, gradual titration is recommended.
    - **Capsules:** Start with 250 mg on Day 1, increasing by 250 mg daily until the target dose (e.g., 2 g) is reached [Australian].
    - **Tablets:** Start with 500 mg on Day 1, increasing by 500 mg daily until the target dose is reached [Australian].
- **Bioequivalence Note:** Bioequivalence between the 250 mg capsule and 500 mg tablet has not been established. Use caution when switching formulations; tablets should not be divided [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustment for Side Effects:** If watery diarrhoea occurs during titration, reduce the dose to the previously tolerated level for three days before attempting to increase again [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to salicylates or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].
- Pathological bleeding tendencies [Australian].
- Active peptic ulcer or erosive gastritis [Australian].
- Concomitant use of anticoagulants [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Renal Monitoring:** Renal function (serum creatinine) should be monitored before treatment, every 3 months for the first year, every 6 months for the subsequent 4 years, and annually thereafter [Australian].
- **Haematological Toxicity:** Although rare, blood dyscrasias may occur. Patients should be instructed to report symptoms such as fever, sore throat, mouth ulcers, or unexplained bruising/bleeding immediately. Treatment should cease if haematotoxicity is suspected [Australian].
- **Respiratory/Allergic Conditions:** Patients with asthma or severe allergies should be monitored for exacerbation of their condition during therapy [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of both liver and kidney function is recommended, particularly in the elderly [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal (Very Common):** Approximately 17% of patients experience diarrhoea, leading to treatment withdrawal in about 6% of cases. Diarrhoea is often watery and dose-related [Australian].
- **Common Effects:** Nausea, abdominal pain, and dyspepsia [Australian].
- **Rare/Isolated Reports:**
    - **Hepatic:** Granulomatous hepatitis, non-specific reactive hepatitis, cholestatic hepatitis, and elevated liver enzymes [Australian].
    - **Renal:** Interstitial nephritis [Australian].
    - **Haematologic:** Leucopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia (including aplastic and haemolytic), and pancytopenia [Australian].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Pericarditis, myocarditis, palpitations, tachycardia, and second-degree heart block [Australian].
    - **Neurologic/Psychiatric:** Dizziness, peripheral neuropathy, tremors, insomnia, and depression [Australian].
    - **Dermatologic:** Photosensitivity, rash, alopecia, and angioneurotic oedema [Australian].
    - **Pulmonary:** Bronchospasm and interstitial lung disease [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants/Heparins:** Co-administration with low molecular weight heparins (LMWH) or heparinoids increases bleeding risk, particularly the risk of haematomas following neuraxial anaesthesia. Salicylates should ideally be discontinued prior to LMWH initiation [Australian]. Increased prothrombin time has been reported with concurrent **Warfarin** [Australian].
- **Thiopurines:** Concurrent use of **6-mercaptopurine** or **thioguanine** increases the risk of myelosuppression. Use the lowest possible doses and monitor blood counts (especially for leucopenia) closely [Australian].
- **Varicella Vaccine:** Avoid salicylate use for six weeks following varicella vaccination due to the theoretical risk of Reye’s syndrome [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C:** Salicylates may increase the risk of foetal toxicity (reduced weights, retarded ossification) as seen in animal studies at 5–20 times the human dose. 
    - **Olsalazine** should only be used in pregnancy if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts of the active metabolite (5-ASA) pass into breast milk. Reports of infant diarrhoea exist. Breastfeeding should generally be discontinued if the mother is using **olsalazine**, unless the benefit of treatment outweighs the risk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in available sources [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established. Use in infants aged 2 years and under is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is mandated beyond those related to renal function. However, because renal function declines with age, regular monitoring is strongly advised [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** There is a risk of renal tubular damage from absorbed 5-ASA. In patients with pre-existing renal disease, monitoring must include urinalysis, BUN, and creatinine [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Monitoring of liver function is advised during therapy [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Olsalazine** is a prodrug consisting of two 5-aminosalicylic acid (5-ASA) molecules linked by an azo-bond. It remains intact until it reaches the colon, where bacterial azoreductases split the bond, releasing the active 5-ASA moiety. 5-ASA acts locally in the colon, though its exact mechanism in ulcerative colitis remains unknown [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 2% of an oral dose is absorbed systemically.
    - **Distribution:** Action is neither pH nor time-release dependent; >95% of the dose reaches the colon.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily converted to 5-ASA in the colon. 5-ASA is partially acetylated to acetyl-5-ASA.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 20% of the converted dose is excreted in the urine. **Olsalazine** sulfate is the major circulating metabolite at steady state [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Comparative Efficacy:** Clinical studies indicate that **olsalazine** is well-tolerated and demonstrates clinical efficacy similar to that of sulfasalazine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Capsules:** Store below 30°C in a dry place. Keep the container tightly closed/airtight [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
