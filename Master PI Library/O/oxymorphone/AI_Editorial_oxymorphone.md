# Editorial Product Information: Oxymorphone Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of oxymorphone hydrochloride for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference point.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulations and the current approved PI for their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Oxymorphone hydrochloride (an opioid agonist).
- **Chemical Class:** Phenanthrene derivative; 4, 5α-epoxy-3, 14-dihydroxy-17-methylmorphinan-6-one hydrochloride.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in provided source (Generic: Aurolife Pharma).
- **ATC Code:** N02AA03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Immediate-release oral tablets:** 5 mg and 10 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aurolife Pharma, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Pain:** Indicated for the management of acute pain severe enough to require an opioid analgesic and for which alternative treatment options (e.g., non-opioid analgesics) are inadequate or not tolerated [US-approved].
- **Limitations of Use:** Due to risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, oxymorphone should be reserved for cases where alternative treatments are insufficient.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Opioid-Naive Patients:** Initiate at a dosing range of 10 mg to 20 mg every 4 to 6 hours as needed for pain.
- **Titration:** Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration. Increase dose only if lower doses are insufficient and the benefits outweigh the risks.
- **Administration:** Must be taken on an empty stomach, at least one hour before or two hours after eating. Co-ingestion with food increases plasma concentrations [US].

### Conversion and Rotation
- **From Other Opioids:** Start with approximately half of the calculated total daily oxymorphone dose, divided into 4 to 6 doses every 4–6 hours.
- **From Parenteral Oxymorphone:** Use a 10:1 oral-to-parenteral ratio (e.g., 10 mg oral for every 1 mg parenteral) [US].

### Safe Discontinuation
- Do not abruptly discontinue in physically dependent patients. Taper by no more than 10% to 25% of the total daily dose every 2 to 4 weeks to avoid serious withdrawal symptoms or suicide risk [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Significant respiratory depression [US].
- Acute or severe bronchial asthma in unmonitored settings or without resuscitative equipment [US].
- Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
- Moderate or severe hepatic impairment [US].
- Hypersensitivity to oxymorphone or any tablet excipients (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Potential for opioid use disorder, overdose, and death. Assess risk prior to and during therapy.
- **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Monitor closely, particularly during initiation and titration.
- **Accidental Ingestion:** Even one dose can be fatal, especially to children.
- **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Prolonged use during pregnancy can be life-threatening to the neonate.
- **Interaction with Benzodiazepines/CNS Depressants:** Concomitant use with alcohol or benzodiazepines may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.
- **REMS:** Healthcare providers are encouraged to complete a REMS-compliant education program.

### Specific Precautions
- **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH) and Allodynia:** Paradoxical increase in pain despite dose increases. Consider dose reduction or opioid rotation [US].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use >1 month. Symptoms include nausea, fatigue, and hypotension.
- **Severe Hypotension:** Use with caution in patients with reduced blood volume or concurrent CNS depressant use. Avoid in circulatory shock.
- **Gastrointestinal Effects:** May cause spasm of the sphincter of Oddi and increase serum amylase. Opioid-induced esophageal dysfunction (OIED) has been reported [US].
- **Seizure Disorders:** May aggravate pre-existing seizure conditions.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported in $\ge$ 2% of patients in clinical trials for acute pain:

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency [US] |

| Nausea | 19% |
| Pyrexia | 14% |
| Somnolence | 9% |
| Vomiting | 9% |
| Pruritus | 8% |
| Headache | 7% |
| Dizziness | 7% |
| Constipation | 4% |
| Confusion | 3% |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory depression, apnea, circulatory depression, hypotension, and anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Fatal interaction. Co-ingestion significantly increases oxymorphone plasma levels. Do not consume alcohol with this medication [US].
- **Benzodiazepines and CNS Depressants:** Additive respiratory depression and sedation.
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Risk of serotonin syndrome (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, MAOIs).
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Avoid use within 14 days of MAOI therapy; may precipitate serotonin syndrome or opioid toxicity [US].
- **Mixed Agonist/Antagonist and Partial Agonists:** (e.g., buprenorphine, nalbuphine) May reduce analgesia or precipitate withdrawal.
- **Anticholinergics:** Increased risk of urinary retention and severe constipation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified under the newer labeling format, but animal data indicates reduced postnatal survival and fetal weight at doses 2.4x to 12.2x the human daily dose.
    - **Risk:** Prolonged use may cause Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Oxymorphone presence in human milk is unknown. Monitor infants for excess sedation and respiratory depression [US].
- **Fertility:** Chronic opioid use may cause reduced fertility in both sexes via effects on the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in patients 0–17 years; not recommended [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher peak plasma levels (approx. 40% higher) observed. Initiate at the low end of the range (e.g., 5 mg) and monitor for CNS/respiratory depression [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Bioavailability increases by 26% to 65% depending on severity (CrCl <80 mL/min). Initiate at 5 mg and titrate slowly [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild:** Bioavailability increases 1.6-fold. Initiate at 5 mg and monitor closely.
    - **Moderate/Severe:** Contraindicated (bioavailability increases 3.7-fold to 12.2-fold) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Full mu-opioid receptor agonist.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Analgesia, respiratory depression, miosis, reduced GI motility, and peripheral vasodilation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute oral bioavailability ~10%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via reduction or conjugation (Oxymorphone-3-glucuronide and 6-OH-oxymorphone). 
    - **Elimination:** Half-life 9–11 hours. <1% excreted unchanged in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Orthopedic Surgery:** Two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials showed 20 mg provided significantly greater pain relief over 8 hours compared to placebo.
- **Abdominal Surgery:** A multiple-dose study showed 10 mg and 20 mg doses provided superior analgesia over 48 hours compared to placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Security:** Must be stored in a secure location out of reach of children to prevent accidental ingestion or theft.
- **Disposal:** If a take-back program is unavailable, unused tablets should be flushed down the toilet [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Schedule II controlled substance (CII); Rx-only.
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
