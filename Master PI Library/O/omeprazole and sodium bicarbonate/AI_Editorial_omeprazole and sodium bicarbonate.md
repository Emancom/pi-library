# Editorial Product Information: Omeprazole and Sodium Bicarbonate

## Editorial Note
- The information in this harmonised editorial was derived from the following source:
    - **United States (FDA)**: Product Information for Omeprazole and Sodium Bicarbonate capsules (Distributed by Proficient Rx LP; Revised 02/2023).
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the prescribing information for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial overview and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only the United States PI was available for review; comparisons to other regional authorities (e.g., Australian TGA, European EMA) were not possible.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients**: Omeprazole (a substituted benzimidazole proton pump inhibitor) and Sodium Bicarbonate (an antacid).
- **Brand Names**: Zegerid (innovator); generic versions available [US].
- **ATC Code**: A02BC01 (Omeprazole); A02AH01 (Combinations) [Not explicitly in source; inferred from INN].
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - **Immediate-release capsules**: 20 mg omeprazole / 1,100 mg sodium bicarbonate.
    - **Immediate-release capsules**: 40 mg omeprazole / 1,100 mg sodium bicarbonate.
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: ScieGen Pharmaceuticals (Manufacturer); Westminster Pharmaceuticals (Distributor); Proficient Rx LP (Repackager) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Duodenal Ulcer**: Short-term treatment of active duodenal ulcer [US].
- **Gastric Ulcer**: Short-term treatment (4–8 weeks) of active benign gastric ulcer [US].
- **Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD)**:
    - Treatment of symptomatic GERD without esophageal erosions (up to 4 weeks) [US].
    - Treatment of erosive esophagitis (4–8 weeks) [US].
- **Maintenance of Healing**: Maintenance of healing of erosive esophagitis (controlled studies limited to 12 months) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults ≥18 years)
- **Active Duodenal Ulcer**: 20 mg once daily for 4 weeks. Some patients may require an additional 4 weeks [US].
- **Benign Gastric Ulcer**: 40 mg once daily for 4 to 8 weeks [US].
- **Symptomatic GERD (no erosions)**: 20 mg once daily for up to 4 weeks [US].
- **Erosive Esophagitis**: 20 mg once daily for 4 to 8 weeks [US].
- **Maintenance of Healing (Erosive Esophagitis)**: 20 mg once daily [US].

### Critical Administration Instructions
- **Empty Stomach**: Must be taken at least one hour before a meal [US].
- **Capsule Integrity**: Capsules must be swallowed intact with water. Do not open the capsule to sprinkle on food; do not use liquids other than water for administration [US].
- **Non-Equivalence of Strengths**: **Two 20 mg capsules are NOT equivalent to one 40 mg capsule.** Both strengths contain the same amount of sodium bicarbonate (1,100 mg). Substituting two 20 mg capsules for one 40 mg capsule results in an excessive delivery of sodium bicarbonate (2,200 mg) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to omeprazole, substituted benzimidazoles, or any component of the formulation. Reported reactions include anaphylaxis, angioedema, bronchospasm, and urticaria [US].
- **Acute Interstitial Nephritis**: A history of PPI-induced acute interstitial nephritis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastric Malignancy**: Symptomatic response to therapy does not preclude the presence of gastric malignancy. Consider further diagnostic testing in adults with suboptimal response or early relapse [US].
- **Acute Interstitial Nephritis (AIN)**: May occur at any point during therapy; typically an idiopathic hypersensitivity reaction. Discontinue immediately if AIN develops [US].
- **Buffer Content (Sodium Bicarbonate)**:
    - Each capsule contains **304 mg (13 mEq) of sodium**. Use with caution in patients on sodium-restricted diets or those with congestive heart failure [US].
    - Contains sodium bicarbonate: use with caution in patients with Bartter’s syndrome, hypokalemia, or acid-base imbalances [US].
    - Chronic use may lead to systemic alkalosis or milk-alkali syndrome if taken with calcium/milk [US].
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD)**: PPI therapy may increase risk, especially in hospitalised patients [US].
- **Bone Fracture**: Long-term, high-dose therapy is associated with increased risk of osteoporosis-related fractures (hip, wrist, spine) [US].
- **Lupus Erythematosus**: Both cutaneous (CLE) and systemic (SLE) lupus have been reported. Discontinue if new symptoms occur and refer to a specialist [US].
- **Interaction with Clopidogrel**: Avoid concomitant use; omeprazole reduces the antiplatelet activity of clopidogrel [US].
- **Nutritional Deficiencies**: Daily long-term use (>3 years) may lead to Vitamin B-12 malabsorption. Hypomagnesemia has been reported (rarely) with prolonged treatment [US].
- **Fundic Gland Polyps**: Risk increases with long-term use (>1 year). Use the shortest duration possible [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (Incidence ≥2%)
The most frequently reported adverse effects in clinical trials include:
- Headache (6.9%)
- Abdominal pain (5.2%)
- Nausea (4.0%)
- Diarrhea (3.7%)
- Vomiting (3.2%)
- Flatulence (2.7%)

### Postmarketing and Serious Effects
- **Nervous System**: Depression, agitation, hallucinations, insomnia, paresthesia, tremors [US].
- **Hepatic**: Elevated liver enzymes; rare cases of hepatocellular or cholestatic hepatitis, liver failure, and hepatic encephalopathy [US].
- **Hematologic**: Rare reports of agranulocytosis (some fatal), pancytopenia, and hemolytic anemia [US].
- **Skin**: Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis (some fatal), and erythema multiforme [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **pH-Dependent Bioavailability**: Omeprazole increases gastric pH, which can significantly decrease the absorption of ketoconazole, atazanavir, iron salts, erlotinib, and mycophenolate mofetil. Conversely, it may increase the absorption of digoxin [US].
- **CYP450 Metabolism**: 
    - **Inhibition**: Omeprazole can prolong the elimination of diazepam, warfarin, and phenytoin. Monitor INR and prothrombin time in patients on warfarin [US].
    - **Clopidogrel**: Avoid use. Omeprazole inhibits CYP2C19, reducing the conversion of clopidogrel to its active metabolite [US].
- **Coadministration with Antiretrovirals**: 
    - **Atazanavir/Nelfinavir**: Avoid use; significant reduction in antiretroviral plasma levels [US].
    - **Saquinavir**: Plasma levels of saquinavir are increased; monitor for toxicity [US].
- **Inhibitors/Inducers of CYP2C19/3A4**:
    - **Voriconazole**: May double omeprazole exposure; usually no dose adjustment required [US].
    - **St. John’s Wort/Rifampin**: May substantially decrease omeprazole concentrations; avoid concomitant use [US].
- **Methotrexate**: PPIs may elevate and prolong serum methotrexate levels, potentially leading to toxicity. Consider temporary PPI withdrawal during high-dose methotrexate administration [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Category C [US]**: There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal data (rats/rabbits) indicate dose-related increases in embryo-lethality and changes in bone morphology at high doses. Use only if potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding**: Omeprazole is excreted in human milk (peak concentration <7% of peak serum level). Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants and tumorigenicity shown in rat studies, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility**: No effect on fertility was observed in animal studies (rats) at doses up to 33.6 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients <18 years of age [US].
- **Geriatric Use**: No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between elderly and younger subjects, though greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out. Bioavailability is increased and elimination rate is decreased in the elderly [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Bioavailability increases to ~100% and plasma half-life increases to 3 hours. Consider dose reduction, particularly for maintenance of healing of erosive esophagitis [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose reduction is necessary [US].
- **Asian Population**: AUC is approximately four-fold higher in Asian subjects compared to Caucasians. **Dose reduction should be considered**, particularly for the maintenance of healing of erosive esophagitis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor that suppresses gastric acid secretion by irreversible inhibition of the $H^+/K^+$ ATPase enzyme system at the secretory surface of the gastric parietal cell. Sodium bicarbonate is included to protect the acid-labile omeprazole from gastric degradation, allowing for immediate-release absorption [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Rapid; peak plasma levels ($T_{max}$) achieved in ~30 minutes. Absolute bioavailability is 30–40% [US].
    - **Distribution**: Protein binding is approximately 95% [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Extensively metabolised by the cytochrome P450 system (primarily CYP2C19) [US].
    - **Excretion**: ~77% eliminated in urine as metabolites; little to no unchanged drug is excreted. Plasma half-life is ~1 hour [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Duodenal Ulcer (DU)**: In a multicenter, double-blind study (n=147), omeprazole 20 mg daily achieved significantly higher healing rates at 2 weeks (41% vs 13% placebo) and 4 weeks (75% vs 27% placebo) [US].
- **Gastric Ulcer (GU)**: In a US study (n=520), omeprazole 40 mg once daily showed healing rates of 55.6% at 4 weeks and 82.7% at 8 weeks, significantly higher than placebo [US].
- **Maintenance of Erosive Esophagitis (EE)**: In a US study (n=406), omeprazole 20 mg once daily maintained endoscopic remission in 70% of patients at 6 months, compared to 11% for placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 25°C (77°F). Excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- **Handling**: Keep the container tightly closed. Protect from light and moisture [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification**: Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
- **Sodium Warning**: Contains 304 mg sodium per capsule; significant for patients on restricted intake [US].
