# Editorial Product Information: Oxaprozin

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for **oxaprozin**. It is intended as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
- This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Oxaprozin
- **Chemical Class**: Propionic acid derivative; Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID).
- **Brand Names**: Daypro (Note: Provided source refers to generic **oxaprozin** manufactured by Dr. Reddy's Laboratories).
- **ATC Code**: M01AE12.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Film-coated tablets: 600 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories Limited [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Osteoarthritis (OA)**: Relief of signs and symptoms [US-approved].
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)**: Relief of signs and symptoms [US-approved].
- **Juvenile Rheumatoid Arthritis (JRA)**: Relief of signs and symptoms in pediatric patients 6 to 16 years of age [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- Use the lowest effective dosage for the shortest duration possible consistent with patient goals.
- **Osteoarthritis**: 1,200 mg (two 600 mg tablets) once daily [US].
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis**: 1,200 mg once daily [US].
- **Individualization/Maximum Dose**: Doses may be adjusted to suit individual needs. The maximum recommended daily dose in adults is 1,800 mg or 26 mg/kg (whichever is lower), administered in divided doses [US].
- **Loading Dose**: For rapid onset of action, a one-time loading dose of 1,200 mg to 1,800 mg (not exceeding 26 mg/kg) may be employed [US].

### Pediatric Dosing (JRA)
For patients aged 6 to 16 years, once-daily dosing is based on body weight:
- **22 to 31 kg**: 600 mg [US].
- **32 to 54 kg**: 900 mg [US].
- **≥ 55 kg**: 1,200 mg [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Most patients tolerate once-daily dosing; however, divided doses may be utilized in patients who cannot tolerate single doses.
- Patients with low body weight should initiate therapy at 600 mg once daily [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to **oxaprozin** or any excipients.
- **Aspirin/NSAID Sensitivity**: History of asthma, urticaria, or other allergic-type reactions following the use of aspirin or other NSAIDs.
- **Surgical Setting**: Peri-operative use in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Cardiovascular (CV) Thrombotic Events**: NSAIDs cause an increased risk of serious CV thrombotic events, including myocardial infarction and stroke, which can be fatal. Risk may occur early and increases with treatment duration.
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Events**: NSAIDs increase the risk of serious GI adverse events including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation of the stomach or intestines, which can be fatal. Elderly patients and those with a history of peptic ulcer disease/GI bleeding are at greater risk.

### General Warnings
- **Hepatotoxicity**: Elevations in ALT/AST (3x ULN) occur in ~1% of patients. Severe hepatic injury (fulminant hepatitis, liver failure) has been reported. Discontinue if liver disease signs develop.
- **Hypertension**: NSAIDs can lead to new-onset or worsening of pre-existing hypertension. Monitor blood pressure throughout therapy.
- **Heart Failure and Edema**: Avoid use in patients with severe heart failure unless benefits outweigh risks. Monitor for worsening symptoms.
- **Renal Toxicity**: Long-term use may cause renal papillary necrosis. Monitor renal function in patients with impairment, heart failure, or dehydration.
- **Serious Skin Reactions**: May cause exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN). Discontinue at first appearance of skin rash [US].
- **DRESS**: Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms has been reported.
- **Anaphylactic Reactions**: Seek emergency help if symptoms occur.
- **Fetal Toxicity**: Avoid use starting at 30 weeks gestation (risk of premature closure of ductus arteriosus). Between 20-30 weeks, limit use to the lowest dose/duration; monitor amniotic fluid if treatment exceeds 48 hours [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (>3%) [US]
- Gastrointestinal: Constipation, diarrhea, dyspepsia, nausea.
- Dermatologic: Rash.

### Incidence >1% [US]
- **Cardiovascular**: Edema.
- **Digestive**: Abdominal pain, anorexia, flatulence, GI ulcers, gross bleeding/perforation, vomiting, elevated liver enzymes.
- **Nervous System**: CNS inhibition (depression, sedation, somnolence, confusion), dizziness, headache, sleep disturbance.
- **Hematologic**: Anemia, increased bleeding time.
- **Other**: Pruritus, tinnitus, abnormal renal function, dysuria/frequency.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Myocardial infarction, stroke, GI perforation/hemorrhage, acute renal failure, interstitial nephritis, hepatitis, agranulocytosis, and life-threatening skin reactions (SJS/TEN).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin)**: Synergistic effect on bleeding; increased risk of serious GI bleeding.
- **Antiplatelets/SSRIs/SNRIs**: Increased risk of bleeding due to inhibition of platelet aggregation.
- **ACE Inhibitors, ARBs, and Beta-blockers**: NSAIDs may diminish antihypertensive effects. In elderly or volume-depleted patients, co-administration may lead to renal deterioration or acute renal failure.
- **Diuretics**: NSAIDs may reduce the natriuretic effect of loop and thiazide diuretics.
- **Digoxin**: May increase serum concentration and prolong half-life.
- **Lithium**: Increases plasma lithium levels and reduces renal clearance.
- **Methotrexate**: Increases risk of methotrexate toxicity due to reduced clearance.
- **Cyclosporine/Pemetrexed**: Increases risk of nephrotoxicity and myelosuppression.
- **Glyburide**: Alters pharmacokinetics; monitor blood glucose during initiation.
- **Laboratory Interactions**: May cause false-positive urine immunoassay screening tests for benzodiazepines [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - [US: Not assigned a specific letter category in the current labeling, but detailed by trimester].
    - First/Second Trimester: Use only if clearly needed.
    - 20-30 Weeks: Limit use; monitor for oligohydramnios.
    - Third Trimester (≥30 weeks): Contraindicated due to risks of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus.
- **Breastfeeding**: Not known if excreted in human milk. Administer to lactating women only if clearly indicated [US].
- **Fertility**: Use of NSAIDs may delay or prevent ovulation, potentially causing reversible infertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy not established under 6 years of age [US]. Approved for JRA in patients 6-16 years.
- **Elderly Use**: Increased risk for CV, GI, and renal adverse reactions. Start at the low end of the dosing range and monitor renal function.
- **Renal Impairment**: Initiate therapy at 600 mg once daily in patients with severe impairment or those on dialysis. Avoid in advanced renal disease unless benefits outweigh risks [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Patients with well-compensated cirrhosis do not require dose reduction, but severe dysfunction requires close monitoring.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Inhibition of cyclooxygenase enzymes (COX-1 and COX-2), leading to decreased prostaglandin synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: 95% absorbed; food may reduce the rate but not the extent of absorption.
    - **Distribution**: 99% protein-bound (primarily albumin). Protein binding is saturable. Penetrates synovial tissue.
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily hepatic via microsomal oxidation (65%) and glucuronic acid conjugation (35%).
    - **Elimination**: 65% in urine, 35% in feces. Elimination half-life ranges from 41.4 to 54.9 hours in adults [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Exhibits analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and antipyretic properties.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Osteoarthritis**: Evaluation in 616 patients showed 600 mg to 1,200 mg/day was comparable to aspirin (2,600-3,200 mg/day) and piroxicam (20 mg/day) [US].
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis**: Evaluation in 646 patients showed 600 mg to 1,800 mg/day was comparable to aspirin (2,600-3,900 mg/day) with a trend toward fewer GI side effects than aspirin [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling**: Keep bottles tightly closed. Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure.
