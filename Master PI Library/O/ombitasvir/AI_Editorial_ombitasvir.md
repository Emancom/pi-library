# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of **levocarnitine injection** for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule involved in mammalian energy metabolism (amino acid derivative).
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile, clear, colorless solution for injection. 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for both acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The standard recommended dose is **50 mg/kg**. This may be administered as a slow bolus (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and not less than every 6 hours.
    - Maximum administered dose reported in clinical settings is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** 
    - Recommended starting dose: **10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg** dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments: Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect vials for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portions as the formulation lacks preservatives.

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Patients:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Perform weekly and monthly monitoring including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **ESRD Patients:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels (normal range 40 to 50 µmol/L).

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Reactions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred—often within minutes of administration. These are most frequently reported in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without a history of seizure activity. In those with pre-existing epilepsy, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### Clinical Precautions
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. In ESRD patients, chronic high-dose oral administration may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) which are normally renally excreted [US].
- **ESRD Outcomes:** While the injection effectively increases carnitine levels in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency $\ge$5% (presented as a unified group for all doses):

| Body System | Adverse Event | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction, Pain, Flu syndrome, Headache, Infection, Abdominal pain, Fever | Common (>10%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, Hypotension, Tachycardia | Common (>10%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, Vomiting, Nausea | Common (>10%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, Cough, Dyspnea, Rhinitis | Common (>10%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness | Common (>10%) |
| **Special Senses** | Amblyopia | 3–6% |

### Less Common/Postmarketing Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, body odor (transient).
- **Neurological:** Seizures.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified, but text indicates use only if clearly needed]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate, well-controlled human studies [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk. Risks to the child from excess intake must be weighed against maternal benefit; discontinuation of the drug or nursing should be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg (see Section 3). The PI does not specify distinct age-related safety profiles but includes pediatric patients under metabolic disorder indications [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis. Oral formulations should be avoided in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is essential for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly post-dialysis) showed that trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients demonstrated dose-dependent increases in pre-dialysis trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
