# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive legal and prescribing requirements. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP), also known as L-carnitine. It is a carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondrial matrix.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection containing 1 g of levocarnitine per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration Instructions:** Administered intravenously as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
- **Acute Management:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered after each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Initiation of therapy may be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine concentrations (<40–50 µmol/L). Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of treatment based on plasma levels [US].

### Monitoring
- It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target 35 to 60 µmol/L) is advised [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Not explicitly listed as a contraindication in provided sources, but severe reactions require permanent discontinuation (see Section 5) [US].
- **Other:** None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, often within minutes of administration. Most cases involve ESRD patients. Discontinue immediately if severe reactions occur [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with the occurrence of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Oral Formulation Safety:** The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound; therefore, interactions involving protein displacement are unlikely [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following exogenous administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), which applies to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily used in ESRD patients. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine over 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in carnitine concentrations, but clinical outcome improvements in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
