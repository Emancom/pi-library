# Editorial Product Information: Ketamine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians must consult local regulations and the current approved PI in their specific region before administration.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ketamine hydrochloride (a chiral compound, though currently presented as a racemic mixture).
- **Chemical Class:** NMDA receptor antagonist; rapidly acting general anaesthetic.
- **Brand Names:** Biomed Ketamine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N01AX03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Clear, colourless isotonic solution for intravenous (IV) infusion. Available as 100 mg ketamine base per 100 mL (1 mg/mL) [Australian]. 
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion (the provided source specifically details the IV route; other brands may offer intramuscular options) [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Biomed Aust Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Sole Anaesthetic Agent:** For diagnostic and surgical procedures that do not require skeletal muscle relaxation. It is best suited for short procedures but may be used with additional doses for longer durations [Australian].
- **Induction of Anaesthesia:** Prior to the administration of other general anaesthetic agents [Australian].
- **Supplementation:** To bolster low-potency anaesthetic agents, such as nitrous oxide [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
As with all general anaesthetics, dosage must be individualised and titrated against the patient's requirements. Response varies based on dose, route, and patient age.

### Induction
- **Intravenous Route:** The initial dose range is 1 mg/kg to 4.5 mg/kg.
- **Average Dose:** 2 mg/kg is typically required to produce 5 to 10 minutes of surgical anaesthesia.
- **Rate of Administration:** Must be administered slowly (over 60 seconds). Rapid administration may lead to respiratory depression, apnoea, and an enhanced pressor response [Australian].

### Maintenance of Anaesthesia
- **Increments:** Doses of one-half to the full induction dose may be repeated as needed.
- **Note:** Involuntary or tonic-clonic movements do not necessarily indicate a lack of anaesthesia or the need for more drug. Total cumulative dose relates directly to recovery time [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Pre-operative Preparation:** Anticholinergic agents (e.g., atropine or hyoscine) should be administered at an appropriate interval prior to induction to reduce hypersalivation.
- **Airway Safety:** Pharyngeal-laryngeal reflexes usually remain active; however, aspiration risk exists, particularly if muscle relaxants are added.
- **Positioning:** Patients should be in a supported position during administration due to the rapid onset of action (approx. 30 seconds) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hazardous Blood Pressure Elevation:** Contraindicated in any condition where a significant rise in blood pressure would be dangerous, including:
    - Severe cardiovascular disease or heart failure.
    - Severe or poorly controlled hypertension.
    - Recent myocardial infarction.
    - History of stroke.
    - Cerebral trauma, intracerebral mass, or haemorrhage [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to ketamine or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Supervision:** Must only be used by or under the direction of medical practitioners experienced in general anaesthesia, airway maintenance, and respiratory support [Australian].
- **Resuscitative Equipment:** Must be immediately available during use.
- **Emergence Reactions:** Occur in approximately 12% of patients. Symptoms include vivid imagery, hallucinations, confusion, and irrational behaviour. Incidence is lower in the very young (≤15 years) and the elderly (>65 years). Reactions may be minimised by reducing verbal and tactile stimulation during recovery [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Monitoring:** Ketamine increases myocardial oxygen consumption. Use with caution in patients with hypovolemia, dehydration, or cardiac disease (ischaemia, CHF). Cardiac function should be monitored continually in patients with hypertension or cardiac decompensation [Australian].
- **Increased Pressure:** Use with extreme caution in patients with pre-anaesthetic elevated cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) pressure or increased intraocular pressure (e.g., glaucoma) [Australian].
- **Psychiatric/Neurological Conditions:** Use with caution in patients with seizures, schizophrenia, or acute psychosis [Australian].
- **Long-term Use/Abuse:** Not indicated for long-term use. Chronic use (especially abuse) is associated with haemorrhagic cystitis, acute kidney injury, and hepatotoxicity (if used >3 days) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| System Organ Class | Frequency/Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Psychiatric** | **Common (~12%):** Emergence reactions (delirium, hallucinations, confusion, abnormal dreams, agitation) [Australian]. |
| **Cardiovascular** | **Frequent:** Elevation of blood pressure and pulse rate. **Uncommon:** Hypotension, bradycardia, arrhythmia [Australian]. |
| **Respiratory** | **Common:** Respiratory stimulation. **Serious:** Severe depression or apnoea (associated with rapid IV administration), laryngospasm [Australian]. |
| **Neurological** | Hypertonia, nystagmus, tonic-clonic movements [Australian]. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, hypersalivation [Australian]. |
| **Renal/Urinary** | **With Long-term use:** Haemorrhagic cystitis, hydronephrosis, ureteral disorders [Australian]. |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Drug-induced liver injury (with extended use >3 days) [Australian]. |
| **Immune System** | Anaphylaxis [Australian]. |
| **Eye** | Diplopia, slight increase in intraocular pressure [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Diazepam:** Increases the half-life of **ketamine** and prolongs its pharmacodynamic effects; dose adjustments may be required.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** May decrease hepatic clearance, requiring a **ketamine** dose reduction.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** May increase clearance, potentially requiring an increased **ketamine** dose [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Barbiturates/Narcotics:** Prolonged recovery time if used concurrently.
- **Halogenated Anaesthetics:** Concurrent use may prolong **ketamine** half-life and increase the risk of bradycardia and hypotension.
- **Neuromuscular Blockers:** **Ketamine** may potentiate the effects of atracurium and tubocurarine (respiratory depression).
- **Sympathomimetics/Thyroid Hormones:** Increased risk of hypertension and tachycardia.
- **Theophylline/Aminophylline:** May significantly reduce the seizure threshold; unpredictable extensor-type seizures have been reported [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** **Ketamine** and barbiturates are chemically incompatible (precipitate formation) and must not be mixed in the same syringe [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Safe use in pregnancy (other than during delivery) has not been established. It crosses the placenta. 
    - Animal studies have shown potential for neurotoxicity in developing brains (see Special Populations). Use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Likely to be excreted in breast milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during **ketamine** use [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** PK parameters are generally similar to adults. 
    - **Neurotoxicity Alert:** Nonclinical data suggests exposure to NMDA antagonists during peak brain development (3rd trimester through age 3) may result in neuronal cell death and cognitive deficits. The clinical significance in humans is undetermined [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Lower incidence of emergence reactions. No specific dose adjustments provided, but titration is essential [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dose reductions should be considered in patients with cirrhosis or liver impairment due to potentially delayed elimination [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Elimination may be delayed; consider dose reductions [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A "dissociative" anaesthetic that selectively interrupts association pathways of the brain. It blocks NMDA receptors and may depress the thalamoneocortical system while affecting the limbic system.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces profound analgesia, normal pharyngeal-laryngeal reflexes, and cardiovascular/respiratory stimulation [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid following parenteral administration.
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributed to highly perfused tissues, including the brain. Volume of distribution: 1–3 L/kg. Plasma protein binding: 20–50%.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism via N-dealkylation to norketamine (active metabolite, 1/6th potency).
    - **Elimination:** Bi- or triexponential pattern. Alpha phase half-life: 10–15 mins (anaesthetic action). Beta phase half-life: approx. 2.5 hours. 90% excreted in urine (mostly as metabolites) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** **Ketamine** has been evaluated in over 105 studies involving more than 10,000 patients and 12,000 procedures. 
- **Outcomes:** Anaesthesia was rated as "excellent" or "good" by surgeons and anaesthetists in 90–93% of cases. Specific applications included burn debridement, neurodiagnostic procedures, orthopaedic manipulations, and cardiac catheterisation [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Inspect for particulate matter or discolouration before use. Single-use only; discard remaining contents [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 8 (Controlled Drug) [Australian].
