# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Note on Source Material:** Although the user prompt referenced "ketanserin," the provided source document is for **levocarnitine injection**. This editorial reflects the provided levocarnitine data. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 
- Information regarding other major jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) was not provided in the source material and is therefore marked as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection for intravenous use: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - Acute Management: A loading dose is often administered in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Every 3 to 4 hours, and no less than every 6 hours.
    - Maintenance: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to starting parenteral therapy.
- Ongoing weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Particulate Matter:** Parenteral products must be inspected visually for discoloration or particulate matter before administration [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Seizures.

### Common Adverse Effects (Clinical Trials in ESRD)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% regardless of causality in ESRD patients include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (30–49%), flu syndrome (25–40%), headache (22–37%), infection (15–24%), abdominal pain (5–21%), fever (5–12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (14–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (10–18%), rhinitis (6–11%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Other:** Body odor, gastritis, and transient nausea/vomiting have also been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    - **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins; therefore, it is unlikely to interact with drugs through protein-binding displacement [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in the current source, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits (3.8 times human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:**
    - Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine following administration. The benefits of maternal therapy must be weighed against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight as specified in the dosage section. See Section 3 for specific metabolic disorder dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. However, caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral forms are used in severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrates for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours.
- **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Clearance:** Total body clearance is approximately 4.00 L/h [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed that trough concentrations reached 54–180 µmol/L after one week (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L). 
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
