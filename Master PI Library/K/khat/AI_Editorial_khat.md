# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine product information, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available source [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance (Metabolic):** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Maintenance:** Administer as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- For ESRD patients, initiation of therapy may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations below 40–50 µmol/L [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].
- **Target Levels:** For metabolic disorders, plasma free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **ESRD Adjustments:** Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions manifest within minutes of intravenous administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase seizure frequency or severity in patients with pre-existing seizure activity. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a history of convulsive disorders [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD or severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
Adverse events reported regardless of causality in dialysis patients include:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), asthenia (9%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), headache (22%), infection (15%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), nausea (8%), vomiting (15%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hypervolemia (5%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%).
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), pharyngitis (23%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and Severe Hypersensitivity:** Reported during post-marketing use [US].
- **Seizures:** Both in patients with and without a prior history of epilepsy [US].

### Other Reported Effects
- **Body Odor:** Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed; less frequent reactions include body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not formally categorised under the lettered system in the provided text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; however, specific supplementation in nursing mothers has not been studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. A risk-benefit assessment should consider the benefits of mother’s treatment versus any potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders follows the weight-based guidelines (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Oral formulations are cautioned against in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine clears accumulated acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitines, which are rapidly excreted. This process helps alleviate metabolic acidosis and autointoxication [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg administered three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL at room temperature [US].

- **United States:** Rx-only [US].
