# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial synthesis: United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data to support safe and effective prescribing.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic name); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, but internationally recognised).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile solution for intravenous injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism which result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer intravenously.
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL in PVC bags [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg given as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours by infusion or IV injection [US].
- **Subsequent Dosing:** Daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg or as clinically required.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis [US]. 
- **Onset:** Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration.
- **Management:** Discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical care. If re-administration is considered, monitor the patient closely for recurrence [US].

### General Precautions
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulations):** While this editorial covers the injection, it is noted that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in renal impairment may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

### Monitoring
- **Plasma Concentrations:** Recommended pre-treatment level: 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Ongoing Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Effect (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, depression, paresthesia, insomnia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash |

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequently, body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (as noted in Warnings) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter category in available sources.
    - **Animal Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders and ESRD apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in the source, though monitoring of renal function is implied by the drug's clearance profile.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. Dosage is titrated based on pre-dialysis trough levels [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine helps clear accumulated toxic acylCoA compounds by forming acylcarnitines, which are rapidly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While carnitine concentrations increased, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial, as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
