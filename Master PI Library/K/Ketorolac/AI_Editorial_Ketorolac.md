# Editorial Product Information: Ketorolac Trometamol (Ophthalmic)

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information documents contributed to this editorial: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ketorolac trometamol ophthalmic solution for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official Product Information (PI) for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive prescribing guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ketorolac trometamol (a member of the pyrrolo-pyrrole group of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs).
- **Brand Names:** ACULAR [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** S01BC05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Ophthalmic solution (eye drops) containing ketorolac trometamol 5 mg/mL (0.5% w/v) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AbbVie Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Seasonal Allergic Conjunctivitis:** Short-term (2–4 weeks) relief of symptoms [Australian].
- **Post-Operative Inflammation:** Short-term (2–4 weeks) prophylaxis and reduction of inflammation following cataract extraction [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Seasonal Allergic Conjunctivitis:** Instill one drop (0.25 mg) into the affected eye(s) four times daily. Treatment may continue for up to four weeks [Australian].
- **Post-operative Inflammation:** Instill one to two drops (0.25–0.5 mg) into the affected eye(s) four times daily. Treatment should commence 24 hours prior to cataract surgery and continue for 2 to 4 weeks post-operatively [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Patients should avoid contact between the dispensing container tip and the eye or surrounding structures to prevent contamination and injury [Australian].
- **Contact Lenses:** Lenses should be removed prior to instillation as the preservative (benzalkonium chloride) may be absorbed by and discolour soft contact lenses. Lenses may be reinserted 15 minutes after administration [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to ketorolac trometamol or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].
- **Cross-sensitivity:** Patients who have previously exhibited sensitivities (e.g., asthma, urticaria, or allergic-type reactions) to acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin), phenylacetic acid derivatives, or other non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in the provided source.
- **Ocular Bleeding:** Topical NSAIDs may increase the bleeding time of ocular tissues (including hyphemas) when used in conjunction with ocular surgery. Use with caution in patients with known bleeding tendencies or those on concomitant medications that prolong bleeding [Australian].
- **Corneal Effects:** Use of topical NSAIDs may lead to keratitis. Susceptible patients (e.g., those with diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, ocular surface disease, or complicated/repeat ocular surgeries) are at increased risk of epithelial breakdown, corneal thinning, erosion, ulceration, or perforation. These events may be sight-threatening. Use beyond 24 hours pre-surgery or 14 days post-surgery may increase the risk and severity of corneal adverse events [Australian].
- **Delayed Healing:** Topical NSAIDs may slow or delay the healing process. This risk is increased when used concomitantly with topical corticosteroids [Australian].
- **Masking of Infection:** Ketorolac may mask the typical signs of an ocular infection [Australian].
- **Respiratory Effects:** Post-marketing reports indicate potential for bronchospasm or asthma exacerbation in patients with a history of asthma or NSAID sensitivity [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Patients showing signs of corneal epithelial breakdown should immediately discontinue use and be closely monitored for corneal health [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on controlled clinical studies [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Transient stinging and burning upon instillation (up to 40%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Conjunctivitis (itching, erythema, scratching), superficial keratitis, keratic precipitates (1%), retinal haemorrhage (1%), cystoid macular oedema (1%), intraocular pressure increase (2%), eye trauma (1%), corneal oedema, eye irritation, eye pain, blurred vision, blepharitis, photophobia. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Eye dryness, corneal infiltrates, ulcerative keratitis, visual disturbance, headaches. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Corneal Ulceration/Perforation:** High-risk patients should be monitored for epithelial breakdown [Australian].
- **Systemic Allergic Reactions:** Reported very rarely in post-marketing experience [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Topical Corticosteroids:** Concomitant use may increase the potential for corneal healing problems [Australian].
- **General Interaction Profile:** No specific clinical interaction studies have been conducted. In clinical trials, no interactions were reported with systemic or ophthalmic antibiotics, sedatives, beta-blockers, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, miotics, mydriatics, or local anaesthetics [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category C. Ketorolac and its metabolites cross the placenta. Safety in human pregnancy has not been established; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Ketorolac is excreted in human breast milk following systemic administration. Ophthalmic use is not recommended in lactating mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Studies in rats showed no impairment of fertility at oral doses significantly higher than the human parenteral AUC [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children under 12 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not available in provided sources [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments not specified in available sources. Use with caution in patients with a history of peptic ulceration [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ketorolac is an NSAID that inhibits the cyclo-oxygenase (COX) enzyme, thereby blocking prostaglandin biosynthesis.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Ocular administration reduces levels of prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) in the aqueous humour. It does not cause pupil constriction or significantly affect intraocular pressure [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Following topical administration, ketorolac is detectable in the aqueous humour. 
    - **Distribution:** Systemic absorption is minimal. In one study, only 4 of 13 subjects had detectable plasma levels after 10 days of topical treatment [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Seasonal Allergic Conjunctivitis:** Two randomised, controlled trials (n=203) demonstrated significant reductions in itching and conjunctival inflammation over a 7-day period compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Post-operative Inflammation:** Two randomised, controlled trials (n=206) showed ketorolac was significantly more effective than placebo in reducing anterior chamber cells and flare. 39% of ketorolac patients achieved a zero score for inflammation after 2 weeks, vs 12% in the placebo group [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C [Australian].
- Protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 24 months. Discard any unused contents 28 days after opening the bottle [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
