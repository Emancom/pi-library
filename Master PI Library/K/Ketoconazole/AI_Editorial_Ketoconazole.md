# Editorial Product Information: Ketoconazole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ketoconazole (synthetic broad-spectrum antifungal agent).
- **Chemical Class:** Imidazole derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Ketoconazole Tablets USP (generic).
- **ATC Code:** J02AB02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral tablet, 200 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured by Strides Pharma Science Limited; Distributed by PD-Rx Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Ketoconazole tablets are restricted for use only when other effective antifungal therapies are unavailable or not tolerated, and when the potential benefits outweigh the risks [US]. 

**Approved Indications [US]:**
- Blastomycosis
- Coccidioidomycosis
- Histoplasmosis
- Chromomycosis
- Paracoccidioidomycosis

**Explicitly NOT Indicated [US]:**
- Onychomycosis
- Cutaneous dermatophyte infections
- Candida infections (vulvovaginal, oropharyngeal, or systemic)
- Fungal meningitis (due to poor cerebrospinal fluid penetration)

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended starting dose is 200 mg (one tablet) administered once daily. If clinical response is insufficient, the dose may be increased to 400 mg once daily [US].
- **Paediatric Dosing (Children >2 years):** A single daily dose of 3.3 mg/kg to 6.6 mg/kg has been utilized. Use in children under 2 years has not been studied [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Absorption is maximal when taken with a meal. For patients with achlorhydria or those taking gastric acid suppressors, administration with an acidic beverage (e.g., non-diet cola) is recommended to improve dissolution [US].
- **Duration of Therapy:** The usual duration for systemic infection is 6 months; therapy should continue until the active fungal infection has subsided [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ketoconazole or any inactive tablet ingredients [US].
- **Liver Disease:** Acute or chronic hepatic impairment [US].
- **Drug Interactions (CYP3A4 Substrates):** Coadministration with drugs that may lead to QT prolongation or serious adverse events is contraindicated, including:
    - **Antiarrhythmics:** Dofetilide, quinidine, disopyramide, dronedarone [US].
    - **Antipsychotics/Anxiolytics:** Lurasidone, pimozide, alprazolam, triazolam, oral midazolam [US].
    - **GI/Cardiovascular:** Cisapride, ranolazine, methadone, eplerenone, felodipine, nisoldipine [US].
    - **Lipid-lowering/Other:** Lovastatin, simvastatin, ergot alkaloids (e.g., ergotamine), irinotecan, tolvaptan, and colchicine (in patients with renal/hepatic impairment) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Hepatotoxicity:** Serious hepatotoxicity, including fatal cases or those requiring liver transplantation, has occurred. Injury may occur even without pre-existing risk factors. Baseline liver function tests (LFTs) are mandatory, followed by weekly ALT monitoring throughout treatment. Therapy must be interrupted if ALT exceeds the upper limit of normal or increases 30% above baseline [US].
- **Boxed Warning: QT Prolongation:** Ketoconazole can increase plasma concentrations of certain drugs (see Contraindications), potentially leading to torsades de pointes and other life-threatening ventricular dysrhythmias [US].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Ketoconazole inhibits adrenal corticosteroid secretion at doses of 400 mg and higher. Adrenal function should be monitored in patients with borderline function or under high stress [US].
- **Hormonal Effects:** Doses of 800 mg/day or higher significantly lower serum testosterone, which may cause gynecomastia, impotence, and oligospermia [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis has been reported after the first dose [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, headache, abdominal pain, diarrhea, abnormal liver function tests [US]. |
| **Clinical Trial Reports** | Vomiting, dizziness, paresthesia, somnolence, photophobia, orthostatic hypotension, epistaxis, dyspepsia, rash, pruritus, alopecia, myalgia, fatigue, fever [US]. |
| **Post-Marketing (Serious)** | Hepatitis, cholestasis, hepatic necrosis, cirrhosis, hepatic failure, thrombocytopenia, anaphylactic shock, angioedema, adrenocortical insufficiency, increased intracranial pressure (papilledema), azoospermia (at doses >400mg) [US]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibition:** Ketoconazole is a potent inhibitor of CYP3A4. It significantly increases the exposure of drugs metabolized by this pathway, necessitating dose reductions or contraindication for many agents (e.g., aripiprazole dose should be reduced by half) [US].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Inhibition:** May increase concentrations of P-gp substrates (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran) [US].
- **Gastric pH-Dependent Absorption:** Drugs that increase gastric pH (PPIs, H2-antagonists, antacids) significantly reduce ketoconazole absorption. Antacids should be given 1 hour before or 2 hours after ketoconazole [US].
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Potent CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, isoniazid) substantially decrease ketoconazole levels and should be avoided for 2 weeks prior to and during therapy [US].
- **Alcohol:** Exceptional cases of a disulfiram-like reaction (flushing, rash, nausea) have been reported [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Noted as potentially teratogenic (syndactyly and oligodactyly) and embryotoxic in animal models at 2x the human dose. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women; use only if potential benefits outweigh fetal risks [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Ketoconazole is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment [US].
- **Fertility:** High doses (800–1600 mg/day) may cause oligospermia or azoospermia; these effects are typically reversible upon discontinuation [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not systematically studied in children of any age. Safety and efficacy in children under 2 years have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical documentation provided does not specify age-related dose adjustments, though monitoring is advised [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Overall pharmacokinetics were not significantly different in patients with renal impairment compared to healthy subjects [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** While PK may not differ significantly, ketoconazole is contraindicated in patients with acute or chronic liver disease [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the fungal cytochrome P-450 dependent enzyme lanosterol 14α-demethylase. This blocks the conversion of lanosterol to ergosterol, leading to cell membrane depletion and impaired fungal cell function [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Requires acidic environment; peak plasma levels (~3.5 mcg/mL) reached in 1–2 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** 99% protein bound (mainly albumin); negligible penetration into CSF [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Converted to inactive metabolites via CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic half-life (2 hours in the first 10 hours; 8 hours thereafter). Primarily excreted via bile/feces (57%) and urine (13%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **High-Dose Safety Trial:** In a trial of 350 patients with metastatic prostate cancer receiving 1200 mg/day, 11 deaths occurred within two weeks. Causality (drug vs. disease/adrenal insufficiency) could not be definitively established [US].
- **Systemic Fungal Trials:** Pivotal trials were used to establish efficacy for systemic mycoses, though specific trial designs were not detailed in the provided document [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture and keep out of reach of children [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
