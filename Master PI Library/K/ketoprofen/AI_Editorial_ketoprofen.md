# Editorial Product Information: Ketoprofen

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ketoprofen for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a clinically focused format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) when prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ketoprofen, a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) of the propionic acid class.
- **Brand Name(s):** Orudis [US].
- **ATC Code:** M01AE03 (Propionic acid derivatives).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Immediate-release capsules: 75 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Marnel Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (Distributed by Allegis Pharmaceuticals, Inc.) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis and Osteoarthritis:** Management of signs and symptoms. [US]
- **Management of Pain:** Treatment of mild-to-moderate pain. [US]
- **Primary Dysmenorrhea:** Treatment of menstrual pain. [US]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Range:** 150 mg to 300 mg daily, divided into three or four doses. [US]
- **Maximum Dose:** 300 mg per day. [US]
- **Administration Note:** To minimise gastrointestinal (GI) side effects, ketoprofen should be taken with food, milk, or antacids. [US]

### Dosing by Indication
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) and Osteoarthritis (OA):** 75 mg three times daily or 50 mg four times daily. [US]
- **Mild-to-Moderate Pain and Dysmenorrhea:** 25 mg to 50 mg every 6 to 8 hours as needed. Doses above 75 mg have not demonstrated superior analgesia. [US]

### Special Considerations
- **Titration:** Adjust dose and frequency to the lowest effective dose for the shortest possible duration based on individual patient response. [US]
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ketoprofen. [US]
- **Cross-Reactivity:** History of asthma, urticaria, or allergic-type reactions following aspirin or other NSAID use. Severe, rarely fatal, anaphylactic reactions have occurred in such patients. [US]
- **Surgical Setting:** Use is contraindicated for the treatment of perioperative pain in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery. [US]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Cardiovascular (CV) Thrombotic Events:** NSAIDs cause an increased risk of serious CV thrombotic events, including myocardial infarction (MI) and stroke, which can be fatal. Risk may occur early in treatment and increases with duration of use. 
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Risk:** NSAIDs increase the risk of serious GI adverse events including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation of the stomach or intestines, which can be fatal. Elderly patients and those with a history of peptic ulcer disease/GI bleeding are at greater risk.

### General Warnings
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Borderline elevations of liver tests occur in up to 15% of patients. Discontinue if clinical signs of liver disease develop or systemic manifestations (e.g., eosinophilia, rash) occur. [US]
- **Hypertension:** NSAIDs can lead to new-onset or worsening of pre-existing hypertension. Blood pressure should be monitored closely. [US]
- **Heart Failure and Edema:** Avoid use in patients with severe heart failure unless benefits outweigh risks. NSAIDs may blunt the CV effects of ACE inhibitors, diuretics, and ARBs. [US]
- **Renal Effects:** Long-term use can result in renal papillary necrosis. Use is not recommended in advanced renal disease. [US]
- **Anaphylactoid Reactions:** May occur in patients without prior exposure; seek emergency help immediately. [US]
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Can cause SJS, TEN, and DRESS (Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms), which can be fatal. Discontinue at the first appearance of skin rash. [US]
- **Masking Inflammation:** Antipyretic and anti-inflammatory activity may mask signs of infection. [US]

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (Incidence >1%) [US]
| Body System | Adverse Effect (Frequency) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Digestive** | Dyspepsia (11%), nausea (3-9%), abdominal pain (3-9%), diarrhea (3-9%), constipation (3-9%), flatulence (3-9%), anorexia, vomiting, stomatitis. |
| **Nervous System** | Headache (3-9%), dizziness, CNS inhibition (malaise, depression) or excitation (insomnia, nervousness, dreams). |
| **Special Senses** | Tinnitus, visual disturbance. |
| **Skin** | Rash. |
| **Urogenital** | Impairment of renal function (edema, increased BUN), urinary tract irritation. |

### Less Common/Serious Adverse Effects (Incidence <1%) [US]
- **Body as a Whole:** Chills, facial edema, infection, anaphylaxis.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, palpitation, tachycardia, congestive heart failure, peripheral vascular disease.
- **Digestive:** Peptic ulcer, GI perforation, hematemesis, intestinal ulceration, hepatic dysfunction, jaundice.
- **Hemic:** Anemia, agranulocytosis, hemolysis, thrombocytopenia, purpura.
- **Respiratory:** Dyspnea, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema.
- **Skin:** Alopecia, exfoliative dermatitis, photosensitivity, SJS/TEN.
- **Urogenital:** Renal failure, interstitial nephritis, nephrotic syndrome, hematuria.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Aspirin:** Concurrent use increases the risk of GI events. Aspirin decreases ketoprofen protein binding and increases its clearance. Combination is generally not recommended. [US]
- **ACE Inhibitors/ARBs:** NSAIDs may diminish the antihypertensive effect of these agents. [US]
- **Diuretics:** NSAIDs may reduce the natriuretic effect of furosemide and thiazides. Monitor for signs of renal failure. [US]
- **Lithium:** NSAIDs increase plasma lithium levels and reduce renal lithium clearance. Monitor for lithium toxicity. [US]
- **Methotrexate:** NSAIDs may increase methotrexate toxicity due to competitive inhibition of renal accumulation. [US]
- **Warfarin:** Synergistic risk of GI bleeding. Monitor closely. [US]
- **Probenecid:** Increases free and bound ketoprofen levels by reducing clearance and protein binding. Combination is not recommended. [US]
- **Antacids:** Magnesium or aluminium hydroxide does not interfere with the rate or extent of ketoprofen absorption. [US]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **General:** Avoid NSAIDs at ~30 weeks gestation and later due to the risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus. [US]
    - **20–30 Weeks:** Use should be limited to the lowest dose/shortest duration. If treatment exceeds 48 hours, consider ultrasound monitoring for oligohydramnios. [US]
    - **Fertility:** NSAIDs may delay or prevent rupture of ovarian follicles, associated with reversible infertility in some women. [US]
- **Breastfeeding:** Secretion in human milk is unknown. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, use is not recommended for nursing mothers. [US]

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients below 18 years have not been established. [US]
- **Geriatric Use (≥65 years):** Use with caution. Elderly patients are at higher risk for GI bleeding and renal dysfunction. 
    - **Over 75 years:** Initial dosage should be reduced. [US]
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild impairment:** Maximum recommended daily dose is 150 mg. [US]
    - **Severe impairment (GFR <25 mL/min/1.73m²):** Maximum recommended daily dose is 100 mg. [US]
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Albumin <3.5 g/dL:** Maximum initial daily dose is 100 mg. Closely monitor patients with alcoholic cirrhosis. [US]

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A non-selective NSAID with analgesic, anti-inflammatory, and antipyretic properties. It inhibits prostaglandin and leukotriene synthesis, possesses antibradykinin activity, and has lysosomal membrane-stabilising actions. [US]
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Ketoprofen is a racemate; only the S-enantiomer possesses pharmacological activity. [US]
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and well-absorbed (>90% bioavailability). Peak plasma levels occur within 0.5 to 2 hours. Food slows the rate of absorption but not the extent (AUC). [US]
    - **Distribution:** >99% protein bound, primarily to albumin. [US]
    - **Metabolism:** Glucuronide conjugation to an unstable acyl-glucuronide (which can convert back to parent drug). No known active metabolites. [US]
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 80% excreted in urine, primarily as glucuronide. Elimination half-life is approximately 2 hours. [US]

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RA and OA:** Efficacy was demonstrated as comparable to aspirin, ibuprofen, naproxen, and indomethacin. A higher rate of GI-related dropouts was noted in some ketoprofen cohorts compared to other NSAIDs. [US]
- **Pain Management:** In postoperative and dysmenorrhea models, 25 mg to 100 mg doses were comparable to 650 mg acetaminophen with 60 mg codeine. Onset of action is typically within 30 minutes. [US]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). [US]
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure. [US]

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription only). [US]
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (US jurisdiction uses Rx-only status).
