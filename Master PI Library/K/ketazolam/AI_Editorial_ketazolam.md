# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) source(s) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It provides a clinically accurate synthesis of the pharmacological profile, dosing requirements, and safety considerations for levocarnitine.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or the specific regulatory requirements for prescribing in any individual jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (L-carnitine), a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (quaternary ammonium compound).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from genetic metabolic disorders [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Acute/Severe Crisis:** A loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 to 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized if clinically required.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is advised.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line immediately following each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Initiation of therapy may be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma carnitine levels <40 to 50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of treatment based on trough concentrations.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions can occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate appropriate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In ESRD patients, safety and efficacy of the oral formulation have not been established; high oral doses in this population may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed. Less frequent reports include body odor and gastritis.
- **ESRD Population (≥5% Frequency):** In clinical trials of hemodialysis patients, the following were reported (regardless of causality):
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%).
    - **Neurological:** Headache (3–37%), dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), abdominal pain (5–21%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), fever (5–12%), asthenia (8–12%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures (post-marketing).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified in modern labeling, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake by the infant must be weighed against the clinical benefits to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug should be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight; see Section 3 for specific metabolic disorder dosing.
- **Elderly:** Not specifically addressed in available sources regarding age-related dose adjustments.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. However, high doses of the *oral* formulation are cautioned against in patients with severely compromised renal function due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L. 
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 24 weeks demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations (reaching 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L respectively) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as it contains no preservative.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only.
