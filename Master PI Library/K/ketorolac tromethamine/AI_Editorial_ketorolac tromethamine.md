# Editorial Product Information: ketorolac tromethamine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **ketorolac tromethamine**
- Chemical class: Nonsteroidal anti‑inflammatory drug (NSAID); member of the pyrrolo‑pyrrole group
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Ophthalmic (topical)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 1991.
- Dates of last revision / updates reported in available source:
  - Recent major change noted for Warnings and Precautions (section 5.5): September 2025.
  - Full labeling revision indicated: July 2026.
- Withdrawals, suspensions, or restrictions: Not specified in available sources.
- Regulatory approval dates for jurisdictions other than the United States: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Temporary relief of ocular itching due to seasonal allergic conjunctivitis.
- Treatment of postoperative inflammation in patients who have undergone cataract extraction.
- If any jurisdictional differences in approved indications exist, they are not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended ocular dose for both listed indications: one drop to the affected eye(s) four times daily.

### Dosing by indication
- Temporary relief of ocular itching due to seasonal allergic conjunctivitis: one drop to affected eye(s) four times daily.
- Treatment of postoperative inflammation following cataract extraction: begin one drop to the affected eye four times daily starting 24 hours after cataract surgery and continue through the first 2 weeks of the postoperative period.

### Administration instructions
- Administer topical ophthalmic preparations as one drop into the conjunctival sac of the affected eye(s).
- If more than one topical ophthalmic medication is being used, administer drops at least 5 minutes apart.
- Avoid contact between the bottle tip and the eye or surrounding structures to reduce risk of contamination.
- Do not administer while wearing contact lenses.
- Discontinue use immediately and seek evaluation if signs of corneal epithelial breakdown occur.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to ketorolac tromethamine or to any component of the formulation.
- Cross‑sensitivity to other NSAIDs or aspirin has been reported; use caution in patients with known hypersensitivity (see Warnings and Precautions).
- No other jurisdiction‑specific contraindications are specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Bolded critical warnings:
  - **Delayed or impaired wound healing:** Topical NSAIDs (including ketorolac tromethamine) may slow or delay healing. Concomitant use of topical NSAIDs and topical corticosteroids may increase healing problems.
  - **Corneal adverse events (potentially sight‑threatening):** Use has been associated with keratitis and, in some susceptible patients, epithelial breakdown, corneal thinning, corneal erosion, corneal ulceration, or corneal perforation.
  - **Cross‑sensitivity / bronchospasm:** Patients with known hypersensitivity to aspirin or other NSAIDs, and those with a history of asthma, may experience bronchospasm or exacerbation of asthma.
  - **Increased bleeding risk:** NSAIDs can interfere with platelet aggregation; ocular bleeding (including hyphema) has been reported in association with ocular surgery.
- Other precautions and clinical details:
  - Patients with complicated ocular surgeries, corneal denervation, corneal epithelial defects, diabetes mellitus, ocular surface diseases (e.g., dry eye syndrome), rheumatoid arthritis, or repeat ocular surgeries within a short interval may be at increased risk for severe corneal adverse events; exercise caution and closely monitor corneal health in these patients.
  - Use beyond 14 days post‑surgery or use beginning more than 1 day prior to surgery may increase risk of corneal adverse events.
  - Avoid allowing the bottle tip to contact the eye or eyelids to reduce contamination risk; contaminated solutions can cause serious ocular infections and vision loss.
  - Do not use while wearing contact lenses; preservative components may be contraindicated in lens wearers or may interact with lenses.
- Monitoring requirements:
  - Monitor for signs of corneal epithelial breakdown or corneal inflammation; discontinue therapy immediately if epithelial breakdown is observed and monitor closely.
  - Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity (e.g., bronchospasm) in patients with history of aspirin/NSAID sensitivity or asthma.
  - Monitor for bleeding in patients with known bleeding disorders or who are receiving medications that may prolong bleeding time, especially in the perioperative ocular setting.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Potential for increased bleeding time due to interference with platelet aggregation observed with some NSAIDs; use caution in patients with known bleeding tendencies or who are receiving medications that may prolong bleeding time. Ocular bleeding (including hyphemas) has been reported with topical NSAID use in conjunction with ocular surgery.
- Cross‑sensitivity/hypersensitivity interactions: patients sensitive to acetylsalicylic acid or other NSAIDs may react to topical ketorolac tromethamine (risk of bronchospasm in susceptible patients).

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Not specified in available sources for topical administration (systemic pharmacokinetic interactions are not described in the available source).

### Compatibility with other topical ophthalmic medications
- Ketorolac tromethamine has been administered concomitantly with other ophthalmic medications such as topical antibiotics, alpha‑agonists, beta‑blockers, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, cycloplegics, and mydriatics without specific interaction issues reported in the available source; administer separate ophthalmic products at least 5 minutes apart.

### Management notes
- When patients are on systemic agents that increase bleeding risk, or have bleeding disorders, consider risk/benefit of topical NSAID use in the perioperative ocular period and monitor closely.
- In patients with known NSAID hypersensitivity or aspirin‑exacerbated respiratory disease, weigh risks and consider alternative therapies.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Risk summary: No adequate and well‑controlled studies in pregnant women are available. Animal studies in rats and rabbits showed no evidence of teratogenicity at clinically relevant doses.
- Clinical considerations: Because prostaglandin‑inhibiting drugs (NSAIDs) can affect the fetal cardiovascular system (e.g., premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus), use of topical ketorolac tromethamine during late pregnancy should be avoided.
- Pregnancy category/system: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- It is not known whether ketorolac administered topically is present in human milk. Because many drugs are excreted in human milk, caution is advised when administering ketorolac tromethamine to a nursing woman.

### Fertility
- Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **ketorolac tromethamine** is an NSAID whose pharmacological effects are thought to result from inhibition of prostaglandin biosynthesis.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Ocular tissue penetration: Two drops of 0.5% ketorolac tromethamine instilled 12 hours and 1 hour prior to cataract extraction produced a mean ketorolac concentration of 95 ng/mL in aqueous humor (8 of 9 eyes; range 40–170 ng/mL).
- Systemic exposure following topical ocular administration: In a study where one drop of 0.5% ketorolac tromethamine was instilled TID into one eye (vehicle into the other) in 26 healthy subjects, ketorolac was detectable in plasma in 5 of 26 subjects (range 11–23 ng/mL) on Day 10. Plasma concentrations after topical ocular dosing were approximately 4–8% of the steady‑state mean minimum plasma concentration observed following four‑times‑daily oral administration of 10 mg ketorolac in humans (oral: 290 ± 70 ng/mL).
- Physicochemical properties (as reported): ketorolac tromethamine is supplied as a racemic mixture; pKa of ketorolac is approximately 3.5; molecular weight reported as ~376.41. (Formulation pH and osmolality are described in available sources but specific container/storage details are not included here.)

### Pharmacodynamics
- Inhibition of prostaglandin synthesis reduces inflammation and provides symptomatic relief of ocular itching associated with allergic conjunctivitis; reduces signs of postoperative intraocular inflammation (cell and flare).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Allergic conjunctivitis: Two controlled clinical studies demonstrated that ketorolac tromethamine ophthalmic solution was significantly more effective than vehicle in relieving ocular itching due to seasonal allergic conjunctivitis.
- Postoperative inflammation (cataract extraction): Two controlled clinical studies showed that patients treated for two weeks with ketorolac tromethamine ophthalmic solution were less likely to have measurable signs of inflammation (cell and flare) compared with patients receiving vehicle.
- Other trial findings:
  - Trials indicate ketorolac tromethamine has no significant effect upon intraocular pressure in general; however, intraocular pressure changes may occur following cataract surgery and should be managed per standard postoperative care.
  - Pharmacokinetic/PK trial data (see section 11) describe aqueous humor and plasma concentrations following topical dosing.
- Trial sizes, detailed efficacy endpoints, statistical measures, and full methodology are not specified in available sources beyond the summary statements above.
