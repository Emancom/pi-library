# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) source: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic/INN); ($R$)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-$N,N,N$-trimethyl-1-propanaminium.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
  - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
  - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours).
  - Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg/day, though doses up to 300 mg/kg/day have been utilized based on clinical requirements.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
  - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
  - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
  - Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations <40–50 µmol/L.

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L).
- **ESRD:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough plasma concentrations and can be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be necessary based on levels.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable and compatible for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** While this PI refers to the injection, it is noted that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Neurological: Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not explicitly lettered under current labelling]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations are provided for both metabolic and ESRD indications (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources (general caution for age-related physiological changes is advised).
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Use of the intravenous form avoids the accumulation of certain metabolites seen with high-dose oral administration in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily feces).
  - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean trough levocarnitine levels increased from ~20 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While increases in carnitine concentrations are well-demonstrated, the definitive effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
