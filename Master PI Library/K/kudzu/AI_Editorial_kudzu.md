# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the following official Product Information (PI) source: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, intended to consolidate clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic management of patients presenting with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This should be administered as a slow bolus injection (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). 

### Administration Instructions
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and no less than every 6 hours.
- **ESRD Administration:** Dose should be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Inspection:** Parenteral products must be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before administration.

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Daily doses may be adjusted based on clinical response, with the highest reported dose being 300 mg/kg.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35 to 60 µmol/L).
- **ESRD Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations below 40–50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. These can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate the risks versus benefits before attempting re-administration.
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine):** The safety of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal insufficiency has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequently confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders. The following frequencies are based on clinical trials in ESRD patients [US]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pharyngitis (23%), Headache (22%), Hypertension (20%), Diarrhea (16%), Infection (15%), Vomiting (15%), Hypotension (14%), Dizziness (13%), Chest pain (12%), Cough increased (9%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain (9%), Asthenia (9%), Accidental injury (10%), Back pain (8%), Fever (7%), Flu syndrome (25%), Nausea (8%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%), Pruritus (5%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, and seizures.
- **Other reported effects:** Body odor and gastritis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. 
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specifically lettered in provided text, but consistent with former Category B]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of treatment to the mother.
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, etc.) were negative. No long-term animal studies for carcinogenic potential have been conducted.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See dosage and administration. [US: Clinical documentation indicates use in premature infants and newborns for secondary deficiency.]
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients [US]. Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral forms are used in place of injection.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following intravenous bolus (20 mg/kg), the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed that mean pre-dialysis levocarnitine concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed that trough levels increase in a dose-dependent manner over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags.
- **Vial Usage:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation lacks preservatives.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not applicable for US jurisdiction; local classifications apply elsewhere.
