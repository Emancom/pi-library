# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)** for Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation). 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized clinical overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory and clinical data into a structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic name); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) via infusion or IV injection.
    - Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations and may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Monitoring
- Pre-therapy plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained.
- Ongoing monitoring (weekly/monthly) should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine concentration: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These can occur within minutes of IV administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine Caution):** While this PI refers to the injectable form, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), pharyngitis (15–27%), headache (3–37%), hypertension (18–21%), nausea/vomiting (8–21%), diarrhea (9–35%), and abdominal pain (5–21%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, accidental injury, asthenia, cough, dyspnea, and dizziness [US].
- **Other Notable Effects:** Body odor and gastritis.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
    - Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress.
    - Seizures (post-marketing reports).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels frequently in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned, but animal reproductive studies (rats/rabbits at 3.8 times human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is naturally present in human milk. Supplemental use in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, exogenous administration increases milk carnitine concentrations. Benefits to the mother should be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as in adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided in available sources; generally, dose selection should be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** IV levocarnitine is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Dose adjustments are based on trough plasma carnitine levels (see Section 3).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors or ESRD, levocarnitine aids in the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are then excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (excluding endogenous carnitine). The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis, 20 mg/kg IV doses three times weekly significantly increased mean plasma levocarnitine concentrations from 20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to trough levels between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Human Prescription Drug).
