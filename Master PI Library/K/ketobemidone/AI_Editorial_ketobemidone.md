# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection, synthesized for healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/generic).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism (amino acid derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2-3 minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Crisis Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours).
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though therapy may require titration. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Timing:** Administer as a slow 2-3 minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating parenteral therapy.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately and do not re-administer if a severe reaction occurs unless the benefits clearly outweigh the risks [US].
- **Seizures:** Reports exist of seizures occurring in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** The safety of long-term high-dose **levocarnitine** has not been evaluated in patients with renal impairment. Chronic oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD clinical trials were often confounded by the underlying pathology. Common reactions (incidence ≥5%) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness, paresthesia |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].
- **Other:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when co-administered with **levocarnitine**. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of treatment or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned under current labeling, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risk-benefit analysis should consider the necessity of the drug to the mother versus potential excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Dosage and Administration; safety and efficacy are established for the treatment of inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** **Levocarnitine** is easily removed by dialysis. Safety of high-dose oral administration in renal failure is not established due to metabolite accumulation (see Warnings) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Levocarnitine** facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn metabolic errors, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Total body clearance is approximately 4.00 L/h [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increased plasma carnitine levels. Trough concentrations rose from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Clinical outcomes related to symptoms like malaise or muscle weakness in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
