# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
*   This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (Food and Drug Administration)**.
*   The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
*   This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the United States PI was available for review; therefore, all information is attributed to the [US] jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
*   **Brand Names:** Not specified in available source (generic Levocarnitine Injection, USP).
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
*   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
*   **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
*   **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine levels (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
*   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
*   **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
*   **Monitoring:** In patients with renal impairment, chronic administration of high oral doses (not specifically the IV formulation) may lead to accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide); however, IV administration bypasses the gastrointestinal conversion to these metabolites [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (ESRD/Dialysis Population)
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
*   **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%), fever (7%).
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%).
*   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), rhinitis (9%), dyspnea (7%).
*   **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).

### Post-marketing Experience
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis have been reported less frequently [US].
*   **Neurological:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended that INR levels be monitored upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned under current labeling, but stated as "use only if clearly needed"]. Animal reproduction studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate carnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. Clinicians must weigh the benefits of maternal therapy against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for inborn errors of metabolism apply to paediatric patients [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For the IV formulation in ESRD, see Section 3 (Dosage and Administration) [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors or ESRD, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While the drug increases carnitine concentrations, the definitive effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. The product does not contain preservatives; any unused portion must be discarded [US].
*   **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags (concentration range 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
