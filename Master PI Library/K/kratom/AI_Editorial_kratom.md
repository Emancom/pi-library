# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the **United States (FDA)** for Levocarnitine Injection. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a structured format.
- This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing or administering this medication in a clinical setting.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects that result in carnitine depletion [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administer as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Precaution):** While this PI describes the intravenous form, it notes that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% frequency) include:

| Body System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, headache, injection site reaction, pain, fever, flu syndrome, asthenia, abdominal pain. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, dyspepsia, anorexia. |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, paresthesia, insomnia, depression. |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis. |
| **Metabolic/Other** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, hyperkalemia, weight changes. |

**Serious/Notable Effects:**
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (postmarketing) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis occur less frequently [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine therapy and following any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women are unavailable. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies on levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers have not been conducted. While studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after administration, the risks to the infant from excess intake must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is consistent with adult weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not detailed in available sources; general caution regarding dosing in patients with decreased organ function applies.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, the accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) is a concern with chronic *oral* dosing in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a carrier molecule essential for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process provides substrates for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) plasma carnitine levels from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Efficacy Note:** While levocarnitine injection effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the definitive impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in this population has not been fully established [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's solution (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) stored at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Integrity:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison/Schedule:** Not specified in available sources as a scheduled controlled substance, but regulated as a prescription drug [US].
