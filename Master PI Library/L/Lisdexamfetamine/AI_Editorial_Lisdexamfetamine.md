# Editorial Product Information: Lisdexamfetamine

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lisdexamfetamine Product Information, synthesised for healthcare professionals to support clinical decision-making. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lisdexamfetamine dimesilate.
- **Chemical Class:** Non-catecholamine sympathomimetic amine; CNS stimulant (prodrug of dexamphetamine).
- **Brand Name:** **VYVANSE** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06BA12.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Capsules:** 20 mg, 30 mg, 40 mg, 50 mg, 60 mg, and 70 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Takeda Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD):** Indicated for the treatment of ADHD in children (aged 6 years and over), adolescents, and adults. Diagnosis should be based on DSM criteria, and treatment should be initiated by a specialist as part of a comprehensive management program [Australian].
- **Binge Eating Disorder (BED):** Indicated for the treatment of moderate to severe BED in adults when non-pharmacological treatment is unsuccessful or unavailable. Must be commenced and managed by a psychiatrist [Australian].
    - *Limitation of Use:* Not indicated or recommended for weight loss or the treatment of obesity [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Once daily in the morning, with or without food. Afternoon doses should be avoided to prevent insomnia.
- **Instructions:** Capsules may be swallowed whole or opened and the entire contents dissolved in water, orange juice, or mixed with yoghurt. The mixture must be consumed immediately and not stored. Capsules must not be divided; the minimum dose is one full capsule [Australian].

### ADHD (Pediatric and Adult)
- **Starting Dose:** 30 mg once daily. A lower starting dose of 20 mg may be considered if clinically indicated.
- **Titration:** Increase in 20 mg increments at weekly intervals.
- **Maximum Dose:** 70 mg/day [Australian].

### Binge Eating Disorder (Adults)
- **Starting Dose:** 30 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** Increments of 20 mg at weekly intervals to reach a target dose of 50 mg or 70 mg.
- **Maximum Dose:** 70 mg/day [Australian].
- **Duration:** Initial treatment period is 12 weeks; ongoing need should be periodically re-evaluated.

### Missed Dose
- Not specifically detailed in the provided source, though general practice for stimulants suggests avoiding late-day catch-up doses to prevent sleep disturbance.

## 4. Contraindications
Lisdexamfetamine is contraindicated in patients with:
- Advanced arteriosclerosis.
- Symptomatic cardiovascular disease (e.g., arrhythmia, ischaemic heart disease).
- Moderate to severe hypertension.
- Hyperthyroidism.
- Glaucoma.
- Phaeochromocytoma.
- Hypersensitivity or idiosyncratic reaction to sympathomimetic amines.
- Agitated states (anxiety, tension).
- History of drug or alcohol abuse.
- Current severe depression, anorexia nervosa, psychotic symptoms, or suicidal tendency.
- Tics or Tourette’s syndrome.
- Concomitant use or use within 14 days of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Abuse Potential):** **VYVANSE** carries a high potential for abuse, misuse, and dependence. Risk must be assessed prior to prescribing and monitored throughout therapy [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Events:** Sudden death, stroke, and myocardial infarction have been reported. Avoid use in patients with known structural cardiac abnormalities, cardiomyopathy, or serious heart rhythm disorders.
- **Blood Pressure/Heart Rate:** Causes modest increases (2–4 mmHg; 3–6 bpm); monitor all patients, especially those with underlying conditions.
- **QTc Prolongation:** Caution in patients with pre-existing prolongation, electrolyte disturbances, or those taking interacting medications.
- **Psychiatric Effects:** May exacerbate pre-existing psychosis or bipolar disorder (risk of mixed/manic episodes). Treatment-emergent psychosis or mania may occur in patients without a prior history.
- **Seizures:** May lower the convulsive threshold. Discontinue if seizures occur.
- **Visual Disturbance:** Blurring and accommodation difficulties have been reported.
- **Growth Suppression:** Monitor height and weight in pediatric patients; treatment interruption may be necessary if growth is not as expected.
- **Peripheral Vasculopathy:** Includes Raynaud’s phenomenon; monitor for digital changes/ulceration.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions primarily reflect sympathomimetic activity.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common ($\ge$10%)** | Decreased appetite, insomnia, headache, dry mouth, weight decreased, upper abdominal pain (pediatric), irritability (pediatric), nausea (pediatric), vomiting (pediatric). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Tachycardia, palpitations, anxiety, dizziness, restlessness, tremor, dyspnoea, diarrhoea, constipation, hyperhidrosis, pyrexia, erectile dysfunction. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Affect lability, depression, tics, somnolence, vision blurred, mydriasis, urticaria, rash. |
| **Incidence Not Known** | Anaphylactic reaction, seizure, Takotsubo cardiomyopathy, intestinal ischaemia, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, angioedema. |

*Serious Adverse Effects:* Sudden cardiac death, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, and psychotic episodes [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated; risk of hypertensive crisis.
- **Urinary pH Modifiers:** 
    - *Acidifying agents* (e.g., ascorbic acid): Increase urinary excretion and decrease half-life.
    - *Alkalinising agents* (e.g., sodium bicarbonate): Decrease urinary excretion and extend half-life.
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome when used with SSRIs, SNRIs, or triptans.
- **Antihypertensives:** Amphetamines may antagonise the effectiveness of these agents.
- **Psychotropic Agents:** Chlorpromazine, haloperidol, and lithium may inhibit the stimulatory effects of lisdexamfetamine.
- **Narcotic Analgesics:** Amphetamines may potentiate the analgesic effect.
- **CYP2D6:** Lisdexamfetamine (dexamphetamine) may be a weak inhibitor of CYP2D6 [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B3. No adequate/controlled studies in humans. Use only if benefits justify potential fetal risks. Infants born to mothers taking amphetamines should be monitored for withdrawal symptoms (irritability, agitation).
- **Breastfeeding:** Amphetamines are excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rat) showed no adverse effects on fertility at doses resulting in 4-6 times human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated for ADHD in ages 6–17. Safety/efficacy in BED or ADHD <6 years not established.
- **Elderly:** Safety/efficacy not established in patients >55 years. Clearance is slightly reduced in older adults (0.7 L/h/kg in ages 55–74 vs 1 L/h/kg in younger adults).
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Severe impairment (GFR 15 to <30 mL/min/1.73m²): Maximum dose 50 mg/day.
    - End-stage renal disease (ESRD): Further reduction required. Lisdexamfetamine and dexamphetamine are not dialysable [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No studies conducted [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A pharmacologically inactive prodrug. Following oral administration, it is rapidly absorbed and hydrolysed—primarily by red blood cells—to L-lysine and dexamphetamine. Dexamphetamine blocks the reuptake of noradrenaline and dopamine and increases their release into the extraneuronal space.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - *Absorption:* Rapid; $T_{max}$ of dexamphetamine is ~3.5 hours. Food/orange juice does not affect AUC but may delay $T_{max}$ by ~1 hour.
    - *Distribution:* No accumulation at steady state.
    - *Metabolism:* Hydrolytic conversion in blood (non-CYP450). Dexamphetamine is further metabolised (CYP2D6 involved in 4-hydroxy-amphetamine formation).
    - *Elimination:* Primarily renal (96% of dose radioactivity recovered in urine). Plasma half-life of lisdexamfetamine is <1 hour.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ADHD:** Efficacy demonstrated in short-term (4-week) and maintenance studies in children, adolescents, and adults. Significant improvements were noted in ADHD-RS scores. In analog classroom/workplace studies, effects were maintained up to 13–14 hours post-dose.
- **BED:** Two 12-week studies in adults showed statistically significant reductions in the number of binge days per week compared to placebo. Maintenance of efficacy was demonstrated over 26 weeks in responders [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on packaging [Australian].
- **Handling:** Ensure child-resistant caps are secured.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
