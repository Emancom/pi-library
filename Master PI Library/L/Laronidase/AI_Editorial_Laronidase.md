# Editorial Product Information: Laronidase

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of laronidase product information for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference point.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Laronidase-rch (recombinant human $\alpha$-L-iduronidase).
- **Chemical Class:** Purified glycosylated form of the human lysosomal enzyme $\alpha$-L-iduronidase; produced by recombinant DNA technology in a Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell line.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Aldurazyme** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AB05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Concentrate for solution for infusion: 500 U (nominal 2.9 mg) in 5.0 mL (100 U/mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Mucopolysaccharidosis I (MPS I):** Indicated for long-term enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) in patients with a confirmed $\alpha$-L-iduronidase deficiency to treat the non-neurological manifestations of the disease [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric):** 100 U/kg (0.58 mg/kg) of actual body weight administered once weekly via intravenous infusion.
- **Pretreatment:** Administration of antipyretics and/or antihistamines is recommended 60 minutes prior to the start of the infusion to reduce the risk of infusion-associated reactions (IARs).
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Preparation:** Must be diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride for Injection.
    - **Final Volume:** Total infusion volume is determined by body weight: 100 mL for patients $\leq$ 20 kg; 250 mL for patients > 20 kg.
    - **Equipment:** Use of PVC containers and PVC infusion sets equipped with an in-line, low protein-binding 0.2 $\mu$m filter is recommended.
    - **Infusion Rate:** Initial rate of 2 U/kg/hr. If tolerated, the rate may be doubled every 15 minutes during the first hour until a maximum rate of 43 U/kg/hr is reached. This maximum rate is maintained for the remaining 2–3 hours.
- **Home Infusion:** May be considered for patients who have tolerated infusions well for several months and have no history of moderate/severe IARs. Must be administered by a qualified healthcare professional with resuscitation equipment available [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known severe, life-threatening hypersensitivity (anaphylactic reaction) to laronidase or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis:** Life-threatening anaphylactic reactions, including respiratory failure, stridor, and hypotension, have been observed. Resuscitation equipment must be immediately available. 
- **Compromised Respiratory Function:** Patients with acute respiratory illness or compromised lung function are at higher risk of serious IARs and require intensified monitoring.
- **Adrenaline Caution:** Use caution if administering adrenaline to patients with MPS I due to the increased prevalence of coronary artery disease in this population.
- **Infusion-Associated Reactions (IARs):** Very common. If a reaction occurs, the infusion should be slowed or temporarily stopped.
- **Immunogenicity:** Nearly all patients develop IgG antibodies (ADA), typically within 3 months. High ADA titers may correlate with a reduced effect on urinary glycosaminoglycans (GAGs). A small number of patients develop IgE antibodies.
- **Monitoring:** Periodic assessment of urinary GAGs and, if clinical decline is noted, ADA/neutralising antibody testing should be considered.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - Upper respiratory tract infection, rash, injection site reactions (including extravasation, swelling, and erythema), hyperreflexia, paresthesia, flushing, and fever.
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Chest pain, abscess, bilirubinaemia, vein disorder, facial oedema, hypotension, dependent oedema, corneal opacity, thrombocytopenia.

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, respiratory arrest, and laryngeal oedema. Post-marketing reports include oxygen saturation decrease and tachycardia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal studies conducted. Metabolism via peptide hydrolysis makes cytochrome P450 interactions unlikely.
- **Specific Interactions:** 
    - **Chloroquine/Procaine:** Should not be administered simultaneously due to a theoretical risk of interference with intracellular uptake of laronidase [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** Must not be mixed with other medicinal products in the same infusion bag.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B2 [Australian]:** Animal studies (rats) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm at 9 times the human dose. However, no adequate studies exist in pregnant women. Use only if clearly necessary.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if laronidase is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women.
- **Fertility:** No clinical data available; animal studies showed no impairment of mating or fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in patients aged 5 to 16 years. Data for patients < 5 years are limited [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include patients $\geq$ 65 years. Dosing should be cautious, reflecting the higher frequency of renal, hepatic, or cardiac impairment.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied. However, laronidase is expected to undergo peptide hydrolysis; therefore, hepatic or renal impairment is not expected to significantly impact pharmacokinetics.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Laronidase provides an exogenous source of the lysosomal enzyme $\alpha$-L-iduronidase. This enzyme facilitates the catabolism of the glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) dermatan sulfate and heparan sulfate. In MPS I, deficiency of this enzyme leads to GAG accumulation in various tissues.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** ERT aims to restore enzymatic activity to hydrolyse accumulated substrate. Clinical effect is measured by reductions in urinary GAGs and liver size.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly cleared from circulation and taken up into lysosomes via mannose-6-phosphate receptors. Volume of distribution ($V_{ss}$) is 0.22–0.44 L/kg.
    - **Elimination:** Mean elimination half-life ($t_{1/2}$) ranges from 1.94 to 3.61 hours. Clearance ($CL$) is 1.7–2.3 mL/min/kg. Clearance may be influenced by antibody formation.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Phase 3 Study:** A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=45) over 26 weeks.
    - **Lung Function:** Laronidase-treated patients showed a 5.9 percentage point mean difference in predicted Forced Vital Capacity (FVC) over placebo (p=0.016).
    - **Walking Ability:** Mean improvement of 38.1 metres in the 6-Minute Walk Test (6MWT) compared to placebo (p=0.066).
    - **Organomegaly:** 72% of patients with baseline hepatomegaly experienced normalisation of liver volume.
    - **Biomarkers:** Rapid and significant decline in urinary GAG excretion (p<0.001).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C. Protect from light.
- **Stability:** Do not freeze or shake.
- **Diluted Solution:** Contains no preservatives. From a microbiological perspective, it should be used immediately. If necessary, it may be stored at 2°C to 8°C for up to 24 hours.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
