# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Note:** While the user instructions referenced "Levodopa with carbidopa and entacapone," the provided source document is for **Levocarnitine Injection**. Consequently, this editorial focuses exclusively on **Levocarnitine**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a synthesis of available regulatory data and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should be every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less frequent than every 6 hours (q6h).
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives.

### Monitoring
- **Plasma Concentrations:** Recommended pre-therapy and at weekly/monthly intervals.
- **Target Range:** Free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history, there may be an increase in seizure frequency or severity [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution):** While this PI covers the injection, it notes that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (frequency $\ge$ 5% regardless of causality):

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence $\ge$ 5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations. |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, dyspepsia. |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight increase/decrease. |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression. |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis. |
| **Skin/Appendages** | Pruritus, rash. |

**Serious/Notable Effects:**
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (Postmarketing).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis (less frequent).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned, but data provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, no adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) for metabolic disorders; clinical monitoring of plasma levels is essential [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specially indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned in renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available source.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic/fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Dialysis Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed significant increases in plasma levocarnitine. Trough levels increased from 21.3 $\pm$ 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 $\pm$ 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature when mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available US source.
