# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine injection** for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is **50 mg/kg** administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours).
    - Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses as high as 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** Recommended starting dose is **10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg** (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Monitor plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs weekly/monthly [US].
- **ESRD:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, particularly in ESRD patients. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine Limitation:** The safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.
- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting, body odor, abdominal pain, diarrhea [US].
- **Cardiovascular (ESRD populations):** Hypertension (approx. 18–21%), hypotension (up to 19%), tachycardia, and arrhythmias [US].
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (10–18%), headache, paresthesia, and seizures (frequency not established) [US].
- **Other:** Injection site reactions (27–38%), fever, pharyngitis, and hypercalcemia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labelling, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8x human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm.] Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) for metabolic disorders; safety and efficacy profiles are consistent with adult populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Highly relevant in ESRD; see Section 3 for specific dialysis dosing. Accumulation of metabolites is a concern with *oral* formulations in this group [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Primarily excreted renally (approx. 76% of an IV dose within 24 hours). Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While carnitine concentrations increase significantly, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in ESRD remains to be fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
