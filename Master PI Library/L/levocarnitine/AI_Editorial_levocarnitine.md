## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **levocarnitine** [Source: US (FDA) PI, DESCRIPTION].  
- Chemical class: endogenous quaternary ammonium compound involved in fatty‑acid transport (described as a carrier molecule facilitating long‑chain fatty acid transport across the mitochondrial inner membrane) [Source: US (FDA) PI, CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY; DESCRIPTION].  
- Chemical name / formula / molecular weight (selected data): (R)-3‑Carboxy-2‑hydroxy‑N,N,N‑trimethyl‑1‑propanaminium (inner salt); Empirical formula C7H15NO3; MW 161.20 g/mol [Source: US (FDA) PI, DESCRIPTION].  
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.  
- Dosage form(s) and strength(s) by jurisdiction: Intravenous sterile solution, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [Source: US (FDA) PI, HOW SUPPLIED; DESCRIPTION].  
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous only (parenteral) [Source: US (FDA) PI, FOR INTRAVENOUS USE ONLY; DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION].  
- Manufacturer / sponsor (by jurisdiction): Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd., Ahmedabad, India; manufactured for Baxter Healthcare Corporation, Deerfield, IL, USA [Source: US (FDA) PI, Manufactured by / Manufactured for (package panel)].

## 3. Approved Indications
- Acute and chronic treatment of patients with an inborn error of metabolism that results in secondary carnitine deficiency [Source: US (FDA) PI, INDICATIONS AND USAGE].  
- Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end stage renal disease (ESRD) who are undergoing dialysis [Source: US (FDA) PI, INDICATIONS AND USAGE].

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Metabolic disorders (inborn errors of metabolism with secondary carnitine deficiency): recommended 50 mg/kg given as a slow 2–3 minute intravenous bolus injection or by infusion; frequently a loading dose is given in severe metabolic crisis followed by equivalent dosing over the next 24 hours; dosing should be q3h or q4h and never less frequently than q6h (all subsequent daily doses generally in the range of 50 mg/kg or as clinically required); highest dose administered reported as 300 mg/kg [Source: US (FDA) PI, DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION].  
- ESRD patients on hemodialysis: recommended starting dose 10–20 mg/kg (dry body weight) as a slow 2–3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session; initiation may be prompted by trough (pre‑dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations below normal (40–50 µmol/L); dose adjustments guided by trough concentrations and downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg after dialysis) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [Source: US (FDA) PI, DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION].

### Administration instructions
- Administer intravenously only; inspect parenteral product visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration where the solution and container permit [Source: US (FDA) PI, DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION].  
- Parenteral compatibility: compatible and stable when mixed in 0.9% sodium chloride or Lactated Ringer’s solutions at concentrations from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL and stored at room temperature (25°C) for up to 24 hours in PVC bags [Source: US (FDA) PI, COMPATIBILITY AND STABILITY].

### Monitoring and therapeutic targets
- Recommended monitoring: obtain a baseline plasma carnitine concentration prior to initiating parenteral therapy and monitor weekly and monthly thereafter; monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, plasma carnitine concentrations (target plasma free carnitine concentration cited as between 35 and 60 µmol/L), and overall clinical condition [Source: US (FDA) PI, DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION].

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources as a boxed warning.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity: serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported following intravenous levocarnitine administration, most often in patients with ESRD on dialysis; some reactions occurred within minutes of administration [Source: US (FDA) PI, WARNINGS].  
  - If a severe hypersensitivity reaction occurs, discontinue levocarnitine and initiate appropriate medical treatment; if re‑administration is considered, monitor closely for recurrence [Source: US (FDA) PI, WARNINGS].  
- Metabolite accumulation in renal impairment: chronic administration of high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely impaired renal function or ESRD may result in accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine‑N‑oxide (TMAO), which are normally renally excreted [Source: US (FDA) PI, PRECAUTIONS — General].  
- Seizures: seizures have been reported in patients receiving oral or intravenous levocarnitine, including patients with or without pre‑existing seizure disorders; in patients with pre‑existing seizure activity, increased frequency and/or severity of seizures has been reported (postmarketing experience) [Source: US (FDA) PI, ADVERSE REACTIONS — Postmarketing Experience].  
- Drug interactions: increases in international normalized ratio (INR) have been reported with concomitant warfarin therapy; INR monitoring is recommended after initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine [Source: US (FDA) PI, PRECAUTIONS — Drug Interactions].  
- Carcinogenesis / mutagenesis / fertility: mutagenicity tests cited indicate levocarnitine is not mutagenic; no long‑term animal carcinogenicity studies reported in the supplied PI [Source: US (FDA) PI, PRECAUTIONS — Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility].

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic plasma carnitine concentrations, blood chemistries, vital signs, and clinical assessment are recommended during therapy; target plasma free carnitine concentration recommended 35–60 µmol/L [Source: US (FDA) PI, DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION].

### Specific clinical precautions
- Pregnancy: reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) did not reveal impaired fertility or fetal harm, but there are no adequate and well‑controlled human studies; use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [Source: US (FDA) PI, PRECAUTIONS — Pregnancy].  
- Nursing mothers: levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers not specifically studied; dairy cow studies show increased milk concentrations after exogenous levocarnitine; weigh risks/benefits of therapy in nursing mothers and consider discontinuation of nursing or levocarnitine treatment [Source: US (FDA) PI, PRECAUTIONS — Nursing Mothers].  
- Pediatric use: see DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION for pediatric recommendations (PI references pediatric dosing guidance) [Source: US (FDA) PI, PRECAUTIONS — Pediatric Use; DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION].  
- Dialysis removal: levocarnitine is readily removed from plasma by dialysis (noted in OVERDOSAGE) — relevant when timing dosing relative to dialysis and when interpreting concentrations [Source: US (FDA) PI, OVERDOSAGE; PHARMACOKINETICS data].

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Warfarin: reports of INR increases with coadministration of levocarnitine have been observed; monitor INR after initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on warfarin [Source: US (FDA) PI, PRECAUTIONS — Drug Interactions].  
- Dialysis: levocarnitine is removed by dialysis and dialysis will affect plasma concentrations (relevant when dosing patients on hemodialysis) [Source: US (FDA) PI, OVERDOSAGE; PHARMACOKINETICS].  
- Other interactions: No additional pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic interactions are specified in the provided PI. If not listed here, interaction data are Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy: Animal reproductive studies (rats and rabbits) up to 3.8 times human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm; there are no adequate and well‑controlled studies in pregnant women; use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [Source: US (FDA) PI, PRECAUTIONS — Pregnancy].  
- Pregnancy category/classification: No jurisdictional pregnancy classification (e.g., US letter category) is provided in the supplied PI beyond the narrative statement above [Source: US (FDA) PI, PRECAUTIONS — Pregnancy].  
- Breastfeeding: Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers not specifically studied in humans; dairy cow studies indicate increased levocarnitine concentration in milk following administration, thus weigh potential infant exposure against maternal benefit and consider discontinuing nursing or levocarnitine if concerns exist [Source: US (FDA) PI, PRECAUTIONS — Nursing Mothers].  
- Fertility: Mutagenicity tests indicate levocarnitine is not mutagenic; no human fertility data beyond animal reproductive studies provided [Source: US (FDA) PI, PRECAUTIONS — Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility; PRECAUTIONS — Pregnancy].

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance that facilitates transport of long‑chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, delivering substrate for β‑oxidation and energy production in tissues (skeletal and cardiac muscle rely primarily on fatty acids for energy) [Source: US (FDA) PI, CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY].

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption (oral): Absolute oral bioavailability reported as ~15.1% for tablets and ~15.9% for oral solution after correction for endogenous plasma concentrations; Tmax for oral formulations reported around 3.3 hours after repeated dosing in healthy volunteers [Source: US (FDA) PI, PHARMACOKINETICS].  
- Distribution: Plasma concentration profile after IV described by two‑compartment model; levocarnitine was not bound to plasma protein or albumin in tests [Source: US (FDA) PI, PHARMACOKINETICS].  
- Elimination: Following single IV administration, ~76% of dose excreted in urine during 0–24 hours (plasma concentrations uncorrected for endogenous levocarnitine); mean distribution half‑life ~0.585 hours; mean apparent terminal elimination half‑life ~17.4 hours [Source: US (FDA) PI, PHARMACOKINETICS].  
- Clearance: Total body clearance mean reported as 4.00 L/h (including endogenous baseline concentrations) [Source: US (FDA) PI, PHARMACOKINETICS].  
- Metabolism and excretion: After oral radiolabeled dosing, major metabolites included trimethylamine N‑oxide (primarily urinary excretion, 8–49% of dose) and γ‑butyrobetaine (primarily fecal, 0.44–45% of dose); urinary excretion of unchanged levocarnitine ~4–8% of dose in that study; fecal excretion of total carnitine <1% [Source: US (FDA) PI, METABOLISM AND EXCRETION].  
- Dialysis removal: Levocarnitine is removed by dialysis (e.g., dialysis reduces plasma concentrations substantially) [Source: US (FDA) PI, OVERDOSAGE; PHARMACOKINETICS; DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION (ESRD studies)].

### Pharmacodynamics
- Clinical pharmacodynamic effects described primarily as restoration of plasma/tissue carnitine concentrations and facilitation of acylcarnitine formation to clear accumulated acyl‑CoA compounds in organic acidemias; clinical outcome benefits in some settings (e.g., clearance of acylCoA compounds) are discussed in CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY [Source: US (FDA) PI, CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY].

## 12. Clinical Trials
- ESRD hemodialysis pharmacokinetic/clinical studies: a 9‑week study in 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis demonstrated increases in peak and trough plasma levocarnitine concentrations with dosing; trough concentrations rose from ~21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks in that cohort; similar studies reported 12‑ and 24‑week mean trough concentrations around 189 µmol/L and 243 µmol/L in separate cohorts receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly [Source: US (FDA) PI, PHARMACOKINETICS].  
- Dose‑ranging in ESRD: A study with 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg three times weekly post‑dialysis (N≈30 per dose group) reported mean trough concentrations increasing with dose (e.g., after 24 weeks: 10 mg/kg ~148 µmol/L; 20 mg/kg ~240 µmol/L; 40 mg/kg ~456 µmol/L) — source presents pharmacokinetic outcomes but notes that while levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations, effects on clinical signs/symptoms and clinical outcomes in this population have not been determined [Source: US (FDA) PI, PHARMACOKINETICS; INDICATIONS AND USAGE].  
- Double‑blind placebo‑controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients: two trials are cited and adverse events ≥5% are reported in the clinical trials adverse reaction table in the supplied PI; the trials contributed the adverse event frequency data presented in ADVERSE REACTIONS [Source: US (FDA) PI, ADVERSE REACTIONS — Clinical Trials Experience].  
- Note: The supplied PI emphasizes pharmacokinetic and safety data and explicitly states that although carnitine concentrations increase with supplementation in ESRD, effects on signs/symptoms and clinical outcomes have not been established [Source: US (FDA) PI, INDICATIONS AND USAGE; PHARMACOKINETICS].

## 14. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) — controlled room temperature; avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing [Source: US (FDA) PI, HOW SUPPLIED; Package/Label Display Panel].  
- Single‑dose vials: discard any unused portion after opening because the formulation contains no preservative [Source: US (FDA) PI, HOW SUPPLIED; Package/Label Display Panel].  
- Compatibility/stability for admixture: compatible with 0.9% sodium chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL and stable at room temperature for up to 24 hours in PVC bags [Source: US (FDA) PI, COMPATIBILITY AND STABILITY].
