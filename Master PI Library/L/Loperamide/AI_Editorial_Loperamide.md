## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Loperamide hydrochloride (a synthetic antidiarrheal).
- **Chemical Class:** Phenylpiperidine derivative; mu-opioid receptor agonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** Imodium [common genericized name]; specific provided source is generic Loperamide Hydrochloride (Mylan Pharmaceuticals).
- **ATC Code:** A07DA03 (Antipropulsives).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Capsules: 2 mg [US].
  - Liquid (non-prescription): 1 mg/5 mL [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mylan Pharmaceuticals Inc. (Distributed by Cardinal Health/Safecor Health) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Nonspecific Diarrhea:** Control and symptomatic relief in patients 2 years of age and older [US].
- **Chronic Diarrhea:** Symptomatic relief in adults associated with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) [US].
- **Ileostomy Discharge:** Reduction of the volume of discharge from ileostomies [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Acute Diarrhea
- **Adults and Pediatrics (13 years and older):** 
  - Initial dose: 4 mg (two capsules).
  - Subsequent: 2 mg after each unformed stool. 
  - Maximum daily dose: 16 mg [US].
- **Pediatrics (2 to 12 years):** 
  - **2–5 years (13–20 kg):** 1 mg three times daily on Day 1 (liquid formulation preferred).
  - **6–8 years (20–30 kg):** 2 mg twice daily on Day 1.
  - **8–12 years (>30 kg):** 2 mg three times daily on Day 1.
  - **Subsequent Days:** 1 mg/10 kg body weight administered only after a loose stool. Total daily dose should not exceed the Day 1 recommended dose [US].

### Chronic Diarrhea (Adults)
- **Initial dose:** 4 mg followed by 2 mg after each unformed stool until diarrhea is controlled.
- **Maintenance:** Reduce dose to meet individual requirements. Average maintenance dose in trials was 4 mg to 8 mg daily. 
- **Maximum dose:** 16 mg daily. If no improvement is seen at 16 mg/day for 10 days, further administration is unlikely to be effective [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Appropriate fluid and electrolyte replacement is essential for all patients with diarrhea.
- If clinical improvement in acute diarrhea is not observed within 48 hours, discontinue use [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pediatric patients <2 years of age:** Due to risks of respiratory depression and serious cardiac adverse reactions [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to loperamide or any excipients [US].
- **Abdominal Pain:** In the absence of diarrhea [US].
- **Acute Dysentery:** Characterized by blood in stools and high fever [US].
- **Acute Ulcerative Colitis:** Risk of toxic megacolon [US].
- **Bacterial Enterocolitis:** Caused by invasive organisms (e.g., *Salmonella, Shigella, Campylobacter*) [US].
- **Pseudomembranous Colitis:** Associated with broad-spectrum antibiotic use (e.g., *C. difficile*) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **Torsades de Pointes and Sudden Death:** Cases of Torsades de Pointes, cardiac arrest, and death have been reported with dosages higher than recommended. Avoid supratherapeutic doses.

### Cardiac Adverse Reactions
- Loperamide can prolong the QT/QTc interval. 
- Avoid use in combination with other QT-prolonging drugs (Class 1A or Class III antiarrhythmics, certain antipsychotics, and antibiotics) [US].
- Avoid in patients with risk factors for QT prolongation (congenital long QT syndrome, electrolyte abnormalities, or history of cardiac arrhythmias) [US].

### Gastrointestinal Risks
- Discontinue promptly if constipation, abdominal distention, or ileus develop. 
- Patients with AIDS should stop therapy at the first sign of abdominal distention (risk of toxic megacolon) [US].

### Hepatic Impairment
- Use with caution due to reduced first-pass metabolism. Monitor closely for signs of central nervous system (CNS) toxicity [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often difficult to distinguish from symptoms of the underlying diarrheal syndrome.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Constipation (2.6% in acute; 5.3% in chronic), Dizziness (1.4% in chronic), Nausea, Abdominal cramps [US]. |
| **Post-marketing/Rare** | **Cardiac:** QT prolongation, Torsades de Pointes, cardiac arrest, syncope. **Skin:** Rash, urticaria, angioedema; extremely rare cases of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN). **Immune:** Anaphylaxis and anaphylactoid reactions. **GI:** Megacolon, paralytic ileus. **Other:** Urinary retention, drowsiness [US]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
Loperamide is a substrate for P-glycoprotein (P-gp), CYP3A4, and CYP2C8.
- **P-gp Inhibitors (e.g., Quinidine, Ritonavir):** Concomitant use can result in a 2- to 3-fold increase in loperamide plasma levels and may increase CNS penetration [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Itraconazole):** Can increase loperamide exposure by 3.8-fold [US].
- **CYP2C8 Inhibitors (e.g., Gemfibrozil):** Can increase loperamide exposure by 2.2-fold [US].
- **Combined CYP3A4 and CYP2C8 Inhibition:** Concomitant itraconazole and gemfibrozil increased loperamide peak plasma concentration by 4.2-fold and total exposure by 12.6-fold [US].
- **Saquinavir:** Loperamide may decrease saquinavir exposure by up to 54%, potentially reducing its therapeutic efficacy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category C [US]:** Teratogenicity studies in rats and rabbits at high doses showed no evidence of harm to the fetus, but high doses (11x human dose) impaired female fertility. Loperamide should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts of loperamide may appear in human milk. Use is not recommended during breastfeeding [US].
- **Fertility:** High doses in animal studies (40 mg/kg/day) produced impairment of both male and female fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
  - Contraindicated under 2 years. 
  - Greater variability in response in pediatric patients; higher risk of CNS effects (altered mental status, somnolence, respiratory depression) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No significant pharmacokinetic differences observed, but elderly patients may be more susceptible to drug-associated QT prolongation [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required as the drug is primarily excreted via feces [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; monitor for CNS toxicity due to increased systemic exposure [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Binds to the opiate receptor in the gut wall, inhibiting the release of acetylcholine and prostaglandins. This slows intestinal motility/peristalsis and increases transit time. It also increases anal sphincter tone [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces fecal volume, increases viscosity/bulk density, and diminishes fluid/electrolyte loss. No tolerance to the antidiarrheal effect has been observed [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations occur at ~5 hours (capsule) or ~2.5 hours (liquid) [US].
  - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is approximately 95%. It is a P-glycoprotein substrate [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily via oxidative N-demethylation (CYP3A4 and CYP2C8) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Apparent half-life is 10.8 hours (range 9.1–14.4 hours). Excretion occurs mainly through the feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Acute Diarrhea:** In 4 placebo-controlled studies (n=231), constipation was the only adverse event reported more frequently than placebo (2.6% vs 0.8%) [US].
- **Chronic Diarrhea:** In 20 placebo-controlled studies (n=285), constipation (5.3%) and dizziness (1.4%) were significantly more frequent than placebo [US].
- **Abuse Potential:** A study of opioid users showed that at 60 mg single doses, loperamide was identified as "dope" by 11% of subjects, indicating a low but present potential for abuse at high doses [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F).
- **Packaging:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers with child-resistant closures [US].
- **Institutional Use:** Unit dose packages provided by Cardinal Health are not child-resistant and are intended for institutional use only [US].
