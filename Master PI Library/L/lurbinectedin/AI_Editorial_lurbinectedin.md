# Editorial Product Information: Lurbinectedin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Regulatory Agencies:** Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA - Australia), Food and Drug Administration (FDA - United States).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of official Product Information (PI) for healthcare professionals. It aims to provide a single, clinically accurate reference by bridging regional documentation.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the local approved PI for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lurbinectedin (an alkylating drug).
- **Brand Name:** **ZEPZELCA**.
- **ATC Code:** L01CX01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 4 mg of lyophilised powder in a single-dose vial for reconstitution.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Specialised Therapeutics Pharma Pty Ltd [TGA]; Jazz Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Extensive-Stage Small Cell Lung Cancer (ES-SCLC) Maintenance:** In combination with atezolizumab (or atezolizumab and hyaluronidase-tqjs [FDA]), for the maintenance treatment of adult patients with ES-SCLC whose disease has not progressed after first-line induction therapy with atezolizumab, carboplatin, and etoposide.
- **Metastatic Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC):** For the treatment of adult patients with metastatic SCLC with disease progression on or after platinum-based chemotherapy. 
    - *Note:* This indication was approved under provisional [TGA] or accelerated [FDA] pathways based on objective response rate and duration of response. Continued approval may be contingent upon verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 3.2 mg/m² administered via intravenous infusion over 60 minutes, repeated every 21 days until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
- **Pre-treatment Requirements:** Initiate only if absolute neutrophil count (ANC) is $\ge$ 1.5 x $10^9$/L (1,500 cells/mm³) and platelet count is $\ge$ 100 x $10^9$/L (100,000/mm³).
- **Combination Dosing:** When administered on the same day as atezolizumab, administer atezolizumab first.
- **Prophylactic Medication:** 
    - **Antiemetics:** Consider corticosteroids (dexamethasone 8 mg IV or equivalent) and serotonin antagonists (ondansetron 8 mg IV or equivalent) prior to infusion.
    - **G-CSF:** Recommended as primary prophylaxis in combination with atezolizumab to reduce the risk of febrile neutropenia [TGA/FDA].
- **Dose Modifications for Toxicity:**
    - **First dose reduction:** 2.6 mg/m² every 21 days.
    - **Second dose reduction:** 2.0 mg/m² every 21 days.
    - **Discontinuation:** Permanently discontinue if the patient cannot tolerate 2.0 mg/m² or requires a dose delay > 2 weeks.
- **Preparation and Administration:**
    - Reconstitute with 8 mL Sterile Water for Injection to a concentration of 0.5 mg/mL.
    - **Central line:** Add to $\ge$ 100 mL diluent (0.9% NaCl or 5% Glucose/Dextrose). Recommended to reduce extravasation risk.
    - **Peripheral line:** Add to $\ge$ 250 mL diluent (0.9% NaCl or 5% Glucose/Dextrose).
    - **Filters:** Polyethersulfone (PES) 0.22 micron in-line filters are recommended. Do not use nylon membrane filters when 0.9% NaCl is the diluent due to adsorption.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known history of significant drug allergy to lurbinectedin or any of the excipients [TGA].
- No contraindications listed in FDA highlights [FDA].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Can cause severe and fatal myelosuppression, including Grade 3-4 neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and anaemia. Monitor blood counts prior to each dose. Febrile neutropenia and sepsis have occurred.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Liver function test elevations (ALT/AST) are common. Monitor LFTs at baseline and periodically. Withhold, reduce, or discontinue based on severity.
- **Extravasation and Tissue Necrosis:** Extravasation can lead to skin and soft tissue injury requiring debridement. Monitor infusion sites closely.
- **Rhabdomyolysis:** Reported in patients treated with lurbinectedin. Monitor creatine phosphokinase (CPK) at baseline and periodically.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential prior to initiation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are categorised as follows based on pooled data (Monotherapy and Combination):
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **General:** Fatigue (up to 77%), pyrexia, peripheral edema.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (up to 37%), constipation, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, decreased appetite.
    - **Respiratory:** Dyspnoea, cough.
    - **Musculoskeletal:** Musculoskeletal pain (up to 33%).
    - **Neurological:** Peripheral neuropathy, headache.
    - **Haematology (Lab):** Decreased lymphocytes, leukocytes, neutrophils, haemoglobin, and platelets.
    - **Chemistry (Lab):** Increased ALT, AST, creatinine, glucose, and alkaline phosphatase; decreased sodium, albumin, magnesium, and calcium.
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Febrile neutropenia, sepsis, pneumonia, respiratory tract infection, chest pain, dysgeusia, pneumonitis.
- **Post-marketing Experience (Frequency not established):**
    - Tumour lysis syndrome.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Coadministration with strong inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole, clarithromycin) increases lurbinectedin exposure. Avoid coadministration. If a moderate inhibitor (e.g., fluconazole, diltiazem) cannot be avoided, consider a 50% dose reduction [FDA] or adjust based on tolerability [TGA].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Avoid strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St. John’s wort) as they may significantly decrease efficacy.
- **Food Interactions:** Avoid grapefruit and Seville oranges during treatment due to CYP3A4 inhibition.
- **Transporters:** Lurbinectedin is a substrate of MDR1 (P-gp). It inhibits OATP1B1, OATP1B3, and OCT1 in vitro.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **TGA Category:** Category D.
    - **FDA Recommendation:** Clinical data are unavailable; however, animal studies show 100% embryolethality at 0.2 times the human dose. Advise of potential fetal risk.
- **Contraception:**
    - **Females:** Use highly effective contraception during and for 7 months after the last dose.
    - **Males:** Use highly effective contraception if having a partner of reproductive potential during and for 4 months after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** No data on presence in human milk. Advise patients not to breastfeed during treatment and for 2 weeks after the final dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in efficacy between patients $\ge$ 65 and < 65 years. However, patients $\ge$ 65 years experienced a higher incidence of serious adverse reactions related to myelosuppression.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild:** No dose adjustment required. 
    - **Moderate (Total bilirubin > 1.5 to 3 x ULN):** Reduce dose to 1.6 mg/m² [FDA]; effect unknown [TGA].
    - **Severe (Total bilirubin > 3 x ULN):** Avoid use; if unavoidable, use 1.6 mg/m² [FDA]; effect unknown [TGA].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild to moderate impairment (CrCl 30-89 mL/min). Not studied in severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) or end-stage renal disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lurbinectedin binds guanine residues in the minor groove of DNA, forming adducts that bend the helix. This disrupts the activity of DNA-binding proteins and transcription factors, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis. It also reduces tumour-associated macrophage infiltration.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** 99% plasma protein bound (albumin and $\alpha$-1-acid glycoprotein); Vd at steady state is 504 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 51 hours; clearance is 11 L/h. 89% excreted in faeces and 6% in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **IMforte Study:** A randomised trial in ES-SCLC maintenance. Lurbinectedin + atezolizumab significantly improved median Overall Survival (13.2 months vs. 10.6 months) and Progression-Free Survival (5.4 months vs. 2.1 months) compared to atezolizumab alone.
- **Study B-005:** A multicentre, single-arm study in metastatic SCLC (second-line). Demonstrated an Overall Response Rate (ORR) of 35% (Investigator-assessed) and a median Duration of Response (DOR) of 5.3 months. Response was higher in platinum-sensitive patients (CTFI $\ge$ 90 days) at 45% vs. 22% in platinum-resistant patients.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Refrigerate at $2^\circ\text{C}$ to $8^\circ\text{C}$. Do not freeze.
- **Shelf Life:** 60 months [TGA].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Stable for 24 hours (including infusion time) at room temperature/ambient light or refrigerated ($2^\circ\text{C}$ to $8^\circ\text{C}$).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia [TGA]:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States [FDA]:** Rx Only.
- **Handling:** Lurbinectedin is a cytotoxic drug. Follow applicable hazardous drug handling and disposal procedures.
