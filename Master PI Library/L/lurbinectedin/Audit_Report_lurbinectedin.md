# Originality Audit Report: lurbinectedin

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 82/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 4
- **Overall Assessment:** Pass

## Verbatim Copied Passages
1. **Passage:** "3.2 mg/m² administered via intravenous infusion over 60 minutes, repeated every 21 days until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity."
   - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA, Section 4.2) / Document 2 (FDA, Section 2.1)
   - **Location:** Editorial Section 3 (General Adult Dosing)
   - **Note:** This is the standard regulatory phrasing for the dosing schedule and is difficult to paraphrase without losing clinical precision.

2. **Passage:** "Lurbinectedin binds guanine residues in the minor groove of DNA, forming adducts that bend the helix."
   - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA, Section 5.1) / Document 2 (FDA, Section 12.1)
   - **Location:** Editorial Section 10 (Mechanism of Action)
   - **Note:** This matches the opening sentence of the Mechanism of Action in both source documents almost exactly.

3. **Passage:** "99% plasma protein bound (albumin and $\alpha$-1-acid glycoprotein); Vd at steady state is 504 L."
   - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA, Section 5.2) / Document 2 (FDA, Section 12.3)
   - **Location:** Editorial Section 10 (Pharmacokinetics)
   - **Note:** While technical data, the parenthetical phrasing "Vd at steady state" and the protein binding list are identical to the source text.

4. **Passage:** "Do not use nylon membrane filters when 0.9% NaCl is the diluent due to adsorption."
   - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA, Section 4.2) / Document 2 (FDA, Section 2.6)
   - **Location:** Editorial Section 3 (Preparation and Administration - Filters)
   - **Note:** Near-verbatim reproduction of the specific warning regarding adsorption to nylon.

## Close Paraphrases
1. **Dose Modifications:** The editorial's section on "First dose reduction" and "Second dose reduction" (2.6 mg/m² and 2.0 mg/m²) follows the exact structure of Table 1 in both source documents.
2. **Warnings:** The "Extravasation and Tissue Necrosis" warning ("Extravasation can lead to skin and soft tissue injury requiring debridement") is a very close paraphrase of the FDA and TGA Warning sections.
3. **Indications Note:** The note regarding "provisional [TGA] or accelerated [FDA] pathways" and that "Continued approval may be contingent upon verification of clinical benefit" follows the mandatory regulatory language found in section 4.1 of the TGA PI and 1.2 of the FDA PI.

## Originality Strengths
- **Harmonization:** The editorial successfully merges distinct regional requirements into a single section (e.g., Section 9: Hepatic Impairment), noting where the FDA provides specific dosing for moderate impairment (1.6 mg/m²) while the TGA lists it as "unknown."
- **Adverse Effects Synthesis:** Instead of just copying the tables, the writer pooled data from "Monotherapy and Combination" trials to categorize frequencies (Very Common vs. Common), which required active restructuring of the source data.
- **Dose Preparation:** The writer synthesized the complex preparation instructions into a concise "Central line" vs "Peripheral line" comparison, which is presented as separate bullet points in the source but merged here for clarity.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Structure:** The editorial uses a custom numbering system (1–14) that does not match the standard 1–10/17 format of the TGA or FDA, indicating a structural reorganization.
- **Terminology:** The editorial uses "Active Ingredient," "Brand Name," and "ATC Code" (L01CX01), the latter of which is not present in the provided source text and was added for clinical utility.
- **Omissions:** The editorial omits highly specific pharmacokinetic parameters (e.g., geometric means of Cmax and AUC) and detailed clinical trial exclusion criteria to maintain an editorial summary level.
- **Categorization:** Use of TGA Pregnancy Category D alongside FDA "Clinical data unavailable" provides a comparative regulatory view not found in either single document.

## Recommendation
**PASS**

The editorial is a high-quality synthesis. While certain technical instructions (dosing, reconstitution, and mechanism of action) remain near-verbatim, this is generally accepted in regulatory medical writing where accuracy regarding concentrations and life-saving instructions is paramount. The author has demonstrated originality through the comparative analysis of regional differences and the restructuring of raw laboratory data into frequency-based categories. No further revision is required.
