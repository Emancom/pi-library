# Editorial Product Information: Liothyronine

## Editorial Note
*   **Jurisdictions Included:** This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information. Clinicians should refer to the local PI approved in their specific region for prescribing purposes.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Liothyronine sodium (synthetic L-triiodothyronine).
*   **Chemical Class:** Thyroid hormone.
*   **Brand Names:** Tertroxin [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **Tablet:** 20 microgram (uncoated, break line) [Australian].
*   **Routes of Administration:** Oral; may be administered via stomach tube in specific clinical scenarios [Australian]. (Note: Intravenous use is mentioned as a clinical alternative but is not the primary form described in this document).
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Myxoedema Coma:** Treatment of coma resulting from myxoedema [Australian].
*   **Severe Thyroid Deficiency:** Management of severe acute hypothyroid states where a rapid and potent effect is desired [Australian].
*   **Hypothyroidism during Thyrotoxicosis Treatment:** Treatment of hypothyroid states arising from the management of thyrotoxicosis [Australian].
*   **Adjunctive Therapy:** Used as an adjunct to carbimazole in thyrotoxicosis to prevent the development of sub-clinical hypothyroidism during treatment [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   Liothyronine should be administered in divided doses, typically two or three times daily [Australian].
*   **Myxoedema:** Unless an immediate effect is required, treatment should commence at 10–20 micrograms every 8 hours. Dosage may be increased incrementally to a total daily maintenance dose of 60 micrograms [Australian].
*   **Thyrotoxicosis (with carbimazole):** 20 micrograms every 8 hours [Australian].

### Specific Scenarios
*   **Myxoedema Coma:** Should be initiated under specialist supervision. A dose of 60 micrograms may be given via stomach tube, followed by 20 micrograms every 8 hours. (Intravenous administration is often preferred where available) [Australian].

### Titration and Administration
*   **Maintenance:** While liothyronine can be used for maintenance, thyroxine is generally preferred for long-term therapy due to its smoother metabolic profile [Australian].
*   **Sub-10 microgram doses:** Tablets may be crushed and triturated with lactose; however, uniform dispersion and bioavailability cannot be guaranteed [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to liothyronine sodium [Australian].
*   **Cardiovascular Disease:** Patients with angina of effort or other cardiovascular disorders [Australian].
*   **Endocrine Disorders:** Untreated adrenal cortical insufficiency or untreated hyperthyroidism [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
*   No boxed warnings were identified in the available source [Australian].

### General Warnings
*   **Obesity Management:** Liothyronine **must not** be used for the treatment of obesity in euthyroid patients. Doses within the daily requirement range are ineffective for weight loss, while larger doses may cause serious or life-threatening toxic effects [Australian].
*   **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use with extreme caution in patients with cardiovascular disorders, including those with angina and the elderly. Patients should report any chest pain, palpitations, or excessive sweating immediately [Australian].
*   **Adrenal Insufficiency:** In cases of severe, prolonged hypothyroidism or hypopituitarism, adrenal deficiency must be corrected (e.g., with glucocorticoids) prior to starting liothyronine to prevent acute adrenal crisis [Australian].

### Monitoring
*   **Thyroid Function:** Periodic assessment of thyroid status via laboratory tests and clinical observation is required [Australian].
*   **Anticoagulation:** Initial increased frequency of prothrombin time monitoring is necessary when starting or stopping therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally related to excessive dosage and resemble symptoms of hyperthyroidism. These are reported as "fairly uncommon" in practice except during the treatment of myxoedema [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiac** | Palpitation, tachycardia, anginal pain, cardiac arrhythmias |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea |
| **Nervous System** | Headache, restlessness, excitability, irritability |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Skeletal muscle cramps, muscle weakness |
| **General/Other** | Flushing, sweating, excessive weight loss, intolerance to heat |

*   **Serious Effects:** Induction or aggravation of congestive heart failure [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Anticoagulants (Oral):** Liothyronine potentiates anticoagulant action by increasing the catabolism of Vitamin K-dependent clotting factors. Dosage reduction of anticoagulants may be necessary [Australian].
*   **Anticonvulsants (e.g., Phenytoin, Carbamazepine):** These agents increase thyroid hormone metabolism and may displace hormones from plasma proteins. Liothyronine may also increase phenytoin levels [Australian].
*   **Cardiac Glycosides:** Liothyronine may increase digitalis requirements due to increased metabolic rate; conversely, it may potentiate digitalis toxicity [Australian].
*   **Cholestyramine:** Reduces gastrointestinal absorption of liothyronine by binding it in the gut lumen [Australian].
*   **Tricyclic Antidepressants:** Liothyronine increases receptor sensitivity to catecholamines, potentially increasing the risk of cardiac arrhythmias [Australian].
*   **Ketamine:** May cause significant hypertension and tachycardia in patients receiving thyroid replacement [Australian].
*   **Antidiabetic Agents:** Insulin or oral hypoglycaemic requirements may increase as thyroid status improves [Australian].
*   **Oral Contraceptives:** Oestrogens increase thyroid-binding globulin, which may increase the required dose of thyroid therapy [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Category A [Australian]:** Drugs which have been taken by a large number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age without any proven increase in the frequency of malformations or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the fetus.
    *   There is very limited placental transfer of thyroid hormones [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Liothyronine is excreted in breast milk; caution should be exercised [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** No data available in source documentation [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** 
    *   Initial dosage: 5 micrograms daily [Australian].
    *   Maintenance: 10–40 micrograms daily, depending on age and response [Australian].
    *   **Infants:** High doses may cause craniosynotosis [Australian].
    *   **Children:** Transient partial hair loss may occur in the first few months of therapy [Australian].
*   **Elderly:** Use with special care due to increased sensitivity to thyroid replacement. Initial dosage should be limited to 5 micrograms daily [Australian].
*   **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments are not specified in the available source [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Exogenous thyroid hormones increase the metabolic rate of body tissues [Australian].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** The biological action is qualitatively similar to thyroxine (T4) but develops within hours and disappears within 24–48 hours of discontinuation, making it more potent and rapid-acting than T4 [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Readily and almost completely absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract [Australian].
    *   **Distribution:** Principally bound to thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG). Plasma half-life is 1–2 days in euthyroidism (prolonged in hypothyroidism, reduced in hyperthyroidism) [Australian].
    *   **Metabolism:** Metabolised by deiodination to inactive di-iodothyronine and mono-iodothyronine, as well as deamination and decarboxylation to tiratricol [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
*   **Protection:** Protect from light [Australian].
*   **Container:** Supplied in HDPE bottles of 100 tablets with child-resistant closures [Australian].
