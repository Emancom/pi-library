# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from the **United States (FDA)**. 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource. It does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP)
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt)
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US]
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Intravenous injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
*   **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:**
    *   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    *   **Initial Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours.
    *   **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though titration may be required based on clinical response and plasma concentrations (highest reported dose: 300 mg/kg).
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration before use.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted to concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if severe reactions occur [US].
*   **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy [US].
*   **Monitoring:** 
    *   **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended.
    *   **ESRD:** Trough (pre-dialysis) levels should be monitored (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, the following adverse events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality):

| Body System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Nausea (8%), Vomiting (15%), Abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%) |

### Other Effects
*   **Body Odor:** Reported with levocarnitine therapy.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and transient nausea/vomiting.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category:** Not specifically assigned a letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Although studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk, the risks of excess intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
*   **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, yeast) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as described in Section 3.
*   **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production.

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated.

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
*   **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis, 20 mg/kg of levocarnitine three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean trough concentrations from ~21 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks of therapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
*   **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
