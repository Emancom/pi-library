# Editorial Product Information: lidocaine + adrenaline

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **lidocaine + adrenaline**
- Chemical class:
  - Lidocaine: amide-type local anaesthetic; membrane-stabilising agent.
  - Adrenaline (epinephrine): potent sympathomimetic amine.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: local injection for dental procedures (infiltration and nerve block).  
- Excipients of clinical relevance: contains a sulfite antioxidant (sodium metabisulfite). Other excipients listed in source: sodium chloride, water for injections; pH adjustment agents may be present. (Packaging/storage/exact presentations not included — not specified in available sources.)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval:
  - 13 August 1991 [Australian].
- Date(s) of last revision/update of source PI:
  - 24 March 2026 [Australian].
- Withdrawals, suspensions or restrictions: Not specified in available sources for any jurisdiction other than the revision history above.
- Where registration dates or revision details are not provided for other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Production of local anaesthesia for routine dental procedures and oral surgery using infiltration and nerve block techniques.
- Solutions containing adrenaline are specifically recommended for oral surgery requiring prolonged anaesthesia and to provide haemostasis.

(If an indication is approved only in a specific jurisdiction, that detail is not provided in available sources; thus all indications above reflect the available source information.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General principles
- Use the lowest dose that produces effective anaesthesia for the planned procedure.
- Dosage depends on injection site, tissue vascularity, patient factors and technique.
- Injection should be given slowly with frequent aspiration to reduce the risk of inadvertent intravascular injection.
- Multiple injections should be spaced in time.
- Local anaesthetic techniques must not be used in the area of inflammation and/or sepsis.

### Recommended typical dosing (average healthy adult ~70 kg; adrenaline-containing solution)
- Infiltration: suggested 1–2 mL.
- Nerve block: suggested 1.5–5 mL.
- Onset (approx): infiltration ~1.5 minutes; block ~4 minutes.
- Duration (approx): pulp: infiltration ~60 minutes, block ~90 minutes; soft tissue ~3 hours.

### Maximum dose guidance
- Recommended upper guide: do not exceed 7 mg/kg of lidocaine at any one time when using adrenaline-containing solutions. Individual patient factors (age, comorbidity, concomitant medications) may require substantially lower doses.

### Paediatric use
- Dose should be reduced commensurate with body weight. Specific pediatric dose tables/age limits are not specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Ensure resuscitative equipment and oxygen are immediately available prior to administration.
- Avoid prolonged contact between solution and metal surfaces (do not preload cartridges onto needles well before injection).
- Cartridges showing discolouration or cracks should be discarded.
- Do not use adrenaline-containing solutions in patients with known sensitivity to sympathomimetic amines or sulfites.

### Titration schedules / Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List of all contraindications from source material:
- Allergy or hypersensitivity to amide-type local anaesthetics.
- Hypersensitivity to any components of the injection solution (e.g. sodium metabisulfite).
- Local anaesthetic techniques must not be used when there is inflammation and/or sepsis in the region of the proposed injection.
- Additional contraindications applicable to adrenaline-containing solutions:
  - Known sensitivity to sympathomimetic amines.
  - Should not be used in most patients with cerebral arteriosclerosis.

(Regional attribution: these contraindications are stated in the available source [Australian]. Equivalent statements for other jurisdictions are not specified in available sources.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed (black box) warnings are specified in the available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Resuscitative equipment and drugs (including oxygen) must be immediately available** to manage potential adverse reactions involving the cardiovascular, respiratory or central nervous systems.
- Injection must be slow with frequent aspiration to avoid inadvertent intravascular injection and systemic toxicity.
- Careful observation of the patient is essential; toxic effects may occur after any local anaesthetic procedure.
- Use with caution in patients with:
  - Severe bradycardia, cardiac conduction disturbances, or severe digitalis intoxication.
  - Hepatic, renal or cardiac disease (metabolite accumulation possible with repeated/prolonged administration).
  - Severe or untreated hypertension, arteriosclerotic heart disease, heart block, cerebral vascular insufficiency, thyrotoxicosis, advanced diabetes, or any condition that may be aggravated by sympathomimetic effects (adrenaline may precipitate angina in ischemic heart disease).
  - Known drug sensitivities; note that cross-sensitivity between ester and amide local anaesthetics is uncommon but possible.
  - Genetic predisposition to malignant hyperthermia — safety of amide local anaesthetics not fully established in these patients.
- Sodium metabisulfite (sulfite) may cause allergic-type reactions including anaphylaxis or asthmatic episodes in susceptible patients (more common in asthmatics).
- Avoid autoclaving of adrenaline-containing cartridges; surface sterilisation methods noted in source (not reproduced here).
- Patients should be warned to avoid biting or traumatizing anaesthetised tissues until normal sensation returns.
- Elderly and patients in poor general health may require special attention and lower doses.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor patients for signs of systemic toxicity (CNS excitation → depression; cardiovascular depression, arrhythmias).
- Patients receiving concurrent amiodarone should be monitored carefully (reported cases of reduced clearance, seizures, severe bradycardia and sinoatrial arrest).
- Monitor blood glucose control in diabetics (adrenaline-induced hyperglycaemia may affect hypoglycaemic therapy).
- Monitor for hypertensive reactions when used with tricyclic antidepressants or non-cardioselective beta‑blockers, and for hypotension/tachycardia when used with phenothiazines or butyrophenones.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Interactions with inhalational anaesthetics may increase risk of serious cardiac arrhythmias—exercise caution when used during or after halogenated inhaled agents.
- Avoid use in combination with ergot-type oxytocics (risk of severe persistent hypertension).
- Use with caution when patient is receiving cardiac glycosides or quinidine (risk of arrhythmias).
- Use with caution when patient is receiving adrenergic neuron blocking agents.
- Phenytoin and other anticonvulsants may enhance lidocaine metabolism; additive cardiac depressant effects with phenytoin have been reported.

## 8. Drug Interactions
(All interactions are presented as in available source material. Regional variations not specified.)

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Amiodarone: Case reports describe reduced lidocaine clearance; small prospective study showed no change. Reported combinations associated with seizures and severe bradyarrhythmias — monitor closely.
- Phenytoin and other anticonvulsants (phenobarbitone, primidone, carbamazepine): appear to enhance lidocaine metabolism; clinical significance uncertain. Phenytoin and lidocaine have additive cardiac depressant effects.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions / clinically significant interactions
- Antiarrhythmic drugs: Amide local anaesthetics should be used cautiously with agents structurally related to local anaesthetics (e.g., mexiletine) — potentiation of cardiac effects may occur.
- Inhalational anaesthetics: Lidocaine decreases the minimum effective concentration of inhalational anaesthetics (e.g., nitrous oxide). Use of adrenaline-containing preparations during or following halogenated inhalational anaesthetics (chloroform, halothane, cyclopropane, trichlorethylene, other halogenated compounds) is associated with risk of serious cardiac arrhythmias.
- Tricyclic antidepressants: Concomitant use with adrenaline-containing solutions may produce severe hypertension — use extreme caution.
- Phenothiazines and butyrophenones: May reduce or reverse pressor effects of adrenaline, potentially causing hypotension with reflex tachycardia.
- Ergot-type oxytocic drugs: Combined use with adrenaline-containing solutions can produce severe, persistent hypertension — contraindicated.
- Adrenergic neuron blocking agents (e.g., guanethidine, debrisoquine, bethanidine): Use adrenaline-containing solutions with caution.
- Cardiac glycosides: Possible interaction leading to arrhythmias — use caution.
- Quinidine: May interact with adrenaline-containing solutions causing cardiac arrhythmias — use caution.
- Hypoglycaemic agents: Adrenaline-induced hyperglycaemia may lead to loss of blood sugar control in diabetic patients on hypoglycaemic therapy — monitor glucose control.
- Beta‑blockers: Non-cardioselective beta‑blockers (e.g., propranolol) can enhance pressor effect of adrenaline, possibly leading to severe hypertension and bradycardia — avoid or use with caution.

### Management notes
- Monitor cardiovascular and neurologic status when combining with interacting drugs.
- Avoid or limit adrenaline-containing local anaesthetics in patients on interacting agents when possible; if used, administer the smallest effective adrenaline dose and monitor closely.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category: Category A [Australian].
- Summary: The safe use of lidocaine during pregnancy has not been definitively established, however it has been used extensively for dental procedures during pregnancy without reported ill effects to mother or fetus in the available source. (Other jurisdictions’ pregnancy categorisations are not specified in available sources.)

### Breastfeeding
- Lidocaine is excreted into breast milk. Amounts transferred following parenteral administration are unlikely to produce significant accumulation in the breast‑fed infant according to the available source.
- Remote possibility of idiosyncratic or allergic reactions in the breast‑fed infant cannot be excluded.

### Fertility
- No data available in the source regarding effects on fertility.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Dose should be reduced in children in proportion to body weight. Specific age/weight dosing tables or approvals are not provided in available sources.

### Elderly use
- Elderly patients and those in poor general health may require special attention and dose reduction because of increased susceptibility to adverse effects.

### Renal impairment
- Lidocaine is primarily metabolised hepatically and excreted by the kidneys as metabolites; accumulation of lidocaine and/or metabolites may occur in renal impairment with prolonged or repeated administration. Specific dose adjustments by eGFR or stage are not provided in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Lidocaine is metabolised mainly in the liver; patients with hepatic disease may have reduced clearance and are at increased risk of systemic toxicity. Specific dosage adjustments by severity are not provided in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Lidocaine**: stabilises neuronal membranes, reversibly preventing initiation and conduction of nerve impulses, producing local anaesthesia.
- **Adrenaline (epinephrine)**: vasoconstrictor; addition reduces absorption rate of lidocaine from injection site, prolonging duration of anaesthesia and providing haemostasis.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Rate of systemic absorption depends on site of administration and use of vasoconstrictor (adrenaline reduces absorption).
- Distribution: Not fully detailed in available sources.
- Metabolism: Lidocaine is metabolised mainly in the liver to multiple metabolites (including 2,6‑xylidine).
- Elimination: Approximately 90% of administered lidocaine is excreted as metabolites; less than 10% is excreted unchanged. Reported elimination half‑life in healthy adults ~1.8 hours (varies with site of injection).
- Pharmacokinetic data for adrenaline when used in combination formulation: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Onset and duration vary by technique and concentration; addition of adrenaline prolongs action by slowing systemic absorption.

### Preclinical safety data
- Genotoxicity: The metabolite 2,6‑xylidine showed mixed genotoxicity results: weakly positive in some in vitro mutation assays and chromosomal aberration tests; in vivo assays for chromosomal damage and DNA damage were negative in the available source. Some covalent DNA binding in rat tissues suggests possible in vivo genotoxicity under certain conditions.
- Carcinogenicity: Long‑term oral studies of 2,6‑xylidine in rats produced nasal tumours and subcutaneous fibromas/fibrosarcomas at high doses; no tumours observed at low doses in that study.
