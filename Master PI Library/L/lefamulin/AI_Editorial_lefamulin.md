# Editorial Product Information: Lefamulin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, incorporating regulatory nuances and clinical data into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for specific prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lefamulin (as lefamulin acetate).
- **Chemical Class:** Pleuromutilin antibacterial.
- **Brand Name:** **XENLETA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Injection:** 150 mg of lefamulin in 15 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride in a single-dose vial for dilution [US].
    - **Tablets:** 600 mg of lefamulin (as acetate), film-coated, blue, oval-shaped [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion and Oral (PO).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Nabriva Therapeutics US, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Community-Acquired Bacterial Pneumonia (CABP):** Indicated for the treatment of adults with CABP caused by susceptible microorganisms, including *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Staphylococcus aureus* (methicillin-susceptible isolates), *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Legionella pneumophila*, *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*, and *Chlamydophila pneumoniae* [US-approved].
- **Usage Note:** To maintain efficacy and prevent resistance, lefamulin should only be used for infections proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible bacteria.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
The recommended dosing regimen for CABP depends on the route of administration [US]:
- **Intravenous:** 150 mg every 12 hours by IV infusion over 60 minutes for 5 to 7 days.
- **Oral:** 600 mg every 12 hours for 5 days.
- **IV-to-Oral Switch:** Patients may be switched from IV 150 mg every 12 hours to Oral 600 mg every 12 hours to complete a treatment course at the clinician's discretion.

### Administration Instructions
- **Oral Administration:** Tablets should be swallowed whole with water (6 to 8 ounces). They must be taken at least 1 hour before a meal or 2 hours after a meal. Tablets must not be crushed or divided [US].
- **Intravenous Administration:** Must be diluted before use. The 15 mL vial should be added to the supplied 250 mL diluent bag containing 10 mM citrate-buffered 0.9% sodium chloride. Infuse over 60 minutes [US].

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible, provided there are at least 8 hours before the next scheduled dose. If less than 8 hours remain, the missed dose should be skipped [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to lefamulin, other pleuromutilin class drugs, or any component of the formulation [US].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates (Oral form only):** Concomitant use of **XENLETA** tablets with sensitive CYP3A4 substrates that prolong the QT interval (e.g., pimozide) is contraindicated [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Prolongation:** Lefamulin has the potential to prolong the QT interval. Use should be avoided in patients with:
    - Known QT prolongation or ventricular arrhythmias (including Torsades de Pointes).
    - Concurrent use of Class IA (e.g., quinidine, procainamide) or Class III (e.g., amiodarone, sotalol) antiarrhythmics.
    - Concurrent use of other QT-prolonging drugs (e.g., antipsychotics, erythromycin, moxifloxacin, tricyclic antidepressants) [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Animal studies indicate potential fetal harm. Pregnancy status should be verified in females of reproductive potential before initiation [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterials. Evaluate patients if diarrhea occurs [US].
- **Monitoring:** ECG monitoring is recommended during treatment in patients predisposed to QT prolongation [US].

### Serious and Less Common Effects
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Occurred in 5.6% of patients in clinical trials [US].
- **Selected Reactions (<2%):** Anemia, thrombocytopenia, atrial fibrillation, palpitations, abdominal pain, constipation, dyspepsia, oropharyngeal/vulvovaginal candidiasis, and urinary retention [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong/Moderate CYP3A or P-gp Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use with **XENLETA** (both IV and PO) unless benefit outweighs risk, as lefamulin exposure and efficacy may be reduced (e.g., rifampin reduces oral lefamulin AUC by 72%) [US].
- **Strong CYP3A or P-gp Inhibitors:** Avoid use with **XENLETA** tablets. Concomitant use with the IV formulation is permitted but requires monitoring for adverse effects [US].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** **XENLETA** tablets increase the exposure of sensitive CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., midazolam AUC increases by 200%). Monitor for toxicities of these substrates [US]. **XENLETA** injection does not affect CYP3A4 substrate exposure.
- **QT Prolonging Drugs:** Avoid concomitant use [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Lettered category not provided in current labeling; however, the PI states lefamulin may cause fetal harm based on animal data (malformations and post-implantation loss in rats/rabbits).
    - **Clinical Recommendation:** Advise females of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during treatment and for 2 days after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on lefamulin in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions, lactating women should pump and discard human milk during treatment and for 2 days after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed reduced percent of live births at doses approximating 0.9 times human IV exposure [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients <18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required, including for patients on hemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh Class C):** Reduce IV dose to 150 mg every 24 hours.
    - **Moderate/Severe (Child-Pugh Class B or C):** **XENLETA** tablets are not recommended [US].
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh Class A):** No adjustment required for IV or Oral [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Systemic pleuromutilin that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the peptidyl transferase center (PTC) of the 50S subunit of the bacterial ribosome [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Approximately 25% (Oral) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Excretion:** Feces (77.3% to 88.5%) and urine (15.5%) [US].
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 8 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The PK-PD index best associated with activity is the 24h free-drug AUC/MIC ratio [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Two multicenter, multinational, double-blind trials (Trial 1 and Trial 2) in 1,289 adults with CABP.
- **Design:** Non-inferiority trials comparing lefamulin to moxifloxacin (with or without linezolid).
- **Outcomes:** Early Clinical Response (ECR) at 72–120 hours was 87.3% to 90.8% for lefamulin, meeting non-inferiority criteria against moxifloxacin [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Injection (Vials):** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Do not freeze [US].
- **Diluent Bags:** Store at 2°C to 25°C (36°F to 77°F).
- **Diluted Solution:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature or 48 hours if refrigerated [US].
- **Tablets:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
