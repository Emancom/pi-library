# Editorial Product Information: Lubiprostone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration - FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lubiprostone for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing recommendations, and safety profiles.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lubiprostone (a bicyclic fatty acid derived from prostaglandin E1).
- **Chemical Class:** Chloride channel activator.
- **Brand Names:** Amitiza (innovator); Lubiprostone (Sun Pharmaceutical Industries, Inc.) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A06AX03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Oval, soft gelatin capsules: 8 mcg (pink, imprinted with "SPI") and 24 mcg (orange, imprinted with "SPI") [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharmaceutical Industries, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Lubiprostone is indicated for:
- **Chronic Idiopathic Constipation (CIC):** Treatment in adult patients [US].
- **Opioid-Induced Constipation (OIC):** Treatment in adult patients with chronic non-cancer pain, including those with chronic pain related to prior cancer or its treatment who do not require frequent opioid dosage escalation [US].
  - *Limitation of Use:* Efficacy in OIC patients taking diphenylheptane opioids (e.g., methadone) has not been established [US].
- **Irritable Bowel Syndrome with Constipation (IBS-C):** Treatment in women ≥18 years of age [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **CIC and OIC:** 24 mcg taken twice daily [US].
- **IBS-C:** 8 mcg taken twice daily [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules must be swallowed whole; do not break, crush, or chew.
- Administer with food and water to minimize the risk of nausea [US].
- Periodically re-evaluate the necessity of continued therapy.

### Hepatic Impairment Adjustments
Dosage should be adjusted based on the degree of hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class) [US]:

| Indication | Moderate Impairment (Child-Pugh B) | Severe Impairment (Child-Pugh C) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CIC and OIC** | 16 mcg twice daily | 8 mcg twice daily |
| **IBS-C** | No adjustment required | 8 mcg once daily |

*Note: If the reduced dose is tolerated but response is inadequate, the dose may be escalated to full strength with monitoring [US].*

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources (standard practice is to skip the missed dose and resume at the next scheduled time; do not double doses).

## 4. Contraindications
- Patients with known or suspected mechanical gastrointestinal obstruction [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Nausea:** Very common; dose-related. Taking with food and water reduces incidence [US].
- **Diarrhea:** Should not be initiated in patients with severe diarrhea. Discontinue if severe diarrhea occurs during treatment [US].
- **Syncope and Hypotension:** Reported in postmarketing settings, sometimes after the first dose or associated with diarrhea/vomiting. Usually resolves after discontinuation or before the next dose [US].
- **Dyspnea:** Patients may experience a sensation of chest tightness or difficulty breathing, typically occurring within 30–60 minutes of the first dose. This usually resolves within 3 hours but may recur with subsequent doses [US].
- **Bowel Obstruction:** Mechanical obstruction must be ruled out before initiating treatment in patients with suggestive symptoms [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data [US]
- **Very Common (>10%):**
  - Nausea (up to 29% in CIC, 11% in OIC).
  - Diarrhea (up to 12% in CIC).
  - Headache (11% in CIC).
- **Common (1–10%):**
  - Abdominal pain/discomfort, flatulence, abdominal distension, vomiting, loose stools.
  - Edema, dizziness, fatigue, dry mouth, dyspepsia.
- **Uncommon (<1%):**
  - Fecal incontinence, muscle cramps, tachycardia, syncope, dysgeusia, hyperhidrosis, pharyngolaryngeal pain.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Severe diarrhea, syncope, hypotension, and ischemic colitis (postmarketing report) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Low potential for interactions. Lubiprostone is not metabolized by the Cytochrome P450 system and does not inhibit major CYP isoforms (3A4, 2D6, 1A2, etc.) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
  - **Methadone/Diphenylheptane Opioids:** May dose-dependently reduce ClC-2 activation, potentially decreasing the clinical efficacy of lubiprostone [US].
  - **Antihypertensives:** Concomitant use may increase the risk of syncope and hypotension [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in the provided source, but clinical data is limited [US].
  - **Animal Data:** Dose-dependent fetal loss observed in guinea pigs. Use in pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No human data on presence in milk or effects on production. In animal studies (rats), neither lubiprostone nor its metabolite M3 were detected in milk. Monitor breastfed infants for diarrhea [US].
- **Fertility:** Reduced implantation sites and live embryos were observed in rats at very high doses (169 times the MRHD) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established. A 12-week trial in pediatric functional constipation (PFC) failed to demonstrate efficacy over placebo [US].
- **Elderly Use:** 
  - **CIC:** Efficacy was consistent with younger adults; however, the incidence of nausea was lower in patients ≥65 years (19% vs 29%) [US].
  - **OIC/IBS-C:** Insufficient data to determine if elderly patients respond differently [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosage adjustment required for Moderate (Child-Pugh B) and Severe (Child-Pugh C) impairment, except for IBS-C in Moderate impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Locally acting chloride channel activator. It specifically activates ClC-2 channels on the apical membrane of the human intestine, increasing chloride-rich intestinal fluid secretion. This increases motility and facilitates the passage of stool [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Low systemic availability; plasma concentrations are generally below the level of quantitation (<10 pg/mL). The active metabolite M3 is measurable [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Rapidly metabolized in the stomach and jejunum via 15-position reduction, alpha-chain beta-oxidation, and omega-chain omega-oxidation [US].
  - **Elimination:** Primary excretion via urine (60%) within 24 hours and feces (30%) within 168 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CIC:** Two 4-week trials showed significantly higher spontaneous bowel movement (SBM) frequencies (5.7–5.9 SBMs/week) compared to placebo (3.5–4.0 SBMs/week) [US].
- **OIC:** Studies demonstrated significantly higher "overall responder" rates (24–27%) versus placebo (15–19%) in patients with chronic non-cancer pain [US].
- **IBS-C:** Two 12-week trials in women showed higher "overall responder" rates based on global symptom relief scores compared to placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F) [US].
- Protect from light and extreme temperatures [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx Only) [US].
