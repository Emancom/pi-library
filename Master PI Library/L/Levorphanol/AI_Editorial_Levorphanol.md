# Editorial Product Information: Levorphanol Tartrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Important:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levorphanol tartrate (opioid agonist; morphinan derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Levorphanol Tartrate Tablets USP.
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablet:** 2 mg (white to off-white, round, flat-faced, debossed with "M" inside a square on one side and a score on the reverse) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** SpecGx LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Management of Severe Pain:** Indicated for pain severe enough to require an opioid analgesic and for which alternative treatment options (e.g., non-opioid analgesics or opioid combination products) are inadequate, not tolerated, or otherwise insufficient [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Opioid-Naïve Patients:** Initiate at 1 mg to 2 mg every 6 to 8 hours as needed. The dose may be increased to 3 mg every 6 to 8 hours if necessary, following an adequate evaluation of response [US].
- **Opioid-Tolerant Patients:** Higher starting doses may be appropriate; however, titration must be cautious due to the drug's long half-life.

### Conversion from Other Opioids
- **Morphine to Levorphanol:** Due to incomplete cross-tolerance, the initial total daily dose of oral levorphanol should be approximately 1/15 to 1/12 of the previous total daily oral morphine dose [US].
- **Potency:** Levorphanol is approximately 4 to 8 times as potent as morphine [US].

### Titration and Maintenance
- **Steady State:** Due to a long terminal half-life (11 to 16 hours), steady-state plasma concentrations are typically reached by the third day of dosing. Care must be taken to allow at least 72 hours between dose adjustments to avoid excessive accumulation and toxicity [US].

### Discontinuation
- Do not abruptly discontinue in physically dependent patients. Taper the dose by 10% to 25% every 2 to 4 weeks to avoid withdrawal symptoms [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Significant respiratory depression [US].
- Acute or severe bronchial asthma in unmonitored settings or without resuscitative equipment [US].
- Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
- Hypersensitivity to levorphanol tartrate or any excipients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Levorphanol exposes users to the risks of opioid addiction and misuse, which can lead to overdose and death.
- **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur, particularly during initiation or dose escalation.
- **Accidental Ingestion:** Even one dose, especially by children, can result in fatal overdose.
- **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Prolonged use during pregnancy can result in life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate.
- **Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or Other CNS Depressants:** May result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.
- **Opioid Analgesic REMS:** Healthcare providers are encouraged to complete a REMS-compliant education program.

### General Warnings
- **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH) and Allodynia:** May occur where pain increases despite dose escalation; requires dose reduction or opioid rotation [US].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, usually after >1 month of therapy.
- **Severe Hypotension:** Use with caution in patients with compromised blood pressure or circulatory shock [US].
- **Increased Intracranial Pressure:** Use with caution in patients with head injury or brain tumors as CO2 retention can further increase intracranial pressure [US].
- **Seizure Disorders:** May increase seizure frequency in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally dose-related and typical of the opioid class.

- **Central Nervous System:** Somnolence, dizziness, confusion, lethargy, abnormal dreams, nervousness, depression, and potentially coma or convulsions [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, constipation, abdominal pain, dry mouth, and biliary tract spasm [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension, palpitations, bradycardia, tachycardia, and cardiac arrest [US].
- **Respiratory:** Apnea, cyanosis, and hypoventilation [US].
- **Genitourinary:** Urinary retention, difficulty urinating, and kidney failure [US].
- **Skin:** Pruritus, urticaria, rash, and sweating [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Serotonin syndrome, anaphylaxis, androgen deficiency (long-term use), and hypoglycemia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Benzodiazepines and CNS Depressants:** Additive effect increases risk of respiratory depression and death [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Concomitant use with SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, or MAOIs may result in serotonin syndrome [US].
- **Mixed Agonist/Antagonist and Partial Opioids:** May reduce analgesic effect or precipitate withdrawal [US].
- **Muscle Relaxants:** Levorphanol may enhance neuromuscular blocking action [US].
- **Diuretics:** Opioids may reduce diuretic efficacy by inducing antidiuretic hormone release [US].
- **Anticholinergic Drugs:** Increased risk of urinary retention and severe constipation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Category:** Not formally assigned a letter category in the provided source, but noted to have insufficient data in humans. Animal studies showed malformations and embryo lethality at 10–12 times the human dose [US].
    - **Labor/Delivery:** Not recommended immediately prior to labor; may cause respiratory depression in the neonate [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levorphanol is likely excreted in human milk (based on morphine data). Monitor infants for excess sedation and respiratory depression [US].
- **Fertility:** Chronic opioid use may cause reduced fertility in both sexes; reversibility is unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 18 years; safety and efficacy not established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity to adverse effects. Initiate at the low end of the dosing range (start at 50% of the usual dose in infirm patients) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; patients may be vulnerable to excessive sedation due to impaired metabolism [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Levorphanol is substantially excreted by the kidney. Monitor renal function and use caution in dose selection [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Full opioid agonist, relatively selective for the mu-opioid receptor, though it binds to others at higher doses [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces analgesia, respiratory depression, miosis, and reduced GI motility. It has no "ceiling effect" for analgesia [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed orally; peak plasma levels in ~1 hour [US].
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributed (<1 hr); 40% protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver to an inactive glucuronide metabolite [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 11 to 16 hours. The glucuronide metabolite accumulates with chronic dosing [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Literature reports indicate levorphanol is effective for preoperative medication, postoperative analgesia, and chronic cancer pain. It has demonstrated efficacy similar to morphine, meperidine, and fentanyl in these settings [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container. Unused tablets should be disposed of via drug take-back programs or, if unavailable, by flushing [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a Schedule II (CII) controlled substance. Requires Rx-only [US].
