# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise format for reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or the local prescribing requirements of a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Levocarnitine** (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Clear, colorless sterile solution for injection, containing 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and not less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg/day, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used based on clinical requirement [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments (downward to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks/benefits before any attempt at re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Thromboembolic Events/INR Monitoring:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used with warfarin. Monitor INR levels after initiating **levocarnitine** or after dose adjustments [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral formulation context):** Safety of oral **levocarnitine** has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral formulations in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients are often confounded by the underlying pathology.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33% [US]), Pain (30% [US]), Flu syndrome (25% [US]), Headache (22% [US]).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20% [US]), Hypotension (14% [US]).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16% [US]), Vomiting (15% [US]).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23% [US]).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13% [US]).

### Common (1–10%)
- **General:** Abdominal pain (9%), Chest pain (12%), Asthenia (9%), Fever (7%), Accidental injury (10%), Infection (15%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia (6%), Palpitations (5%), Cardiovascular disorder (5%).
- **Digestive:** Nausea (8%), Anorexia (5%), Dyspepsia (5%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%), Peripheral edema (5%), Weight decrease (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%).
- **Nervous System:** Insomnia (4%), Paresthesia (5%), Depression (5%).
- **Other:** Pruritus (5%), Eye disorder (3%), Kidney failure (6%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Concomitant use may result in increased INR. Management: Monitor INR levels regularly after starting **levocarnitine** or changing the dose [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** **Levocarnitine** is not bound to plasma proteins or albumin; therefore, it is unlikely to cause displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but label states "should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed"]. Animal reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders (50 mg/kg) apply to pediatric patients. Clinical PK data includes premature infants and newborns, where deficiency is defined by age-related normal concentrations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** **Levocarnitine** is easily removed by dialysis. Note that chronic high-dose oral administration is cautioned in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Levocarnitine** is a carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, **levocarnitine** administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normal plasma free carnitine levels are generally 40 to 50 µmol/L [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials of ESRD patients on chronic hemodialysis, **levocarnitine** (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg) effectively increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels remained stable or increased over 12–24 weeks. However, the specific effect of this increase on clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
