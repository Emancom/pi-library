# Editorial Product Information: lamivudine + zidovudine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **lamivudine + zidovudine**
- Chemical class: Not specified in available sources
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection as monotherapy or in combination with other antiretroviral agents for adults and paediatric patients where appropriate dosing can be achieved.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General adult dosing
- Adults and adolescents weighing ≥ 30 kg: one tablet twice daily, providing a combined total daily dose equivalent to 600 mg zidovudine and 300 mg lamivudine.

### Paediatric dosing (by bodyweight)
- Children 21–30 kg: one-half tablet in the morning and one whole tablet in the evening.
- Children 14–21 kg: one-half tablet twice daily.
- Children <14 kg: fixed-dose tablet is not recommended because accurate dosing cannot be achieved; use separate oral solutions of lamivudine and zidovudine.
- Children <3 months of age: insufficient data to make specific dosing recommendations.

Notes:
- The paediatric weight-based recommendations were derived from pharmacokinetic modelling and supporting clinical data for the individual components; zidovudine overexposure can occur in some children in the 14–30 kg range and close safety monitoring is recommended.
- If dose modification or discontinuation of one component is required (for example in renal or hepatic impairment or haematologic toxicity), use the separate lamivudine and zidovudine formulations to permit appropriate dose adjustments.

### Administration instructions
- May be administered with or without food. Food reduces the peak plasma concentration (Cmax) and delays time to peak (Tmax) of lamivudine but does not reduce the overall amount absorbed.
- For patients unable to swallow intact tablets, tablets may be crushed and the entire crushed dose mixed with a small amount of semi-solid food or liquid and consumed immediately to ensure full dose delivery.

### Dose modification and interruption
- Significant anaemia or significant granulocytopenia require dose interruption until marrow recovery:
  - Anaemia: haemoglobin < 7.5 g/dL or reduction of >25% from baseline.
  - Granulocytopenia: granulocyte count < 750 cells/mm3 or reduction of >50% from baseline.
- For less severe haematologic abnormalities, dose reduction (of individual agents) may be adequate. As the fixed-dose combination cannot be adjusted, individual component formulations should be used when dose adjustment is required.
- In patients with pre-existing bone marrow compromise (e.g., haemoglobin < 9 g/dL or granulocyte count < 1,000 cells/mm3), consider more frequent haematologic monitoring and use individual agents if dose adjustment is anticipated.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources beyond dose-reduction guidance above.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to lamivudine, zidovudine, or any ingredient of the product.
- Abnormally low neutrophil counts (specified threshold): neutrophils < 0.75 x 10^9/L.
- Abnormally low haemoglobin levels (specified threshold): haemoglobin < 7.5 g/dL (4.65 mmol/L).
- Any additional contraindications beyond the above: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Patients may continue to experience opportunistic infections and other HIV-related complications while on antiretroviral therapy; ongoing specialist clinical follow-up is required.
- Haematologic toxicity: zidovudine-containing regimens are commonly associated with anaemia and granulocytopenia; pancytopenia has been reported and is usually reversible on discontinuation. Frequency and timing: haemoglobin reductions may occur as early as 2–4 weeks; granulocytopenia typically occurs after 6–8 weeks.
- Hypersensitivity: sensitisation reactions including anaphylaxis have been reported with zidovudine; evaluate rashes promptly.
- Pancreatitis: rare cases reported with lamivudine and zidovudine; stop therapy immediately if clinical or laboratory evidence of pancreatitis appears.
- Lipoatrophy: zidovudine exposure has been associated with loss of subcutaneous fat (face, limbs, buttocks); incidence/severity related to cumulative exposure and may be only partially reversible.
- Metabolic effects: increases in serum lipids and blood glucose have been reported during antiretroviral therapy; consider monitoring and manage clinically.
- Lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis (including fatal cases) have been reported with nucleoside analogue therapy, including lamivudine and zidovudine; majority of reported cases occurred in women. Suspend therapy in any patient with clinical or laboratory evidence suggestive of lactic acidosis or hepatic steatosis.
- Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS) may occur after initiating antiretroviral therapy; evaluate and treat inflammatory symptoms promptly. Autoimmune conditions (e.g., Graves' disease, polymyositis, Guillain–Barré syndrome) have been reported in the context of immune restoration and may present atypically.
- Hepatitis B co-infection: discontinuation of lamivudine in patients with chronic HBV coinfection can lead to hepatitis exacerbation; monitor liver function and HBV markers when stopping lamivudine in coinfected patients.
- Osteonecrosis has been reported particularly in patients with advanced HIV disease and/or long-term antiretroviral exposure; evaluate persistent joint pain or stiffness.
- Use with ribavirin: zidovudine-containing regimens may exacerbate ribavirin-associated anaemia; co-administration of ribavirin and zidovudine is not advised.
- Use of paracetamol: concomitant paracetamol use has been associated with increased incidence of granulocytopenia in patients receiving zidovudine; exercise caution with chronic paracetamol use.
- Concomitant drugs metabolised by glucuronidation: exercise caution because zidovudine is primarily eliminated via hepatic glucuronidation and co-administration may potentiate toxicity.

### Monitoring requirements
- Haematologic monitoring:
  - Patients with advanced symptomatic HIV disease: at least every 2 weeks for the first 3 months of therapy, then at least monthly thereafter.
  - Patients with early HIV disease: monitoring frequency may be less (e.g., every 1–3 months) depending on clinical status.
  - More frequent monitoring is recommended for patients with declines in haemoglobin (>25% from baseline) or neutrophils (>50% from baseline).
- In renal impairment and in patients with pre-existing marrow compromise, monitor haematologic indices closely; dose adjustment of zidovudine may be required.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of lactic acidosis and hepatic steatosis; assess liver function tests as clinically indicated.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use individual lamivudine and zidovudine formulations when dose adjustment is required for renal or hepatic impairment or for haematologic toxicity.
- Exercise caution in elderly patients due to possible age-related renal function decline and altered haematologic parameters.
- Haemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis have little effect on zidovudine elimination; haemodialysis may be more efficient than peritoneal dialysis at removing the glucuronide metabolite.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Overview
- Both lamivudine and zidovudine should be considered when evaluating interactions; lamivudine is predominantly renally cleared with limited metabolism, while zidovudine is primarily eliminated by hepatic glucuronidation.

### Key pharmacokinetic interactions (clinically relevant)
- Lamivudine
  - Transporter interactions: in vitro lamivudine is a substrate of MATE1, MATE2-K and OCT2 and of hepatic uptake transporter OCT1; lamivudine shows weak/no inhibition of several transporters (OATP1B1, OATP1B3, BCRP, P-gp, MATE1/2-K, OCT3) and inhibits OCT1/OCT2 in vitro at high concentrations. At therapeutic exposures, clinically significant transporter-mediated interactions are considered unlikely for most drugs.
  - Sorbitol (oral solutions): co-administration of sorbitol solutions (3.2 g, 10.2 g, 13.4 g) with a single 300 mg lamivudine dose produced dose-dependent decreases in lamivudine exposure (AUC reductions ~14%, 32%, 36% respectively) and reductions in Cmax (~28%, 52%, 55% respectively). Avoid chronic co-administration of sorbitol-containing medicines with lamivudine when possible; if unavoidable, monitor HIV-1 viral load more frequently.
  - Trimethoprim (as trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 160/800 mg): increases lamivudine plasma concentrations; generally no lamivudine dose adjustment needed unless the patient has renal impairment. Effects of higher doses of trimethoprim are unknown.
  - In vitro reductions in lamivudine anti-HIV activity reported with ciprofloxacin, pentamidine and ganciclovir; clinical significance unknown.
  - Lamivudine may inhibit intracellular phosphorylation of zalcitabine and emtricitabine; concurrent use with zalcitabine is not recommended. Lamivudine is not recommended in combination with emtricitabine or emtricitabine-containing products because of overlapping resistance mechanisms (M184V) and potential reduced efficacy.

- Zidovudine
  - Elimination: primarily by hepatic glucuronidation; caution with co-administered drugs sharing this pathway.
  - Atovaquone: concomitant atovaquone decreased rate of zidovudine glucuronidation and increased zidovudine steady-state AUC by ~33% while decreasing glucuronide metabolite peak by ~19%; short-term PCP therapy with atovaquone is unlikely to cause clinically significant zidovudine toxicity at usual zidovudine dosages (500–600 mg/day), but monitor during prolonged atovaquone therapy.
  - Clarithromycin: clarithromycin tablets reduce zidovudine absorption; separate administration by at least 2 hours to avoid this interaction.
  - Stavudine: zidovudine may inhibit intracellular phosphorylation of stavudine; concomitant use is not recommended.
  - Paracetamol (acetaminophen): chronic paracetamol use concomitant with zidovudine was associated with increased incidence of neutropenia in a controlled trial; exercise caution.
  - Co-administration with drugs metabolised by glucuronidation may potentiate toxicity of either agent due to competition for glucuronidation pathways.

### Clinical interaction management
- When dose modification is required for renal or hepatic impairment, or when significant drug–drug interactions are expected, discontinue the fixed-dose combination and use separate lamivudine and zidovudine products to permit individual dose titration.
- Monitor haematologic indices closely with concomitant agents associated with bone marrow suppression (e.g., trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole, ganciclovir, pentamidine) and with chronic paracetamol use.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Fixed-dose tablet dosing guidance is provided for children weighing 14–30 kg (see Section 4). The tablet is not suitable for children <14 kg because dosing cannot be accurately adjusted; oral solutions of the individual components should be used for younger children.
- Children <3 months: insufficient data to provide dosing recommendations.
- Paediatric dosing recommendations were based on pharmacokinetic modelling and clinical data for the individual components; increased zidovudine exposure may occur in some children in the 14–30 kg range and close safety monitoring is recommended.

### Elderly
- Specific data on elderly patients are not available; exercise special care because of age-associated decreases in renal function and potential alterations in haematologic parameters.

### Renal impairment
- Both lamivudine and zidovudine exposures are increased in renal impairment due to decreased clearance.
- Recommendation: for creatinine clearance ≤ 50 mL/min, dosage adjustment may be necessary; because the fixed-dose combination cannot be adjusted, use separate lamivudine and zidovudine formulations to permit appropriate dosing.
- In advanced renal failure, zidovudine Cmax and AUC are increased (example data: zidovudine Cmax rose from ~4.0 to ~6.2 μmol/L; AUC rose from ~5.2 to ~11.7 μmol⋅hr/L in uraemic patients), and the glucuronide metabolite shows marked accumulation (metabolite Cmax increased from ~14.9 to ~31.6 μmol/L; AUC substantially increased). The metabolite half-life may be prolonged (example: from ~0.9 hr to ~8.0 hr).
- Haemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis have no significant effect on zidovudine elimination; haemodialysis may remove the glucuronide metabolite more efficiently than peritoneal dialysis.

### Hepatic impairment
- Limited data suggest zidovudine accumulation may occur in patients with hepatic impairment due to decreased glucuronidation; dose adjustments may be necessary but specific recommendations are not provided.
- In severe hepatic impairment, consider using separate zidovudine and lamivudine formulations to allow dose modification and monitor patients closely for signs of intolerance.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Lamivudine: food reduces Cmax and delays Tmax but does not reduce total absorption (AUC).
  - Zidovudine: absorption variably reported; clarithromycin can reduce zidovudine absorption (separate dosing by ≥2 hours to avoid interaction).
- Distribution:
  - Not explicitly detailed in the available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Lamivudine: limited metabolism; mainly renal clearance; substrate of certain transporters (MATE1, MATE2-K, OCT2, OCT1) in vitro.
  - Zidovudine: eliminated predominantly via hepatic glucuronidation to an inactive glucuronide metabolite; this pathway can be affected by co-administered agents (e.g., atovaquone decreased conversion to glucuronide).
- Elimination:
  - Lamivudine: primarily renal elimination; clearance decreased in renal impairment with prolonged half-life.
  - Zidovudine: in renal failure, systemic exposure (AUC) increased about 100% and Cmax increased about 50% relative to healthy subjects; half-life of zidovudine itself not substantially altered in some studies, but glucuronide metabolite accumulates markedly with prolonged half-life.
  - Haemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis have little effect on zidovudine elimination.
- Notable quantitative interaction data:
  - Sorbitol co-administration with lamivudine oral solution: AUC reductions of approximately 14%–36% and Cmax reductions of approximately 28%–55% depending on sorbitol dose.
  - Trimethoprim increased lamivudine plasma levels; no dose adjustment required unless renal impairment is present.
  - Atovaquone increased zidovudine AUC by ~33% and decreased glucuronide Cmax by ~19%.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Not specified in detail in the available sources beyond clinical effects (antiretroviral activity) and class-associated toxicities such as lactic acidosis.
