# Editorial Product Information: Liraglutide

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of liraglutide, synthesising clinical and regulatory information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Physicians should consult the current local PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Liraglutide (human glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) analogue).
- **Brand Name(s):** ARX-Liraglutide [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A10BJ02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Solution for injection in a pre-filled pen.
    - 6 mg/mL (one 3 mL pen contains 18 mg of liraglutide) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Cipla Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Liraglutide is indicated as a supplement to a calorie-restricted diet and increased physical activity for chronic weight management in adults with an initial Body Mass Index (BMI) of:
- $\ge$ 30 kg/m² (obese), or
- $\ge$ 27 kg/m² to < 30 kg/m² (overweight) when accompanied by at least one weight-related comorbidity (e.g., hypertension, dyslipidaemia, obstructive sleep apnoea, or dysglycaemia such as type 2 diabetes or pre-diabetes) [Australian].

**Clinical threshold:** Treatment should be re-evaluated and potentially discontinued if a patient has not achieved a loss of at least 5% of their initial body weight after 12 weeks of treatment at the 3.0 mg daily dose [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** 0.6 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** Increase the daily dose in 0.6 mg increments at intervals of at least one week to improve gastrointestinal tolerability.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 3.0 mg once daily. Doses higher than 3.0 mg are not recommended [Australian].

### Titration Schedule
| Week | Daily Dose |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 | 0.6 mg |
| 2 | 1.2 mg |
| 3 | 1.8 mg |
| 4 | 2.4 mg |
| Maintenance | 3.0 mg |

### Administration Instructions
- Administer via subcutaneous injection once daily at any time, independent of meals.
- Injection sites: Abdomen, thigh, or upper arm. 
- Sites must be rotated within the same region to reduce the risk of cutaneous amyloidosis [Australian].
- Do not administer intravenously or intramuscularly.

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed and it is within 12 hours of the usual time, the patient should take the dose as soon as possible.
- If less than 12 hours remain until the next scheduled dose, the missed dose should be skipped. 
- Do not take a double dose to compensate for a missed one [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to liraglutide or any of the product's excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources [Australian].
- **Thyroid C-cell Tumours:** Animal studies (mice/rats) showed dose-dependent and exposure-dependent C-cell adenomas and carcinomas. The relevance to humans is currently considered low but cannot be entirely excluded. Exercise caution in patients with thyroid disease [Australian].
- **Pancreatitis:** Acute pancreatitis has been observed with GLP-1 receptor agonists. Patients should be informed of characteristic symptoms (persistent, severe abdominal pain). Discontinue permanently if pancreatitis is confirmed. Not recommended in patients with a history of pancreatitis [Australian].
- **Cholelithiasis and Cholecystitis:** Substantial weight loss is associated with gallstones; however, clinical trials showed an increased incidence of cholelithiasis and cholecystitis in liraglutide patients compared to placebo.
- **Heart Rate:** A mean increase in resting heart rate of 2 to 3 beats per minute (bpm) has been observed. Resting heart rate should be monitored, and treatment discontinued if a sustained increase occurs [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Acute renal failure and worsening of chronic renal failure have been reported, often associated with nausea, vomiting, or diarrhoea leading to volume depletion. Use caution when initiating or escalating doses in patients with renal impairment [Australian].
- **Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation:** Monitor patients for depression, suicidal thoughts, or unusual changes in mood. Discontinue treatment if symptoms emerge. Not recommended for patients with a history of major depressive disorder or other major psychiatric disorders [Australian].
- **Not a Substitute for Insulin:** Liraglutide must not be used as a substitute for insulin in patients with diabetes mellitus. It should not be used in combination with insulin [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, and headache.
- **Common (1–10%):** Dyspepsia, abdominal pain (upper), abdominal distension, eructation, flatulence, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), dry mouth, gastritis, injection site reactions, fatigue, asthenia, dizziness, dysgeusia, insomnia, cholelithiasis, increased lipase, and increased amylase [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Pancreatitis, delayed gastric emptying, dehydration, malaise, cholecystitis, tachycardia, and urticaria [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Anaphylactic reactions, angioedema, acute renal failure, and renal impairment [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** Identified in 0.3% of liraglutide-treated patients in clinical trials.
- **Acute Gallbladder Disease:** Often requiring cholecystectomy.
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Risk is increased when used concomitantly with insulin secretagogues (e.g., sulfonylureas) in patients with type 2 diabetes. 

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Liraglutide has a low potential for CYP-related interactions or plasma protein binding displacement.
- **Gastric Emptying:** Liraglutide causes a delay in gastric emptying, which may impact the absorption of concomitantly administered oral medications. 
    - **Warfarin:** No interaction study performed; however, more frequent monitoring of International Normalised Ratio (INR) is recommended upon initiation [Australian].
    - **Paracetamol/Atorvastatin/Digoxin/Lisinopril/Oral Contraceptives:** Clinical studies showed no requirements for dose adjustments, though minor delays in $T_{max}$ or reductions in $C_{max}$ were noted [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** Liraglutide should not be mixed with other injectable products (e.g., infusion fluids).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **[Australian Category B3]:** Animal studies have shown evidence of increased embryofetal death and skeletal abnormalities. There are limited data in pregnant women. Liraglutide should not be used during pregnancy; treatment must be discontinued if pregnancy occurs or is planned [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if liraglutide is excreted in human milk. Due to lack of experience and animal data showing reduced neonatal growth in suckling pups, liraglutide must not be used during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at exposures significantly higher than human doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children and adolescents under 18 years of age. Use is not indicated in this population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 65 years):** No dose adjustment required based on age. However, experience in patients $\ge$ 75 years is limited, and use is not recommended in this age group. Use caution in those aged 65–74 years due to higher potential for gastrointestinal reactions [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild or Moderate (CrCL $\ge$ 30 mL/min): No dose adjustment required.
    - Severe or End-Stage Renal Disease (CrCL < 30 mL/min): Use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended for use in patients with any degree of hepatic insufficiency [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Liraglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist with 97% homology to human GLP-1. It increases satiety and decreases hunger signals in the brain (hypothalamus), leading to reduced food intake and weight loss. It also improves glycaemic control by stimulating insulin secretion and lowering glucagon secretion in a glucose-dependent manner.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Slow absorption after subcutaneous injection; maximum concentration ($C_{max}$) reached at approximately 11 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Mean apparent volume of distribution is 20–25 L. Extensively bound to plasma proteins (>98%).
    - **Metabolism:** Endogenously metabolised like large proteins; no specific organ is the primary route of elimination.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 13 hours, making it suitable for once-daily administration [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
The SCALE (Satiety and Clinical Adiposity—Liraglutide Evidence) clinical development program included over 5,000 patients across four Phase 3 trials:
- **SCALE Obesity and Pre-diabetes:** Showed significant weight loss compared to placebo over 56 and 160 weeks. Liraglutide reduced the risk of developing type 2 diabetes by approximately 80% (HR 0.2) over 160 weeks [Australian].
- **SCALE Diabetes:** Evaluated obese/overweight patients with type 2 diabetes; liraglutide achieved superior weight loss and glycaemic improvement compared to placebo.
- **SCALE Sleep Apnoea:** Demonstrated reduction in the severity of obstructive sleep apnoea alongside weight loss.
- **SCALE Maintenance:** Showed that liraglutide helped maintain weight loss previously achieved through a low-calorie diet alone [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Pens:** Store in a refrigerator (2°C to 8°C). Do not freeze.
- **After First Use:** Store for up to 30 days at room temperature (below 30°C) or in a refrigerator (2°C to 8°C).
- **General Handling:** Keep the pen cap on to protect from light. Discard the needle after every injection to prevent contamination and leakage [Australian].
