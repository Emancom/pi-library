# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the United States (US) product documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; a naturally occurring carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

### Monitoring
- **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is advised. Normal free carnitine levels are typically between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine in the provided source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately [US].
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in both patients with and without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### General Precautions
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral formulations in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Monitor INR in patients concurrently taking warfarin.

- **Other Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis occur less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a lettered category]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in lactating humans are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders (50 mg/kg) is applicable to paediatric patients. In neonates and premature infants, secondary carnitine deficiency is defined by plasma levels below age-related norms [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document.
- **Renal Impairment:** See **Dosage and Administration** for ESRD-specific dosing. Note that while IV levocarnitine is used in this population, oral formulations carry risks of metabolite accumulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and ATP production. It also aids in the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Dose-Ranging Study:** A study of ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine levels. After 24 weeks, mean trough concentrations were 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L, respectively (compared to a baseline of ~21 µmol/L) [US].
- **Clinical Outcome Note:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine plasma concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical signs/symptoms or long-term outcomes in this population remains undetermined by existing pivotal data [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
