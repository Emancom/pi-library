# Editorial Product Information: Lanreotide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lanreotide (as acetate) for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information. Clinicians should refer to the local approved PI for prescribing requirements in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lanreotide acetate (a synthetic somatostatin analogue).
- **Brand Names:** **Mytolac** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H01CB03 (Antigrowth hormones).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Solution for injection in a pre-filled syringe: 60 mg, 90 mg, and 120 mg [Australian].
    - Formulation: A supersaturated, semi-solid, prolonged-release solution.
- **Route of Administration:** Deep subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amdipharm Mercury (Australia) Pty Ltd (An ADVANZ PHARMA company) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Based on available source data, lanreotide is indicated for:
- **Acromegaly:** Treatment when growth hormone (GH) and IGF-1 levels remain abnormal after surgery/radiotherapy, or in patients refractory to dopamine agonists [Australian-approved].
- **Carcinoid Syndrome:** Relief of symptoms associated with carcinoid (neuroendocrine) tumours [Australian-approved].
- **Gastroenteropancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumours (GEP-NETs):** Treatment of unresectable, locally advanced, or metastatic disease in adults [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Acromegaly:** 
    - Naive patients: Start with 60 mg every 28 days.
    - Switching from microparticle formulations: 60 mg (if previously every 14 days), 90 mg (if previously every 10 days), or 120 mg (if previously every 7 days) [Australian].
    - Adjustment: Titrate based on GH/IGF-1 levels. Patients controlled on 120 mg may extend the dosing interval to every 42–56 days [Australian].
- **Carcinoid Syndrome:** Recommended starting dose is 60 mg to 120 mg every 28 days, adjusted based on symptomatic relief [Australian].
- **GEP-NETs:** 120 mg administered every 28 days [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Administered via deep subcutaneous injection in the superior external quadrant of the buttock or the upper outer thigh.
- **Healthcare Professional Administration:** Initially required.
- **Self-Administration:** Patients or carers may administer the dose if they are well-controlled, motivated, and adequately trained. Self-injection must occur in the upper outer thigh [Australian].
- **Technique:** Do not fold the skin; insert the needle rapidly to its full length, perpendicular to the skin. Alternate injection sites between the right and left sides.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to lanreotide, related peptides, or any excipients [Australian].
- **Lactation:** Use is contraindicated during breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Glycaemic Control:** Lanreotide inhibits insulin and glucagon secretion. Patients may experience hypoglycaemia or hyperglycaemia. Monitor blood glucose upon initiation or dose change; adjust diabetes treatment as needed. Insulin requirements may decrease [Australian].
- **Cholelithiasis:** Lanreotide reduces gallbladder motility. Gallbladder echography is recommended at initiation and every 6 months. Post-marketing reports include cholecystitis, cholangitis, and pancreatitis requiring cholecystectomy [Australian].
- **Bradycardia:** May decrease heart rate. Use caution in patients with pre-existing cardiac disorders or sinus bradycardia. Monitor heart rate in at-risk patients [Australian].
- **Thyroid Function:** Slight decreases in thyroid function may occur. Monitor thyroid function tests where clinically indicated [Australian].
- **Pancreatic Function:** Pancreatic exocrine insufficiency (PEI) has been observed, manifesting as steatorrhoea, weight loss, or bloating. Screen and treat symptomatic patients according to clinical guidelines [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily gastrointestinal and consistent with the somatostatin analogue class.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea, loose stools, abdominal pain, cholelithiasis. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, flatulence, constipation, steatorrhoea; Hypoglycaemia, hyperglycaemia, diabetes mellitus; Dizziness, headache; Sinus bradycardia; Alopecia; Asthenia, fatigue; Injection site reactions (pain, mass, induration, nodule, pruritus). |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Insomnia; Hot flushes; Discoloured faeces; Bodyweight decrease. |
| **Not Known** | Angioedema, anaphylaxis; Pancreatitis, cholecystitis; Injection site abscess. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Cholelithiasis complications (cholecystitis, cholangitis) and symptomatic bradycardia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Bradycardia-inducing Drugs:** Concomitant use with drugs such as beta-blockers may have additive effects on heart rate reduction; dose adjustment may be necessary [Australian].
- **Ciclosporin A:** Lanreotide may reduce the intestinal absorption of ciclosporin, decreasing its bioavailability. Monitor ciclosporin levels and adjust dosage [Australian].
- **Bromocriptine:** Somatostatin analogues may increase the availability of bromocriptine [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Lanreotide may decrease the metabolic clearance of compounds metabolised by CYP450 enzymes (due to GH suppression). Use caution with narrow therapeutic index drugs (e.g., quinidine, terfenadine) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C:** Not recommended during pregnancy. 
    - Data are limited (<300 outcomes). Animal studies show no teratogenicity but suggest potential foetal growth retardation due to GH suppression [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if lanreotide is excreted in human milk. Animal studies showed growth retardation in nursing offspring [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed decreased fertility index and increased pre-implantation loss [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established; use is not advised [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Half-life is increased, but due to the wide therapeutic window, no dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance is significantly reduced in severe impairment (half-life approximately doubled). However, no dose adjustment is recommended due to the wide therapeutic window, though monitoring is advised [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is reduced by approximately 30% in moderate-to-severe impairment. No dose adjustment is required, but monitoring of liver function is recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lanreotide is a peptide inhibitor of endocrine, neuroendocrine, exocrine, and paracrine functions. It has high affinity for peripheral somatostatin receptors (SSTR2 and SSTR5), primarily inhibiting growth hormone secretion with higher selectivity compared to insulin inhibition [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces GH and IGF-1 levels. In GEP-NET patients, it significantly reduces plasma chromogranin A and urinary 5-HIAA [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~63% (SC). Steady-state is reached after 4–5 injections.
    - **Distribution:** Limited extravascular distribution (Vd ~13 L).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life at steady state is approximately 43.6 days [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **GEP-NETs (Study 726):** A Phase III, 96-week trial showed that lanreotide 120 mg significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS) compared to placebo (PFS not reached vs. 72 weeks, respectively). This represents a 53% reduction in the risk of disease progression or death (Hazard Ratio 0.47) [Australian].
- **Acromegaly:** Switch studies demonstrated that monthly lanreotide solution for injection was non-inferior to microparticle formulations administered every 7 to 14 days in maintaining GH and IGF-1 control [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze). Protect from light [Australian].
- **Excursions:** Once removed from refrigeration, the product in its sealed pouch may be returned to the refrigerator up to three times. Total cumulative time out of the refrigerator must not exceed 72 hours at temperatures below 40°C [Australian].
