# Editorial Product Information: Regadenoson

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of regadenoson for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Regadenoson (generic name); a low-affinity A2A adenosine receptor agonist and coronary vasodilator.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Lexiscan** [US].
- **ATC Code:** C01EB21.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injection: Single-dose pre-filled syringe containing 0.4 mg/5 mL (0.08 mg/mL) of regadenoson [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Astellas Pharma US, Inc. (Marketed); HF Acquisition Co LLC, DBA HealthFirst (Distributor) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pharmacologic Stress Agent:** Indicated for radionuclide myocardial perfusion imaging (MPI) in patients who are unable to undergo an adequate exercise stress test [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard dose is a single 5 mL (0.4 mg regadenoson) intravenous injection.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer as a rapid injection (within approximately 10 seconds) into a peripheral vein.
    - Use a 22-gauge or larger catheter/needle.
    - Immediately follow the injection with a 5 mL saline flush.
    - Administer the radionuclide MPI imaging agent 10 to 20 seconds after the saline flush. The radionuclide may be injected into the same catheter as the regadenoson [US].
- **Pre-procedural Instructions:** Patients must avoid methylxanthine-containing products (e.g., caffeinated coffee, tea, chocolate, or medications containing caffeine, aminophylline, or theophylline) for at least 12 hours prior to the MPI [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter or discoloration before use. Only clear, colorless solutions should be administered [US].

## 4. Contraindications
Regadenoson is contraindicated in patients with:
- Second- or third-degree atrioventricular (AV) block [US].
- Sinus node dysfunction [US].
- *Note:* These contraindications do not apply if the patient has a functioning artificial pacemaker [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myocardial Ischemia:** Fatal and nonfatal myocardial infarction (MI), ventricular arrhythmias, and cardiac arrest have occurred. Avoid use in patients with symptoms or signs of acute myocardial ischemia (e.g., unstable angina, cardiovascular instability) due to the risk of serious reactions. Ensure cardiac resuscitation equipment and trained staff are available during administration [US].
- **Sinoatrial (SA) and AV Nodal Block:** Adenosine receptor agonists can depress SA and AV nodes. Cases of first-, second-, and third-degree block, or sinus bradycardia requiring intervention, have been reported [US].
- **Atrial Fibrillation/Flutter:** New-onset or recurrent atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response and atrial flutter have been reported post-injection [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, cardiac/respiratory arrest, and skin reactions (urticaria, rash) have occurred. Resuscitative equipment must be immediately available [US].
- **Hypotension:** Regadenoson induces vasodilation. Increased risk of serious hypotension exists in patients with autonomic dysfunction, hypovolemia, stenotic valvular heart disease, or carotid artery disease [US].
- **Hypertension:** Clinically significant increases in blood pressure may occur, particularly in patients with pre-existing hypertension or when MPI includes low-level exercise [US].
- **Bronchoconstriction:** May induce dyspnea and respiratory compromise in patients with COPD or asthma. Bronchodilator therapy and resuscitative measures should be available [US].
- **Seizure:** Regadenoson may lower the seizure threshold. Methylxanthine use is not recommended in patients who experience a seizure following administration [US].
- **Cerebrovascular Accident:** Both hemorrhagic and ischemic strokes have occurred [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typically transient, beginning shortly after dosing and resolving within 15–30 minutes (except for headache, which may last longer).

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dyspnea (28%), Headache (26%), Flushing (16%), Chest discomfort (13%), Angina pectoris or ST segment depression (12%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness (8%), Chest pain (7%), Nausea (6%), Abdominal discomfort (5%), Dysgeusia (5%), Feeling hot (5%) |
| **ECG Changes** | Rhythm or conduction abnormalities (26%), PACs (7%), PVCs (14%), First-degree AV block (3%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Post-marketing reports include cardiac arrest, MI, ventricular arrhythmias, asystole, symptomatic hypotension with TIA, seizures, and stroke [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Methylxanthines (Caffeine, Theophylline, Aminophylline):** These are non-specific adenosine receptor antagonists that interfere with the vasodilatory activity of regadenoson. Withhold for at least 12 hours prior to MPI [US].
- **Aminophylline:** May be used therapeutically (50 mg to 250 mg IV) to attenuate severe or persistent adverse reactions to regadenoson [US].
- **Dipyridamole:** May increase the activity of regadenoson. If possible, withhold dipyridamole for at least two days before administration [US].
- **Cardioactive Drugs:** In clinical trials, regadenoson was safely administered alongside beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, ACE inhibitors, nitrates, and cardiac glycosides without apparent effect on efficacy [US].
- **CYP450 Interactions:** Regadenoson does not inhibit major CYP enzymes (1A2, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4) in human liver microsomes [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified as a letter grade in current labeling; however, there are no available data on regadenoson use in pregnant women to inform drug-associated risks. Animal studies showed fetal toxicity (reduced weights, ossification delays, or malformations) only at doses that also caused maternal toxicity [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if regadenoson is excreted in human milk. Due to the risk of serious cardiac reactions in the infant, nursing mothers should pump and discard breast milk for **10 hours** following regadenoson administration [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies to evaluate effects on fertility have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required. Patients >75 years of age showed a similar adverse event profile to younger patients but had a slightly higher incidence of hypotension (2% vs ≤1%) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required, including in patients with end-stage renal disease or those on dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. Regadenoson pharmacokinetics have not been specifically evaluated in this population, but >55% of the dose is excreted unchanged in urine [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Regadenoson is a low-affinity agonist for the A2A adenosine receptor. Activation of this receptor produces coronary vasodilation and increases coronary blood flow (CBF). It has a 10-fold lower affinity for the A1 receptor and weak affinity for A2B and A3 receptors [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Characterized by a 3-compartment model.
    - **Metabolism:** Unknown in humans; no detectable metabolites in liver microsomes.
    - **Elimination:** The plasma concentration-time profile shows an initial half-life of 2–4 minutes, an intermediate half-life of 30 minutes, and a terminal half-life of approximately 2 hours.
    - **Excretion:** 57% of the dose is excreted unchanged in the urine [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Mean coronary blood flow velocity increases to more than twice baseline within 30 seconds and returns to less than twice baseline within 10 minutes [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Agreement Studies:** Two randomized, double-blind trials (Studies 1 and 2) compared regadenoson to adenosine (Adenoscan). The trials demonstrated that regadenoson was similar to adenosine in assessing the extent of reversible perfusion abnormalities, with an average agreement rate of approximately 62-63% [US].
- **Inadequate Exercise Stress:** An open-label study showed that regadenoson administered either 3 minutes or 1 hour after inadequate exercise provided similar results to a rest-only regadenoson scan. However, serious cardiac reactions were slightly higher when administered 3 minutes after exercise [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature, 25°C (77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose container; discard any unused portion [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:**
    - **US:** Rx-only (Prescription Drug) [US].
