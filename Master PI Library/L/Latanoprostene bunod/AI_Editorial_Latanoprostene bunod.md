# Editorial Product Information: Latanoprostene bunod

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of latanoprostene bunod for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction for the purposes of prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Latanoprostene bunod (a prostaglandin F2α analogue and nitric oxide donor).
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specifically named in the provided source [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Topical ophthalmic solution 0.024% (0.24 mg/mL) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Ophthalmic (topical).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Gland Pharma Limited [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reduction of Intraocular Pressure (IOP):** Indicated for patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** One drop in the conjunctival sac of the affected eye(s) once daily in the evening [United States].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Do not exceed once-daily dosing; frequent administration of prostaglandin analogues may diminish the IOP-lowering effect [United States].
    - If using multiple topical ophthalmic drugs, space administrations by at least five (5) minutes [United States].
    - To avoid contamination, ensure the dropper tip does not touch the eye, surrounding structures, or any other surface [United States].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None:** No contraindications are listed in the available source [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Pigmentation:** May cause increased brown pigmentation of the iris, periorbital tissue (eyelid), and eyelashes. Iris pigmentation is likely permanent, whereas periorbital and eyelash changes are typically reversible upon discontinuation [United States].
- **Eyelash Changes:** Gradual changes may occur, including increased length, thickness, number, and pigmentation of lashes. These are usually reversible upon treatment cessation [United States].
- **Intraocular Inflammation:** Use with caution in patients with a history of iritis or uveitis. Generally, avoid use in patients with active intraocular inflammation as the condition may be exacerbated [United States].
- **Macular Edema:** Reports of macular edema (including cystoid macular edema) have occurred with prostaglandin analogues. Use with caution in aphakic patients, pseudophakic patients with a torn posterior lens capsule, or those with known risk factors for macular edema [United States].
- **Bacterial Keratitis:** Risk of bacterial contamination of multiple-dose containers, particularly if the corneal epithelium is compromised or if patients have concurrent corneal disease [United States].
- **Contact Lens Use:** The solution contains benzalkonium chloride, which may be absorbed by soft contact lenses. Lenses should be removed prior to instillation and may be reinserted 15 minutes post-administration [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials (up to 12 months) include:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Conjunctival hyperemia (6%), eye irritation (4%), eye pain (3%), instillation site pain (2%) |
| **Discontinuation (<1%)** | Approximately 0.6% of patients discontinued due to ocular hyperemia, conjunctival irritation/edema, eye pain, blurred vision, punctate keratitis, or foreign body sensation |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Not specified as "serious" in the source, but the potential for permanent iris colour change and vision loss from contaminated containers is highlighted [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Management Notes:** Maintain a 5-minute interval between latanoprostene bunod and other topical ophthalmic medications [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in this source version, but human data are unavailable. Animal studies in rabbits showed latanoprostene bunod is abortifacient and teratogenic at intravenous doses $\ge$ 0.28 times the clinical dose. Adverse outcomes included embryofetal lethality and structural malformations (e.g., anomalies of the great vessels, domed head, and skeletal anomalies) [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data exist regarding the presence of the drug in human milk or its effects on milk production/the infant. Consider developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the maternal clinical need [United States].
- **Fertility:** Not directly tested for latanoprostene bunod; however, latanoprost acid (a metabolite) showed no effect on male or female fertility in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in patients aged 16 years and younger is **not recommended** due to safety concerns regarding increased pigmentation following long-term chronic use [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy observed between geriatric and younger adult patients [United States].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A dual-action agent that lowers IOP by increasing aqueous humor outflow through both the trabecular meshwork (via nitric oxide signaling) and the uveoscleral routes (via prostaglandin F2α analogue activity) [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly metabolised in the eye to latanoprost acid and butanediol mononitrate. Plasma concentrations of the parent drug and butanediol mononitrate are typically below the limit of quantitation post-dose [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Latanoprost acid is further metabolised by the liver via fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation. Butanediol mononitrate is metabolised to 1,4-butanediol and nitric oxide [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Rapid; latanoprost acid plasma levels generally fall below the limit of quantitation within 15 minutes of administration [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** IOP reduction begins 1 to 3 hours after administration, with peak effect reached at 11 to 13 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- In controlled trials up to 12 months in patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension (average baseline IOP of 26.7 mmHg), once-daily evening administration of latanoprostene bunod 0.024% reduced IOP by up to 7 to 9 mmHg [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened bottles:** Store refrigerated at $2^\circ\text{C}$ to $8^\circ\text{C}$ ($36^\circ\text{F}$ to $46^\circ\text{F}$) [United States].
- **Opened bottles:** May be stored at $2^\circ\text{C}$ to $25^\circ\text{C}$ ($36^\circ\text{F}$ to $77^\circ\text{F}$) for up to 8 weeks [United States].
- **Shipping:** May be maintained at temperatures up to $40^\circ\text{C}$ ($104^\circ\text{F}$) for a maximum of 14 days [United States].
- **Handling:** Protect from light and freezing [United States].
