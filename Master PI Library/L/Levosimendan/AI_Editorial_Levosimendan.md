# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided for **Levocarnitine Injection** from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians should refer to local regulatory guidelines for prescribing and administration within their specific region.
- *Editorial clarification:* While the introductory task description mentioned Levosimendan, the provided source documentation pertains exclusively to **Levocarnitine**. This editorial reflects the provided Levocarnitine source [US].

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not specified in source, provided for clinical context).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution for intravenous use [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h); frequency should never be less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels (normal range 40–50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No specific contraindications are listed in the available source; however, serious hypersensitivity reactions have been reported [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitoring should include weekly and monthly blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target range: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Note):** While this PI is for injection, it is noted that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are reported based on clinical trials in patients with ESRD on chronic hemodialysis.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30%), headache (22%), abdominal pain (21%), fever (12%), accidental injury (12%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14–19%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16–35%), vomiting (15–21%), nausea (12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (11%) [US].
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (13–18%), paresthesia (12%) [US].

### Common (1–10%)
- **General:** Chest pain, flu syndrome, infection, asthenia, back pain [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, palpitations, arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (up to 15%), hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes [US].
- **Musculoskeletal:** Leg cramps [US].
- **Other:** Anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia, pruritus, eye disorders, taste perversion [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity:** Laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. It is unknown if supplemental levocarnitine is excreted in human milk. Weigh the benefits of breastfeeding against the risk of excess carnitine intake for the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders (50 mg/kg) is applicable to paediatric patients; however, clinical trials specifically for ESRD primarily reflect adult populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients. The drug is easily removed by dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels rose from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While the drug increases carnitine concentrations, the effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].
