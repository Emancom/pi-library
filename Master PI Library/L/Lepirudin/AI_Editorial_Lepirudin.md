# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial review is based on official Product Information (PI) from the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Doses should be given every 3 or 4 hours; intervals should never be longer than 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg or as clinically required.
- **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL.
- **Stability:** Once mixed in PVC bags, the solution remains stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C).

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, sometimes within minutes of administration. Primarily reported in dialysis patients. Discontinue immediately if reactions occur.
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed.
- **General Precautions:** The safety of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal impairment has not been established; chronic high-dose oral use in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). 
- **Monitoring:** 
    - **Plasma Levels:** Recommended trough levels are between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
    - **ESRD:** Monitor pre-dialysis plasma concentrations weekly or monthly.
    - **General:** Monitor blood chemistry, vital signs, and overall clinical condition.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting. 
- **Other Reported Effects:** Body odor, gastritis, and abdominal pain.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures.

### Adverse Events in ESRD Patients (≥5% Incidence)
In clinical trials of dialysis patients, the following events occurred regardless of causality:
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), chest pain (12%), palpitations (5%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Systemic/General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), asthenia (9%), fever (7%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), insomnia (4%), depression (5%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Category not formally lettered in the revised text, but the PI states "should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed."
    - **Animal Data:** No evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human surface area dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Risks to the infant from excess intake must be weighed against maternal benefit.
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects seen in standard tests; long-term carcinogenicity studies have not been conducted.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg body weight, applicable to paediatric patients.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources regarding dose adjustments.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing adjustments in ESRD are based on pre-dialysis trough levels (Target: 40–50 µmol/L).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This allows for beta-oxidation and subsequent energy production in skeletal and cardiac muscle. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and gamma-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients on hemodialysis results in increased plasma concentrations of free carnitine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
