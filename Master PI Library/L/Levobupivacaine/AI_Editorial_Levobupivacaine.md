# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the following official Product Information (PI) source: United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- Administer intravenously as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- Parenteral products should be visually inspected for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg as a slow bolus or infusion.
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40 to 50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately and initiate appropriate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without a history of seizure activity. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Thromboembolic Events/INR:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with warfarin.
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of high-dose **oral** levocarnitine has not been established in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD, as it may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). The IV formulation bypasses gastrointestinal metabolism to these compounds [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting. Less frequent effects include body odor and gastritis.
- **ESRD Patient Trial Data (Frequency ≥5%):**
    - **General:** Abdominal pain (21%), headache (12%–37%), infection (10%–24%), injection site reaction (27%–38%), pain (21%–35%), fever (5%–12%), asthenia (8%–12%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18%–21%), hypotension (3%–19%), tachycardia (5%–9%).
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9%–35%), vomiting (9%–21%), nausea (5%–12%).
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6%–15%), hypervolemia (3%–12%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15%–27%), cough increase (10%–18%), dyspnea (3%–11%).
    - **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10%–18%), paresthesia (3%–12%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Concomitant use may increase INR. **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [US]: Category C. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. Weigh the risks to the child against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing for ESRD is specific to the dialysis setting (see Section 3). The accumulation of metabolites from oral administration is a concern in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean plasma levocarnitine concentrations from 20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to troughs of 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Evaluated 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group). Trough concentrations increased proportionally with the dose over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) at 25°C [US].
