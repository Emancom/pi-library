# Originality Audit Report: lifileucel

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 88/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 2
- **Overall Assessment:** Pass

## Verbatim Copied Passages
1. **The Therapeutic Indication Statement**
    - **Editorial Text:** "...indicated for the treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic melanoma who have previously been treated with a PD-1 blocking antibody, and if BRAF V600 mutation positive, a BRAF inhibitor with or without a MEK inhibitor." (Section 2)
    - **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA) Section 4.1 and Document 2 (FDA) Indications and Usage section.
    - **Note:** While this is a verbatim copy, in regulatory editorial writing, the "Approved Indication" is typically required to be kept exact to ensure clinical accuracy and legal compliance.

2. **Filter Requirements**
    - **Editorial Text:** "A Y-type blood filter with a 150-260 micron pore size. Do NOT use a leukocyte-depleting filter." (Section 3)
    - **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA) Section 4.2 ("A Y-type blood filter with a pore size in the range of 150-260 microns is required. Do NOT use a leukocyte-depleting filter.")
    - **Note:** This is near-verbatim.

## Close Paraphrases
1. **Premedication Instructions**
    - **Editorial Text:** "Administer paracetamol (acetaminophen) and diphenhydramine (or other H1-antihistamine) 30 to 60 minutes before infusion to reduce allergic risk." (Section 3)
    - **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA) Section 4.2. The editorial substitutes "to reduce allergic risk" for the source's "to reduce the possibility of an allergic reaction."
2. **Pharmacokinetics (TCR Tracking)**
    - **Editorial Text:** "Persistence is tracked via unique TCR clonotypes in peripheral blood. TCR clonotypes peaked at Day 4 (83%) and typically remained between 37% and 41% at 12 months." (Section 10)
    - **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA) Section 5.2. The editorial condenses highly technical language but retains the exact statistical peak and range.

## Originality Strengths
- **Harmonisation of Disparate Data:** The editorial excels at identifying and presenting differences in data between the two agencies, particularly in Section 1 (Cell Concentration ranges) and Section 11 (Clinical Trials), where it explains why the TGA (n=87) and FDA (n=73) efficacy sets differ despite coming from the same study (C-144-01).
- **Consolidation of Safety Data:** Section 6 (Adverse Effects) creates a new tabular structure that merges frequency data from the TGA with specific mentions of differences in Capillary Leak Syndrome rates between jurisdictions.
- **Structural Reorganisation:** The editorial follows a logical "Clinical Overview" flow rather than the strict 1-30+ page regulatory format of the source PIs.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Jurisdictional Comparison:** The editorial explicitly side-by-sides TGA and FDA requirements (e.g., the specific cell counts and the differences in "Accelerated Approval" status).
- **Omission of Logistics:** The editorial removes localized logistical details such as the "34-day" manufacturing wait time mentioned in the Australian PI, focusing instead on the clinical administration.
- **Clarification of Terminology:** The editorial adds parenthetical equivalents for international clarity, such as "paracetamol (acetaminophen)."
- **Combined Contraindications:** Section 4 adds an "Editorial Note" reminding clinicians to check the contraindications for the auxiliary agents (IL-2 and lymphodepletion), which are listed separately in the source PIs.

## Recommendation
**PASS.**

The editorial is a high-quality synthesis. While the core indication and specific technical filter requirements are verbatim, this is standard practice in medical regulatory writing to prevent "interpretation errors" regarding the legal scope of the drug's use. The writer has successfully transformed two dense, 20+ page technical documents into a harmonised, easy-to-navigate editorial summary while maintaining original synthesis in the majority of the explanatory text. No revision is required.
