# Editorial Product Information: Lovastatin

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for lovastatin. It is designed as a concise reference for healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial summary and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing decisions in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lovastatin (a cholesterol-lowering agent derived from *Aspergillus terreus*).
- **Chemical Class:** HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin); inactive lactone prodrug.
- **Brand Names:** Available primarily as generic **Lovastatin** Tablets USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Oral tablets in 10 mg, 20 mg, and 40 mg strengths [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** PD-Rx Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (Repackager) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypercholesterolemia:** Adjunct to dietary modification for the reduction of elevated total cholesterol (Total-C) and LDL-C in patients with primary hypercholesterolemia (Types IIa and IIb) when non-pharmacological interventions are insufficient [US].
- **Primary Prevention of Coronary Heart Disease (CHD):** In individuals without symptomatic cardiovascular disease but with average to moderately elevated Total-C/LDL-C and below-average HDL-C, to reduce the risk of myocardial infarction, unstable angina, and coronary revascularization procedures [US].
- **Coronary Heart Disease Progression:** Indicated to slow the progression of coronary atherosclerosis in patients with established CHD as part of a strategy to lower Total-C and LDL-C to target levels [US].
- **Adolescent Patients (Heterozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia):** Adjunct to diet to reduce Total-C, LDL-C, and apolipoprotein B in boys and girls (at least one year post-menarche), aged 10–17 years, following an inadequate trial of diet therapy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The typical range is 10 mg to 80 mg per day, administered as a single dose or in two divided doses. The maximum recommended daily dose is 80 mg [US].
- **Administration:** Doses should be taken with meals. Single daily doses are most effective when taken with the evening meal [US].
- **Titration:** Adjustments should occur at intervals of 4 weeks or longer, based on the patient's individual therapeutic goals [US].
- **Indication-Specific Dosing:**
    - **Adolescents (10–17 years):** Range of 10–40 mg/day. Doses exceeding 40 mg/day have not been studied in this population [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to any component of the formulation [US].
- **Hepatic Disease:** Active liver disease or unexplained, persistent elevations in serum transaminases [US].
- **Pregnancy and Breastfeeding:** Use is contraindicated as cholesterol and its biosynthetic products are essential for fetal development; potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants [US].
- **Drug Interactions:** Concurrent use of potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, HIV protease inhibitors, erythromycin, clarithromycin, nefazodone, and cobicistat-containing products) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myopathy and Rhabdomyolysis:** Risk is dose-related. Patients must report unexplained muscle pain, tenderness, or weakness immediately. Therapy should be suspended if myopathy is suspected or if the patient faces an acute, serious condition predisposing to renal failure (e.g., sepsis, major surgery, trauma) [US].
- **Immune-Mediated Necrotizing Myopathy (IMNM):** Rare reports of this autoimmune myopathy (proximal muscle weakness and elevated serum CK persisting despite treatment discontinuation) have occurred [US].
- **Liver Dysfunction:** Persistent increases in serum transaminases (over 3x the upper limit of normal) have been observed. Liver enzyme tests are recommended before starting therapy and thereafter if clinically indicated [US].
- **Endocrine Effects:** Increases in HbA1c and fasting serum glucose have been reported with statin use [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic creatine kinase (CK) determinations may be considered for patients initiating therapy or undergoing dose increases [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Contraindicated due to significantly increased lovastatin plasma levels and myopathy risk [US].
- **Gemfibrozil/Cyclosporine:** Concomitant use should be avoided [US].
- **Amiodarone:** Do not exceed 40 mg of lovastatin daily [US].
- **Calcium Channel Blockers & Others:** For patients taking danazol, diltiazem, dronedarone, or verapamil, the lovastatin dose should not exceed 20 mg/day [US].
- **Grapefruit Juice:** Avoid consumption, as it significantly increases lovastatin concentrations [US].
- **Coumarin Anticoagulants:** Bleeding and increased prothrombin time have been reported; monitor prothrombin time carefully at the start of therapy and after dose changes [US].
- **Niacin/Fibrates:** Increased risk of myopathy; weigh benefits against risks [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X** [US]. Contraindicated. Lovastatin should be discontinued immediately if pregnancy is recognized.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if lovastatin is excreted in human milk, but its potential for serious neonatal toxicity requires cessation of nursing or the drug [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for adolescent boys and girls (10–17 years) with heFH. Safety/efficacy not established in children <10 years or pre-pubertal patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Plasma levels may be approximately 45% higher, and advanced age (≥65 years) is a predisposing factor for myopathy. Use with caution [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** In severe renal insufficiency (CrCl <30 mL/min), doses above 20 mg/day should be implemented cautiously and only if deemed necessary [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in active liver disease or unexplained transaminase elevations [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive inhibition of HMG-CoA reductase, the enzyme catalyzing the rate-limiting step in cholesterol biosynthesis. This leads to an induction of LDL receptors and reduced VLDL-C/LDL-C concentrations [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 30% of an oral dose is absorbed; bioavailability is <5% due to extensive first-pass hepatic extraction. 
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4; metabolized into its active β-hydroxyacid form.
    - **Elimination:** 83% excreted in feces (primarily via bile); 10% in urine [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Maximum therapeutic response is typically achieved within 4 to 6 weeks [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **EXCEL Study:** A 48-week trial of 8,245 patients showing dose-related reductions in LDL-C (up to 40% at 40 mg BID) [US].
- **AFCAPS/TexCAPS:** A primary prevention study in 6,605 participants demonstrating a 37% risk reduction in major coronary events over a median of 5.1 years [US].
- **CCAIT/MARS:** Angiographic trials showing lovastatin significantly slowed the progression of coronary atherosclerosis [US].
- **ACAPS:** Demonstrated regression of carotid intimal-medial thickness (IMT) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light and store in a well-closed, light-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as **Rx-only** [US].
