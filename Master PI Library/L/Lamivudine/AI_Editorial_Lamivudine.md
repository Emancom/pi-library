# Editorial Product Information: Lamivudine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lamivudine (INN); a nucleoside analogue reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI).
- **Brand Name(s):** **3TC** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AF05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Film-coated Tablets:** 150 mg (scored or unscored) and 300 mg [Australian].
    - **Oral Solution:** 10 mg/mL (strawberry/banana flavoured) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ViiV Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated for the treatment of HIV-infected adults and children in combination with other antiretroviral agents [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Adults, Adolescents, and Children ($\ge$ 25 kg):**
    - 300 mg daily, administered either as 150 mg twice daily or 300 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Paediatric Dosing (3 months to < 25 kg):**
    - **Oral Solution:** 5 mg/kg twice daily or 10 mg/kg once daily [Australian].
    - **Tablets (Weight-band based):**
        - 14 kg to < 20 kg: 150 mg total daily dose (75 mg twice daily or 150 mg once daily).
        - 20 kg to < 25 kg: 225 mg total daily dose (75 mg AM/150 mg PM, or 225 mg once daily) [Australian].
- **Infants (< 3 months):** Data are insufficient to propose specific dosage recommendations [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food. While food reduces peak plasma concentration ($C_{max}$) and delays time to peak ($T_{max}$), the total absorption (AUC) remains unaffected [Australian].
- Tablets should ideally be swallowed whole. For patients unable to swallow, tablets may be crushed and added to a small amount of semi-solid food or liquid, to be consumed immediately [Australian].
- Patients switching between oral solution and tablets should follow formulation-specific dosing due to differences in bioavailability.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to lamivudine or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Monotherapy:** Lamivudine is not recommended for use as monotherapy [Australian].
- **Lactic Acidosis and Severe Hepatomegaly with Steatosis:** Reported with nucleoside analogues, including fatal cases. Caution is advised in patients with known risk factors for liver disease (particularly in women) [Australian].
- **Hepatitis B (HBV) Co-infection:** Discontinuation of lamivudine in patients co-infected with HIV and HBV may lead to severe acute exacerbations of hepatitis. Monitor liver function and HBV replication closely if lamivudine is discontinued [Australian].
- **Pancreatitis:** Reported in patients receiving lamivudine, particularly children with prior NRTI exposure. Treatment should be suspended immediately if clinical signs or laboratory abnormalities (e.g., elevated amylase) suggestive of pancreatitis occur [Australian].
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome:** Inflammatory reactions to latent or opportunistic infections may occur during the initial phase of treatment [Australian].
- **Sorbitol Interaction:** Co-administration of sorbitol-containing medicines with lamivudine oral solution may decrease lamivudine exposure. Avoid chronic co-administration where possible [Australian].
- **Diabetes:** The oral solution contains 3 g of sucrose per adult dose [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trial data:
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache, nausea, malaise, fatigue, diarrhoea, abdominal pain/discomfort [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Vomiting, fever, insomnia, cough, nasal symptoms, rash, alopecia, musculoskeletal pain, arthralgia, and transient elevations in liver enzymes (AST, ALT) or serum amylase [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Neutropenia, anaemia, and thrombocytopenia [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Pancreatitis, rhabdomyolysis, and lactic acidosis [Australian].
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** Pure red cell aplasia, paraesthesia, and peripheral neuropathy [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Trimethoprim/Sulfamethoxazole:** Increases lamivudine exposure (AUC) by approximately 40%. No dose adjustment is required unless the patient has renal impairment [Australian].
- **Sorbitol:** Chronic co-administration of sorbitol solution (e.g., in other liquid medicines) reduces lamivudine AUC and $C_{max}$ in a dose-dependent manner [Australian].
- **Zalcitabine:** Lamivudine may inhibit the intracellular phosphorylation of zalcitabine; concurrent use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Emtricitabine:** Due to similarities in resistance patterns (M184V mutation) and intracellular phosphorylation pathways, combination use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Ciprofloxacin/Ganciclovir:** In vitro data suggest reduced anti-HIV activity; clinical significance is unknown [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy [Australian Category B3]:** Lamivudine crosses the placenta. Data from the Antiretroviral Pregnancy Registry (over 11,000 exposures) do not show an increased risk of major birth defects compared to the background rate. Use should be considered only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** HIV-infected women are advised not to breastfeed to avoid postnatal transmission of HIV. Lamivudine is excreted in human milk at concentrations similar to maternal serum [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no evidence of impaired fertility at exposures up to 70 times the clinical dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Higher doses (8 mg/kg/day) were historically required to match adult exposure. Current weight-band dosing for tablets is based on pharmacokinetic modelling. Tablets are preferred over the oral solution due to higher bioavailability and lower risk of resistance development [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No specific data available; caution is advised due to the increased frequency of decreased renal function in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose reduction is required for patients with $Cl_{cr} < 50$ mL/min.
    - **Adults ($\ge$ 25 kg):**
        - $Cl_{cr}$ 30–49 mL/min: 150 mg once daily.
        - $Cl_{cr}$ 15–29 mL/min: 150 mg first dose, then 100 mg once daily.
        - $Cl_{cr}$ 5–14 mL/min: 150 mg first dose, then 50 mg once daily.
        - $Cl_{cr} < 5$ mL/min: 50 mg first dose, then 25 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is necessary in moderate to severe hepatic dysfunction unless accompanied by renal impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lamivudine is phosphorylated intracellularly to its active metabolite, lamivudine 5'-triphosphate. This metabolite acts as a substrate for and a competitive inhibitor of HIV reverse transcriptase. Incorporation into the viral DNA chain results in chain termination [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; absolute bioavailability is 80–85% in adults and 58–66% in children [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Mean volume of distribution is 1.3 L/kg. Penetration into the cerebrospinal fluid is limited (CSF/serum ratio ~0.12) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal hepatic metabolism (<10%) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Predominantly excreted unchanged in the urine via active tubular secretion. Plasma half-life is 18–19 hours; intracellular half-life of the triphosphate is 16–19 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CAESAR Study:** Demonstrated that adding lamivudine to zidovudine-containing regimens significantly reduced the risk of HIV progression (57% reduction) and death (60% reduction) [Australian].
- **EPV 20001:** Established the non-inferiority of 300 mg once-daily dosing compared to 150 mg twice-daily dosing in treatment-naive adults [Australian].
- **ARROW Trial:** Confirmed that once-daily dosing in paediatric patients is non-inferior to twice-daily dosing, though tablet formulations provided better virological suppression than oral solutions [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Oral Solution:** Store below 25°C. Discard one month after first opening [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
