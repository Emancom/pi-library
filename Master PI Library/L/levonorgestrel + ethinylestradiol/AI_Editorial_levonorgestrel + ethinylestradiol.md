## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- One tablet taken once daily, at approximately the same time each day, for 28 consecutive days; subsequent packs are started the day after the previous pack is completed.
- Each pack contains active tablets followed by a phase of placebo tablets (placebo tablets are taken during the hormone‑free interval).

### Initiation and switching
- No preceding hormonal contraceptive use in prior month: start tablet-taking on Day 1 of the natural menstrual cycle (first day of menstrual bleeding). Use additional non‑hormonal contraception for the first 14 days of tablet-taking.
- Switching from another combined hormonal contraceptive (oral or vaginal ring): start on the day after the last active tablet of the previous combined oral contraceptive, or on the day of ring removal.
- Switching from progestogen‑only methods:
  - Minipill: may switch on any day.
  - Implant or levonorgestrel‑releasing IUS: start on day of removal.
  - Injectable: start when the next injection is due.
  - In these switches, advise additional barrier contraception for the first 14 days of tablet-taking.
- Following first‑trimester abortion: may start immediately; additional non‑hormonal contraception required for 14 days.
- Following delivery or second‑trimester abortion: advised to start 21–28 days after delivery or second‑trimester abortion; additional non‑hormonal contraception for 14 days. If intercourse has occurred since delivery/abortion, exclude pregnancy before starting or wait until first menses.

### How to delay or shift bleeding
- To delay a withdrawal bleed: continue active tablets from the next pack immediately instead of starting placebo tablets; can extend for as long as needed (may cause breakthrough bleeding/spotting). Resume normal pack sequence after the extension.
- To shift cycle day: shorten the placebo interval by the desired number of days (shorter placebo increases likelihood of absent withdrawal bleed and/or breakthrough bleeding).

### Missed active tablets (guidance)
- Placebo/tablets during hormone‑free interval: these may be discarded if needed (do not prolong placebo interval unintentionally).
- For active tablet doses, management depends on time missed and week of cycle:
  - If less than 12 hours late: take missed active tablet as soon as remembered; contraceptive protection maintained.
  - If more than 12 hours late: contraceptive protection may be reduced. Two basic rules apply:
    1. Active tablet‑taking must not be interrupted for longer than 7 consecutive days.
    2. Seven consecutive days of uninterrupted active tablet‑taking are necessary to restore adequate suppression of ovulation.
  - Practical guidance:
    - Week 1 (first week of active tablets): If >12 hours late for any active tablet, take the last missed active tablet as soon as remembered (may mean taking two tablets in one day). Continue usual schedule and use additional barrier contraception for 7 days. If intercourse occurred in the preceding 7 days, consider possibility of pregnancy.
    - Week 2: Take the last missed active tablet as soon as remembered (may take two in one day) and continue usual schedule. If the woman had taken tablets correctly in the 7 days before the first missed tablet, extra precautions are not required; if not, or if >1 active tablet missed, use extra precautions for 7 days.
    - Week 3: Because the hormone‑free interval is imminent, follow one of two options — provided the woman had taken tablets correctly for the 7 days preceding the first missed tablet:
      1. Take the last missed active tablet as soon as remembered (may take two in one day), continue active tablets at usual times until active tablets in the pack are finished, then discard the placebo tablets and start the next pack immediately (withdrawal bleed unlikely until end of active tablets in next pack; breakthrough bleeding/spotting possible).
      2. Alternatively, stop taking tablets from the current pack, have a tablet‑free interval of up to 7 days (including days missed), then start the next pack.
      - If the woman missed tablets and has no withdrawal bleed in the subsequent hormone‑free interval, consider pregnancy.
- Vomiting/diarrhoea: If severe gastrointestinal disturbance occurs, absorption may be incomplete. If vomiting occurs within 3–4 hours after taking an active tablet, follow the missed‑tablet recommendations above. If the woman does not wish to change her usual schedule after vomiting, she should take additional tablet(s) from a different pack as needed.

## 5. Contraindications
(Include all contraindications listed in the available source. Where a contraindication is jurisdiction‑specific it is labelled.)
- Presence or risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE):
  - Current VTE (patient on anticoagulants) or history of deep venous thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE).
  - Known hereditary or acquired predisposition for VTE (e.g. APC‑resistance including Factor V Leiden, antithrombin‑III deficiency, protein C deficiency, protein S deficiency).
  - Major surgery with prolonged immobilisation.
  - High risk of VTE due to the presence of multiple risk factors.
- Presence or risk of arterial thromboembolism (ATE):
  - Current ATE or history of ATE (e.g. myocardial infarction or stroke) or prodromal condition (e.g. angina, transient ischaemic attack).
  - Known hereditary or acquired predisposition for ATE (e.g. hyperhomocysteinaemia, antiphospholipid antibodies).
  - History of migraine with focal neurological symptoms.
  - High risk of ATE due to multiple risk factors or one serious risk factor (e.g. diabetes mellitus with vascular complications, severe hypertension, severe dyslipoproteinaemia).
- Pancreatitis, or a history of pancreatitis if associated with severe hypertriglyceridaemia.
- Severe hepatic disease or history of severe hepatic disease while liver function tests have not returned to normal.
- Concomitant use with specified direct‑acting antiviral agents for hepatitis C: glecaprevir, pibrentasvir, sofosbuvir, velpatasvir, voxilaprevir, ombitasvir, paritaprevir or dasabuvir, and combinations containing these agents — concomitant use is contraindicated. [Australian]
- Presence or history of liver tumours (benign or malignant).
- Known or suspected sex‑steroid‑influenced malignancy (e.g. genital organs or breasts).
- Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding.
- Known or suspected pregnancy.
- Hypersensitivity to any ingredient of the product.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Thromboembolic risk:
  - Epidemiological data associate combined hormonal contraceptive (CHC) use with increased risk of both venous and arterial thrombotic/thromboembolic events (e.g. DVT, PE, myocardial infarction, stroke). These events are rare in women without additional risk factors.
  - The risk of VTE with CHC use is highest during the first year of first‑time use and may be increased when restarting after a break of ≥4 weeks.
  - The risk of VTE in pregnancy and the postpartum period is higher than the VTE risk associated with CHC use: pregnancy estimated ~5–20 per 10,000 women over 9 months; postpartum period ~45–65 per 10,000 women over 12 weeks.
  - CHCs containing levonorgestrel (as in this product) are associated with a lower VTE risk relative to some other progestogens.
  - Provided population risk estimates (annual risk of developing VTE):
    - Women not using CHC and not pregnant: ~2 per 10,000 per year.
    - Women using a CHC containing levonorgestrel, norethisterone or norgestimate: ~5–7 per 10,000 per year.
    - Women using CHC containing etonogestrel or norelgestromin: ~6–12 per 10,000 per year.
    - Women using CHC containing drospirenone, gestodene, desogestrel or cyproterone: ~9–12 per 10,000 per year.
    - For some progestogens (chlormadinone, dienogest, nomegestrol) risk estimates are not yet established.
  - VTE associated with CHC use can be life‑threatening and may be fatal (reported case fatality ~1–2%).
  - Rarely, thromboses have been reported in other vascular beds (hepatic, mesenteric, renal, cerebral, retinal).
- Risk factors for VTE and ATE:
  - VTE risk factors include: obesity (BMI >30 kg/m²), prolonged immobilisation or major surgery, temporary immobilisation (e.g. long‑haul air travel >4 hours particularly if other risk factors present), positive family history of VTE (sibling or parent, especially if <50 years), hereditary thrombophilia markers (APC resistance/Factor V Leiden, antithrombin‑III deficiency, protein C/S deficiency), cancer, systemic lupus erythematosus, haemolytic uraemic syndrome, chronic inflammatory bowel disease, sickle cell disease, increasing age (especially >35 years), and smoking.
  - ATE risk factors include: age >35 years, smoking, hypertension, obesity, positive family history of premature arterial thromboembolism, hyperhomocysteinaemia, antiphospholipid antibodies, migraine (particularly with focal neurological symptoms), diabetes mellitus with vascular complications, valvular heart disease, atrial fibrillation, dyslipoproteinaemia, and systemic lupus erythematosus.
  - If multiple risk factors are present, the absolute risk may rise substantially; CHC use is contraindicated when the overall risk is judged to be high.
- Advice prior to elective surgery:
  - In women at risk of prolonged immobilisation (including major surgery), consider stopping this CHC at least four weeks before elective surgery and not restarting until two weeks after complete remobilisation; use alternative contraception to avoid unintended pregnancy. Consider antithrombotic prophylaxis if the product has not been discontinued in advance.
- Referral for suspected hereditary predisposition:
  - If hereditary thrombophilia is suspected, refer the woman to a specialist for evaluation before CHC use.
- Migraine:
  - An increase in frequency or severity of migraine during CHC use — particularly development of focal neurological signs — may require immediate discontinuation because it may be prodromal for cerebrovascular events.
- Tumours and cancer risk:
  - Persistent infection with human papillomavirus (HPV) is the main risk factor for cervical cancer; some epidemiological studies suggest long‑term CHC use may modestly increase that risk, although confounding by screening and sexual behaviour cannot be excluded.
  - A pooled meta‑analysis noted a modestly increased relative risk for breast cancer associated with CHC use (reported pooled RR ≈ 1.24). Further details and follow‑up data are not specified in available sources.
- Patient information and urgent action:
  - Inform women about the signs and symptoms of VTE and ATE and advise them to seek immediate medical attention if symptoms develop and to inform the clinician they are taking a CHC.
  - Symptoms of DVT may include unilateral leg swelling, pain/tenderness of the leg, increased warmth, redness or discolouration. Symptoms of PE may include sudden unexplained shortness of breath, rapid breathing, sudden cough (possibly with haemoptysis), sharp chest pain, severe light‑headedness, or rapid/irregular heartbeat.
  - Symptoms of stroke/TIA may include sudden numbness or weakness (especially on one side), sudden difficulty walking, dizziness, loss of balance or coordination, sudden confusion, slurred speech, sudden visual loss or diplopia, sudden severe headache, or loss of consciousness.
  - Symptoms of myocardial infarction may include chest pain/pressure or discomfort radiating to arm/back/jaw, sweating, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, extreme weakness, or irregular heartbeat.
- Other precautions:
  - If any of the conditions listed as contraindications appear for the first time during treatment, the product should be stopped and the woman evaluated.

### Monitoring requirements
- No routine laboratory monitoring is specified in the available sources.
- Clinical monitoring: assess for signs/symptoms of thromboembolism, hypertension, severe hepatic dysfunction, new onset migraine with focal signs, and any breast or genital malignancy indicators as clinically indicated.
- If hepatic enzymes are abnormal or severe hepatic disease develops, discontinue until values normalize.

### Specific clinical precautions
- In case of severe gastrointestinal disturbance and if vomiting occurs within 3–4 hours after tablet intake, consider the tablet as missed and advise according to missed‑tablet guidance.
- Rhythm (temperature/cervical mucus) methods are unreliable while using CHCs and should not be used as sole additional contraception.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Contraindicated combinations (may reduce safety or efficacy)
- Concomitant use with the following direct‑acting antiviral agents for hepatitis C is contraindicated: glecaprevir, pibrentasvir, sofosbuvir, velpatasvir, voxilaprevir, ombitasvir, paritaprevir, dasabuvir, and combinations containing these agents. [Australian]

### Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions
- Impaired absorption (e.g. severe vomiting, severe diarrhoea) may reduce contraceptive efficacy; if vomiting occurs within 3–4 hours of taking an active tablet treat as a missed dose (see Section 4).
- Other specific interacting medicinal products (e.g. hepatic enzyme inducers, interactions affecting steroid hormone metabolism or anticoagulants) are referenced in the source PI but comprehensive lists and management recommendations are not present in the available excerpt. Therefore, further interaction detail is: Not specified in available sources.

### Clinically significant interactions — management notes
- For the listed contraindicated hepatitis C agents: concomitant use is contraindicated; alternative contraceptive method should be used while receiving these antivirals.
- For gastrointestinal losses (vomiting/diarrhoea): follow missed‑tablet guidance and consider additional barrier contraception.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Known or suspected pregnancy is a contraindication to use.
- Use after abortion or delivery:
  - Following first‑trimester abortion: may start immediately; use additional contraception for 14 days.
  - Following delivery or second‑trimester abortion: recommended to start 21–28 days postpartum; use additional contraception for 14 days. If intercourse has occurred since delivery, exclude pregnancy before starting or delay until first menses.
