# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   The following jurisdictions’ Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available data into a single clinical reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official PI or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [United States].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [United States].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
*   **Carnitine Deficiency in ESRD:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
*   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
*   **Loading Dose:** In patients with severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses over the subsequent 24 hours.
*   **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours.
*   **Titration:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required based on clinical response. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
*   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
*   **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
*   **Monitoring and Adjustment:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels <40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels.

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when diluted in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL.

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if severe reactions occur.
*   **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.
*   **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD or severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]).
*   **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to treatment and monitor weekly or monthly. Monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target: 35–60 µmol/L).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
*   Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation.
*   **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, yeast) were negative. No long-term animal studies have evaluated carcinogenic potential.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders; 10–20 mg/kg for ESRD); see Section 3 for specific instructions.
*   **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided in source document; generally, dose selection should be cautious, typically starting at the low end of the dosing range.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety and efficacy of the **oral** formulation in patients with renal impairment have not been established.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing the substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    *   **Excretion:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).
    *   **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normal plasma free carnitine levels are defined as 40–50 µmol/L; deficiency is defined as <20 µmol/L.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed that trough concentrations reached steady state (54–180 µmol/L) within one week. 
*   **Dose-Ranging Trials:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
*   **Clinical Outcomes:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) has not been definitively determined.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of an opened vial, as the formulation contains no preservatives.
