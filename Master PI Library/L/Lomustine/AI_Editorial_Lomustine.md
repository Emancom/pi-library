# Editorial Product Information: Lomustine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating available regulatory data into a single, clinically applicable reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lomustine (a nitrosourea derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Antitumour agent; alkylating cytotoxic agent.
- **Brand Name(s):** **CeeNU** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01AD02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 40 mg oral capsules (white/dark green, marked CPL 3031/40mg) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Lomustine is indicated for the treatment of the following conditions [Australian]:
- **Brain Tumours:** Palliative treatment of both primary and metastatic brain tumours, used either as a component of combination chemotherapy or as an adjunct to surgery and/or radiotherapy.
- **Hodgkin’s Disease:** Utilised as alternative secondary therapy in patients with disseminated disease that is refractory to established treatment regimens.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Standard Dose:** 130 mg/m² administered as a single oral dose every 6 weeks [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustment for Bone Marrow Compromise:** In patients with pre-existing compromised bone marrow function, the initial dose should be reduced to 100 mg/m² every 6 weeks [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Fasting:** Capsules should be taken on an empty stomach to reduce the incidence of nausea and vomiting [Australian].
- **Dose Integrity:** Due to the 6-week cycle, it is critical to ensure the dose is not vomited. Patients should be informed that capsules may come in different colours/types to achieve the total prescribed dose [Australian].

### Titration and Cycle Management
Subsequent doses must be adjusted based on the haematologic response (nadir) of the preceding dose. A new course should not be initiated until blood elements return to acceptable levels (Platelets >100,000/mm³; Leucocytes >4,000/mm³). This recovery typically requires at least 6 weeks [Australian].

**Haematologic Dosage Adjustment Guide [Australian]:**
| Nadir After Prior Dose: Leucocytes (/mm³) | Nadir After Prior Dose: Platelets (/mm³) | Percentage of Next Dose to be Given |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ≥ 4,000 | ≥ 100,000 | 100% |
| 3,000 – 3,999 | 75,000 – 99,999 | 100% |
| 2,000 – 2,999 | 25,000 – 74,999 | 70% |
| < 2,000 | < 25,000 | 50% |

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to lomustine or any components of the formulation [Australian].
- **Severe Myelosuppression:** Contraindicated in patients with marked, pre-existing bone marrow suppression [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Delayed Haematological Toxicity:** The primary dose-limiting toxicity is delayed myelosuppression. Thrombocytopenia occurs approximately 4 weeks post-dose, and leucopenia occurs at approximately 6 weeks. Both may persist for 1–2 weeks. Toxicity is cumulative [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Fibrosis:** Dose-related pulmonary toxicity (infiltrates/fibrosis) can occur, sometimes months or years after treatment. Risk increases significantly at cumulative doses >1100 mg/m², though cases have been reported at 600 mg/m² [Australian].
- **Secondary Malignancies:** Long-term use of nitrosoureas is associated with an increased risk of secondary malignancies and bone marrow dysplasias [Australian].
- **Hepatic and Renal Impairment:** Animal studies suggest potential for delayed liver damage and renal toxicity (elevated BUN, decreased kidney size). Regular monitoring of LFTs and renal function is required [Australian].
- **Live Vaccines:** Concomitant use with live virus vaccines is contraindicated as it may lead to fatal systemic vaccine disease due to immunosuppression [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea and vomiting (45–100%), typically occurring 45 minutes to 6 hours post-ingestion and lasting up to 24 hours. Anorexia may persist for several days [Australian].
    - **Haematological:** Delayed leucopenia (65% of patients <5,000/mm³; 36% <3,000/mm³) and thrombocytopenia (generally more severe than leucopenia) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - **Hepatic:** Transient elevations in transaminases, alkaline phosphatase, and bilirubin [Australian].
- **Uncommon/Rare (<1%):**
    - **Neurological:** Disorientation, lethargy, ataxia, and dysarthria.
    - **Dermatological:** Alopecia.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Stomatitis.
    - **Renal:** Progressive azotaemia and renal failure (primarily with large cumulative doses) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Live Vaccines:** Increased risk of fatal systemic infection; avoid administration during treatment [Australian].
- **Myelosuppressive Agents:** Concurrent use requires additional dosage adjustments due to additive bone marrow suppression [Australian].
- **Food:** Ingestion with food may increase nausea; empty stomach administration is recommended [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Lomustine is carcinogenic, mutagenic, and teratogenic in animal studies. It should not be used in pregnant women unless the benefit clearly outweighs the risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Metabolites are excreted in breast milk and may be harmful. Breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May cause irreversible infertility. Men are advised not to father children during treatment and for 6 months thereafter. Sperm conservation should be discussed prior to therapy [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No specific paediatric data provided, though delayed pulmonary fibrosis has been noted in children treated for intracranial tumours [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific recommendations, but cautious dosing is advised as renal excretion of metabolites is the primary clearance route, and elderly patients may have reduced renal function [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Severe renal insufficiency may increase toxicity due to reduced excretion of metabolites. Regular monitoring is essential during therapy [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lomustine is a cell-cycle non-specific alkylating agent. It acts through the alkylation of nucleic acids and carbamoylation of proteins, resulting in the inhibition of DNA and RNA synthesis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma levels of metabolites occur between 1 and 4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Highly lipid-soluble; crosses the blood-brain barrier effectively. CSF levels are approximately 30% of plasma levels.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly degraded (no intact drug detectable in plasma); undergoes extensive metabolism into active moieties.
    - **Elimination:** Predominantly renal (60% recovered in urine within 48 hours) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Historical screening by the National Cancer Institute (US) in the 1950s and 1960s established the efficacy of the nitrosourea class against CNS-located tumours (e.g., L1210 leukaemia) due to their lipid solubility [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** As a cytotoxic agent, specialised procedures for handling and disposal must be followed. Only the exact number of capsules for a single dose should be dispensed [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
