# Editorial Product Information: Levodopa and Carbidopa

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Levodopa (an aromatic amino acid) and Carbidopa monohydrate (an inhibitor of aromatic amino acid decarboxylase).
- **Chemical Class:** Dopaminergic agent and decarboxylase inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** **APO-LEVODOPA/CARBIDOPA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Uncoated tablets (4:1 ratio): Levodopa 100 mg / Carbidopa 25 mg (expressed as anhydrous) [Australian].
    - Uncoated tablets (10:1 ratio): Levodopa 250 mg / Carbidopa 25 mg (expressed as anhydrous) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Parkinson’s Disease and Syndrome:** Indicated for the management of symptoms including rigidity, bradykinesia, tremor, dysphagia, sialorrhoea, and postural instability [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Titration:** Dosage must be individualised and determined by careful titration for each patient.
- **Carbidopa Requirement:** Optimal inhibition of extracerebral decarboxylation typically requires 70 mg to 100 mg of carbidopa per day. Doses below this range increase the risk of nausea and vomiting.
- **Initial Dosage:** For patients not currently on levodopa, therapy is ideally initiated with one 100/25 mg tablet three times daily. This may be increased by one tablet daily or every other day until a maximum of eight 100/25 mg tablets daily is reached.

### Transferring from Levodopa Monotherapy
- Levodopa monotherapy must be discontinued at least 12 hours before starting combination therapy (24 hours for slow-release formulations).
- The starting dose should provide approximately 20% of the previous daily levodopa dose.

### Maintenance and Maximum Dose
- If higher levodopa doses are required, the 250/25 mg tablet should be substituted.
- **Maximum Recommended Dose:** Eight tablets of 250/25 mg per day (equivalent to 2 g levodopa and 200 mg carbidopa) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- If therapy is interrupted, the usual daily dose may be resumed once the patient can take oral medication.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concomitant use with non-selective MAOIs is contraindicated. These must be stopped at least two weeks before starting levodopa/carbidopa. (Selective MAO-B inhibitors like selegiline may be used concomitantly at recommended doses).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to any component of the formulation.
- **Narrow-Angle Glaucoma:** Contraindicated due to risk of increased intraocular pressure.
- **Melanoma:** Because levodopa may activate malignant melanoma, it is contraindicated in patients with suspicious, undiagnosed skin lesions or a history of melanoma [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Abrupt withdrawal or dose reduction of dopaminergic agents can trigger a symptom complex resembling NMS (rigidity, hyperpyrexia, mental changes, elevated CPK).
- **Dyskinesias:** Combination therapy may induce dyskinesias more rapidly than levodopa alone due to increased central dopamine levels; dose reduction may be required.
- **Somnolence and Sudden Sleep Onset:** Levodopa is associated with somnolence and very rare episodes of sudden sleep onset without warning. Patients must be cautioned regarding driving or operating machinery.
- **Mental Health:** Monitor all patients for depression, suicidal tendencies, or antisocial behaviour. Use with caution in patients with a history of psychosis.
- **Impulse Control Disorders:** Patients should be monitored for pathological gambling, hypersexuality, increased libido, and compulsive spending or eating.
- **Cardiovascular/Respiratory Disease:** Use with caution in patients with severe cardiovascular or pulmonary disease, bronchial asthma, or endocrine disorders.
- **Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Caution is required in patients with a history of atrial, nodal, or ventricular arrhythmias; cardiac function should be monitored during initial titration.
- **Glaucoma:** Patients with chronic wide-angle glaucoma may be treated if intraocular pressure is well-controlled and monitored.
- **Melanoma Monitoring:** Patients with Parkinson's disease have an increased risk of melanoma. Frequent skin examinations by qualified professionals (e.g., dermatologists) are recommended [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Dyskinesias (including choreiform and dystonic movements).
- **Common (1–10%):** Nausea, hallucinations, confusion, dizziness, and dream abnormalities.
- **Other Reported Effects:**
    - **Nervous System:** Somnolence, sudden sleep onset, ataxia, numbness, increased hand tremor, muscle cramps, peripheral neuropathy.
    - **Psychiatric:** Depression, paranoid ideation, psychotic episodes, agitation, anxiety, increased libido.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Orthostatic hypotension, palpitations, chest pain, hypertension, phlebitis.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Vomiting, anorexia, GI bleeding, duodenal ulcer, dry mouth, constipation, dark saliva.
    - **Genitourinary:** Urinary retention, incontinence, dark urine, priapism, urinary tract infection.
    - **Serious/Rare:** Haemolytic anaemia (extremely rare), agranulocytosis, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, convulsions (causal relationship not established) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antihypertensive Agents:** Risk of symptomatic postural hypotension; dosage adjustment of antihypertensives may be necessary.
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants:** Rare reports of hypertension and dyskinesia when used concomitantly.
- **Iron:** Bioavailability of both carbidopa and levodopa is decreased when ingested with ferrous sulfate or ferrous gluconate.
- **Dopamine D2 Antagonists (e.g., Phenothiazines, Butyrophenones, Risperidone):** May reduce the therapeutic effect of levodopa.
- **Isoniazid, Phenytoin, and Papaverine:** May reverse the beneficial effects of levodopa.
- **Dopamine-Depleting Agents (e.g., Reserpine, Tetrabenazine):** Concomitant use is not recommended.
- **Dietary Protein:** Absorption of levodopa may be impaired in patients on a high-protein diet due to competition with certain amino acids.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Animal studies (rabbits) have shown visceral and skeletal malformations. Use in women of childbearing potential requires weighing benefits against potential hazards.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levodopa is excreted in human breast milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** No data available in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years have not been established; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical experience indicates no specific differences in response compared to younger adults, though titration should follow standard guidelines.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. Periodic evaluation of hepatic and renal function is recommended during extended therapy.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levodopa is the metabolic precursor of dopamine and crosses the blood-brain barrier to relieve Parkinsonian symptoms. Carbidopa is a peripheral decarboxylase inhibitor that does not cross the blood-brain barrier. It prevents the peripheral conversion of levodopa to dopamine, increasing the amount of levodopa available to the CNS and reducing peripheral side effects (like nausea).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Levodopa is rapidly absorbed from the GI tract. Peak plasma levels of radioactivity occur in 1.5 to 5 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Levodopa is extensively metabolised to dopamine, adrenaline, and noradrenaline. Carbidopa is metabolised to various acids but does not undergo conjugation.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 35% of carbidopa is excreted in the urine within seven hours. Levodopa metabolites are primarily excreted in the urine (80-90% as phenylcarboxylic acids).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Keep in a tightly closed container, protected from light and moisture.
- **Container Types:** Aluminium/Aluminium blister packs or HDPE bottles with child-resistant closures [Australian].
