## 3. Approved Indications
- Hypertension — may be used as monotherapy or in combination with other antihypertensive classes. Note: sufficient data not provided to support use in severe hypertension or renovascular hypertension.  
- Congestive heart failure — to be used in patients with heart failure; recommended administration together with a diuretic.  
- Acute myocardial infarction — for haemodynamically stable patients (not in cardiogenic shock and systolic BP >100 mmHg); may be initiated within 24 hours of onset of MI.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General
- Administer once daily. There is no clinically significant effect of food on absorption; tablets may be taken before, during or after meals.
- Titrate to blood pressure or clinical response with appropriate monitoring.

### Hypertension (adult)
- Usual recommended starting dose in uncomplicated hypertensive patients not on diuretics: 5–10 mg once daily.
- Usual maintenance dose: 10–20 mg once daily.
- Some patients may require doses up to 40 mg/day.
- If blood pressure is not controlled, consider adding a low‑dose diuretic (e.g. hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg provides additive effect). After adding a diuretic, lisinopril dose may be reduced as needed.
- Dosage adjustments should be made at 2–4 week intervals according to response.

### Patients receiving diuretics or who are salt/volume depleted
- If possible, discontinue diuretic therapy for 2–3 days prior to initiating lisinopril to reduce risk of symptomatic hypotension.
- If diuretics cannot be stopped, initial lisinopril dose should be 2.5 mg once daily, followed by 5 mg once daily thereafter; titrate according to blood pressure response.
- If required, diuretic therapy may be resumed gradually.

### Renal impairment (adult)
- Dosage should be adjusted according to creatinine clearance as indicated in the source material (table reproduced exactly as presented in available sources):

| Creatinine clearance (mL/min) | Starting dose (mg/day) |
|---:|:---|
| 30 – 70 | 10 – 30 |
| 10 – 30 | 5.0 – 10 |
| < 10 (including patients on dialysis) | 2.5 – 5 / 2.5 |

- Dosage and/or frequency should be adjusted depending on blood pressure response; titrate upward until control achieved or to a maximum of 20 mg daily (see regional notes below).

### Elderly
- Pharmacokinetic studies indicate that maximum blood levels and AUC may be approximately doubled in older patients; make dosage adjustments with particular caution and monitor clinical response.

### Congestive heart failure
- Initiate under close medical supervision.
- Patients not adequately controlled by diuretics (and digitalis where indicated): start lisinopril 2.5 mg once daily.
- Increase dose in increments of no greater than 10 mg at intervals of no less than 2 weeks.
- Usual dosing range: 5–20 mg once daily.
- Dose should not be titrated solely by symptoms; higher doses may not give additional symptomatic relief.
- The optimal upper dose is not fully determined; one reported comparison showed 35 mg/day more effective than 5 mg/day but evidence for intermediate doses was not provided.

### Acute myocardial infarction
- Treatment may be started within 24 hours of symptom onset in haemodynamically stable patients.
- Typical initiation/titration schedule: 5 mg orally as first dose, 5 mg after 24 hours, 10 mg after 48 hours, then 10 mg once daily thereafter.
- Patients with low systolic BP (≤120 mmHg) when treatment is started or during first 3 days after MI should receive a lower starting dose of 2.5 mg orally.
- If hypotension occurs (systolic BP ≤100 mmHg), a maintenance dose of 5 mg may be given with temporary reductions to 2.5 mg if necessary.
- If prolonged hypotension occurs (systolic BP <90 mmHg for >1 hour), discontinue lisinopril.
- Continue treatment for 6 weeks (clinical monitoring as indicated).
- Lisinopril is compatible with intravenous or transdermal glyceryl trinitrate.

### Administration/titration summary
- Single daily dosing; adjust at 2–4 week intervals for hypertension and at least every 2 weeks for heart failure titration.
- Specific renal dosing guidance reproduced above.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications reported in the available sources:
- Hypersensitivity to lisinopril dihydrate or any component of the product.
- History of hereditary and/or idiopathic angioedema.
- History of angioedema associated with previous treatment with an ACE inhibitor.
- Pregnancy. **Lisinopril is contraindicated in pregnancy.**
- Patients undergoing haemodialysis with polyacrylonitrile (AN69) high‑flux membranes (risk of anaphylactoid reactions when ACE inhibitors are used concurrently).
- During low‑density lipoprotein (LDL) apheresis with dextran sulphate within the framework of dialysis treatment (risk of severe hypersensitivity reactions).
- Concomitant use with aliskiren-containing medicines in patients with diabetes mellitus (type 1 or 2) or with moderate to severe renal impairment (GFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m²) — combination is contraindicated in these populations.
- Any other contraindication explicitly stated in local/regional product labelling should be followed (regional variations not specified in sources).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
- No boxed warning text is specified in the available sources. If a jurisdiction has a boxed warning it is not specified in the provided material.

### General warnings and precautions
- Angioedema: Severe, potentially life‑threatening angioedema (including laryngeal edema) has occurred. Angioedema may occur at any time during therapy, most commonly in the first week but also after long‑term use. Prompt discontinuation and monitoring are required if angioedema occurs; airway management and emergency treatment (including epinephrine and oxygen) may be necessary. Angioedema incidence is reported to be higher in Afro‑Caribbean patients. Patients with a history of angioedema not related to ACE inhibitors may be at increased risk.
- Anaphylactoid reactions: Anaphylactoid reactions reported when ACE inhibitors are used during hymenoptera venom desensitisation; consider temporary withholding of ACE inhibitor during desensitisation.
- Symptomatic hypotension: Can occur after initiation, particularly in volume‑depleted patients (diuretics, vomiting, diarrhea), in renovascular disease, severe CHF, or impaired renal function. Initiate therapy at lower doses and monitor closely in high‑risk patients; manage acute hypotension with supine positioning and IV normal saline if needed. Transient hypotension does not necessarily contraindicate further dosing.
- Hypotension in acute MI: Do not start in patients with systolic BP ≤100 mmHg or in cardiogenic shock. During first 3 days after MI reduce dose if systolic BP ≤120 mmHg. If hypotension persists (systolic BP <90 mmHg for >1 hour) withdraw.
- Dual RAAS blockade: Concomitant use of ACE inhibitors with ARBs or aliskiren increases risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia and renal dysfunction including acute renal failure. Dual blockade should generally be avoided; if considered necessary use only under specialist supervision with close monitoring. ACE inhibitors and ARBs should not be used together in patients with diabetic nephropathy.
- Neutropenia/agranulocytosis: Cases have been reported (often in patients with pre‑existing renal dysfunction or collagen vascular disease and/or concomitant immunosuppressants). Consider periodic hematologic monitoring in patients with diseases affecting bone marrow function or receiving myelotoxic therapy.
- Renal function impairment: Increases in BUN and serum creatinine may occur, particularly in patients with bilateral or unilateral renal artery stenosis, or in those on diuretics. ACE inhibitors may precipitate renal failure in patients dependent on angiotensin II‑mediated efferent arteriolar vasoconstriction. Avoid in known or suspected bilateral renal artery stenosis. If renal dysfunction develops in acute MI (serum creatinine >265 μmol/L or doubling from baseline) consider withdrawal. Do not initiate in MI patients with serum creatinine >177 μmol/L and/or proteinuria >500 mg/24 h.
- Combination with NSAIDs and thiazide diuretics: Concurrent use of an ACE inhibitor, NSAID (including COX‑2 inhibitors) and a thiazide diuretic increases risk of renal impairment. Use with caution and monitor renal function.
- Hyperkalaemia: ACE inhibition can lead to increased serum potassium (≥5.5 mEq/L), particularly in patients with renal impairment, on potassium‑sparing diuretics or supplements, or using potassium‑containing salt substitutes; diabetics (especially elderly) may be at higher risk. Monitor serum electrolytes periodically.
- Surgery/anaesthesia: Hypotension during anaesthesia may be more pronounced in patients receiving ACE inhibitors; volume expansion may be required.
- Cough: Dry, persistent, non‑productive cough may occur and is a recognized class effect of ACE inhibitors. Consider switching class if cough is severe.
- Dermatologic reactions: Pruritic maculopapular rashes, photosensitivity and rare severe skin reactions (e.g. lichenoid eruptions, psoriasis exacerbation, pemphigus‑like rash, Stevens‑Johnson syndrome) have been reported.
- Taste disturbance (dysgeusia): Reported with ACE inhibitor class; usually occurs within first weeks and may resolve despite continued treatment.
- Diabetes: Monitor glycaemic control closely during the first month in patients treated with oral antidiabetics or insulin.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic monitoring: blood pressure, renal function (serum creatinine, urea), serum electrolytes (including potassium and sodium), and where clinically indicated, full blood count.
- In patients at high risk of neutropenia or bone marrow suppression, consider periodic hematologic monitoring.
- Monitor for signs of angioedema; instruct patients to report any facial, oral or airway swelling immediately.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use with caution in patients with aortic stenosis or hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (vasodilator effects).
- Correct volume depletion or salt depletion prior to initiation where possible.
- Avoid co‑administration with aliskiren in patients with diabetes or significant renal impairment (GFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m²).
- Avoid use with polyacrylonitrile high‑flux dialysis membranes (AN69) and during LDL apheresis with dextran sulphate.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Diuretics: Concomitant diuretic therapy may increase risk of symptomatic hypotension on initiation. If possible, discontinue diuretic prior to initiation or start lisinopril at a reduced dose (see Dosage).
- Potassium‑sparing diuretics, potassium supplements, potassium‑containing salt substitutes: Increased risk of hyperkalaemia; monitor serum potassium and avoid combined use unless necessary and monitored.
- NSAIDs (including COX‑2 inhibitors) ± thiazide diuretics: Combination with an ACE inhibitor increases risk of renal impairment (including acute renal failure); monitor renal function closely when starting or using together, particularly in elderly or those with pre‑existing renal impairment.
- Dual RAAS blockade (ACE inhibitor + ARB or ACE inhibitor + aliskiren): Concomitant use increases risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia and decreased renal function. Concomitant use with aliskiren is contraindicated in patients with diabetes or with GFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m². Avoid combined use unless under specialist supervision with frequent monitoring.
- Anesthetic agents: Increased susceptibility to hypotension during surgery/anaesthesia in patients on ACE inhibitors; consider perioperative management (e.g., volume expansion) and temporary withholding per local guidelines.
- Hymenoptera venom desensitisation: ACE inhibitors associated with sustained anaphylactoid reactions during desensitisation procedures; temporary withholding may avoid reactions.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- No specific pharmacokinetic interactions with cytochrome P450 substrates or inhibitors are specified in the available sources.
- No specific absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion interactions with other commonly used drugs are detailed in the available sources beyond the pharmacodynamic interactions noted above.

### Other clinically significant interactions / considerations
- Hemodialysis with polyacrylonitrile (AN69) high‑flux membranes and LDL apheresis with dextran sulphate: use of ACE inhibitors in combination with these extracorporeal treatments has been associated with severe anaphylactoid reactions and should be avoided.
- Glyceryl trinitrate (IV or transdermal): compatible with lisinopril per available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Safety and effectiveness in children: Not established / Not specified in available sources. (Source states use in children has not been established.)

### Elderly
- Pharmacokinetic studies indicate increased exposure (Cmax and AUC approximately doubled); exercise caution with dosing and titrate carefully while monitoring blood pressure and renal function.

### Renal impairment
- See Dosage section for creatinine clearance table reproduced from source.
- Avoid in patients with known or suspected bilateral renal artery stenosis; use with caution in unilateral renal artery stenosis as deterioration may not be apparent from routine urea/creatinine measures.
- In patients with severe CHF whose renal function depends on RAAS activity, ACE inhibitor therapy may be associated with oliguria, progressive azotemia and sometimes acute renal failure and death; start at low doses under close supervision.
- In acute MI, do not initiate if serum creatinine >177 μmol/L and/or proteinuria >500 mg/24 h. If renal dysfunction develops during treatment (serum creatinine >265 μmol/L or a doubling from pre‑treatment), consider withdrawal.

### Hepatic impairment
- Use with particular caution in patients with pre‑existing liver abnormalities. Baseline liver function tests recommended and close monitoring advised. Specific dosing adjustments by severity are not specified in the available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- **Lisinopril dihydrate is an angiotensin‑converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor.** It inhibits ACE, reducing conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II; this reduces angiotensin II‑mediated vasoconstriction and aldosterone secretion, and can lead to decreased sodium and water retention and lowering of blood pressure. Reduced aldosterone may predispose to hyperkalaemia.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: No clinically significant effect of food on absorption; allows dosing without regard to meals.
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism: Not specified in available sources.
- Elimination: Not specified in available sources.
- Special populations: Older patients show increased exposure (Cmax and AUC approximately doubled). Renal impairment affects dosing needs; details reproduced in Dosage section.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Lowers systemic vascular resistance and blood pressure primarily through inhibition of angiotensin II formation. Effects on renal hemodynamics can reduce glomerular filtration pressure in patients dependent on efferent arteriolar tone (e.g., renal artery stenosis), potentially causing deterioration of renal function.
