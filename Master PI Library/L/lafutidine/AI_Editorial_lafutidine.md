# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. At the time of writing, only United States regulatory documentation was provided for review; information for other regions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) is not specified in the available source.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Dosing Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Monitoring and Adjustment:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough concentrations and may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue treatment immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While specific to chronic high-dose oral administration, clinicians should be aware that severely compromised renal function can lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% frequency in clinical trials) include:

| Body System | Adverse Events (≥5% Frequency) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, abdominal pain, asthenia |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, vascular disorder |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, dyspepsia, anorexia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, paresthesia, depression, insomnia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Urogenital** | Kidney failure, urinary tract infection |

**Other Noted Effects:**
- **Body Odor:** Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed; less frequent reactions include gastritis and a distinct body odor [US].
- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specifically lettered in provided source]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Although levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk, the risks of excess intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg weight (see Section 3) [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically detailed in the source, though general dosing principles apply.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine in renal failure has not been evaluated [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical data demonstrate that 20 mg/kg IV after dialysis increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations. In a 9-week study (n=12), mean trough concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough levels [US].
- **Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the impact on clinical outcomes in ESRD patients has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentration) [US].
