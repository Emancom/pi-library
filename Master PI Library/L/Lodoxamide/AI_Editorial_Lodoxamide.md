# Editorial Product Information: Lodoxamide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lodoxamide for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference point. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved documentation for definitive prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lodoxamide trometamol (a mast cell stabiliser).
- **Brand Names:** Lomide [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile eye drop solution containing 1.78 mg/mL lodoxamide trometamol (equivalent to 1.0 mg/mL lodoxamide) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Seasonal Allergic Conjunctivitis (SAC):** For the symptomatic treatment and prophylaxis in adults and children four years of age and older [Australian].
- **Vernal Keratoconjunctivitis (VKC):** For the treatment of signs and symptoms in adults and children four years of age and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Children $\ge$ 4 years):** Instil one drop into the affected eye(s) four times daily at regular intervals [Australian].
- **Seasonal Allergic Conjunctivitis:** 
    - **Prophylaxis:** For patients with regular seasonal symptoms, treatment should ideally commence approximately one week prior to the anticipated start of the allergy season [Australian].
    - **Duration:** Continue treatment throughout the duration of the allergy season [Australian].
- **Vernal Keratoconjunctivitis:** While symptomatic improvement is typically observed within a few days, treatment for up to four weeks may be required. Therapy can be maintained for up to three months if necessary to sustain improvement [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - If using other ophthalmic medications, wait at least 10 minutes between instillations [Australian].
    - Patients should be advised that the efficacy of the medication is dependent upon adherence to the regular dosing schedule [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to lodoxamide trometamol or any of the excipients (e.g., benzalkonium chloride) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Precautions:** Lodoxamide eye drops are strictly for topical ophthalmic use and are **not for injection** [Australian].
- **Contact Lenses:** 
    - The solution contains **benzalkonium chloride**, which may cause ocular irritation and is known to discolour soft contact lenses [Australian].
    - Due to the nature of vernal keratoconjunctivitis, patients are generally advised against wearing contact lenses during treatment.
    - If soft contact lenses are worn, they must be removed before instillation and not re-inserted for at least 15 minutes post-dosing [Australian].
- **Patient Monitoring:** If symptoms do not improve within a few days or if unmanageable symptoms occur, clinical reassessment is required [Australian].
- **Visual Impairment:** Temporary blurred vision may occur upon instillation. Patients should not drive or operate machinery until vision clears [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Lodoxamide is generally well-tolerated. Ocular side effects are typically mild and transient.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** ($\ge$ 10%) | Ocular discomfort (transient burning/stinging) [Australian] |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Blurred vision, ocular pruritus, dry eye, increased lacrimation, ocular hyperaemia [Australian] |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Eye pain, ocular/conjunctival oedema, foreign body sensation, eye discharge, eye irritation, dizziness, headache, nausea, eyelid exfoliation, drug intolerance [Australian] |
| **Rare** (< 0.1%) | Corneal erosion, corneal scarring, keratitis, blepharitis, visual impairment, drug hypersensitivity, somnolence, dysgeusia, rash, abdominal discomfort [Australian] |

**Post-Marketing Experience:** Palpitations have been identified as an adverse reaction; however, frequency cannot be estimated from available data [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No specific drug interaction studies have been conducted [Australian].
- **Concomitant Therapy:** During clinical trials, no interactions were observed when used concurrently with:
    - Ophthalmic or systemic corticosteroids.
    - Antibiotics (ciprofloxacin, gentamicin, sulfacetamide, tetracycline, tobramycin).
    - Other ophthalmic agents (naphazoline, antazoline, ketorolac, timolol, dipivefrine) [Australian].
- **Management Note:** A 10-minute interval between different ophthalmic products is recommended to prevent washout of the medication [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** Animal studies (rats and rabbits at 100 mg/kg/day) showed no evidence of developmental toxicity. Human data is limited. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if lodoxamide is excreted in human milk. A risk-benefit assessment should consider the importance of the drug to the mother and the potential risk to the breastfed infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment of reproductive function. Human data are not available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children under four years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No special precautions or dosage adjustments are required for the elderly [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments are not provided in the available sources; systemic absorption following topical administration is minimal [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lodoxamide is a mast cell stabiliser. It inhibits Type I immediate hypersensitivity reactions by preventing the release of histamine and other inflammatory mediators (including SRS-A/peptido-leukotrienes) from mast cells. It also inhibits eosinophil chemotaxis [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The drug prevents calcium influx into mast cells upon antigen stimulation. It possesses no intrinsic vasoconstrictor, antihistaminic, or cyclo-oxygenase inhibitory activity [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Systemic levels are typically undetectable or minute following topical application [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** In oral studies, the primary route of elimination is urinary (83%). The elimination half-life is approximately 8.5 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** In seasonal allergic conjunctivitis, lodoxamide demonstrated efficacy comparable to sodium cromoglycate (2% and 4%), with significant improvement often seen within 3–7 days [Australian].
- **VKC Superiority:** Lodoxamide was shown to be superior to both placebo and sodium cromoglycate in treating vernal keratoconjunctivitis, significantly reducing the requirement for adjunctive topical steroids in moderate-to-severe cases [Australian].
- **Long-term use:** Studies involving 12 months of treatment showed no evidence of tachyphylaxis or rebound exacerbation upon cessation [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Discard the bottle 28 days after the initial opening to prevent contamination [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:**
    - **Australian:** Schedule 2 (Pharmacy Only Medicine).
