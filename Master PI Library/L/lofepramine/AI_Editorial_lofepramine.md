# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance into a single, concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). It should be administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Acute Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and no less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance (Metabolic):** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Metabolic:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations <40–50 µmol/L. Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Not specified in available sources, though hypersensitivity is listed under warnings [US].
- **General:** None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These can occur within minutes of injection. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** In ESRD patients, the safety of chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine has not been established; however, intravenous administration is used to bypass GI metabolites (TMA/TMAO) that accumulate in renal impairment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), fever (12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8% common/12% at high dose).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (13%).

### Common (1–10%)
- **Digestive:** Abdominal pain, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia, melena.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes.
- **Nervous:** Anxiety, depression, insomnia, paresthesia.
- **Other:** Body odor, gastritis, anemia, leg cramps, pruritus, rhinitis, dyspnea.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and Severe Hypersensitivity:** Laryngeal edema and bronchospasm [US].
- **Neurologic:** New-onset or worsening seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    - **Management:** Monitor INR levels at treatment initiation and following any dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, milk concentrations increase with exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Oral levocarnitine is not recommended for long-term use in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO), but this is not a concern for the intravenous formulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Oral bioavailability is low, at approximately 15% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trough Concentrations:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses post-dialysis increased mean trough carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only.
