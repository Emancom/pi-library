# Editorial Product Information: Lacosamide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lacosamide product information, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. It integrates clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lacosamide (functionalised amino acid).
- **Brand Names:** LACOSACOR [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 50 mg (pink), 100 mg (dark yellow), 150 mg (peach), and 200 mg (blue) [Australian].
    - Note: Oral solution and injection formulations are available via other brands/sponsors [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral (tablets).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Lacosamide is indicated for the following uses:
- **Monotherapy:** Treatment of partial-onset seizures (with or without secondary generalisation) in patients with epilepsy aged 16 years and older [Australian].
- **Add-on Therapy:** 
    - Treatment of partial-onset seizures (with or without secondary generalisation) in patients with epilepsy aged 4 years and older [Australian].
    - Treatment of primary generalised tonic-clonic seizures (PGTCS) in patients with idiopathic generalised epilepsy aged 4 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food. Tablets must be swallowed whole and must not be divided [Australian].
- **Frequency:** Twice daily (BID).
- **Loading Dose (Adults/Adolescents $\ge$ 50 kg):** Treatment may be initiated with a single 200 mg loading dose, followed approximately 12 hours later by 100 mg BID. Loading doses should be administered under medical supervision due to increased CNS adverse risks [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication (Adults)
- **Monotherapy (Partial-onset seizures):**
    - Starting dose: 100 mg twice daily (200 mg/day).
    - Titration: Increase by 50 mg BID (100 mg/day) at weekly intervals based on response.
    - Maximum dose: 600 mg/day (300 mg BID) [Australian].
- **Add-on Therapy (Partial-onset or PGTCS):**
    - Starting dose: 50 mg twice daily (100 mg/day).
    - Initial therapeutic dose: Increase to 100 mg twice daily after one week.
    - Titration: Further increases of 50 mg BID weekly.
    - Maximum dose: 400 mg/day (200 mg BID) [Australian].

### Discontinuation
- Lacosamide should be withdrawn gradually. A tapering schedule of 200 mg/week is recommended [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to lacosamide or any excipients [Australian].
- **Cardiac Conduction:** Known second or third-degree atrioventricular (AV) block [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation:** Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behavior. Patients should be monitored for the emergence or worsening of depression or unusual changes in mood [Australian].
- **Dizziness and Ataxia:** Treatment is associated with dizziness and gait disturbance, which may increase the risk of accidental injury or falls [Australian].
- **Cardiac Rhythm and Conduction:**
    - **PR Interval Prolongation:** Lacosamide can cause dose-dependent PR interval prolongation. 
    - **Cardiac Conditions:** Use with caution in patients with underlying proarrhythmic conditions (e.g., myocardial ischemia, heart failure, structural heart disease) or those on PR-prolonging medications.
    - **Monitoring:** Obtaining an ECG before initiation and after titration to steady-state is recommended in high-risk patients [Australian].
- **Multiorgan Hypersensitivity (DRESS):** Rare but serious drug reactions with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms have been reported. Discontinue if suspected [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often dose-related and may be alleviated by dose reduction.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dizziness, headache, nausea, diplopia. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vertigo, blurred vision, vomiting, constipation, flatulence, fatigue, gait disturbance, asthenia, tremor, somnolence, nystagmus, depression, pruritus, nasopharyngitis [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Syncope, AV block (first-degree), liver function test abnormalities [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%) / Post-marketing** | Atrial fibrillation/flutter, ventricular tachyarrhythmia, asystole, SJS, TEN, agranulocytosis, DRESS [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **PR-Prolonging Medications:** Use with caution when co-administering with sodium channel blockers (e.g., carbamazepine, lamotrigine, pregabalin) or antiarrhythmics [Australian].
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Strong enzyme inducers (e.g., phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine) may decrease systemic exposure (AUC) of lacosamide by approximately 25% [Australian].
- **CYP Interactions:** Lacosamide has low interaction potential. *In vitro* data suggests potential inhibition of CYP2C19, though *in vivo* trials with omeprazole showed no clinically relevant effect [Australian].
- **Protein Binding:** Interaction via protein binding competition is unlikely (<15% bound) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B3. There are no adequate data in pregnant women. Animal studies showed no teratogenicity but noted increased stillborn pups at maternally toxic doses. Use only if clearly necessary [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Lacosamide is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug based on the benefit-risk profile [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data. No impairment of fertility was observed in rat studies at exposures 1–2 times the human clinical dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Not recommended for children < 4 years of age.
    - Not indicated for monotherapy in children.
    - **Weight-based dosing:** Tablets are not suitable for children < 50 kg; these patients require weight-based dosing (mg/kg) using other formulations [Australian].
- **Elderly ($\ge$ 65 years):** No dose reduction is generally necessary unless renal function is impaired [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild-to-Moderate ($Cl_{Cr} > 30$ mL/min):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe ($Cl_{Cr} \le 30$ mL/min) or ESRD:** Maximum dose of 250 mg/day (Adults). Paediatric patients should have a 25% reduction in their maximum dose.
    - **Haemodialysis:** A supplemental dose of up to 50% of the divided daily dose is recommended immediately after dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild-to-Moderate:** Maximum dose of 300 mg/day (Adults). Paediatric patients should have a 25% reduction in their maximum dose.
    - **Severe:** Use only if benefits outweigh risks; careful clinical monitoring is required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selectively enhances the slow inactivation of voltage-gated sodium channels, leading to the stabilisation of hyperexcitable neuronal membranes [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and complete; ~100% bioavailability. $C_{max}$ reached within 0.5 to 4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution ~0.6 L/kg; <15% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP2C19 (minor), 2C9, and 3A4 to an inactive O-desmethyl metabolite.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life ~13 hours. 95% excreted in urine (40% as unchanged drug) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy:** In a non-inferiority trial against carbamazepine CR, lacosamide (200–600 mg/day) demonstrated similar 6-month seizure freedom rates (89.5% vs 91.2%) [Australian].
- **Add-on Therapy:** Three pivotal trials (SP667, SP754, SP755) in adults showed significant reductions in seizure frequency at doses of 200 mg (in some trials), 400 mg, and 600 mg daily compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below $25^\circ C$ [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
