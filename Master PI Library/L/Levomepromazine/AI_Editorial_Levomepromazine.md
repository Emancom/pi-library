## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corp) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often given, followed by equivalent doses over the next 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported clinical dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Regular weekly and monthly monitoring is advised. Optimal free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication based on available US source documentation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These can occur within minutes of administration, primarily in patients with ESRD. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately and initiate appropriate medical care [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients both with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** While the IV safety is established for ESRD, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (regardless of causality) include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), chest pain (12%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), depression (5%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).

### Post-marketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema.
- **Other:** Reports of body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    - **Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine therapy or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but descriptive]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders); specific safety profiles in pediatric ESRD patients are generally consistent with adult data [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources; standard geriatric caution is generally advised due to potential decreases in renal function.
- **Renal Impairment:** IV administration is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Monitor trough levels to prevent excessive accumulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations, which are often depleted by dialysis and reduced meat intake.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study (ESRD):** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
