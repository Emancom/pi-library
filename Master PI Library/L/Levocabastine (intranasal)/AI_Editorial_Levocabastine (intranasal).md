# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for Levocarnitine injection. It is designed as a clinically accurate reference for healthcare professionals to compare and contrast regulatory requirements and clinical data.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is for informational purposes for healthcare professionals and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

*Note: While the provided context mentioned Levocabastine (intranasal) from Australia, the source material provided for analysis is exclusively for **Levocarnitine injection** (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) as approved by the US (FDA). This editorial reflects the content of the provided source.*

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Clear, colorless sterile solution for injection. 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Maintenance/Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h).
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to parenteral therapy. 
- Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** No specific contraindications are listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon a severe reaction [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (≥5%):** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, and pain.
- **Cardiovascular (≥5%):** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Diarrhea, dyspepsia, and vomiting (reported in up to 35% in some dialysis cohorts) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis.
- **Other:** Body odor and gastritis have been noted [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but text indicates "Category B" style findings]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine concentration in milk increases following exogenous administration in dairy cows. In humans, the risk to the infant of excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients; however, specific age-stratified data is limited in the provided source.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in the provided source.
- **Renal Impairment:** See "Dosage and Administration" for ESRD-specific protocols. Note that IV administration avoids the accumulation of metabolites associated with high-dose oral use in renal failure [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Carnitine deficiency is biochemically defined as plasma concentrations <20 µmol/L at one week post-term [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Elimination:** Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels rose from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Efficacy Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the effects on clinical outcomes or specific signs/symptoms of deficiency in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (US does not use the Australian SUSMP scheduling).
