# Editorial Product Information: Linaclotide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of linaclotide product information, synthesized for healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinical decisions should be based on the official PI approved in the practitioner's region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Linaclotide (a guanylate cyclase-C agonist).
- **Brand Name(s):** **LINZESS** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - **Oral Capsules:** 72 mcg, 145 mcg, and 290 mcg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AbbVie, Inc. and Ironwood Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Irritable Bowel Syndrome with Constipation (IBS-C):**
  - Adults [US].
  - Pediatric patients 7 years of age and older [US].
- **Chronic Idiopathic Constipation (CIC):**
  - Adults [US].
- **Functional Constipation (FC):**
  - Pediatric patients 2 years of age and older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer once daily on an empty stomach at least 30 minutes before the first meal of the day.
- Capsules must be swallowed whole; do not crush or chew.
- **Alternative Administration:** For patients with dysphagia or those requiring enteral feeding, capsules may be opened and the beads sprinkled on one teaspoon of room-temperature applesauce or mixed with 30 mL of room-temperature water. The mixture should be consumed immediately without chewing the beads [US].

### Adult Dosing
- **IBS-C:** 290 mcg once daily [US].
- **CIC:** 145 mcg once daily. A lower dose of 72 mcg daily may be utilized based on patient presentation or tolerability [US].

### Pediatric Dosing
- **IBS-C (Ages 7–17 years):** 145 mcg once daily [US].
- **FC (Ages 2–17 years):** 72 mcg once daily [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a dose is missed, skip the missed dose and resume at the next scheduled time. Do not double the dose to compensate for a missed one [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pediatric patients under 2 years of age:** Due to the risk of serious, potentially fatal dehydration [US].
- **Mechanical Gastrointestinal Obstruction:** Known or suspected [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Risk of Serious Dehydration in Pediatric Patients [US]
- **LINZESS** is strictly contraindicated in children less than 2 years of age. In neonatal mice, linaclotide administration caused deaths due to dehydration resulting from increased fluid secretion. 

### Diarrhea
- Diarrhea is the most common adverse effect. Severe diarrhea has been reported, occasionally leading to dizziness, syncope, hypotension, and electrolyte imbalances (hypokalemia, hyponatremia) requiring hospitalization.
- **Management:** If severe diarrhea occurs, suspend treatment and rehydrate the patient [US].

### Monitoring
- Clinicians should monitor for signs of dehydration and electrolyte disturbances, particularly in patients experiencing severe diarrhea.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Adult Frequency Data (IBS-C and CIC)
| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (LINZESS) | Frequency (Placebo) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Diarrhea** | 16% – 20% | 3% – 5% |
| **Abdominal Pain** | 7% | 5% – 6% |
| **Flatulence** | 4% – 6% | 2% – 5% |
| **Abdominal Distension** | 2% – 3% | 1% – 2% |

### Pediatric Frequency Data
- **Diarrhea:** Reported in 4% – 8% of pediatric patients across clinical trials [US].

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Hematochezia, nausea, rectal hemorrhage.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, and rash (including urticaria) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted. However, linaclotide and its active metabolite are minimally absorbed systemically, and in vitro studies show no interaction with the Cytochrome P450 system or common transporters (e.g., P-gp) [US].
- **Management:** Clinically significant interactions with systemic medications are not anticipated.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **US Category:** Not specified in available sources (current labeling follows PLLR format). 
- **Summary:** Maternal use is not expected to result in fetal exposure due to negligible systemic absorption. Animal studies at high doses showed no embryo-fetal developmental effects in rats or rabbits, though maternal toxicity was noted in mice [US].

### Breastfeeding
- Linaclotide and its active metabolite were not detected in the milk of lactating women during therapeutic dosing. Breastfeeding is generally considered compatible with maternal use, but the benefits should be weighed against the clinical need and potential infant effects [US].

### Fertility
- No effects on fertility or reproductive function were observed in animal studies at oral doses up to 100,000 mcg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric Use
- **Contraindicated:** Patients < 2 years of age.
- **Approved:** FC (ages 2+) and IBS-C (ages 7+). Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients younger than the approved age thresholds for these specific indications [US].

### Geriatric Use
- No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between elderly (≥ 65 years) and younger patients. However, dose selection should be cautious due to a higher frequency of co-morbidities and concomitant drug therapies [US].

### Renal and Hepatic Impairment
- No dose adjustments are required. Systemic clearance is not a significant factor as metabolism and elimination occur locally within the gastrointestinal tract [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- Linaclotide is a synthetic 14-amino acid peptide that acts as a guanylate cyclase-C (GC-C) agonist. It binds to GC-C on the luminal surface of the intestinal epithelium, increasing intracellular and extracellular cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP).
- **Effects:** Elevation of cGMP stimulates chloride and bicarbonate secretion into the lumen via the CFTR channel, increasing intestinal fluid and accelerating transit. Extracellular cGMP also reduces the activity of pain-sensing nerves, potentially decreasing visceral pain [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Negligible; plasma levels are generally below the limit of quantitation.
- **Distribution:** Limited to the gastrointestinal tract.
- **Metabolism:** Proteolytically degraded within the GI tract to an active metabolite (MM-419447) via loss of a tyrosine moiety.
- **Elimination:** Primarily excreted in the feces as the active metabolite [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **IBS-C (Adults):** Two 26-week trials showed significant improvement in the "Combined Responder" endpoint (reduction in abdominal pain and increase in CSBMs) vs. placebo.
- **CIC (Adults):** Trials using 145 mcg and 72 mcg doses demonstrated significant increases in CSBM frequency.
- **Pediatrics:** Efficacy in FC (2–17 years) and IBS-C (7–17 years) was supported by trials showing significant improvement in SBM frequency and abdominal pain where applicable [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F).
- **Handling:** Keep in the original container to protect from moisture. Do not remove the desiccant packet from the bottle. Keep the bottle tightly closed in a dry place [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only (Rx Only).
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified in available sources.
