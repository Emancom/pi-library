# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for **levocarnitine** injection. It is designed as a concise, clinically accurate reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This editorial summary is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local PI for definitive regulatory requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
  - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
  - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and no less frequently than every 6 hours).
  - Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
  - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
  - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenterals visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **ESRD:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) levels below 40 to 50 µmol/L. Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These may occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of seizure activity [US].
- **Oral vs. Intravenous Administration:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) which are normally excreted in the urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting. In dialysis trials: Hypertension (18–21%), Diarrhea (9–35%), Headache (3–37%), Pharyngitis (15–27%), and injection site reactions (27–38%) [US].
- **Other Reported Effects:** Body odor, gastritis, abdominal pain, fever, chest pain, and palpitations.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
  - Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity.
  - Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Note:** Monitor INR levels at treatment initiation and following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C logic applied]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Dosage and Administration; dosing is weight-based [US].
- **Elderly:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Avoid high-dose **oral** levocarnitine due to metabolite (TMA/TMAO) accumulation. Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria for oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
  - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine compounds that are rapidly excreted [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly after dialysis), mean trough levocarnitine levels rose from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations. Note: While carnitine levels increase, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].

- **Legal Classification:** 
  - **United States:** Rx-only.
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
