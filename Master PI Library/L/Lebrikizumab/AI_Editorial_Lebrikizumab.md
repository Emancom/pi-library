# Editorial Product Information: Lebrikizumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, intended to consolidate clinical and regulatory data into a single, accessible reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lebrikizumab (a recombinant humanised IgG4 monoclonal antibody).
- **Chemical Class:** Interleukin-13 (IL-13) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **EBGLYSS** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** D11AH08.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Solution for injection in a single-dose pre-filled pen (autoinjector) containing 250 mg of lebrikizumab in 2 mL (125 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Eli Lilly Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Atopic Dermatitis (AD):** Treatment of moderate to severe atopic dermatitis in adults and adolescent patients (12 years of age and older, weighing at least 40 kg) who are candidates for systemic therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents ≥40 kg)
- **Initial Loading Dose:** 500 mg (administered as two 250 mg injections) at Week 0 and Week 2.
- **Induction Phase:** 250 mg administered subcutaneously every two weeks (Q2W) until Week 16.
- **Maintenance Phase:** For patients achieving an adequate clinical response at Week 16, the recommended maintenance dose is 250 mg every four weeks (Q4W).

### Clinical Response Considerations
- **Non-responders:** If no clinical response is observed by Week 16, consideration should be given to discontinuing treatment.
- **Partial Responders:** For patients with an inadequate response at Week 16, Q2W dosing may be continued until an adequate response is achieved, after which they may transition to Q4W maintenance. Discontinuation should be considered if response remains inadequate after 24 weeks of Q2W dosing [Australian].
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Lebrikizumab may be used with or without topical corticosteroids (TCS) or topical calcineurin inhibitors (TCI).

### Administration Instructions
- **Injection Sites:** Abdomen, thigh, or the back of the upper arm. The back of the upper arm should only be injected by a caregiver or healthcare professional.
- **Technique:** Rotate injection sites. Do not inject into skin that is tender, bruised, erythematous, or affected by active dermatitis/lesions.
- **Training:** Patients and caregivers must receive appropriate training on subcutaneous injection technique before self-administration.

### Missed Dose
- Instruct patients to administer the missed dose as soon as possible. Dosing should then resume at the regularly scheduled time [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to lebrikizumab or any of the excipients (histidine, glacial acetic acid, sucrose, polysorbate 20) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Reports of angioedema and urticaria have occurred. If a serious reaction is observed, discontinue the medication immediately and initiate standard of care.
- **Conjunctivitis and Keratitis:** These reactions have been frequently reported. Patients should be advised to report new or worsening ocular symptoms. If conjunctivitis does not resolve with standard treatment, an ophthalmological examination is required [Australian].
- **Helminth Infections:** It is unknown if IL-13 inhibition affects the immune response to parasites. Pre-existing helminth infections should be treated prior to starting therapy. If a patient becomes infected during treatment and does not respond to antihelminthics, lebrikizumab should be paused until the infection clears [Australian].
- **Vaccinations:** Completion of age-appropriate immunisations is recommended prior to initiation. **Avoid the use of live vaccines** concurrently with lebrikizumab [Australian].
- **Corticosteroid Reduction:** Do not discontinue systemic, topical, or inhaled corticosteroids abruptly upon starting lebrikizumab. Reductions must be gradual and supervised by a physician to avoid withdrawal symptoms or unmasking underlying conditions [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate. The frequency data below is derived from 16-week placebo-controlled trials [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infections** | Conjunctivitis | Common (7.1%) |
| | Nasopharyngitis | Common (4.4%) |
| | Herpes zoster | Uncommon (0.4%) |
| **Eye Disorders** | Allergic conjunctivitis | Common (4.4%) |
| | Dry eye | Common (1.4%) |
| | Keratitis | Uncommon (0.5%) |
| **General Disorders** | Injection site reactions | Common (2.9%) |
| **Nervous System** | Headache | Common (4.4%) |

- **Note on Eosinophilia:** Generally transient and does not typically lead to treatment discontinuation.
- **Note on Conjunctivitis:** Most cases recovered or resolved while continuing treatment.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Systemic Immunomodulators:** The safety and efficacy of lebrikizumab in combination with other systemic immunomodulatory agents or phototherapy have not been evaluated [Australian].
- **Cytochrome P450:** Formal drug interaction studies have not been conducted.
- **Live Vaccines:** No data available on the response to live vaccines; co-administration is not recommended [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. There are limited data in pregnant women. Human IgG is known to cross the placental barrier. While animal studies (monkeys) showed no embryofetal toxicity at doses 18 times the human exposure, a precautionary approach is recommended. Avoid use during pregnancy unless the potential benefit justifies the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data exists on the presence of lebrikizumab in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. Since maternal IgG is excreted in human milk, the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the mother [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in monkeys showed no effects on male or female reproductive organs or menstrual cycles [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in adolescents (12 to <18 years) weighing ≥40 kg. Use in children <12 years or adolescents <40 kg is not established [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** Limited data (n=123 in clinical trials). No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed, but data are insufficient to determine if they respond differently than younger patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not expected to undergo significant renal elimination. Population PK analysis indicates markers of renal function do not affect pharmacokinetics [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not expected to undergo significant hepatic elimination. Population PK analysis indicates markers of hepatic function do not affect pharmacokinetics [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lebrikizumab is a high-affinity IgG4 monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to soluble IL-13. This prevents the formation of the IL-4Rα/IL-13Rα1 heterodimer complex, thereby inhibiting IL-13 signaling. Unlike other agents, it does not significantly inhibit IL-4 signaling.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment leads to reductions in Type 2 inflammatory biomarkers, including serum periostin, total IgE, TARC (CCL17), PARC (CCL18), and MCP-4 (CCL13) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is approximately 86%. Peak serum concentration ($T_{max}$) is reached in 7–8 days.
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is approximately 5.14 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to be degraded into small peptides via catabolic pathways, similar to endogenous IgG.
    - **Elimination:** Mean elimination half-life is approximately 24.5 days. Clearance is roughly 0.154 L/day [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** ADvocate 1, ADvocate 2 (monotherapy), and ADhere (concomitant TCS).
- **Primary Endpoint:** Proportion of patients achieving IGA 0 (clear) or 1 (almost clear) with a ≥2-point improvement at Week 16.
- **Efficacy Results (Week 16):**
    - **ADvocate 1:** 43.1% (Lebrikizumab) vs 12.7% (Placebo) achieved IGA 0/1.
    - **ADvocate 2:** 33.2% (Lebrikizumab) vs 10.8% (Placebo) achieved IGA 0/1.
    - **ADhere:** 41.2% (Lebrikizumab + TCS) vs 22.1% (Placebo + TCS) achieved IGA 0/1.
- **Long-term Data:** In the ADvocate trials, of those responding at Week 16, approximately 77% maintained IGA 0/1 response through Week 52 with Q4W dosing [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Room Temperature:** May be kept outside refrigeration at temperatures up to 30°C for a single period of up to 7 days. If not used within this window, the product must be discarded. Do not return to the refrigerator once warmed [Australian].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Do not shake.
- **Latex:** The pre-filled pen plunger is not made with natural rubber latex [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** This medicinal product is subject to additional monitoring in Australia [Australian].
