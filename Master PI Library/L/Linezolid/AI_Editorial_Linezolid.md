# Editorial Product Information: Linezolid

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, coherent reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local approved PI for definitive prescribing guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Linezolid.
- **Chemical Class:** Oxazolidinone antibacterial.
- **Brand Names:** AKM Linezolid [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 600 mg [Australian].
    - Note: Solution for injection and granules for oral suspension are mentioned in clinical data but are not available under the AKM brand [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral (tablets); Intravenous (noted as an alternative for other brands).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Gram-positive Infections:** Treatment of suspected or proven infections caused by Gram-positive organisms resistant to multiple antibiotic classes, specifically including methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus* species (MRSA) and vancomycin-resistant *Enterococcus* species (VRE) [Australian].
- **Limitation of Use:** Linezolid is active against Gram-positive bacteria only. It has no clinical activity against Gram-negative pathogens. If a concomitant Gram-negative pathogen is documented or suspected, specific Gram-negative therapy must be initiated [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children ≥12 years)
- **Standard Dose:** 600 mg administered orally (or IV) twice daily (bid) [Australian].
- **Duration by Indication:**
    - **Pneumonia (Nosocomial or Community-acquired):** 10–14 consecutive days.
    - **Skin and Soft Tissue Infections:** 400 mg to 600 mg orally (or 600 mg IV) bid for 10–14 days.
    - **Enterococcal Infections (including bacteraemia):** 600 mg bid for 14–28 consecutive days.
- **Maximum Duration:** Treatment should not exceed 28 days due to the risk of serious adverse effects [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **IV-to-Oral Switch:** Patients may be switched from parenteral to oral formulations when clinically indicated. No dose adjustment is required as linezolid has approximately 100% oral bioavailability [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to linezolid or any excipients [Australian].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use or use within two weeks of taking any medicinal product that inhibits MAO A or B (e.g., phenelzine) [Australian].
- **Uncontrolled Conditions (unless monitored for blood pressure increases):** Uncontrolled hypertension, pheochromocytoma, or thyrotoxicosis [Australian].
- **Concomitant Medications (unless monitored):** Directly or indirectly acting sympathomimetic agents (e.g., pseudoephedrine), vasopressors (e.g., adrenaline, dopamine), and serotonergic agents (e.g., SSRIs, triptans, pethidine) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Anaemia, leucopenia, pancytopenia, and thrombocytopenia have been reported. **Monitoring Requirement:** Complete blood counts (CBC) should be monitored weekly, particularly in those treated for >2 weeks, those with pre-existing myelosuppression, severe renal or hepatic impairment, or those on concomitant bone-marrow-suppressing drugs [Australian].
- **Neuropathy:** Peripheral and optic neuropathy have been reported, primarily with treatment >28 days. Optic neuropathy may progress to loss of vision. **Monitoring:** Prompt ophthalmic evaluation is required for any new visual symptoms (changes in acuity, color vision, or field) [Australian].
- **Lactic Acidosis:** Reported with linezolid use. Patients with recurrent nausea, vomiting, or unexplained acidosis require immediate medical evaluation [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Reported with co-administration of serotonergic agents, including SSRIs and opioids. Monitor for cognitive dysfunction, hyperpyrexia, and hyperreflexia [Australian].
- **Catheter-Related Infections:** An imbalance in mortality was observed in a trial of patients with catheter-related bloodstream infections, specifically in those with Gram-negative or mixed infections. Linezolid is not approved for catheter-related bloodstream infections [Australian].
- **Pseudomembranous Colitis:** Consider this diagnosis in patients presenting with diarrhoea during or after treatment [Australian].
- **Other Precautions:** Hyponatraemia/SIADH (monitor serum sodium in the elderly and those on diuretics) and rhabdomyolysis [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 22% of patients experience adverse reactions. Most are mild to moderate [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhoea (4.2%), Nausea (3.3%), Headache (2.1%), Vomiting (1.2%), Taste perversion (1.1%), Abnormal liver function tests (1.0%), Vaginal candidiasis (1.1%) |
| **Serious/Post-marketing** | Pancytopenia, Thrombocytopenia, Sideroblastic anaemia, Optic/Peripheral neuropathy, Lactic acidosis, Convulsions, Angioedema, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis, Rhabdomyolysis |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibition:** Linezolid is a reversible, non-selective MAOI. It may enhance the pressor response to sympathomimetic and adrenergic agents. Initial doses of vasopressors (dopamine, adrenaline) should be reduced and titrated [Australian].
- **Tyramine Interaction:** Avoid excessive ingestion of foods high in tyramine (e.g., mature cheese, yeast extracts, fermented soya products) to prevent pressor responses. Intakes <100 mg tyramine are generally tolerated [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Agents:** Potential for serotonin syndrome when combined with SSRIs, tricyclic antidepressants, triptans, pethidine, or buspirone [Australian].
- **Cytochrome P450:** Linezolid is not metabolised by and does not induce/inhibit clinically significant CYP isoforms (1A2, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4). No interactions are expected with warfarin or phenytoin [Australian].
- **Rifampicin:** Co-administration decreased linezolid Cmax by 21% and AUC by 32%. The clinical significance is unknown [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3**. There are no adequate data in pregnant women. Animal studies show reproductive toxicity (increased embryo death and decreased foetal weight). Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Animal data suggest linezolid passes into breast milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued prior to administration [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Reversibly decreased fertility and altered spermatogenesis observed in male rats at exposure levels equivalent to humans. No effects observed in dogs [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Birth through 11 years:** 10 mg/kg orally or IV every 8 hours. 
    - **Pre-term neonates (<7 days old, <34 weeks gestation):** Initial dose 10 mg/kg every 12 hours, increasing to every 8 hours by 7 days of life [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No significant pharmacokinetic alterations; no dose adjustment required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe impairment. However, metabolites may accumulate in severe insufficiency. **Haemodialysis:** Approximately 30% of a dose is removed; linezolid should be administered *after* dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild-to-moderate impairment. Use in severe hepatic insufficiency only if benefits outweigh risks [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selectively inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 23S ribosomal RNA of the 50S subunit. This prevents the formation of the 70S initiation complex [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Generally bacteriostatic against enterococci and staphylococci; bactericidal for most strains of streptococci [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and extensive (100% bioavailability). Cmax reached within 1–2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Readily distributes to well-perfused tissues; protein binding ~31%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via non-enzymatic oxidation to inactive metabolites (A and B).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (30–35% as parent drug, 40% as metabolite A, 10% as metabolite B). Elimination half-life is 5–7 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Nosocomial Pneumonia:** Efficacy equivalent to vancomycin (clinical cure rates ~53% vs 52%) [Australian].
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia:** Efficacy equivalent to ceftriaxone/cefpodoxime (cure rates 82–83%) [Australian].
- **MRSA Infections:** Comparable efficacy to vancomycin in pneumonia and skin/soft tissue infections [Australian].
- **VRE Infections:** High-dose (600 mg bid) showed superior cure rates compared to low-dose (200 mg bid) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 36 months [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
