# Editorial Product Information: Lemborexant

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lemborexant for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lemborexant (a competitive dual orexin receptor antagonist).
- **Brand Name:** **DAYVIGO** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05CH06 (Orexin receptor antagonists).
- **Dosage Form and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 5 mg (pale yellow, round, biconvex, debossed with "5" and "LЄM").
    - Film-coated tablets: 10 mg (orange, round, biconvex, debossed with "10" and "LЄM").
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Eisai Australia Pty. Ltd. [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Insomnia:** Treatment of insomnia in adults, characterised by difficulties with sleep onset and/or sleep maintenance, in accordance with the latest Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM) criteria [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 5 mg once nightly.
- **Maximum Dose:** 10 mg once nightly if the 5 mg dose is well-tolerated but a greater clinical effect is required.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Take within a few minutes of going to bed.
    - Ensure at least 7 hours remain before the planned time of awakening.
    - May be taken with or without food; however, time to sleep onset may be delayed if taken with or soon after a meal.
- **Clinical Guidance:** Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources (standard clinical practice suggests skipping the dose if 7 hours of sleep are not possible).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Narcolepsy:** Contraindicated in all patients with narcolepsy.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to lemborexant or any excipients (including lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **CNS Depressant Effects and Daytime Impairment:** Lemborexant can impair wakefulness even when used as prescribed. The risk of next-day somnolence increases with higher doses or if taken with less than a full night's sleep. Co-administration with other CNS depressants (e.g., opioids, benzodiazepines, alcohol) increases this risk.
- **Complex Sleep Behaviours:** Reports exist of sleep-walking, sleep-driving, and engaging in other activities while not fully awake. **Discontinue immediately** if a patient experiences complex sleep behaviour.
- **Suicidal Ideation/Worsening of Depression:** Incidence of suicidal ideation was slightly higher in lemborexant groups vs. placebo in trials. Use with caution in patients with depressive disorders; prescribe the lowest feasible quantity of tablets.
- **Sleep Paralysis, Hallucinations, and Cataplexy-like Symptoms:**
    - Sleep paralysis (inability to move for several minutes during sleep-wake transitions).
    - Hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations.
    - Cataplexy-like symptoms (e.g., sudden leg weakness) can occur without an identified trigger.
- **Co-morbidities:** Evaluate for primary medical or psychiatric causes if insomnia persists after 7–10 days of treatment.
- **Respiratory Function:** Use with caution in patients with compromised respiratory function (e.g., severe COPD or OSA), as clinically meaningful effects cannot be excluded despite neutral findings in mild-to-moderate study populations.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally dose-dependent.

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency Category | Incidence (5 mg) | Incidence (10 mg) | Placebo |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Somnolence** | Common | 5.0% - 6.6% | 8.2% - 10.5% | 1.3% - 1.6% |
| **Headache** | Common | 5.9% | 4.5% | 3.4% |
| **Nightmare/Abnormal Dreams** | Common | 0.9% | 2.2% | 0.9% |

- **Serious/Other Effects:** Sleep paralysis (up to 1.6% at 10 mg), complex sleep behaviours, and palpitations. 
- **Discontinuation Rates:** 3.5% (5 mg) and 6.1% (10 mg) compared to 2.7% for placebo. Somnolence was the primary cause of discontinuation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A Inhibitors:**
    - **Strong or Moderate (e.g., itraconazole, fluconazole):** Avoid concomitant use. AUC increases approximately 4-fold.
    - **Weak (e.g., fluoxetine):** Maximum recommended dose is 5 mg once nightly.
- **CYP3A Inducers:**
    - **Strong or Moderate (e.g., rifampicin):** Avoid concomitant use; efficacy may be significantly reduced (97% reduction in exposure).
- **Gastric pH Modifiers:** H2 blockers and PPIs may be co-administered; no clinically significant effect on overall exposure.
- **Effect on Other Drugs:** Lemborexant is a weak inducer of CYP2B6 (e.g., bupropion); however, it does not clinically induce or inhibit CYP3A4.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Avoid consumption. Alcohol increases lemborexant $C_{max}$ and AUC and has additive negative effects on cognitive performance.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category: B1.** (Drugs taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an increase in the frequency of malformation).
    - Animal studies showed embryofetal toxicity and growth delays only at exposures >100 times the human dose. Use only if potential benefits justify potential risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Lemborexant and its metabolites are present in human milk (Relative Infant Dose approx. 2%). Monitor breastfed infants for excessive sedation. 
- **Fertility:** No observed effects on male rat fertility; irregular oestrous cycles noted in female rats at very high exposures (>60x MRHD).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 18 years of age; safety and efficacy not established.
- **Elderly Use ($\ge 65$ years):** No dose adjustment required. However, the incidence of somnolence is higher at the 10 mg dose (9.8%) compared to younger adults (7.7%).
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Maximum recommended dose is 5 mg once nightly.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lemborexant is a competitive antagonist of both orexin receptors (OX1R and OX2R), with a higher affinity for OX2R. It suppresses the wake-promoting drive of the orexin neuropeptide signalling system.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Does not prolong the QTc interval at doses up to 7.5 times the maximum recommended dose.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; $T_{max}$ is 1–3 hours. High-fat/high-calorie meals delay $T_{max}$ by 2 hours and decrease $C_{max}$ by 23%.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 1970 L; highly protein-bound (approx. 94%).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4, and to a lesser extent CYP3A5. M10 is the major circulating metabolite.
    - **Excretion:** Primarily faecal (57.4%) and urinary (29.1%).
    - **Half-life:** Effective half-life is 17 hours (5 mg) to 19 hours (10 mg).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 303 (SUNRISE 2):** A 6-month (extended to 12 months) trial in adults $\ge 18$ years. Both 5 mg and 10 mg doses demonstrated significant superiority over placebo in patient-reported sleep onset latency (sSOL), sleep efficiency (sSEF), and wake after sleep onset (sWASO).
- **Study 304 (SUNRISE 1):** A 1-month trial in females $\ge 55$ years and males $\ge 65$ years using polysomnography (PSG). Lemborexant demonstrated significant improvements in latency to persistent sleep (LPS) and sleep maintenance compared to placebo and zolpidem ER.
- **Key Outcome:** No evidence of rebound insomnia or withdrawal effects following discontinuation.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C.
- **Packaging:** Keep in the original container to protect from moisture.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring in Australia (Black Triangle Scheme) to allow quick identification of new safety information.
