# Editorial Product Information: Lenacapavir

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions Contributed:** Australian (TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the product information for **Sunlenca** (lenacapavir). It is designed for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lenacapavir (as sodium).
- **Chemical Class:** Multistage, selective HIV-1 capsid inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Sunlenca** [Australian].
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 463.5 mg/1.5 mL (309 mg/mL) lenacapavir in a single-dose vial [Australian].
    - **Tablets:** 300 mg lenacapavir film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral (tablets) and Subcutaneous (injection).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Gilead Sciences Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Multidrug-Resistant HIV-1:** Indicated in combination with other antiretrovirals for the treatment of adults with multidrug-resistant HIV-1 infection for whom a suppressive antiviral regimen cannot otherwise be constructed [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The regimen consists of an oral initiation phase followed by maintenance via subcutaneous injection every 6 months (26 weeks).

### Initiation Schedule [Australian]
| Treatment Time | Dosage and Route |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Day 1** | 600 mg orally (2 x 300 mg tablets) |
| **Day 2** | 600 mg orally (2 x 300 mg tablets) |
| **Day 8** | 300 mg orally (1 x 300 mg tablet) |
| **Day 15** | 927 mg by subcutaneous injection (2 x 1.5 mL injections) |

### Maintenance Schedule [Australian]
- **Dose:** 927 mg (2 x 1.5 mL injections).
- **Frequency:** Once every 6 months (26 weeks) from the date of the last injection (window of +/- 2 weeks).

### Administration Instructions
- **Oral:** May be taken with or without food. Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not chew, crush, or split.
- **Subcutaneous:** Administered into the abdomen by a healthcare professional. Do **not** administer intradermally. 
- **Vomiting:** If the patient vomits within 3 hours of an oral dose, a replacement dose should be taken. If vomiting occurs >3 hours after dosing, no replacement is necessary.

### Missed Doses and Bridging [Australian]
- **Missed Oral Initiation:** Specific protocols apply based on whether the delay is less than or greater than 6 days.
- **Delayed Injection (<2 weeks):** Administer the 927 mg maintenance dose as soon as possible.
- **Oral Bridging (Delay >2 weeks anticipated):** 300 mg orally once every 7 days for up to 26 weeks. The maintenance injection must be resumed within 7 days of the last oral dose.
- **Re-initiation:** If the injection is delayed >2 weeks and oral bridging was not used, restart the initiation regimen from Day 1.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Strong Inducers:** Co-administration with strong inducers of CYP3A, P-gp, and UGT1A1 is contraindicated due to the risk of significant decreases in lenacapavir plasma concentrations, leading to loss of therapeutic effect and resistance development. Examples include:
    - Rifampicin
    - Carbamazepine, Phenytoin
    - St John’s wort (Hypericum perforatum) [Australian]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Resistance Risk Post-Discontinuation:** Due to the long half-life of lenacapavir, residual concentrations can persist for up to 9 months. If treatment is stopped, it is essential to initiate an alternative suppressive regimen within 28 weeks of the last injection to prevent the emergence of viral resistance.
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome (IRIS):** Reported in patients treated with combination antiretroviral therapy. Inflammatory reactions to asymptomatic opportunistic pathogens (e.g., CMV, Mycobacterium, Pneumocystis) may occur, typically in the first few weeks/months.
- **Injection Site Reactions (ISRs):** Very common. Improper intradermal administration may lead to serious reactions including necrosis or ulceration.
- **Long-acting Properties and Interactions:** Residual concentrations may affect the exposure of sensitive CYP3A substrates initiated within 9 months of the last dose.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on the **CAPELLA** trial (Week 156 analysis) [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reactions (65%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhoea (7%), Nausea (6%) |

### Serious/Significant Effects
- **Injection Site Reactions:** While mostly mild (Grade 1) or moderate (Grade 2), 6% of participants experienced Grade 3 reactions. Nodules and indurations may persist; median durations were 288 days and 190 days respectively.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities (Grades 3-4):** Creatinine increase (13%), Glycosuria (6%), Hyperglycaemia (5%), Proteinuria (4%), and elevations in ALT/AST/Bilirubin (3%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Profile
- **Substrate:** CYP3A, P-gp, and UGT1A1.
- **Inhibitor:** Moderate inhibitor of CYP3A. Also inhibits P-gp.
- **Not an Inhibitor:** BCRP (clinically meaningful), OATP.

### Clinically Significant Interactions [Australian]
- **Moderate Inducers (e.g., Efavirenz):** Co-administration not recommended.
- **Strong Inhibitors (Combined CYP3A/P-gp/UGT1A1):** Co-administration with agents like atazanavir/cobicistat is not recommended.
- **Narrow Therapeutic Index Substrates:** Caution is required with sensitive CYP3A or P-gp substrates (e.g., Digoxin, Midazolam, certain PDE-5 inhibitors, and anticoagulants).
- **Corticosteroids:** Use of systemic dexamethasone may decrease lenacapavir levels. Lenacapavir may increase corticosteroid exposure, potentially leading to Cushing’s syndrome and adrenal suppression.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B1. There are no adequate/controlled studies in humans. Animal studies showed no evidence of teratogenicity. Use only if the benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if lenacapavir is excreted in human milk. To avoid HIV transmission and potential drug toxicity, breastfeeding is generally not recommended for mothers living with HIV.
- **Fertility:** No human data available. Rat studies showed no effect on male or female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe impairment (CrCl ≥15 mL/min). Not studied in end-stage renal disease (ESRD).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild to moderate impairment (Child-Pugh Class A or B). Not studied in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lenacapavir is a multistage HIV-1 capsid inhibitor. It binds directly to the interface between capsid protein (CA) subunits, interfering with:
    1. Capsid-mediated nuclear uptake of HIV-1 proviral DNA.
    2. Virus assembly and release.
    3. Capsid core formation.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Activity is specific to HIV-1 and HIV-2. No clinically relevant effect on QTcF intervals.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability is low (6–10%); peak plasma at 4 hours. Subcutaneous absorption is slow and complex (peak at 77–84 days).
    - **Distribution:** High protein binding (>98.5%).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A and UGT1A1 (oxidation, N-dealkylation, glucuronidation).
    - **Elimination:** Fecal (76%), Urine (<1%). Median half-life: 10–12 days (oral); 8–12 weeks (subcutaneous).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CAPELLA Study (GS-US-200-4625):** A Phase 2/3 partially randomised, placebo-controlled trial in 72 heavily treatment-experienced adults with multidrug-resistant HIV-1.
- **Primary Endpoint:** Proportion of participants achieving ≥0.5 log10 copies/mL reduction in HIV-1 RNA at the end of a 14-day functional monotherapy period.
- **Results:** 87.5% of the lenacapavir group achieved the primary endpoint vs 16.7% for placebo. At Week 156, 65% achieved viral suppression (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL) when used with an optimised background regimen (OBR) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Injection/Tablets:** Store below 30 °C. 
- **Protection:** Store in the original package. Keep vials in the carton until preparation to protect from light.
- **Handling:** Once drawn into syringes, injections should be administered as soon as possible [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
