# Editorial Product Information: Lorlatinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lorlatinib for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the current approved PI in their specific region before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lorlatinib (anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) and ROS1 tyrosine kinase inhibitor).
- **Brand Names:** 
    - **LORVIQUA** [Australian]
    - **Lorbrena** [US]
- **ATC Code:** L01ED05
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 25 mg (tan, round) and 100 mg (lavender, oval) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** Treatment of patients with anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK)-positive locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC, as determined by a validated test [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 100 mg taken orally once daily, continued as long as clinical benefit is derived.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - May be taken with or without food.
    - Tablets should be swallowed whole; do not chew, crush, or split.
    - Do not ingest if the tablet is broken or cracked.
- **Missed Dose:** 
    - If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered unless it is within 4 hours of the next scheduled dose. 
    - Do not double the dose to make up for a missed one.
- **Dose Titration/Modifications:** 
    - Dose reductions for toxicity follow a tiered approach:
        - **First reduction:** 75 mg once daily.
        - **Second reduction:** 50 mg once daily.
    - **Discontinuation:** Permanently discontinue if the patient cannot tolerate the 50 mg daily dose.
- **Specific Interaction Adjustments:**
    - **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors:** If co-administration is unavoidable, reduce the starting lorlatinib dose from 100 mg to 75 mg once daily.
    - **Fluconazole:** If concomitant use is unavoidable, reduce the starting dose to 75 mg once daily [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to lorlatinib or any excipients in the formulation.
- **Strong CYP3A Inducers:** Concomitant use with strong CYP3A inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, St. John’s wort) is strictly contraindicated due to the risk of severe hepatotoxicity (AST/ALT elevations) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hyperlipidaemia:** Very common; significant increases in serum cholesterol and triglycerides. Monitor at 2, 4, and 8 weeks post-initiation, then periodically. Initiate or increase lipid-lowering therapy as required.
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects:** Includes psychotic effects, seizures, and changes in cognition, mood, speech, and mental status. May require dose modification or discontinuation.
- **Atrioventricular (AV) Block:** PR interval prolongation and AV block have been reported. Monitor ECG prior to treatment and monthly thereafter.
- **Lipase and Amylase Elevations:** Risk of pancreatitis. Monitor levels prior to start and regularly thereafter.
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** Severe or life-threatening cases have occurred. Evaluate any patient with worsening respiratory symptoms immediately.
- **Hypertension:** Control blood pressure before starting treatment; monitor after 2 weeks and then monthly.
- **Hyperglycaemia:** Monitor fasting serum glucose before starting and periodically thereafter.
- **Hepatotoxicity (Inducer interaction):** Discontinue strong CYP3A inducers for at least 3 plasma half-lives before starting lorlatinib.

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Most frequent serious reactions include cognitive effects and pneumonitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Lorlatinib as Substrate:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4 and UGT1A4. 
        - *Strong CYP3A inhibitors* (e.g., itraconazole, ritonavir) increase lorlatinib exposure.
        - *Strong CYP3A inducers* (e.g., rifampicin) significantly decrease lorlatinib exposure (AUC decreased by 85%) and are contraindicated.
    - **Lorlatinib as Inducer:** Lorlatinib induces CYP3A4 and CYP2B6. 
        - *CYP3A Substrates:* Concurrent use with narrow therapeutic index drugs (e.g., alfentanil, ciclosporin, hormonal contraceptives) should be avoided as their efficacy may be reduced.
    - **Transporters:** Potential to inhibit P-gp, BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OCT1, MATE1, and OAT3. Caution is advised with narrow therapeutic index P-gp substrates (e.g., digoxin).
- **Proton-Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):** Rabeprazole had minimal effect; no dose adjustment required for PPIs, H2-antagonists, or antacids.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** Lorlatinib can cause fetal harm. Embryolethality and malformations were seen in animal studies. 
    - **Contraception:** Females of childbearing potential must use highly effective non-hormonal contraception during treatment and for 21 days after the final dose. Males with female partners of reproductive potential must use effective contraception (including condoms) during treatment and for 14 weeks after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment and for 7 days after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** Male fertility may be compromised; men should seek advice on fertility preservation prior to treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients <18 years.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall dose adjustment recommended for patients ≥65 years, though data are limited.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (eGFR ≥30 mL/min):** No dose adjustment.
    - **Severe (eGFR <30 mL/min):** Reduce starting dose to 75 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild:** No dose adjustment.
    - **Moderate to Severe:** Not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** ATP-competitive, brain-penetrant, small-molecule inhibitor of ALK and ROS1 tyrosine kinases. It is designed to inhibit tumor cells carrying ALK mutations that confer resistance to other ALK inhibitors (e.g., G1202R).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; Tmax ~1.2 hours. Absolute bioavailability ~80.8%.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding ~66%. Crosses the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily CYP3A4 and UGT1A4. Autoinduction of its own metabolism occurs at steady state.
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life ~23.6 hours. 47.7% recovered in urine (mostly metabolites), 40.9% in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CROWN Study (First-line):** Randomised Phase 3 trial vs. crizotinib in treatment-naive ALK-positive NSCLC. 
    - **PFS:** Hazard Ratio 0.28 (95% CI: 0.19, 0.41; p<0.0001).
    - **Intracranial Response:** 82% intracranial ORR in patients with measurable lesions vs. 23% for crizotinib.
- **Study B7461001 (Previously Treated):** Single-arm study in patients who failed prior ALK TKIs. 
    - **ORR:** 47.2% for those with 1 or more prior ALK TKIs. 
    - **Intracranial ORR:** 53% in patients with baseline brain metastases.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Packaging:** Available in aluminium foil blisters or HDPE bottles [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring in Australia (Black Triangle Scheme).
