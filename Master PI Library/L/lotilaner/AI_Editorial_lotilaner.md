# Editorial Product Information: Lotilaner, Moxidectin, Praziquantel, and Pyrantel

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following official Product Information (PI) source: **United States (FDA)** for **Credelio Quattro**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination product containing lotilaner, moxidectin, praziquantel, and pyrantel for use by healthcare professionals (veterinary). 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory requirements for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** 
    - **Lotilaner:** Isoxazoline class ectoparasiticide.
    - **Moxidectin:** Semisynthetic macrocyclic lactone (milbemycin class) endectocide.
    - **Praziquantel:** Isoquinolone anthelmintic.
    - **Pyrantel (as pamoate salt):** Tetrahydropyrimidine nematocide.
- **Brand Names:** **Credelio Quattro**, **Credelio Quattro-CA1** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (QP54AB52 proposed/related).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Flavoured chewable tablets available in five strengths [US]:
    - 56.25 mg lotilaner / 0.056 mg moxidectin / 14.25 mg praziquantel / 14.25 mg pyrantel
    - 112.5 mg lotilaner / 0.113 mg moxidectin / 28.5 mg praziquantel / 28.5 mg pyrantel
    - 225 mg lotilaner / 0.225 mg moxidectin / 57 mg praziquantel / 57 mg pyrantel
    - 450 mg lotilaner / 0.45 mg moxidectin / 114 mg praziquantel / 114 mg pyrantel
    - 900 mg lotilaner / 0.9 mg moxidectin / 228 mg praziquantel / 228 mg pyrantel
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Elanco US Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
The following indications are approved in the United States:
- **Heartworm Prevention:** Prevention of heartworm disease caused by *Dirofilaria immitis*.
- **Ectoparasite Treatment and Control:** 
    - Treatment and prevention of flea infestations (*Ctenocephalides felis*).
    - Treatment and control of tick infestations, including *Amblyomma americanum* (lone star tick), *Dermacentor variabilis* (American dog tick), *Ixodes scapularis* (black-legged tick), *Rhipicephalus sanguineus* (brown dog tick), and *Haemaphysalis longicornis* (longhorned tick).
    - Prevention of *Borrelia burgdorferi* (Lyme disease) infections by killing *Ixodes scapularis* vectors.
- **Endoparasite Treatment and Control:**
    - **Roundworms:** Immature adult and adult *Toxocara canis* and adult *Toxascaris leonina*.
    - **Hookworms:** Fourth-stage larvae, immature adult, and adult *Ancylostoma caninum* and adult *Uncinaria stenocephala*.
    - **Tapeworms:** *Dipylidium caninum*, *Taenia pisiformis*, and *Echinococcus granulosus*.
- **Conditional Indication [US]:** Treatment of infestations caused by New World screwworm (*Cochliomyia hominivorax*) larvae (myiasis).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult Dogs and Puppies)
- **Minimum Dosage:** 20 mg/kg lotilaner, 0.02 mg/kg moxidectin, 5 mg/kg praziquantel, and 5 mg/kg pyrantel [US].
- **Frequency:** Once monthly.
- **Age/Weight Requirements:** For use in dogs and puppies 8 weeks of age and older, weighing 3.3 lbs (1.5 kg) or greater [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Requirement:** Must be administered with food or within 30 minutes of feeding to ensure optimal lotilaner bioavailability [US].
- **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs within one hour of administration, redose with a new tablet.
- **Heartworm Prevention:** Administer year-round or at monthly intervals starting within one month of the first seasonal exposure to mosquitoes.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Give the missed dose immediately and resume the monthly schedule [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- There are no known contraindications for the use of this product [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Human Safety:** Not for human use. Keep out of reach of children. Wash hands after handling [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions:** Lotilaner is an isoxazoline. This class is associated with potential neurologic adverse reactions, including tremors, ataxia, and seizures. Use with caution in dogs with a history of seizures or neurologic disorders [US].
- **Heartworm Testing:** Dogs should be tested for existing heartworm infections prior to administration. This product is not effective against adult *D. immitis*. Infected dogs should be treated with an adulticide [US].
- **New World Screwworm (NWS):** Effective treatment requires concurrent mechanical removal of larvae, lesion cleaning, and appropriate wound care [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies observed in a 330-day US field study (N=191):
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Diarrhea (11%)
    - Vomiting (9.4%)
    - Lethargy (6.3%)
    - Anorexia (5.8%)
    - Dermatitis (5.2%)
    - Weight loss (3.1%)
    - Pruritus (1.6%)
    - Alopecia (1.0%)
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Seizure (0.5%), Ataxia (0.5%), Nystagmus (0.5%), Anisocoria (0.5%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Cluster seizures and ataxia were reported in clinical studies. One geriatric dog experienced vomiting, ataxia, and nystagmus two days after dosing [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Concurrent Use:** No adverse reactions were associated with the concurrent use of vaccines, antimicrobials, anthelmintics, antiemetics, steroids, NSAIDs, anesthetics, or analgesics in field safety studies [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy/Lactation:** Safe use has not been evaluated in breeding, pregnant, or lactating dogs [US].
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for puppies $\ge$ 8 weeks old and $\ge$ 3.3 lbs [US].
- **Avermectin-Sensitive Collies:** In a safety study of healthy Collies (P-gp sensitive), 5X maximum labeled doses resulted in transient mild depression, salivation, and vomiting. All dogs recovered without treatment [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources. Note that increased bile acids were observed in some dogs at 3X dosing in safety studies [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- **Lotilaner:** Inhibits insect/acarine GABA-gated chloride channels, causing uncontrolled neuromuscular activity and death.
- **Moxidectin:** Interferes with chloride channel-mediated neurotransmission in parasites, leading to paralysis and death.
- **Praziquantel:** Causes muscular paralysis in tapeworms and tegument disruption via calcium ion influx.
- **Pyrantel:** Depolarising neuromuscular-blocking agent causing paralysis and expulsion of nematodes.

### Pharmacokinetics [US]
- **Absorption:** High oral bioavailability for lotilaner (93% fed). Bioavailability is lower for praziquantel (41%) and pyrantel (31%). Peak concentrations ($T_{max}$) are reached between 0.75 hours (praziquantel) and 10 hours (lotilaner).
- **Distribution/Metabolism:** Not detailed in source.
- **Elimination:** Mean plasma half-lives are approximately 33.6 days (806h) for lotilaner, 26.4 days (634h) for moxidectin, 3.64 hours for praziquantel, and 4.87 hours for pyrantel.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Heartworm:** 100% effective in laboratory studies and field trials (N=156) over 11 months [US].
- **Endoparasites:** $\ge$ 97% effective against target roundworms and hookworms; 100% effective against *E. granulosus* [US].
- **Ectoparasites:** 100% effective against fleas within 24 hours. Tick efficacy $\ge$ 97.1% at 48 hours for specified species [US].
- **NWS Myiasis:** Conditional approval based on 100% overall effectiveness (expulsion and larvicidal activity) in a study of 11 dogs [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 15–25°C (59–77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 5–40°C (41–104°F) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Caution: Federal law restricts this drug to use by or on the order of a licensed veterinarian (Rx-only).
- **Legal Status:** **Credelio Quattro-CA1** is conditionally approved by the FDA pending full demonstration of effectiveness for the New World screwworm indication [US].
