# Editorial Product Information: Losartan Potassium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information approved for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Losartan potassium.
- **Chemical Class:** Non-peptide, orally active angiotensin II receptor (type AT1) antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** COZAAR [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 50 mg film-coated tablets (white, oval, scored, marked "952") [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Organon Pharma Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** For the treatment of hypertension, either as monotherapy or in combination with other antihypertensive agents (e.g., thiazide diuretics) [Australian].
- **Renal Protection in Type 2 Diabetic Patients with Proteinuria:** Indicated to delay the progression of renal disease in hypertensive type 2 diabetic patients with proteinuria (defined as urinary albumin to creatinine ratio ≥ 300 mg/g) [Australian].
- **Stroke Risk Reduction:** Indicated to reduce the risk of stroke in hypertensive patients with left ventricular hypertrophy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** The usual starting and maintenance dose is 50 mg once daily.
- **Dose Range:** 25 mg to 100 mg total daily dose, administered once or twice daily.
- **Titration:** If the antihypertensive response at 50 mg once daily is insufficient, 25 mg twice daily may be attempted before increasing the total daily dose. Maximal antihypertensive effect is typically achieved within 3 to 6 weeks.

### Special Populations Dosing
- **Intravascular Volume Depletion:** Consider a starting dose of 25 mg once daily for patients treated with high-dose diuretics or those with volume depletion [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** A lower starting dose should be considered for patients with a history of hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No initial dosage adjustment is required for patients with renal impairment, including those on dialysis [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No initial dosage adjustment is generally necessary for the elderly [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- May be administered with or without food.
- Tablets are scored to allow for dose division if required.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated in women who are pregnant [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Hypersensitivity to losartan potassium or any of the excipients (e.g., lactose monohydrate) [Australian].
- **Dual Blockade:** Must not be co-administered with aliskiren in patients with diabetes [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Foetal Toxicity:** **BOXED WARNING (Implied):** Drugs acting on the renin-angiotensin system (RAS) can cause injury and death to the developing foetus. Discontinue losartan immediately upon detection of pregnancy [Australian].
- **Hypotension and Electrolyte/Fluid Imbalance:** Symptomatic hypotension may occur in patients with intravascular volume depletion. These conditions should be corrected prior to initiation, or a lower starting dose should be used.
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Increased risk in patients with renal impairment, with or without diabetes. Monitoring of serum potassium is advised.
- **Renal Function Impairment:** Susceptible individuals (e.g., those with severe CHF or bilateral renal artery stenosis) may experience changes in renal function, including acute renal failure.
- **Triple Therapy Risk:** The combination of an ACE inhibitor or ARB with an NSAID/COX-2 inhibitor and a thiazide diuretic (the "triple whammy") significantly increases the risk of renal impairment, especially in the elderly.
- **Angioedema:** Losartan has been associated with rare cases of angioedema (swelling of the larynx, glottis, face, lips, and pharynx).
- **Grapefruit Juice:** Contains components that inhibit CYP450 enzymes; consumption should be avoided as it may lower the concentration of the active metabolite, reducing efficacy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Treatment is generally well-tolerated. Discontinuation rates in trials were low (2.3% for losartan vs 3.7% for placebo) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>1%)** | Dizziness (3.5%), Upper respiratory infection (7.9%), Cough (3.4%), Diarrhoea (2.4%), Back pain (1.8%), Insomnia (1.4%), Sinus disorder (1.5%) |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | Orthostatic effects, syncope, angina pectoris, palpitations, tachycardia, rash, dyspepsia, myalgia, arm/hip/knee pain |
| **Rare (<0.1%) / Post-marketing** | Anaphylactic reactions, angioedema (including intestinal), vasculitis (Henoch-Schoenlein purpura), hepatitis, rhabdomyolysis, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, migraine, dysgeusia, erectile dysfunction |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Aliskiren:** Dual blockade of the RAAS increases the risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia, and renal dysfunction. Do not use in patients with diabetes or renal impairment (GFR < 60 mL/min) [Australian].
- **Potassium-Sparing Agents:** Concomitant use with potassium-sparing diuretics (e.g., spironolactone), potassium supplements, or salt substitutes containing potassium may lead to hyperkalaemia.
- **Lithium:** Losartan may reduce lithium excretion, leading to increased serum lithium levels; careful monitoring is required.
- **NSAIDs/COX-2 Inhibitors:** May attenuate the antihypertensive effect and increase the risk of worsening renal function (including acute renal failure).
- **Rifampicin and Fluconazole:** Reported to reduce levels of the active carboxylic acid metabolite.
- **Grapefruit Juice:** Avoid consumption as it may reduce the therapeutic effect [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian].** Losartan use during the second and third trimesters reduces foetal renal function and increases neonatal morbidity and mortality. Adverse outcomes include skull hypoplasia, anuria, hypotension, and death. If pregnancy is detected, discontinue losartan as soon as possible.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if losartan is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for adverse effects on the nursing infant, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility at doses up to 150-200 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in efficacy or safety were observed between older and younger patients, though higher sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment needed for GFR > 30 mL/min. For GFR < 30 mL/min or dialysis, AUCs are increased by 50-100%, but no initial dose adjustment is required unless volume depleted [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Plasma concentrations are significantly higher (5-fold) in patients with cirrhosis. A lower starting dose is recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Losartan is a selective AT1 receptor antagonist. It blocks the vasoconstrictor and aldosterone-secreting effects of angiotensin II. It does not inhibit ACE (kininase II) and thus does not prevent the breakdown of bradykinin.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed (33% bioavailability) with significant first-pass metabolism.
    - **Metabolism:** Approximately 14% of an oral dose is converted by CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 to an active carboxylic acid metabolite (E-3174), which is 10 to 40 times more potent than losartan.
    - **Distribution:** Both losartan and E-3174 are >99% bound to plasma proteins (primarily albumin).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~2 hours (losartan) and 6–9 hours (E-3174). Excretion is biliary (58%) and urinary (35%).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Losartan causes a persistent decrease in serum uric acid (uricosuric effect) and has no effect on autonomic reflexes [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **LIFE Study:** A large study in 9,193 hypertensive patients with ECG-documented LVH. Losartan (vs atenolol) reduced the risk of the primary composite endpoint (CV death, stroke, and MI), primarily driven by a 25% relative risk reduction in stroke (p=0.001) [Australian].
- **RENAAL Study:** Conducted in 1,513 type 2 diabetic patients with proteinuria. Losartan reduced the relative risk of the primary composite endpoint (doubling of serum creatinine, ESRD, or death) by 16.1% (p=0.022) [Australian].
