# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. Information from other major regulatory authorities (e.g., Australian (TGA), European (EMA), Canadian (Health Canada)) was not provided for this synthesis.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local regulatory documentation before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
    - **Oral formulations:** 330 mg tablets and 1 g/10 mL oral solution (mentioned in pharmacokinetic data) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (for the evaluated injection product).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from genetic metabolic disorders [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2–3 minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often utilized in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and not less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered in clinical practice [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2–3 minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels (target: 40–50 µmol/L). Adjustments may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistry, and vital signs is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known based on current clinical documentation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These typically occur in ESRD patients and may manifest within minutes of administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase seizure frequency or severity in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Administration):** Safety of high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD is not established due to the potential accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - **Injection site reactions:** (up to 38% in some trials) [US].
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (up to 35%), vomiting (up to 21%), nausea [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (up to 21%) [US].
    - **Nervous System:** Headache, dizziness [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, body odor (associated with chronic use), anemia, fever, and hypercalcemia [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and increased seizure activity [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    - **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified as a letter grade, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal constituent of human milk. While studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine after exogenous administration, human data are lacking. The risks to the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Recommended dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; generally, dose selection should be cautious given the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for ESRD; however, chronic high-dose oral administration should be avoided due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for beta-oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normal plasma free carnitine levels are approximately 40–50 µmol/L [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg post-dialysis three times weekly increased mean trough levocarnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Special Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the product contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
