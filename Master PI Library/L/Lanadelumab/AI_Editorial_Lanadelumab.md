# Editorial Product Information: Lanadelumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local approved PI for full prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lanadelumab (a fully human IgG1/κ-light chain monoclonal antibody).
- **Chemical Class:** Plasma kallikrein inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **TAKHZYRO** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B06AC05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Solution for injection in a single-dose pre-filled syringe: 300 mg/2 mL and 150 mg/1 mL. 
    - Solution for injection in a single-dose vial: 300 mg/2 mL.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Takeda Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Routine Prevention of Hereditary Angioedema (HAE):** Indicated for the prevention of recurrent attacks in patients aged 2 years and older with C1-esterase-inhibitor deficiency or dysfunction [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Starting Dose:** 300 mg administered subcutaneously every 2 weeks.
- **Dose Reduction:** In patients who are well-controlled (e.g., attack-free) on treatment, a reduction to 300 mg every 4 weeks may be considered, particularly in patients with low body weight.

### Paediatric Dosing
- **12 to <18 years:** 300 mg every 2 weeks. A reduction to every 4 weeks may be considered if the patient is well-controlled for at least 26 weeks.
- **6 to <12 years:** 150 mg every 2 weeks. A reduction to every 4 weeks may be considered if well-controlled for 26 weeks.
- **2 to <6 years:** 150 mg every 4 weeks.
- **Under 2 years:** Safety and efficacy not established; use is not recommended [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Sites:** Inject into the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm.
- **Vial Use:** Requires aseptic withdrawal using an 18-gauge needle and administration with a 27-gauge needle. Once prepared, the syringe must be used within 2 hours at room temperature or within 8 hours if refrigerated (2°C to 8°C).
- **Pre-filled Syringe:** Ready-to-use; do not shake.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a dose is missed, it should be administered as soon as possible.
- **Minimum intervals between doses:** 
    - Adults and children 6–17 years: At least 10 days.
    - Children 2–5 years: At least 24 days.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to lanadelumab or any excipient (including dibasic sodium phosphate dihydrate, citric acid monohydrate, histidine, sodium chloride, and polysorbate 80).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Attacks:** **TAKHZYRO** is for prophylactic use only. It must not be used to treat an acute HAE attack. Patients should maintain access to rescue medications.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Severe reactions have occurred. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction is suspected.
- **Laboratory Test Interference (aPTT):** Lanadelumab can increase activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) by interacting with the contact system reagents used in the assay. This does not reflect a clinical bleeding risk and is not associated with changes in International Normalised Ratio (INR).
- **Traceability:** The name and batch number of the biological product must be recorded to improve monitoring.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reactions (pain, erythema, bruising, swelling, pruritus) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, maculopapular rash, myalgia, elevated ALT/AST, hypersensitivity (pruritus, tongue tingling) |
| **Post-marketing** | Injection site urticaria (frequency unknown) |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** While no specific serious drug-related events were highlighted as "frequent" in trials, severe hypersensitivity is a recognized risk.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No dedicated studies have been conducted. Based on its nature as a monoclonal antibody, standard cytochrome P450-mediated interactions are not expected.
- **Concomitant Meds:** Population PK analysis showed no effect on lanadelumab clearance when used with analgesics, antibacterials, antihistamines, or anti-inflammatory drugs.
- **Rescue Meds:** Use of icatibant or plasma-derived C1-INH during breakthrough attacks did not affect lanadelumab pharmacokinetics.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. Data in pregnant women are very limited. Animal studies (monkeys) at 28-fold human exposure showed no developmental toxicity. Use should only be considered if the benefit outweighs the potential risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk is unknown. In monkeys, milk excretion was minimal (0.2% of maternal plasma levels). Consider the benefits of breastfeeding against the mother's clinical need for the drug.
- **Fertility:** No human data. Animal studies showed no impact on male or female fertility markers.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Approved for $\ge$ 2 years. Safety and efficacy in adolescents and children were consistent with adult findings.
- **Elderly (>65 years):** Limited data available (n=11 in studies). No dose adjustment is required based on available information [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with mild or moderate renal impairment. No data/recommendations are available for severe impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific studies have been conducted; however, as a monoclonal antibody, hepatic impairment is not expected to affect clearance.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lanadelumab is a potent, reversible inhibitor of active plasma kallikrein. By inhibiting the proteolysis of high-molecular-weight-kininogen (HMWK), it prevents the generation of bradykinin, the primary mediator of HAE-related swelling and pain.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is approximately 4–5 days.
    - **Distribution:** $V_d$ is approximately 14.5 L (adults). Body weight is an important covariate (higher exposure in lighter patients).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 14–15 days.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Provides sustained control of plasma kallikrein activity. Maximum reduction of cHMWK (cleaved HMWK) formation is approximately 53.7%.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **HELP Study:** A pivotal Phase 3 trial in patients $\ge$ 12 years. 300 mg every 2 weeks reduced the monthly attack rate by 87% compared to placebo. 44% of patients in this arm remained attack-free over the 26-week period.
- **SPRING Study:** An open-label trial in children aged 2 to <12 years. Results showed a 94.8% reduction in attack rate from baseline, with safety results consistent with those in older populations.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Excursions:** Once removed from refrigeration, the product may be stored below 25°C for a single period of up to 14 days. If not used within this window, it must be discarded (vials can be returned to the fridge if stored under 25°C, provided the total time out of the fridge does not exceed 14 days).
- **Light:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
