# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- *Note:* While the user context mentioned an Australian source, the provided documentation is specifically the US FDA-approved labeling for Baxter Healthcare Corporation. Consequently, this document reflects US-approved indications, dosing, and safety data.
- **Purpose:** This document serves as a harmonized clinical overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), a naturally occurring carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US]. 
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - **Acute Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough concentrations below 40 to 50 µmol/L.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenterals visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on hemodialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Oral Formulation Safety:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of the oral form in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Patients on warfarin require frequent International Normalized Ratio (INR) monitoring after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trials Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), headache (22%), chest pain (12%), fever (7–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Serious Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngospasm, and bronchospasm.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (in patients with or without prior history).
- **Other:** Body odor, gastritis, and transient nausea/vomiting have been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in INR when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels at therapy initiation and after any dose changes [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase in dairy cows following exogenous administration. Risks to the child from excess carnitine must be weighed against maternal benefits. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Note that the **oral** form is not recommended for chronic use in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), ~76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x/week after dialysis), mean trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from ~20 µmol/L (pre-therapy) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx only). Poison schedule classification is not applicable under US FDA labeling; it is regulated as a human prescription drug [US].
