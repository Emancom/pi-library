# Editorial Product Information: Lecanemab

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by synthesizing official regulatory guidance from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). In instances where clinical guidance or restrictions differ between agencies, the more comprehensive or conservative recommendation has been prioritized to ensure maximum patient safety, with the source agency noted in brackets.

---

# 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** LEQEMBI  
**Active Ingredient:** Lecanemab (referred to as lecanemab-irmb in the United States)

Lecanemab is a recombinant humanized immunoglobulin gamma 1 (IgG1) monoclonal antibody directed against aggregated forms of amyloid beta (Aβ). It is produced in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells and has a molecular weight of approximately 150 kDa.

# 2. Approved Indications
LEQEMBI is indicated for the treatment of Alzheimer’s disease. Treatment should be initiated in patients with:
*   Mild cognitive impairment (MCI) due to Alzheimer’s disease, or
*   Mild dementia stage of Alzheimer's disease.

**Clinical Requirements:**
*   **Amyloid Confirmation:** The presence of amyloid beta pathology must be confirmed via validated testing (e.g., PET imaging or CSF analysis) prior to starting treatment.
*   **Genotype Restrictions:** In some jurisdictions, treatment is specifically restricted to patients who are apolipoprotein E ε4 (ApoE ε4) non-carriers or heterozygotes [TGA]. Prescribers should note that clinical benefit has not been established in ApoE ε4 homozygotes, who also face a significantly higher risk of serious adverse events.

# 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known serious hypersensitivity to lecanemab or any of its excipients (including arginine hydrochloride, histidine, and polysorbate 80).
*   **MRI Abnormalities:** Treatment should not be initiated in patients with baseline MRI findings suggestive of cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA), including:
    *   Prior intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) greater than 1 cm in diameter.
    *   More than 4 microhemorrhages (FDA) / More than 2 microhemorrhages (TGA).
    *   Evidence of superficial siderosis or vasogenic edema (ARIA-E). [TGA]
*   **Co-morbidities:** Severe white matter disease or any clinical condition that prevents safe MRI monitoring. [TGA]
*   **Anticoagulation:** Treatment should not be initiated in patients receiving ongoing anticoagulant therapy, with the exception of stable, low-dose aspirin. [TGA]

# 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Amyloid-Related Imaging Abnormalities (ARIA)
Lecanemab can cause ARIA characterized by edema or sulcal effusions (ARIA-E) and/or hemosiderin deposition (ARIA-H), which includes microhemorrhage and superficial siderosis. 
*   **Incidence:** ARIA usually occurs early in treatment (within the first 14 to 24 weeks).
*   **Symptomatology:** While often asymptomatic, ARIA can manifest as headache, confusion, visual disturbances, dizziness, or seizures. Serious and fatal events, including large intracerebral hemorrhages (>1 cm), have occurred.
*   **ApoE ε4 Risk:** The risk of ARIA is substantially higher in ApoE ε4 homozygotes (approx. 45% incidence vs. 13% in non-carriers). Testing for ApoE ε4 status is required prior to initiation.

### Monitoring Requirements
A recent brain MRI (within 1 year [FDA] or 6 months [TGA]) must be available before starting treatment. Follow-up MRIs are mandatory prior to the **3rd, 5th, 7th, and 14th infusions**. Enhanced clinical vigilance is required during the first 14 weeks of therapy.

### Concomitant Medications
*   **Thrombolytics:** Caution is advised when considering tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) in patients on lecanemab, as it may increase the risk of fatal ICH. 
*   **Antithrombotics:** Patients taking antiplatelets or anticoagulants may be at higher risk for ICH. If a patient requires the commencement of anticoagulants during therapy, lecanemab should be paused. [TGA]

### Hypersensitivity and Infusion-Related Reactions
Infusion-related reactions (IRRs) occur in approximately 26% of patients, typically during the first dose. Symptoms include fever, chills, nausea, and blood pressure changes. Severe reactions, including anaphylaxis and angioedema, have been reported.

# 5. Dosage and Administration
### Initial Dosing (Intravenous)
The recommended starting dosage is **10 mg/kg** administered as an intravenous infusion over approximately one hour, **once every 2 weeks**.

### Maintenance Dosing
After 18 months of treatment, clinicians may consider transitioning patients to a maintenance regimen [FDA]:
*   **Intravenous:** 10 mg/kg once every 4 weeks.
*   **Subcutaneous (where available):** 360 mg once weekly via autoinjector.

### Missed Doses
If a dose is missed, it should be administered as soon as possible. For subcutaneous doses, if more than 6 days have passed since the missed dose, wait until the next scheduled day to resume.

### Treatment Discontinuation
Clinicians should consider discontinuing treatment if the patient progresses to moderate or severe Alzheimer's dementia, as effectiveness in these stages has not been established. [TGA]

# 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions (incidence ≥10% and higher than placebo) include:
*   **Infusion-related reactions** (26%)
*   **ARIA-H** (including microhemorrhages and superficial siderosis) (14%)
*   **ARIA-E** (edema/effusion) (13%)
*   **Headache** (11%)

Other noted reactions include falls, nausea, vomiting, and atrial fibrillation (3%). Subcutaneous administration may lead to injection site reactions such as erythema and induration.

# 7. Drug Interactions
No dedicated drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted. As a monoclonal antibody, lecanemab is degraded by proteolytic enzymes and is not expected to interact with cytochrome P450 enzymes or be affected by small-molecule medications.

# 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** There are no adequate data on use in pregnant women. Animal reproductive studies have not been conducted. Lecanemab is not recommended during pregnancy [TGA/FDA].
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if lecanemab is excreted in human milk. The developmental benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need for the drug and potential risks to the infant.

# 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** The mean central volume of distribution at steady state is 3.24 L.
*   **Elimination:** Lecanemab has a terminal half-life of **5 to 7 days**. It is cleared at a rate of 0.370 L/day.
*   **Steady State:** Reached after 6 weeks of bi-weekly dosing.
*   **Special Populations:** No clinically significant differences in pharmacokinetics were observed based on age, sex, race, or body weight. No dose adjustments are required for patients with renal or hepatic impairment as the drug is not eliminated through those routes.

# 10. Mechanism of Action
Lecanemab is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity to soluble Aβ protein aggregates (protofibrils) and fibrillar Aβ (amyloid plaques). By targeting these species, lecanemab facilitates the clearance of existing plaques and prevents the further accumulation of amyloid beta in the brain, thereby slowing clinical decline in early Alzheimer’s disease.

# 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened Vials/Autoinjectors:** Store in a refrigerator at **2°C to 8°C**.
*   **Light Protection:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
*   **Handling:** Do not freeze or shake.
*   **Subcutaneous Autoinjector (FDA):** If necessary, may be stored at room temperature (up to 25°C) for a single period of up to 14 days; do not return to the refrigerator after it reaches room temperature.
*   **Diluted IV Solution:** After dilution, immediate use is recommended. If not used immediately, store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C for up to 4 hours.
