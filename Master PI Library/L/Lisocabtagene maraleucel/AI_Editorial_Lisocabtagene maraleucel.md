# Editorial Product Information: Lisocabtagene maraleucel

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs), specifically clinical pharmacists and prescribers.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult the local regulatory approved PI before prescribing or administering this therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lisocabtagene maraleucel; a CD19-directed genetically modified autologous T cell immunotherapy. 
- **Chemical Class:** Autologous cellular immunotherapy.
- **Brand Name(s):** **BREYANZI** [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01XL07.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Suspension for intravenous infusion [US].
    - A single dose consists of a 1:1 ratio of CAR-positive viable T cells of the CD8 and CD4 components.
    - Each component is supplied separately in one to four single-dose 5 mL vials. Each mL contains ≥ 1.5 × 10⁶ to 70 × 10⁶ CAR-positive viable T cells [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Juno Therapeutics, Inc., a Bristol-Myers Squibb Company [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Lisocabtagene maraleucel** is indicated for the treatment of:
- **Large B-cell lymphoma (LBCL):** Adult patients with relapsed or refractory (R/R) LBCL, including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) not otherwise specified (including DLBCL arising from indolent lymphoma), high-grade B-cell lymphoma, primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma, and follicular lymphoma grade 3B, who have:
    - Disease refractory to first-line chemoimmunotherapy or relapse within 12 months of first-line chemoimmunotherapy; or
    - Disease refractory to first-line chemoimmunotherapy or relapse after first-line chemoimmunotherapy and are ineligible for hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) due to comorbidities or age; or
    - Relapsed or refractory disease after two or more lines of systemic therapy [US].
- **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL) or Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma (SLL):** Adult patients with R/R CLL or SLL who have received at least two prior lines of therapy, including a Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor and a B-cell lymphoma 2 (BCL-2) inhibitor [US-approved under accelerated approval].
- **Follicular Lymphoma (FL):** Adult patients with R/R FL who have received two or more prior lines of systemic therapy [US].
- **Mantle Cell Lymphoma (MCL):** Adult patients with R/R MCL who have received at least two prior lines of therapy, including a BTK inhibitor [US].
- **Marginal Zone Lymphoma (MZL):** Adult patients with R/R MZL who have received at least two prior lines of systemic therapy [US].

**Limitations of Use:** Not indicated for the treatment of primary central nervous system lymphoma [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
For autologous and intravenous use only. Dosing is based on the number of CAR-positive viable T cells.
- **LBCL (after one line of therapy):** 90 to 110 × 10⁶ CAR-positive viable T cells [US].
- **LBCL (after two or more lines of therapy):** 50 to 110 × 10⁶ CAR-positive viable T cells [US].
- **CLL/SLL, FL, MCL, and MZL:** 90 to 110 × 10⁶ CAR-positive viable T cells [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Pre-treatment:** Administer lymphodepleting chemotherapy (Fludarabine 30 mg/m²/day and Cyclophosphamide 300 mg/m²/day IV for 3 days) prior to infusion. Infuse **BREYANZI** 2 to 7 days after completion of lymphodepletion [US].
- **Premedication:** 30 to 60 minutes prior to infusion, administer acetaminophen (650 mg orally) and diphenhydramine (25-50 mg IV or orally) or another H1-antihistamine. Avoid prophylactic systemic corticosteroids [US].
- **Thawing:** Thaw all vials at the same time at room temperature. Administer within 2 hours of removal from frozen storage [US].
- **Infusion:** Do NOT use a leukodepleting filter. Flush tubing with normal saline before and after each component. Administer the CD8 component first, followed immediately by the CD4 component. Infusion rate is approximately 0.5 mL/minute [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS):** May be fatal or life-threatening. Do not administer to patients with active infection or inflammatory disorders.
- **Neurologic Toxicities:** May be fatal or life-threatening. Can occur concurrently with CRS, after CRS resolution, or in the absence of CRS.
- **Secondary Hematological Malignancies:** T cell malignancies have occurred following treatment with CD19-directed genetically modified autologous T cell immunotherapies.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Immune Effector Cell-Associated Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis-Like Syndrome (IEC-HS):** Fatal or life-threatening reactions have occurred [US].
- **Serious Infections:** Monitor for signs of infection. Prophylactic antimicrobials should be administered per institutional guidelines. Avoid use in patients with clinically significant active systemic infections [US].
- **Viral Reactivation:** Hepatitis B virus (HBV) reactivation can occur. Perform screening for HBV, HCV, and HIV before cell collection [US].
- **Prolonged Cytopenias:** Grade 3 or higher cytopenias may persist for several weeks [US].
- **Hypogammaglobulinemia:** B-cell aplasia and low IgG levels may occur; monitor and consider immunoglobulin replacement [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis (potentially due to DMSO), may occur [US].
- **Live Vaccines:** Safety not studied. Vaccination with live virus vaccines is not recommended for at least 6 weeks before lymphodepletion, during treatment, and until immune recovery [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Monitor patients daily for at least 7 days following infusion for CRS and neurologic toxicities [US].
- Instruct patients to remain within proximity of a healthcare facility for at least 2 weeks post-infusion [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (Incidence ≥ 30%)
- **LBCL:** Fever, CRS, fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, and nausea [US].
- **CLL/SLL:** CRS, encephalopathy, fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, nausea, edema, and diarrhea [US].
- **FL, MZL:** CRS [US].
- **MCL:** CRS, fatigue, musculoskeletal pain, and encephalopathy [US].

### Laboratory Abnormalities (Grade 3-4)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Neutrophil count decrease, lymphocyte count decrease, white blood cell decrease, hemoglobin decrease, and platelet count decrease [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **CRS:** Occurred in 56% of patients; Grade ≥ 3 in 3.4%. Median onset 5 days [US].
- **Neurologic Toxicity:** Occurred in 32% of patients; Grade ≥ 3 in 10%. Median onset 8 days [US].
- **IEC-HS:** Occurred in 0.9% of patients; may be fatal [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **HIV Nucleic Acid Tests:** Due to limited spans of identical genetic material between HIV and the lentivirus used for manufacture, some commercial HIV nucleic acid tests may yield false-positive results [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** No data available in pregnant women. Not recommended for pregnant women. Based on the mechanism of action, fetal toxicity (B-cell lymphocytopenia/hypogammaglobulinemia) may occur if cells cross the placenta [US]. 
- **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk and effects on the breastfed infant are unknown. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No data on the effects of **BREYANZI** on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No clinically important differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between patients ≥ 65 years and younger patients [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments for the cell therapy itself are not specified. HCPs should consult the fludarabine and cyclophosphamide PIs for adjustments during lymphodepletion [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A CD19-directed autologous T cell immunotherapy. The chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) contains an FMC63-derived scFv for CD19 recognition, a 4-1BB (CD137) costimulatory domain to enhance expansion, and a CD3-zeta activation domain to initiate T cell cytotoxicity against CD19-expressing cells [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** B-cell aplasia is an on-target effect and was observed in the majority of patients for up to 1 year [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Expansion:** Peak expansion in peripheral blood generally occurs 10–14 days post-infusion.
    - **Persistence:** Present in peripheral blood for an estimated median of 12.0 to 18.0 months across different indications [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1 (TRANSFORM):** Randomized study in LBCL after one line of therapy; showed superior event-free survival (EFS) compared to standard of care (10.1 months vs 2.3 months) [US].
- **Study 2 (PILOT):** Single-arm study in transplant-ineligible LBCL after one line of therapy; ORR was 80% with a 54% CR rate [US].
- **Study 3 (TRANSCEND NHL 001):** Evaluated R/R LBCL and MCL after 2+ lines; demonstrated durable responses (CR rate 54% in LBCL; 67.6% in MCL) [US].
- **Study 4 (TRANSCEND CLL 004):** Evaluated R/R CLL/SLL; ORR was 45% with a 20% CR rate [US].
- **Study 5 (TRANSCEND FL):** Evaluated R/R FL and MZL; ORR was 97.1% in FL and 95.5% in MZL [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store in the vapor phase of liquid nitrogen (≤ -130°C) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Handling:** Use universal precautions for handling genetically modified human blood cells. Do not filter or irradiate [US].
