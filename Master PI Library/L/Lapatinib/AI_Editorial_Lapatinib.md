# Editorial Product Information: Lapatinib

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of **lapatinib** based on official regulatory product information. It is intended as a concise reference for healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lapatinib (as ditosilate monohydrate), a 4-anilinoquinazoline kinase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** Tykerb [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EH01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 250 mg film-coated tablets (equivalent to 405 mg lapatinib ditosilate monohydrate).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Lapatinib** is indicated for the treatment of HER2-positive (ErbB2) overexpressing advanced or metastatic breast cancer in the following combinations:
- **With an Aromatase Inhibitor:** For post-menopausal women with hormone receptor-positive disease for whom hormonal therapy is indicated [Australian].
- **With Capecitabine:** For patients who have progressed after treatment with an anthracycline, a taxane, and prior trastuzumab in the metastatic setting [Australian].
- **With Paclitaxel:** For first-line treatment in the metastatic setting when trastuzumab is considered inappropriate [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **With Capecitabine:** 1250 mg (5 tablets) once daily continuously [Australian].
- **With Paclitaxel or Aromatase Inhibitors:** 1500 mg (6 tablets) once daily continuously [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be taken at least one hour before or one hour after food.
- The daily dose should be taken at the same time each day and should not be divided.
- **Lapatinib** bioavailability is significantly increased by food (3- to 4-fold increase in AUC) [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a dose is missed, it should not be replaced. The patient should resume with the next scheduled daily dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **lapatinib** or any of the excipients (e.g., microcrystalline cellulose, sodium starch glycollate, povidone) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiac Toxicity:** Associated with decreases in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF). LVEF must be evaluated prior to initiation and monitored every 8–12 weeks during treatment. **Lapatinib** should be interrupted if LVEF drops below the institutional lower limit of normal or if symptomatic heart failure occurs [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Severe hepatotoxicity has been reported (ALT/AST >3x ULN and bilirubin >1.5x ULN). Liver function tests should be monitored before treatment and every 4–6 weeks during therapy. Risk is increased in carriers of the HLA alleles DQA1*02:01 and DRB1*07:01 [Australian].
- **Diarrhoea:** Very common and may be severe. Early intervention with anti-diarrhoeal agents (e.g., loperamide) is critical. Interruption or dose reduction may be required [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** **Lapatinib** should be discontinued if pulmonary symptoms indicative of ILD/pneumonitis (Grade 3 or greater) occur [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** A concentration-dependent increase in the QTc interval has been observed. Use with caution in patients with hypokalaemia, hypomagnesemia, or congenital long QT syndrome [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Reactions:** Discontinue if life-threatening reactions (e.g., Stevens-Johnson syndrome or toxic epidermal necrolysis) are suspected [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea (may lead to dehydration), nausea, vomiting, rash (dermatitis acneform), anorexia, fatigue, dyspepsia, dry skin, stomatitis, palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia, alopecia, mucosal inflammation, pain in extremity, back pain, insomnia, epistaxis. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Decreased LVEF, hepatotoxicity, nail disorders (paronychia), headache, pruritus, skin fissures. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypersensitivity reactions (including anaphylaxis). |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Decreased LVEF, severe diarrhoea, severe hepatotoxicity, and serious skin reactions [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, grapefruit juice) Increase **lapatinib** exposure. If a strong inhibitor must be co-administered, consider a dose reduction to 500 mg/day [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, St. John's wort) Decrease **lapatinib** exposure. Doses should be titrated gradually based on tolerability [Australian].
- **Gastric pH-Affecting Drugs:** (e.g., esomeprazole) Solubility is pH-dependent. Solubility and absorption may decrease if gastric pH is increased.
- **Effect on Other Drugs:** **Lapatinib** inhibits CYP3A4, CYP2C8, and P-glycoprotein (P-gp) at clinical concentrations. It may increase the AUC of substrates such as midazolam (45% increase), digoxin (98% increase), and paclitaxel (23% increase) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category C. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal studies indicate potential for fetal harm (skeletal variations, abortions). Use is not recommended during pregnancy. Effective contraception should be used during and for 5 days after the last dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been established. It is recommended that women do not breastfeed while on therapy and for at least 5 days after the last dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed an increase in post-implantation loss at high doses. The effect on human fertility is unknown [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data. No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed, though greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied. Renal impairment is unlikely to affect pharmacokinetics as <2% of the dose is excreted via the kidneys [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. In patients with severe pre-existing impairment (Child-Pugh Class C), dose reduction is recommended (e.g., from 1250 mg to 750 mg/day or 1500 mg to 1000 mg/day) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A potent, reversible, and selective inhibitor of the intracellular tyrosine kinase domains of ErbB1 (EGFR) and ErbB2 (HER2). It inhibits tumour cell proliferation and growth in vitro and in vivo [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Highly variable; peak plasma concentration (Cmax) reached at approximately 4 hours. Steady state achieved within 6–7 days.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (>99%) to albumin and alpha-1 acid glycoprotein.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4 and CYP3A5.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily eliminated via faeces (median 27% as unchanged drug). The effective half-life at steady state is approximately 24 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **EGF100151:** A Phase III trial in HER2+ metastatic breast cancer showed that the combination of **lapatinib** + capecitabine significantly prolonged Time to Tumour Progression (TTP) (8.5 vs 4.5 months) and Progression-Free Survival (PFS) compared to capecitabine alone [Australian].
- **EGF104535:** A Phase III trial demonstrating that **lapatinib** + paclitaxel improved overall survival (27.8 vs 20.5 months) and PFS (9.7 vs 6.5 months) compared to paclitaxel alone as first-line therapy [Australian].
- **EGF30008:** A Phase III trial in post-menopausal women showing that **lapatinib** + letrozole significantly improved PFS (8.2 vs 3.0 months) compared to letrozole alone in the HER2+ subgroup [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C [Australian].
- Shelf life and specific expiry dates are found on the product packaging.
- Tablets should be returned to a pharmacist for safe disposal [Australian].
