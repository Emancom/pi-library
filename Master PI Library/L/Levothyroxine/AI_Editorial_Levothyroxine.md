# Editorial Product Information: Levothyroxine Sodium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levothyroxine sodium for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data to facilitate quick reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levothyroxine sodium (synthetic levo-isomer of thyroxine).
- **Chemical Class:** Thyroid hormone (synthetic T4).
- **Brand Name(s):** **APO-LEVOTHYROXINE** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H03AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Tablets:** 25 µg, 50 µg, 75 µg, 100 µg, 125 µg, 150 µg, and 200 µg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Southern Cross Pharma Pty Ltd; Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Management of demonstrated thyroid hormone deficiency (hypothyroidism) [Australian].
- Suppression of thyrotropin (TSH) for the management of TSH-responsive tumours of the thyroid [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- **Dosing Schedule:** Single daily dose, taken in the morning on an empty stomach.
- **Timing:** At least 30 minutes, preferably 60 minutes, before food or other medications. Fasting is essential as food impairs absorption [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Dosage must be individualised based on clinical response and biochemical monitoring (TSH and serum thyroxine).

### Adult Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** 50 µg to 100 µg daily.
- **Titration:** Increase by 25 µg to 50 µg at intervals of no less than 4 weeks.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 100 µg to 200 µg daily.
- **Elderly/Cardiac Patients:** In patients >60 years or those with ischaemic heart disease, initiate at 25 µg to 50 µg daily.

### Switching Brands
- **Bioequivalence Warning:** **APO-LEVOTHYROXINE** is not bioequivalent on a same-dose basis with **ELTROXIN**. Switching between these brands requires TSH monitoring and potential dose adjustment [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to thyroxine (rare) [Australian].
- Untreated hyperthyroidism or thyrotoxicosis [Australian].
- Uncorrected primary or secondary adrenal insufficiency [Australian].
- Acute myocardial infarction (uncomplicated by hypothyroidism), acute myocarditis, or acute pancarditis [Australian].
- Combination therapy of levothyroxine and antithyroid agents for hyperthyroidism during pregnancy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bioequivalence:** Avoid interchanging brands (specifically **APO-LEVOTHYROXINE** and **ELTROXIN**) without a monitoring plan [Australian].
- **Cardiac Disorders:** Extreme caution is required in patients with cardiovascular disease. If cardiovascular effects occur, reduce the dose. Initial doses as low as 12.5–25 µg may be required for those with angina [Australian].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Corticosteroid replacement must precede levothyroxine initiation to avoid an Addisonian crisis [Australian].
- **Obesity:** Levothyroxine must not be used for weight loss. In euthyroid patients, hormonal doses are ineffective for weight reduction; high doses can cause life-threatening toxicity [Australian].
- **Bone Mineral Density:** Long-term therapy (especially at suppressive doses in post-menopausal women) is associated with increased bone resorption and decreased mineral density [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference (Biotin):** Biotin may cause falsely high or low results in thyroid immunoassays (biotin/streptavidin interaction). Discontinue high-dose biotin or inform laboratory personnel before testing [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally associated with excessive dosing, manifesting as symptoms of hyperthyroidism or thyrotoxicosis.

| System | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiovascular** | Palpitations, tachycardia, chest pain, arrhythmias, increased BP, myocardial ischaemia/infarction, cardiac failure. |
| **Nervous System** | Irritability, anxiety, tremors, insomnia, headache, restlessness, psychosis, seizures (rare). |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Abdominal cramps, nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting. |
| **Dermatological** | Sweating, flushing, rash, urticaria, angioedema, transient hair loss (children). |
| **Metabolic/Other** | Weight loss, heat intolerance, pyrexia, fatigue, decreased bone mineral density. |
| **Immune** | Hypersensitivity reactions, anaphylaxis. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Absorption Inhibitors:** Ion-exchange resins (cholestyramine, colestipol), sucralfate, calcium/aluminium/magnesium/iron supplements, proton pump inhibitors, and orlistat decrease absorption. Separate administration by 4–6 hours [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** Levothyroxine increases the effects of anticoagulants; dose reduction of the anticoagulant may be required based on prothrombin time [Australian].
- **Antidiabetic Agents:** May increase requirements for insulin or oral hypoglycaemics [Australian].
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Antiepileptics (phenytoin, carbamazepine), rifampicin, and St John’s Wort increase hepatic clearance, potentially requiring higher levothyroxine doses [Australian].
- **Estrogens:** May increase thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG), increasing levothyroxine requirements in hypothyroid patients [Australian].
- **Beta-blockers (e.g., Propranolol):** May inhibit the peripheral conversion of T4 to T3 [Australian].
- **Lab Interference:** Biotin (as noted in Section 5).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category A** [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Requirements typically increase during pregnancy (often by 25–40%). Serum TSH should be monitored every 3–4 weeks during the first and second trimesters [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levothyroxine is excreted into breast milk in negligible amounts and is considered compatible with breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No information available on human fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric Use
- **Neonates:** Monitor for circulatory collapse in very low birth weight preterm neonates due to immature adrenal function.
- **Growth:** Monitor for premature epiphyses fusion. 
- **Dosing Guide (Congenital/Acquired Hypothyroidism):**

| Age | Approx. Dose/kg/day |
| :--- | :--- |
| 0–6 months | 8 µg |
| 6–12 months | 6 µg |
| 1–5 years | 5 µg |
| 6–12 years | 4 µg |
| >12 years | 2 µg |

### Elderly Use
- Patients are more sensitive to thyroid hormones. Start with low doses (25–50 µg) and titrate slowly to avoid sudden increases in metabolic demand [Australian].

### Renal and Hepatic Impairment
- No specific dose adjustments are evidenced for renal failure or cirrhosis, but thyroid function tests require careful interpretation in these populations [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levothyroxine is a synthetic T4 that is peripherally converted to T3 (the active hormone). It regulates the metabolic rate of body tissues and is essential for cell growth and differentiation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 50% to 75% absorbed, primarily in the jejunum, ileum, and duodenum.
    - **Distribution:** Extensively protein-bound to thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG), pre-albumin, and albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily in the liver and kidneys to triiodothyronine (T3).
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is approximately 6–7 days in euthyroid patients (9–10 days in hypothyroidism; 3–4 days in hyperthyroidism). Excreted in faeces [Australian].
