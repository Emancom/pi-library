# Editorial Product Information: Leflunomide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- **Note:** At the time of this editorial preparation, only the Australian PI was provided for review. As such, regional comparisons (e.g., with US, European, or Canadian sources) are restricted to the available data.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Leflunomide
- **Chemical Class:** Isoxazole immunomodulatory agent
- **Brand Names:** APO-LEFLUNOMIDE [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L04AA13 (Not specified in available source)
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 10 mg tablets: White, round, biconvex [Australian]
    - 20 mg tablets: Yellow, round, biconvex with a scoreline on one side [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Active Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Indicated for the treatment of adults with active rheumatoid arthritis [Australian].
- **Active Psoriatic Arthritis:** Indicated for the treatment of active psoriatic arthritis. Note: Not indicated for psoriasis independent of arthritic manifestations [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Loading Dose:** Therapy is typically initiated with 100 mg once daily for three days. To reduce the risk of hepatic or haematological toxicity (especially when used with methotrexate or other immunosuppressants), the loading dose may be omitted [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dosing:**
    - **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** 20 mg once daily. If not well tolerated, the dose may be reduced to 10 mg daily. Doses exceeding 20 mg/day are not recommended [Australian].
    - **Psoriatic Arthritis:** 20 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole with sufficient liquid. Absorption is unaffected by food intake [Australian].
- **Monitoring for Adjustments:** Dose adjustments or discontinuation may be required based on liver enzyme and haematological parameter monitoring [Australian].
- **Therapeutic Response:** Clinical improvement may be observed within four weeks, with potential for further improvement over four to six months of treatment [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to leflunomide, teriflunomide, or any tablet excipients [Australian].
- Impaired liver function [Australian].
- Severe immunodeficiency states (e.g., AIDS) [Australian].
- Significant bone marrow impairment or significant anaemia, leukopenia, or thrombocytopenia due to causes other than RA or PsA [Australian].
- Severe, uncontrolled infections [Australian].
- Pregnancy and women of childbearing potential not using reliable contraception [Australian].
- Breastfeeding [Australian].
- Severe hypoproteinaemia [Australian].
- History of Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), or erythema multiforme [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Severe liver injury, including fatal cases, has been reported. Most occur within the first six months. **Monitoring:** ALT and AST must be checked at baseline, monthly for the first six months, and every 6–8 weeks thereafter. If co-administered with methotrexate, monthly monitoring is mandatory [Australian].
- **Haematotoxicity:** Rare cases of pancytopenia and agranulocytosis have occurred. **Monitoring:** CBC (including differential and platelets) must be checked at baseline, monthly for the first six months, and every 6–8 weeks thereafter [Australian].
- **Long Half-Life and Washout:** The active metabolite (A771726) has a half-life of 1–4 weeks. If serious adverse effects occur or pregnancy is desired, an accelerated washout procedure using cholestyramine or activated charcoal is required [Australian].
- **Skin Reactions:** Risk of SJS, TEN, and DRESS (Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms). Therapy must be stopped immediately if skin/mucosal reactions are suspected [Australian].
- **Infections and Tuberculosis:** Evaluation for latent or active TB is required before initiation. Risk of opportunistic infections and sepsis is increased [Australian].
- **Respiratory:** Rare, potentially fatal interstitial lung disease (ILD) has been reported. New or worsening pulmonary symptoms require investigation [Australian].
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Reported in some patients, particularly those over 60 years old or with diabetes [Australian].
- **Blood Pressure:** Blood pressure must be checked before starting and monitored periodically during treatment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Diarrhoea, elevated liver enzymes (ALT/AST), hair loss (alopecia), rash, hypertension [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, weight loss, headache, dizziness, asthenia, paraesthesia, bronchitis, pruritus, eczema, dry skin, tenosynovitis [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Urticaria, taste disturbances, anxiety, tendon rupture, hypophosphataemia, thrombocytopenia (< 100 x 10⁹/L) [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Eosinophilia, leukopenia (< 2 x 10⁹/L), pancytopenia, severe hypertension, hepatitis, jaundice/cholestasis, ILD [Australian].
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** Agranulocytosis, anaphylaxis, severe liver injury (hepatic failure/necrosis), SJS/TEN, DRESS, vasculitis, pancreatitis, peripheral neuropathy [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Methotrexate:** Increased risk of hepatotoxicity when combined; no pharmacokinetic interaction identified [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Rare reports of increased prothrombin time. Close INR monitoring is recommended [Australian].
- **Rifampicin:** May increase peak levels of the active metabolite A771726 by approximately 40% [Australian].
- **CYP2C8 Substrates (e.g., Repaglinide, Paclitaxel):** A771726 inhibits CYP2C8; monitor for increased exposure [Australian].
- **CYP1A2 Substrates (e.g., Duloxetine, Theophylline):** A771726 may be a weak inducer; potential for reduced efficacy of these agents [Australian].
- **OAT3 Substrates (e.g., Cefaclor, Penicillin G, Methotrexate):** A771726 inhibits OAT3; caution advised [Australian].
- **BCRP and OATP1B1/B3 Substrates (e.g., Rosuvastatin):** Increased exposure possible. Rosuvastatin dose should not exceed starting dose [Australian].
- **Live Vaccines:** Not recommended. Wait at least 6 months after stopping leflunomide before administering a live vaccine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category X [Australian]**. Leflunomide is teratogenic. It is contraindicated. 
- **Pregnancy Washout:** Women wishing to become pregnant must undergo a washout (cholestyramine 8 g TID for 11 days or charcoal 50 g QID for 11 days). Two separate plasma tests must confirm A771726 levels < 0.02 mg/L at least 14 days apart [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Animal studies indicate leflunomide or its metabolites pass into breast milk [Australian].
- **Male Fertility:** Available data do not suggest increased risk of male-mediated fetal toxicity, but men wishing to father a child should consider a washout procedure to minimize potential risk [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for use in patients under 18 years. Safety and effectiveness have not been fully evaluated [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Caution in patients over 75 years; safety and efficacy data are limited in this group [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Caution is advised. In single-dose studies, the free fraction of the active metabolite was doubled in patients with chronic renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated due to the risk of hepatotoxicity and the role of the liver in drug metabolism [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Leflunomide is rapidly converted to its active metabolite, A771726. It inhibits the enzyme dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH), which is essential for *de novo* pyrimidine synthesis. This leads to an anti-proliferative effect on activated lymphocytes [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** ~80% bioavailable. Peak levels of A771726 occur 6–12 hours post-dose [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Extensively protein-bound (>99.3%) to albumin [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised in the gut wall and liver to A771726 [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted in both urine (43%) and faeces (48%). Biliary recycling contributes to the long half-life (~2 weeks) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Efficacy was established in three major trials (MN301, US301, MN302) comparing leflunomide to placebo, sulfasalazine, and methotrexate. Leflunomide was significantly superior to placebo and comparable to the active comparators in reducing RA signs and symptoms (ACR20/50/70 responses) and slowing joint destruction [Australian].
- **Psoriatic Arthritis:** A 6-month randomised study (n=188) showed 59% of leflunomide patients achieved a PsARC response compared to 29.7% for placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C in a dry place [Australian].
- Keep the container tightly closed [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** S4 — Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
