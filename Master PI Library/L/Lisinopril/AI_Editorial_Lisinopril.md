# Editorial Product Information: Lisinopril

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lisinopril, synthesised from official product information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lisinopril dihydrate
- **Chemical Class:** Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor; synthetic peptide derivative (lysine analogue of enalaprilat).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Lisinopril [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C09AA03
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral Tablets: 5 mg, 10 mg, and 20 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Management of mild to moderate essential hypertension. It may be used as monotherapy or in combination with other antihypertensive classes [Australian].
- **Congestive Heart Failure:** Treatment of heart failure, typically as adjunctive therapy with diuretics [Australian].
- **Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI):** Treatment of hemodynamically stable patients (systolic BP >100 mmHg, no cardiogenic shock) within 24 hours of onset to improve survival and prevent left ventricular dysfunction [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Administer as a single daily dose, with or without food [Australian].
- **Requirement for 2.5 mg Dose:** If a 2.5 mg dose is required, use alternative products; 5 mg tablets in this range should not be halved as they lack a breakline [Australian].

### Hypertension
- **Initial Dose:** 5 mg to 10 mg once daily.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 10 mg to 20 mg once daily.
- **Maximum Dose:** Up to 40 mg daily may be required.
- **Dose Titration:** Adjust at 2 to 4-week intervals based on clinical response.
- **Diuretic-Treated Patients:** Discontinue diuretics 2–3 days prior to initiation if possible. If diuretics must continue, initiate lisinopril at 2.5 mg [Australian].

### Congestive Heart Failure
- **Initial Dose:** 2.5 mg once daily under close medical supervision.
- **Titration:** Increase by increments of no more than 10 mg at intervals of at least 2 weeks.
- **Target Range:** 5 mg to 20 mg once daily [Australian].

### Acute Myocardial Infarction
- **Initiation (within 24 hours):** 5 mg orally.
- **Subsequent Doses:** 5 mg after 24 hours, 10 mg after 48 hours, followed by 10 mg daily.
- **Duration:** Continue for 6 weeks.
- **Low Systolic BP (≤120 mmHg):** Initiate with a lower dose of 2.5 mg [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to lisinopril or any excipients.
- History of hereditary or idiopathic angioedema.
- History of angioedema associated with previous ACE inhibitor therapy.
- Pregnancy [Australian].
- Concomitant use with aliskiren in patients with diabetes mellitus (Type I or II) or renal impairment (GFR <60 mL/min/1.73m²) [Australian].
- Concomitant use with high-flux polyacrylonitrile-metalylsulphonate membranes (e.g., AN69) during hemodialysis or LDL apheresis with dextran sulphate [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Angioedema:** May occur at any time during therapy (most common in the first week). Laryngeal, glottic, or tongue involvement may be fatal; requires immediate emergency management (e.g., adrenaline). Risk is higher in Afro-Caribbean patients [Australian].
- **Anaphylactoid Reactions:** Reported during hymenoptera venom desensitisation and dialysis with high-flux membranes.
- **Symptomatic Hypotension:** Likely in volume or salt-depleted patients (e.g., those on diuretics, vomiting, diarrhea, or dialysis). In AMI, lisinopril should be withdrawn if prolonged hypotension (systolic BP <90 mmHg for >1 hour) persists [Australian].
- **Neutropenia/Agranulocytosis:** Use with caution in patients with collagen vascular disease or renal impairment; periodic haematologic monitoring is recommended [Australian].
- **Dual Blockade of the RAAS:** Concomitant use of ACE inhibitors with ARBs or aliskiren increases risks of hypotension, hyperkalaemia, and renal failure; generally avoid [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Rare association with a syndrome starting with cholestatic jaundice progressing to fulminant hepatic necrosis. Discontinue if jaundice or marked liver enzyme elevations occur [Australian].
- **Cough:** Characteristically dry and non-productive; resolves upon discontinuation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally mild and transient.
- **Very Common (>10%):** Dizziness (especially in CHF patients, reported at 14.2%) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Headache, hypotension, cough, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, rash, asthenia/fatigue, renal dysfunction [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Vertigo, paraesthesia, mood alterations, sleep disturbances, palpitations, chest pain, syncope, dyspepsia, hyperkalaemia, taste disturbance [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Angioedema, psoriasis, alopecia, urticaria, acute renal failure, uraemia, gynaecomastia, SIADH, hyponatraemia [Australian].
- **Serious Effects:** Myocardial infarction or cerebrovascular accident (secondary to excessive hypotension), pancreatitis, hepatic failure, and bone marrow depression (anaemia, thrombocytopenia, leucopenia).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Diuretics:** Additive hypotensive effects.
- **Lithium:** Increased serum lithium levels and risk of toxicity.
- **NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors):** May attenuate antihypertensive effect and increase risk of renal functional deterioration, especially in the elderly or volume-depleted [Australian].
- **Potassium-Sparing Diuretics/Supplements:** Increased risk of hyperkalaemia. Includes spironolactone, triamterene, amiloride, and potassium-containing salt substitutes [Australian].
- **Antidiabetics (Insulin/Oral agents):** Potential for increased blood glucose-lowering effects and hypoglycaemia, particularly during the first weeks of therapy [Australian].
- **mTOR and NEP Inhibitors:** Concomitant use (e.g., sirolimus, everolimus, racecadotril) increases the risk of angioedema [Australian].
- **Gold:** Nitritoid reactions (flushing, nausea, hypotension) reported with injectable gold therapy [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Exposure in the second and third trimesters is associated with foetotoxicity (renal failure, hypotension, hyperkalaemia, skull hypoplasia) and neonatal death. If pregnancy is detected, lisinopril must be discontinued immediately.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if lisinopril is secreted in human milk. Use is not recommended in nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on reproductive performance were observed in rat studies at doses up to 300 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children [Australian].
- **Elderly:** AUC and peak plasma levels are approximately doubled in older patients compared to younger adults; dose adjustments should be made with caution [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing must be adjusted based on creatinine clearance (CrCl):
    - **CrCl 30–70 mL/min:** 5–10 mg/day start.
    - **CrCl 10–30 mL/min:** 2.5–5 mg/day start.
    - **CrCl <10 mL/min (including dialysis):** 2.5 mg/day start [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with particular caution; baseline liver function tests and close monitoring are advised [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Peptidyl dipeptidase inhibitor. It prevents the conversion of angiotensin I to the potent vasoconstrictor angiotensin II, leading to reduced vasopressor activity and decreased aldosterone secretion [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Results in reduced blood pressure in both supine and standing positions without compensatory tachycardia. Onset of activity occurs within 1–2 hours, with peak effects at approximately 6 hours [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Mean extent of absorption is ~25%. Not affected by food.
    - **Distribution:** Does not bind to serum proteins other than ACE. Crosses the blood-brain barrier poorly.
    - **Metabolism:** None; lisinopril is not metabolised.
    - **Excretion:** Excreted entirely unchanged in the urine. Effective accumulation half-life is 12.6 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **GISSI-3 Trial:** Evaluated 19,394 patients post-AMI. Lisinopril initiated within 24 hours showed an 11% reduction in mortality at 6 weeks compared to control (6.4% vs 7.2%) [Australian].
- **EUCLID Study:** Investigated normotensive IDDM patients; lisinopril reduced the urinary albumin excretion rate (AER) by 18.8% over 24 months [Australian].
- **ATLAS Study (CHF):** Comparison of high (32.5/35 mg) vs. low (2.5/5 mg) doses in heart failure. High-dose therapy showed a 12% risk reduction in the combined endpoint of mortality and all-cause hospitalisation [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Handling:** Unused medicine should be returned to a pharmacy for disposal [Australian].
