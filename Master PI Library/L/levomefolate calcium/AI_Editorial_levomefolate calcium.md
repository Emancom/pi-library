## 3. Approved Indications
- Adjunctive nutritional management in Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) for individuals with suboptimal L‑methyltetrahydrofolate levels in cerebrospinal fluid, plasma, and/or red blood cells, particularly as an adjunct to antidepressant therapy. [US]
- Adjunctive nutritional management in schizophrenia for individuals who have or are at risk for hyperhomocysteinemia and who present with negative symptoms and/or cognitive impairment, particularly as an adjunct for individuals stabilized on antipsychotics. [US]
- Indication statement: Intended for the distinct nutritional requirements of the above patient populations as a medical food; indicated regardless of MTHFR C677T polymorphism genotype. [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- Usual adult dose: 7.5 mg to 15 mg once daily, given with or without food, or as directed under medical supervision. [US]

### Dosing by indication
- Major Depressive Disorder (adjunctive): 7.5–15 mg once daily (use as adjunct to antidepressant as clinically indicated). [US]
- Schizophrenia (adjunctive): 7.5–15 mg once daily (use as adjunct to antipsychotic therapy in stabilized patients as clinically indicated). [US]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Masking of vitamin B12 deficiency: Folic acid administered at daily doses above 0.1 mg may obscure the hematologic manifestations of vitamin B12 deficiency while not correcting neurologic manifestations. Levomefolate calcium may be less likely than folic acid to mask vitamin B12 deficiency; nevertheless, folate therapy alone is inadequate for treatment of vitamin B12 deficiency. Clinicians should consider assessment for vitamin B12 deficiency where clinically appropriate. [US]
- Bipolar disorder: A major depressive episode may be the initial presentation of bipolar disorder. Treating a bipolar patient with an antidepressant alone may precipitate mixed/manic episodes. Levomefolate calcium is not an antidepressant, but 5‑MTHF has been shown to enhance antidepressant effects; caution is recommended in patients with a history of bipolar illness and patients with depressive symptoms should be screened for bipolar disorder prior to use. [US]
- Allergic reactions: Allergic reactions have been reported with oral levomefolate calcium; be alert for signs of hypersensitivity. [US]
- Medical food status: Product is a medical food intended for dietary management of specific metabolic/nutritional imbalances and should be used under medical supervision. [US]

### Monitoring requirements
- Evaluate for signs of vitamin B12 deficiency when clinically indicated.
- Screen for bipolar disorder in patients presenting with depressive symptoms prior to initiating adjunctive therapy.
- Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity/allergic reaction during therapy.
- For patients on interacting medications (see Section 8), monitor relevant clinical parameters (e.g., antiepileptic drug levels, seizure control) as clinically appropriate. [US]

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use caution in patients receiving anticonvulsant therapy due to potential interactions affecting folate levels and anticonvulsant pharmacokinetics. [US]
- Use caution in patients receiving drugs or conditions known to reduce folate status (see Section 8). [US]

## 8. Drug Interactions
- General note: Inform patients to tell their healthcare provider about all concomitant medications. Specific interactions reported in available sources include:

- Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) — including, but not limited to, phenytoin, carbamazepine, primidone, valproic acid, phenobarbital and lamotrigine:
  - AEDs have been shown to impair folate absorption and increase folate metabolism.
  - Concurrent use of folic acid has been associated with enhanced phenytoin metabolism and lowered phenytoin blood levels with risk of breakthrough seizures. Clinicians should be aware of potential effects on AED pharmacokinetics and therapeutic effect. [US]

- Capecitabine:
  - Folinic acid (5‑formyltetrahydrofolate) may increase the toxicity of capecitabine. The source lists this interaction in the context of folate agents; caution is advised when combining folate derivatives with capecitabine. [US]

- Dihydrofolate reductase inhibitors (DHFR inhibitors) — aminopterin, methotrexate, pyrimethamine, triamterene, trimethoprim:
  - DHFR inhibitors block conversion of folic acid to active forms and lower plasma and red blood cell folate levels; coadministration may affect folate status. [US]

- Fluoxetine:
  - Fluoxetine exerts a noncompetitive inhibition of 5‑MTHF active transport in the intestine; possible reduction in intestinal uptake of 5‑MTHF noted. [US]

- Isotretinoin:
  - Reduced folate levels have occurred in some patients taking isotretinoin. [US]

- Nonsteroidal anti‑inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) — ibuprofen, naproxen, indomethacin, sulindac:
  - NSAIDs have been shown in laboratory experiments to inhibit some folate‑dependent enzymes. [US]

- Oral contraceptives:
  - Serum folate levels may be depressed by oral contraceptive therapy. [US]

- Methylprednisolone:
  - Reduced serum folate levels have been observed after treatment with methylprednisolone. [US]

- Pancreatic enzyme preparations:
  - Reduced folate levels have occurred in some patients taking pancreatic extracts/enzymes. [US]

- Pentamidine:
  - Reduced folate levels have been seen with prolonged intravenous pentamidine. [US]

- Metformin:
  - Metformin treatment in patients with type 2 diabetes decreases serum folate. [US]

- Warfarin:
  - Warfarin therapy can produce significant impairment in folate status after approximately 6 months of therapy. [US]

- Interaction management notes (derived from reported interaction observations):
  - Be aware of potential reductions in folate status with the listed agents and medical conditions; monitor and manage folate status as clinically appropriate.
  - For AEDs (particularly phenytoin), consider monitoring drug levels and seizure control if folate supplementation is used concomitantly. [US]

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **levomefolate calcium** provides a biologically active form of folate (L‑5‑methyltetrahydrofolate, 5‑MTHF) intended to address specific metabolic/nutritional imbalances associated with depression and schizophrenia by supplying the active folate moiety required for one‑carbon metabolism and methylation reactions. 5‑MTHF has been shown to enhance the antidepressant effects of conventional antidepressants.
