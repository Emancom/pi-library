# Editorial Product Information: Lorazepam

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lorazepam (a benzodiazepine)
- **Brand Names:** APO-Lorazepam [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Immediate-release tablets: 1 mg and 2.5 mg [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Southern Cross Pharma Pty Ltd (Sponsor); Arrotex Pharmaceuticals (Distributor) [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anxiety Disorders:** Management of anxiety disorders or short-term relief of anxiety symptoms, including anxiety associated with depressive symptoms [Australian]. 
- **Note on Anxiety:** Anxiety associated with the stress of daily life typically does not require pharmacological intervention with anxiolytics [Australian].
- **Pre-surgical Medication:** For administration on the night before surgery and/or 1–2 hours prior to the procedure [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Anxiety:** Average daily dosage is 2–3 mg, administered in divided doses. The range is 1–10 mg per day [Australian].
- **Insomnia (due to anxiety/stress):** 1–2 mg as a single daily dose, usually at bedtime [Australian].
- **Pre-surgical:** 2–4 mg the night before and/or 1–2 hours before the procedure [Australian].

### Duration of Treatment
- Treatment for anxiety or insomnia should not exceed **4 weeks**, including the dose tapering phase. Continuous long-term use is not recommended [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Dosage must be individualised based on patient response and previous exposure to anxiolytic agents (higher initial doses may be needed for those previously treated) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to benzodiazepines [Australian].
- Chronic Obstructive Airways Disease (COPD) with incipient respiratory failure [Australian].
- Sleep apnoea [Australian].
- Use as monotherapy in patients with depression or anxiety associated with depression due to the risk of suicide [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided sources.
- **CNS Depression:** Patients must be cautioned against operating heavy machinery or motor vehicles until they are certain the medication does not cause dizziness or drowsiness. Impairment may persist the day following use [Australian].
- **Tolerance and Dependence:** Tolerance to sedative effects can develop. Dependence may occur, and withdrawal symptoms (ranging from insomnia and anxiety to convulsions and psychosis) can manifest even after short-term use (one week). Doses should be tapered gradually [Australian].
- **Suicidality:** Benzodiazepines may unmask or aggravate suicidal tendencies in patients with depression or schizophrenia [Australian].
- **Paradoxical Reactions:** Use should be discontinued if the patient experiences acute rage, stimulation, excitement, or agitation [Australian].
- **Memory Impairment:** Transient amnesia or memory impairment (anterograde amnesia) has been reported [Australian].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** Periodic blood counts and liver function tests are recommended during long-term therapy, as rare instances of blood dyscrasias and elevated liver enzymes have occurred [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are most common at the start of therapy and typically decrease with continued use or dose reduction.

| System Organ Class | Common Reactions | Less Common Reactions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous System** | Sedation, dizziness, anterograde amnesia | Disorientation, headache, sleep disturbances |
| **Musculo-Skeletal** | Unsteadiness, weakness | Not specified |
| **Psychiatric** | Not specified | Agitation, depression |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Not specified | Nausea, vomiting, change in appetite |
| **Other** | Not specified | Rash, dry mouth, hypersalivation, eye-function disturbances |

- **Serious Effects:** Paradoxical reactions (stimulation, rage) and significant respiratory depression, especially in overdose or with concomitant CNS depressants [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Additive CNS depressant effects occur when combined with alcohol, barbiturates, sedatives, tricyclic antidepressants, non-selective MAO inhibitors, phenothiazines, antipsychotics, muscle relaxants, antihistamines, narcotic analgesics, and anaesthetics [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** The Cytochrome P450 system is not involved in the disposition of lorazepam; therefore, P450-mediated interactions are not expected [Australian].
- **Anticonvulsants:** Interactions may alter serum concentrations of either the benzodiazepine or the anticonvulsant; frequent monitoring is recommended [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** Lorazepam may potentiate the effects of drugs with anticholinergic properties (e.g., atropine) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Benzodiazepines cross the placenta and may cause hypotonia, reduced respiratory function, and hypothermia in the newborn. Avoid use during the first trimester. Use in late pregnancy may require neonatal ventilation.
- **Breastfeeding:** Lorazepam is excreted in human milk. Use with caution as it may cause drowsiness and feeding difficulties in the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) at 20 mg/kg showed no impairment of fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not established or recommended for children under 16 years of age [Australian].
- **Paediatric Neurotoxicity:** Note that exposure to anaesthetic/sedative drugs in early life or late pregnancy may affect brain development, though the clinical significance is yet to be determined [Australian].
- **Elderly/Debilitated:** Initial dosage should be limited to 1–2 mg/day in divided doses. These patients are more susceptible to sedation, ataxia, and dizziness, increasing the risk of falls [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution and consider dose reduction. In patients with hepatitis or alcoholic cirrhosis, pharmacokinetics remain largely unaltered, but caution is advised in renal impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potentiates synaptic or pre-synaptic inhibition mediated by gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) within the central nervous system [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed; peak plasma concentrations reached in ~2 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** ~90% protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised in the liver, primarily into the inactive glucuronide [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is approximately 12–16 hours. 70–75% is excreted as glucuronide in the urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data were specified in the available source documents [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C. Protect from light and maintain in the original container [Australian].
- Shelf life is specified on the product packaging [Australian].
