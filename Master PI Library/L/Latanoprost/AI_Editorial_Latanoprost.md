# Editorial Product Information: Latanoprost

## Editorial Note
- This editorial was developed using the following jurisdiction’s Product Information:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Australian Product Information – APO-LATANOPROST (Latanoprost) Eye Drops.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals based on available regulatory data.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Latanoprost (a prostaglandin F2α analogue).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective prostanoid FP receptor agonist.
- **Brand Names:** APO-LATANOPROST [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** S01EE01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile, isotonic ophthalmic solution containing 50 micrograms/mL of latanoprost. One drop contains approximately 1.5 micrograms of the active ingredient.
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Reduction of elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with:
    - Open-angle glaucoma.
    - Ocular hypertension.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Elderly):** The recommended dose is one eye drop in the affected eye(s) once daily.
- **Administration Timing:** Optimal efficacy is achieved when administered in the evening.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - To minimize systemic absorption, pressure should be applied to the tear duct (nasolacrimal occlusion) immediately following application.
    - If using multiple topical ophthalmic medications, they must be administered at least five minutes apart.
    - Contact lenses should be removed prior to instillation and may be reinserted after 15 minutes.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, treatment should continue with the next scheduled dose as normal.
- **Maximum Dose:** Dosage must not exceed once daily; more frequent administration has been shown to decrease the IOP-lowering effect.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to latanoprost or any excipients (including benzalkonium chloride, sodium chloride, and phosphate buffers).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Iris Pigmentation Changes:** Latanoprost may cause a permanent increase in brown iris pigment by increasing melanin content in stromal melanocytes. This is most common in patients with mixed-colored irides (e.g., blue-brown, grey-brown). Changes are typically concentric around the pupil and may lead to heterochromia in unilateral treatment.
- **Eyelid and Eyelash Changes:**
    - **Eyelashes:** May increase in length, thickness, pigmentation, and number. These changes are typically reversible upon discontinuation.
    - **Eyelid Skin:** Darkening of the palpebral skin may occur (reversible).
- **Macular Oedema:** Cases of macular oedema, including cystoid macular oedema, have been reported. Use with caution in aphakic patients, pseudophakic patients with a torn posterior lens capsule, or patients with known risk factors for macular oedema.
- **Glaucoma Subtypes:** There is limited or no clinical experience in treating inflammatory, neovascular, congenital, or acute closed-angle glaucoma.
- **Herpetic Keratitis:** Use with caution in patients with a history of herpetic keratitis. Avoid use in cases of active herpes simplex keratitis.
- **Asthma:** Use with caution in patients with severe or brittle asthma, as clinical experience is limited.
- **Benzalkonium Chloride:** The preservative may be absorbed by soft contact lenses and may cause eye irritation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily ocular and related to the site of administration.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Iris hyperpigmentation; eye irritation (burning, stinging, itching, grittiness, foreign body sensation); eyelash/vellus hair changes. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Mild to moderate ocular hyperaemia; punctate keratitis (usually asymptomatic); blepharitis; eye pain; conjunctivitis; blurred vision; eyelid oedema. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Iritis; uveitis; keratitis; macular oedema; photophobia; chest pain; asthma; dyspnoea. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Corneal oedema/erosion; periorbital oedema; trichiasis; deepening of the eyelid sulcus; acute asthma attacks; herpetic keratitis. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Macular oedema, iritis/uveitis, and acute asthma exacerbations.
- **Post-Marketing Reports:** Angina, palpitations, unstable angina, nausea, vomiting, and pseudophakic iris cysts.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Prostaglandin Analogues:** Concomitant ophthalmic use of two or more prostaglandins or derivatives is not recommended, as paradoxical elevations in IOP have been reported.
- **Thiomersal:** In vitro studies show that precipitation occurs when latanoprost is mixed with eye drops containing thiomersal. If used concurrently, space administration by at least five minutes.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B3 [Australian]:** Safety in human pregnancy has not been established. Animal studies (rabbits) have shown embryo-fetal toxicity (total litter resorption) at high intravenous doses. Latanoprost should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Latanoprost and its metabolites may pass into breast milk. Use with caution and only when the benefits clearly outweigh the potential risks to the nursing infant.
- **Fertility:** No effect on male or female fertility was observed in animal studies (rats) at intravenous doses up to 250 µg/kg.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in children is not recommended as safety and efficacy have not been studied in this population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustments are required; safety and efficacy profiles are similar to younger adults.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, systemic exposure is low following topical application.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Latanoprost is a selective prostanoid FP receptor agonist. It reduces IOP by increasing the uveoscleral outflow of aqueous humour. It has no significant effect on the production of aqueous humour or the blood-aqueous barrier.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** IOP reduction begins 3–4 hours after administration, reaching a maximum effect at 8–12 hours. Reduction is maintained for at least 24 hours.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed through the cornea; peak concentration in aqueous humour is reached in approximately 2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Primarily distributed to the anterior segment, conjunctivae, and eyelids. 90% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Hydrolyzed in the cornea to the active latanoprost acid. The acid is primarily metabolized in the liver via beta-oxidation.
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is approximately 17 minutes. Metabolites are excreted primarily in the urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- In three pivotal 6-month trials, latanoprost 50 µg/mL once daily was compared to timolol 5 mg/mL twice daily.
- **Efficacy:** Latanoprost reduced IOP by 27–34% from a baseline of 24.4–25.2 mmHg. In a meta-analysis, 61% of patients achieved a 30% reduction in diurnal IOP compared to 40% for timolol.
- **Long-term Safety:** A 48-month open-label study (n=519) confirmed that while iris pigmentation changes occur (mostly within the first 24 months), they are not associated with adverse clinical ocular consequences.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Bottles:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze. Protect from light by keeping the bottle in the outer carton.
- **Opened Bottles:** Store below 25°C. The product must be discarded 4 weeks after first opening.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
