# Editorial Product Information: Lasmiditan

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documentation from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- At the time of preparation, Product Information from other major regulatory authorities (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided for synthesis.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Lasmiditan (as hemisuccinate).
- **Chemical Class**: Serotonin (5-HT) 1F receptor agonist (selective, non-triptan).
- **Brand Name(s)**: **REYVOW** [United States].
- **ATC Code**: N02CX07.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - Film-coated tablets: 50 mg (light gray, oval) and 100 mg (light purple, oval) [United States].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Eli Lilly and Company [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Migraine Treatment**: Indicated for the acute treatment of migraine attacks with or without aura in adult patients [United States-approved].
- **Limitations of Use**: **Lasmiditan** is not indicated for the preventive treatment of migraine [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: The recommended oral dose is 50 mg, 100 mg, or 200 mg as needed.
- **Dose Frequency**: No more than one dose should be administered within a 24-hour period. A second dose has not been demonstrated to be effective for the same migraine attack [United States].
- **Administration Instructions**: Tablets must be swallowed whole; they should not be crushed, split, or chewed. The drug may be taken with or without food.
- **Safety Restriction**: Patients should only take a dose if they can wait at least 8 hours before driving or operating machinery (see Section 5).
- **Treatment Frequency**: The safety of treating more than 4 migraine attacks in a 30-day period has not been established.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None**: There are no contraindications listed in the available source [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Driving Impairment**: Significant driving impairment occurs following administration. Patients must be advised not to drive or operate machinery for at least 8 hours after every dose. Patients who cannot adhere to this restriction should not use **lasmiditan**. Because the drug causes somnolence, patients may not be able to accurately assess their own level of impairment.
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Depression**: The drug causes CNS depression, including dizziness and sedation. Caution is required if used concurrently with alcohol or other CNS depressants.
- **Serotonin Syndrome**: Potential for serotonin syndrome, particularly when co-administered with other serotonergic drugs (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, MAOIs). Symptoms may include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular hyperactivity. If suspected, discontinue treatment.
- **Medication Overuse Headache (MOH)**: Overuse of acute migraine treatments (≥10 days per month) may lead to exacerbated headache frequency. Detoxification may be necessary.
- **Vital Sign Changes**:
    - **Heart Rate**: May cause a mean decrease of 5–10 beats per minute (bpm).
    - **Blood Pressure**: May cause transient increases in blood pressure. In geriatric patients, a mean increase of 7 mmHg in systolic BP was noted 1 hour post-dose [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are dose-related. The most common reactions (≥5% and greater than placebo) include:
- **Very Common (>10%)**: Dizziness [United States].
- **Common (1–10%)**: Fatigue (including asthenia and malaise), paresthesia (including oral/hypoesthesia), sedation (including somnolence), nausea and/or vomiting.
- **Uncommon to Rare**: Vertigo, visual impairment, palpitations, anxiety, tremor, restlessness, sleep abnormalities, muscle spasms, and confusion.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity**: Reported in 0.2% of patients, including angioedema, rash, and photosensitivity reactions. Discontinue drug if serious reactions occur.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants**: Alcohol and other sedative medications may potentiate CNS depression and impairment.
- **Serotonergic Drugs**: Increased risk of serotonin syndrome when used with SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, MAOIs, or St. John’s Wort.
- **Heart Rate Lowering Drugs**: Potential for additive bradycardic effects. Caution is advised when used with propranolol or other drugs that lower heart rate.
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Substrates**: **Lasmiditan** inhibits P-gp. Co-administration with sensitive P-gp substrates (e.g., digoxin) where small concentration changes lead to toxicity is not recommended [United States].
- **Breast Cancer Resistance Protein (BCRP) Substrates**: Co-administration does not appear to clinically affect BCRP substrates like rosuvastatin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **United States**: Category not specified under current labeling rules, but animal data indicates potential for fetal harm (increased skeletal variations, reduced fetal weight, and increased mortality at exposures higher than human doses). A pregnancy exposure registry is available.
- **Breastfeeding**: There are no data on presence in human milk or effects on the infant. In animal studies (rats), the drug was excreted in milk at levels 3 times higher than maternal plasma. The benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug.
- **Fertility**: No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal studies (rats).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [United States].
- **Elderly Use**: Dizziness and blood pressure increases are more frequent in patients ≥65 years. Dosing should generally start at the low end of the range.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dosage adjustment is required for patients with any degree of renal impairment (including severe, eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²).
- **Hepatic Impairment**: 
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B)**: No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C)**: Not studied and not recommended [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: High-affinity agonist at the 5-HT 1F receptor. Unlike triptans, it does not have significant activity at 5-HT 1B/1D receptors, which is thought to reduce vasoconstrictive potential.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Rapid; median Tmax of 1.8 hours. A high-fat meal increases Cmax and AUC by ~20% but is not considered clinically significant.
    - **Distribution**: Plasma protein binding is approximately 55–60%.
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily via non-CYP enzymes (ketone reduction is the major pathway). It is not significantly metabolised by MAO-A or MAO-B.
    - **Elimination**: Geometric mean half-life (t½) is approximately 5.7 hours. Renal excretion is a minor pathway (~3% unchanged).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies**: Two Phase 3 randomized, placebo-controlled trials (Studies 1 and 2) evaluated the 50 mg, 100 mg, and 200 mg doses.
- **Primary Outcomes**: In both studies, the percentage of patients achieving pain freedom at 2 hours and freedom from their Most Bothersome Symptom (MBS - nausea, photophobia, or phonophobia) at 2 hours was significantly higher with **lasmiditan** than with placebo.
- **Efficacy Data**: Pain freedom at 2 hours was achieved by approximately 28–39% of patients across doses compared to 15–21% for placebo [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
- **Handling**: Keep in original packaging to protect from moisture.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification**: 
    - **United States**: Schedule V (CV) controlled substance due to potential for abuse (euphoria and hallucinations reported in ~1% of patients).
- **Legal Classification**: Rx-only [United States].
