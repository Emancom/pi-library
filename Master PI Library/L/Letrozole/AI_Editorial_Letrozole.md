# Editorial Product Information: Letrozole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the provided Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: ARX-LETROZOLE (Letrozole) Tablets.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of letrozole for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or relevant local guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI was provided for review; therefore, regional variations from other jurisdictions (e.g., US, Europe) are not specified unless otherwise noted in available sources.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Letrozole (a non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor).
- **Chemical Class**: Antineoplastic agent; non-steroidal competitive inhibitor of the aromatase enzyme.
- **Brand Names**: ARX-LETROZOLE [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: L02BG04.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: 2.5 mg film-coated tablets (yellow, circular, biconvex).
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Cipla Australia Pty Ltd (Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hormone Receptor-Positive Breast Cancer**: Treatment of postmenopausal women with hormone receptor-positive (HR+) breast cancer. This includes:
    - Adjuvant treatment of early breast cancer.
    - Extended adjuvant treatment of early breast cancer (following 5 years of standard tamoxifen therapy).
    - First-line treatment of advanced or metastatic HR+ or hormone-unknown breast cancer.
    - Treatment of advanced breast cancer in women with disease progression following anti-oestrogen therapy.
- **Note**: Letrozole is not indicated for hormone receptor-negative disease. Safety and efficacy for neoadjuvant use have not been established [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose**: One 2.5 mg tablet daily.
- **Administration**: Taken orally, with or without food.
- **Treatment Duration**:
    - **Adjuvant setting**: Continue for 5 years or until tumour relapse.
    - **Extended adjuvant setting**: Optimal duration is unknown; pivotal studies planned for 5 years of treatment.
    - **Metastatic disease**: Continue until tumour progression is evident.
- **Missed Dose**: Should be taken as soon as remembered. If it is nearly time for the next dose, skip the missed dose. Do not double doses, as over-proportional systemic exposure has been observed at doses above 2.5 mg [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known allergy to letrozole or any excipients (e.g., lactose).
- **Endocrine Status**: Premenopausal endocrine status.
- **Pregnancy**: Contraindicated due to potential foetal harm [Australian Category D].
- **Lactation**: Contraindicated during breastfeeding.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Verification of Menopause**: In patients where menopausal status is unclear, LH, FSH, and/or oestradiol levels must be confirmed before initiating therapy.
- **Bone Effects**: Oestrogen deprivation leads to decreased Bone Mineral Density (BMD). Osteoporosis and bone fractures are reported. Monitoring of bone health (e.g., DEXA scan) is recommended during treatment.
- **Tendon Disorders**: Use is associated with tendonitis and tenosynovitis (uncommon) and tendon rupture (rare). Patients should be monitored for these symptoms [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Exposure (AUC) and half-life are approximately doubled in patients with severe hepatic cirrhosis (Child-Pugh score C). These patients require close supervision.
- **Renal Impairment**: Use with caution in patients with creatinine clearance <10 mL/min, as data are insufficient.
- **Driving and Machinery**: Fatigue, dizziness, and somnolence (uncommon) have been reported; caution is advised when performing tasks requiring alertness.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily linked to oestrogen deprivation. Approximately 80% of patients in the adjuvant setting may experience adverse effects.

| **Very Common (>10%)** | Hot flushes, arthralgia, increased sweating, fatigue (including asthenia/malaise), hypercholesterolaemia. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Depression, headache, dizziness, vertigo, palpitations, hypertension, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, constipation, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, alopecia, dry skin, rash, myalgia, bone pain, osteoporosis, bone fractures, arthritis, back pain, vaginal bleeding, peripheral oedema, weight increase, anorexia, appetite increase. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Urinary tract infection, tumour pain, leucopenia, anxiety, irritability, somnolence, insomnia, memory impairment, dysaesthesia (paraesthesia/hypoaesthesia), taste disturbance, cerebrovascular accident, carpal tunnel syndrome, cataract, eye irritation, blurred vision, tachycardia, ischaemic cardiac events (angina, MI), thrombophlebitis, dyspnoea, cough, stomatitis, dry mouth, increased hepatic enzymes, hyperbilirubinaemia, jaundice, pruritus, urticaria, increased urinary frequency, vaginal discharge, vaginal dryness, breast pain, general oedema, pyrexia, thirst, weight loss. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Tamoxifen and Anti-oestrogens**: Co-administration of tamoxifen (20 mg daily) significantly reduces letrozole plasma levels (by approximately 38%). Avoid concomitant use of tamoxifen, other anti-oestrogens, or oestrogen-containing therapies.
- **CYP450 Interactions**:
    - Letrozole is metabolised by **CYP3A4** and **CYP2A6**.
    - **Strong Inhibitors**: (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, methoxsalen) may increase letrozole concentrations.
    - **Strong Inducers**: (e.g., phenytoin, rifampicin, St John’s Wort) may decrease letrozole concentrations.
- **Effect on Other Drugs**: In vitro, letrozole inhibits **CYP2C19**. Caution is advised for drugs with a narrow therapeutic index primarily cleared by CYP2C19 (e.g., phenytoin, clopidogrel).
- **No Significant Interaction**: Clinical studies showed no relevant interactions with cimetidine, warfarin, benzodiazepines, barbiturates, NSAIDs, paracetamol, frusemide, or omeprazole [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category D [Australian]**. Letrozole is contraindicated. Pharmacological action involves the disruption of oestrogen-dependent events. Animal studies show increased foetal malformations, resorptions, and foetal death. Spontaneous abortions and congenital anomalies (e.g., labial fusion) have been reported post-marketing.
- **Breastfeeding**: Contraindicated. It is unknown if letrozole is excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility**: Letrozole may impair fertility. In animal models, decreased mating and fertility were observed, alongside reproductive organ changes (ovarian hyperplasia, uterine atrophy).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Not recommended. Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents (up to 18 years) have not been established.
- **Elderly Use**: No dose adjustment required.
- **Renal Impairment**: No adjustment for CrCl ≥30 mL/min. Exercise caution/careful risk-benefit assessment for CrCl <10 mL/min [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. Close supervision is mandatory for severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C); no specific dose advice is available due to insufficient data.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Potent, highly specific, non-steroidal competitive inhibitor of the aromatase enzyme (a cytochrome P450 subunit). It prevents the conversion of adrenal androgens (androstenedione, testosterone) to oestrone and oestradiol in peripheral tissues and tumour tissue, reducing systemic oestrogen levels by 75–95%.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Rapid and complete (99.9% bioavailability). Food slightly slows the rate but not the extent of absorption.
    - **Distribution**: 60% protein bound (mainly to albumin). Rapidly distributes to tissues (Vd ~1.87 L/kg).
    - **Metabolism**: Metabolised to an inactive carbinol metabolite via CYP3A4 and CYP2A6.
    - **Elimination**: Primarily renal excretion (88% as metabolites). Terminal half-life is approximately 2 days. Steady state is reached in 2–6 weeks with daily dosing.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BIG 1-98 (Adjuvant)**: A large-scale study (n >8000) comparing letrozole vs. tamoxifen. Letrozole demonstrated superior Disease-Free Survival (DFS) compared to tamoxifen (HR 0.81 at 26 months). Long-term analysis (73 months) confirmed DFS benefit (HR 0.88).
- **MA-17 (Extended Adjuvant)**: Conducted in women who completed 5 years of tamoxifen. Letrozole reduced the risk of recurrence by 42% compared to placebo (HR 0.58).
- **Study 025 (Metastatic)**: Compared letrozole to tamoxifen as first-line therapy. Letrozole was superior in Time to Progression (9.4 vs 6.0 months) and Objective Response Rate (32% vs 21%).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store below 25°C.
- **Handling**: Keep in the original container to protect from moisture.
- **Shelf Life**: Noted on the external packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
