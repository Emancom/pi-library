# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow 2-3 minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Acute Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2-3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **ESRD:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are supranormal [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None specified [US].

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** While this document covers the injection, it is noted that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Monitor INR in patients receiving concurrent warfarin therapy.
- In ESRD, monitor trough carnitine levels to guide dose downward titration [US].

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, body odor (transient nausea/vomiting are common in general use) [US].
- **Serious Effects:** Seizures, anaphylaxis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. 
    - **Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter category in source [US].
    - **Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentration in milk following exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal therapy against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See **Section 3: Dosage and Administration** (dosing is weight-based) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - For ESRD patients on dialysis, specific dosing (10–20 mg/kg) is provided.
    - Safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn metabolic errors, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine compounds, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, the mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous carnitine) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12) receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis, trough concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the effect of this supplementation on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
