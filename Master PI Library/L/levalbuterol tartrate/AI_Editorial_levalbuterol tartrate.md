# Editorial Product Information: levalbuterol tartrate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **levalbuterol tartrate**
- Chemical class: beta-adrenergic agonist (sympathomimetic)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: inhalation (oral inhalation)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval/registration: 1999 [US]
- Date of last revision/update in available source: Revised 7/2025 [US]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Other jurisdictions' approval dates or revision history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment or prevention of bronchospasm in patients with reversible obstructive airway disease, adults, adolescents, and children 4 years of age and older. [US]
- Use in pediatric patients below 4 years of age: not indicated (clinical trial did not demonstrate efficacy and showed increased asthma-related adverse events). [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adults and children ≥ 4 years)
- Recommended usual dose: 2 inhalations every 4 to 6 hours. In some patients, 1 inhalation every 4 hours may provide adequate relief.
- Do not routinely administer more frequently or give a larger number of inhalations than recommended. If a previously effective regimen fails, this may indicate asthma destabilization and the patient should be re-evaluated with consideration of anti-inflammatory therapy (e.g., corticosteroids).

### Dosage by indication
- Bronchospasm (treatment or prevention): follow the general dosing above. No other indication-specific dosing regimens are specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- For oral inhalation only.
- Shake well before use.
- Avoid spraying into the eyes.
- Prime the inhaler before first use and if not used for >3 days by releasing 4 test sprays into the air away from the face.
- Clean the actuator with warm water at least once weekly and allow to air-dry thoroughly. Failure to clean and dry the actuator may result in cessation of medication delivery; wash actuator if medication build-up or blockage occurs.
- Canister contains a dose indicator showing remaining actuations; the indicator advances after every tenth actuation. Discard the inhaler when the dose indicator reads zero (corresponding to the labeled number of actuations).
- Each labeled actuation delivers 59 mcg levalbuterol tartrate (equivalent to 45 mcg levalbuterol free base) from the mouthpiece after priming. Container actuation count and per-actuation content are specified in available sources.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **levalbuterol tartrate**, racemic albuterol, or any component of the product. Reactions reported include urticaria, angioedema, rash, bronchospasm, anaphylaxis, and oropharyngeal edema. [All sources]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed (major) warnings specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Paradoxical bronchospasm**: inhaled administration can cause paradoxical bronchospasm, which may be life‑threatening. If this occurs, discontinue levalbuterol immediately and institute alternative therapy. Paradoxical bronchospasm may occur with the first use of a new canister.
- **Deterioration of asthma**: increased need for doses or decreased response may signal clinical deterioration; reassess the patient and treatment regimen promptly and consider anti‑inflammatory therapy including corticosteroids.
- **Not a substitute for corticosteroids**: beta‑agonist therapy alone may not adequately control asthma in many patients; consider early addition of anti‑inflammatory agents when indicated.
- **Cardiovascular effects**: sympathomimetic agents can cause clinically significant cardiovascular effects (tachycardia, changes in blood pressure, arrhythmias). ECG changes (T-wave flattening, QTc prolongation, ST depression) have been reported; clinical significance unknown. Use with caution in patients with existing cardiovascular disease (e.g., coronary insufficiency, cardiac arrhythmias, hypertension).
- **Do not exceed recommended dose**: excessive use of inhaled sympathomimetics has been associated with fatalities; avoid overuse.
- **Immediate hypersensitivity reactions**: anaphylaxis, angioedema, urticaria, rash, bronchospasm, and oropharyngeal edema have been reported; discontinue if suspected.
- **Hypokalemia**: beta‑agonists may produce significant decreases in serum potassium (likely via intracellular shifting), which can have adverse cardiovascular effects; decreases are usually transient and do not always require supplementation.
- **Use in patients with coexisting conditions**: exercise caution in patients with convulsive disorders, hyperthyroidism, diabetes mellitus, or those unusually responsive to sympathomimetics. Large doses of racemic albuterol have been reported to aggravate diabetes and ketoacidosis.
- **Labor and delivery / tocolysis**: because beta‑agonists may interfere with uterine contractility, use during labor should be reserved for cases where benefit outweighs risk. Use for tocolysis is not approved; serious adverse events (including maternal pulmonary edema) have been reported with beta‑agonists used for premature labor.
  
### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor clinical status of asthma control; reassess if loss of efficacy or increased use occurs.
- Monitor serum potassium in situations where risk of hypokalemia is increased (e.g., concomitant non–potassium-sparing diuretics, high dose beta‑agonist use, underlying metabolic derangements).
- Consider ECG monitoring and caution in patients with significant cardiac disease.
- Consider monitoring serum digoxin concentrations in patients receiving digoxin concomitantly.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid concomitant use with other short‑acting sympathomimetic aerosols or systemic adrenergic drugs unless under careful medical supervision due to potential for additive cardiovascular effects.
- Use caution if patient is receiving monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) or tricyclic antidepressants or within 2 weeks of their discontinuation due to potentiation of cardiovascular effects.
- Beta‑adrenergic blocking agents may antagonize the bronchodilator effect and may precipitate severe bronchospasm; avoid in most asthmatic patients unless benefits outweigh risks, and consider cardioselective agents if necessary.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Other short‑acting sympathomimetic aerosol bronchodilators or epinephrine: may potentiate effect; concomitant use is not recommended.
- Beta‑blockers: beta‑adrenergic blocking agents may antagonize the bronchodilator effect of levalbuterol and may precipitate severe bronchospasm in asthmatics; avoid in most asthmatic patients. If absolutely required (e.g., certain cardiac indications), consider cardioselective beta‑blockers and use with extreme caution.
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) and tricyclic antidepressants: may potentiate the cardiovascular effects of albuterol/levalbuterol; administer with extreme caution and consider alternative therapy if possible. Avoid use within 2 weeks of discontinuation of MAOIs.
- Diuretics (non–potassium‑sparing, e.g., loop or thiazide): may exacerbate ECG changes or hypokalemia; coadministration may worsen hypokalemia — consider monitoring potassium levels and use caution.
  
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Digoxin: single‑dose racemic albuterol administration produced mean serum digoxin decreases of approximately 16% to 22% in normal volunteers. The clinical significance for patients chronically receiving digoxin and inhaled levalbuterol is unclear; prudent to monitor serum digoxin levels when coadministered.

### Clinically significant interactions and management notes
- Avoid coadministration with other short‑acting sympathomimetics to prevent additive cardiovascular and beta‑adrenergic effects.
- In patients on non‑potassium‑sparing diuretics or with other risk factors for hypokalemia, monitor potassium and manage accordingly.
- Monitor ECG and cardiac status in patients with significant cardiac disease or those on medications that can prolong QT interval; use caution with drugs that potentiate sympathomimetic effects.
- Evaluate serum digoxin concentrations if coadministered.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Human data: No adequate and well‑controlled studies in pregnant women; clinical data unavailable.
- Animal data: Oral levalbuterol HCl in pregnant rabbits produced no teratogenicity at doses up to 25 mg/kg/day (~750× the MRHDID on mg/m^2 basis). However, developmental studies with racemic albuterol showed teratogenic findings in mice (cleft palate) and rabbits (cranioschisis) at doses somewhat higher than human therapeutic exposures. Inhalation developmental studies with racemic albuterol sulfate did not show teratogenicity at high exposures in one study but teratogenicity was reported in other species/studies at higher exposures.
- Clinical considerations: Poorly controlled asthma in pregnancy is associated with increased maternal and fetal risks (e.g., preeclampsia, prematurity, low birth weight); pregnant patients should be closely monitored and asthma control optimized. Use during labor should be considered only if benefits justify potential risks. Use for tocolysis is not approved and has been associated with serious maternal adverse events (e.g., pulmonary edema).
- Pregnancy classification by jurisdiction (if available): Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Data on presence of levalbuterol in human milk, effects on the breastfed infant, or effects on milk production are not available. Consider the benefits of breastfeeding, the mother's clinical need for levalbuterol, and potential adverse effects on the breastfed child.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Levalbuterol tartrate** is a beta‑adrenergic agonist (sympathomimetic) that produces bronchodilation. It exerts bronchodilatory effects consistent with beta‑adrenergic receptor activation.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Per‑actuation delivered dose: each actuation delivers 59 mcg levalbuterol tartrate (equivalent to 45 mcg levalbuterol free base) from the mouthpiece after priming.
- Further pharmacokinetic parameters (absorption rates, distribution volumes, metabolism pathways, elimination half‑life, effect of hepatic/renal impairment) are not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Beta‑agonist effects include bronchodilation and systemic sympathomimetic effects (e.g., tremor, tachycardia, effects on blood glucose and potassium).
- May produce hypokalemia (intracellular shift) and changes in blood glucose; ECG changes (T‑wave flattening, QTc prolongation, ST segment depression) have been reported.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Adults and adolescents (≥12 years)
- Two randomized, multicenter, double‑blind, active‑ and placebo‑controlled 8‑week trials enrolled 748 adult and adolescent patients with asthma to compare levalbuterol inhalation aerosol (90 mcg per actuation; 2 actuations dosing) versus a marketed racemic albuterol HFA inhaler and an HFA‑134a placebo inhaler. Safety data from these trials identified adverse events more frequent with levalbuterol versus placebo including asthma (≈9%), pharyngitis (≈8%), rhinitis (≈7%), pain (≈4%), and dizziness (≈3%). Trial designs were randomized, double‑blind, and included active and placebo comparators.

### Children 4–11 years
- A randomized, double‑blind 4‑week trial in 150 children aged 4–11 compared levalbuterol inhalation aerosol (90 mcg per actuation) with racemic albuterol inhaler and placebo. Reported adverse events more frequent than placebo included vomiting (≈11%), accidental injury (≈9%), pharyngitis (≈7%), and bronchitis (≈3%). Systemic beta‑adrenergic adverse events (e.g., tremor, nervousness) were low and similar across treatment groups.

### Children birth to <4 years
- A 4‑week, multicenter, randomized, modified‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial in 196 pediatric patients birth to <4 years (administered via a valved holding chamber with mask) evaluated levalbuterol (45 mcg and 90 mcg) versus levalbuterol inhalation solution and placebo. The primary endpoint (mean change in Pediatric Asthma Caregiver Assessment total score) demonstrated no statistical difference between levalbuterol inhalation aerosol and placebo. An increased incidence of treatment‑emergent asthma‑related adverse events was reported in levalbuterol groups compared with placebo; one discontinuation for asthma in levalbuterol group vs none in placebo.

### Interpretation
- The clinical trial program supports efficacy and safety for patients 4 years and older when used as directed. Trials indicate an adverse event profile consistent with sympathomimetic therapy and the specific events and rates above.
