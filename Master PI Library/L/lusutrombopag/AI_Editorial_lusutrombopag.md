# Editorial Product Information: Lusutrombopag

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinically relevant reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lusutrombopag (a thrombopoietin [TPO] receptor agonist).
- **Brand Name:** Mulpleta [US].
- **ATC Code:** B01AC26 (Not specified in available sources, but standard for this class).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated tablets, 3 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured for Vancocin Italia S.r.l. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Thrombocytopenia in Chronic Liver Disease (CLD):** Treatment of thrombocytopenia in adult patients with chronic liver disease who are scheduled to undergo a medical or dental procedure [US-approved]. 

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended dose is 3 mg orally once daily.
- **Dosing Schedule:** 
    - Treatment should commence 8 to 14 days prior to the scheduled procedure.
    - The duration of therapy is 7 days.
    - The procedure should be performed 2 to 8 days after the final dose of lusutrombopag.
- **Administration:** Tablets may be taken with or without food.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the patient should take it as soon as possible on the same day. The patient should return to the normal schedule the following day.
- **Restriction of Use:** Lusutrombopag should not be administered to patients with CLD in an attempt to normalise platelet counts. It has only been studied as a single 7-day regimen [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None reported in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Thrombotic/Thromboembolic Complications:** As a TPO receptor agonist, lusutrombopag is associated with an increased risk of thrombotic events in patients with CLD. Portal vein thrombosis has been reported in clinical trials (1% in both active and placebo groups). 
- **Risk Assessment:** Clinicians should consider the increased risk when prescribing to patients with known pro-thrombotic risk factors, including:
    - Genetic conditions (e.g., Factor V Leiden, Prothrombin 20210A, Antithrombin deficiency, Protein C or S deficiency).
    - Presence of prior thrombosis or absence of hepatopetal blood flow.
- **Monitoring:** Platelet counts must be obtained prior to starting therapy and no more than 2 days before the scheduled procedure. Monitor for signs and symptoms of thromboembolic events [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - **Headache:** Reported in 5% of patients (vs 4% for placebo) [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - **Portal Vein Thrombosis:** Identified post-procedure via imaging [US].
- **Clinical Trial Data:** In pooled data, the incidence of serious adverse events was 5% in the lusutrombopag group compared to 7% in the placebo group. No adverse reactions led to the discontinuation of the drug [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP Enzymes:** Lusutrombopag is primarily metabolised by CYP4 enzymes, including CYP4A11. It has a low potential to inhibit or induce major CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2C9, 3A4, etc.).
    - **Transporters:** Lusutrombopag is a substrate of P-glycoprotein (P-gp) and Breast Cancer Resistance Protein (BCRP).
- **Clinically Significant Interactions:**
    - **P-gp/BCRP Inhibitors:** Co-administration with cyclosporine (P-gp/BCRP inhibitor) did not result in clinically significant changes in exposure.
    - **Multivalent Cations:** Co-administration with antacids containing calcium carbonate did not significantly affect exposure.
    - **Midazolam:** Lusutrombopag does not affect the pharmacokinetics of midazolam (a CYP3A substrate) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned under current PLLR rules]. There are no available data on use in pregnant women. Animal studies in rats showed adverse developmental outcomes (low body weight, skeletal variations, and decreased fertility in offspring) at exposures 23 to 251 times the human clinical exposure. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There is no data regarding the presence of the drug in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child, breastfeeding is **not recommended** during treatment and for at least **28 days** after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no effect on fertility at doses up to 100 mg/kg/day (approx. 176–252 times human exposure) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical experience has not identified differences in response between elderly and younger patients, though data for patients ≥65 years are limited [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically meaningful effect was observed in mild to moderate impairment (CLcr 30 to <90 mL/min). Data are limited for severe renal impairment (CLcr <30 mL/min) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class A and B). In patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C), exposure decreased by 20–30%, but ranges overlapped with other classes; no specific dose adjustment is recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lusutrombopag is an orally bioavailable, small-molecule TPO receptor agonist. It interacts with the transmembrane domain of human TPO receptors on megakaryocytes, stimulating proliferation and differentiation of megakaryocytic progenitor cells and inducing megakaryocyte maturation.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Platelet count increases are dose-dependent. Median time to reach peak platelet count was 12 days in patients not receiving transfusions. It does not prolong the QT interval at 8 times the recommended dose.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is 6 to 8 hours. High-fat meals do not affect AUC or Cmax.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>99.9%).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 27 hours. Fecal excretion accounts for 83% (16% as unchanged drug) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **L-PLUS 1 & L-PLUS 2:** Two randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials evaluated patients with CLD and platelet counts <50 x 10⁹/L.
- **Primary Outcomes:**
    - **L-PLUS 1:** 78% of the lusutrombopag group required no platelet transfusion prior to the procedure, compared to 13% of the placebo group.
    - **L-PLUS 2:** 65% of the lusutrombopag group required no platelet transfusion or rescue therapy for bleeding, compared to 29% of the placebo group.
- **Duration of Response:** The median duration of platelet count maintenance (≥50 x 10⁹/L) was 19 to 22 days in treated patients who did not require transfusion [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
- **Handling:** Keep in the original child-resistant blister pack to protect from environment [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources [US].
