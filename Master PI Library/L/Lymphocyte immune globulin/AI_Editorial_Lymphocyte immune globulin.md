# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection based on official Product Information from the United States. It is intended as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic name); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    *   Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    *   In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    *   Dosing frequency: Every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
    *   Maintenance doses typically range from 50 mg/kg/day, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg/day have been used [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   Starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   Administration: Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   Dose adjustments: May be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
*   The formulation contains no preservatives; discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
*   Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration.
*   Management: Discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical care. Carefully weigh the risk/benefit before re-administration [US].

### Seizures
*   Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
*   **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations (normal range: 40 to 50 µmol/L) [US].

### General Precautions
*   **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of oral formulations have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
The following reactions are common (>10%) in the ESRD population (regardless of causality):
*   **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), headache (22%), fever (7–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%) [US].
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%) [US].
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].

### Clinical Trial Observations
*   Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Gastritis and body odor are less frequent [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity reactions.
*   Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concurrently with levocarnitine.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned in provided source]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation for the mother [US].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis. Note the precaution regarding accumulation of metabolites with oral formulations [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared from the blood [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
*   **Excretion:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours.
*   **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV doses of 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean trough carnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L by week 8.
*   **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increase, the specific effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
*   **Stability:** When diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL), the solution is stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].
