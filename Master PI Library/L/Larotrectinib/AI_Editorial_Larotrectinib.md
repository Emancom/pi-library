# Editorial Product Information: Larotrectinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **larotrectinib** for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical practice in any specific jurisdiction. Practitioners should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved documentation before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Larotrectinib (present as larotrectinib sulfate).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective tropomyosin receptor kinase (TRK) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **VITRAKVI** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EX12.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Hard Capsules:** 25 mg and 100 mg [Australian].
    - **Oral Solution:** 20 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bayer Australia Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Solid Tumours with NTRK Gene Fusion:** Treatment of adult and paediatric patients with locally advanced or metastatic solid tumours that:
    - Possess a neurotrophic tyrosine receptor kinase (NTRK) gene fusion without a known acquired resistance mutation.
    - Are metastatic or where surgical resection is likely to result in severe morbidity.
    - Have progressed following treatment or have no satisfactory alternative therapy [Australian-approved under provisional status].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Adults:** 100 mg taken orally, twice daily.
- **Paediatric (1 month to 18 years):** 100 mg/m² (Body Surface Area) taken orally, twice daily, with a maximum of 100 mg per dose.

### Administration Instructions
- **Capsules:** Swallow whole with water; do not open, chew, or crush due to bitter taste.
- **Oral Solution:** Administer by mouth or enterally via nasogastric (NG) or gastric feeding tube using an oral syringe (1 mL or 5 mL).
- **Food:** May be taken with or without food.
- **NG Tube Specifics:** Flush tube with at least 10 mL water before and after administration (0.5–1 mL for neonates/fluid-restricted patients). Do not mix with feeding formulas to avoid tube blockage [Australian].

### Dose Modifications (Toxicity Management)
| Dose Modification | Adult/Paediatric (BSA ≥1.0 m²) | Paediatric (BSA <1.0 m²) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First Reduction** | 75 mg twice daily | 75 mg/m² twice daily |
| **Second Reduction** | 50 mg twice daily | 50 mg/m² twice daily |
| **Third Reduction** | 100 mg once daily | 25 mg/m² twice daily |

- **Discontinuation:** Permanently discontinue if the patient cannot tolerate the third dose modification.
- **Liver Function Abnormalities:** Withhold **larotrectinib** for Grade 3 or 4 ALT/AST elevations until resolution; resume at a lower dose if benefit outweighs risk. Permanently discontinue if ALT/AST ≥3 x ULN with bilirubin ≥2 x ULN [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Do not take a double dose to make up for a missed dose. If the patient vomits after a dose, do not take an additional dose; take the next dose at the scheduled time.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **larotrectinib** or any excipients used in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neurologic Reactions:** Dizziness, gait disturbance, and paraesthesia have been reported, typically within the first three months. Dosing may need to be withheld, reduced, or discontinued based on severity.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevations in ALT, AST, and bilirubin are common. Most occur within the first 3 months.
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **Liver Function Tests (LFTs):** Monitor ALT, AST, and bilirubin before the first dose, every 2 weeks for the first month, then monthly for the next 6 months, and periodically thereafter.
- **Effects on Ability to Drive/Use Machines:** Patients should be cautioned until they are certain the therapy does not cause adverse neurologic effects.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The majority of adverse reactions reported were Grade 1 or 2.

### Frequency Table (Pooled Analysis)
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (≥10%)** | ALT increased (33%), AST increased (31%), Anaemia (29%), Vomiting (30%), Constipation (27%), Diarrhoea (27%), Nausea (25%), Fatigue (26%), Dizziness (22%), Myalgia (18%), Neutropenia (17%), Weight increase (18%), Leukopenia (14%), Alkaline phosphatase increased (10%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Muscular weakness, Paraesthesia, Gait disturbance, Dysgeusia, Thrombocytopenia. |
| **Not Known** | Liver injury (specifically cases with ALT/AST ≥3 x ULN and bilirubin ≥2 x ULN). |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Grade 4 events reported include decreased neutrophil count, ALT/AST increase, increased alkaline phosphatase, decreased leukocyte count, and decreased platelet count.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole, grapefruit) increase **larotrectinib** concentrations. Avoid co-administration; if unavoidable, reduce **larotrectinib** dose by 50%.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, St. John’s Wort) decrease **larotrectinib** concentrations. Avoid co-administration; if unavoidable, double the **larotrectinib** dose.
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** **Larotrectinib** is a weak inhibitor of CYP3A4. Exercise caution with narrow therapeutic index substrates (e.g., fentanyl, sirolimus, tacrolimus).
- **Transporters:** **Larotrectinib** is a substrate for P-gp and BCRP.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian: Category C]. Animal studies show potential for foetal harm. **Larotrectinib** crosses the placenta. Females of childbearing potential should use highly effective contraception during and for one month after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is unknown. Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment and for 3 days after the final dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No clinical data available; animal studies showed no effect on male fertility but reversible effects on female reproductive organs in rats (decreased uterine weight, atrophy) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients from 1 month of age. Safety profile is generally consistent with adults, though vomiting and transaminase elevations may be more frequent in children.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for patients ≥65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for any degree of renal impairment, including end-stage renal disease [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No adjustment.
    - **Moderate to Severe (Child-Pugh B or C):** Reduce the starting dose by 50% [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** An ATP-competitive and highly selective inhibitor of the TRK family (TRKA, TRKB, TRKC). It prevents the constitutive kinase activity driven by *NTRK* gene fusions, which otherwise activate downstream pathways for cell proliferation and survival.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~34%. Peak plasma levels (Cmax) are reached in ~1 hour.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is ~70%. Vss is 48 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4/5.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 3 hours. Excreted via faeces (58%) and urine (39%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Data:** Based on a pooled analysis of 164 patients (excluding primary CNS tumours) across three studies (Study 1, NAVIGATE, and SCOUT).
- **Primary Outcomes:**
    - **Overall Response Rate (ORR):** 73% (95% CI: 65, 79).
    - **Complete Response (CR):** 19%.
    - **Duration of Response (DOR):** Median not reached at the time of analysis; 76% of responses lasted ≥12 months.
- **Primary CNS Tumours:** In a subset of 24 patients, the ORR was 21% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Capsules:** Store below 25°C. Excursions between 15°C and 30°C permitted [Australian].
- **Oral Solution:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on packaging.
