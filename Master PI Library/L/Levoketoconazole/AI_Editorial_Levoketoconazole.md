# Editorial Product Information: Levoketoconazole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- At the time of writing, official PI documents for other regions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison. Consequently, jurisdictional variations are primarily noted where the source specifies its regional status.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levoketoconazole (the 2S, 4R enantiomer of ketoconazole).
- **Chemical Class:** Cortisol synthesis inhibitor; imidazole derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** **RECORLEV** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 150 mg film-coated tablets (round, biconvex, pink, imprinted with "LEV; 150") [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Xeris Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Endogenous Hypercortisolemia:** Indicated for the treatment of endogenous hypercortisolemia in adult patients with Cushing’s syndrome for whom surgery is not an option or has not been curative [United States].
- **Limitations of Use:** **RECORLEV** is specifically not approved for the treatment of fungal infections; its safety and efficacy for antifungal use have not been established [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial Dosage:** 150 mg orally twice daily, administered with or without food.
- **Titration:** Dosage may be increased by 150 mg daily every 2 to 3 weeks.
- **Maximum Dosage:** 1200 mg daily (administered as 600 mg twice daily).
- **Maintenance:** Once the maintenance dose is achieved, monitor 24-hour urine free cortisol (UFC) every 1–2 months or as clinically indicated.

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Baseline Requirements:** Obtain baseline liver tests (ALT, AST, total bilirubin) and an electrocardiogram (ECG). Correct hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia prior to initiation.
- **Ongoing Monitoring:**
    *   **Liver Tests:** Weekly for the first 6 weeks; every 2 weeks for the next 6 weeks; monthly for the next 3 months; and then as clinically indicated.
    *   **ECG:** Before every dose increase and routinely thereafter.
    *   **Cortisol:** Monitor 24-hour UFC or morning serum/plasma cortisol periodically.

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, the patient should take the next dose at the regularly scheduled time; they should not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Cirrhosis, acute liver disease, or poorly controlled chronic liver disease; baseline AST or ALT >3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN); history of drug-induced liver injury (DILI) due to ketoconazole or any azole antifungal [United States].
- **Cardiac Conditions:** Baseline QTcF interval >470 msec; history of torsades de pointes, ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, or long QT syndrome (including family history) [United States].
- **Drug Interactions:**
    *   Concomitant use of drugs that cause QT prolongation associated with ventricular arrhythmias.
    *   Concomitant use with sensitive substrates of CYP3A4 or those that are substrates of both CYP3A4 and P-gp.
- **Other:** Known hypersensitivity to levoketoconazole, ketoconazole, or any excipients; recurrent symptomatic cholelithiasis; extensive metastatic liver disease.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [United States]
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Cases of fatal hepatotoxicity or those requiring liver transplantation have been reported with oral ketoconazole. **RECORLEV** is associated with serious hepatotoxicity. Evaluate liver enzymes prior to and during treatment.
- **QT Prolongation:** **RECORLEV** is associated with dose-related QT interval prolongation, which may lead to life-threatening ventricular dysrhythmias such as torsades de pointes. Perform ECG prior to and during treatment.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypocortisolism:** Treatment may lead to low cortisol levels and life-threatening adrenal insufficiency. Monitor for nausea, vomiting, fatigue, hypotension, and hypoglycemia. Dosage reduction or interruption may be necessary.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Anaphylaxis has been reported with oral ketoconazole. Discontinue if a serious reaction occurs.
- **Decreased Testosterone:** Levoketoconazole may lower serum testosterone in both men (potentially causing gynecomastia, impotence, oligospermia) and women (causing decreased libido and mood changes).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions
Based on clinical trial data for patients with Cushing's syndrome:
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea/vomiting (30-37%), hypokalemia (15-29%), hypertension (20-24%), hemorrhage/contusion (23-40%), headache (21-38%), fatigue (18-39%), abdominal pain/dyspepsia (15-33%), erythema (43%), arthritis (28%), upper respiratory infection (18-28%), myalgia (26%), arrhythmia (19-24%), back pain (22%), and insomnia (22%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Adrenal insufficiency (reported in 7-10% of patients across studies), decreased appetite (13%), dry mouth (11%), alopecia (6-11%), diarrhea (15-19%), and dizziness (15%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Serious hepatotoxicity (Drug-induced liver injury occurred in 13% of trial participants).
- Life-threatening QT prolongation.
- Adrenal insufficiency.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Effect of RECORLEV on Other Drugs:**
    *   **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitor:** Significantly increases plasma concentrations of CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., midazolam, atorvastatin, felodipine).
    *   **Transporter Inhibition:** Inhibits P-gp, OCT2, and MATE1 (e.g., increases metformin exposure).
- **Effect of Other Drugs on RECORLEV:**
    *   **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Concomitant use is not recommended. Avoid use from 2 weeks before and during **RECORLEV** treatment.
    *   **Gastric Acid Modulators:** Absorption is pH-dependent. Neutralizers (e.g., antacids) should be taken at least 2 hours after **RECORLEV**. Avoid gastric acid suppressors (PPIs, H2-receptor antagonists) [United States].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Disulfiram-like reactions (flushing, rash, nausea) have been reported with ketoconazole and alcohol. Avoid excessive alcohol consumption.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: No Category Letter assigned under current labeling rules]. Available data from racemic ketoconazole are insufficient to determine drug-associated risks. Animal studies show embryotoxic effects and fetal malformations at doses equal to or less than the maximum recommended human dose. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Advise patients not to breastfeed during treatment and for one day after the final dose due to the risk of serious adverse reactions (e.g., liver toxicity) in the infant [United States].
- **Fertility:** May impair both male and female fertility based on animal data and testosterone-lowering effects.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years have not been established [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently than younger adults.
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific experience with **RECORLEV**. Racemic ketoconazole PK was not significantly altered in renal failure, but caution is advised.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with cirrhosis or significant liver disease (see Section 4).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits key steroidogenic enzymes: CYP11B1 (11β-hydroxylase), CYP11A1 (cholesterol side-chain cleavage enzyme), and CYP17A1 (17α-hydroxylase). This action reduces the synthesis of cortisol and testosterone.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations (Tmax) occur at 1.5 to 2 hours. High-fat meals increase AUC by 30%.
    *   **Distribution:** High protein binding (99.3%).
    *   **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized in the liver via CYP3A4.
    *   **Elimination:** Half-life of 4 to 6 hours after multiple doses. Primary excretion is via bile/feces (57%) and urine (13%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1 (SONICS):** An open-label titration/maintenance study followed by an 8-week randomized withdrawal phase. 52.4% of patients in the **RECORLEV** group maintained normal mUFC compared to 5.6% in the placebo group [United States].
- **Study 2 (LOGICS):** A multicenter, single-arm, open-label study where 30.9% of patients achieved mUFC normalization at the end of a 6-month maintenance phase without dose increases.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [United States].
- **Packaging:** Dispense in original containers with child-resistant closures.

- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [United States].
