# Editorial Product Information: Lansoprazole

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **lansoprazole** for use by healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Lansoprazole** (a substituted benzimidazole).
- **Chemical Class:** Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Lansoprazole ODT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A02BC03 (Not specified in provided source).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Orally disintegrating tablets (ODT): 15 mg and 30 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lupin Australia Pty Ltd / Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
### Adult Indications
- Healing and long-term management of reflux oesophagitis [Australian].
- Healing and long-term management of duodenal ulcer [Australian].
- Healing of benign gastric ulcer [Australian].
- Eradication of *H. pylori* (in combination with appropriate antibiotics) for peptic ulcer or chronic gastritis [Australian].
- Relief of acid-related dyspepsia (reflux-like or ulcer-like symptoms) [Australian].
- Treatment of benign peptic lesions non-responsive to H2-receptor antagonists [Australian].

### Paediatric Indications (Aged 6 to 17 years)
- Treatment of gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD), including all grades of oesophagitis [Australian].
- Healing of erosive oesophagitis [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- **Lansoprazole** should be taken once daily in the morning before food for optimal acid inhibition [Australian].
- **ODT Formulation:** Should be placed on the tongue and sucked until dispersed. The resulting enteric-coated pellets are swallowed with saliva. Alternatively, the tablet may be swallowed whole with water. 
- **Crucial Note:** Tablets and pellets must NOT be crushed or chewed [Australian].

### Adult Dosing
- **Reflux Oesophagitis:** 30 mg once daily for 4 weeks; may extend for 4 additional weeks if not healed. Maintenance: 15 mg or 30 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Duodenal Ulcer:** 30 mg once daily for 4 weeks. Maintenance: 15 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Gastric Ulcer:** 30 mg once daily for 8 weeks [Australian].
- **Acid-related Dyspepsia:** 15 mg or 30 mg once daily for 2–4 weeks [Australian].
- **H. pylori Eradication:** 30 mg twice daily for 7 days, combined with two antibiotics (e.g., amoxicillin 1 g twice daily and either metronidazole 400 mg or clarithromycin 250 mg twice daily) [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (Short-term, up to 12 weeks)
- **6–11 Years:**
    - ≤ 30 kg: 15 mg once daily.
    - \> 30 kg: 30 mg once daily [Australian].
- **12–17 Years:** 
    - Erosive oesophagitis: 30 mg once daily for up to 8 weeks.
    - Non-erosive GORD: 15 mg once daily for up to 8 weeks [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **lansoprazole**, other PPIs, or any excipients [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- Co-administration with **atazanavir** (due to significantly reduced protease inhibitor exposure) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Exclusion of Malignancy:** Symptomatic response to **lansoprazole** does not preclude gastric malignancy; malignancy should be excluded before treating gastric ulcers or dyspepsia in middle-aged or older patients with new/changed symptoms [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Infections:** PPI-induced hypochlorhydria may slightly increase the risk of infections such as *Salmonella*, *Campylobacter*, and *Clostridium difficile* [Australian].
- **Long-term Use Risks (>1 year):**
    - **Fractures:** Modest increase in risk of hip, wrist, and spine fractures, particularly in the elderly or those with risk factors [Australian].
    - **Hypomagnesaemia:** Reported rarely; can lead to tetany, arrhythmias, or seizures. Monitor magnesium before and during treatment if used long-term or with digoxin/diuretics [Australian].
    - **B12 Deficiency:** Potential malabsorption of cyanocobalamin with daily use exceeding 3 years [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Includes SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP. Discontinue at the first sign of a skin rash or hypersensitivity [Australian].
- **Subacute Cutaneous Lupus Erythematosus (SCLE):** Rare association; discontinue if lesions occur, especially in sun-exposed areas [Australian].
- **Acute Tubulointerstitial Nephritis (TIN):** May occur at any point; requires immediate discontinuation [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interferences:** Stop PPIs at least 14 days before Chromogranin A (CgA) measurements to avoid false-positive results for neuroendocrine tumours [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
**Lansoprazole** is generally well-tolerated with mild, transient effects.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache, dizziness, diarrhoea, constipation, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, nausea, vomiting, flatulence, skin rash, urticaria, pruritus, fatigue, malaise |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Not specifically categorised in available sources |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Paraesthesia, taste disturbances, depression, confusion, hallucinations, jaundice, hepatitis, angioedema, wheezing, anaphylaxis, TIN, haematological effects (thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia), gynaecomastia, erectile dysfunction |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Rebound acid hypersecretion upon withdrawal; SCARs (SJS, TEN, DRESS, AGEP); SCLE; hypomagnesaemia; interstitial pneumonia [Australian] |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **pH-Dependent Absorption:** May interfere with the bioavailability of **ketoconazole**, **ampicillin esters**, **iron salts**, and **digoxin** [Australian].
- **HIV Protease Inhibitors:** Contraindicated with **atazanavir**; not recommended with **nelfinavir** [Australian].
- **Tacrolimus:** May increase tacrolimus whole blood levels, especially in CYP2C19 intermediate/poor metabolisers [Australian].
- **Methotrexate:** Concomitant use (especially at high doses) may elevate and prolong methotrexate serum levels; consider temporary PPI withdrawal [Australian].
- **Mycophenolate Mofetil:** May reduce exposure to the active metabolite (mycophenolic acid) [Australian].
- **Sucralfate and Antacids:** Delayed absorption of **lansoprazole**; take **lansoprazole** at least one hour before these agents [Australian].
- **CYP450 System:** **Lansoprazole** is a weak inducer. Monitor patients on **warfarin**, **theophylline**, **phenytoin**, or **carbamazepine** [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category **B3**. Animal studies showed increased foetal mortality in rabbits (>10 mg/kg/day). Use only if the benefit clearly outweighs the potential risk to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Secreted in animal milk; human data is absent. Use during breastfeeding should be avoided [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Human male fertility effects not evaluated [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for ages 6–17. Insufficient data for use in paediatric patients with renal or hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No dosage adjustment generally required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required in adults [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** **Lansoprazole** is substantially metabolised in the liver. Metabolism is prolonged in severe impairment; consider dose adjustment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the H+/K+-ATPase (proton pump) in gastric parietal cells, blocking the final phase of acid secretion [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** A single 30 mg dose inhibits stimulated acid secretion by ~80%. Repeated dosing results in 90% inhibition [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is 80–90%. Absorption is affected by food, but acid inhibition is not significantly clinical affected [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 98% protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively hepatic [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Metabolites are excreted via renal (up to 50%) and biliary routes. Elimination half-life is 1–2 hours, but pharmacological effect lasts 24 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Reflux Oesophagitis:** Pivotal trials showed **lansoprazole** 15/30 mg was significantly superior to placebo and ranitidine, and equal in efficacy to omeprazole 20 mg in maintaining remission over 12 months [Australian].
- **H. pylori:** Combination therapy showed eradication rates of up to 90%, specifically with clarithromycin-based regimens [Australian].
- **Paediatrics:** Open-label and double-blind studies in children (1–17 years) confirmed significant reduction in GORD symptoms and high healing rates for erosive oesophagitis [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C in the original container [Australian].
- Protect from moisture and light (refer to packaging for specific shelf life) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
