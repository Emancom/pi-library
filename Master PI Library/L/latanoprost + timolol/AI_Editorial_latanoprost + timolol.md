# Editorial Product Information: latanoprost + timolol

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **latanoprost + timolol**
- Chemical class:
  - latanoprost: prostaglandin analogue (prostaglandin F analogue) — described in sources as a prostaglandin/prostaglandin analogue component
  - timolol: non‑selective beta‑adrenergic (beta‑blocker) agent — described in sources as the beta‑adrenergic component
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Ophthalmic (topical eye drops)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Reduction of elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with open‑angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension who are insufficiently responsive to beta‑blockers, prostaglandins or other IOP‑lowering medications.
- Important prescribing note: **latanoprost + timolol should not be used to initiate glaucoma therapy** (combination product is intended for patients already requiring both agents or insufficiently controlled on monotherapy/other agents).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- Recommended dosage: one drop in the affected eye(s) once daily.
- Do not administer more than once daily; latanoprost component is most effective with once‑daily dosing.
- If one dose is missed, resume usual dosing at the next scheduled time (i.e., skip the missed dose and continue with the next dose as normal).

### Dosing by indication
- Indication‑specific dosing beyond the general recommendation is not specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Administer one drop into each affected eye once daily.
- If more than one topical ophthalmic product is used, allow an interval of at least 5 minutes between different products.
- To reduce systemic absorption, apply digital pressure to the nasolacrimal duct (nasolacrimal occlusion) immediately after instillation.
- Remove contact lenses prior to instillation; contact lenses may be reinserted 15 minutes after administration because the product contains benzalkonium chloride which may be absorbed by lenses and interact with lens preservatives.
- Each bottle is for individual patient use only. Discard 4 weeks after opening. (Note: storage and shelf‑life details beyond discard after opening are not included here.)

### Titration and switching
- The product is not recommended to initiate glaucoma therapy. If response is inadequate on once‑daily combination therapy, consider use of the individual agents (including timolol given twice daily) as clinically appropriate.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to latanoprost, timolol maleate, or any component of the product.
- Reactive airway disease including bronchial asthma, a history of bronchial asthma, or severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).
- Sinus bradycardia, sick sinus syndrome, sino‑atrial block.
- Second‑ or third‑degree atrioventricular block.
- Overt cardiac failure or cardiogenic shock.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- **Systemic absorption of the timolol component can produce effects characteristic of systemic beta‑blockade (including bronchospasm and cardiac effects) — severe and occasionally fatal outcomes (e.g., death due to bronchospasm in asthma; death associated with cardiac failure) have been reported with timolol. Use with caution in patients with respiratory or cardiac disease.**

### General warnings and precautions
- Systemic absorption: Topically applied ophthalmic agents may be systemically absorbed; monitor for systemic beta‑blocker effects.
- Cardiovascular/respiratory:
  - Use caution in patients with bronchospastic disease. Timolol may cause or exacerbate bronchospasm, including fatal events in patients with asthma.
  - Beta‑blocker component may aggravate Prinzmetal’s angina, severe peripheral and central circulatory disorders, bradycardia, and hypotension.
  - Use with caution in patients with first‑degree heart block; avoid in second‑ or third‑degree AV block (contraindicated).
  - Cardiac failure should be controlled before initiating therapy; monitor patients with severe cardiac disease for signs of heart failure.
- Perioperative considerations:
  - Gradual withdrawal of systemic beta‑adrenergic blocking agents before major surgery may be considered.
  - Beta‑blockade may blunt beta‑adrenergically mediated reflex responses during anaesthesia and may complicate management of hypotension or bradycardia; inform anaesthesiologists if patient is receiving timolol.
- Hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis:
  - Patients with a history of atopy or severe anaphylactic reactions may show increased sensitivity to allergens while on beta‑blockers and may be refractory to usual doses of adrenaline.
- Concomitant therapy:
  - The IOP‑lowering effect or systemic beta‑blockade may be exaggerated when administered concurrently with oral beta‑blocking agents.
  - Avoid concomitant ophthalmic administration of two prostaglandin analogues; paradoxical elevations in IOP have been reported with concurrent ophthalmic prostaglandin analogues.
  - Use of two or more topical beta‑blockers or two or more topical prostaglandins/prostaglandin analogues is not recommended.
- Hypoglycaemia/diabetes:
  - Beta‑blockers may increase the hypoglycaemic effects of antidiabetic agents and can mask signs and symptoms of hypoglycaemia; exercise caution in patients with diabetes, especially labile diabetics on insulin or sulfonylureas.
- Hyperthyroidism:
  - Beta‑blockers may mask certain signs of hyperthyroidism and abrupt withdrawal may precipitate symptom worsening.
- Myasthenia gravis:
  - Beta‑blocker therapy may aggravate symptoms.
- Ophthalmic effects:
  - Iris pigmentation: latanoprost may increase brown pigmentation of the iris; typically gradual, may be permanent, and unilateral treatment can produce heterochromia. Patients with mixed‑colour irides are at higher risk.
  - Eyelid and eyelash changes: may include darkening of palpebral skin, increased eyelash length/thickness/number, pigmentation changes, misdirected growth; some eyelid skin darkening may be reversible; eyelash changes reversible on discontinuation.
  - Macular oedema (including cystoid macular oedema): reported primarily in aphakic or pseudophakic patients with torn posterior lens capsule or patients with known risk factors for macular oedema; use with caution in these patients.
  - Herpetic keratitis: use with caution in patients with history of herpetic keratitis; avoid in active herpes simplex keratitis and in patients with history of recurrent herpetic keratitis associated with prostaglandin analogues.
  - Choroidal detachment has been reported after filtration procedures with ocular hypotensives.
  - Ophthalmic beta‑blockers may induce or worsen dry eye and may exacerbate corneal disease; treat corneal disease patients with caution.
- Use with contact lenses: product contains benzalkonium chloride, which can be absorbed by contact lenses and may interact with thiomersal‑containing lens solutions to form precipitates. Remove contact lenses before instillation and reinsert after 15 minutes.
- Use in elderly: No specific data available in sources to indicate different dosing or adverse event frequency; general caution advised as with systemic beta‑blocker effects.
- Paediatric use: Not recommended; safety and effectiveness in children have not been established.
- Laboratory tests: No specific effects on laboratory tests specified in available sources.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for respiratory symptoms (wheezing, dyspnoea) and for cardiac signs (bradycardia, hypotension, heart failure).
- Monitor IOP to assess efficacy; reassess if inadequate response.
- Periodically observe for changes in iris pigmentation, eyelid skin changes, eyelash growth, and signs of macular oedema, particularly in at‑risk patients (aphakic, pseudophakic with capsule tear).
- Monitor blood glucose and diabetic control when used in patients with diabetes.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Additive hypotension and/or bradycardia may occur when timolol ophthalmic drops are used with:
    - Oral beta‑blocking agents (risk of potentiated systemic beta‑blockade)
    - Calcium channel blockers
    - Catecholamine‑depleting drugs
    - Antiarrhythmics (including amiodarone, quinidine)
    - Digitalis glycosides
    - Parasympathomimetics
    - Narcotics
    - Monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors
  - Concomitant use with oral beta‑blockers may exaggerate systemic beta‑blockade effects (decreased heart rate, hypotension).
  - Timolol may block systemic effects of administered beta‑agonists (e.g., adrenaline) — inform anaesthesiologists and emergency clinicians.
  - Concomitant ophthalmic prostaglandin analogues: paradoxical elevations in IOP have been reported with administration of two prostaglandin analogues; avoid using more than one topical prostaglandin or prostaglandin analogue.
  - Combining two topical beta‑blockers is not recommended.
  - Beta‑blockers may increase hypoglycaemic effects of antidiabetic agents and may mask symptoms of hypoglycaemia; concomitant use with sulfonylureas may increase risk of severe hypoglycaemia.
- Pharmacokinetic / metabolic interactions:
  - Potentiation of systemic beta‑blockade (e.g., decreased heart rate, depression) has been reported when timolol is co‑administered with CYP2D6 inhibitors (examples reported: quinidine, fluoxetine, paroxetine).
- Other interactions and administration considerations:
  - Mydriasis has occasionally been reported when timolol is given with adrenaline.
  - In vitro precipitation occurs when thiomersal‑containing eye drops are mixed with benzalkonium chloride (the preservative in this product); if thiomersal‑containing ophthalmic products are used, administer with an interval of at least 5 minutes between applications.
  - Several contact lens soaking solutions contain thiomersal; do not co‑administer without appropriate interval and remove lenses prior to instillation.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Australian pregnancy categorisation: **Category C** (as stated in available source).
- Animal data (summarised in available source):
  - Latanoprost and/or its metabolites cross the placenta in rats. In rabbits, latanoprost caused embryo‑foetal toxicity (increased late resorption, reduced foetal weight) at certain intravenous doses; no embryo‑foetal effects at the lowest tested doses in rabbits and at tested doses in rats up to specified levels.
  - Timolol maleate was not teratogenic in mice, rats and rabbits at lower doses; embryo‑foetal effects (increases in resorptions, foetal variations, delayed ossification) were seen at high doses in animal studies.
- Human data: No adequate and well‑controlled studies in pregnant women were reported in available source material.
- Recommendation: Use during pregnancy is not recommended / product should not be used during pregnancy (per available source guidance).

### Breastfeeding
- Latanoprost: limited animal data; latanoprost and metabolites may pass into breast milk (limited experimental animal and no human data available in sources).
- Timolol: has been detected in human milk following oral and ocular administration.
- Recommendation: Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from systemic exposure to timolol (and possible latanoprost exposure), a decision should be made whether to discontinue breastfeeding or discontinue the drug, taking into account the importance of therapy to the mother.

### Fertility
- Fertility studies with the combination product have not been conducted.
- Animal data for individual agents reported no effects on fertility at specified doses in rats (as summarised in available source).

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Specific, detailed mechanism(s) of action for the combination product are not specified in available sources in mechanistic terms for the combination. (The components are described in sources as a prostaglandin/prostaglandin analogue component and a beta‑adrenergic blocker component.)

### Pharmacokinetics
- Active concentrations reported in available source:
  - Product concentration: latanoprost 50 micrograms/mL and timolol 5 mg/mL (6.83 mg timolol maleate) in aqueous buffer (pH 6.0) as stated in available source.
  - Each drop contains approximately 1.5 µg latanoprost and 150 µg timolol (values reported in available source).
- Systemic absorption can occur after topical ocular administration; measures such as nasolacrimal occlusion can minimise systemic exposure.
- Full ADME (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination) parameters for the combination product are not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Pharmacodynamic parameters (onset, peak effect, duration) specific to the combination product are not comprehensively described in available sources beyond the clinical dosing recommendation (once daily) and statements that latanoprost is most effective with once‑daily dosing.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- The available source reports pooled data from randomized, double‑blind comparative clinical trials including studies referred to as 004, 005 and 053:
  - Study designs: randomized, double‑blind comparative trials; two trials included a 6‑month double‑blind period (studies 004 and 005) and one included a 12‑month double‑blind period (study 053).
  - Treatment arms included the combination product (latanoprost + timolol), latanoprost monotherapy, and timolol monotherapy.
  - Reported patient numbers in the pooled safety tables: combination product N=394; latanoprost N=414; timolol N=415 (as provided in source adverse event tables).
  - Primary efficacy endpoints and detailed efficacy results are not described in the available source material included here.
  - Safety findings: adverse events summarized in Sections 6 and 7 above were derived from these trials; several ocular and systemic adverse events were reported at varying frequencies across treatment arms.
- Additional clinical experience: post‑marketing reports for single agents (latanoprost or timolol) contributed further adverse event information (see Section 7).
