# Editorial Product Information: Lercanidipine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information regarding other regulatory authorities (e.g., US FDA, European EMA, Health Canada, Japanese PMDA) was not provided for this synthesis; therefore, jurisdictional variations are limited to the available source.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lercanidipine hydrochloride, a dihydropyridine calcium channel antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** ARX-Lercanidipine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C08CA13.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 10 mg (yellow, biconvex, scored) and 20 mg (pink, biconvex, scored) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Indicated for the treatment of essential hypertension [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg orally once daily. 
- **Titration:** Based on individual patient response, the dose may be increased to 20 mg once daily. Dose titration should be gradual, typically occurring over two weeks, as the maximal antihypertensive effect may take this long to manifest.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be taken at least 15 minutes before a meal. Bioavailability is significantly increased when taken with or after food.
- **Maximum Dose:** Doses above 20 mg are not recommended as they are unlikely to increase efficacy but are associated with an increased risk of adverse effects.
- **Combination Therapy:** May be co-administered with diuretics, ACE inhibitors, or beta-blockers [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to lercanidipine, any other dihydropyridine, or any excipient.
- Left ventricular outflow tract obstruction.
- Untreated congestive cardiac failure.
- Unstable angina pectoris.
- Recent myocardial infarction (within one month).
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- Severe renal impairment (GFR < 30 mL/min), including patients on dialysis.
- **Specific Co-administrations [Australian]:**
    - Strong inhibitors of CYP3A4 (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir).
    - Ciclosporin.
    - Grapefruit or grapefruit juice.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ischaemic Heart Disease:** Caution is required in patients with ischaemic heart disease. Some dihydropyridines may rarely cause precordial pain or angina; in rare cases, pre-existing angina may increase in frequency, duration, or severity.
- **Left Ventricular Dysfunction:** Care is required in patients with asymptomatic left ventricular dysfunction.
- **Peritoneal Dialysis:** Associated with cloudy peritoneal effluent due to increased triglyceride concentration. This is reversible upon drug withdrawal and should not be mistaken for infective peritonitis.
- **Sick Sinus Syndrome:** Use with caution in patients without a pacemaker.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Anticonvulsants (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine) and rifampicin may reduce efficacy.
- **Excipients:** Contains lactose; not recommended for patients with galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Treatment is generally well-tolerated, with most events related to the drug's vasodilatory effects.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache, flushing, palpitations, tachycardia, peripheral oedema, dizziness. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Nausea, dyspepsia, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, somnolence, asthenia, vertigo. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypotension, angina pain, myocardial infarction, dyspnoea, vomiting, rash, pruritus, myalgia, polyuria, migraine. |

- **Post-marketing/Serious Effects:** Gingival hypertrophy, syncope, angioedema, and isolated reversible increases in serum transaminases.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP3A4):** Lercanidipine is a substrate of CYP3A4.
    - **Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin) are contraindicated. Cimetidine at high doses (800 mg/day) may increase lercanidipine levels.
    - **Inducers:** Rifampicin and anticonvulsants may significantly reduce plasma levels.
- **Ciclosporin:** Contraindicated; co-administration increases lercanidipine levels 3-fold.
- **Alcohol:** Should be avoided as it may potentiate vasodilating antihypertensive effects.
- **Simvastatin:** May cause an increase in simvastatin AUC (56%). It is recommended to dose lercanidipine in the morning and simvastatin in the evening.
- **Digoxin:** Monitor patients for signs of digoxin toxicity; Cmax may increase by 33%.
- **Metoprolol/Beta-blockers:** Metoprolol may reduce lercanidipine bioavailability by 50%.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C [Australian]**. Not recommended during pregnancy or for women of childbearing potential unless effective contraception is used. Animal studies indicate foetal visceral abnormalities and skeletal delays at maternally toxic doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** Highly lipophilic; distribution into milk is expected. Use is not recommended in lactating women.
- **Fertility:** Reversible biochemical changes in spermatozoa heads have been reported with calcium channel blockers, potentially impairing fecundation.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients under 18 years; use is not recommended.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is generally required, but special caution should be exercised during treatment initiation.
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment (GFR < 30 mL/min). Caution is advised in mild to moderate impairment when increasing the dose from 10 mg to 20 mg.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic disease. Use with caution in moderate impairment as bioavailability and hypotensive effects may be increased.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A dihydropyridine calcium antagonist that selectively inhibits the transmembrane influx of calcium into cardiac and vascular smooth muscle. It possesses high vascular selectivity and a prolonged antihypertensive effect due to a high membrane partition coefficient.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Completely absorbed; absolute bioavailability is ~10% due to high first-pass metabolism. Tmax: 1.5–3 hours. Food increases bioavailability up to 4-fold.
    - **Distribution:** Rapid and extensive; protein binding exceeds 98%.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by CYP3A4 to inactive metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 8–10 hours; therapeutic activity lasts 24 hours. Approximately 50% of the dose is excreted in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Essential Hypertension:** In placebo-controlled trials (4–16 weeks), lercanidipine 10–20 mg significantly lowered systolic and diastolic blood pressure compared to placebo. 24-hour ambulatory monitoring confirmed the effect is maintained throughout the dosing interval.
- **Long-term Efficacy:** Studies up to 12 months showed no development of tolerance; the majority of patients were maintained on 10 mg once daily.
- **Isolated Systolic Hypertension:** Shown to be efficacious in elderly patients, significantly reducing systolic blood pressure compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
