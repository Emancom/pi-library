# Editorial Product Information: Leucovorin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **Leucovorin**
- Chemical class: Reduced folate (5‑formyl derivative of tetrahydrofolic acid); racemic mixture; biologically active component is the (-)-L isomer (leucovorin, also known as citrovorum factor or (-)-folinic acid).
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral and parenteral (IM, IV).  
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form physical descriptions, or schedule listings (not provided).

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date(s) of last revision/update of source PI(s): Revised: 7/2026 (as stated in available source material).
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- To diminish the toxicity and counteract the effects of impaired methotrexate elimination and to treat inadvertent overdosage of folic acid antagonists.  
- Use to counteract hematologic toxicity from other folic acid antagonists (e.g., trimethoprim, pyrimethamine) is referenced; lower daily doses (5–15 mg) have been recommended by some investigators for such indications.  
- If an indication differs by jurisdiction in the available sources: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adults)
- Oral administration is an approved route; however, oral absorption is saturable at doses >25 mg and therefore oral dosing greater than 25 mg is not recommended.
- Doses greater than 25 mg should be administered parenterally.

### Dosing for impaired methotrexate elimination or inadvertent overdosage of folic acid antagonists
- Leucovorin rescue should begin as promptly as possible after an inadvertent overdosage and within 24 hours of methotrexate administration when delayed excretion is present.
- Recommended initial regimen (as stated in available source): Leucovorin 15 mg (10 mg/m   ) administered IM, IV, or PO every 6 hours until serum methotrexate level is less than 10   M.
  - In the presence of gastrointestinal toxicity (nausea, vomiting) or inability to absorb oral drug, parenteral administration is preferred.
- Monitoring: Serum creatinine and serum methotrexate concentrations should be measured at 24‑hour intervals.
- Escalation for delayed elimination or renal impairment: If 24‑hour serum creatinine has increased by 50% over baseline or if the 24‑hour methotrexate level is greater than 5 x 10   M or the 48‑hour level is greater than 9 x 10   M, increase leucovorin to 150 mg (100 mg/m   ) IV every 3 hours until the methotrexate level is less than 10   M.
- Hydration and alkalinization: Concomitant hydration (approximately 3 L/day) and urinary alkalinization with sodium bicarbonate are recommended; adjust bicarbonate to maintain urine pH at 7 or greater.
- For patients with delayed early methotrexate elimination who develop reversible non‑oliguric renal failure, continue hydration, urinary alkalinization, close monitoring of fluid and electrolytes, and leucovorin rescue until serum methotrexate has fallen to below 0.05 micromolar and renal function has recovered.
- If significant clinical toxicity was observed in a course, consider extending leucovorin rescue in subsequent courses for an additional 24 hours (total 14 doses over 84 hours) when indicated.

### Dosing for other folate antagonist toxicities
- For folic acid antagonists with less affinity for mammalian dihydrofolate reductase than methotrexate (e.g., trimethoprim, pyrimethamine), substantially lower doses of leucovorin have been recommended; some investigators suggest 5–15 mg/day.

### Administration instructions
- Parenteral administration is preferable when there is any risk of vomiting or malabsorption.
- Do not rely on leucovorin to prevent methotrexate‑related nephrotoxicity due to precipitation of drug/metabolites in the kidney.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Leucovorin is inappropriate therapy for pernicious anemia and other megaloblastic anemias due to vitamin B12 deficiency; hematologic improvement can occur while neurologic deterioration continues.
- No other contraindications are specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed or prominent warnings
- Not specified as a boxed (formal) warning in the available sources; clinically important warnings are listed below.

### General warnings and precautions
- Timing of rescue: The effectiveness of leucovorin in preventing hematologic toxicity decreases as the time interval between antifolate (e.g., methotrexate) administration and leucovorin rescue increases. Administer rescue as promptly as possible.
- Monitoring of serum methotrexate concentration is essential to determine optimal leucovorin dose and duration.
- Delayed methotrexate excretion may result from third‑space fluid accumulations (e.g., ascites, pleural effusion), renal insufficiency, or inadequate hydration; higher or prolonged leucovorin dosing may be required under these circumstances.
- Leucovorin does not prevent methotrexate nephrotoxicity arising from drug/metabolite precipitation.
- Leucovorin may enhance toxicity of fluorouracil (5‑FU); severe and fatal enterocolitis, diarrhea, and dehydration have been reported in elderly patients receiving weekly leucovorin plus fluorouracil; some patients had concomitant granulocytopenia and fever.
- Concomitant use of leucovorin with trimethoprim‑sulfamethoxazole for acute treatment of Pneumocystis carinii (jirovecii) pneumonia in patients with HIV infection was associated with increased rates of treatment failure and mortality in a placebo‑controlled study.
- Excessive amounts of leucovorin may negate the chemotherapeutic effects of folic acid antagonists.

### Monitoring requirements
- Serial serum methotrexate concentrations and serum creatinine measurements at 24‑hour intervals during rescue.
- Monitor fluid and electrolyte status in patients with delayed methotrexate elimination or renal dysfunction.
- Monitor for signs of enhanced fluorouracil toxicity (severe diarrhea, enterocolitis, dehydration) when co‑administered.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use parenteral route if vomiting or impaired absorption is suspected.
- Reevaluate concomitant medications that may interfere with methotrexate elimination or protein binding when laboratory abnormalities or clinical toxicities occur.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Fluorouracil (5‑FU): Leucovorin can enhance the cytotoxicity/toxicity of 5‑FU. Severe and fatal gastrointestinal toxicity (enterocolitis, diarrhea, dehydration) has been reported, particularly in elderly patients receiving weekly leucovorin plus 5‑FU. Monitor closely when these agents are combined; consider risk/benefit and be vigilant for signs of severe GI toxicity and infection.
- Trimethoprim‑sulfamethoxazole (co‑trimoxazole): Concomitant use of leucovorin with trimethoprim‑sulfamethoxazole for acute Pneumocystis treatment in patients with HIV was associated with increased treatment failure and higher mortality in one placebo‑controlled study. Exercise caution and consider alternative strategies; monitor clinical response.
- High doses of leucovorin may reduce the efficacy of intrathecal methotrexate; small quantities of systemically administered leucovorin enter CSF primarily as 5‑methyltetrahydrofolate and remain well below usual methotrexate concentrations following intrathecal administration, but high systemic doses could be problematic.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Folic acid in large amounts (not specifically leucovorin) may counteract the antiepileptic effect of phenobarbital, phenytoin, and primidone and may increase seizure frequency in susceptible children.
- Drugs or conditions that delay methotrexate elimination (e.g., third‑space fluid, renal insufficiency, concomitant drugs that interfere with methotrexate elimination or albumin binding) will affect the need for and dosing of leucovorin rescue. Reassess concomitant medicines that may interact with methotrexate when laboratory abnormalities or clinical toxicities are observed.

### Management notes
- Monitor methotrexate serum concentrations and renal function to guide leucovorin dosing and duration.
- Use parenteral leucovorin if gastrointestinal absorption is unreliable.
- Consider avoiding or carefully monitoring combinations with fluorouracil or trimethoprim‑sulfamethoxazole as noted above.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Leucovorin** is a reduced folate (5‑formyl derivative of tetrahydrofolic acid). It provides one‑carbon units for folate‑dependent biochemical reactions without requiring reduction by dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR). The biologically active component of the racemic mixture is the (-)-L isomer (citrovorum factor).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Following oral administration, leucovorin is rapidly absorbed and increases plasma/serum folate activity; oral absorption is saturable at doses above 25 mg.
- Time to peak concentration (mean): approximately 1.72 ± 0.08 hours (reported in a study of 20 normal men given a single oral 15 mg dose).
- Peak serum folate concentration observed (mean): approximately 268 ± 18 ng/mL (assayed microbiologically).
- Apparent serum half‑disappearance time: approximately 3.5 hours (reported).
- Relative bioavailability and dose dependency: Oral AUCs were 12% greater than equal IM doses and equivalent to IV for the same amounts in the reported study. Apparent bioavailability was reported as approximately 97% for 25 mg, 75% for 50 mg, and 37% for 100 mg (demonstrating saturable absorption).
- Distribution/metabolism/elimination: Small quantities of systemically administered leucovorin enter the CSF primarily as 5‑methyltetrahydrofolate; detailed distribution, metabolism, and elimination parameters beyond those above are not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Repletes reduced folate pools and bypasses DHFR, thereby antagonizing the effects of folic acid antagonists (e.g., methotrexate) on normal tissues and reducing hematologic and other toxicities associated with antifolate therapy.
