# Editorial Product Information: Lanthanum Carbonate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information regarding other major jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not available in the provided source material and is therefore marked as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active ingredient:** Lanthanum carbonate hydrate (a phosphate binder).
- **Brand name(s):** **FOSRENOL** [Australian].
- **ATC code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage form(s) and strength(s):** 
    - Chewable tablets: 500 mg, 750 mg, and 1000 mg (as lanthanum) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/sponsor:** Takeda Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hyperphosphataemia in adults:** Indicated for the reduction of serum phosphate levels in adult patients with chronic renal failure who are undergoing haemodialysis or continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The total daily dose should be divided and taken with or immediately after meals (usually three times daily).
- **Initial Dosing:** The starting dose is determined based on the patient's pre-treatment serum phosphate level [Australian]:
    - >1.8 to ≤2.4 mmol/L: 750 mg/day.
    - >2.4 to ≤2.9 mmol/L: 1500 mg/day.
    - >2.9 mmol/L: 2250 mg/day.
- **Titration:** Serum phosphate levels must be monitored every 2–3 weeks. The dose should be titrated until an acceptable phosphate level is reached. Most patients require 1500–3000 mg per day [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - **Crucial:** Tablets must be chewed completely before swallowing to prevent serious gastrointestinal complications.
    - Tablets may be crushed as an aid to chewing but must **not** be swallowed whole [Australian].
- **Missed dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to lanthanum carbonate or any excipients [Australian].
- Bowel obstruction, ileus, and faecal impaction [Australian].
- Hypophosphataemia [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** Serious cases of obstruction, ileus, subileus, and gastrointestinal perforation have been reported, some requiring surgery. Risks are higher in patients with altered GI anatomy (e.g., diverticular disease, peritonitis, history of surgery/cancer) or hypomotility disorders. Treatment should be re-evaluated if severe constipation develops [Australian].
- **Chewing Requirement:** Failure to chew tablets completely is linked to serious GI complications [Australian].
- **Tissue Deposition:** Lanthanum deposits in bone, liver, and the stomach wall in animals. While human bone studies for up to 4.5 years showed no adverse effects on bone health, the long-term clinical impact of deposition in other tissues is unknown [Australian].
- **Abdominal X-rays:** Lanthanum carbonate is radio-opaque and may give an appearance typical of an imaging agent on abdominal X-rays [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of serum phosphate and calcium is required [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 24% of patients in clinical trials reported drug-related adverse reactions. Most are gastrointestinal in nature and can be minimised by taking the medication with food.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, allergic skin reactions (rash, urticaria, pruritus) [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Constipation, dyspepsia, flatulence, hypocalcaemia [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Gastroenteritis, laryngitis, eosinophilia, hyperparathyroidism, hypercalcaemia, hyperglycaemia, hyper/hypophosphataemia, anorexia, dizziness, taste alteration, vertigo, eructation, IBS, dry mouth, oesophagitis, stomatitis, ileus, subileus, alopecia, arthralgia, myalgia, osteoporosis, fatigue, peripheral oedema [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Intestinal perforation, tooth injury [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Gastrointestinal perforation and bowel obstruction are the most critical serious risks [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Binding Potential:** Lanthanum may bind to drugs with anionic functions (e.g., carboxyl, carbonyl, and hydroxyl groups) in the GI tract.
- **Antacids:** Drugs known to interact with antacids (e.g., chloroquine, hydroxychloroquine, ketoconazole) should not be taken within 2 hours of lanthanum [Australian].
- **Quinolone Antibiotics:** Bioavailability of ciprofloxacin is reduced by 50%. Quinolones should be taken 2 hours before or 4 hours after lanthanum [Australian].
- **Levothyroxine:** Absorption is reduced by ~40%. A 2-hour dosing gap is recommended, with closer TSH monitoring [Australian].
- **Tetracyclines/Doxycycline:** Co-administration should be avoided within a 2-hour window [Australian].
- **Gastric pH:** Lanthanum may increase gastric pH; interactions with pH-dependent drugs (e.g., PPIs) have not been formally studied but should be considered [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **[Australian Category B3]:** No evidence of teratogenicity in animals, but increased implantation loss and delayed skeletal ossification occurred at high doses. Safety in human pregnancy is not established. Use is not recommended unless benefits clearly outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** There is evidence of lanthanum excretion in human milk. Women taking lanthanum should stop breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies at high doses showed no alteration of mating or fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients <18 years. Potential risks to growing bones (e.g., growth retardation) are unknown [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required; effectiveness is similar in patients over and under 65 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in this population; however, patients may develop hypocalcaemia. Monitoring of serum calcium is essential [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Lanthanum is not metabolised by the liver but is excreted in bile. Caution is advised in patients with marked reduction in bile flow, as elimination may be slower, potentially increasing tissue deposition [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lanthanum ions released in the acidic gastric environment have a high affinity for dietary phosphate. They form highly insoluble lanthanum phosphate complexes that cannot be absorbed and are excreted in the faeces [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces phosphate absorption without a direct effect on PTH secretion, though PTH may fluctuate based on calcium/phosphate changes [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Extremely low; absolute oral bioavailability is <0.002% in humans.
    - **Distribution:** Predominantly stays in the GI tract. Absorbed fraction is >99.7% bound to plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Not metabolised.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via faeces (>90%). Renal clearance is negligible (0.000031%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 301:** A 25-week study compared lanthanum to calcium carbonate. Both effectively reduced serum phosphate to target levels (approx. 1.8 mmol/L), with 66% of the lanthanum group achieving control [Australian].
- **Study 302:** A 4-week placebo-controlled maintenance study showed that phosphate levels remained stable in the lanthanum group (1.9 mmol/L) but rose significantly in the placebo group (2.5 mmol/L) [Australian].
- **Bone Health:** Long-term biopsy studies (up to 4.5 years) showed no evidence of osteomalacia or mineralisation defects compared to standard therapy [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
