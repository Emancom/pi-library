# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or the local regulatory requirements for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the US (FDA) source was available for analysis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN), an amino acid derivative and a carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection containing 1 g of levocarnitine per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism which result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - Subsequent daily doses are generally 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administration: Slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating parenteral therapy.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. These can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment and initiate appropriate care [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In patients with renal impairment, chronic administration of high oral doses (not typically applicable to the IV route) may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO). However, safety and efficacy of the oral form in renal insufficiency have not been established [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects listed below were reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients at a frequency ≥5%, regardless of causality [US].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache (22%), Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Nausea (8-12%), Vomiting (15-21%), Diarrhea (16-35%), Abdominal pain (21% at 10mg dose), Infection (15-24%), Injection site reaction (27-38%), Pain (21-35%), Flu syndrome (15-29%), Chest pain (6-15%), Asthenia (8-12%), Accidental injury (8-12%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Fever, Tachycardia, Palpitations, Arrhythmia, Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Peripheral edema, Weight increase/decrease, Depression, Dizziness, Paresthesia, Insomnia, Dyspnea, Pharyngitis, Cough increase, Rhinitis, Pruritus, Rash |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified in provided source]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight, similar to adults (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Oral levocarnitine should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine clears accumulated acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitines, which are then excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Dose-Ranging Study:** In a study of ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg IV three times per week, mean trough levocarnitine concentrations increased proportionally with the dose. After 24 weeks, trough levels reached 148 ± 50, 240 ± 60, and 456 ± 162 µmol/L respectively, compared to a baseline of approximately 21 µmol/L [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
