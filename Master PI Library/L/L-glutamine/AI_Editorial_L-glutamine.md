# Editorial Product Information: L-glutamine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of L-glutamine oral powder for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data to support evidence-based practice.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** L-glutamine (Amino acid)
- **Brand Names:** L-GLUTAMINE (Camber Pharmaceuticals, Inc.) [US]
- **ATC Code:** A16AA03 (Amino acids and derivatives)
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral powder, 5 grams per paper-foil-plastic laminate packet [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured for Camber Pharmaceuticals, Inc. by Annora Pharma Pvt. Ltd. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Sickle Cell Disease:** Indicated to reduce the acute complications of sickle cell disease in adult and paediatric patients 5 years of age and older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult and Paediatric Dosing (Age $\ge$ 5 years)
L-glutamine is administered twice daily, with the specific dose determined by body weight as follows:

| Weight (kg) | Weight (lb) | Dose per Administration | Total Daily Dose | Packets per Day |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| < 30 kg | < 66 lb | 5 g | 10 g | 2 |
| 30 to 65 kg | 66 to 143 lb | 10 g | 20 g | 4 |
| > 65 kg | > 143 lb | 15 g | 30 g | 6 |

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Mix the powder immediately before ingestion. 
- **Vehicle:** Each dose should be mixed in 8 oz. (240 mL) of a cold or room-temperature beverage (e.g., water, milk, apple juice) or 4 to 6 oz. of food (e.g., applesauce, yogurt) [US].
- **Cautions:** Do not use hot food or liquids. Complete dissolution is not required for administration.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as the patient remembers. Patients should not double the dose to compensate for a missed one [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Monitoring:** While no specific laboratory monitoring is mandated by the manufacturer, clinicians should note that clinical trials excluded patients with renal insufficiency or uncontrolled liver disease [US].
- **General Precautions:** Patients should be advised on the correct preparation and the requirement for weight-based dosing.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reaction data is based on clinical trials where L-glutamine was compared to placebo over 48 weeks.

### Common Adverse Reactions (Incidence > 10%) [US]
| Adverse Reaction | L-glutamine (N=187) | Placebo (N=111) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Constipation | 21% | 18% |
| Nausea | 19% | 14% |
| Headache | 18% | 15% |
| Abdominal Pain* | 17% | 16% |
| Cough | 16% | 14% |
| Pain in extremity | 13% | 7% |
| Back pain | 12% | 5% |
| Chest pain | 12% | 8% |
*\*Includes abdominal pain and upper abdominal pain.*

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Treatment discontinuation due to adverse reactions occurred in 2.7% of patients. Discontinuations were due to single cases of hypersplenism, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, burning sensation, and hot flashes [US].
- Deaths occurred in 1.6% (3/187) of the treatment group vs 0% in the placebo group; however, none were considered related to L-glutamine treatment [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No category assigned]. There are no available data regarding L-glutamine use in pregnant women to assess the risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted.
- **Breastfeeding:** No data exist regarding the presence of L-glutamine in human milk, its effects on milk production, or the breastfed infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug and potential risks [US].
- **Fertility:** The potential for impairment of human fertility is unknown; animal studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established in patients $\ge$ 5 years of age based on studies involving 110 paediatric patients. Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients < 5 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently. Dosing should be cautious, generally starting at the low end of the range to account for potential reductions in hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The pharmacokinetics of L-glutamine have not been studied in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** The pharmacokinetics of L-glutamine have not been studied in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
The exact mechanism in sickle cell disease (SCD) is not fully elucidated. Sickle red blood cells (RBCs) are highly susceptible to oxidative stress, which contributes to hemolysis and vaso-occlusive events. L-glutamine may improve the NAD redox potential in sickle RBCs by increasing the availability of reduced glutathione, thereby regulating and preventing oxidative damage [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Follows a saturable absorption process. After a single dose of 0.1 g/kg, peak concentration (Cmax) of ~150 mcg/mL occurs at 30 minutes. Cmax occurs at approximately one hour following multiple doses [US].
- **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is approximately 750 mL/kg.
- **Metabolism:** Metabolism is the primary clearance route. Endogenous L-glutamine is used in the synthesis of proteins, nucleotides, and amino sugars.
- **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life is approximately one hour (based on IV bolus data). Urinary excretion is < 0.3% [US].
- **Food Effect:** No significant change in concentration was observed with food; the drug may be taken without regard to meals [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Efficacy was established in a Phase 3, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (NCT01179217) involving 230 patients (5–58 years) with sickle cell anaemia or sickle $\beta^0$-thalassemia who had $\ge$ 2 painful crises in the preceding year.

- **Primary Outcome:** L-glutamine reduced the median number of sickle cell crises by 25% (3 vs 4 in the placebo group, p=0.005) over 48 weeks.
- **Secondary Outcomes:** L-glutamine treatment resulted in:
    - Fewer hospitalisations for sickle cell pain (median 2 vs 3).
    - Fewer cumulative days hospitalised (median 6.5 vs 11).
    - Lower incidence of acute chest syndrome (8.6% vs 23.1%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from direct sunlight [US].
