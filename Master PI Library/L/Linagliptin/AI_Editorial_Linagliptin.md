# Editorial Product Information: Empagliflozin and Linagliptin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the fixed-dose combination of empagliflozin and linagliptin for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for definitive prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Empagliflozin (a sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 [SGLT2] inhibitor) and Linagliptin (a dipeptidyl peptidase-4 [DPP-4] inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **GLYXAMBI** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A10BD19.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - Film-coated tablets [Australian]: 
        - 10 mg empagliflozin / 5 mg linagliptin (pale yellow, arc triangular).
        - 25 mg empagliflozin / 5 mg linagliptin (pale pink, arc triangular).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Boehringer Ingelheim [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM):** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults when treatment with both empagliflozin and linagliptin is appropriate [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:**
    - **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/5 mg once daily [Australian].
    - **Dose Escalation:** If the starting dose is tolerated but requires additional glycaemic control, the dose may be increased to 25 mg/5 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Switching Therapy:** Patients already on empagliflozin should transition to a **GLYXAMBI** dose that maintains their current empagliflozin exposure [Australian].
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food at any time of the day [Australian].
- **Combination with Secretagogues/Insulin:** A reduction in the dose of insulin or sulfonylureas may be necessary to mitigate the risk of hypoglycaemia [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to empagliflozin, linagliptin, or any of the product's excipients [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²), end-stage renal disease (ESRD), or patients on dialysis [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA):** SGLT2 inhibitors increase the risk of DKA, a potentially fatal condition. Cases may present with atypical "euglycaemic" blood glucose levels (<13.8 mmol/L). Therapy should be discontinued immediately if DKA is suspected. Risk factors include low carbohydrate diets, acute illness, surgery, or insulin deficiency [Australian].
- **Surgery:** Treatment should be ceased prior to major surgery or procedures involving prolonged fasting due to the risk of DKA [Australian].
- **Pancreatitis:** Acute pancreatitis has been reported with linagliptin. Discontinue treatment permanently if pancreatitis is suspected [Australian].
- **Necrotizing Fasciitis of the Perineum (Fournier’s Gangrene):** A rare but life-threatening infection reported with SGLT2 inhibitors. Prompt evaluation and treatment (antibiotics/surgical debridement) are required for patients presenting with pain, erythema, or swelling in the genital area accompanied by fever or malaise [Australian].
- **Volume Depletion:** Empagliflozin-induced osmotic diuresis may cause symptomatic hypotension, particularly in the elderly (>75 years), patients with low systolic BP, or those on diuretics [Australian].
- **Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs):** Increased risk of serious UTIs, including urosepsis and pyelonephritis. Temporary interruption may be considered for recurrent infections [Australian].
- **Bullous Pemphigoid:** Reported with DPP-4 inhibitors. Discontinue if suspected [Australian].
- **Lower Limb Amputation:** While not definitively linked to empagliflozin in pooled analyses, a class effect for SGLT2 inhibitors has been suggested; regular foot care is essential [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Not specifically categorised as >10% in initial combined trials, but hypoglycemia is common in triple therapy combinations [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Infections: Urinary tract infection, nasopharyngitis, vaginal moniliasis, vulvovaginitis, balanitis, and other genital infections [Australian].
    - Gastrointestinal: Constipation [Australian].
    - Musculoskeletal: Arthralgia [Australian].
    - Respiratory: Cough [Australian].
    - Investigations: Increased serum lipids, increased lipase [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Volume depletion, pruritus, rash, dysuria, increased blood creatinine, and decreased eGFR [Australian].
- **Rare/Very Rare (<0.1%):** Mouth ulceration (rare), tubulointerstitial nephritis (very rare) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Insulin and Sulfonylureas:** Increased risk of hypoglycaemia; dose reduction of the secretagogue may be required [Australian].
- **Diuretics:** Potential for enhanced diuretic effects, increasing risks of dehydration and hypotension [Australian].
- **Lithium:** Empagliflozin may increase renal lithium excretion, potentially lowering serum lithium levels. Monitor lithium concentrations closely [Australian].
- **UGT Inducers:** Empagliflozin is metabolised by UGT; however, clinically relevant interactions are not expected [Australian].
- **P-gp/CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Rifampicin):** May reduce the steady-state exposure and efficacy of linagliptin; efficacy may be sub-optimal [Australian].
- **P-gp/CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Ritonavir):** May increase linagliptin exposure, but no dose adjustment is required [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Limited data in humans. Animal studies showed adverse effects on renal development in the second and third trimesters. Avoid use during pregnancy unless clearly necessary [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Nonclinical data show excretion into milk. A risk to newborns/infants cannot be excluded. Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies do not indicate direct or indirect harmful effects on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients <18 years; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Patients ≥75 years are at increased risk of volume depletion. Initiation is not recommended in this age group [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - eGFR ≥30 mL/min/1.73 m²: No dose adjustment required [Australian].
    - eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²: Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Empagliflozin:** Inhibits SGLT2 in the proximal renal tubules, reducing glucose reabsorption and increasing urinary glucose excretion (UGE) [Australian].
    - **Linagliptin:** Reversibly binds to and inhibits DPP-4, preventing the degradation of incretin hormones (GLP-1 and GIP), thereby increasing insulin secretion and decreasing glucagon secretion in a glucose-dependent manner [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; median $T_{max}$ ~1.5 hours for both components. Food does not have a clinically relevant effect on exposure [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Empagliflozin protein binding is ~86%. Linagliptin shows concentration-dependent protein binding (saturable) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Empagliflozin undergoes glucuronidation (UGT2B7, 1A3, 1A8, 1A9). Linagliptin metabolism is a minor elimination pathway [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Empagliflozin is excreted via faeces (41%) and urine (54%). Linagliptin is primarily eliminated unchanged via the faecobiliary route (~80%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Factorial Design:** In patients inadequately controlled on metformin, the combination of empagliflozin and linagliptin provided statistically significant improvements in $HbA_{1c}$ and fasting plasma glucose (FPG) compared to the individual components alone [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Safety:**
    - **EMPA-REG OUTCOME:** Empagliflozin demonstrated a reduction in the risk of cardiovascular death and MACE-3 in adults with T2DM and established CV disease [Australian].
    - **CARMELINA:** Linagliptin demonstrated cardiovascular and renal safety in patients with T2DM and high CV/renal risk [Australian].
    - **CAROLINA:** Linagliptin demonstrated non-inferiority to glimepiride regarding 3P-MACE over a median follow-up of 6.25 years [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C [Australian].
- Shelf life is specified on the secondary packaging (refer to the ARTG for official details) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
