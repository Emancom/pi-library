# Editorial Product Information: Insulin Glargine and Lixisenatide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination product **insulin glargine** and **lixisenatide** for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements. As only the United States (FDA) source was provided for this synthesis, regional variations from other authorities (e.g., Australian TGA, European EMA) are not reflected unless otherwise noted.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** **Insulin glargine** (a basal insulin analog) and **lixisenatide** (a glucagon-like peptide-1 [GLP-1] receptor agonist).
- **Brand Name:** **SOLIQUA 100/33** [US].
- **ATC Code:** A10AE54.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection (solution). Each mL contains 100 units of insulin glargine and 33 mcg of lixisenatide. Supplied in a 3 mL prefilled, disposable, single-patient-use SoloStar pen [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi-Aventis U.S. LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus:** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** 
    - Not recommended for combination use with other GLP-1 receptor agonists.
    - Not indicated for the treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis.
    - Safety and efficacy have not been established in combination with prandial insulin.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Administer once daily via subcutaneous injection within one hour prior to the first meal of the day.
- **Dosing Range:** The SoloStar pen delivers doses from 15 to 60 units per injection. The maximum daily dose is 60 units (representing 60 units of insulin glargine and 20 mcg of lixisenatide) [US].
- **Initiation:**
    - **Naive to basal insulin/GLP-1 RA (or on <30 units basal insulin):** Start at 15 units once daily.
    - **Inadequately controlled on 30–60 units basal insulin (with or without GLP-1 RA):** Start at 30 units once daily.
- **Titration:** Adjust dosage by 2 to 4 units once weekly based on fasting plasma glucose targets and metabolic needs.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Inject into the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm.
    - Rotate injection sites within the same region to reduce the risk of lipodystrophy and localized cutaneous amyloidosis.
    - **Do not** administer intravenously or via an insulin pump.
    - **Do not** dilute or mix with other insulin products.
- **Missed Doses:** Resume the once-daily regimen with the next scheduled dose. Do not administer an extra dose to compensate for a missed one.

## 4. Contraindications
- During episodes of hypoglycemia.
- Known hypersensitivity to **insulin glargine**, **lixisenatide**, or any constituent excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** GLP-1 receptor agonists have been associated with pancreatitis, including fatal cases. Monitor for persistent, severe abdominal pain. Discontinue immediately if suspected.
- **Anaphylaxis and Hypersensitivity:** Severe, life-threatening allergic reactions can occur. Discontinue if symptoms of anaphylaxis arise.
- **Medication Errors:** Risk of accidental mix-ups with other insulin products. Patients must check the pen label before every injection. The pen must never be shared between patients, even if the needle is changed.
- **Hypoglycemia:** Risk is increased with intensive glycemic control and changes in meal patterns or physical activity. Patients with renal or hepatic impairment or hypoglycemia unawareness are at higher risk.
- **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI):** Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea may lead to volume depletion and subsequent AKI. Monitor renal function in patients with severe gastrointestinal (GI) reactions. Use is not recommended in end-stage renal disease (eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 m²).
- **Severe GI Disease:** Not recommended in patients with severe gastroparesis.
- **Hypokalemia:** Insulin may cause a potassium shift into the intracellular space, potentially leading to respiratory paralysis or arrhythmias.
- **Fluid Retention/Heart Failure:** Concomitant use with PPAR-gamma agonists (e.g., thiazolidinediones) may cause dose-related fluid retention and exacerbate heart failure.
- **Acute Gallbladder Disease:** Events such as cholelithiasis or cholecystitis have been reported.
- **Pulmonary Aspiration:** Reported in patients on GLP-1 RAs undergoing elective surgery under general anesthesia or deep sedation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are reported for the combination product based on pooled clinical trial data.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (10.0%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nasopharyngitis (7.0%), Diarrhea (7.0%), Upper respiratory tract infection (5.5%), Headache (5.4%) |
| **Other Reactions** | Hypoglycemia (rates vary by prior therapy), Injection-site reactions (1.7%), Lipodystrophy, Weight gain |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, acute pancreatitis, acute kidney injury, and severe hypoglycemia.
- **Postmarketing Reports:** Localized cutaneous amyloidosis, ileus, intestinal obstruction, and alopecia have been identified during post-approval use of the individual components.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Delayed Gastric Emptying:** Lixisenatide slows gastric emptying, which may reduce the rate of absorption of oral medications. 
    - **Oral Contraceptives:** Administer at least 1 hour before or 11 hours after the injection.
    - **Antibiotics/Acetaminophen:** Administer at least 1 hour before the injection.
- **Glucose Metabolism Modifiers:** 
    - Drugs increasing hypoglycemia risk: Antidiabetics, ACE inhibitors, fibrates, fluoxetine, MAOIs, and salicylates. 
    - Drugs decreasing blood glucose-lowering effect: Atypical antipsychotics, corticosteroids, diuretics, estrogens, and thyroid hormones.
    - **Beta-blockers/Clonidine:** May blunt the symptoms of hypoglycemia.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned; clinical data limited]. Animal studies with lixisenatide show potential for visceral and skeletal defects at doses causing maternal toxicity. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** No data regarding presence in human milk or effects on milk production. Endogenous insulin is present in milk. The developmental benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need for the drug.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies did not show direct adverse effects on fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in patients under 18 years of age.
- **Geriatric Use:** (≥65 years): Caution is advised; initial dosing and titration should be conservative to avoid hypoglycemia, which may be harder to detect in the elderly.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild/Moderate:** No dose adjustment required, but close monitoring for GI side effects and renal function changes is recommended.
    - **Severe (eGFR 15–30 mL/min):** Limited clinical experience; monitor closely.
    - **End-Stage (eGFR <15 mL/min):** Not recommended.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied. Frequent glucose monitoring is advised.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Insulin glargine:** Lowers blood glucose by stimulating peripheral glucose uptake and inhibiting hepatic glucose production.
    - **Lixisenatide:** A GLP-1 RA that enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppresses glucagon secretion, and slows gastric emptying.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median $t_{max}$ for lixisenatide is 2.5 to 3.0 hours. Glargine shows no pronounced peak.
    - **Distribution:** Lixisenatide is 55% protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Glargine is metabolized into active metabolites M1 and M2. Lixisenatide is eliminated via glomerular filtration and proteolytic degradation. Half-life is approximately 3 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study A (OAD Uncontrolled):** In patients uncontrolled on metformin (± second OAD), the combination resulted in a mean $HbA_{1c}$ reduction of 1.6% at 30 weeks, compared to 1.3% for glargine alone and 0.9% for lixisenatide alone.
- **Study B (Basal Insulin Uncontrolled):** In patients uncontrolled on 15–40 units of basal insulin, the combination achieved a mean $HbA_{1c}$ reduction of 1.1% compared to 0.6% for insulin glargine alone at 30 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Pens:** Store in a refrigerator between 2°C and 8°C (36°F–46°F). Do not freeze. Protect from light.
- **In-Use Pens:** Store at room temperature (up to 25°C / 77°F). Do not refrigerate.
- **Shelf Life (In-Use):** Discard the pen 28 days after first use. Remove the needle after every injection.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. SoloStar pen is a single-patient-use device.
