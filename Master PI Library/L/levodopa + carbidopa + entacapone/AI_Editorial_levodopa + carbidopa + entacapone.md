# Editorial Product Information: levodopa + carbidopa + entacapone

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **levodopa + carbidopa + entacapone**
- Chemical class:
  - Levodopa: dopamine precursor (aromatic L‑amino acid decarboxylase substrate)
  - Carbidopa: peripheral aromatic L‑amino acid decarboxylase (DDC) inhibitor
  - Entacapone: catechol‑O‑methyltransferase (COMT) inhibitor
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Management of patients with Parkinson’s disease who are experiencing motor fluctuations.
- Use limited to adult patients; not recommended for children and adolescents under age 18 due to lack of data on safety and efficacy.

(Note: No additional region‑specific indications were specified in the available sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Product is supplied in fixed combinations of levodopa : carbidopa : entacapone in strengths corresponding to levodopa/carbidopa amounts combined with 200 mg entacapone per tablet. Available levodopa/carbidopa strengths (with 200 mg entacapone) include: 50/12.5/200 mg, 75/18.75/200 mg, 100/25/200 mg, 125/31.25/200 mg, 150/37.5/200 mg, and 200/50/200 mg.
- Take orally. One tablet constitutes one treatment dose. Tablets must be taken whole; do not subdivide.
- Patients should be instructed to take only one fixed‑combination tablet at each administration (i.e., one tablet per dose).

### Maximum daily exposure limits and practical limits
- Maximum recommended daily entacapone dose: 2000 mg. Therefore:
  - For strengths with 200 mg entacapone per tablet, do not exceed 10 tablets/day for the 50/12.5/200, 75/18.75/200, 100/25/200, 125/31.25/200, and 150/37.5/200 strengths (10 × 200 mg = 2000 mg entacapone).
  - For the 200/50/200 mg strength, maximum daily tablets differ because of carbidopa exposure: using a conservative maximum carbidopa exposure of 375 mg/day (as discussed in available sources), the maximum is 7 tablets/day of the 200/50/200 mg strength.
- Maximum total daily levodopa administered as the fixed‑combination product should not exceed 1500 mg.

### Dose optimisation and titration
- Optimum daily dosage must be determined by careful titration for each patient.
- Preferably, daily dosing is optimised using one of the available fixed strengths; adjust dose by changing strength and/or dosing frequency.
- Entacapone enhances the effects of levodopa; after initiating the combination, particularly in patients with dyskinesia, levodopa dosage may need to be reduced by approximately 10–30% within days to weeks. Reduce levodopa by decreasing dose amount and/or extending dose intervals according to clinical response.

### Switching from other levodopa treatments
- Patients already taking standard‑release levodopa + carbidopa (or benserazide) together with entacapone tablets at doses that match a fixed‑combination tablet may be switched directly to the corresponding fixed‑combination tablet (one‑to‑one replacement).
- If current levodopa/DDC inhibitor dose does not correspond to available fixed strengths, carefully titrate the fixed‑combination to approximate the patient’s total daily levodopa dose.
- For patients on levodopa + benserazide with entacapone: discontinue levodopa/benserazide the previous night and start the fixed combination the next morning at a levodopa dose equal to or slightly (≈5–10%) higher than prior levodopa dose.
- Do not directly switch patients with significant dyskinesias and daily levodopa dose > 800 mg from levodopa/DDC inhibitor alone to the fixed‑combination. In such patients, introduce entacapone as a separate medication first and adjust levodopa as needed before switching to the fixed‑combination.

### Dose adjustment during treatment
- Increase frequency or strength when more levodopa is required, within maximum recommended dose limits.
- When less levodopa is required, decrease frequency, extend dosing intervals, or use a lower strength.
- If other levodopa products are used concomitantly, observe the maximum total dose constraints for levodopa, carbidopa and entacapone.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to levodopa, carbidopa, entacapone or any excipient.
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding (use contraindicated).
- Severe hepatic impairment (contraindicated).
- Narrow‑angle glaucoma.
- Phaeochromocytoma (increased risk of hypertensive crisis).
- Concomitant use with non‑selective monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAO‑A and MAO‑B) (e.g., phenelzine, tranylcypromine) is contraindicated.
- Concomitant use of a selective MAO‑A inhibitor plus a selective MAO‑B inhibitor and the combination product is contraindicated.
- Prior history of neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS) and/or non‑traumatic rhabdomyolysis.
- Presence of suspicious undiagnosed skin lesions or a history of malignant melanoma (levodopa may activate malignant melanoma).

(These contraindications reflect the full list provided in the available source materials.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Monitor for mental status changes, depression with suicidal tendencies and other serious antisocial behaviour; use caution in patients with current or past psychosis.
- Concomitant administration of dopamine D2 receptor‑blocking antipsychotics may reduce antiparkinsonian efficacy; observe patients carefully for loss of effect or worsening symptoms.
- May induce orthostatic hypotension; exercise caution when coadministering other agents that cause orthostatic hypotension.
- Entacapone in combination with levodopa has been associated with somnolence and sudden sleep onset; advise caution when driving or operating machinery.
- Entacapone potentiates levodopa and may precipitate or exacerbate dyskinesias; levodopa dose reduction may be required but may not fully eliminate dyskinesias.
- Discontinuation or abrupt reduction of dopaminergic therapy, including entacapone, has been associated with a syndrome resembling NMS; when stopping therapy consider slow withdrawal and monitor for symptoms; increase levodopa if clinically necessary.
- Prolonged or persistent diarrhoea may indicate colitis; discontinue drug if suspected and investigate.
- Progressive anorexia, asthenia and weight loss warrant medical evaluation including liver function assessment.
- Dopamine dysregulation syndrome (DDS) — addictive behaviour and excessive use of medication — may occur; warn patients and caregivers.
- Monitor regularly for impulse control disorders (pathological gambling, increased libido/hypersexuality, compulsive spending/buying, binge/compulsive eating, punding); review treatment if such behaviours develop.
- Use with caution in patients with ischaemic heart disease or history of arrhythmias; monitor cardiac function during initial dose adjustment if cardiac disease present.
- Exercise caution in patients with chronic wide‑angle glaucoma (monitor intraocular pressure).
- Use cautiously in patients with biliary obstruction, hepatic disease, severe cardiovascular or pulmonary disease, bronchial asthma, renal disease or endocrine disease.
- Periodic evaluation of hepatic, haematopoietic, cardiovascular and renal function is recommended during prolonged therapy.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of NMS (rigidity, hyperthermia, mental status changes, autonomic instability, elevated CPK) and rhabdomyolysis.
- Monitor weight in patients with diarrhoea or anorexia/asthenia and perform liver function tests if significant weight loss or anorexia occurs.
- Monitor INR in patients receiving warfarin when initiating or stopping therapy with the combination product (see Interactions).
- Monitor for impulse control and behavioural changes; involve caregivers in monitoring.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use cautiously in mild to moderate hepatic impairment; dose reduction may be necessary. Severe hepatic impairment is a contraindication.
- Renal impairment: entacapone pharmacokinetics are not affected by renal insufficiency; limited data on levodopa/carbidopa in renal impairment — use cautiously in severe renal impairment including dialysis patients.
- If general anaesthesia is required, the combination may be continued as long as the patient can take oral medication; if stopped temporarily, restart at previous dose as soon as oral therapy is feasible.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Major contraindicated interactions
- Concomitant use with non‑selective MAO inhibitors (both MAO‑A and MAO‑B inhibitors, e.g., phenelzine, tranylcypromine) — contraindicated.
- Concomitant use of a selective MAO‑A inhibitor plus a selective MAO‑B inhibitor together with the combination product — contraindicated.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Dopamine D2 receptor antagonists (e.g., phenothiazines, butyrophenones, risperidone, isoniazid): may reduce therapeutic effects of levodopa.
- Antihypertensive drugs: symptomatic postural hypotension may occur when levodopa is added to patients receiving antihypertensives; adjust antihypertensive dose if necessary.
- Antidepressants: rare reports of hypertension and dyskinesia with tricyclic antidepressants + levodopa/carbidopa.
- Metoclopramide: may increase levodopa bioavailability via increased gastric emptying but may antagonise levodopa effect because of dopamine receptor antagonism; use cautiously.
- Co‑administration with other antiparkinsonian medications (dopamine agonists, selegiline, amantadine) may increase dopaminergic adverse effects (e.g., dyskinesia); doses of concomitant antiparkinsonian drugs may need adjustment.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Warfarin: entacapone shows in vitro affinity for CYP2C9; in interaction study entacapone did not change S‑warfarin plasma levels but increased R‑warfarin AUC by ~18% and increased INR on average by ~13%. Monitor INR when initiating or stopping therapy.
- Phenytoin and papaverine: may reduce the therapeutic effect of levodopa; monitor for loss of effect.
- Pyroxidine (vitamin B6): oral pyridoxine (10–25 mg) can increase peripheral decarboxylation of levodopa, reversing its effects; carbidopa blocks this action — the fixed‑combination can be given with supplemental pyridoxine.
- Drugs metabolised by COMT: entacapone may interfere with metabolism of catechol‑containing drugs (isoprenaline, adrenaline, noradrenaline, dopamine, dobutamine, alpha‑methyldopa, apomorphine, paroxetine) and potentiate their action — monitor carefully. Intravenous administration of isoprenaline/adrenaline with a single 400 mg dose of entacapone (without DDC inhibitor) produced mean maximal heart rate changes ~50% and ~80% higher respectively than with placebo in a study; caution regardless of route of administration (including inhalation).
- Drugs affecting biliary excretion: as most entacapone is excreted in bile, exercise caution when coadministering drugs that interfere with biliary excretion.
- Protein binding / displacement: entacapone binds to human albumin binding site II (also binds diazepam, ibuprofen); displacement interactions not fully characterised — clinical interaction studies with diazepam and NSAIDs have not been carried out.

### Other interactions / administration considerations
- Food: levodopa absorption competes with certain amino acids; absorption may be impaired in some patients on high‑protein diets.
- Iron: levodopa and entacapone may chelate with iron in the gastrointestinal tract. Separate administration by at least 2–3 hours.
- Concomitant use with selegiline (selective MAO‑B inhibitor): entacapone may be used in combination with selegiline, but the daily dose of selegiline should not exceed 10 mg.

### Management notes
- Monitor INR with warfarin when initiating or discontinuing the combination.
- Monitor for increased dopaminergic adverse effects when used with other antiparkinsonian agents; consider levodopa dose reduction.
- Use caution with catecholamine drugs; monitor heart rate and blood pressure and consider dose adjustments.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- **Not recommended** for use in children below 18 years due to lack of data on safety and efficacy.

### Elderly use
- No routine dosage adjustment required for elderly patients (pharmacokinetic section referenced in source). However, limited clinical data may be available; use with usual geriatric caution and monitor for adverse effects.
- Note: one section in the source stated "No data available" under a heading referring to elderly use; overall the available material advises no routine dose adjustment but to exercise general clinical caution.

### Renal impairment
- Entacapone pharmacokinetics are not affected by renal insufficiency.
- There are no particular studies on levodopa and carbidopa pharmacokinetics in renal impairment. Use cautiously in patients with severe renal impairment, including those on dialysis.

### Hepatic impairment
- Severe hepatic impairment: contraindicated.
- Mild to moderate hepatic impairment: use cautiously; dose reduction may be necessary.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Levodopa is a dopamine precursor that is converted to dopamine in the brain, restoring dopaminergic transmission in Parkinson’s disease.
- Carbidopa is a peripheral aromatic L‑amino acid decarboxylase (DDC) inhibitor that reduces peripheral conversion of levodopa to dopamine, increasing central levodopa availability and reducing peripheral adverse effects such as nausea.
- Entacapone is a peripheral catechol‑O‑methyltransferase (COMT) inhibitor that prolongs levodopa plasma half‑life and enhances levodopa availability by reducing peripheral methylation.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Levodopa absorption can be impaired by high‑protein meals due to competition with amino acids; entacapone effect on absorption specifics not detailed in available sources.
- Distribution: Entacapone binds to human albumin binding site II. Specific volumes of distribution not provided.
- Metabolism: Entacapone shows in vitro affinity for CYP2C9; its primary metabolic/excretory route is biliary excretion.
- Elimination: Most entacapone excretion occurs via bile.
- Specific PK parameters (half‑life, Cmax, Tmax, bioavailability for each component): Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Entacapone potentiates the effect of levodopa; concomitant entacapone increases levodopa exposure/effect and may increase dopaminergic adverse effects such as dyskinesia.
- Interaction potential with drugs metabolised by COMT or affecting CYP2C9 substrates (e.g., warfarin) as described in Interactions.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Trials referenced in source material include NOMECOMT, SEESAW, CELOMEN and UK‑IRISH trials. Key reported findings from these data:
  - Tabled trial outcome (percentage of patients reducing levodopa dose by 4–6 weeks after initiating entacapone), stratified by baseline levodopa dose and presence of dyskinesias:
    - Baseline levodopa < 600 mg/day (n=180): Patients without dyskinesias — 4%; Patients with dyskinesias — 31%.
    - Baseline levodopa 600–800 mg/day (n=180): Patients without dyskinesias — 21%; Patients with dyskinesias — 43%.
    - Baseline levodopa > 800 mg/day (n=180): Patients without dyskinesias — 28%; Patients with dyskinesias — 66%.
  - Clinical trial safety signals reported:
    - Hallucinations: observed in approximately 4.0% in trials; discontinuation and hospitalisation rates greater with entacapone than placebo (discontinuation 0.8% vs 0%; hospitalisation 1.0% vs 0.3%).
    - Dyskinesia: reported more frequently with entacapone (1.5%) than placebo (0.8%) in trials.
  - Trial designs, primary endpoints and full efficacy outcomes are not specified in the available sources beyond the data summarized above.
