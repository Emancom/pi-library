# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following information is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the **US (FDA)** for **Levocarnitine Injection** (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **Note:** While the user instructions referenced a document for Leuprorelin (Australia), the provided source file is exclusively for Levocarnitine (US). This editorial focuses on the provided Levocarnitine data.
- This document serves as a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer intravenously as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Dosing Schedule:** Often initiated with a loading dose in severe metabolic crisis, followed by equivalent doses over 24 hours. Administer every 3 or 4 hours; frequency should never be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** Up to 300 mg/kg has been administered [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administered into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Normal range is 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Monthly and weekly monitoring of plasma carnitine concentrations (target 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistry, and vital signs is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue treatment immediately if severe reactions occur [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with existing conditions, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic high doses of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). The safety of the **oral** form has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in ESRD patients (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality) [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, vascular disorder |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, dyspepsia, nausea, vomiting |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, seizures, and laryngeal edema [US].
- **Commonly Observed:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly lettered in text, but reflects previous Category B logic]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk following administration. Risks of excess intake by the infant should be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders applies to the paediatric population [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Trough concentrations should guide dosing to avoid excessive levels [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities caused by toxic organic acid accumulation and corrects low plasma carnitine levels [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Efficacy was demonstrated in two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials. In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg after dialysis, trough carnitine levels increased from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Stability:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) in PVC bags [US].
