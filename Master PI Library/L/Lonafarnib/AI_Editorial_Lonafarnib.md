# Editorial Product Information: Lonafarnib

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lonafarnib, intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lonafarnib (a farnesyltransferase inhibitor).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ZOKINVY** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Hard capsules: 50 mg (opaque yellow, imprinted "LNF" and "50") and 75 mg (opaque light orange, imprinted "LNF" and "75") [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sentynl Therapeutics, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hutchinson-Gilford Progeria Syndrome (HGPS):** Indicated to reduce the risk of mortality in patients 12 months of age and older with a body surface area (BSA) of 0.39 $m^2$ or greater [US].
- **Processing-Deficient Progeroid Laminopathies:** Indicated for patients 12 months of age and older with a BSA of 0.39 $m^2$ or greater who have either [US]:
    - Heterozygous *LMNA* mutation with progerin-like protein accumulation.
    - Homozygous or compound heterozygous *ZMPSTE24* mutations.
- **Limitations of Use:** Not indicated for other Progeroid Syndromes or processing-proficient Progeroid Laminopathies [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric):** Dosing is based on Body Surface Area (BSA).
    - **Starting Dosage:** 115 $mg/m^2$ twice daily with morning and evening meals. 
    - **Maintenance Dosage:** After 4 months of treatment, the dose should be increased to 150 $mg/m^2$ twice daily.
- **Rounding:** Total daily doses must be rounded to the nearest 25 mg increment [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Capsules should be swallowed whole with water. Do not chew.
    - **Patients unable to swallow capsules:** The contents may be mixed with approximately 5–10 mL of Ora-Blend SF, Ora-Plus, or orange juice, or 1–2 teaspoonfuls of applesauce. The mixture must be consumed within 10 minutes of preparation [US].
    - **Restrictions:** Do not mix with juices containing grapefruit or Seville (bitter) oranges.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible with food, up to 8 hours before the next scheduled dose. If less than 8 hours remain, skip the missed dose and resume the regular schedule [US].

## 4. Contraindications
**ZOKINVY** is contraindicated with the following [US]:
- **Strong CYP3A inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin).
- **Strong or moderate CYP3A inducers:** (e.g., rifampin, St. John’s Wort, phenytoin).
- **Midazolam:** Due to the risk of extreme sedation or respiratory depression.
- **Specific Statins:** Lovastatin, simvastatin, or atorvastatin (due to risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QTc Interval Prolongation:** Lonafarnib increases the QTc interval. Avoid use in patients with symptomatic bradycardia, hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, or those taking other QTc-prolonging drugs. Electrolytes should be corrected prior to initiation [US].
- **Drug Interactions:** Significant risk of reduced efficacy or increased toxicity when co-administered with CYP3A modulators or substrates.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Monitoring is required for:
    - Electrolytes (hyperkalaemia, hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hypercalcemia).
    - Myelosuppression (neutropenia, lymphopenia, reduced haemoglobin/haematocrit).
    - Liver enzymes (AST and ALT elevations) [US].
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Based on animal data showing kidney lesions; renal function should be monitored regularly [US].
- **Retinal Toxicity:** Animal studies showed decline in low-light vision. Regular ophthalmological evaluations are recommended [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** May cause fetal harm. Effective contraception is required for females of reproductive potential [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions occur very commonly, particularly during the first four months of therapy.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Vomiting (90%), Diarrhea (81%), Infection (78%), Nausea (56%), Decreased appetite (53%), Fatigue (51%), Upper respiratory tract infection (51%), Abdominal pain (48%), Musculoskeletal pain (48%), Electrolyte abnormalities (43%), Weight loss (37%), Headache (37%), Myelosuppression (35%), Increased AST (35%), Increased ALT (27%), Hypertension (29%), Decreased bicarbonate (33%), Cough (33%), Ocular changes (24%), Constipation (22%), Epistaxis (21%), Pyrexia (14%), Rash (11%), Cerebral ischemia (11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Pruritus (8%), Mucositis (8%), Flatulence (6%), Depressed mood (5%), Dehydration (5%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** QTc prolongation, severe GI reactions leading to dehydration, and hypertension [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP3A:** Lonafarnib is a sensitive CYP3A substrate and a strong, mechanism-based inhibitor of CYP3A [US].
    - **CYP2C19:** Lonafarnib is a moderate inhibitor of CYP2C19.
    - **P-gp and OATP1B:** Lonafarnib is a weak inhibitor of P-glycoprotein and OATP1B.
- **Clinically Significant Interactions:**
    - **Midazolam:** Co-administration is contraindicated. If midazolam is required, **ZOKINVY** must be discontinued for 10–14 days before and 2 days after midazolam use [US].
    - **Statins:** Lovastatin, simvastatin, and atorvastatin are contraindicated.
    - **Loperamide:** Avoid use in children <2 years. If co-administered in older patients, do not exceed 1 mg once daily initially [US].
    - **Grapefruit/Seville Oranges:** Avoid consumption as they may increase lonafarnib levels [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **United States:** No human data available. Based on animal studies (rats/rabbits) showing embryo-fetal toxicity and skeletal malformations at human-equivalent exposures, use in pregnancy is likely to cause fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available regarding presence in human milk or effects on the infant. Lonafarnib is excreted in rat milk. The benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the potential adverse effects on the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggests potential for impaired fertility in both males (testicular toxicity) and females (decreased implantation) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients 12 months and older. Safety and efficacy in infants under 12 months have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include patients aged 65 and older. Pharmacokinetic studies in healthy geriatric subjects showed slightly higher exposure, but this was not deemed clinically relevant [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not studied in patients with renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in patients with hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lonafarnib inhibits farnesyltransferase. This prevents the farnesylation of progerin and progerin-like proteins, thereby reducing their accumulation in the inner nuclear membrane [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is unknown. Taking with a high-fat meal reduces $C_{max}$ by 55% and AUC by 29% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound ($\ge$ 99%).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A; also shows autoinhibition (clearance reduced by 75% after multiple doses) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is approximately 4–6 hours. Approximately 62% is excreted in faeces and <1% in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** The efficacy was established using an **Observational Cohort Survival Study** which retrospectively compared 62 treated patients (from two Phase 2 open-label trials) to a natural history cohort [US].
- **Primary Outcome:** Treated patients with HGPS showed a significantly increased mean lifespan. At the last follow-up (up to 11 years), the mean survival time was 8.0 years for the lonafarnib group vs 5.5 years for the untreated group—a mean survival benefit of 2.5 years [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at $20^\circ C$ to $25^\circ C$ ($68^\circ F$ to $77^\circ F$).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between $15^\circ C$ and $30^\circ C$ ($59^\circ F$ to $86^\circ F$) [US].
- **Dispensing:** Supplied in bottles of 30 capsules [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only).
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Poison schedule/legal classification not specified in available source documents.
