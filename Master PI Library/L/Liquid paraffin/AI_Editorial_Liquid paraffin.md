# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note

- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory and clinical information into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the local regulatory authority’s approved labelling before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt; a naturally occurring derivative of the amino acid lysine.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) / Hemodialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Initial Crisis Dosing:** A loading dose is often administered in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h).
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised.

- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are supra-normal.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients. 
    - *Management:* Discontinue immediately and initiate appropriate medical treatment. Weigh risks versus benefits before considering re-administration.

### Clinical Precautions
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed.
- **Oral Formulation Restriction:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to parenteral therapy. 
- **Ongoing Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is advised, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg doses.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), Pharyngitis (15–27%), Hypertension (18–21%), Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Headache (3–37%), Dizziness (10–18%), Pain (21–35%), Fever (5–12%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, Asthenia, Chest pain, Flu syndrome, Infection, Palpitations, Tachycardia, Anorexia, Nausea, Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Peripheral edema, Depression, Paresthesia, Dyspnea, Rhinitis, Pruritus, Rash, Eye disorder, Urinary tract infection. |
| **Serious Adverse Effects** | **Anaphylaxis**, **Seizures**, and **Severe Hypertension**. |

*Note: In ESRD patients, the incidence of these reactions is difficult to distinguish from underlying pathology.*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. 
    - *Management:* Monitor INR levels closely upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified [US label states "should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed"]. Animal reproductive studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for use; dosing is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in provided sources; clinical monitoring is recommended as for other adults.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Oral formulations should be avoided in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.

### Pharmacodynamics
In ESRD patients, levocarnitine injection increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normal plasma free carnitine levels are defined as 40 to 50 µmol/L; treatment aims to maintain levels above 35 µmol/L.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
- **Bioavailability:** Oral bioavailability is low (approximately 15–16%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Special Precautions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL.
