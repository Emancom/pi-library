# Editorial Product Information: Lithium Carbonate

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial consolidation and must not be used as a substitute for the official, current Product Information approved by the regulatory authority in the specific jurisdiction where the clinician is practicing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lithium carbonate (Antimanic agent).
- **Brand Names:** LITHICARB [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05AN01 (Lithium).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablet:** 250 mg film-coated, oval-shaped, bevel-edged with a breakline [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Mania:** For both the acute treatment and long-term prophylaxis of manic episodes [Australian].
- **Bipolar Disorder:** Treatment and prophylaxis of manic-depressive illness; noted to be less effective for depressive phases [Australian].
- **Unipolar Depressive Illness:** Prophylaxis in recurrent endogenous unipolar depression; potentially useful as treatment if a family history of lithium response exists [Australian].
- **Schizo-affective and Chronic Schizophrenia:** Effective in specific cases, particularly those with a history of affective symptoms [Australian].
- **Character or Personality Disorders:** Use in young people with clear evidence of cyclothymia [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Dosage must be individualized based on serum levels and clinical response. An adequate sodium and fluid intake is essential during therapy.

### Acute Mania
A rapid escalation program is typically employed to reach therapeutic serum levels [Australian]:
- **Day 1:** 500 mg to 1,000 mg in divided doses.
- **Day 2:** 1,250 mg to 1,750 mg in divided doses.
- **Day 3:** 1,500 mg to 2,000 mg in divided doses.
- **Serum Target:** Aim for levels between 0.8 and 1.6 mmol/L. **Do not exceed 2 mmol/L** [Australian].

### Maintenance/Prophylaxis
Once remission is achieved (typically 7–14 days), dosage should be reduced stepwise to maintenance levels [Australian]:
- **Standard Dose:** Usually 500 mg to 1 g daily, administered in one or two doses.
- **Monitoring:** Maintain satisfactory basal blood levels within the therapeutic range.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be taken with food to minimize gastrointestinal upset (nausea) [Australian].
- **Steady-State Monitoring:** Samples for serum levels should be drawn in the morning, approximately 12 hours after the previous evening dose. Initial levels should be checked no later than 3 days after starting therapy [Australian].

### Monitoring Schedule
- **Initial Phase:** At least twice weekly [Australian].
- **Stabilization:** Weekly for the first month, then monthly for one year [Australian].
- **Long-term:** Quarterly thereafter, or ad hoc if symptoms recur [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Significant cardiovascular disease [Australian].
- Significant renal disease [Australian].
- Frank hypothyroidism [Australian].
- Conditions associated with hyponatremia (e.g., Addison's disease, dehydration, severely debilitated states, or patients on low-sodium diets) [Australian].
- Use of diuretics without appropriate dosage adjustment and monitoring [Australian].
- Hypersensitivity to lithium or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Narrow Therapeutic Index:** Toxicity is closely related to serum concentrations and can occur at levels near the therapeutic range [Australian].
- **Brugada Syndrome:** Lithium may unmask Brugada syndrome via inhibition of cardiac sodium channels. Avoid in these patients; if essential, perform serial ECGs in a controlled environment [Australian].
- **Renal Effects:** Chronic therapy may reduce renal concentrating ability (nephrogenic diabetes insipidus). Long-term use (>10 years) in patients with severe renal impairment has been associated with microcysts, oncocytomas, and collecting duct renal carcinoma [Australian].
- **Encephalopathic Syndrome:** Reported in patients treated with lithium and neuroleptics (e.g., haloperidol). Monitor for weakness, fever, confusion, and extrapyramidal symptoms; discontinue immediately if signs appear [Australian].
- **Endocrine Effects:** May cause hypercalcemia, hyperparathyroidism, goiter, and hypothyroidism. Monitor calcium and thyroid function regularly [Australian].
- **Bariatric Surgery:** Patients may require lower maintenance doses; monitor levels closely until weight stabilizes [Australian].
- **Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT):** Discontinue lithium temporarily before ECT to reduce delirium risk [Australian].
- **Surgery:** It is advised to discontinue lithium 24 hours prior to major surgery and restart once electrolyte balance is confirmed [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally dose-dependent and related to serum concentrations.

- **Very Common (>10%) / Common (1–10%):** Fine hand tremor, polyuria, thirst, fatigue, and transient nausea [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Anorexia, vomiting, diarrhea, metallic taste, abdominal pain, excessive salivation [Australian].
- **Central Nervous System:** Vertigo, muscle weakness, dazed feeling, headache, slurred speech, ataxia, nystagmus, seizures, somnolence, and hallucinations [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular:** Arrhythmia, hypotension, peripheral circulatory collapse, bradycardia, and non-specific T-wave changes [Australian].
- **Dermatological:** Acne, psoriasis exacerbation, alopecia, folliculitis, and DRESS (drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms) [Australian].
- **Endocrine/Metabolic:** Weight gain, euthyroid goiter, hypothyroidism, hypercalcemia, and hypermagnesemia [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Acute renal failure, nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, irreversible brain damage (with neuroleptics), and coma vigil (expressionless awareness) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Increased Lithium Levels:** NSAIDs (especially indomethacin and piroxicam), ACE inhibitors (e.g., captopril, enalapril), Angiotensin II receptor antagonists, and diuretics (paradoxical antidiuretic effect) [Australian].
- **Decreased Lithium Levels:** SGLT2 inhibitors, acetazolamide, urea, xanthines (theophylline), and alkalinizing agents like sodium bicarbonate [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Neuroleptics:** Potential for severe neurotoxicity and encephalopathic syndrome [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Agents (SSRIs):** Risk of precipitating Serotonin Syndrome [Australian].
- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** May increase neurotoxicity; lithium levels should be kept at the lower therapeutic end [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blockers:** Lithium may prolong the duration of action [Australian].
- **Ziprasidone:** Potential for augmented QTc interval prolongation [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Lithium crosses the placenta. Data indicate risks of cardiovascular malformations (Ebstein's anomaly) and neonatal thyroid dysfunction. It is strongly recommended to discontinue lithium before planned pregnancy unless the benefit clearly outweighs the risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Lithium is excreted in breast milk. Bottle feeding is recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate interference with fertility and fetal development [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 12 years of age due to lack of safety and efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Use with extreme caution. Reduced renal clearance results in a longer half-life; toxicity may occur at concentrations tolerated by younger patients. Lower dosages are frequently required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Adequate renal function is essential for excretion. Dosage reduction and frequent monitoring are required in impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (lithium is primarily renally excreted).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** While the precise mechanism is not fully elucidated, it is indicated for the management of manic-depressive psychoses [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in detail in the provided source, though it notes primary renal excretion and a 12-hour interval required to reach steady-state basal levels [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits a narrow therapeutic window; inhibits cardiac sodium channels at high levels [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 2 years [Australian].
- **Container:** White HDPE bottles with polypropylene child-resistant caps [Australian].
