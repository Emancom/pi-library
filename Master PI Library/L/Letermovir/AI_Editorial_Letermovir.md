# Editorial Product Information: Letermovir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs), synthesising clinical data into a structured, concise format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Letermovir (a non-nucleoside cytomegalovirus [CMV] inhibitor).
- **Chemical Class:** Quinolazoline.
- **Brand Name:** PREVYMIS [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AX18.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 240 mg (yellow, oval, debossed "591") and 480 mg (pink, oval, debossed "595") [Australian].
    - **Concentrated injection for infusion:** 240 mg/12 mL or 480 mg/24 mL single-dose vials (20 mg/mL solution) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral; Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme (Australia) Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Haematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant (HSCT):** Prophylaxis of CMV infection or disease in adult CMV-seropositive recipients [R+] of an allogeneic HSCT [Australian].
- **Kidney Transplant:** Prophylaxis of CMV disease in adult kidney transplant recipients at high risk (Donor CMV seropositive/Recipient CMV seronegative [D+/R-]) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Standard Dose:** 480 mg administered once daily [Australian].
- **Cyclosporin Co-administration:** Dosage must be decreased to 240 mg once daily if letermovir is co-administered with cyclosporin [Australian].
    - If cyclosporin is started after letermovir, the next letermovir dose should be reduced to 240 mg.
    - If cyclosporin is discontinued, the next letermovir dose should be increased to 480 mg.
    - No adjustment is needed for temporary interruptions of cyclosporin due to high levels.

### Indication-Specific Timing
- **HSCT:** Initiate on the day of transplant and no later than 28 days post-HSCT. Continue through 100 days post-transplant. Duration may be extended to 200 days in patients at high risk for late CMV infection [Australian].
- **Kidney Transplant:** Initiate on the day of transplant and no later than 7 days post-transplant. Continue through 200 days post-transplant [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Oral:** Administer with or without food. Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not crush, chew, or divide.
- **Intravenous:** Administer via peripheral or central line over approximately 60 minutes.
    - **Filter Required:** Must use a sterile 0.2 micron or 0.22 micron polyethersulfone (PES) in-line filter.
    - **Preparation:** Dilute in 250 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% dextrose. Mix gently; do not shake.
    - **Incompatibility:** Do not use IV bags or infusion sets containing polyurethane or the plasticizer diethylhexyl phthalate (DEHP).
- **Interchangeability:** Tablets and injection may be used interchangeably at the physician's discretion with no dose adjustment required (except for cyclosporin co-administration).

### Missed Dose
- Take the missed dose as soon as remembered. If it is nearly time for the next dose, skip the missed dose and resume the regular schedule. Do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to letermovir or any inactive ingredients.
- **Specific Drug Interactions [Australian]:**
    - **Pimozide:** Risk of QT prolongation and Torsades de Pointes due to increased pimozide concentrations.
    - **Ergot Alkaloids (e.g., ergotamine, dihydroergotamine):** Risk of ergotism.
    - **Pitavastatin or Simvastatin:** Contraindicated **only when** letermovir is co-administered with **cyclosporin** (risk of myopathy/rhabdomyolysis).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Drug Interactions:** Letermovir is a moderate inhibitor of CYP3A and an inhibitor of OATP1B1/3. Concomitant use with drugs that have a narrow therapeutic range (e.g., alfentanil, fentanyl, quinidine) requires close monitoring [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended for patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C). Also not recommended for patients with moderate hepatic impairment combined with moderate/severe renal impairment [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use caution with the IV formulation in patients with CrCl <50 mL/min due to potential accumulation of the vehicle hydroxypropylbetadex; monitor serum creatinine closely [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Monitor for signs of CMV reactivation and adjust therapy if prophylaxis fails. Frequent INR monitoring is required when co-administered with warfarin [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥10% and >Placebo)
Based on HSCT trial data (P001) [Australian]:
- **Nausea:** 27% (vs 23% placebo)
- **Diarrhoea:** 26% (vs 24% placebo)
- **Vomiting:** 19% (vs 14% placebo)
- **Peripheral Oedema:** 14% (vs 9% placebo)
- **Cough:** 14% (vs 10% placebo)
- **Headache:** 14% (vs 9% placebo)
- **Fatigue:** 13% (vs 11% placebo)
- **Abdominal Pain:** 12% (vs 9% placebo)

### Serious and Notable Effects
- **Cardiac Events:** Higher rate in letermovir (13%) vs placebo (6%) in HSCT recipients. Most common were tachycardia (4%) and atrial fibrillation (3%).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Reported following IV infusion in isolated cases.
- **Kidney Transplant (P002):** Diarrhoea occurred in 32% of letermovir patients (vs 29% for valganciclovir). Rates of leukopenia (26%) and neutropenia were significantly lower than with valganciclovir (64%) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Letermovir as Substrate:** Metabolised by OATP1B1/3, P-gp, and UGT1A1/3.
    - **Inhibitors (e.g., cyclosporin):** Increase letermovir levels (reduce dose to 240 mg).
    - **Inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St John's Wort):** Significantly decrease letermovir levels; co-administration is not recommended.
- **Letermovir as Inhibitor:**
    - **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Increases exposure to midazolam, tacrolimus, sirolimus, and atorvastatin (atorvastatin dose should not exceed 20 mg/day).
    - **OATP1B1/3 Substrates:** Increases exposure to statins and glyburide.
    - **CYP2C8 Substrates:** May increase levels (e.g., repaglinide).
- **Letermovir as Inducer:**
    - **CYP2C9/2C19 Substrates:** May decrease exposure to warfarin (monitor INR) and voriconazole [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3 [Australian]**. No adequate human data. Animal studies showed embryofetal toxicity at maternally toxic doses (shortened umbilical cords, skeletal malformations). Use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk is unknown. In rats, letermovir was present in milk but did not affect pup growth. Consider the benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother's clinical need.
- **Fertility:** Testicular toxicity was observed in male rats but not in mice or monkeys. No evidence of testicular toxicity was seen in human clinical trials [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required based on age; safety and efficacy were similar to younger adults.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment based on renal function alone. No data available for end-stage renal disease (CrCl <10 mL/min) or dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild (Child-Pugh A) to moderate (Child-Pugh B) impairment. Avoid in severe (Child-Pugh C) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the CMV DNA terminase complex (specifically affecting subunits pUL51, pUL56, or pUL89). This prevents viral DNA processing and packaging, interfering with virion maturation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** ~94% in healthy subjects; ~35% in HSCT recipients; ~60% in kidney transplant recipients.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (98.7%).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal excretion (93.3%), mostly as unchanged parent drug.
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 12 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **HSCT (Trial P001):** Double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Letermovir significantly reduced clinically significant CMV infection by Week 24 post-HSCT (37.5% failure vs 60.6% in placebo). All-cause mortality was lower in the letermovir group (12.1% vs 17.2% at Week 24) [Australian].
- **Kidney Transplant (Trial P002):** Non-inferiority trial against valganciclovir. Letermovir demonstrated non-inferiority for the incidence of CMV disease through Week 52 (10.4% vs 11.8% for valganciclovir). Importantly, letermovir showed a lower incidence of neutropenia/leukopenia [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets:** Store below 30°C in the original package until use.
- **Concentrated Injection:** Store vials below 25°C (excursions 15–30°C permitted). Store in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Diluted Solution:** Use as soon as possible. Stable for 24 hours at room temperature or 48 hours under refrigeration (2–8°C), including infusion time [Australian].

- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
