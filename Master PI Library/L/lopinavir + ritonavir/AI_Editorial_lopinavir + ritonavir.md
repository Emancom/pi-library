# Editorial Product Information: lopinavir + ritonavir

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **lopinavir + ritonavir**
- Chemical class:
  - Lopinavir: HIV-1 protease inhibitor
  - Ritonavir: protease inhibitor with potent CYP3A inhibitory (pharmacokinetic enhancing) activity
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of last revision/update of the provided product information: 13 March 2026.
- Date of first registration/approval: Not specified in available sources.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of HIV-1 infection in combination with other antiretroviral agents in adults and children aged 2 years and older. This indication is supported by analyses of plasma HIV RNA and CD4 cell counts from controlled clinical studies as reported in the product information.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General principles
- Dosage strengths commonly referenced: 400 mg lopinavir / 100 mg ritonavir per dosing occasion (i.e., two 200/50 mg units) and oral solution concentration expressed as 80 mg lopinavir / 20 mg ritonavir per mL.
- Tablet administration: swallow whole; do not chew, break or crush. Tablets may be taken with or without food.
- Oral solution administration: should be taken with food. Consider total excipient exposure (alcohol, propylene glycol) when prescribing, especially for infants.

### Adults
- Recommended standard adult dose (tablets): 400/100 mg twice daily (two 200/50 mg tablets twice daily).
- Alternative once-daily regimen (tablets): 800/200 mg once daily (four 200/50 mg tablets once daily) may be used in patients with fewer than three lopinavir-associated mutations. Insufficient data to support once-daily administration in patients with three or more lopinavir-associated mutations.
- Oral solution (adults who cannot take tablets): 5 mL (equivalent to 400/100 mg) twice daily with food. A once-daily oral solution dose of 10 mL may be used in patients with fewer than three lopinavir-associated mutations.
- Concomitant in treatment‑experienced patients with reduced susceptibility suspected (by history or laboratory evidence): when used with efavirenz, nevirapine, amprenavir, or nelfinavir, consider increasing lopinavir/ritonavir to 500/125 mg twice daily (equivalent oral solution volume noted in paediatric section) — see Interactions for details about efavirenz-specific adjustments.

### Paediatric patients
- Indicated for children aged 2 years and older.
- Adult tablet dosing (400/100 mg twice daily) may be used in children ≥ 35 kg.
- Once-daily administration is not recommended in paediatric patients.
- Recommended paediatric dosing (oral solution 80 mg/20 mg per mL):
  - General regimen: 230/57.5 mg/m2 (or 12/3 mg/kg for children < 15 kg; or 10/2.5 mg/kg for children ≥ 15 kg) twice daily, taken with food, up to a maximum of 400/100 mg (5 mL) twice daily.
  - When co-administered with efavirenz or nevirapine, consider increased dosing to 300/75 mg/m2 (or 13/3.25 mg/kg for children < 15 kg; or 11/2.75 mg/kg for children ≥ 15 kg) twice daily, taken with food.
- Paediatric weight-based oral solution tables (volumes expressed as mL of 80 mg/20 mg per mL solution) — dosing specified as administered dose:
  - Without efavirenz/nevirapine/amprenavir:
    - 7 to <15 kg: 12 mg/kg twice daily
      - 7–10 kg: 1.25 mL (100/25 mg)
      - >10–<15 kg: 1.75 mL (140/35 mg)
    - 15–40 kg: 10 mg/kg twice daily
      - 15–20 kg: 2.25 mL (180/45 mg)
      - >20–25 kg: 2.75 mL (220/55 mg)
      - >25–30 kg: 3.5 mL (280/70 mg)
      - >30–35 kg: 4.0 mL (320/80 mg)
      - >35–40 kg: 4.75 mL (380/95 mg)
    - >40 kg: follow adult dosing recommendations
  - With efavirenz/nevirapine/amprenavir:
    - 7 to <15 kg: 13 mg/kg twice daily
      - 7–10 kg: 1.5 mL (120/30 mg)
      - >10–<15 kg: 2.0 mL (160/40 mg)
    - 15–45 kg: 11 mg/kg twice daily
      - 15–20 kg: 2.5 mL (200/50 mg)
      - >20–25 kg: 3.25 mL (260/65 mg)
      - >25–30 kg: 4.0 mL (320/80 mg)
      - >30–35 kg: 4.5 mL (360/90 mg)
      - >35–40 kg: 5.0 mL (400/100 mg)
    - >40 kg: follow adult dosing for concomitant therapy

### Special dosing considerations with concomitant drugs
- For efavirenz 600 mg once daily: increasing lopinavir/ritonavir to 500/125 mg twice daily produced lopinavir exposures similar to standard 400/100 mg twice daily without efavirenz. A higher increase to 600/150 mg twice daily produced approximately 36% higher lopinavir exposure and 56–92% higher ritonavir exposure compared to 400/100 mg twice daily alone; clinical use should consider increased exposures and potential toxicity.
- For nevirapine, amprenavir, or nelfinavir co-administration in treatment-experienced patients where reduced lopinavir susceptibility is suspected, consider increasing lopinavir/ritonavir to 500/125 mg twice daily.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to lopinavir, ritonavir, or any excipients.
- Co-administration with drugs that are highly dependent on CYP3A for clearance and for which elevated plasma concentrations are associated with serious and/or life‑threatening reactions. The following drugs/classes are listed as contraindicated for co‑administration:
  - Alpha1‑adrenoreceptor antagonist: alfuzosin hydrochloride
  - Analgesic sodium channel blocker: suzetrigine
  - Antianginal: ranolazine
  - Antiarrhythmic: dronedarone
  - Antibiotic: fusidic acid
  - Anticancer agents: neratinib, apalutamide
  - Antigout: colchicine in patients with renal and/or hepatic impairment
  - Antihistamines: astemizole, terfenadine
  - Antipsychotics: blonanserin, lurasidone, pimozide
  - Benzodiazepines: midazolam, triazolam
  - Ergot derivatives: ergotamine, dihydroergotamine, ergometrine, methylergometrine
  - Gastrointestinal motility agent: cisapride
  - Herbal product: St John’s Wort (Hypericum perforatum)
  - Hepatitis C direct acting antiviral: elbasvir/grazoprevir
  - HMG‑CoA reductase inhibitors: lovastatin, simvastatin
  - Microsomal triglyceride transfer protein (MTTP) inhibitor: lomitapide
  - Long acting beta‑adrenoreceptor agonist: salmeterol
  - PDE5 inhibitor: sildenafil when used for treatment of pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) — contraindicated in that context
- Oral solution–specific contraindications:
  - Children below the age of 2 years (due to propylene glycol content)
  - Pregnant women (due to propylene glycol content)
  - Patients with hepatic failure
  - Patients with renal failure
  - Patients treated with disulfiram or metronidazole (risk from propylene glycol)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Highlighted warnings
- Not specified as a single boxed warning in the provided sources; however the following critical safety concerns are emphasised and should be treated as high-priority clinical warnings:
  - **Severe hepatic dysfunction and transaminase elevations have been reported, including fatalities in patients with advanced disease or underlying hepatitis.**
  - **Oral solution contains propylene glycol and alcohol; it is contraindicated in children <2 years, pregnant women, renal or hepatic failure, and patients on disulfiram or metronidazole.**

### General warnings and precautions
- Metabolic effects:
  - New onset diabetes mellitus, exacerbation of pre‑existing diabetes, hyperglycaemia; diabetic ketoacidosis has been reported. Consider blood glucose monitoring and manage hyperglycaemia per clinical judgment.
- Pancreatitis:
  - Pancreatitis, including fatal cases, reported; marked triglyceride elevations are a risk factor. Patients with advanced HIV or previous pancreatitis may be at increased risk.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Kaletra is primarily metabolised hepatically. Exercise caution in patients with hepatic impairment. Pharmacokinetic data indicate ~30% increase in lopinavir plasma concentrations and decreased protein binding in patients co‑infected with HCV with mild to moderate hepatic impairment. Not studied in severe hepatic impairment.
  - Patients with underlying hepatitis B or C or significant baseline transaminase elevations may be at increased risk for further transaminase increases; increased AST/ALT monitoring is recommended, notably during the first several months of therapy.
- Resistance/cross‑resistance:
  - Cross‑resistance among protease inhibitors can occur; effect on subsequent protease inhibitor efficacy is under investigation.
- Hemorrhagic events in haemophilia:
  - Increased bleeding (spontaneous skin haematomas, haemarthrosis) reported in haemophilia A and B patients receiving protease inhibitors.
- Fat redistribution:
  - Redistribution of body fat (peripheral wasting, central obesity, dorsocervical fat pad, breast enlargement) associated with combination antiretroviral therapy; mechanism and long‑term consequences unknown.
- Cardiac conduction:
  - QT interval prolongation and torsades de pointes have been reported post‑marketing (causality not established); avoid in congenital long QT, hypokalaemia, or with other QT‑prolonging drugs.
  - PR interval prolongation: modest asymptomatic PR prolongation reported; rare reports of second‑ or third‑degree AV block in patients with pre‑existing conduction disease or concomitant drugs that prolong PR interval (e.g., verapamil, atazanavir). Use with caution in such patients.
- Lipids:
  - Increases in total cholesterol and triglycerides observed; perform baseline and periodic lipid testing. Manage lipid disorders appropriately.
- Immune reconstitution syndrome:
  - Occurrence reported following initiation of combination antiretroviral therapy; may unmask or worsen opportunistic infections and autoimmune conditions (e.g., Graves’ disease, polymyositis, Guillain‑Barre syndrome).
- Use in the elderly:
  - Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers ≥65 years to determine differences; exercise caution in elderly due to potential for decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function and concomitant disease/drug therapy.
- Paediatric use:
  - Safety and pharmacokinetics not established in children <6 months. Oral solution contraindicated in children <2 years (see Contraindications).

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic:
  - Liver function tests (AST/ALT, bilirubin) — increased monitoring for patients with hepatitis B/C or baseline elevations.
  - Lipid profile (total cholesterol, triglycerides) prior to starting therapy and periodically thereafter.
  - Blood glucose monitoring for hyperglycaemia or new‑onset diabetes.
  - ECG monitoring when used in patients at risk for QT or PR prolongation or when used with other drugs that prolong these intervals.
  - Consider monitoring creatine kinase (CK) if myopathy is suspected.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Consider potential for clinically significant drug interactions due to potent inhibition of CYP3A and enzyme induction effects (notably CYP2C19 and glucuronidation) — review all concomitant medications.
- For oral solution, calculate total alcohol and propylene glycol exposure from all medications to avoid excipient toxicity, especially in infants.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Mechanistic overview
- **Inhibitor of CYP3A in vivo**, leading to increased concentrations of drugs primarily metabolised by CYP3A.
- **Substrate of CYP3A**: lopinavir is metabolised by CYP3A; inducers of CYP3A may reduce lopinavir concentrations and impair efficacy.
- Demonstrated to **induce its own metabolism** and the biotransformation of certain drugs via induction of CYP enzymes and glucuronidation.
- **Potent inducer of CYP2C19** activity in vivo.
- Does not inhibit CYP2D6, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2E1, CYP2B6 or CYP1A2 at clinically relevant concentrations (the product information states no inhibition of several CYP isoforms except CYP3A; note regulatory text indicates a potent inducer effect on CYP2C19).

### Clinically significant interactions and management notes
- General: Co‑administration with drugs primarily metabolised by CYP3A may cause large increases (>3‑fold AUC) in those drugs; such co‑administration can increase or prolong therapeutic and adverse effects. Consult detailed drug interaction references when co‑prescribing.
- Contraindicated concomitant drugs: see Section 5 (Contraindications) for a list of drugs that must not be co‑administered due to risk of severe/life‑threatening reactions.
- Antiretroviral interactions:
  - NRTIs:
    - Stavudine and lamivudine: no change in lopinavir PK observed when co‑administered.
    - Didanosine:
      - With tablets: didanosine is recommended to be administered on an empty stomach; thus it may be co‑administered with tablets without regard to food.
      - With oral solution: didanosine should be given one hour before or two hours after the oral solution (because the oral solution should be taken with food while didanosine is taken on an empty stomach).
    - Zidovudine and abacavir: lopinavir/ritonavir induces glucuronidation and may reduce zidovudine and abacavir plasma concentrations; clinical significance unknown.
    - Tenofovir: lopinavir/ritonavir has been shown to increase tenofovir concentrations; monitor patients for tenofovir‑associated adverse events.
    - General: Increased CPK, myalgia, myositis, and rarely rhabdomyolysis have been reported with protease inhibitors, particularly when combined with NRTIs.
  - NNRTIs:
    - Nevirapine: in healthy volunteers no change in lopinavir PK was noted, but in HIV‑positive paediatric subjects a decrease in lopinavir concentrations was observed with nevirapine co‑administration. Lopinavir concentrations may be decreased in adults as well; avoid once‑daily dosing of lopinavir/ritonavir with nevirapine.
    - Efavirenz:
      - Efavirenz induces CYP3A and can reduce lopinavir concentrations. Increasing lopinavir/ritonavir dose to 500/125 mg twice daily when co‑administered with efavirenz 600 mg once daily resulted in lopinavir exposures similar to standard dosing without efavirenz.
      - Increasing to 600/150 mg twice daily produced higher lopinavir (~36% increase) and ritonavir (56–92% increase) exposures compared with 400/100 mg twice daily without efavirenz; interpret and use cautiously.
    - Delavirdine: has potential to increase lopinavir concentrations.
    - Rilpivirine: co‑administration increases rilpivirine concentrations; specific dose adjustments not required per the rilpivirine product information (refer to rilpivirine prescribing information).
    - Etravirine: co‑administration decreases etravirine concentrations; no dose adjustment of etravirine required per etravirine product information.
  - Protease inhibitors:
    - Amprenavir / fosamprenavir: lopinavir/ritonavir expected to increase amprenavir exposure and co‑administration results in decreased lopinavir concentrations; lopinavir/ritonavir should not be administered once daily with amprenavir or fosamprenavir (safety and efficacy of certain combinations not established).
    - Indinavir: lopinavir/ritonavir expected to increase indinavir exposure; dose reduction of indinavir may be necessary when co‑administered with lopinavir/ritonavir 400/100 mg twice daily.
    - Nelfinavir: lopinavir/ritonavir expected to increase nelfinavir exposure; co‑administration results in decreased lopinavir concentrations; lopinavir/ritonavir should not be administered once daily with nelfinavir.
    - Ritonavir: additional ritonavir increases lopinavir AUC (e.g., adding 100 mg ritonavir twice daily increased lopinavir AUC by 33% and Cmin by 64% compared to lopinavir/ritonavir 400/100 mg twice daily).
    - Saquinavir: lopinavir/ritonavir expected to increase saquinavir concentrations; saquinavir dose may need reduction when co‑administered.
    - Tipranavir: dual‑boosted PI regimens (e.g., tipranavir/ritonavir with lopinavir/ritonavir) reduced lopinavir exposures substantially in a clinical study — caution with such combinations; appropriate dosing and safety not established.
- Other classes:
  - Calcium channel blockers, immunosuppressants, PDE5 inhibitors, HMG‑CoA reductase inhibitors, and many other CYP3A substrates: plasma concentrations may be increased; monitor for increased effects/toxicity and adjust doses as needed or avoid co‑administration. Specific contraindicated HMG‑CoA reductase inhibitors: lovastatin and simvastatin (see Contraindications).
  - Herbal products: St John’s Wort (Hypericum perforatum) is contraindicated due to induction of CYP3A and loss of virologic response.
- Note regarding sildenafil:
  - Sildenafil is contraindicated when used for PAH. Specific dosing and management for erectile dysfunction when co‑administered with lopinavir/ritonavir are not specified in the available sources.
- Summary advice:
  - Review all concomitant medications for CYP3A substrates, inducers or inhibitors, and for drugs metabolised by CYP2C19 or glucuronidation pathways. When uncertainty exists, consult comprehensive interaction references and consider therapeutic drug monitoring where available.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Indicated for children aged 2 years and older.
- Safety and pharmacokinetic profiles in infants below six months have not been established.
- Oral solution contraindicated in children <2 years (propylene glycol).
- Specific weight‑ and mg/kg/m2 dosing tables provided (see Section 4).

### Elderly
- Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers ≥65 years to determine differential responses. Use caution reflecting possible decreased hepatic, renal or cardiac function and multiple comorbidities/drugs.

### Renal impairment
- Oral solution is contraindicated in patients with renal failure (propylene glycol toxicity risk).
- Specific dosing adjustments by eGFR or renal stage for tablets: Not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Kaletra is primarily metabolised by the liver; exercise caution in hepatic impairment.
- Pharmacokinetic data in mild–moderate hepatic impairment indicate approximately a 30% increase in lopinavir plasma concentrations and decreased protein binding; not studied in severe hepatic impairment.
- Oral solution contraindicated in hepatic failure.
- Specific dosing adjustments by Child‑Pugh class or severity: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Lopinavir: HIV‑1 protease inhibitor.
- Ritonavir: protease inhibitor that functions as a pharmacokinetic enhancer primarily by inhibiting CYP3A‑mediated metabolism of lopinavir, thereby increasing lopinavir plasma concentrations.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Tablets may be taken with or without food; oral solution should be taken with food.
- Distribution:
  - Plasma protein binding may decrease in patients with hepatic impairment.
- Metabolism:
  - Lopinavir is principally metabolised by the liver via CYP3A.
  - Ritonavir strongly inhibits CYP3A and affects metabolism of other drugs.
  - The combination has been shown to induce its own metabolism and increase biotransformation of some drugs via CYP enzymes and glucuronidation; it is a potent inducer of CYP2C19 activity in vivo.
- Elimination:
  - Specific elimination half‑life, clearance and excretion parameters: Not specified in available sources.
- Special population effects:
  - Mild to moderate hepatic impairment: ~30% increase in lopinavir concentrations and decreased protein binding reported.
  - Renal impairment effects on lopinavir PK: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Antiretroviral activity: reduction in plasma HIV RNA and increases in CD4 cell counts demonstrated in controlled studies (details not specified in the available sources).
