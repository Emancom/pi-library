# Editorial Product Information: Levonorgestrel and Ethinylestradiol

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial:
    - Australian (TGA): Product Information — Eleanor 150/30 ED (levonorgestrel and ethinylestradiol) film-coated tablet.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of the combined oral contraceptive (COC) containing levonorgestrel and ethinylestradiol for use by healthcare professionals.
- This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Levonorgestrel (synthetic progestogen) and Ethinylestradiol (synthetic estrogen).
- **Brand Name(s):** Eleanor 150/30 ED [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Combined oral contraceptive pack containing 28 tablets.
    - Active tablet: Yellow, round, biconvex, film-coated, containing 150 micrograms levonorgestrel and 30 micrograms ethinylestradiol.
    - Placebo tablet: White to off-white, round, biconvex, film-coated, inactive [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lupin Australia Pty Ltd; Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Oral contraception [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One tablet daily at approximately the same time for 28 consecutive days.
- **Cycle Initiation [Australian]:**
    - **No preceding hormonal use:** Start on Day 1 of the natural menstrual cycle. Barrier contraception is required for the first 14 days.
    - **Switching from COC/Vaginal Ring:** Start the day after the last active tablet of the previous pack or the day of ring removal.
    - **Switching from Progestogen-only methods:** May switch any day from a minipill, or on the day of removal of an implant/IUS. Use barrier methods for the first 14 days.
    - **Post-abortion (1st Trimester):** Start immediately; barrier methods required for the first 14 days.
    - **Post-delivery/2nd Trimester Abortion:** Start 21–28 days post-procedure. If starting later, exclude pregnancy and use barrier methods for the first 14 days.
- **Missed Dose Instructions [Australian]:**
    - **< 12 hours late:** Contraceptive protection is not reduced. Take the tablet immediately and continue as normal.
    - **> 12 hours late:** Protection may be reduced.
        - **Week 1:** Take the last missed tablet immediately (even if two are taken at once). Use barrier methods for 7 days. Consider pregnancy if intercourse occurred in the preceding 7 days.
        - **Week 2:** Take the last missed tablet immediately. If tablets were taken correctly in the 7 days prior, no extra precautions are needed. Otherwise, use barrier methods for 7 days.
        - **Week 3:** Either start the next pack immediately (omitting the placebo interval) or stop active tablets and enter the 7-day placebo interval immediately.
- **Gastrointestinal Disturbances:** If vomiting occurs within 3–4 hours of an active tablet, absorption may be incomplete; follow missed dose protocols.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Presence or risk of Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Current VTE (on anticoagulants), history of DVT or pulmonary embolism, known hereditary/acquired predisposition (e.g., APC resistance, Factor V Leiden, Protein C/S deficiency), major surgery with prolonged immobilisation.
- **Presence or risk of Arterial Thromboembolism (ATE):** Current or history of ATE (myocardial infarction, stroke), prodromal conditions (angina pectoris, TIA), known predisposition (e.g., hyperhomocysteinaemia), history of migraine with focal neurological symptoms.
- **High Vascular Risk:** Multiple risk factors or one serious risk factor (e.g., diabetes with vascular symptoms, severe hypertension, severe dyslipoproteinaemia).
- **Hepatic Disease:** Presence or history of severe hepatic disease (until liver function returns to normal); presence or history of liver tumours (benign or malignant).
- **Malignancy:** Known or suspected sex steroid-influenced malignancies (e.g., breast or genital organs).
- **Other:** Pancreatitis (current or history) associated with severe hypertriglyceridemia; undiagnosed vaginal bleeding; known or suspected pregnancy; hypersensitivity to ingredients.
- **Drug-Specific:** Concomitant use with Hepatitis C antivirals containing glecaprevir, pibrentasvir, sofosbuvir, velpatasvir, voxilaprevir, ombitasvir, paritaprevir, or dasabuvir [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **VTE/ATE Risk:** Use of any CHC increases VTE risk, particularly in the first year of use or after a break of ≥4 weeks. COCs containing levonorgestrel are associated with the lowest VTE risk among CHCs.
- **Tumours:** 
    - **Cervical:** Slightly increased risk noted with long-term use, though possibly confounded by HPV and sexual behaviour.
    - **Breast:** Slightly increased relative risk (RR 1.24) in current users, which diminishes over 10 years post-discontinuation.
    - **Liver:** Benign or malignant tumours reported rarely, potentially leading to life-threatening intra-abdominal haemorrhage.
- **ALT Elevations:** Potential for significant ALT elevations when used with specific Hepatitis C direct-acting antivirals.
- **Metabolic Factors:** Hypertriglyceridemia increases pancreatitis risk. Monitor women with diabetes for changes in insulin resistance.
- **Other Precautions:** Chloasma (avoid UV exposure); potential for exogenous estrogens to exacerbate hereditary angioedema.
- **Medical Review:** A complete history and physical exam (including BP, breast, and pelvic assessment) should occur prior to initiation and annually.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Not specifically categorised in provided source. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, abdominal pain, increased weight, headache, depressed/altered mood, breast pain, and breast tenderness [Australian]. |
| **Serious** | Venous thromboembolism (DVT, PE), arterial thromboembolism (MI, stroke, TIA), liver tumours, and breast/cervical cancer. |

- **Other reported effects (association not confirmed):** Changes in vaginal discharge, breast hypertrophy, vomiting, diarrhoea, acne, hirsutism, alopecia, rash, urticaria, erythema nodosum/multiforme, contact lens intolerance, migraine, and changes in libido [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzyme Inducers (Decreased COC Efficacy):** Medicines inducing CYP3A4 (e.g., phenytoin, barbiturates, carbamazepine, rifampicin, oxcarbazepine, topiramate, felbamate, griseofulvin, and St John’s Wort) increase sex hormone clearance. Use barrier methods during and for 28 days after discontinuation.
- **Enzyme Inhibitors (Increased COC Levels):** Strong/moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., azole antifungals, verapamil, macrolides, diltiazem, grapefruit juice) can increase plasma concentrations of LNG/EE.
- **Effect on Other Medicines:** COCs may increase concentrations of ciclosporin or decrease concentrations of lamotrigine.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Risk of ALT elevation with Hepatitis C antivirals (e.g., ombitasvir/paritaprevir/dasabuvir).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3:** Contraindicated. If pregnancy occurs, discontinue immediately. Epidemiological data do not suggest teratogenic effects from inadvertent early pregnancy exposure.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraceptive steroids may reduce milk quantity and change composition. Small amounts are excreted in milk. Not generally recommended until the child is completely weaned [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in source.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated only after menarche [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Not indicated after menopause [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied in this population [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in women with severe hepatic disease until liver function markers return to normal [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primary action via inhibition of ovulation (gonadotropin suppression) and secondary changes in cervical secretion to prevent sperm entry.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Ethinylestradiol:** Rapidly absorbed (Tmax 1–2h); 60% bioavailability due to first-pass metabolism; 98.5% bound to albumin; metabolised by aromatic hydroxylation; half-life ~24 hours.
    - **Levonorgestrel:** Rapidly and completely absorbed (Tmax ~1h); 82% bioavailability; highly bound to SHBG (64%) and albumin (35%); metabolised by CYP3A4; half-life ~20 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Provides cycle regularity, reduced dysmenorrhea, and lighter bleeding. Higher-dose COCs (>35 mcg EE) are associated with reduced risks of certain cysts and cancers; this remains to be confirmed for lower-dose formulations [Australian].
