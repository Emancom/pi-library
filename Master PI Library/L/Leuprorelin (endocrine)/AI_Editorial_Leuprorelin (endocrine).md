# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine (Injection)

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA). [Note: While this template is designed to harmonize multiple sources, only the United States Product Information (USPI) was available for analysis.]
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals, synthesizing clinical data into a structured reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) mandated by local regulatory authorities. Prescribers should consult the current PI approved in their specific jurisdiction before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class**: Carrier molecule (amino acid derivative).
- **Brand Name(s)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - Injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism which result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders**: 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg given as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - **Severe Metabolic Crisis**: A loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the following 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maintenance**: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
    - **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration**: Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustments**: Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be made based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations, ideally as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels.
- **Metabolic Disorders**: Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended.
- **ESRD**: Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations (normal range: 40 to 50 µmol/L).

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These can occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management.

### Clinical Precautions
- **Seizures**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with established seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.
- **Oral Formulation Restriction**: The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Analysis (ESRD/Hemodialysis Context)
- **Very Common (>10%)**: 
    - General: Injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), headache (3–37%), flu syndrome (15–29%), chest pain (6–15%).
    - Cardiovascular: Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%).
    - Digestive: Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
    - Nervous/Respiratory: Dizziness (10–18%), pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Common (1–10%)**: 
    - Abdominal pain, fever, tachycardia, arrhythmia, weight increase/decrease, hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, leg cramps, pruritus, and taste perversion.
- **Serious Adverse Effects**: 
    - Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity reactions.
    - Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Clinical Note**: Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**: Not Specified in Available Sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - [US]: Not formally assigned a letter category in the provided document, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding**: 
    - Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing is based on body weight (mg/kg). Clinical trials have included paediatric patients, and dosing for metabolic disorders is similar to adults.
- **Elderly Use**: Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, dose selection for elderly patients should be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment**: Primary indication includes ESRD patients on dialysis. Oral formulations should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination**: Mean terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed significant increases in plasma levocarnitine. Pre-dialysis (trough) levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study**: In studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg, a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations was observed over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcomes Note**: While levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) has not been definitively determined in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions**: Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility**: Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL).
