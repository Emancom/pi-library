# Editorial Product Information: Levofloxacin

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of levofloxacin, synthesized from official product information to assist healthcare professionals in clinical decision-making. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levofloxacin (as hemihydrate), a synthetic broad-spectrum fluoroquinolone antibacterial agent. It is the S-enantiomer of the racemate ofloxacin.
*   **Brand Names:** Levofloxacin Tablets USP [US].
*   **ATC Code:** J01MA12.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   Film-coated tablets: 250 mg, 500 mg, and 750 mg [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Hetero Labs Limited (Manufacturer); RemedyRepack Inc. (Repackager) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Levofloxacin is indicated for the treatment of adults (≥18 years) with mild, moderate, and severe infections caused by susceptible isolates of specific microorganisms. Selected indications also apply to pediatric patients (≥6 months).
*   **Respiratory Tract Infections:** 
    *   Nosocomial Pneumonia [US].
    *   Community-Acquired Pneumonia (7–14 day and 5-day regimens) [US].
    *   Acute Bacterial Exacerbation of Chronic Bronchitis [US].
    *   Acute Bacterial Sinusitis (5-day and 10–14 day regimens) [US].
*   **Skin and Skin Structure Infections (SSSI):** 
    *   Complicated and Uncomplicated SSSI [US].
*   **Urinary Tract Infections (UTI):** 
    *   Chronic Bacterial Prostatitis [US].
    *   Complicated UTI (5-day and 10-day regimens) [US].
    *   Acute Pyelonephritis (5-day and 10-day regimens) [US].
    *   Uncomplicated UTI [US].
*   **Biothreat and Special Pathogens:** 
    *   Inhalational Anthrax (Post-Exposure) in adults and pediatric patients ≥6 months [US].
    *   Plague in adults and pediatric patients ≥6 months [US].

*Note: For Uncomplicated UTI, Acute Bacterial Exacerbation of Chronic Bronchitis, and Acute Bacterial Sinusitis, levofloxacin should be reserved for patients who have no alternative treatment options due to the risk of serious adverse reactions.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults with CrCl ≥50 mL/min)
Standard dosing is typically administered every 24 hours.
*   **Nosocomial Pneumonia:** 750 mg every 24 hours for 7–14 days.
*   **Community-Acquired Pneumonia:** 500 mg every 24 hours (7–14 days) or 750 mg every 24 hours (5 days).
*   **Complicated SSSI:** 750 mg every 24 hours for 7–14 days.
*   **Uncomplicated SSSI:** 500 mg every 24 hours for 7–10 days.
*   **Chronic Bacterial Prostatitis:** 500 mg every 24 hours for 28 days.
*   **Acute Bacterial Sinusitis:** 750 mg every 24 hours (5 days) or 500 mg every 24 hours (10–14 days).
*   **Inhalational Anthrax/Plague:** 500 mg every 24 hours (Duration: 60 days for Anthrax; 10–14 days for Plague).

### Administration Instructions
*   Tablets may be administered without regard to food [US].
*   **Chelation Therapy:** Administer levofloxacin at least 2 hours before or 2 hours after antacids (containing magnesium or aluminum), sucralfate, metal cations (iron, zinc), or buffered didanosine [US].
*   **Hydration:** Maintain adequate hydration to prevent crystalluria [US].

### Missed Dose
If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible up to 8 hours before the next scheduled dose. If less than 8 hours remain, the missed dose should be skipped [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to levofloxacin or other fluoroquinolone antibacterials [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
*   **Serious Adverse Reactions:** Fluoroquinolones are associated with disabling and potentially irreversible serious adverse reactions that can occur together, including tendinitis, tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, and CNS effects. Discontinue immediately if these occur.
*   **Exacerbation of Myasthenia Gravis:** Levofloxacin may exacerbate muscle weakness. Avoid use in patients with a known history of myasthenia gravis.

### General Warnings
*   **Hepatotoxicity:** Severe, sometimes fatal, hepatotoxicity has been reported, usually within 14 days of starting therapy. Discontinue if hepatitis symptoms develop [US].
*   **Aortic Aneurysm and Dissection:** Increased risk within two months of use, particularly in the elderly. Use only if no alternatives are available in high-risk patients [US].
*   **QT Prolongation:** Avoid in patients with known QT prolongation, uncorrected hypokalemia, or those taking Class IA or III antiarrhythmics [US].
*   **Central Nervous System Effects:** Risk of seizures, increased intracranial pressure, toxic psychoses, and tremors. Use with caution in patients with CNS disorders [US].
*   **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterials; can range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis [US].
*   **Blood Glucose Disturbances:** Risk of symptomatic hyper- and hypoglycemia, usually in diabetic patients on concomitant oral hypoglycemics or insulin [US].
*   **Photosensitivity:** Advise patients to avoid excessive sunlight or UV exposure [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (Frequency ≥3%)
*   Nausea, headache, diarrhea, insomnia, constipation, and dizziness [US].

### Uncommon to Rare (0.1% to 1%)
*   **Psychiatric:** Anxiety, agitation, confusion, depression, hallucinations, nightmare.
*   **Nervous System:** Tremor, convulsions, paresthesia, vertigo, hypertonia, syncope.
*   **Cardiac:** Palpitations, ventricular tachycardia, cardiac arrest.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, stomatitis, pancreatitis, esophagitis.
*   **Hepatobiliary:** Abnormal hepatic function, increased enzymes.

### Postmarketing Experience (Serious)
*   Pancytopenia, aplastic anemia, anaphylactic shock, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, rhabdomyolysis, interstitial nephritis, and ruptured tendons (Achilles and others) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Multivalent Cations:** Antacids, sucralfate, and multivitamins containing iron/zinc significantly decrease levofloxacin absorption. Space by at least 2 hours [US].
*   **Warfarin:** Levofloxacin may enhance the anticoagulant effect. Monitor INR and prothrombin time closely [US].
*   **Antidiabetic Agents:** Potential for altered blood glucose levels. Monitor glucose closely [US].
*   **NSAIDs:** Concomitant use with fluoroquinolones may increase the risk of CNS stimulation and convulsive seizures [US].
*   **Theophylline:** Levofloxacin does not significantly affect theophylline levels in healthy volunteers, but monitoring is recommended as other quinolones cause significant increases [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category:** Not assigned under current PLR labeling, but case reports/observational studies have not identified a clear drug-associated risk of major birth defects [US].
    *   **Animal Data:** Fetal toxicity observed in rats at doses ~1.2 times the MRHD; no teratogenicity observed at higher doses in rats or rabbits [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levofloxacin is present in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in the infant, a lactating woman may consider pumping and discarding milk during treatment and for 2 days after the final dose, unless treating for anthrax exposure (where risk-benefit may favor breastfeeding) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Use is limited to Inhalational Anthrax (Post-Exposure) and Plague in patients ≥6 months. Levofloxacin is not recommended for other pediatric infections due to the risk of musculoskeletal disorders (arthralgia, osteochondrosis) [US].
    *   Tablets are only suitable for children weighing ≥30 kg [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of tendon rupture (especially with corticosteroid use) and fatal hepatotoxicity. Aortic aneurysm risk is higher in this population [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Adjust dose for CrCl <50 mL/min [US].

| CrCl (mL/min) | Initial Dose | Subsequent Doses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **For 750 mg regimen** | | |
| 20 to 49 | 750 mg | 750 mg every 48 hours |
| 10 to 19 | 750 mg | 500 mg every 48 hours |
| Hemodialysis/CAPD | 750 mg | 500 mg every 48 hours |
| **For 500 mg regimen** | | |
| 20 to 49 | 500 mg | 250 mg every 24 hours |
| 10 to 19 | 500 mg | 250 mg every 48 hours |

*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Due to limited metabolism, pharmacokinetics are not expected to be significantly altered [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibition of bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV, essential for bacterial DNA replication, transcription, repair, and recombination [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    *   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; bioavailability ~99%. Peak plasma concentration (Cmax) reached in 1–2 hours.
    *   **Distribution:** Widely distributed into body tissues, including skin, blister fluid, and lungs. Protein binding is 24% to 38%.
    *   **Metabolism:** Minimal metabolism to desmethyl and N-oxide metabolites.
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily excreted unchanged in the urine (87%). Elimination half-life is approximately 6 to 8 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Nosocomial Pneumonia:** In a study comparing levofloxacin (750 mg) to imipenem/cilastatin followed by ciprofloxacin, success rates were 58.1% for levofloxacin vs. 60.6% for the comparator [US].
*   **Community-Acquired Pneumonia (5-day):** A study comparing 750 mg (5 days) to 500 mg (10 days) showed comparable success rates (90.9% vs. 91.1%) [US].
*   **Inhalational Anthrax:** Effectiveness was established using a surrogate endpoint based on plasma concentrations in humans and survival data in rhesus monkeys [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
*   Keep in a tightly closed container [US].
