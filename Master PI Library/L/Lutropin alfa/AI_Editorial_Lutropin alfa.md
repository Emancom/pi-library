# Editorial Product Information: Lutropin alfa

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lutropin alfa for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical practice in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lutropin alfa (recombinant human luteinising hormone [r-hLH]).
- **Chemical Class:** Gonadotrophin hormone; glycoprotein.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Luveris** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile, lyophilised powder for injection containing 75 IU of lutropin alfa [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Follicular Development Stimulation:** Indicated for use in association with a recombinant follicle stimulating hormone (FSH) preparation for the stimulation of follicular development in women diagnosed with severe LH and FSH deficiency [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Initiation:** Treatment must be supervised by a physician experienced in managing fertility disorders. 
- **Standard Regimen:** Treatment usually commences with a daily dose of 75 IU of lutropin alfa administered concomitantly with 75–150 IU of FSH.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Dosing should be individualised based on follicular size (via ultrasound) and oestradiol response. If an FSH dose increase is required, it should preferably occur at 7- to 14-day intervals in increments of 37.5 to 75 IU [Australian].
- **Duration:** If a response is not achieved within 3 weeks, the cycle should be abandoned and restarted in a subsequent cycle with higher starting doses [Australian].

### Final Maturation and Ovulation
- Once optimal follicular response is achieved, a single dose of 250 micrograms of recombinant hCG (r-hCG) or 5,000 IU to 10,000 IU of hCG is administered 24 to 48 hours after the final lutropin alfa and FSH injections. 
- Patients are advised to have coitus on the day of, and the day following, hCG administration [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** The powder must be reconstituted with the provided solvent (sterile water for injections) immediately prior to use.
- **Co-administration:** Lutropin alfa and follitropin alfa may be mixed in the same syringe for a single injection [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to gonadotrophins or any listed excipients.
- Malignancies: Ovarian, uterine, or mammary carcinoma.
- Tumours: Active, untreated tumours of the pituitary gland or hypothalamus.
- Ovarian Conditions: Ovarian enlargement or cysts of unknown aetiology (unrelated to polycystic ovarian disease).
- Gynaecological: Haemorrhage of unknown origin.
- Pregnancy and lactation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ovarian Hyperstimulation Syndrome (OHSS):** Controlled ovarian stimulation carries a risk of OHSS, a condition characterized by marked ovarian enlargement, high serum sex steroids, and increased vascular permeability. Severe OHSS may lead to ascites, pleural effusion, and rarely, thromboembolic events or ovarian torsion. If high risk is identified, hCG should be withheld, and the patient advised to avoid coitus or use barrier contraception for at least 4 days [Australian].
- **Multiple Pregnancy:** The risk of multiple births (primarily twins) is increased compared to natural conception. 
- **Pregnancy Loss:** Incidence of miscarriage or abortion is higher in patients undergoing ovulation induction than in the general population.
- **Thromboembolic Events:** Women with recognized risk factors or personal/family history may be at increased risk during gonadotrophin therapy.
- **Porphyria:** Patients with porphyria or a family history should be closely monitored; treatment should cease if the condition deteriorates.
- **Underlying Conditions:** Patients should be evaluated for hypothyroidism, adrenocortical deficiency, and hyperprolactinemia, and receive appropriate treatment prior to starting therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often associated with the pharmacodynamic consequences of ovarian stimulation.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Injection site reactions (mild) |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Headache, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain/discomfort, mild-to-moderate OHSS, ovarian cyst, breast pain, pelvic pain, injection site reactions (moderate) |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Not specified in source |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Adnexal torsion, haemoperitoneum (reported with human menopausal gonadotrophin therapy) |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Thromboembolism (usually associated with severe OHSS), anaphylactic reactions and shock [Australian] |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Compatibility:** **Luveris** should not be mixed with other medicinal products in the same syringe except for follitropin alfa. 
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Studies have shown that co-administration with follitropin alfa does not significantly alter the activity or pharmacokinetic properties of either active substance [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. Lutropin alfa is contraindicated during pregnancy. While inadvertent exposure data are limited and do not currently suggest teratogenic effects, animal studies have shown embryonic resorptions and adverse effects on postnatal survival/growth at clinically relevant doses [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Secretion of r-hLH or its metabolites has been observed in the milk of lactating rats [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Indicated specifically for the enhancement of fertility through follicular development.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No data available; however, caution and close monitoring are recommended as the impact of impairment has not been studied [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lutropin alfa binds to the shared LH/hCG receptor on ovarian theca (and granulosa) cells. In theca cells, it stimulates the secretion of androgens, which are then converted to oestradiol by aromatase in FSH-stimulated granulosa cells. This synergy is essential for follicle maturation and steroidogenesis [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The primary clinical effect is an increase in oestradiol secretion by follicles, supporting the action of FSH.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributed (initial t1/2 ~1 hour); steady-state volume of distribution is 10–14 L.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 10–12 hours. Total clearance is ~2 L/h; <5% of the dose is excreted unchanged in urine.
    - **Bioavailability:** Approximately 60% following SC administration [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study 6253:** A Phase II dose-finding study in women with severe LH/FSH deficiency. Success rates (defined by follicular development and oestradiol levels) were dose-dependent: 0% (placebo), 14.3% (25 IU), 44.4% (75 IU), and 50.0% (225 IU) [Australian].
- **Pivotal Study 21008:** A Phase III double-blind, randomized trial (n=39). Adequate follicular development was achieved in 65.4% of the 75 IU lutropin alfa group compared to 15.4% in the placebo group (p=0.006) [Australian].
- **Overall Pregnancy Rate:** Across five development studies (n=170), the pregnancy rate was approximately 31% in the 75 IU dose group [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Lyophilised Product:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Must be injected immediately as it contains no antimicrobial preservatives [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
