# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and does not substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 or 4 hours; frequency should never be less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized based on clinical requirements.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) prior to therapy and monitor weekly or monthly thereafter.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Normal range is 40 to 50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are supranormal.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration before administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, therapy must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical management initiated.
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.
- **Renal Impairment (Oral formulation precaution):** While this PI concerns the IV formulation, it is noted that chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients regardless of causality:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction, Pain, Flu syndrome, Headache, Chest pain, Abdominal pain, Fever | Common |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, Hypotension, Tachycardia | Common |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, Vomiting, Nausea | Common |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, Hypervolemia | Common |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, Cough increase, Dyspnea, Rhinitis | Common |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness, Paresthesia | Common |

### Postmarketing and Other Reactions
- **Neurological:** Seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting, gastritis.
- **Other:** Body odor (less frequent).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored in patients receiving warfarin therapy upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not categorized]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the child from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against maternal benefits. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have been established; dosing is weight-based (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, levocarnitine injection increases plasma carnitine concentrations and may alleviate metabolic abnormalities associated with carnitine deficiency (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Total body clearance is approximately 4.00 L/h.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is demonstrated, the specific effect of supplementation on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined in the provided clinical trial data [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC plastic bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL).
