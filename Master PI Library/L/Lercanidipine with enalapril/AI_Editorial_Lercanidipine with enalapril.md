# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for levocarnitine injection. It is designed as a professional reference for healthcare providers to facilitate clinical decision-making.
*   **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a clinical synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), a carrier molecule required for long-chain fatty acid transport across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    *   Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg.
    *   Administration: Slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or infusion.
    *   Acute Crisis: A loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and must not be less frequent than every 6 hours (q6h).
    *   Maintenance: Daily doses of 50 mg/kg or higher may be required based on clinical response. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   Starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   Administration: Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   Dose Adjustments: Adjustments may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
*   The formulation contains no preservative; any unused portion must be discarded.

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of IV administration. If a severe reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical management initiated [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
*   **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
*   **Oral vs. IV Use in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
*   **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** Monitoring should be conducted weekly and monthly. For metabolic disorders, free carnitine levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
*   **General Monitoring:** Assessment of blood chemistries, vital signs, and overall clinical condition is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
Adverse events reported in patients on chronic hemodialysis, regardless of causality, include:
*   **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (21%), headache (12%–37%), infection (15%–24%), injection site reaction (27%–38%), pain (21%–35%), fever (5%–12%), asthenia (8%–12%).
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18%–21%), hypotension (3%–19%), tachycardia (5%–9%), chest pain (6%–15%).
*   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9%–35%), nausea (5%–12%), vomiting (9%–21%).
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15%–27%), cough increase (10%–18%), dyspnea (3%–11%).
*   **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6%–15%), hypervolemia (3%–12%).

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis (infrequent), transient nausea/vomiting.
*   **Metabolic:** Body odor (infrequent).
*   **Neurological:** Seizures (Postmarketing experience).
*   **Immunological:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. Recommendation: Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **Category:** Not explicitly categorized by letter, but animal reproduction studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm.
*   **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].

### Breastfeeding
*   **Safety Information:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation has not been specifically studied in nursing mothers. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration.
*   **Recommendation:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug should be considered [US].

### Fertility
*   No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). Specific definitions for carnitine deficiency are provided for newborns and premature infants (plasma concentrations below age-related normal levels) [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Evaluated extensively in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Use caution and monitor for metabolite accumulation if considering oral formulations in patients with severe renal compromise [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
*   **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Total body clearance is approximately 4.00 L/h [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (N=12), 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed that trough carnitine concentrations increase in a dose-dependent manner over 12 and 24 weeks.
*   **Clinical Outcomes Note:** While levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population remains undetermined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
*   **Stability:** When diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
*   **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in provided US sources.
