# Editorial Product Information: Levamlodipine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levamlodipine product information for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available data to provide a concise clinical reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only the United States PI was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levamlodipine maleate.
- **Chemical Class:** Dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker (the pharmacologically active S-isomer of racemic amlodipine).
- **Brand Names:** **Conjupri** [US].
- **ATC Code:** C08CA01 (Amlodipine).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Tablets: 2.5 mg (functionally scored to provide a 1.25 mg dose) and 5 mg [US]. 
  - Note: 5 mg of levamlodipine provides similar systemic exposure (Cmax and AUC) to 10 mg of racemic amlodipine besylate [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** CSPC Ouyi Pharmaceutical Co. Ltd [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Indicated for the treatment of hypertension in adults and paediatric patients aged 6 years and older, either as monotherapy or in combination with other antihypertensive agents [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 2.1 Adult Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** 2.5 mg once daily.
- **Maximum Dose:** 5 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** Adjust dosage according to blood pressure goals. Typically, titration steps should occur every 7 to 14 days. More rapid titration is permissible if clinically warranted and the patient is frequently assessed.
- **Small, Fragile, or Elderly Patients:** May be initiated at 1.25 mg once daily. This lower starting dose is also recommended when adding levamlodipine to existing antihypertensive therapy.

### 2.2 Paediatric Dosing (Ages 6–17 years)
- **Effective Dose:** 1.25 mg to 2.5 mg once daily. 
- **Important Limitation:** Doses exceeding 2.5 mg daily have not been studied in paediatric patients [US].

### 2.3 Administration
- Administer orally once daily.
- Bioavailability is not affected by the presence of food.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to amlodipine or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Symptomatic Hypotension:** Possible, particularly in patients with severe aortic stenosis. However, due to the gradual onset of action, acute hypotension is unlikely.
- **Increased Angina or Myocardial Infarction:** Worsening angina and acute myocardial infarction may develop following the initiation or dose increase of levamlodipine, particularly in patients with severe obstructive coronary artery disease.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Because levamlodipine is extensively metabolised by the liver and has a prolonged elimination half-life (56 hours) in patients with impaired hepatic function, titration should proceed slowly in those with severe hepatic insufficiency.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate. The most common reaction is edema, which is dose-dependent.

### 6.1 Dose-Related Adverse Reactions (Incidence in Amlodipine Clinical Trials)
| Adverse Reaction | 2.5 mg (N=275) | 5.0 mg (N=296) | 10 mg* (N=268) | Placebo (N=520) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Edema** | 1.8% | 3.0% | 10.8% | 0.6% |
| **Dizziness** | 1.1% | 3.4% | 3.4% | 1.5% |
| **Flushing** | 0.7% | 1.4% | 2.6% | 0.0% |
| **Palpitation** | 0.7% | 1.4% | 4.5% | 0.6% |
*\*Note: Levamlodipine 5 mg is bioequivalent to amlodipine 10 mg.*

### 6.2 Non-Dose-Related Reactions (>1.0% incidence)
- Fatigue (4.5%)
- Nausea (2.9%)
- Abdominal pain (1.6%)
- Somnolence (1.4%)

### 6.3 Post-Marketing Experience
- **Infrequent reports:** Gynecomastia, jaundice, and hepatic enzyme elevations (consistent with cholestasis or hepatitis). 
- **Neurological:** Potential association with extrapyramidal disorder [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### 7.1 Impact of Other Drugs on Levamlodipine
- **CYP3A Inhibitors:** Co-administration with moderate to strong CYP3A inhibitors (e.g., diltiazem, itraconazole, clarithromycin) increases systemic exposure to amlodipine. Dose reduction may be necessary; monitor for symptoms of hypotension and edema.
- **CYP3A Inducers:** Monitor blood pressure closely when co-administered, as quantitative effects on exposure are not well-defined.
- **Sildenafil:** Monitor for increased hypotension when used in combination.

### 7.2 Impact of Levamlodipine on Other Drugs
- **Simvastatin:** Levamlodipine increases simvastatin exposure. **Limit simvastatin dose to 20 mg daily** in patients receiving levamlodipine [US].
- **Immunosuppressants (Cyclosporine/Tacrolimus):** Amlodipine may increase the systemic exposure of these agents. Frequent monitoring of trough blood levels is recommended, with dose adjustments as appropriate. 

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No specific category; data insufficient to inform drug-associated risk]. Animal studies showed decreased litter size and increased intrauterine deaths at doses 10-20 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD). Hypertension in pregnancy itself poses significant risks to the mother (pre-eclampsia) and fetus (growth restriction).
- **Breastfeeding:** Limited data indicate amlodipine is present in human milk (median relative infant dose estimated at 4.2%). No adverse effects on breastfed infants have been observed [US].
- **Fertility:** No effects on rat fertility were observed at doses up to 8 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients 6–17 years of age. Safety and efficacy in patients <6 years are not established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance is decreased in the elderly, resulting in an AUC increase of 40–60%. Start dosing at the low end of the range (1.25 mg) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics are not significantly influenced by renal impairment. Patients may receive the usual initial dose.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is decreased. Titrate slowly in patients with severe impairment. Initial dose of 1.25 mg may be required [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A dihydropyridine calcium antagonist that inhibits the transmembrane influx of calcium ions into vascular smooth muscle and cardiac muscle. It acts as a peripheral arterial vasodilator, reducing peripheral vascular resistance and blood pressure.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces vasodilation without significant changes in heart rate or plasma catecholamine levels with chronic dosing. It does not affect sinoatrial nodal function or atrioventricular conduction.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations occur 6–12 hours post-dose. Absolute bioavailability is 64–90%.
  - **Distribution:** Approximately 93% protein bound.
  - **Metabolism:** Extensively (90%) converted to inactive metabolites via the liver.
  - **Elimination:** Biphasic; terminal elimination half-life is approximately 30–50 hours. Steady-state is reached after 7–8 days of daily dosing.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Hypertension:** Efficacy established in 15 double-blind, placebo-controlled studies. Once-daily administration provides statistically significant reductions in supine and standing blood pressure 24 hours post-dose (averaging 12/6 mmHg standing and 13/7 mmHg supine) [US].
- **Paediatric Hypertension:** A study of 268 patients (6–17 years) demonstrated that 2.5 mg and 5 mg doses of amlodipine significantly lowered systolic blood pressure more than placebo, though the magnitude was less than 5 mmHg [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers. Keep out of reach of children.
