# Editorial Product Information: Lincomycin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lincomycin for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.

It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information approved by the relevant regulatory authority in a specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should always consult the official PI for the jurisdiction in which they are practicing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lincomycin (as hydrochloride monohydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Lincosamide antibiotic.
- **Brand Name(s):** Lincocin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01FF02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 600 mg/2 mL (300 mg/mL) vial [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) and Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Lincomycin is indicated for the treatment of serious infections caused by susceptible strains of gram-positive aerobes (e.g., staphylococci, streptococci, and pneumococci). Its use should be restricted to patients allergic to penicillin or those for whom penicillin therapy is deemed inappropriate by clinical judgement. [Australian]

Approved indications include:
- **Upper Respiratory Tract Infections:** Tonsillitis, pharyngitis, otitis media, sinusitis, scarlet fever, and as adjuvant therapy for diphtheria.
- **Lower Respiratory Tract Infections:** Acute and chronic bronchitis, and pneumonia.
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** Cellulitis, furuncles, abscesses, impetigo, acne, and wound infections.
- **Bone and Joint Infections:** Osteomyelitis and septic arthritis.
- **Septicaemia and Endocarditis:** Selected cases (though bactericidal agents are generally preferred).
- **Bacillary Dysentery:** Caused by *Shigella* (due to high drug levels attained in the bowel). [Australian]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
Lincomycin must NOT be administered as an IV bolus. It requires dilution and slow infusion. If significant diarrhoea occurs during therapy, the medication should be discontinued. For beta-haemolytic streptococcal infections, treatment must continue for at least 10 days.

### Adult Dosing [Australian]
- **Intramuscular:** 
    - Serious infections: 600 mg every 24 hours.
    - More serious infections: 600 mg every 12 hours or more frequently based on severity.
- **Intravenous Infusion:**
    - Serious infections: 600 mg to 1 g every 8–12 hours.
    - Life-threatening situations: Doses up to 8 g daily have been utilised.

### Paediatric Dosing (over 1 month of age) [Australian]
- **Intramuscular:** 
    - Serious infections: 10 mg/kg/day as a single injection.
    - More serious infections: 10 mg/kg every 12 hours or more often.
- **Intravenous Infusion:** 10–20 mg/kg/day in divided doses, depending on infection severity.

### IV Dilution and Infusion Rates [Australian]
Doses are diluted in at least 100 mL of a compatible solution (e.g., Normal Saline, 5% or 10% Glucose) and infused over no less than one hour.
- **600 mg dose:** 100 mL diluent / 1 hour.
- **1 g dose:** 100 mL diluent / 1 hour.
- **2 g dose:** 200 mL diluent / 2 hours.
- **3 g dose:** 300 mL diluent / 3 hours.
- **4 g dose:** 400 mL diluent / 4 hours.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to lincomycin or clindamycin. [Australian]
- Treatment of minor bacterial infections or viral infections. [Australian]
- Use in newborns (due to benzyl alcohol content). [Australian]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Risk of Colitis:** Use can lead to severe *Clostridium difficile* associated diarrhoea (CDAD) and colitis, which may be fatal. This diagnosis should be considered in any patient presenting with diarrhoea up to several weeks after treatment cessation. Drugs that inhibit peristalsis (e.g., opiates) are contraindicated in these cases. [Australian]
- **Administration Warning:** Severe cardiopulmonary reactions may occur if administered at higher concentrations or rates than recommended. Bolus IV injection is prohibited. [Australian]
- **Hypersensitivity and SCARs:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis and Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR) such as Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), have been reported. Discontinue immediately if these occur. [Australian]
- **Benzyl Alcohol Toxicity:** The injection contains benzyl alcohol (9.45 mg/mL), which is associated with "Gasping Syndrome" in premature infants and newborns. [Australian]
- **Meningitis:** Although it enters the CSF, levels are inadequate; lincomycin is not indicated for meningitis. [Australian]
- **Monitoring:** Periodic liver and renal function tests and blood counts are recommended during prolonged therapy. [Australian]

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhoea, vomiting, nausea. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Vaginal infection, rash, urticaria. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Stomatitis, pruritus. |
| **Not Known** | Pseudomembranous colitis, pancytopenia, agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia, anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN, jaundice, hypotension (after rapid IV), sterile abscess/pain at IM site. |

*Note: Hypotension and cardio-respiratory arrest have been reported following rapid intravenous administration.* [Australian]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Neuromuscular Blocking Agents:** Lincomycin possesses neuromuscular blocking properties and may enhance the action of other blocking agents (e.g., tubocurarine, pancuronium). Use with caution. [Australian]
- **Physical Incompatibilities:** Lincomycin is physically incompatible with **novobiocin**, **kanamycin**, and **phenytoin**. [Australian]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category A [Australian]:** Taken by a large number of pregnant women without a proven increase in the frequency of malformations or other direct/indirect harmful effects on the human foetus.
    - Lincomycin crosses the placenta; cord serum levels are approximately 25% of maternal levels. [Australian]
- **Breastfeeding:** Lincomycin is excreted in breast milk (0.5 to 2.4 micrograms/mL). Use is not recommended during lactation unless alternative feeding arrangements are made. [Australian]
- **Fertility:** No data available in humans; no effects on fertility observed in rat studies up to 1000 mg/kg/day. [Australian]

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated in newborns. Use in infants >1 month is based on weight-based dosing (see Section 3). Monitor for benzyl alcohol toxicity. [Australian]
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available, but older patients with severe illness may tolerate diarrhoea less well and require close monitoring. [Australian]
- **Renal Impairment:** In severe renal impairment, the recommended dose should be reduced to **25% to 30%** of the standard dose. [Australian]
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Serum half-life may be doubled in patients with liver dysfunction. Dose and frequency reduction should be considered. Use in patients with pre-existing liver disease is not recommended unless clinically essential. [Australian]

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lincomycin binds to the 50S subunit of the bacterial ribosome, inhibiting protein synthesis. It is predominantly bacteriostatic. [Australian]
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak serum levels (IM) occur at 60 minutes.
    - **Distribution:** Distributes to most body tissues; poor CSF penetration.
    - **Metabolism:** Partially inactivated in the liver.
    - **Excretion:** Excreted via bile and urine. The biological half-life is approximately 5 hours. Neither haemodialysis nor peritoneal dialysis effectively removes lincomycin from the blood. [Australian]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C. 
- Protect from light. 
- Do not freeze. [Australian]
