# Editorial Product Information: Lumateperone

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lumateperone, synthesising official product information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the United States (FDA) source was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lumateperone (as lumateperone tosylate).
- **Chemical Class:** Atypical antipsychotic.
- **Brand Name:** **CAPLYTA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** N05AX16 (not explicitly in source, but standard classification).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Oral Capsules: 42 mg, 21 mg, and 10.5 mg [US].
  - Note: Strengths are expressed as the active base; 42 mg of lumateperone is equivalent to 60 mg of lumateperone tosylate [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Intra-Cellular Therapies, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia:** Treatment of schizophrenia in adults [US].
- **Bipolar Depression:** Treatment of depressive episodes associated with bipolar I or II disorder in adults, utilized as either monotherapy or as adjunctive therapy with lithium or valproate [US].
- **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** Adjunctive therapy with antidepressants for the treatment of MDD in adults [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dosage is 42 mg administered orally once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** May be taken with or without food [US].
- **Titration:** Dose titration is not required for the initiation of therapy [US].
- **Dose Adjustments for Concomitant Medications:**
  - **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Reduce dosage to 10.5 mg once daily [US].
  - **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Reduce dosage to 21 mg once daily [US].
  - **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Concomitant use should be avoided [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to lumateperone or any components of the formulation. Observed reactions include pruritus, rash (allergic dermatitis, papular, or generalized), and urticaria [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Increased Mortality in Elderly Patients with Dementia-Related Psychosis):** Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs are at an increased risk of death. **CAPLYTA** is not approved for the treatment of these patients [US].
- **Boxed Warning (Suicidal Thoughts and Behaviors):** Antidepressants increase the risk of suicidal thoughts and behaviors in pediatric and young adult patients. Safety and effectiveness have not been established in pediatric patients [US].
- **Cerebrovascular Adverse Reactions:** Increased incidence of stroke and transient ischemic attack (TIA) observed in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis [US].
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal symptom complex; discontinue immediately if suspected and provide intensive monitoring [US].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia (TD):** Risk increases with duration of treatment and cumulative dose, particularly in the elderly. Consider discontinuation if signs/symptoms appear [US].
- **Metabolic Changes:** Monitor for hyperglycemia/diabetes mellitus (assess fasting glucose), dyslipidemia (obtain fasting lipid profile), and weight gain [US].
- **Leukopenia, Neutropenia, and Agranulocytosis:** Monitor CBC in patients with pre-existing low WBC or history of drug-induced leukopenia/neutropenia. Discontinue if ANC < 1000/mm³ [US].
- **Orthostatic Hypotension and Syncope:** Monitor heart rate and blood pressure, especially in patients with known cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease [US].
- **Seizures:** Use with caution in patients with a history of seizures or conditions that lower the seizure threshold [US].
- **Potential for Cognitive and Motor Impairment:** Caution patients regarding operating heavy machinery or motor vehicles until they are certain the therapy does not adversely affect them [US].
- **Body Temperature Dysregulation:** Use with caution in patients experiencing conditions that may increase core body temperature (e.g., strenuous exercise, extreme heat, dehydration) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Schizophrenia:** Somnolence/Sedation (24%) [US].
- **Bipolar Depression (Monotherapy):** Headache (14%), Somnolence/Sedation (13%) [US].
- **Bipolar Depression (Adjunctive):** Somnolence/Sedation (13%), Dizziness (11%) [US].
- **MDD (Adjunctive):** Headache (19%), Dizziness (17%), Dry Mouth (13%), Somnolence/Sedation (12%) [US].

### Common (1–10%)
- **Schizophrenia:** Nausea (9%), Dry Mouth (6%), Dizziness (5%), Creatine Phosphokinase increased (4%), Fatigue (3%), Vomiting (3%), Hepatic Transaminases increased (2%), Decreased appetite (2%) [US].
- **Bipolar Depression:** Nausea (8-9%), Dizziness (8%), Dry mouth (5%), Diarrhea (3-4%), Vomiting (4%), Upper respiratory tract infection (2-3%), Blurred vision (3%), Increased blood prolactin (2%) [US].
- **MDD (Adjunctive):** Nausea (9%), Fatigue (8%), Diarrhea (5%), Tremor (4%), Vomiting (3%), Vertigo (3%), Insomnia (3%) [US].

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Nervous System Disorders:** Burning sensation, including skin burning sensation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:**
  - **Strong (e.g., Itraconazole):** Increases lumateperone exposure by 3.8-fold. Reduce dose to 10.5 mg [US].
  - **Moderate (e.g., Diltiazem):** Increases lumateperone exposure by 2.3-fold. Reduce dose to 21 mg [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Rifampin):** Decreases lumateperone exposure by 98%. Avoid concomitant use [US].
- **Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs/SNRIs):** Monitor for SRI-associated adverse reactions (e.g., serotonin syndrome, hyponatremia) due to lumateperone’s moderate serotonin transporter (SERT) activity [US].
- **UGT Inhibitors (e.g., Valproic Acid, Probenecid):** No clinically significant interaction observed [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Risk Summary:** Neonates exposed to antipsychotics during the third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms [US].
  - **Category:** Not specified by letter category in the current US labeling (PLLR format), but animal studies showed no malformations at clinically relevant doses. Higher doses in rats resulted in increased perinatal deaths and decreased pup weight [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Lumateperone and its metabolites are present in human milk at very low levels (relative infant dose [RID] of 0.06% of the maternal weight-adjusted dose). There are no data on the effects on milk production or the breastfed infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential impairment of male and female fertility (decreased sperm motility, changes in sperm morphology, and estrus cycle irregularities) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of death in patients with dementia-related psychosis. Generally, no dose adjustment is required for elderly patients without hepatic impairment, though monitoring for falls and orthostatic hypotension is advised [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild to severe renal impairment (eGFR ≥ 15 mL/min/1.73m²). Use is not recommended for eGFR < 15 mL/min/1.73m² as it has not been studied [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
  - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment required [US].
  - **Moderate/Severe (Child-Pugh B/C):** Reduce dosage to 21 mg once daily [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The exact mechanism is unknown. Efficacy may be mediated through antagonist activity at central serotonin 5-HT2A receptors and partial agonist activity at central dopamine D2 receptors [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** High affinity for 5-HT2A (Ki = 0.54 nM) and moderate affinity for D2 (Ki = 32 nM). Also binds to serotonin transporters, D1, D4, and adrenergic alpha-1 receptors [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is approximately 4.4%. Tmax occurs within 1-2 hours. High-fat meals decrease Cmax by 33% but increase AUC by 9% [US].
  - **Distribution:** Protein binding is 97.4%. Vd is ~4.1 L/kg [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized via CYP3A4, 2C8, 1A2, and UGTs (1A1, 1A4, 2B15) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~18 hours. 58% excreted in urine and 29% in feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Schizophrenia:** Efficacy demonstrated in two 4-week placebo-controlled trials showing significant reduction in PANSS total scores [US].
- **Bipolar Depression:** Demonstrated efficacy in a 6-week monotherapy trial and a 6-week adjunctive trial (with lithium or valproate) showing significant improvement in MADRS total scores [US].
- **MDD (Adjunctive):** Demonstrated efficacy in two 6-week placebo-controlled trials where lumateperone plus an antidepressant significantly improved MADRS total scores compared to an antidepressant plus placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
