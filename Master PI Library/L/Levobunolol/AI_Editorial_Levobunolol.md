# Editorial Product Information: Levobunolol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating available regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levobunolol hydrochloride (a noncardioselective beta-adrenoceptor blocking agent).
- **Chemical Class:** Beta-adrenergic receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** Levobunolol Hydrochloride Ophthalmic Solution (Bausch & Lomb) [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01ED03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Ophthalmic solution 0.5% (5 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Ophthalmic (topical use in the eye only).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bausch & Lomb Incorporated [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Open-Angle Glaucoma:** Approved for lowering intraocular pressure (IOP) [US].
- **Ocular Hypertension:** Approved for lowering elevated IOP [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Starting Dose:** Instil 1 to 2 drops of levobunolol hydrochloride 0.5% into the affected eye(s) once daily [US].
- **Maintenance/Severe Cases:** In patients with severe or poorly controlled glaucoma, the frequency may be increased to twice daily [US].

### Administration Instructions
- For ophthalmic use only.
- If more than one topical ophthalmic drug is being used, the drugs should be administered at least 10 minutes apart [Not specified in source, but standard clinical practice; source specifies not to use two topical beta-blockers simultaneously].
- Patients wearing soft contact lenses should remove them prior to administration and wait at least 15 minutes before reinsertion due to the preservative benzalkonium chloride [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- Careful monitoring of IOP is advised when initiating therapy or changing dosages.
- Dosages exceeding one drop twice daily have not been shown to provide additional efficacy [US].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Respiratory Disease:** Bronchial asthma, a history of bronchial asthma, or severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) [US].
- **Cardiac Conditions:** Sinus bradycardia, second- and third-degree atrioventricular (AV) block, overt cardiac failure, or cardiogenic shock [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to levobunolol hydrochloride or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Systemic Absorption:** Like other topically applied ophthalmic agents, levobunolol is absorbed systemically. The same adverse reactions associated with systemic beta-blockers may occur, including severe respiratory and cardiac reactions (and rarely, death) [US].
- **Cardiac Failure:** Beta-blockade may further depress myocardial contractility and precipitate more severe failure. At the first sign of cardiac failure, therapy should be discontinued [US].
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** This product contains sodium metabisulfite, which may cause allergic-type reactions (including anaphylaxis and asthmatic episodes) in susceptible individuals [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Diabetes Mellitus:** Beta-blockers may mask the signs and symptoms of acute hypoglycemia (e.g., tachycardia) in patients with labile diabetes or those subject to spontaneous hypoglycemia [US].
- **Thyrotoxicosis:** Therapy may mask clinical signs of hyperthyroidism. Abrupt withdrawal should be avoided as it may precipitate a thyroid storm [US].
- **Muscle Weakness:** Beta-blockade has been reported to potentiate muscle weakness consistent with myasthenic symptoms (e.g., diplopia, ptosis, generalized weakness) [US].
- **Vascular Insufficiency:** Use with caution in patients with Raynaud’s phenomenon or cerebrovascular insufficiency [US].
- **Surgical Precaution:** Gradual withdrawal of beta-blockers prior to major surgery may be appropriate, as beta-blockade impairs the heart's ability to respond to reflex stimuli [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Ocular Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Transient burning and stinging (up to 1 in 3 patients) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Blepharoconjunctivitis (up to 1 in 20 patients) [US].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Iridocyclitis, decreased corneal sensitivity [US].
- **Frequency Not Specified:** Keratitis, blepharoptosis, visual disturbances (including refractive changes), diplopia, and ptosis [US].

### Systemic Effects
- **Cardiovascular:** Bradycardia, arrhythmia, hypotension, syncope, heart block, cerebral ischemia, congestive heart failure, palpitation, and cardiac arrest [US].
- **Respiratory:** Bronchospasm (primarily in patients with pre-existing disease), respiratory failure, dyspnea, and nasal congestion [US].
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Headache, dizziness, fatigue, lethargy, depression, confusion, and paresthesia [US].
- **Dermatological:** Urticaria, pruritus, localized/generalized rash, alopecia, and Stevens-Johnson Syndrome [US].
- **Digestive:** Nausea and diarrhea [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Oral Beta-Blockers:** Potential for additive effects on systemic beta-blockade and/or intraocular pressure [US].
- **Catecholamine-Depleting Drugs (e.g., Reserpine):** Risk of additive effects leading to hypotension and/or marked bradycardia [US].
- **Calcium Antagonists:** Risk of AV conduction disturbances, left ventricular failure, and hypotension. Avoid simultaneous use in patients with impaired cardiac function [US].
- **Digitalis:** Concomitant use with beta-blockers may increase AV conduction time [US].
- **Epinephrine:** While levobunolol alone has little effect on pupil size, mydriasis may occur when used concomitantly with epinephrine [US].
- **Phenothiazines:** Potential for additive hypotensive effects due to mutual inhibition of metabolism [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Levobunolol was found to be fetotoxic in rabbits at doses 200–700 times the human dose. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if levobunolol is excreted in human milk. Because many drugs (including systemic beta-blockers) are excreted in milk, caution should be exercised [US].
- **Fertility:** Studies in rats showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility at doses up to 1,800 times the recommended human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between elderly and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levobunolol is a noncardioselective beta-adrenoceptor blocking agent, showing equipotency at $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ receptors. The primary ocular mechanism is the reduction of aqueous humor production [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
- Levobunolol has minimal local anesthetic (membrane-stabilising) activity and no significant intrinsic sympathomimetic activity [US].
- It reduces IOP with little to no effect on pupil size or accommodation [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Onset of Action:** Within one hour after treatment [US].
- **Peak Effect:** Between 2 and 6 hours [US].
- **Duration:** Significant IOP reduction can be maintained for up to 24 hours following a single dose [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Long-term Studies:** In controlled studies of approximately two years, 0.5% levobunolol twice daily controlled IOP in approximately 80% of subjects, with a mean decrease of 7–8 mmHg [US].
- **Short-term Studies:** In a three-month clinical study, once-daily application of 0.5% levobunolol controlled IOP in 72% of subjects, with a mean decrease of 7.0 mmHg [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 15°C to 25°C (59°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light. Keep the cap immediately replaced after use [US].
