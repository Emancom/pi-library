# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Practitioners should consult their local regulatory guidelines and the current manufacturer-provided PI for legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), an amino acid derivative/carrier molecule.
- **Brand Names:** Noted generically as Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source; derived from standard clinical reference).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often provided in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; should not be administered less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, adjusted based on clinical requirement. Maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of IV administration. If a severe reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - **Metabolic Disorders:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
    - **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) levels (normal range 40–50 µmol/L) to guide dosing [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine Caution:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High oral doses in ESRD may lead to accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trials in ESRD patients on hemodialysis.

- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), Pain (30–49%), Flu syndrome (25–40%), Headache (3–37%), Chest pain (6–15%), Abdominal pain (5–21%), Fever (5–12%), Accidental injury (8–12%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–19%), Rhinitis (6–11%) [US].
    - **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), Paresthesia (3–12%) [US].
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), Hypervolemia (3–12%) [US].

- **Common (1–10%):**
    - **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, Palpitations, Atrial fibrillation, Arrhythmia [US].
    - **Digestive:** Anorexia, Constipation, Dyspepsia, Melena [US].
    - **Musculoskeletal:** Leg cramps, Myalgia [US].
    - **Other:** Asthenia, Back pain, Infection, Anemia, Peripheral edema, Weight increase/decrease, Depression, Insomnia, Pruritus, Rash, Taste perversion [US].

- **Postmarketing Experience (Serious/Significant):**
    - **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history, seizure frequency or severity may increase [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concurrently with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins; therefore, displacement interactions are unlikely [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned in OCR, but specifies use only if clearly needed]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women exist [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks to the infant; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders are applicable to paediatric patients; specifically, the 50 mg/kg dose is standard [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing is specifically tailored for ESRD patients on hemodialysis (see Section 3). High-dose oral formulations are not recommended in severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine compounds which are rapidly excreted [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic/Bioavailability Trials:** Studies in healthy volunteers established that oral tablets and solutions are bioequivalent, with absolute bioavailability of approximately 15% after correction for endogenous levels [US].
- **ESRD Trials:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients demonstrated that doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations, although clinical outcome improvements (signs/symptoms) were not definitively determined in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].
