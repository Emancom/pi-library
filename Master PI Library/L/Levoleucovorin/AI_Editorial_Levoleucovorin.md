# Editorial Product Information: Levoleucovorin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levoleucovorin, synthesised from official Product Information (PI) for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Only the United States (FDA) PI was provided for this synthesis; information regarding other jurisdictions is marked as "Not specified in available sources."

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levoleucovorin (as disodium salt). It is the pharmacologically active levo-isomer of d,l-leucovorin.
- **Brand Names:** **KHAPZORY** [US]
- **ATC Code:** V03AF04 (Leucovorin: V03AF03)
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - [US]: Lyophilized powder for injection: 175 mg levoleucovorin in a single-dose vial for reconstitution.
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) only. **Do not administer intrathecally.**
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - [US]: Acrotech Biopharma Inc.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **High-Dose Methotrexate Rescue:** For rescue after high-dose methotrexate therapy in adults and paediatric patients with osteosarcoma [US].
- **Folic Acid Antagonist Toxicity/Elimination:** For diminishing toxicity associated with overdosage of folic acid antagonists or impaired methotrexate elimination in adults and paediatric patients [US].
- **Metastatic Colorectal Cancer:** In combination with fluorouracil for the treatment of adults with metastatic colorectal cancer [US].

**Limitations of Use:**
- Not indicated for pernicious anaemia or megaloblastic anaemias secondary to Vitamin B12 deficiency; use carries a risk of neurological progression despite haematologic remission [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- **Route:** For intravenous administration only. **Contraindicated for intrathecal use.**
- **Compatibility:** The disodium salt formulation (KHAPZORY) is compatible with fluorouracil and may be combined in the same infusion bag for up to 72 hours at 20°C to 25°C [US].

### Rescue after High-Dose Methotrexate (MTX)
Recommendations assume an MTX dose of 12 g/m² administered over 4 hours.
- **Initial Dose:** 7.5 mg (approx. 5 mg/m²) intravenously every 6 hours.
- **Timing:** Start 24 hours after the beginning of the MTX infusion.
- **Duration:** Continue until MTX levels fall below 5 x 10⁻⁸ M (0.05 micromolar).
- **Dose Adjustments:** Dosing must be adjusted based on MTX elimination rates and serum creatinine levels. For delayed early elimination or acute renal injury, the dose may be increased to 75 mg every 3 hours until MTX levels are < 1 micromolar [US].

### Overdosage of Folic Acid Antagonists
- **Timing:** Start as soon as possible after overdose or within 24 hours of delayed MTX elimination.
- **Dose:** 7.5 mg (approx. 5 mg/m²) every 6 hours until MTX levels fall below 5 x 10⁻⁸ M.
- **Monitoring:** Monitor serum creatinine and MTX levels every 24 hours. Increase dose to 50 mg/m² every 3 hours if creatinine increases by 50% or MTX levels exceed specific thresholds at 24/48 hours [US].

### Metastatic Colorectal Cancer (with Fluorouracil)
Two common regimens include:
1. **Regimen A:** Levoleucovorin 100 mg/m² (IV injection over ≥3 mins) followed by fluorouracil 370 mg/m² daily for 5 days.
2. **Regimen B:** Levoleucovorin 10 mg/m² (IV injection) followed by fluorouracil 425 mg/m² daily for 5 days.
- **Cycle:** Repeat every 4 weeks for two courses, then every 4–5 weeks if toxicities have resolved. Do not adjust levoleucovorin dosage for toxicity [US].

### Preparation
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute the 175 mg vial with 3.6 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection (final concentration 50 mg/mL).
- **Dilution:** Further dilute with 0.9% NaCl or 5% Dextrose to a concentration between 0.5 mg/mL and 5 mg/mL.
- **Stability:** Reconstituted or diluted solutions may be stored at room temperature for up to 12 hours. Protect from light [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Patients with a history of severe hypersensitivity to leucovorin products, folic acid, or folinic acid [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Intrathecal Administration:** **Fatalities** have been reported with the intrathecal administration of leucovorin; levoleucovorin must be administered intravenously only [US].
- **Enhanced Fluorouracil Toxicity:** Levoleucovorin increases the toxicity of fluorouracil. Gastrointestinal toxicities (stomatitis, diarrhoea) may be severe or fatal, particularly in the elderly. Therapy should not be initiated/continued in patients with GI symptoms until resolved [US].
- **Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) Interaction:** Concomitant use with d,l-leucovorin has been associated with increased treatment failure and morbidity in HIV patients being treated for *Pneumocystis jiroveci* pneumonia [US].
- **Monitoring:** Daily monitoring of serum creatinine and MTX levels is mandatory during MTX rescue [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### High-Dose Methotrexate Rescue
- **Very Common (≥20%):** Stomatitis, vomiting [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Nausea, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, typhlitis, dyspnea, dermatitis, confusion, neuropathy, renal function abnormal, taste perversion [US].

### Combination with Fluorouracil (Colorectal Cancer)
- **Very Common (>50%):** Stomatitis (72%), diarrhoea (70%), nausea (62%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Vomiting (40%), asthenia/fatigue (29%), dermatitis (29%), alopecia (26%), anorexia (24%), abdominal pain (14%) [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Severe enterocolitis, dehydration, and death have been reported, primarily when used in combination with fluorouracil in elderly patients [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antiepileptic Drugs:** High doses of folic acid may counteract the effects of phenobarbital, phenytoin, and primidone, potentially increasing seizure frequency in children [US].
- **Fluorouracil:** Pharmacodynamic enhancement of both therapeutic and toxic effects of fluorouracil [US].
- **TMP-SMX:** Potential for increased treatment failure in *Pneumocystis jiroveci* pneumonia [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [US]: **Category not assigned.** Limited data in pregnant women. Levoleucovorin is often used with MTX or fluorouracil, both of which can cause embryo-fetal harm. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted with levoleucovorin.
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available regarding the presence of levoleucovorin in human milk or its effects on the infant or milk production [US].
- **Fertility:** No studies have evaluated the impact of levoleucovorin on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - [US]: Safety and efficacy established for MTX rescue and folic acid antagonist toxicity (studied in patients aged 6 years and older). Not established for metastatic colorectal cancer.
- **Elderly Use:** 
    - [US]: Clinical trials in osteosarcoma did not include patients ≥65 years. In colorectal cancer trials, adverse reaction profiles were similar between older and younger patients, though the elderly are generally at higher risk for severe GI toxicity from fluorouracil.
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires intensive monitoring of serum creatinine and MTX levels; dose adjustments for levoleucovorin are necessary for patients with evidence of acute renal injury [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levoleucovorin is the active isomer of 5-formyl tetrahydrofolic acid. It does not require reduction by dihydrofolate reductase to participate in one-carbon moiety transfer. In MTX rescue, it bypasses the metabolic block caused by folic acid antagonists. In CRC, it stabilizes the binding of fluorodeoxyuridinemonophosphate (FdUMP) to thymidylate synthase, enhancing the inhibition of DNA synthesis [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Small quantities enter the cerebrospinal fluid.
    - **Metabolism:** Converted to 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate and then to 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (the primary circulating folate).
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life is approximately 5.1 to 6.8 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Osteosarcoma:** Efficacy in MTX rescue was supported by an open-label study of 16 patients (ages 6–21) where the adverse reaction profile was consistent with expected outcomes for high-dose MTX therapy [US].
- **Colorectal Cancer:** A randomized trial (NCCTG) compared levoleucovorin/5-FU to d,l-leucovorin/5-FU. The trial failed to show superior overall survival for levoleucovorin over the racemic mixture, but established comparable efficacy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from light. Keep in original carton [US].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Use within 12 hours if stored at room temperature [US].
