# Editorial Product Information: Lurasidone

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lurasidone hydrochloride, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for the most current and legally binding information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Lurasidone hydrochloride (Atypical antipsychotic; benzisothiazol derivative).
- **Brand Names**: APO-Lurasidon [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: N05AE05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Film-coated tablets: 20 mg, 40 mg, and 80 mg. 
    - *Note: In the Australian jurisdiction, the 20 mg strength is listed but may have limited availability or specific supply restrictions noted in clinical practice.* [Australian]
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia**: Treatment of schizophrenia in adults and adolescents aged 13 to 17 years [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults)**:
    - **Starting Dose**: 40 mg once daily.
    - **Maintenance Range**: 40 mg to 80 mg once daily is effective for most patients.
    - **Maximum Dose**: 160 mg once daily [Australian].
    - **Titration**: Initial dose titration is not required. Dose increases should be based on clinical response and physician judgement.
- **Adolescent Dosing (13–17 years)**:
    - **Starting Dose**: 40 mg once daily.
    - **Maximum Dose**: 80 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - **Food Requirement**: Must be taken with food (at least 350 to 1000 calories). Food increases Cmax and AUC by approximately 3-fold and 2-fold, respectively [Australian].
- **Missed Dose**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Switching Antipsychotics**: Supervision is required due to varying pharmacodynamic and pharmacokinetic profiles between agents [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to lurasidone hydrochloride or any formulation excipients.
- Concomitant use with **strong CYP3A4 inhibitors** (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir, voriconazole).
- Concomitant use with **strong CYP3A4 inducers** (e.g., rifampin, St. John’s wort, phenytoin, carbamazepine).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Elderly Patients with Dementia-Related Psychosis**: Increased risk of fatality and cerebrovascular adverse events (e.g., stroke). Lurasidone is NOT approved for this population [Australian].
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)**: Potentially fatal; requires immediate discontinuation of all antipsychotic therapy.
- **Tardive Dyskinesia**: Risk of potentially irreversible, involuntary movements. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary.
- **Metabolic Changes**:
    - **Hyperglycemia/Diabetes**: Cases of extreme hyperglycemia (sometimes fatal) reported with atypical antipsychotics. Monitor glucose in at-risk patients.
    - **Dyslipidemia**: Monitor lipid profiles.
    - **Weight Gain**: Clinical monitoring of weight is recommended.
- **Hyperprolactinemia**: Lurasidone elevates prolactin levels, which may suppress reproductive function and decrease bone density if chronic.
- **Orthostatic Hypotension and Syncope**: Use with caution in patients with known cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease.
- **Seizure Threshold**: Use cautiously in patients with a history of seizures or conditions that lower the threshold.
- **Suicide Risk**: Close supervision of high-risk patients is essential.
- **Leukopenia, Neutropenia, and Agranulocytosis**: Monitor CBC in patients with pre-existing low WBC or history of drug-induced leukopenia. Discontinue if absolute neutrophil count <1000/mm³.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Category: Adults
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Akathisia, somnolence, extrapyramidal disorder, nausea. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vomiting, dyspepsia, insomnia, anxiety, agitation, dizziness, restlessness, tachycardia, vision blurred, abdominal pain, rash, pruritus, hypertension, increased creatinine phosphokinase. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anaemia, angina pectoris, bradycardia, vertigo, dysphagia, gastritis, hyponatremia, dysarthria, syncope, tardive dyskinesia, amenorrhea, erectile dysfunction. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Leukopenia, neutropenia, AV block 1st degree, sudden death, rhabdomyolysis, NMS, seizures, renal failure, angioedema. |

### Adolescent Nuances
- Common events in adolescents (incidence ≥5%) include somnolence, nausea, akathisia, vomiting, and dizziness [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors**:
    - **Strong**: Contraindicated.
    - **Moderate (e.g., diltiazem)**: Reduce lurasidone dose to half of the original dose. If starting lurasidone while on a moderate inhibitor, the recommended start is 20 mg/day (max 80 mg/day) [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers**:
    - **Strong**: Contraindicated.
- **Grapefruit/Grapefruit Juice**: Avoid, as it inhibits CYP3A4 and increases lurasidone levels.
- **CNS Actives/Alcohol**: Use with caution due to additive central effects.
- **Digoxin and Midazolam**: Lurasidone slightly increases levels of these substrates; however, no dose adjustment of digoxin or midazolam is typically required [Australian].
- **Lithium**: No dose adjustment required for either drug when co-administered.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Australian Category**: B1. 
    - **Clinical Note**: Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus. Neonates exposed in the third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms.
- **Breastfeeding**: Excreted in rat milk; human data is absent. Breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility**: Animal studies showed prolonged oestrus and delayed mating in females, but no effects on male fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety/efficacy established for ages 13–17 in schizophrenia. Not recommended for children under 13 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No specific dose adjustment for age alone, but caution is advised due to higher prevalence of renal/cardiovascular comorbidities.
- **Renal Impairment**:
    - **Mild (CrCl 50–80 mL/min)**: No adjustment.
    - **Moderate to Severe (CrCl <50 mL/min)**: Starting dose 20 mg; maximum dose 80 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**:
    - **Mild**: No adjustment.
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B)**: Starting dose 20 mg; maximum dose 80 mg once daily.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C)**: Not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Antagonist at Dopamine D2 and 5-HT2A receptors. It also acts as an antagonist at 5-HT7, α2C-adrenergic, and a partial agonist at 5-HT1A. It has no appreciable affinity for H1 or M1 receptors.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: 9–19% absorbed; Tmax in 1–3 hours.
    - **Distribution**: 99% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism**: Primary metabolism via CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination**: Mean half-life is 18 hours. Total excretion is 89% (80% in faeces, 9% in urine).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Schizophrenia**: Efficacy established in five 6-week, placebo-controlled trials. Instruments used included PANSS and BPRSd. Doses of 40 mg, 80 mg, 120 mg, and 160 mg all demonstrated superiority to placebo. 
- **Maintenance**: A 12-month study vs. quetiapine XR showed lurasidone was associated with a lower probability of relapse (23.7% vs. 33.6%) [Australian].
- **Adolescent Schizophrenia**: A 6-week study in patients 13–17 years showed significant improvement in PANSS total score at doses of 40 mg and 80 mg compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Shelf life is specified on the packaging and the ARTG public summary.
