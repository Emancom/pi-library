# Editorial Product Information: Loxapine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidance for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Loxapine (present as loxapine succinate).
- **Chemical Class:** Dibenzoxazepine compound (a subclass of tricyclic antipsychotics).
- **Brand Names:** Loxapine Capsules, USP [United States].
- **ATC Code:** N05AH01 (Loxapine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Oral Capsules: Equivalent to 5 mg, 10 mg, 25 mg, and 50 mg of loxapine base [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** RemedyRepack Inc. (Repackager); Lannett Company, Inc. (Original Manufacturer) [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia:** Indicated for the treatment of schizophrenia. Efficacy has been established in both newly hospitalised and chronically hospitalised patients with acute illness [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Administered in divided doses, typically two to four times daily. Dosage must be individualised based on symptom severity and previous response to antipsychotic therapy [United States].
- **Initial Dosage:** 10 mg twice daily is the standard starting dose. In severely disturbed patients, an initial total daily dose of up to 50 mg may be appropriate [United States].
- **Titration:** Dosage should be increased fairly rapidly over the first 7 to 10 days until symptoms are effectively controlled [United States].
- **Maintenance Range:** The usual therapeutic range is 60 mg to 100 mg daily.
- **Minimum Effective Dose:** For long-term therapy, dosage should be reduced to the lowest level compatible with symptom control; many patients are maintained on 20 mg to 60 mg daily [United States].
- **Maximum Dose:** Daily dosages exceeding 250 mg are not recommended [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **CNS Depression:** Contraindicated in comatose states or severe drug-induced depressed states (e.g., from alcohol, barbiturates, or narcotics) [United States].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to dibenzoxazepines [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Increased Mortality in Elderly Patients with Dementia-Related Psychosis:** Elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis treated with antipsychotic drugs are at an increased risk of death. Loxapine is not approved for this population [United States].
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** A potentially fatal complex characterized by hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, and autonomic instability. Immediate discontinuation and intensive monitoring are required [United States].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia (TD):** Potentially irreversible, involuntary movements may develop. Risk increases with duration of treatment and total cumulative dose. Discontinuation should be considered if signs appear [United States].
- **Seizure Threshold:** Loxapine lowers the convulsive threshold; use with extreme caution in patients with a history of convulsive disorders [United States].
- **Falls:** May cause somnolence, postural hypotension, and motor instability, leading to falls and fractures. Fall risk assessments are recommended [United States].
- **Hematologic Effects:** Events of leukopenia, neutropenia, and agranulocytosis have been reported. Patients with a history of low white blood cell (WBC) counts should be monitored frequently; treatment should be discontinued if WBC declines in the absence of other factors [United States].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use with caution in patients with cardiovascular disease. Increased pulse rates and transient hypotension are common [United States].
- **Anticholinergic Action:** Use cautiously in patients with glaucoma or urinary retention [United States].
- **Ocular Toxicity:** Pigmentary retinopathy and lenticular pigmentation have been observed with other antipsychotics; the possibility of such toxicity with loxapine cannot be excluded [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Lorazepam:** Rare reports of significant respiratory depression, stupor, and hypotension when used concomitantly [United States].
- **CNS-Active Drugs:** Caution is advised when combining loxapine with other CNS-active agents due to the potential for additive effects; this combination has not been systematically evaluated [United States].
- **Epinephrine:** Should **not** be used to treat loxapine-induced hypotension, as loxapine may inhibit its vasopressor effect, potentially further lowering blood pressure [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned in provided source; however, loxapine should only be used if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [United States].
    - **Neonatal Risk:** Neonates exposed to antipsychotics during the third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms (e.g., agitation, respiratory distress, feeding disorders) [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** The extent of excretion into human milk is unknown, though it occurs in animals. Administration to nursing women should be avoided if clinically possible [United States].
- **Fertility:** Rodent studies showed an increase in mammary neoplasms after chronic administration, though human epidemiological results are inconsistent [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of mortality in those with dementia-related psychosis. The risk of tardive dyskinesia is highest among the elderly, especially women [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Renal failure has been reported following overdosage [United States]. Specific dose adjustments for chronic renal impairment are not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Hepatocellular injury has been reported in association with treatment; monitoring is advised [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The exact mode of action is not established. It is observed to change the excitability of subcortical inhibitory areas, resulting in tranquilization and suppression of aggressive behavior [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Sedation is typically seen within 20 to 30 minutes of administration, peaking between 1.5 and 3 hours, and lasting up to 12 hours [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Virtually complete following oral administration.
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly removed from plasma and distributed to tissues (lungs, brain, spleen, heart, kidney).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized.
    - **Excretion:** Mainly excreted within the first 24 hours. Metabolites are excreted in the urine as conjugates and in the feces as unconjugated forms [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Efficacy was established in clinical studies involving both newly and chronically hospitalized acutely ill patients with schizophrenia. Specific trial identifiers or primary outcome data were not detailed in the provided source [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [United States].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, child-resistant container [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only).
