# Editorial Product Information: Lenvatinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lenvatinib clinical data for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI. Clinicians should refer to the local approved labelling for prescribing in their specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lenvatinib (as mesilate), a multiple receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **ARX-LENVATINIB** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Hard capsules: 4 mg and 10 mg strengths [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Eisai Australia Pty Ltd (Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Lenvatinib is indicated for the following malignancies:
- **Differentiated Thyroid Cancer (DTC):** Progressive, locally advanced, or metastatic, radioactive iodine (RAI) refractory DTC [Australian].
- **Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC):** 
    - First-line treatment of advanced RCC in combination with pembrolizumab [Australian].
    - Treatment of advanced RCC in combination with everolimus following one prior vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) targeted therapy [Australian].
- **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC):** First-line treatment of unresectable HCC [Australian].
- **Endometrial Carcinoma (EC):** In combination with pembrolizumab for advanced EC that is not microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) or mismatch repair deficient (dMMR), in patients who have progressed after prior systemic therapy and are not candidates for surgery or radiation [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment should be supervised by a clinician experienced in anticancer therapies. Capsules should be taken at the same time daily, with or without food.

### Dosing by Indication
- **RAI-refractory DTC:** 24 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Advanced RCC (First-line):** 20 mg once daily with pembrolizumab (200 mg every 3 weeks or 400 mg every 6 weeks) [Australian].
- **Advanced RCC (Previously treated):** 18 mg once daily with 5 mg everolimus once daily [Australian].
- **Unresectable HCC:** Weight-based dosing [Australian]:
    - $\geq$ 60 kg: 12 mg once daily.
    - < 60 kg: 8 mg once daily.
- **Endometrial Carcinoma:** 20 mg once daily in combination with pembrolizumab [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules should be swallowed whole.
- **Suspension Preparation:** Capsules can be dispersed in water, apple juice, or milk without breaking or crushing. Place capsules in approximately 20 mL of liquid, wait 10 minutes for the shell to disintegrate, then stir for 3 minutes. Suspensions can be administered orally or via feeding tube (PVC, PUR, or silicone) [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
If a dose is missed and cannot be taken within 12 hours, the dose should be skipped and the next dose taken at the scheduled time [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to lenvatinib or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Additional Monitoring:** This product is subject to additional monitoring in Australia (Black Triangle Scheme) to facilitate rapid identification of new safety information [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** High incidence; BP must be controlled prior to treatment and monitored regularly (weekly for month 1, then every 2 weeks for month 2, then monthly). Serious complications like aortic dissection have been reported [Australian].
- **Proteinuria:** Monitor urine protein regularly; discontinue if nephrotic syndrome occurs [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Liver function tests (LFTs) required before initiation and periodically. Higher frequency of hepatic failure in HCC patients [Australian].
- **Renal Failure/Impairment:** Primary risk factors include dehydration/hypovolemia from GI toxicity. Close monitoring is required for patients with baseline renal function < 60 mL/min [Australian].
- **Cardiac Dysfunction:** Serious events including cardiac failure and decreased ejection fraction reported; monitor for signs of decompensation [Australian].
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES/RPLS):** Reported in < 1% of patients. Confirm via MRI [Australian].
- **Vascular Events:** Arterial thromboembolisms (MI, CVA) have occurred. Discontinue following an arterial thrombotic event [Australian].
- **Fistula and Perforation:** Increased risk of GI and non-GI fistulae (e.g., tracheal, oesophageal). Discontinue in patients with Grade 4 fistulae or tracheobronchial involvement [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** Monitor ECG and electrolytes (potassium, magnesium, calcium) [Australian].
- **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ):** Dental examination recommended prior to treatment; avoid invasive dental procedures if possible [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data
- **Very Common (>10%):** Hypertension, fatigue, diarrhoea, arthralgia/myalgia, decreased appetite, weight loss, nausea, stomatitis, headache, vomiting, proteinuria, palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia (PPE) syndrome, abdominal pain, dysphonia, and hypothyroidism [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Pneumonia, hypertension, dehydration, arterial thromboembolism, hepatic failure, GI perforation, and cardiac failure [Australian].

### Dose Modifications
Adverse reactions frequently require dose interruption or reduction. For DTC, the first reduction is to 20 mg; for RCC and EC, the first reduction is to 14 mg [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Effect of Other Drugs:** Lenvatinib may be co-administered with CYP3A4, P-gp, and BCRP inhibitors or inducers without dose adjustment. Gastric pH-altering agents (PPIs, H2 blockers) do not significantly impact exposure [Australian].
- **Effect of Lenvatinib:** Lenvatinib inhibits MATE1 but has minimal/no effect on P-gp, BCRP, OATs, OCTs, or OATPs [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic:** Caution when used with agents known to cause ONJ (e.g., bisphosphonates) or those acting on the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Lenvatinib is embryotoxic and teratogenic in animals. It should not be used during pregnancy unless clearly necessary.
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use highly effective contraception during and for at least one month after treatment. Hormonal contraceptives may require an added barrier method [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established; risk to newborns cannot be excluded. Lenvatinib should not be used while breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show testicular and ovarian toxicity; human effects are unknown [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Safety and efficacy in children aged 2 to <18 years not established. Not for use in children < 2 years due to safety concerns in animal models (growth/developmental lesions) [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Use with caution in patients $\geq$ 75 years due to reduced tolerability [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Severe (CrCL < 30 mL/min): Adjust starting dose to 14 mg (DTC) or 10 mg (RCC/EC) [Australian].
    - Not recommended for use in HCC with severe renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Adjust starting dose to 14 mg (DTC) or 10 mg (RCC/EC) [Australian].
    - Not recommended for use in HCC with Child-Pugh B or C [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits VEGF receptors (VEGFR1, 2, 3) and FGF receptors (FGFR1, 2, 3, 4). Also inhibits PDGFR$\alpha$, KIT, and RET [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; $T_{max}$ 1–4 hours. Food delays $T_{max}$ by 2 hours but does not affect extent of absorption [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 98–99% protein bound (mainly albumin) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised, primarily via CYP3A4 (demethylation) and aldehyde oxidase [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life approximately 28 hours. Excretion is roughly 2/3 faecal and 1/4 urinary [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SELECT (DTC):** Showed significant PFS improvement (18.3 months vs 3.6 months for placebo) [Australian].
- **CLEAR (RCC):** Lenvatinib + pembrolizumab significantly improved PFS (23.9 months) vs sunitinib (9.2 months) [Australian].
- **REFLECT (HCC):** Demonstrated non-inferiority in OS compared to sorafenib [Australian].
- **Study 309 (EC):** Lenvatinib + pembrolizumab improved OS (17.4 months) compared to investigator's choice chemotherapy (12.0 months) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Blisters:** Provided in polyamide/aluminium/PVC/aluminium blisters [Australian].
- **Suspension Stability:** If not used immediately, the suspension may be refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 24 hours [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
