# Editorial Product Information: Labetalol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority:
    - **Australian (TGA)**
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their specific region before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Labetalol hydrochloride (a dual alpha-1 and non-selective beta-adrenoceptor antagonist).
- **Brand Names:** Labetalol SXP [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C07AG01 [Not specifically cited in provided source; confirmed by standard pharmaceutical reference].
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Solution for injection:** 50 mg/10 mL (5 mg/mL) clear, colourless sterile solution in glass ampoules [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer:** Southern XP IP Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Hypertension:** For emergency treatment when a prompt and urgent reduction of blood pressure is essential [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
- Intended for IV use in hospitalised patients only.
- Patients must remain **supine** during administration and for up to three hours afterward to avoid excessive postural hypotension [Australian].
- Dosage must be individualised based on the severity of hypertension and patient response.

### Intravenous Bolus Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial dose:** 20 mg by slow IV injection over approximately 2 minutes.
- **Subsequent doses:** If response is inadequate, 40 mg may be given 10–20 minutes after the initial dose. 
- **Titration:** Further doses of 80 mg may be given at 10–20 minute intervals (up to three 80 mg doses).
- **Maximum dose:** 300 mg per 24-hour period [Australian].

### Intravenous Infusion (Adult)
- **Preparation:** Dilute to a 1 mg/mL concentration using 5% Dextrose or Sodium Chloride/Dextrose Injection [Australian].
- **Hypertension in Pregnancy:** Commence at 20 mg/hr. Increase by 20 mg/hr every 20 minutes as needed. Maximum rate: 160 mg/hr [Australian].
- **Other Severe Hypertension:** Infuse at approximately 2 mg/min until a satisfactory response is obtained, then discontinue. The typical effective range is 50–200 mg. 
- **Phaeochromocytoma:** Higher doses may be required [Australian].
- **Maximum dose:** 300 mg per 24-hour period [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- History of asthma or obstructive pulmonary disease (risk of bronchospasm).
- Bradycardia (<45–50 bpm).
- Sick sinus syndrome (including sino-atrial block).
- Second or third-degree heart block.
- Cardiogenic shock.
- Hypersensitivity to labetalol.
- Symptomatic or uncontrolled heart failure.
- Severe peripheral circulatory disturbances.
- Metabolic acidosis.
- Untreated phaeochromocytoma.
- Prinzmetal's angina.
- Hypotension.
- Right ventricular failure secondary to pulmonary hypertension or significant right ventricular hypertrophy.
- Use following acute myocardial infarction where peripheral vasoconstriction suggests low cardiac output [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Do not discontinue abruptly, particularly in patients with ischaemic heart disease. Taper over 1–2 weeks to prevent exacerbation of angina or arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Hepatic Injury:** Rare but severe hepatocellular injury has been reported. Monitor liver function at the first sign of dysfunction; discontinue immediately if liver injury or jaundice occurs [Australian].
- **Postural Hypotension:** A substantial drop in blood pressure on standing is expected; establish tolerance for upright position before ambulation [Australian].
- **Intraoperative Floppy Iris Syndrome (IFIS):** Observed during cataract surgery in patients currently or previously treated with alpha-1 blockers (including labetalol) [Australian].
- **Anesthesia:** Labetalol may mask compensatory responses to haemorrhage (tachycardia). Inform the anaesthetist if the patient is receiving labetalol [Australian].
- **Airway Disease:** While contraindicated in asthma, if bronchospasm occurs, treat with a beta-2 agonist (e.g., salbutamol) and potentially IV atropine [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most effects are transient and occur early in treatment.
- **Common (1–10%):** Dizziness, headache, nausea, tiredness, and tingling of the scalp [Australian].
- **Cardiac/Vascular:** Postural hypotension (very common if moved prematurely), bradycardia, heart block, palpitations, and exacerbation of intermittent claudication [Australian].
- **Respiratory:** Nasal congestion; bronchospasm in susceptible patients [Australian].
- **Hepato-biliary:** Raised LFTs, jaundice, hepatitis, and hepatic necrosis (rare) [Australian].
- **Dermatologic:** Sweating, reversible lichenoid rash, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) exacerbation, and reversible alopecia [Australian].
- **Reproductive/Breast:** Ejaculatory failure, nipple pain, and Raynaud's phenomenon of the nipple [Australian].
- **Laboratory:** Rare reports of positive antinuclear antibodies (ANA) and hyperkalaemia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Calcium Antagonists:** Verapamil and diltiazem may cause severe hypotension, bradycardia, and asystole. Use with extreme caution [Australian].
- **Class I Antiarrhythmics & Amiodarone:** Risk of potentiated effects on conduction and negative inotropy [Australian].
- **Clonidine:** Increased risk of rebound hypertension; discontinue labetalol several days before tapering clonidine [Australian].
- **MAOIs:** Concomitant use is not recommended (except MAO-B inhibitors) [Australian].
- **Bioavailability Enhancers:** Cimetidine, hydralazine, and alcohol may increase labetalol levels [Australian].
- **NSAIDs & Corticosteroids:** May antagonise the hypotensive effects of labetalol [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** May cause falsely elevated urinary catecholamines, metanephrine, and VMA when measured by fluorimetric methods. May produce false-positive urine screens for amphetamines [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Labetalol crosses the placental barrier. While used for hypertension in the 2nd/3rd trimester, it may cause foetal/neonatal bradycardia, hypotension, and respiratory depression. Use in the 1st trimester is not recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in breast milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Use with caution; start at the lower end of the dosing range. Elimination may be reduced, and the blood pressure response may be more pronounced [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** AUC is increased by approximately 25%. Monitor closely [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific dose adjustment is generally required, but monitor for hyperkalaemia, especially in transplant patients [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Labetalol competitively inhibits alpha-1 adrenoceptors (reducing peripheral resistance) and non-selective beta-adrenoceptors (protecting the heart from reflex tachycardia). 
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The beta:alpha antagonism ratio is approximately 6.9:1 following IV administration [Australian]. It possesses some membrane-stabilising activity but no intrinsic sympathomimetic activity (ISA).
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Extensive extravascular distribution; ~50% protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via conjugation to inactive glucuronide metabolites.
    - **Excretion:** Metabolites are excreted in urine and bile. Plasma half-life is 3.5–6.3 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Severe Hypertension of Pregnancy:** Randomised controlled trials have shown IV labetalol (initial 20 mg bolus) to be effective compared to IV hydralazine, with approximately 83% of patients achieving target BP with 1 or 2 doses [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in the original package to protect from light.
- **Diluted solution:** Use as soon as possible. If necessary, store at 2°–8°C for up to 24 hours, or 6 hours at room temperature [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** S4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
