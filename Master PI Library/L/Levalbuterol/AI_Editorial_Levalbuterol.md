# Editorial Product Information: Levalbuterol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**. 
- At the time of this writing, PI documents from other major jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not available for harmonization. Consequently, the clinical recommendations and regulatory statuses presented herein primarily reflect United States (US) labeling.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a synthesis for clinical reference and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levalbuterol hydrochloride (the (R)-enantiomer of racemic albuterol).
- **Chemical Class:** Beta₂-adrenergic receptor agonist.
- **Brand Names:** Levalbuterol Inhalation Solution, USP (Generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** R03AC18.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Inhalation solution (unit-dose vials for nebulization): 0.31 mg/3 mL, 0.63 mg/3 mL, and 1.25 mg/3 mL [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral inhalation via nebulization.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ritedose Pharmaceuticals, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reversible Obstructive Airway Disease:** Indicated for the treatment or prevention of bronchospasm in adults, adolescents, and children 6 years of age and older with reversible obstructive airway disease [US].
- **Note:** Safety and efficacy in children under 6 years of age have not been established; clinical trials in this age group failed to meet primary efficacy endpoints and showed an increased frequency of asthma-related adverse events [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Children (6–11 years):** 0.31 mg administered three times daily by nebulization. The routine dose should not exceed 0.63 mg three times daily [US].
- **Adults and Adolescents (≥12 years):** 0.63 mg administered three times daily (every 6 to 8 hours) by nebulization. 
- **Maximum Dose:** In patients with more severe asthma or those with an inadequate response to 0.63 mg, a dose of 1.25 mg three times daily may be used [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer only via a standard jet nebulizer connected to an air compressor [US].
- Do not use the solution if it is not colorless.
- Patients using the 1.25 mg dose should be monitored closely for systemic beta-adrenergic effects.

### Missed Dose
- Not explicitly specified in available sources; however, standard clinical practice suggests taking the missed dose as soon as remembered, unless it is nearly time for the next scheduled dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of hypersensitivity to levalbuterol, racemic albuterol, or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Paradoxical Bronchospasm:** Like other inhaled beta-agonists, levalbuterol can produce life-threatening paradoxical bronchospasm. This typically occurs with the first use of a new vial. If this occurs, discontinue levalbuterol immediately and institute alternative therapy [US].
- **Deterioration of Asthma:** Increased requirements for levalbuterol may signify a destabilization of asthma, necessitating an urgent re-evaluation of the patient's treatment regimen and consideration of anti-inflammatory therapy (e.g., corticosteroids) [US].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use with caution in patients with underlying cardiovascular disorders (coronary insufficiency, hypertension, cardiac arrhythmias). Significant changes in heart rate, blood pressure, and ECG (T-wave flattening, QTc prolongation, ST-segment depression) may occur [US].
- **Excessive Use:** Fatalities have been reported in association with excessive use of inhaled sympathomimetic drugs [US].
- **Immediate Hypersensitivity:** Reactions such as urticaria, angioedema, rash, and anaphylaxis may occur [US].
- **Coexisting Conditions:** Use with caution in patients with convulsive disorders, hyperthyroidism, or diabetes mellitus, and in those unusually responsive to sympathomimetic amines [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Short-Acting Bronchodilators:** Avoid concomitant use of other short-acting sympathomimetic aerosol bronchodilators to prevent deleterious cardiovascular effects [US].
- **Beta-blockers:** May antagonize the therapeutic effect of levalbuterol and produce severe bronchospasm in asthmatic patients. If required, use cardioselective beta-blockers with extreme caution [US].
- **Diuretics:** Potential for ECG changes and/or hypokalemia may be acutely worsened by non-potassium-sparing diuretics (e.g., loop or thiazide diuretics) [US].
- **Digoxin:** Levalbuterol (as racemic albuterol) may decrease serum digoxin levels by up to 22%. Monitor digoxin levels in patients receiving both medications [US].
- **MAOIs and Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs):** Use with extreme caution (or within 2 weeks of discontinuation) as they may potentiate the effect of levalbuterol on the vascular system [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified under current PLLR labeling, but clinical data are limited.
    - **Risk Summary:** There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies showed no teratogenicity in rabbits at 108 times the maximum recommended human daily inhalation dose (MRHDID). However, racemic albuterol has shown teratogenicity in mice and rabbits at doses near the therapeutic range.
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Poorly controlled asthma in pregnancy increases the risk of preeclampsia, prematurity, and low birth weight. Use during labor should be restricted to patients where benefits clearly outweigh the risk of interfering with uterine contractility [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of levalbuterol in human milk or its effects on the breastfed child. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be considered along with the mother's clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** No fertility studies have been conducted with levalbuterol hydrochloride; studies with racemic albuterol in rats showed no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Approved:** Children 6 years and older [US].
    - **Not Recommended:** Children under 6 years due to lack of efficacy and safety concerns (increased asthma-related events in trials) [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Only a small number of patients ≥65 years were studied. Treatment should generally be initiated at the lower end of the dosing range (0.63 mg) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Albuterol is substantially excreted by the kidney. Caution is advised when administering high doses to patients with renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not evaluated in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levalbuterol is a relatively selective beta₂-adrenergic receptor agonist. Activation of these receptors on airway smooth muscle leads to the activation of adenylate cyclase and an increase in intracellular cyclic AMP. This activates protein kinase A, inhibiting myosin phosphorylation and lowering intracellular calcium, resulting in smooth muscle relaxation across all airways [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Enantioselectivity:** Levalbuterol is the (R)-enantiomer of albuterol. The (R)-enantiomer has approximately 100-fold greater affinity for beta₂-receptors than the (S)-enantiomer.
- **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by SULT1A3 (sulfotransferase). (R)-albuterol is preferentially metabolized in the gastrointestinal tract.
- **Elimination:** 80% to 100% is excreted renally. Less than 20% is detected in feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adults/Adolescents (≥12 years):** A 4-week, multicenter trial (n=362) showed levalbuterol 0.63 mg and 1.25 mg provided significantly greater bronchodilation (FEV₁) than placebo. The 1.25 mg dose demonstrated the largest mean percent change from baseline [US].
- **Pediatrics (6–11 years):** A 3-week trial (n=316) demonstrated that levalbuterol 0.31 mg and 0.63 mg were effective and comparable to racemic albuterol [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light and excessive heat. Keep vials in the protective foil pouch.
- **Shelf Life After Opening:** Once the foil pouch is opened, vials must be used within 2 weeks. Vials removed from the pouch should be used within 1 week [US].

- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only).
