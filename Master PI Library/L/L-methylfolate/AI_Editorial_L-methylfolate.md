## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** L-methylfolate calcium (also known as levomefolate calcium).
- **Chemical Class:** Biologically active folate (6S-5-methyltetrahydrofolic acid).
- **Brand Names:** L-METHYLFOLATE CALCIUM (Bryant Ranch Prepack/Virtus Pharmaceuticals) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Coated tablets: 7.5 mg and 15 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured for Virtus Pharmaceuticals, LLC; Repackaged by Bryant Ranch Prepack [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** Indicated for the clinical dietary management of metabolic imbalances in patients with MDD who have suboptimal L-methylfolate levels in cerebrospinal fluid, plasma, and/or red blood cells. Primarily used as adjunctive nutritional support for patients currently on antidepressant therapy [US].
- **Schizophrenia:** Indicated for the distinct nutritional requirements of patients with schizophrenia (or at risk for hyperhomocysteinemia) presenting with cognitive impairment or negative symptoms. Used as adjunctive support for patients stabilised on antipsychotics [US].
- **Genotype Consideration:** Indicated regardless of the patient's MTHFR C677T polymorphism genotype [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard therapeutic range is 7.5 mg to 15 mg administered once daily [US].
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food [US].
- **Medical Supervision:** This product is classified as a medical food and must be used under the direction and supervision of a healthcare provider [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to L-methylfolate calcium or any excipient (e.g., FD&C dyes, talc, carnauba wax) contained in the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **B12 Deficiency Masking:** Folate doses exceeding 0.1 mg daily may mask the haematological symptoms of Vitamin B12 deficiency (pernicious anaemia) while allowing neurological damage to progress. While L-methylfolate is potentially less likely to mask these symptoms than folic acid, folate therapy alone remains inadequate for B12 deficiency [US].
- **Bipolar Disorder Screening:** Patients presenting with depressive symptoms should be screened for bipolar disorder risk. While L-methylfolate is not an antidepressant, it may enhance antidepressant effects. In patients at risk for bipolar illness, antidepressants (and potentially their enhancers) may precipitate manic or mixed episodes [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic assessment of folate levels in plasma, red blood cells, or cerebrospinal fluid may be appropriate given the indication for "suboptimal levels" [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
L-methylfolate may interact with medications that affect folate metabolism or where folate levels influence the drug's safety/efficacy:
- **Antiepileptics (AEDs):** Phenytoin, carbamazepine, valproic acid, and lamotrigine can impair folate absorption. Conversely, concurrent folate may enhance phenytoin metabolism, potentially leading to breakthrough seizures [US].
- **Capecitabine:** Toxicity of capecitabine may be increased when used with folates [US].
- **Dihydrofolate Reductase Inhibitors (DHFIs):** Methotrexate, pyrimethamine, and trimethoprim block the conversion of folic acid to active forms; however, they also lower plasma/RBC folate levels [US].
- **Fluoxetine:** May noncompetitively inhibit active transport of 5-MTHF in the intestine [US].
- **Folate-Lowering Agents:** Reduced folate levels have been associated with the use of Metformin, Oral Contraceptives, Isotretinoin, NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen, naproxen), Methylprednisolone, Pentamidine, and Pancreatic enzymes [US].
- **Warfarin:** Folate status may be impaired following long-term (6-month) warfarin therapy [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** L-methylfolate is the primary biologically active diastereoisomer of folate and the form found in circulation. It is a necessary cofactor for the synthesis of monoamine neurotransmitters (serotonin, norepinephrine, and dopamine) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Unlike synthetic folic acid, L-methylfolate does not require conversion by the dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR) enzyme and is directly available for use in the methionine cycle [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Derived from food sources; specific ADME parameters (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion) are not detailed in the available medical food PI [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Protective Measures:** Protect from heat, light, and moisture. Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers [US].
