# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for levocarnitine, designed as a concise reference for healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This editorial is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction and should not be used as the sole basis for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from metabolic errors [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
*   **Adult and Paediatric Dosing:** 50 mg/kg as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
*   **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours.
*   **Maintenance:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
*   **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported clinical dose is 300 mg/kg.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
*   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
*   **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
*   **Adjustment:** Dosing should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels (normal range 40–50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on clinical response and levels.

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, occasionally within minutes of administration. Most cases involve patients with ESRD on dialysis. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon suspicion of a severe reaction [US].
*   **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, the frequency or severity of seizures may increase [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Clinicians should monitor plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistry, and vital signs [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (up to 35%), vomiting (up to 21%), nausea (up to 12%).
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (up to 21%), hypotension (up to 19%).
*   **General:** Injection site reaction (up to 38%), pain (up to 35%), headache (up to 37%), flu-like syndrome (up to 29%), pharyngitis (up to 27%).

### Common (1–10%)
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, abdominal pain, constipation, dyspepsia.
*   **Cardiovascular:** Chest pain, tachycardia, palpitations, arrhythmia.
*   **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, weight decrease/increase.
*   **Neurological:** Dizziness, paresthesia, insomnia, depression.
*   **Other:** Body odor (fishy smell), anemia, pruritus, fever.

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
*   Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/Current labeling]: Animal reproductive studies (rats and rabbits at 3.8 times human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if levocarnitine is excreted in human milk; however, studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is consistent with adult weight-based recommendations [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients. However, caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if transition to high-dose oral therapy is considered [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    *   **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis resulted in a significant increase in plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks of therapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

*   **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only).
