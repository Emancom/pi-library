# Editorial Product Information: levodopa + carbidopa

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **levodopa + carbidopa**
- Chemical class:
  - Levodopa: aromatic amino acid (dopamine precursor)
  - Carbidopa: aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase inhibitor (does not cross blood–brain barrier)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawals, suspensions or restrictions: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of Parkinson’s disease and Parkinsonian syndrome, for relief of symptoms including rigidity and bradykinesia.
- Also frequently helpful for management of tremor, dysphagia, sialorrhoea and postural instability associated with Parkinson’s disease and syndrome.
- Use in children (<18 years): not recommended; safety and effectiveness not established.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
General principles
- Individualize dosing by careful titration to clinical response and tolerability.
- Carbidopa is included to inhibit extracerebral decarboxylation of levodopa and thereby increase levodopa availability to the brain and reduce peripheral adverse effects.
- Peripheral dopa‑decarboxylase saturation by carbidopa is reported at approximately 70–100 mg/day; provision of at least this amount reduces peripheral adverse effects (e.g., nausea/vomiting).

Typical adult initiation and titration
- Usual initial regimen: start with the product strength that delivers an effective levodopa:carbidopa ratio appropriate for the patient and titrate.
- An example starting regimen (as described in available sources): levodopa/carbidopa 100/25 (dose unit notation used in sources) one tablet three times daily (this provides approximately 75 mg carbidopa/day). Increase by one tablet daily or every other day as needed.
- Dose increases should be gradual and guided by symptom control and adverse effects; full effect often achieved within about 7 days after initiating carbidopa/levodopa (responses have been reported within one day or after a single dose).
- For patients requiring higher levodopa doses, higher levodopa:carbidopa ratio preparations may be used and titrated similarly.

Switching from levodopa alone
- Levodopa should be discontinued at least 12 hours before initiating levodopa + carbidopa (24 hours if switching from slow‑release levodopa).
- When switching, select a total daily levodopa + carbidopa dose that provides approximately 20% of the prior levodopa daily dose; suggested starting schedules in sources:
  - Patients previously taking <1500 mg levodopa/day: start one 100/25 unit dose three or four times daily.
  - Patients previously taking >1500 mg levodopa/day: suggested starting dose one 250/25 unit dose three or four times daily.
- Monitor closely during conversion as therapeutic and adverse responses may occur more rapidly when carbidopa is added (involuntary movements/dyskinesia may emerge and require dose reduction).

Maintenance and maximum doses
- Maintenance: individualise and adjust to desired effect; ensure at least ~70–100 mg carbidopa/day for optimal extracerebral decarboxylase inhibition.
- Maximum recommended daily dose (per available sources): up to eight tablets of levodopa/carbidopa 250/25 per day (equivalent to 200 mg carbidopa and 2000 mg levodopa daily). Experience with total daily carbidopa doses >200 mg is limited.
- Expressed per body weight example from sources: for a 70 kg patient, this maximum equals approximately 3 mg/kg carbidopa and 30 mg/kg levodopa.

Administration instructions
- Take by mouth. Specific product formulation handling (tablet appearance, film coating, etc.) not specified in this editorial.
- Levodopa absorption may be impaired by high‑protein meals because levodopa competes with dietary amino acids; consider timing of dosing relative to protein intake if clinically relevant.
- If temporary interruption (e.g., perioperative): therapy may be continued if patient can take oral medications; otherwise resume usual daily dose when oral intake permits.

Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to levodopa, carbidopa or any component of the product.
- Narrow angle glaucoma.
- Concomitant therapy with non‑selective monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors: non‑selective MAO inhibitors must be discontinued at least 2 weeks prior to initiating levodopa + carbidopa.
- Pre‑existing suspicious undiagnosed skin lesions or a personal history of malignant melanoma: levodopa may activate melanoma; avoid use in such patients.

Region‑specific note
- Concomitant use with an MAO inhibitor selective for MAO‑B (e.g., selegiline) has been described as permissible at the manufacturer's recommended dose in some sources; however, concomitant use with selegiline has also been associated with reports of severe orthostatic hypotension. Exercise caution if co‑administering MAO‑B inhibitors. [Australian]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
Boxed/major warnings
- **Risk of activating malignant melanoma**: patients with Parkinson’s disease have a higher baseline risk of melanoma; monitor skin regularly and evaluate suspicious lesions. Periodic dermatologic examination by an appropriate clinician is advised.
- **Somnolence and sudden sleep onset**: somnolence and rare episodes of sudden sleep onset during daily activities have been reported; patients should be warned about driving and operating machinery and should refrain from such activities if they experience somnolence or sudden sleep onset.
- **Impulse control disorders / compulsive behaviour**: dopaminergic treatment may be associated with impulse control disorders (pathological gambling, hypersexuality, compulsive spending, binge/compulsive eating, punding, compulsive medication use). Monitor patients regularly and review therapy if such behaviours occur.

General precautions
- Dyskinesias may emerge or worsen when carbidopa is added to levodopa therapy because more levodopa reaches the brain; dose reduction may be required.
- Monitor for mental changes, psychosis, depression with suicidal ideation, and other behavioural disturbances; use psychoactive drugs cautiously with close observation for loss of antiparkinsonian effect.
- Use with caution in patients with a history of seizures/convulsions.
- Cardiovascular: exercise caution in patients with atrial, nodal or ventricular arrhythmias; monitor cardiac function during initial dose adjustment. Symptomatic postural hypotension has been reported; use cautiously in patients on antihypertensives, and adjust antihypertensive therapy as needed when initiating levodopa + carbidopa.
- Abrupt withdrawal risk: a neuroleptic malignant syndrome–like syndrome (muscular rigidity, hyperthermia, mental changes, elevated CPK) has been reported with abrupt reduction or discontinuation of antiparkinsonian agents; observe patients carefully when the drug is reduced or stopped, especially with concomitant neuroleptics.
- Use cautiously in patients with severe cardiovascular, pulmonary disease, bronchial asthma, renal, hepatic or endocrine disease.
- Gastrointestinal: possible upper GI haemorrhage in patients with peptic ulcer disease.
- Periodic monitoring: hepatic, haematopoietic, cardiovascular and renal function evaluations are recommended during extended therapy.
- Laboratory test interactions: levodopa/carbidopa may cause false positives for urinary ketone tests (test tapes); may produce false-negative glucose tests with glucose‑oxidase methods.

Monitoring requirements
- Regular skin examinations for melanoma.
- Periodic liver and renal function tests, complete blood counts (monitor for haematologic abnormalities), and cardiovascular evaluation as clinically indicated.
- Monitor for impulse control disorders and new or worsening psychiatric symptoms.
- Monitor for somnolence/sudden sleep onset and counsel patients accordingly.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions and management notes available from the sources:

Interactions requiring caution or monitoring
- Antihypertensive agents: additive risk of symptomatic postural hypotension; monitor blood pressure and adjust antihypertensive dosing when initiating levodopa + carbidopa.
- Tricyclic antidepressants: rare reports of hypertension and dyskinesia when co‑administered.
- Non‑selective MAO inhibitors: contraindicated; discontinue MAO inhibitors at least 2 weeks prior to starting levodopa + carbidopa.
- MAO‑B inhibitors (e.g., selegiline): some sources state concomitant use at recommended MAO‑B doses may be acceptable, but severe orthostatic hypotension has been reported with combined therapy; use caution and monitor [Australian].
- Dopamine D2 receptor antagonists (e.g., phenothiazines, butyrophenones, risperidone): may reduce therapeutic effects of levodopa → observe for loss of efficacy.
- Isoniazid: may reduce levodopa effect.
- Dopamine‑depleting agents (e.g., reserpine, tetrabenazine): concomitant use not recommended.
- Phenytoin and papaverine: reported to reverse beneficial effects of levodopa in some reports → observe carefully if co‑administered.
- Iron (ferrous sulfate or ferrous gluconate): decreases bioavailability of levodopa and/or carbidopa when ingested concomitantly; avoid co‑administration or separate dosing times.
- Pyridoxine (vitamin B6): pyridoxine in oral doses of 10–25 mg has been reported to reverse antiparkinsonian effects of levodopa alone; carbidopa prevents this reversal. In studies, high doses (100–500 mg/day) of pyridoxine did not reverse therapeutic effect when carbidopa was co‑administered.

Clinical management
- Review concomitant medications that may antagonise levodopa action (dopamine antagonists, dopamine‑depleting agents) and adjust therapy as clinically indicated.
- Counsel patients to avoid taking iron supplements at the same time as levodopa + carbidopa; separate dosing when possible.
- Monitor blood pressure when initiating or changing doses of levodopa + carbidopa in patients on antihypertensives or MAO inhibitors.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
Pregnancy
- Pregnancy classification (Australian): Category B3.
- Animal data: levodopa produced visceral and skeletal malformations in rabbits at certain doses; levodopa ± carbidopa combinations in some animal studies produced malformations in rabbits similar to levodopa alone. Carbidopa alone showed no teratogenicity in mice and rabbits at tested doses. Data are insufficient to determine human teratogenic risk.
- Recommendation: use in women of childbearing potential requires weighing anticipated benefit against potential fetal risk.

Breastfeeding
- Limited data: levodopa has been reported in human breast milk in at least one nursing mother. Excretion of carbidopa in human milk not clearly established.
- Recommendation: because many drugs are excreted in human milk and potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant exists, levodopa + carbidopa should not be used by nursing mothers; either discontinue the drug or discontinue nursing.

Fertility
- No data available in sources regarding effects on fertility.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
Paediatric use
- Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years have not been established; use in patients <18 years not recommended.

Elderly
- Extensive clinical experience exists in elderly patients; follow general dosing and titration recommendations and monitor for age‑related adverse effects (orthostatic hypotension, confusion, somnolence). No specific dose adjustment algorithm provided in available sources.

Renal impairment
- Use with caution in patients with renal disease; periodic evaluation of renal function recommended during extended therapy. Specific dose adjustments by eGFR or stage not specified.

Hepatic impairment
- Use with caution in patients with hepatic disease; periodic evaluation of hepatic function recommended during extended therapy. Specific dose adjustments by severity not specified.

## 11. Pharmacology
Mechanism of action
- **Levodopa** is the metabolic precursor of dopamine; symptomatic benefit in Parkinson’s disease is presumed due to conversion of levodopa to dopamine in the brain.
- **Carbidopa** inhibits peripheral aromatic L‑amino acid decarboxylase (does not cross the blood–brain barrier), thereby reducing extracerebral conversion of levodopa to dopamine, increasing central availability of levodopa and reducing peripheral dopaminergic adverse effects.

Pharmacodynamics
- Co‑administration of carbidopa with levodopa markedly increases plasma levodopa concentrations relative to levodopa alone while reducing plasma dopamine and homovanillic acid (HVA) concentrations (principal metabolites), reflecting reduced peripheral conversion.

Pharmacokinetics (as reported)
- Absorption (carbidopa): Tmax of radioactivity after oral dose reported at ~2–4 hours in healthy subjects and ~1.5–5 hours in patients with Parkinson’s disease.
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism and excretion: Partial information in source documents; full details not specified in available sources.
- Other: Pyridoxine can reverse levodopa effects when levodopa is given alone; co‑administration of carbidopa prevents this effect.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key pivotal trial summaries: Not specified in available sources. The source material did not provide trial design details or primary outcome measures applicable to this consolidated PI.

Overdose (additional practical note)
- Management of acute overdosage is similar to that for levodopa alone, except pyridoxine is not effective to reverse effects when carbidopa is co‑administered.
- Supportive care: maintain airway, administer IV fluids judiciously, monitor ECG and treat arrhythmias as needed, consider possibility of co‑ingested agents, and provide general supportive measures. The value of dialysis is unknown based on available sources.
