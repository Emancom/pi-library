# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (Food and Drug Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine product information, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory authorities for the most current and region-specific requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for this molecule).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Crisis:** Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. A loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** Recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administration should be via slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

### Dosing Adjustments
- **ESRD:** Initiation of therapy may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels below 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These can occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before attempting re-administration [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to initiating therapy for metabolic disorders. Weekly and monthly monitoring should follow, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **ESRD Monitoring:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations to guide dose adjustments [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
The following were reported in ESRD clinical trials regardless of causality [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), accidental injury (10%), asthenia (9%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), nausea (8%), vomiting (15%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase (9%), pharyngitis (23%), rhinitis (9%).

### Serious and Post-marketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea, vomiting, gastritis, and body odor have been observed [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/specified in new format; however, studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks of excess intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg) as specified in Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are then rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12) receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis three times weekly, mean trough concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While levocarnitine increases plasma concentrations, the impact on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].
