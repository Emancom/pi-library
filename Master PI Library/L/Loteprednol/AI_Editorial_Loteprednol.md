# Editorial Product Information: Loteprednol Etabonate

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial clinical overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single, coherent reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Loteprednol etabonate (a topical anti-inflammatory corticosteroid).
- **Chemical Class:** Corticosteroid; structurally related to prednisolone but lacking the number 20 position ketone group to facilitate predictable transformation to inactive metabolites.
- **Brand Names:** Loteprednol Etabonate Ophthalmic Suspension [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01BA14.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Ophthalmic suspension 0.5% (5 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharmaceutical Industries, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Steroid-Responsive Inflammatory Conditions:** Treatment of inflammation of the palpebral and bulbar conjunctiva, cornea, and anterior segment of the globe (e.g., allergic conjunctivitis, acne rosacea, superficial punctate keratitis, herpes zoster keratitis, iritis, cyclitis) [US].
- **Infective Conjunctivitides:** Treatment of selected infective conditions where the inherent hazards of steroid use are accepted to reduce edema and inflammation [US].
- **Post-operative Inflammation:** Treatment of inflammation following ocular surgery [US].
- **Clinical Limitation:** Loteprednol etabonate is less effective than prednisolone acetate 1% for the treatment of acute anterior uveitis and should not be used when a more potent corticosteroid is required for this indication [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Steroid-Responsive Disease:** Apply one to two drops into the conjunctival sac of the affected eye(s) four times daily [US].
- **Titration:** During the initial week of treatment, the frequency may be increased up to one drop every hour if clinically necessary. Patients should be advised not to discontinue therapy prematurely [US].
- **Post-operative Inflammation:** Apply one to two drops into the conjunctival sac of the operated eye(s) four times daily, beginning 24 hours after surgery and continuing through the first two weeks of the post-operative period [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Shake the bottle vigorously before use [US].
- **Contamination Prevention:** Advise patients not to allow the dropper tip to touch any surface [US].
- **Contact Lenses:** Patients should not wear soft contact lenses during treatment as the formulation contains benzalkonium chloride [US].

### Monitoring/Review
- If signs/symptoms do not improve after two days, the patient must be re-evaluated [US].
- Initial prescriptions and renewals beyond 14 days must only be made after a slit-lamp examination with magnification and, where appropriate, fluorescein staining [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Viral Infections:** Most viral diseases of the cornea and conjunctiva, including epithelial herpes simplex keratitis (dendritic keratitis), vaccinia, and varicella [US].
- **Mycobacterial and Fungal Infections:** Mycobacterial infection of the eye and fungal diseases of ocular structures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known or suspected hypersensitivity to loteprednol etabonate, other corticosteroids, or any ingredient in the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ocular Hypertension/Glaucoma:** Prolonged use may result in glaucoma, optic nerve damage, and defects in visual acuity/fields. Use with caution in patients with pre-existing glaucoma. Intraocular pressure (IOP) must be monitored if used for 10 days or longer [US].
- **Cataract Formation:** Prolonged use is associated with posterior subcapsular cataract formation [US].
- **Delayed Healing:** Use after cataract surgery may delay healing and increase the incidence of bleb formation [US].
- **Secondary Infections:** Corticosteroids may suppress the immune response, increasing the risk of secondary ocular infections (fungal, bacterial, or viral). In conditions causing thinning of the cornea or sclera, topical steroids may lead to perforation [US]. 
- **Masking Infection:** In acute purulent conditions, steroids may mask or enhance existing infections [US].
- **Herpes Simplex:** Use in patients with a history of herpes simplex requires extreme caution as steroids may prolong or exacerbate the infection [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Ocular Adverse Effects
- **Common (5%–15%):** Abnormal vision/blurring, burning on instillation, chemosis (swelling), discharge, dry eyes, epiphora (tearing), foreign body sensation, itching, injection (redness), and photophobia [US].
- **Uncommon (<5%):** Conjunctivitis, corneal abnormalities, eyelid erythema, keratoconjunctivitis, ocular irritation/pain/discomfort, papillae, and uveitis [US].
- **Class-Related Effects:** Elevated IOP (incidence of ~2% for use ≥28 days), optic nerve damage, posterior subcapsular cataract formation, secondary infection, and globe perforation in cases of corneal/scleral thinning [US].

### Non-Ocular Adverse Effects
- **Occurring in <15% of patients:** Headache, rhinitis, and pharyngitis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Note:** While systemic absorption is limited, the potential for interactions common to other corticosteroids should be considered if used concomitantly with other systemic or ophthalmic medications.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C]. Animal studies (oral administration) have shown embryotoxicity and teratogenicity (e.g., delayed ossification, meningocele, limb flexures, umbilical hernia) at doses significantly higher than the maximum clinical dose. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if topical ophthalmic administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable quantities in human milk. Caution should be exercised when administered to a nursing woman [US].
- **Fertility:** Oral studies in rats showed no impairment of fertility at doses up to 600 times (males) and 300 times (females) the maximum clinical dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dosage adjustments or precautions are noted in the available source; however, general monitoring for IOP and cataracts remains pertinent.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; systemic exposure following topical administration is negligible.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Induces phospholipase $A_2$ inhibitory proteins (lipocortins), which control the biosynthesis of inflammatory mediators (prostaglandins and leukotrienes) by inhibiting the release of arachidonic acid [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Systemic absorption is limited. Plasma levels of the parent drug and its primary inactive metabolite ($\Delta^1$ cortienic acid etabonate) were below the limit of quantitation (1 ng/mL) in healthy volunteers following intensive dosing (8 times daily for 2 days or 4 times daily for 42 days) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Undergoes extensive metabolism to inactive carboxylic acid metabolites [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Highly lipid-soluble, enhancing cellular penetration. Structurally modified to ensure predictable transformation to inactive metabolites [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Uveitis:** Controlled studies showed loteprednol 0.5% was less effective (72% resolution) than prednisolone acetate 1% (87% resolution). The incidence of clinically significant IOP increase ($\ge$10 mmHg) was 1% for loteprednol vs 6% for prednisolone [US].
- **Giant Papillary Conjunctivitis (GPC):** Demonstrated efficacy in reducing signs and symptoms after 1 week of treatment, continuing for up to 6 weeks [US].
- **Seasonal Allergic Conjunctivitis (SAC):** Demonstrated efficacy in reducing symptoms during peak pollen exposure [US].
- **Post-operative Inflammation:** Placebo-controlled studies confirmed efficacy in treating anterior chamber inflammation (cell and flare) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store upright between 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Keep out of reach of children [US].
