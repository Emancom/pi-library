# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational use by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and no less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - Subsequent daily maintenance doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - Administration: Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- For ESRD patients, therapy may be prompted when trough plasma levocarnitine levels fall below the normal range of 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical management [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** It is recommended to obtain baseline levels and conduct weekly/monthly monitoring. The target plasma free carnitine concentration should generally be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **INR Monitoring:** Increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) has been reported when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. Monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
The following events were reported in ESRD clinical trials regardless of causality [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%), chest pain (12%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Neurological/Nervous:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), depression (5%), insomnia (4%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Concomitant use may result in an increased INR. Close monitoring of anticoagulation status is required [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound, suggesting a low potential for interactions involving protein-binding displacement [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Pregnancy Category not explicitly lettered, but follows current labeling standards]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Clinical data in pregnant women are lacking; use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following administration. Clinicians must weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation for the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or treatment should be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight, applicable across age groups as guided by clinical response and plasma levels [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed; however, dose selection should generally be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function in this population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients on dialysis, IV administration is indicated. High-dose **oral** levocarnitine is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrates for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the direct effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) at room temperature [US].
- **Vials:** Single-dose only; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
