# Editorial Product Information: Lomitapide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) source:
    - **US (FDA)**: JUXTAPID (lomitapide) capsules, for oral use.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lomitapide for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lomitapide mesylate (a microsomal triglyceride transfer protein inhibitor).
- **Brand Names:** Juxtapid [US].
- **ATC Code:** C10AX12.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - Oral capsules: 2 mg, 5 mg, 10 mg, 20 mg, and 30 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Chiesi USA, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia (HoFH):** Indicated as an adjunct to a low-fat diet and other lipid-lowering treatments (e.g., LDL apheresis, statins) to reduce low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) in adult and pediatric patients aged 2 years and older [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** The safety and efficacy of lomitapide have not been established in patients with hypercholesterolemia who do not have HoFH [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Prior to Initiation
- **Laboratory Assessment:** Measure ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase, and total bilirubin.
- **Pregnancy Status:** Obtain a negative pregnancy test in females of reproductive potential.
- **Dietary Counseling:** Initiate a low-fat diet providing <20% of energy from fat or <30 g of fat daily.
- **Supplements:** Initiate daily nutritional supplementation containing vitamin E and essential fatty acids (ALA, EPA, DHA, and linoleic acid) due to potential malabsorption [US].

### 3.2 Adult Dosing (Ages 18+)
- **Initial Dose:** 5 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** Based on safety and tolerability. Escalate at intervals: 5 mg (Weeks 0-2), 10 mg (Weeks 3-6), 20 mg (Weeks 7-10), 40 mg (Weeks 11-14).
- **Maximum Dose:** 60 mg once daily [US].

### 3.3 Pediatric Dosing (Ages 2-17)
- **Ages 2 to 15 years:** Initial dose 2 mg once daily.
- **Ages 16 to 17 years:** Initial dose 5 mg once daily.
- **Maximum Recommended Doses:**
    - 2 to 10 years: 20 mg daily.
    - 11 to 17 years: 40 mg daily [US].

### 3.4 General Administration
- Take once daily with water and **without food**, at least 2 hours after the evening meal. Administration with food increases the risk of gastrointestinal adverse effects.
- Swallow capsules whole. If patients (particularly children) are unable to swallow, the capsule may be opened and the contents sprinkled on one tablespoon of apple sauce or mashed banana [US].

### 3.5 Missed Dose
- Take the dose at the usual time the next day. If dosing is interrupted for more than one week, contact a healthcare provider before restarting [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Absolutely contraindicated due to potential fetal harm [US].
- **Concomitant Medications:** Use with moderate or strong Cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4) inhibitors [US].
- **Hepatic Status:** Moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C) or active liver disease, including unexplained persistent elevations of serum transaminases [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### 5.1 Boxed Warning: Risk of Hepatotoxicity [US]
Lomitapide can cause elevations in transaminases and increases in hepatic fat (steatosis). 
- **Monitoring:** Transaminases must be measured before initiation and regularly during treatment.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Reduce or withhold dose if ALT or AST is $\ge$ 3x the upper limit of normal (ULN). Discontinue for clinically significant liver toxicity.
- **Long-term Risk:** Hepatic steatosis may be a risk factor for progressive liver disease, including steatohepatitis and cirrhosis.

### 5.2 Restricted Distribution (REMS)
Due to hepatotoxicity risks, lomitapide is available in the United States only through a restricted program called the **JUXTAPID REMS Program** [US].

### 5.3 Embryo-Fetal Toxicity
Lomitapide may cause fetal harm. Effective contraception is required for females of reproductive potential during treatment and for two weeks after the final dose [US].

### 5.4 Gastrointestinal (GI) Effects
GI reactions occur in the vast majority of patients (93% of adults; 72% of pediatrics). These may affect the absorption of concomitant oral medications. A low-fat diet is essential to mitigate these effects [US].

### 5.5 Reduced Vitamin Absorption
Lomitapide reduces the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins and fatty acids. Daily supplementation is mandatory (see Section 3.1) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Frequency Data
| Adverse Reaction | Adult Incidence (%) | Pediatric (5-17y) Incidence (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Diarrhea | 79% | 51% |
| Nausea | 65% | 12% |
| Abdominal Pain | 34% | 53% |
| Vomiting | 34% | 28% |
| Dyspepsia | 38% | Not specified |
| Weight Loss | 24% | Not specified |
| Elevated Transaminases | 17% | 53% |

### 6.2 Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Significant transaminase elevations ($\ge$ 3x ULN) occurred in 34% of adults and 14% of pediatrics in clinical trials.
- **Severe GI Distress:** Diarrhea and vomiting leading to volume depletion and hospitalization have been reported in post-marketing experience [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### 7.1 Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Strong/Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Contraindicated. Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) increase lomitapide exposure 27-fold [US].
- **Weak CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., atorvastatin, oral contraceptives). Reduce lomitapide dose by half [US].
- **Warfarin:** Lomitapide increases warfarin plasma concentrations by ~30%. Monitor INR regularly and adjust warfarin dose as needed [US].
- **P-gp Substrates:** Lomitapide inhibits P-gp; consider dose reduction of P-gp substrates [US].

### 7.2 Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Statins:** Lomitapide approximately doubles the exposure to simvastatin and lovastatin. Limit simvastatin to 20 mg daily (or 40 mg if previously tolerated) to reduce myopathy risk [US].
- **Bile Acid Sequestrants:** Separate dosing by at least 4 hours [US].
- **Hepatotoxic Agents:** Use with caution with agents like isotretinoin, amiodarone, or methotrexate [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Contraindicated.** No human data available, but teratogenic in rats and ferrets at clinically relevant exposures. Pregnancy category is not explicitly lettered in the revised US PI but the drug is strictly contraindicated [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. It is unknown if lomitapide is excreted in human milk, but potential for serious adverse reactions (hepatotoxicity) exists [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility observed in animal studies at 4-5 times human exposure [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for ages 2 years and older. Safety not established for children < 2 years [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Use with caution; start at the low end of the dosing range due to higher frequency of decreased organ function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - End-stage renal disease (ESRD) on dialysis: Reduce maximum dose (Adults: 40 mg; 11-17y: 30 mg; 2-10y: 15 mg).
    - Mild/moderate/severe renal impairment (not on dialysis): Not studied; exposure may increase by >50% [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild (Child-Pugh A): Reduce maximum dose (Adults: 40 mg; 11-17y: 30 mg; 2-10y: 15 mg).
    - Moderate/Severe (Child-Pugh B or C): Contraindicated [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Directly binds and inhibits microsomal triglyceride transfer protein (MTP) in the lumen of the endoplasmic reticulum. This prevents the assembly of apo B-containing lipoproteins (chylomicrons in enterocytes and VLDL in hepatocytes), resulting in reduced plasma LDL-C [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** ~7%.
    - **Tmax:** ~6 hours.
    - **Protein Binding:** 99.8%.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively hepatic via CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life ~40 hours. Excretion via urine (53-60%) and feces (33-35%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Trial:** A 78-week, single-arm, open-label study of 29 adults with HoFH showed a mean reduction in LDL-C of 40% at Week 26. Reductions were sustained through 78 weeks [US].
- **Pediatric Trial:** A 104-week, open-label trial of 43 patients (ages 5-17) showed a mean LDL-C reduction of 49% at Week 24. Reductions were sustained over 2 years, though some patients required dose adjustments due to transaminase elevations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- Keep the container tightly closed and protect capsules from moisture [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Special Status:** Subject to Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) [US].
