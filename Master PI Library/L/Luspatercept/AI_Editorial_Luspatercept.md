# Editorial Product Information: Luspatercept

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration) and **Singapore** (Health Sciences Authority - via included packaging labels).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Luspatercept-aamt
- **Chemical Class:** Erythroid maturation agent; recombinant fusion protein (modified human activin receptor type IIB linked to human IgG1 Fc domain).
- **Brand Name:** **REBLOZYL**
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:**
    - Lyophilized powder for injection (white to off-white) in single-dose vials: 25 mg and 75 mg [US, Singapore].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Celgene Corporation, a Bristol-Myers Squibb Company [US, Singapore].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Beta Thalassemia:** Treatment of anemia in adult patients who require regular red blood cell (RBC) transfusions [US-approved].
- **Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS) - ESA-naïve:** Treatment of anemia in erythropoiesis stimulating agent (ESA)-naïve adult patients with very low- to intermediate-risk MDS who may require regular RBC transfusions [US-approved].
- **Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS) - ESA-refractory/intolerant:** Treatment of anemia failing an ESA and requiring $\ge$ 2 RBC units over 8 weeks in adult patients with very low- to intermediate-risk MDS with ring sideroblasts (MDS-RS) or with myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative neoplasm with ring sideroblasts and thrombocytosis (MDS/MPN-RS-T) [US-approved].
- **Limitations of Use:** **REBLOZYL** is not a substitute for RBC transfusions in patients requiring immediate correction of anemia [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Starting Dose:** 1 mg/kg once every 3 weeks by subcutaneous injection.
- **Preparation:** Reconstitute with Sterile Water for Injection. The final concentration is 50 mg/mL.
- **Administration:** Inject into the upper arm, thigh, or abdomen. If the volume exceeds 1.2 mL, divide the dose into separate similar-volume injections at different sites.

### Titration by Indication
- **Beta Thalassemia:**
    - If no reduction in transfusion burden after $\ge$ 2 consecutive doses (6 weeks) at 1 mg/kg, increase to 1.25 mg/kg every 3 weeks.
    - **Maximum Dose:** 1.25 mg/kg [US].
    - Discontinue if no response after 9 weeks at the maximum dose.
- **Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS):**
    - **ESA-naïve:** Titrate to maintain hemoglobin (Hgb) between 10 g/dL and 12 g/dL.
    - **ESA-refractory/intolerant:** Increase dose if the patient is not RBC transfusion-free.
    - **Titration Steps:** 1 mg/kg $\rightarrow$ 1.33 mg/kg $\rightarrow$ 1.75 mg/kg.
    - **Maximum Dose:** 1.75 mg/kg [US].
    - Discontinue if no response after 9 weeks at the maximum dose.

### Dose Modifications for Toxicity/Hgb Levels
- **High Hemoglobin:** 
    - [US-Beta Thalassemia]: If Hgb $\ge$ 11.5 g/dL (absent transfusion), interrupt dose. Restart when Hgb $\le$ 11 g/dL.
    - [US-MDS]: If Hgb $\ge$ 12 g/dL (absent transfusion), interrupt dose. Restart when Hgb $\le$ 11 g/dL.
- **Rapid Hgb Rise:** If Hgb increases $> 2$ g/dL within 3 weeks (absent transfusion), reduce the dose by one level.
- **Grade 3/4 Adverse Reactions:** Interrupt treatment. Restart at a lower dose once resolved to Grade $\le$ 1. Discontinue if the delay exceeds 12–15 weeks (jurisdiction/indication dependent) [US].

### Missed Doses
- Administer the missed dose as soon as possible and continue with at least 3 weeks between subsequent doses [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None listed** in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Thrombosis/Thromboembolism:** Increased risk of serious events (DVT, pulmonary embolism, stroke) especially in Beta Thalassemia patients. Risk factors include splenectomy and concomitant hormone replacement therapy. Consider thromboprophylaxis in high-risk patients.
- **Hypertension:** Reported in 11.4% of patients. Monitor blood pressure prior to each administration and treat new-onset or worsening hypertension.
- **Extramedullary Hematopoietic (EMH) Masses:** Reported in patients with Beta Thalassemia (3.2% in transfusion-dependent; 6.3% in non-transfusion-dependent). Serious complications include spinal cord compression. Discontinue treatment for serious EMH complications.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** May cause fetal harm based on animal data. Advise of potential risk and the need for effective contraception.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Fatigue, headache, musculoskeletal pain, arthralgia, dizziness/vertigo, nausea, diarrhea, cough, abdominal pain, dyspnea, COVID-19, peripheral edema, hypertension, and hypersensitivity [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Cerebrovascular accident and deep vein thrombosis (1% in Beta Thalassemia trials).
    - Syncope (reported in MDS trials).
    - Renal impairment (8% in MDS-RS trials).
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Increased ALT/AST, increased total bilirubin, and decreased GFR/creatinine clearance were frequently observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No clinically significant differences in luspatercept PK were observed when used concomitantly with iron-chelating agents [US].
- **General Note:** Luspatercept and its metabolites do not selectively penetrate the CNS.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [US: Narrative Risk Summary]: Based on animal studies (rats/rabbits), luspatercept can cause embryo-fetal mortality and structural abnormalities at exposures above the maximum recommended human dose. 
    - **Pregnancy Testing:** Recommended for females of reproductive potential prior to starting treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Luspatercept was detected in the milk of lactating rats. Breastfeeding is **not recommended** during treatment and for 3 months after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Based on animal data, may impair female fertility; effects were reversible in rats after a 14-week recovery period [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established. Not recommended for use in pediatric patients [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** 
    - **MDS:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy between patients $\ge$ 65 years and younger patients.
    - **Beta Thalassemia:** Insufficient number of patients $\ge$ 65 years to determine if they respond differently [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically significant PK differences in mild-to-moderate impairment (eGFR 30–89 mL/min). Effect of severe impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min) is unknown [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No clinically significant PK differences in mild-to-severe hepatic impairment (based on bilirubin/AST/ALT) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Luspatercept is a recombinant fusion protein that binds specific TGF-$\beta$ superfamily ligands. By trapping these ligands, it diminishes Smad2/3 signaling, which is abnormally elevated in models of Beta Thalassemia and MDS. This promotes erythroid maturation through differentiation of late-stage erythroid precursors (normoblasts).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is approximately 5–6 days post-dose. SC injection site (arm, thigh, abdomen) does not significantly affect absorption.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent $V_d$ is 7.1 L (Beta Thalassemia) to 9.6 L (MDS).
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is approximately 11 days (Beta Thalassemia) to 14 days (MDS).
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to be catabolized into small peptides and amino acids by general catabolic processes.
- **Immunogenicity:** Anti-drug antibodies (ADA) occurred in 1.8% (Beta Thalassemia) to 6.3% (MDS) of patients. While mean trough concentrations were lower in ADA-positive Beta Thalassemia patients, no identified clinical effect on safety or efficacy was established [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BELIEVE Trial (Beta Thalassemia):** 21.4% of patients achieved $\ge$ 33% reduction in RBC transfusion burden (weeks 13–24) compared to 4.5% in the placebo group [US].
- **COMMANDS Trial (ESA-naïve MDS):** 58.5% of patients achieved transfusion independence $\ge$ 12 weeks with concurrent Hgb increase $\ge$ 1.5 g/dL (weeks 1–24) vs 31.2% for epoetin alfa [US].
- **MEDALIST Trial (ESA-refractory/intolerant MDS-RS):** 37.9% of patients achieved transfusion independence $\ge$ 8 weeks (weeks 1–24) vs 13.2% for placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store vials refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US, Singapore].
- **Handling:** Keep in original carton to protect from light. **Do not freeze.**
- **Shelf Life (Reconstituted):**
    - Room temperature (20°C to 25°C): Up to 8 hours.
    - Refrigerated (2°C to 8°C): Up to 24 hours.
