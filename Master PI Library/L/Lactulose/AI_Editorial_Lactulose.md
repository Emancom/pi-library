# Editorial Product Information: Lactulose

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (Food and Drug Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of lactulose (powder for oral solution) for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis of available regulatory information and is not intended to replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. The source material provided for this synthesis corresponds to the United States (FDA) approved labeling for PAI Pharma's lactulose crystals.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lactulose (synthetic disaccharide: 4-O-β-D-Galactopyranosyl-D-fructofuranose).
- **Chemical Class:** Colonic acidifier/Osmotic laxative.
- **Brand Names:** Lactulose for Oral Solution (PAI Pharma) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A06AD11.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Single-dose packets containing 10 g or 20 g of lactulose crystals for reconstitution [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** PAI Holdings, LLC dba PAI Pharma [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Constipation:** Indicated for the treatment of constipation. In patients with a history of chronic constipation, therapy is used to increase the frequency of bowel movements and the number of days on which movements occur [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard starting dose is 10 g to 20 g daily.
- **Titration:** The dose may be increased to 40 g daily if clinical response is insufficient.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - The contents of one packet must be dissolved in approximately 4 ounces (half a glass) of water prior to administration.
    - The resulting solution may range from colorless to slightly pale yellow.
- **Expected Onset of Action:** Patients should be advised that 24 to 48 hours may be required to produce a bowel movement due to the time required for the drug to reach the colon and exert its effect.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Galactosemia/Low Galactose Diet:** Lactulose is contraindicated in patients requiring a low galactose diet, as the product contains small amounts of galactose and lactose (total sum <0.3 g per 10 g of lactulose) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Electrocautery Procedures:** A theoretical risk of explosion exists during proctoscopy or colonoscopy if hydrogen gas (H₂) has accumulated in significant concentrations. Patients on lactulose undergoing these procedures should have a thorough bowel cleansing with a non-fermentable solution. While CO₂ insufflation is a safeguard, it is generally considered redundant if proper cleansing is performed [US].
- **Diabetes Mellitus:** Due to the presence of galactose and lactose, caution is advised when treating diabetic patients [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Elderly or debilitated patients receiving therapy for more than six months should undergo periodic monitoring of serum electrolytes (potassium, chloride, and carbon dioxide) [US].
- **General Precautions:** Patients should contact a physician if an unusual diarrheal condition develops.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Precise frequency data are not established in the available source documents.

- **Gastrointestinal:** 
    - Flatulence and intestinal cramps are common at the start of therapy and are typically transient.
    - Nausea and vomiting have been reported.
- **Excessive Dosage Effects:** High doses can lead to diarrhea, which may cause complications such as:
    - Fluid loss
    - Hypokalemia
    - Hypernatremia

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Nonabsorbable Antacids:** Preliminary data suggest that concurrent use of nonabsorbable antacids may inhibit the lactulose-induced drop in colonic pH. This may lead to a reduced therapeutic effect of lactulose [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered in the current source, but noted that reproduction studies in mice, rats, and rabbits at doses 3 to 6 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** Should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if lactulose is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised when lactulose is administered to a nursing woman [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no deleterious effects on breeding, conception, or parturition.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** See Section 5 for electrolyte monitoring requirements during long-term use in elderly or debilitated patients.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lactulose is a synthetic disaccharide that is not hydrolysed by human GI enzymes. It reaches the colon unchanged, where colonic bacteria break it down into lactic acid, formic acid, and acetic acid.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The acid metabolites increase osmotic pressure and acidify colonic contents, resulting in increased stool water content and softening of the stool.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed from the GI tract.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised in the colon by bacteria.
    - **Elimination:** Urinary excretion is 3% or less and is completed within 24 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Chronic Constipation:** Source documentation indicates that in patients with chronic constipation, lactulose increases both the frequency of bowel movements and the number of days on which they occur. Specific pivotal trial data summaries were not provided in the source PI.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature, 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
- **Packaging:** Single-dose packets are not child-resistant [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified for other jurisdictions in the provided source.
