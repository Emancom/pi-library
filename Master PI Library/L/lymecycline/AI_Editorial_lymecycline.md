# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection, synthesized from available Product Information (PI) for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. The information provided herein is based on the source document from the United States (FDA) for the product manufactured by Baxter Healthcare Corporation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Errors:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Dosing Interval:** Should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **Monitoring:** Daily doses should be adjusted based on clinical response and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L).

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment if levels are adequate [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenterals visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no known contraindications listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with known seizure history, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in renal failure may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality):

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Chest pain (12%), Fever (7%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Atrial fibrillation (2%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%) |
| **Neurologic** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures are the most significant serious risks identified [US].
- **Other Reported Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting, gastritis, and body odor have been observed.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not Categorized by letter]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women are lacking. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight; see Section 3 for specific instructions related to metabolic disorders [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** **Levocarnitine injection** is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Use caution and monitor trough levels. Note that *oral* formulations are not recommended for chronic use in severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine plasma concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific signs, symptoms, or clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].
