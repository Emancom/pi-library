# Editorial Product Information: Levetiracetam

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levetiracetam, specifically focused on the oral solution formulation, intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for the most current legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levetiracetam, a pyrrolidone derivative.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Levetiracetam [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N03AX14.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral solution (clear and colourless), 100 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral (may be diluted in water).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Southern Cross Pharma Pty Ltd / Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Monotherapy:** Treatment of partial onset seizures (with or without secondary generalisation) in patients from 16 years of age with newly diagnosed epilepsy [Australian].
- **Add-on Therapy:**
    - Partial onset seizures (with or without secondary generalisation) in adults and children aged 4 years and older [Australian].
    - Myoclonic seizures in adults and adolescents from 12 years of age with juvenile myoclonic epilepsy (JME) [Australian].
    - Primary Generalised Tonic-Clonic (PGTC) seizures in adults and children from 4 years of age with idiopathic generalised epilepsy (IGE) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administered in two equally divided doses daily.
- May be taken with or without food.
- Therapy may be initiated with either oral or intravenous administration; conversion between forms does not require titration.

### Monotherapy (Patients ≥16 years)
- **Starting Dose:** 250 mg twice daily.
- **Titration:** Increase to an initial therapeutic dose of 500 mg twice daily after two weeks. Further increments of 250 mg twice daily every two weeks may be made based on clinical response.
- **Maximum Dose:** 1,500 mg twice daily.

### Add-on Therapy
- **Adults (≥18 years) and Adolescents (≥50 kg):**
    - Initial dose: 500 mg twice daily (can be started on day one).
    - Titration: Increments or decrements of 500 mg twice daily every two to four weeks.
    - Maximum dose: 1,500 mg twice daily.
- **Children (4–11 years) and Adolescents (<50 kg):**
    - Initial dose: 10 mg/kg twice daily.
    - Titration: Increments or decrements of 10 mg/kg twice daily every two weeks.
    - Maximum dose: 30 mg/kg twice daily.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available source.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to levetiracetam, other pyrrolidone derivatives, or any excipients (including methyl/propyl hydroxybenzoate and maltitol) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation:** Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behaviour. Patients should be monitored for the emergence or worsening of depression or unusual changes in mood.
- **Psychiatric Reactions:** Levetiracetam may cause behavioural changes (aggression, agitation, anger, anxiety, hostility, irritability) and psychotic symptoms.
- **Seizure Worsening:** A paradoxical reaction of seizure worsening may occur, particularly at the start of treatment or during dose escalation.
- **Haematologic Effects:** Decreased blood cell counts (neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, and pancytopenia) have been reported. Monitoring of complete blood counts is advised in patients with significant weakness, pyrexia, or recurrent infections.
- **DRESS/Multiorgan Hypersensitivity:** Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) has been reported. Discontinue immediately if suspected.
- **QT Prolongation:** Rare cases have been observed in post-marketing surveillance.
- **Withdrawal:** Gradual withdrawal is recommended to minimise the risk of increased seizure frequency.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Distribution
- **Very Common (>10%):** Somnolence, asthenia, infection (primarily community-acquired upper respiratory infections), headache, fatigue.
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - *Gastrointestinal:* Abdominal pain, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, nausea, vomiting.
    - *Nervous System:* Amnesia, anxiety, ataxia, convulsion, depression, dizziness, emotional lability, insomnia, nervousness, tremor, vertigo.
    - *Other:* Anorexia, rash, diplopia, nasopharyngitis.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%) to Rare (<0.1%):** Leukopenia, neutropenia, pancytopenia, suicide attempt/ideation, pancreatitis, hepatic failure, rhabdomyolysis, acute kidney injury.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Japanese Population Nuance:** The prevalence of **rhabdomyolysis** and increased blood creatine phosphokinase is significantly higher in Japanese patients compared to non-Japanese patients. Evidence also suggests a potential predisposition in the Japanese population to **neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS)** [Japanese/Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Levetiracetam does not inhibit or induce major hepatic cytochrome P450 (CYP) isoforms or glucuronyl transferase.
- **Probenecid:** Inhibits the renal clearance of the major metabolite (ucb L057) but does not affect the parent drug levetiracetam.
- **Antiepileptic Drugs (AEDs):** Does not influence serum levels of phenytoin, carbamazepine, valproic acid, phenobarbitone, lamotrigine, gabapentin, or primidone. However, enzyme-inducing AEDs may increase levetiracetam clearance by 22% [Australian].
- **Other:** No significant interaction with oral contraceptives (ethinyloestradiol/levonorgestrel), digoxin, or warfarin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B3 [Australian]:** Levetiracetam crosses the placenta. Post-marketing data on >1800 monotherapy exposures do not suggest an increase in major congenital malformations. However, concentrations may decrease significantly during pregnancy (especially the 3rd trimester).
    - Clinical management should include monitoring serum concentrations and folic acid supplementation.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levetiracetam is excreted in human breast milk. A decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the importance of the drug to the mother.
- **Fertility:** No human data available. Animal studies showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility at doses up to 6x the maximum clinical dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children ≥4 years for partial onset and PGTC seizures. Data are insufficient for children under 4 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Dose adjustment is required for elderly patients with compromised renal function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Daily dose must be individualised based on creatinine clearance ($CL_{cr}$):
    - **Normal ($\ge80$ mL/min):** 500–1500 mg twice daily.
    - **Mild (50–79 mL/min):** 500–1000 mg twice daily.
    - **Moderate (30–49 mL/min):** 250–750 mg twice daily.
    - **Severe (<30 mL/min):** 250–500 mg twice daily.
    - **End-stage (Dialysis):** 500–1000 mg once daily (with a 250–500 mg supplemental dose post-dialysis).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment. In severe impairment ($CL_{cr} < 60$ mL/min), a 50% reduction in the maintenance dose is recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Not fully elucidated. Appears unrelated to traditional AED mechanisms. It binds to the synaptic vesicle protein **2A (SV2A)**, which is involved in vesicle fusion and neurotransmitter exocytosis. It also partially inhibits N-type $Ca^{2+}$ currents and modulates GABA/glycine-gated currents.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and near-complete ($>100\%$ bioavailability). $C_{max}$ reached at 1.3 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Protein binding is low ($<10\%$).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via enzymatic hydrolysis of the acetamide group (not CYP-dependent). Major metabolite (ucb L057) is pharmacologically inactive.
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is approximately $7 \pm 1$ hours in adults. 95% of the dose is excreted in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy:** A non-inferiority study against carbamazepine CR in newly diagnosed patients (≥16 years) showed 73.0% of levetiracetam subjects were seizure-free for 6 months at the last evaluated dose compared to 72.8% for carbamazepine [Australian].
- **Add-on Therapy:** Multiple randomised, placebo-controlled trials confirmed significant reductions in seizure frequency for partial onset, myoclonic (JME), and PGTC seizures in both adult and paediatric populations (≥4 years).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Store in the original container.
- **Shelf Life:** Once opened, the oral solution must be discarded after 7 months [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** S4 – Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
