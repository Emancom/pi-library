# Editorial Product Information: Lamotrigine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lamotrigine (chemical class: substituted asymmetric triazine).
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Lamotrigine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N03AX09 (Antiepileptics).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Dispersible/chewable tablets: 25 mg, 50 mg, 100 mg, and 200 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Epilepsy (Adults and Children >2 years):**
    - Treatment of partial and generalised seizures [Australian].
    - Initial "add-on" therapy [Australian].
    - Monotherapy following the withdrawal of concomitant antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) [Australian].
    - **Note:** Initial monotherapy in newly diagnosed paediatric patients is not recommended [Australian].
- **Lennox-Gastaut Syndrome:** Add-on therapy for associated seizures [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- **Titration:** Strict adherence to escalation schedules is essential to reduce the risk of severe skin rash.
- **Administration:** Tablets may be swallowed whole, chewed, or dispersed in a small volume of water.
- **Restarting Therapy:** If the interval since the last dose exceeds five half-lives, escalation to the maintenance dose must follow the appropriate titration schedule.

### Adult Titration (Epilepsy, >12 years)
- **Monotherapy:** 25 mg/day (Weeks 1–2), 50 mg/day (Weeks 3–4), then increase by 50–100 mg every 1–2 weeks. Usual maintenance: 100–200 mg/day [Australian].
- **Add-on with Valproate:** 25 mg every alternate day (Weeks 1–2), 25 mg/day (Weeks 3–4). Usual maintenance: 100–200 mg/day [Australian].
- **Add-on with Enzyme Inducers (e.g., Carbamazepine, Phenytoin):** 50 mg/day (Weeks 1–2), 100 mg/day in divided doses (Weeks 3–4). Usual maintenance: 200–400 mg/day [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (2–12 years)
- Weight-based dosing is required. 
- **Maintenance range:** 1–5 mg/kg/day (with valproate) or 5–15 mg/kg/day (with enzyme inducers) [Australian].
- If the calculated dose is <25 mg, strengths of 2 mg or 5 mg from other manufacturers must be used [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **lamotrigine** or any excipient in the formulation (e.g., aspartame) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Severe Skin Reactions):** Potentially life-threatening rashes, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), have been reported. Risk is higher in children and when exceeding titration limits or co-administering with valproate. Discontinue at the first sign of rash [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity Syndrome (DRESS):** May involve fever, lymphadenopathy, and multi-organ failure. Early symptoms may appear without a rash [Australian].
- **Aseptic Meningitis:** Reported in both adults and children; symptoms include headache, fever, and nuchal rigidity [Australian].
- **Haemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis (HLH):** A life-threatening syndrome of pathological immune activation. Evaluate patients with fever or rash immediately [Australian].
- **Suicidality:** Increased risk of suicidal thoughts/behaviour observed with all AEDs. Monitor for mood changes [Australian].
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Ethinyloestradiol/levonorgestrel can decrease lamotrigine levels by ~50%. Dosing adjustments are required when starting or stopping hormonal contraceptives [Australian].
- **Brugada-type ECG:** Very rare association; caution in patients with Brugada syndrome [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Skin rash, headache, dizziness, somnolence, ataxia, diplopia, blurred vision, nausea, vomiting [Australian] |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nystagmus, insomnia, tremor, diarrhoea, aggression, irritability, tiredness [Australian] |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anorexia, transient leucopenia/thrombocytopenia, alopecia, photosensitivity [Australian] |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | SJS, aseptic meningitis [Australian] |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | TEN, DRESS, HLH, pancytopenia, agranulocytosis, hepatic failure, rhabdomyolysis [Australian] |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzyme Inhibitors (Valproate):** Inhibits glucuronidation, doubling the half-life of lamotrigine. Requires dose reduction [Australian].
- **Enzyme Inducers (Carbamazepine, Phenytoin, Rifampicin):** Increase lamotrigine clearance, reducing half-life to ~14 hours. Requires dose increase [Australian].
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Increase clearance; lamotrigine may also modestly increase levonorgestrel clearance, potentially affecting efficacy [Australian].
- **OCT 2 Substrates:** Lamotrigine inhibits OCT 2; caution with narrow-therapeutic-index substrates like dofetilide [Australian].
- **Atazanavir/Ritonavir:** Reduces lamotrigine plasma concentrations [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category D. Registry data suggests a potential increase in oral cleft malformations. Use only if benefits outweigh risks. Do not abruptly discontinue AEDs due to seizure risk. Folate supplementation (5 mg/day) is recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Lamotrigine passes into breast milk; infant serum levels can reach 50% of maternal levels. Potential for pharmacological effects in infants. Weigh benefits against risks [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Reduced fertility seen in animal studies at toxic doses; no human data [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children >2 years for epilepsy. Safety/efficacy in children <2 years not established [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Use with caution; monitor regularly as they are more likely to have intercurrent illnesses or polypharmacy [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Exercise caution. Reduced maintenance doses may be required in end-stage renal failure [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Reduce initial, escalation, and maintenance doses by ~50% in moderate (Child-Pugh B) and ~75% in severe (Child-Pugh C) impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits voltage-gated sodium channels, stabilising neuronal membranes and suppressing the release of excitatory neurotransmitters (primarily glutamate) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and complete; peak plasma levels at ~2.5 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 55% protein-bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via glucuronyl-transferases (UGT) to inactive metabolites [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal; mean half-life in healthy adults is ~29 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Epilepsy:** Efficacy demonstrated in six double-blind crossover studies. Monotherapy trials showed efficacy comparable to phenytoin and carbamazepine [Australian].
- **Paediatric Epilepsy:** Open trials (ages 2–12) showed effective seizure reduction; 34% of patients had a ≥50% reduction in seizures [Australian].
- **Lennox-Gastaut:** Placebo-controlled study showed a 32% reduction in major motor seizures compared to 9% for placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Protect from light and moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** S4 - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
