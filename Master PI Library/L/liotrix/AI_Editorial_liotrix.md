# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This harmonised editorial document was developed using the following primary source: United States (FDA) Product Information for Levocarnitine Injection by Baxter Healthcare Corporation.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a single, coherent, and clinically accurate synthesis of the product information for healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information approved by the regulatory authority in the jurisdiction where a clinician is practicing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administration should occur as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Metabolic Crisis:** Initial loading doses may be followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing intervals should be every 3 or 4 hours, and no less frequent than every 6 hours [US].
- **ESRD Adjustments:** Dosage adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on clinical response and plasma concentrations [US].
- **Target Levels:** For metabolic disorders, plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- There are currently no known contraindications listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Note):** While this editorial concerns the injection, clinicians should note that high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Reactions:** Transient nausea and vomiting are frequently observed. Other less frequent reactions include body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Clinical Trial Data (ESRD):** In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality):
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), headache (3–37%), chest pain (6–15%), and flu syndrome (15–29%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), and tachycardia (5–9%).
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), abdominal pain (5–21%), and vomiting (9–21%).
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulation therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but descriptive data provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following exogenous administration. The benefits of maternal therapy must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders in paediatric patients follows the 50 mg/kg recommendation. Secondary carnitine deficiency in neonates and premature infants is defined by plasma levels below age-related normals [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific clinical studies in the elderly were not detailed; generally, dose selection should be cautious, reflecting the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Patients with ESRD on dialysis require specific dosing (10–20 mg/kg) post-dialysis. Oral levocarnitine is not recommended for long-term use in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; follows a two-compartment model after IV bolus. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed a significant increase in pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While carnitine concentrations increased, clinical outcomes regarding signs and symptoms of deficiency in this population were not determined by these trials [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Use only single-dose vials and discard any unused portion, as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources [US].
