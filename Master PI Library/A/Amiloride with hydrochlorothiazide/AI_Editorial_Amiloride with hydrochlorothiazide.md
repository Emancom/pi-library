# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- At the time of writing, official PI documents for other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) were not provided for comparison. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The standard recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This should be administered as a slow bolus (over 2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (calculated based on dry body weight). Administration should occur as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often employed during acute crises, followed by an equivalent total dose administered over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and no less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Monitoring:** Pre-therapy plasma carnitine levels should be established. Ongoing monitoring (weekly and monthly) should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations. The target free carnitine concentration is generally 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].
- **ESRD Adjustments:** Dose adjustments in dialysis patients may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels falling below 40–50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for discoloration or particulate matter prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s solution in concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon signs of hypersensitivity [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of convulsive activity [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. In ESRD patients, chronic high-dose oral administration may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally cleared renally [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in patients with ESRD are often confounded by the underlying pathology.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Events reported at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, fever, flu syndrome, asthenia, abdominal pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, hyperkalemia, peripheral edema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, depression, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Other** | Pruritus, rash, anemia, eye disorders, urinary tract infection |

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis occur less frequently [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Significant increases in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been reported when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Perform frequent INR monitoring in patients receiving warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following any dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified in text]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response, it should be used in pregnancy only if clearly required [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Animal data (dairy cows) indicate levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. The benefits of maternal therapy must be weighed against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations are integrated into the general metabolic and ESRD dosing guidelines. Specific age-related secondary carnitine deficiency definitions are used (e.g., in newborns, levels below age-related normals) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients on dialysis, the drug is used to replace dialytic losses. Oral formulations are cautioned against in this population due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It acts as a carrier molecule that facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process provides the necessary substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with metabolic errors or ESRD, levocarnitine helps clear accumulated acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted. This prevents "autointoxication" and life-threatening acidosis [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Oral tablets and solution are bioequivalent, with an absolute bioavailability of approximately 15% after correcting for endogenous levels [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed that trough levels increased from a baseline of 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L by week 8 [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcome Note:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness) has not been definitively determined in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation is preservative-free [US].

- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
