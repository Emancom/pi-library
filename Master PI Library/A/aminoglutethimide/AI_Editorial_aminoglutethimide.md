# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are adequate [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry and vital signs. The target plasma free carnitine concentration is between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These can occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon signs of a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine (both oral and intravenous) has been associated with reports of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring in Renal Impairment:** While the injection is used in ESRD, the safety/efficacy of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in these patients is not established, as it may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Frequency ≥5% (ESRD Clinical Trials)
| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, abdominal pain, asthenia |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcaemia, hypervolaemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |

### Other Notable Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and body odor (less frequent) [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (postmarketing) [US].
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis and other severe hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned, but equivalent to Category B]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies on levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after administration. The benefits of maternal therapy must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight, applicable to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources; however, clinical studies generally included adult populations [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Major indication includes ESRD patients on dialysis. Dose adjustments are guided by plasma trough levels [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with organic acidopathies, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are quickly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In 9-week and 24-week studies of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, levocarnitine injection (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels rose from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190-243 µmol/L. However, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population remains undetermined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Vials do not contain preservatives; discard any unused portion of an opened vial [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available US sources.
