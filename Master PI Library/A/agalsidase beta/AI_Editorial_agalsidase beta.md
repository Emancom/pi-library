# Editorial Product Information: Agalsidase Beta (Fabrazyme)

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by synthesizing official regulatory data from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Where clinical guidelines or safety specifications differ between these jurisdictions, the more comprehensive or conservative information has been included and labeled accordingly.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Fabrazyme  
**Active Ingredient:** Agalsidase beta  

Agalsidase beta is a recombinant human $\alpha$-galactosidase A enzyme produced by recombinant DNA technology in a Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) mammalian cell expression system. It has the same amino acid sequence as the naturally occurring human enzyme.

## 2. Approved Indications
Fabrazyme is indicated for long-term enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) in patients with a confirmed diagnosis of Fabry disease ($\alpha$-galactosidase A deficiency). 

**Pediatric Use:**
*   The FDA approves use in adult and pediatric patients aged 2 years and older [FDA].
*   The TGA notes that while safety and efficacy have been specifically investigated in children aged 8–16 years, treatment for those under 8 years of age may be initiated based on a physician's risk/benefit analysis when clearly required [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
Treatment is contraindicated in patients with clinical evidence of severe, life-threatening hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis) to agalsidase beta or any of the product's excipients [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis
Life-threatening hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported. These reactions may occur during the initial course of therapy or after extended treatment. Administration must occur in a clinical setting equipped with appropriate medical monitoring and emergency support, including cardiopulmonary resuscitation equipment and epinephrine. If a severe hypersensitivity reaction occurs, the infusion must be discontinued immediately.

### Infusion-Associated Reactions (IARs)
IARs are common (occurring in approximately 59% of patients) and are defined as adverse reactions occurring on the same day as the infusion. Symptoms often include chills, pyrexia, nausea, vomiting, and headache. Most IARs can be managed by reducing the infusion rate, temporarily halting the infusion, or administering additional pre-medications.

### Immunogenicity and Antibody Formation
Most patients develop IgG antibodies to agalsidase beta, typically within the first three months of treatment. High antibody titers or persistent IgG formation are associated with a higher frequency of IARs. Testing for IgE antibodies should be considered for patients who experience suspected allergic reactions.

### Advanced Fabry Disease
Patients with compromised cardiac function due to advanced Fabry disease should be monitored closely, as they may be at higher risk for severe complications arising from infusion reactions.

## 5. Dosage and Administration

### Recommended Dosage
The standard dose is **1 mg/kg of body weight** administered every two weeks via intravenous infusion.

### Pre-treatment
To reduce the risk of IARs, it is recommended to pre-treat patients with antipyretics (e.g., paracetamol) and antihistamines approximately one hour prior to the infusion. In cases of severe or recurrent reactions, corticosteroids may also be considered.

### Infusion Rate and Titration
*   **Initial Rate:** The starting rate should not exceed 0.25 mg/min (15 mg/hour).
*   **Patients $\ge$ 30 kg:** If the initial rate is tolerated, the rate may be increased in increments of 0.05 to 0.08 mg/min with subsequent infusions. The minimum infusion duration is typically 1.5 hours [FDA].
*   **Patients < 30 kg:** The maximum infusion rate should remain at 0.25 mg/min (15 mg/hour) [FDA].

### Preparation and Reconstitution
Vials (5 mg or 35 mg) must be reconstituted with Sterile Water for Injection to a concentration of 5 mg/mL. The solution should be clear and colorless; do not use if there is discoloration or particulate matter. The reconstituted dose is then further diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride for Injection.

### Home Infusion
Home infusion may be considered for patients who are tolerating their treatments well. This must be performed under the supervision of a trained healthcare professional and following a thorough clinical evaluation [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequently reported adverse reactions include:
*   **General:** Chills, pyrexia, fatigue, and peripheral edema.
*   **Nervous System:** Headache, paresthesia, and dizziness.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain.
*   **Respiratory:** Upper respiratory tract infections, cough, and dyspnea.
*   **Vascular/Skin:** Hypertension, flushing, and rash.

**Post-marketing Experience:** 
Rare reports include leukocytoclastic vasculitis, renal failure, and one reported case of membranous glomerulonephritis [TGA/FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted.
*   **Prohibited Mixing:** Do not infuse Fabrazyme in the same intravenous line with other medicinal products.
*   **Theoretical Inhibitors:** Co-administration with chloroquine, amiodarone, benoquin, or gentamicin is not recommended due to a theoretical risk of inhibiting intracellular $\alpha$-galactosidase activity [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
*   **Registry Data:** Data from the Fabry Disease Registry have not identified a drug-associated risk for major birth defects or miscarriage [FDA].
*   **Precautionary Stance:** As a precautionary measure, some guidelines suggest avoiding use during pregnancy unless the potential benefit justifies the risk [TGA]. 
*   **Classification:** TGA Category B2.

### Breastfeeding
Agalsidase beta is excreted in human milk in small amounts. Because the effects on the nursing infant are unknown, a decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, taking into account the importance of the treatment to the mother.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
Agalsidase beta exhibits non-linear pharmacokinetics, with the area under the curve (AUC) increasing more than proportionately with the dose. 
*   **Half-life:** The elimination half-life is dose-independent and ranges from approximately 45 to 120 minutes.
*   **Pediatric PK:** In children, the formation of anti-drug antibodies has been observed to increase plasma concentrations (AUC and Cmax) by 2- to 4-fold; however, this increase has not been correlated with changed efficacy or safety profiles [FDA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Fabry disease is caused by a deficiency of the lysosomal enzyme $\alpha$-galactosidase A, leading to the accumulation of glycosphingolipids, primarily globotriaosylceramide (GL-3), in various tissues. Fabrazyme provides an exogenous source of the enzyme. Once administered, it is internalized by cells and transported to the lysosomes, where it catalyzes the hydrolysis of GL-3 into ceramide dihexoside and galactose.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Vials:** Store under refrigeration between 2°C and 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Do not use beyond the expiration date.
*   **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** The product contains no preservatives. It is stable for up to 24 hours when stored between 2°C and 8°C. To reduce the risk of microbial contamination, it should be used as soon as possible after dilution.
*   **Filtering:** Use an in-line, low protein-binding 0.2 µm filter during administration.
