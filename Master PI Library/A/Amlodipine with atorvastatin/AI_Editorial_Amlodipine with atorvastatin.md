# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **US (FDA)** for Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection, solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) provided in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses administered every 3 or 4 hours (and never less frequently than every 6 hours) via intravenous injection or infusion. 
    - Subsequent daily maintenance doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. Doses as high as 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration should occur as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Baseline Assessment:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating parenteral therapy.
- **Ongoing Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs.
- **Dose Adjustment (ESRD):** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation is preservative-free.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, often within minutes of intravenous administration. Most reported cases occurred in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks versus benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine (both oral and intravenous) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution):** While this editorial focuses on the injection, it is noted that safety for the *oral* formulation has not been established in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed. Less frequent reactions include body odor and gastritis.
- **ESRD-Specific Experience (≥5% Frequency):** In clinical trials, the following were reported in dialysis patients regardless of causality:
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), headache (3–37%), fever, and flu syndrome.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), and arrhythmia.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), abdominal pain (5–21%), and vomiting (9–21%).
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough (10–18%), and dyspnea.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dosage adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in provided source [US].
    - Animal reproductive studies (rats and rabbits) using 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. The benefits of maternal therapy should be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders and ESRD are provided on a mg/kg basis and apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients; however, caution is advised regarding metabolite accumulation if oral forms are used (see Section 5) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and ATP production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are then rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetics:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed that mean pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels increased from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** In a study (n≈30 per group), doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg administered three times weekly post-dialysis resulted in dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels at 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
- **Special Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].
