# Editorial Product Information: Atazanavir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Prescriber Notice:** This document is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, current Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction when prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Atazanavir (as sulfate).
- **Chemical Class:** Azapeptide HIV-1 protease inhibitor (PI).
- **Brand Names:** **ATAZANAVIR MYL** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Hard-shell gelatin capsules: 150 mg, 200 mg, and 300 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd (Mylan) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated for the treatment of HIV-1 infection in combination with other antiretroviral agents. This indication is supported by analyses of plasma HIV-1 RNA levels and CD4 cell counts from controlled clinical trials [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Must be taken **whole** and **with food** to enhance bioavailability.
- **Requirement for Ritonavir:** Atazanavir without ritonavir is not recommended for treatment-experienced patients with prior virologic failure [Australian].

### Adult Dosing
- **Therapy-Naïve Patients:** 
    - **Atazanavir** 400 mg once daily, OR 
    - **Atazanavir** 300 mg plus **ritonavir** 100 mg once daily.
- **Therapy-Experienced Patients:** 
    - **Atazanavir** 300 mg plus **ritonavir** 100 mg once daily.

### Concomitant Therapy Adjustments [Australian]
- **Tenofovir (DF):** Administer **atazanavir** 300 mg with **ritonavir** 100 mg and **tenofovir** 300 mg (all as a single daily dose with food).
- **Efavirenz (Naïve Patients):** Co-administration is generally not recommended. If unavoidable, increase **atazanavir** to 400 mg with **ritonavir** 100 mg (single dose with food) and **efavirenz** 600 mg (on an empty stomach, preferably at bedtime).
- **H2-Receptor Antagonists (Naïve):** Dose should not exceed famotidine 40 mg equivalent BID. Administer **atazanavir** 300 mg / **ritonavir** 100 mg simultaneously with or at least 10 hours after the H2-antagonist.
- **Proton-Pump Inhibitors (Naïve):** Dose should not exceed omeprazole 20 mg equivalent daily. Administer **atazanavir** 300 mg / **ritonavir** 100 mg approximately 12 hours after the PPI.

### Missed Doses
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **atazanavir** or any excipients (e.g., lactose monohydrate) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe hepatic insufficiency (Child-Pugh Class C) [Australian].
- **Contraindicated Drug Combinations (due to potential for serious toxicity or loss of efficacy):**
    - **Rifampicin**
    - **Lurasidone** (when boosted with ritonavir)
    - **Simvastatin** or **lovastatin**
    - **Lomitapide**
    - **Alfuzosin**
    - **Salmeterol**
    - **Cisapride, pimozide**
    - **Midazolam** (oral), **triazolam**
    - **Ergot derivatives** (e.g., ergotamine, dihydroergotamine)
    - **St. John’s wort** (*Hypericum perforatum*)
    - **Elbasvir/grazoprevir** or **glecaprevir/pibrentasvir**
    - **Sildenafil** when used for pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hyperbilirubinemia and Jaundice:** Most patients experience asymptomatic elevations in indirect (unconjugated) bilirubin. This is reversible upon discontinuation. **Atazanavir** should be used with caution in patients with underlying hepatitis B or C [Australian].
- **Cardiac Conduction Effects:** **Atazanavir** prolongs the PR interval. Use with caution in patients with pre-existing conduction system disease or those taking other drugs that prolong the PR interval (e.g., certain beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, digoxin). Rare cases of second-degree AV block have been reported [Australian].
- **Dermatological Reactions:** Rash occurs in approximately 21% of patients, typically mild-to-moderate maculopapular eruptions within the first 3 weeks. However, severe reactions including **Stevens-Johnson syndrome** and **DRESS** have been reported; discontinue if severe rash develops [Australian].
- **Nephrolithiasis and Cholelithiasis:** Reported during post-marketing surveillance. Temporary interruption or discontinuation may be necessary if signs/symptoms occur [Australian].
- **Lactic Acidosis:** Cases (sometimes fatal) have occurred when used with nucleoside analogues. Risk factors include female gender and obesity [Australian].
- **Metabolic Effects:** Associated with fat redistribution (lipodystrophy), new-onset diabetes, and hyperglycaemia [Australian].
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome:** Inflammatory responses to residual opportunistic infections (e.g., MAC, CMV, PCP, TB) may occur during the initial treatment phase [Australian].
- **Hemophilia:** Increased spontaneous bleeding has been reported in patients with Type A and B hemophilia receiving PIs [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequencies in Adults
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea (20%), jaundice (13%–19%), diarrhoea (10%), headache, scleral icterus [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, vomiting, dyspepsia, rash, fatigue, asthenia, lipodystrophy syndrome, insomnia, dizziness [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Peripheral neuropathy, syncope, hypertension, dyspnea, pancreatitis, hepatitis, alopecia, pruritus, arthralgia, myalgia, hematuria, nephrolithiasis, gynecomastia [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, angioedema, second- and third-degree AV block, QTc prolongation, Torsades de pointes, chronic kidney disease [Australian].

### Paediatric Considerations
- **Very Common (>10% in 6–18 year olds):** Cough (21%), fever (18%), jaundice/scleral icterus (15%), rash (14%), vomiting (12%) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** **Atazanavir** is a substrate and inhibitor of **CYP3A4** and an inhibitor of **UGT1A1**. It is also a weak inhibitor of **CYP2C8** [Australian].
- **Acid-Lowering Agents:** **Atazanavir** solubility decreases as pH increases. Reduced plasma concentrations occur with antacids, H2-antagonists, and PPIs. Refer to Section 3 for dosing separation requirements [Australian].
- **Direct-Acting Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs):** Concomitant use with ritonavir-boosted **atazanavir** may increase exposures of **betrixaban, dabigatran, edoxaban, rivaroxaban,** and **apixaban**, increasing bleeding risk [Australian].
- **Phosphodiesterase (PDE5) Inhibitors:** Significant increases in concentration; use reduced doses (e.g., **sildenafil** 25 mg every 48 hours; **tadalafil** 10 mg every 72 hours) [Australian].
- **Antiepileptics:** **Carbamazepine, phenytoin,** and **phenobarbital** may decrease **atazanavir** levels. Boosted **atazanavir** is recommended if these are used [Australian].
- **Antimycobacterials:** **Rifabutin** dose should be reduced by up to 75% [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B2** [Australian].
    - Should be used during pregnancy only if potential benefits justify the risks. **Atazanavir** 300 mg/ritonavir 100 mg may provide insufficient exposure in the 2nd/3rd trimester; 400 mg/100 mg is recommended in treatment-experienced pregnant women in these stages [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Atazanavir** is detected in human milk. Mothers should be instructed **not to breast-feed** to avoid HIV transmission and potential serious adverse reactions in infants [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effects on mating or fertility observed in animal studies (rats) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Recommended for patients **6 to 18 years of age** weighing at least 15 kg. **Contraindicated** in infants below **3 months of age** due to the risk of kernicterus [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available; clinical experience has not identified differences in response [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for patients with renal impairment or those on hemodialysis (if treatment-naïve). Should **not** be used in treatment-experienced patients with end-stage renal disease managed by hemodialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in mild-to-moderate insufficiency (Child-Pugh A and B). Consider dose reduction to 300 mg QD for Class B. **Contraindicated** in severe impairment (Class C). Boosted **atazanavir** is not recommended in moderate-to-severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selectively inhibits HIV-1 protease, preventing the processing of viral gag-pol proteins and the formation of mature infectious virions [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is ~2.5 hours. Nonlinear PK (greater than dose-proportional increase in AUC). Significant food effect (70% increase in AUC with a light meal) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 86% protein bound (binds to AAG and albumin) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatically metabolised by **CYP3A4** [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is ~7 hours (unboosted) and ~18 hours (boosted) in healthy volunteers. Excreted mainly in faeces (79%) and urine (13%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study AI424-138:** A 96-week study comparing **atazanavir/ritonavir** (300/100 mg) to **lopinavir/ritonavir** in naïve patients. At 96 weeks, **atazanavir/ritonavir** demonstrated non-inferiority with 74% vs 68% achieving HIV RNA < 50 copies/mL [Australian].
- **Study AI424-034:** Compared **atazanavir** (400 mg) to **efavirenz** (600 mg) in naïve patients over 48 weeks. Responders (< 400 c/mL): 67% (**atazanavir**) vs 63% (**efavirenz**) [Australian].
- **Study AI424-045:** Conducted in treatment-experienced patients, showing non-inferiority of **atazanavir/ritonavir** vs **lopinavir/ritonavir** [Australian].
