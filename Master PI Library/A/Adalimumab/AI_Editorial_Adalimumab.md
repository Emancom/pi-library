# Editorial Product Information: Adalimumab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of adalimumab for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult the current approved PI in their specific region before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Adalimumab (recombinant human IgG1 monoclonal antibody).
- **Chemical Class:** Tumour Necrosis Factor (TNF) blocker.
- **Brand Names:** **ABRILADA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AB04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Solution for injection in single-dose pre-filled syringe: 20 mg/0.4 mL, 40 mg/0.8 mL [Australian].
  - Solution for injection in single-dose pre-filled pen: 40 mg/0.8 mL [Australian].
  - Solution for injection in single-dose vial: 40 mg/0.8 mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Adalimumab is indicated for the treatment of various immune-mediated inflammatory diseases:
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):** Moderate to severely active RA in adults, including those recently diagnosed and methotrexate-naïve [Australian].
- **Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA):** 
  - Polyarticular JIA in patients ≥2 years with inadequate response to DMARDs [Australian].
  - Enthesitis-related arthritis in children with inadequate response to conventional therapy [Australian].
- **Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA):** Moderate to severely active PsA in adults with inadequate DMARD response [Australian].
- **Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS):** Active AS in adults [Australian].
- **Crohn’s Disease (CD):** Moderate to severe CD in adults and children (≥6 years) to induce/maintain remission [Australian].
- **Ulcerative Colitis (UC):** Moderate to severe UC in adults with inadequate response to conventional therapy [Australian].
- **Psoriasis:** 
  - Moderate to severe chronic plaque psoriasis in adults [Australian].
  - Severe chronic plaque psoriasis in children/adolescents (from 4 years) [Australian].
- **Hidradenitis Suppurativa (HS):** Active moderate to severe HS in adults and adolescents (from 12 years) [Australian].
- **Uveitis:** Non-infectious intermediate, posterior, and pan-uveitis in adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **RA, PsA, AS:** 40 mg fortnightly.
- **Crohn’s Disease / Ulcerative Colitis:** Induction with 160 mg at Day 0 (as four injections in one day or two per day for two days), followed by 80 mg at Day 14. Maintenance: 40 mg fortnightly starting Day 28 [Australian].
- **Psoriasis / Uveitis:** Initial dose of 80 mg, followed by 40 mg fortnightly starting one week after the initial dose [Australian].
- **Hidradenitis Suppurativa:** 160 mg at Day 0, 80 mg at Day 15, then 40 mg weekly or 80 mg fortnightly starting Day 29 [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Administered via subcutaneous injection into the thigh or abdomen.
- Rotate injection sites. Avoid areas where the skin is tender, bruised, red, or hard.
- Patients may self-inject after proper training and physician determination of appropriateness [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to adalimumab or any excipients [Australian].
- Severe infections, including sepsis, active tuberculosis (TB), and opportunistic infections [Australian].
- Concurrent administration with anakinra (IL-1 receptor antagonist) [Australian].
- Moderate to severe heart failure (NYHA Class III/IV) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infections:** Increased susceptibility to serious infections (bacterial, mycobacterial, fungal, viral). Therapy should not be initiated in patients with active infections [Australian].
- **Tuberculosis:** All patients must be screened for active or latent TB prior to initiation. Prophylaxis for latent TB must be started before adalimumab therapy [Australian].
- **Hepatitis B Reactivation:** Reactivation has occurred in chronic carriers; monitor carriers closely during and after therapy [Australian].
- **Malignancies:** Increased risk of lymphoma and other malignancies. Rare cases of hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTCL) reported, particularly in young adults with IBD using concomitant azathioprine or 6-mercaptopurine [Australian].
- **Neurologic Events:** Rare cases of demyelinating disease (e.g., Multiple Sclerosis, Guillain-Barré syndrome). Exercise caution in patients with pre-existing demyelinating disorders [Australian].
- **Hematologic:** Rare reports of pancytopenia/aplastic anaemia. Advise patients to seek immediate medical attention for signs of blood dyscrasias [Australian].
- **Congestive Heart Failure:** Use with caution in mild heart failure (NYHA I/II); discontinue if symptoms worsen [Australian].
- **Vaccinations:** Live vaccines should be avoided during treatment and for 5 months after the last dose in infants exposed in utero [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Respiratory tract infections (upper/lower), injection site reactions (erythema, itching, pain, swelling), headache, musculoskeletal pain, elevated lipids, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, and rash [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Systemic infections (sepsis, influenza), skin cancer (basal/squamous cell), leucopenia, anaemia, hypertension, elevated liver enzymes (ALT/AST), and pruritus [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Opportunistic infections, TB, lymphoma, melanoma, arrhythmia, and congestive heart failure [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Pancytopenia, cardiac arrest, and multiple sclerosis [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Serious infections (TB, sepsis), malignancies (lymphoma, HSTCL), and demyelinating disorders [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anakinra / Abatacept:** Concurrent use is not recommended due to increased risk of serious infections [Australian].
- **Methotrexate:** No dose adjustment required for either drug. Methotrexate reduces adalimumab clearance by 29–44% but does not require dosage modification [Australian].
- **Live Vaccines:** Contraindicated during treatment [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Adalimumab crosses the placenta and may affect the immune response of the newborn. Use only if clearly needed. Women of childbearing potential should use contraception for at least 5 months after the last dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in human milk at very low concentrations (0.1% to 1% of maternal serum levels). Systemic exposure to the infant is expected to be low as the protein is likely degraded in the GI tract. Use requires consideration of the mother's clinical need vs. potential infant risk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Effect on human fertility has not been investigated [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for Polyarticular JIA (≥2 years), Enthesitis-related arthritis (≥6 years), Crohn’s Disease (≥6 years), Psoriasis (≥4 years), and HS (≥12 years). Dosing is generally weight-based [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** Higher incidence of serious infections and malignancies compared to younger populations. Exercise caution [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Adalimumab has not been studied in these populations; no dosage recommendations available [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Specific binding to TNF-alpha, neutralising its biological function by blocking interaction with p55 and p75 cell surface TNF receptors. It also modulates biological responses induced by TNF, such as leukocyte migration [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Slow absorption; peak serum concentration (Cmax) reached approximately 5 days after a 40 mg SC dose. Bioavailability is ~64% [Australian].
  - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution (Vss) ranges from 4.7 to 6.0 L. Synovial fluid concentrations are 31–96% of serum levels [Australian].
  - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 2 weeks (range 10–20 days). Clearance is generally <12 mL/h [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Biosimilarity:** The comparability of ABRILADA to the reference product (Humira) was demonstrated in Study B5381002 (RA patients) and Study B5381007 (PK similarity in healthy volunteers). No clinically meaningful differences in efficacy, safety, or immunogenicity were observed [Australian].
- **Pivotal RA Trials:** Studies RA I-V established efficacy using ACR20/50/70 response rates and radiographic retardation of structural damage [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- Keep in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Room Temperature Storage:** A single syringe, pen, or vial may be stored at up to 30°C for a maximum of 30 days. Discard if not used within this period [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
