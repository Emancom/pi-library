# Editorial Product Information: Angiotensin II

## Editorial Note
- This editorial was developed using official Product Information from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local regulatory documents for full prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Angiotensin II (synthetic human peptide).
- **Brand Name(s):** Angiotensin II Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** C01CA05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injection: 2.5 mg/mL (equivalent to 2.9 mg angiotensin II acetate) in a 1 mL single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion (must be diluted prior to use).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Gland Pharma Limited [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Distributive Shock:** To increase blood pressure in adults with septic or other distributive shock [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** 20 nanograms (ng)/kg/min via continuous intravenous infusion.
- **Titration:** Monitor blood pressure response and titrate every 5 minutes by increments of up to 15 ng/kg/min to achieve or maintain target blood pressure.
- **Maximum Dosing:** 
    - During the first 3 hours of treatment: Do not exceed **80 ng/kg/min**.
    - Maintenance dose: Should not exceed **40 ng/kg/min**.
- **Minimum Dosing:** Doses as low as 1.25 ng/kg/min may be utilised.
- **Discontinuation:** Once the underlying shock has sufficiently improved, down-titrate every 5 to 15 minutes by increments of up to 15 ng/kg/min based on blood pressure.

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted in 0.9% sodium chloride to a final concentration of either 5,000 ng/mL (standard) or 10,000 ng/mL (for fluid-restricted patients).
- **Route:** Administration through a central venous line is recommended [US].
- **Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before administration. The solution should be clear and aqueous.

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Risk of Thrombosis:** There is a significant potential for venous and arterial thrombotic and thromboembolic events. In clinical trials, the incidence was 13% in the treatment group versus 5% in the placebo group. The most common imbalance was deep vein thrombosis (DVT). 
- **Prophylaxis:** Concurrent venous thromboembolism (VTE) prophylaxis is recommended [US].
- **Monitoring:** Continuous blood pressure monitoring is required during administration.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) Inhibitors:** Concomitant use may **increase** the patient’s response to Angiotensin II [US].
- **Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers (ARB):** Concomitant use may **decrease** the patient’s response to Angiotensin II [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** No sufficient data exist on Angiotensin II use in pregnant women to determine drug-associated risks. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted.
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Septic or distributive shock is a medical emergency; delaying treatment in pregnant women increases the risk of maternal and fetal morbidity and mortality.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if Angiotensin II is excreted in human milk. No data are available regarding effects on the breastfed child or milk production. 
- **Fertility:** Animal fertility studies have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** In clinical trials, 48% of patients were aged 65 years or older. No significant differences in safety or efficacy were observed between older and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance is not dependent on renal function; therefore, pharmacokinetics are not expected to be influenced by renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is not dependent on hepatic function; therefore, pharmacokinetics are not expected to be influenced by hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A naturally occurring peptide hormone of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS). It causes vasoconstriction and increased aldosterone release by binding to the G-protein coupled-angiotensin II receptor type 1 (AT1) on vascular smooth muscle, stimulating Ca²⁺/calmodulin-dependent phosphorylation of myosin.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Median time to reach target Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) is approximately 5 minutes.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised by aminopeptidase A and ACE2 into smaller fragments (angiotensin III and angiotensin 1-7).
    - **Elimination:** The plasma half-life of intravenous Angiotensin II is less than one minute.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ATHOS-3 Trial:** A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study (n=321) in adults with septic or other distributive shock remaining hypotensive despite fluid and vasopressor therapy.
- **Primary Outcome:** 70% of patients treated with Angiotensin II achieved the target MAP (≥ 75 mmHg or a ≥ 10 mmHg increase without increasing baseline vasopressors) at 3 hours, compared to 23% of placebo patients (p < 0.0001).
- **Secondary Outcome:** 28-day mortality was 46% for Angiotensin II vs 54% for placebo (hazard ratio 0.78).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Diluted Solution:** May be stored at room temperature or under refrigeration. Discard any prepared solution after 24 hours.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard unused portion.
