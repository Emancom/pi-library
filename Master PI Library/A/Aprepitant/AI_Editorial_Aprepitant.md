# Editorial Product Information: Aprepitant

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational use by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Aprepitant (a substance P neurokinin 1 [NK1] receptor antagonist).
- **Brand Name(s):** **APREPITANT LUPIN** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A04AD12.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Hard gelatin capsules: 40 mg (yellow/white), 80 mg (white), 125 mg (pink/white), and 165 mg (blue/white) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Sponsor:** Southern Cross Pharma Pty Ltd; Distributed by Generic Health Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (CINV):** Prevention of acute and delayed nausea and vomiting associated with initial and repeat courses of highly emetogenic (HEC) and moderately emetogenic (MEC) cancer chemotherapy. It is used as part of a multi-agent antiemetic regimen [Australian].
- **Postoperative Nausea and Vomiting (PONV):** Prevention of nausea and vomiting following surgical procedures [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Aprepitant** may be taken with or without food.
- **Special Instruction (165 mg dose):** Administration with a high-fat meal increases systemic exposure by 47%. It is recommended to take the 165 mg dose with or without a light (low-fat) meal [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **CINV (1-Day Regimen):** 165 mg orally approximately 1 hour prior to chemotherapy on Day 1 only [Australian].
- **CINV (3-Day Regimen):** 
    - Day 1: 125 mg orally 1 hour prior to chemotherapy.
    - Days 2 and 3: 80 mg orally once daily in the morning [Australian].
- **PONV:** 40 mg orally within 3 hours prior to the induction of anaesthesia [Australian].

### Co-administration with Corticosteroids
Due to pharmacokinetic interactions, the dose of concomitant corticosteroids (e.g., **dexamethasone**, **methylprednisolone**) must be reduced (typically by 50% for oral dexamethasone) when used with the 3-day or 165 mg aprepitant regimens [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **aprepitant** or any excipients in the formulation.
- **Concurrent Drug Therapy:** Contraindicated with **pimozide**, **terfenadine**, **astemizole**, or **cisapride**. Inhibition of CYP3A4 by **aprepitant** may lead to elevated plasma concentrations of these drugs, potentially resulting in serious or life-threatening reactions [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **CYP3A4 Interaction:** **Aprepitant** is a dose-dependent inhibitor of CYP3A4. Caution is required when co-administering with oral drugs primarily metabolised via CYP3A4. The inhibitory effect is greater for oral vs. intravenous substrates [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Co-administration may result in a clinically significant decrease in INR. In patients on chronic **warfarin** therapy, INR should be closely monitored for 2 weeks following each **aprepitant** course, particularly at days 7–10 [Australian].
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** The efficacy of hormonal contraceptives may be reduced during and for 28 days after **aprepitant** administration. Patients should use alternative or back-up contraceptive methods during and for one month after the last dose [Australian].
- **Hereditary Issues:** Capsules contain sugars; patients with rare fructose intolerance, glucose-galactose malabsorption, or sucrase-isomaltase insufficiency should avoid this medicine [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse effects are mild to moderate in intensity.
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - **CINV:** Hiccups (4.6%), ALT increased (2.8%), dyspepsia (2.6%), constipation (2.4%), headache (2.0%), decreased appetite (2.0%), fatigue (1.4%).
    - **PONV:** Constipation (8.5%), nausea (8.5%), pruritus (7.6%), pyrexia (5.9%), hypotension (5.7%), headache (5.0%), bradycardia (4.4%) [Australian].
- **Uncommon to Rare (<1%):** 
    - Febrile neutropenia, palpitations, hot flush, cough, dysuria, and various gastrointestinal disturbances (e.g., dry mouth, flatulence).
- **Serious Post-marketing Reactions:** 
    - Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), angioedema, urticaria, and anaphylactic reactions [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** **Aprepitant** (125mg/80mg or 165mg) is a moderate inhibitor. It increases AUC of **dexamethasone** (2.2-fold) and **midazolam** (up to 3.3-fold). Caution with narrow therapeutic index drugs like **cyclosporine**, **sirolimus**, and **tacrolimus** [Australian].
- **CYP2C9 Induction:** **Aprepitant** induces CYP2C9, reducing concentrations of **S-warfarin** and **tolbutamide** [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., **ketoconazole**) increase **aprepitant** levels 5-fold. Strong inducers (e.g., **rifampicin**) decrease **aprepitant** levels 11-fold [Australian].
- **Other:** No clinically significant interaction with **digoxin** or **5-HT3 antagonists** (ondansetron, granisetron) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits showed no evidence of foetal harm at 1.5 times human exposure. Use only if clearly needed as human data is lacking [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Aprepitant** is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is generally not recommended during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effects on mating performance or fertility were observed in rat studies at doses up to 1000 mg/kg twice daily [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** No dosage adjustment is necessary. Bioavailability is slightly higher in the elderly, but not clinically meaningful [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required for severe renal insufficiency or end-stage renal disease (ESRD) on haemodialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild to moderate impairment (Child-Pugh 5–9). No clinical data available for severe impairment (Child-Pugh >9) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective antagonist of human substance P neurokinin-1 (NK1) receptors. It has little to no affinity for dopamine or serotonin (5-HT3) receptors. It acts centrally to inhibit emesis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 60–65%. Peak plasma concentration (Tmax) occurs at approximately 4 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** >95% protein bound; crosses the blood-brain barrier. Volume of distribution (Vdss) is ~66 L [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 oxidation; minor metabolism via CYP1A2 and CYP2C19 [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via metabolism; not renally excreted. Terminal half-life ranges from 9 to 13 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **HEC/MEC:** Trials demonstrated that the addition of **aprepitant** to a regimen of **ondansetron** and **dexamethasone** significantly improved "Complete Response" (no emesis and no rescue therapy) compared to standard therapy alone. Effectiveness was maintained over multiple cycles of chemotherapy [Australian].
- **PONV:** Two trials in patients undergoing open abdominal surgery showed **aprepitant** 40 mg was superior to **ondansetron** 4 mg IV in preventing vomiting within the first 24–48 hours post-surgery [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in the original packaging to protect from moisture [Australian].
