# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview of **Levocarnitine Injection** for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the approved labeling in their specific region before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - Administration should occur as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough plasma concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial [US].

### Monitoring
- For metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels, followed by weekly and monthly monitoring.
- The target plasma free carnitine concentration is generally between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, therapy must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical management initiated [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While this applies primarily to high-dose **oral** levocarnitine, clinicians should be aware that in patients with severely compromised renal function, chronic administration may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in the urine [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In ESRD patients, trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels should be monitored to guide dose adjustments. Normal concentrations are typically 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in clinical trials (specifically in ESRD patients on hemodialysis) with a frequency ≥5% include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), chest pain (6–15%), headache (3–37%), fever (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%), asthenia (8–12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%), insomnia (3–6%), depression (5–6%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).

**Post-Marketing Experience:**
- **Serious Events:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].
- **Neurological:** Seizures (new onset or increased frequency) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended that INR levels be monitored upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins, suggesting a low potential for protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Pregnancy Category not specified in current labeling, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.] Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. The risks to the infant of excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No long-term animal studies have been performed to evaluate carcinogenic potential; however, mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, S. cerevisiae) were negative [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have been established for the treatment of carnitine deficiency. Dosing is based on body weight (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data for the elderly are not detailed in the source document; however, clinical monitoring is advised as per standard protocols for renal and metabolic management.
- **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients on hemodialysis, IV levocarnitine is indicated to replace dialytic losses. However, high-dose **oral** levocarnitine is NOT recommended in patients with severely compromised renal function due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are quickly eliminated. This helps alleviate autointoxication and metabolic acidosis [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Following a single IV dose, approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8 [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** In studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses, mean trough concentrations at 24 weeks were 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L respectively. While carnitine levels increased, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of an opened vial as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC plastic bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
