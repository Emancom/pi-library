# Editorial Product Information: Atracurium Besilate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Atracurium besilate; a highly selective, competitive, non-depolarising neuromuscular blocking agent.
- **Brand Names:** Atracurium Besilate Medsurge [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Solution for injection: 10 mg/mL atracurium besilate [Australian].
    - Available in 2.5 mL (25 mg) and 5 mL (50 mg) glass ampoules [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) injection or infusion only. **DO NOT ADMINISTER INTRAMUSCULARLY** due to the risk of tissue irritation and lack of clinical data [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Medsurge Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Adjunct to general anaesthesia to facilitate tracheal intubation [Australian].
- Skeletal muscle relaxation during surgical procedures or controlled ventilation [Australian].
- Facilitation of mechanical ventilation in patients within the Intensive Care Unit (ICU) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Atracurium must only be administered after unconsciousness has been induced to avoid patient distress. It must not be mixed with alkaline solutions (e.g., barbiturates) in the same syringe or administered through the same needle, as inactivation or precipitation may occur [Australian].

### Adult Dosing
- **Initial Bolus (Intubation):** 0.3 to 0.6 mg/kg IV. This typically provides adequate relaxation for 15 to 35 minutes.
- **Maintenance Bolus:** 0.08 to 0.10 mg/kg is recommended during prolonged procedures. The first maintenance dose is usually required 20 to 45 minutes after the initial bolus.
- **Continuous Infusion:** 
    - Initial rate of 9 to 10 µg/kg/min may be needed to restore block.
    - Maintenance rate of 5 to 9 µg/kg/min (range 2 to 15 µg/kg/min) is generally sufficient to maintain 89% to 99% blockade under balanced anaesthesia [Australian].

### Special Procedural Dosing
- **Cardiopulmonary Bypass:** Standard infusion rates apply. During induced hypothermia (25–26°C), the rate of inactivation is reduced; full block may be maintained at approximately half the original infusion rate [Australian].
- **Following Suxamethonium:** 0.3 to 0.4 mg/kg is recommended after the patient has recovered from suxamethonium-induced block [Australian].

### Reversal
- Blockade can be reversed using an anticholinesterase (e.g., neostigmine) in combination with an anticholinergic (e.g., atropine).
- Reversal is typically attempted 20–35 minutes after the initial bolus or 10–30 minutes after a maintenance dose, once muscle twitch recovery has commenced.
- Complete reversal usually occurs within 8–10 minutes [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to atracurium, cisatracurium, or benzenesulfonic acid [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Respiratory Paralysis:** Atracurium paralyses respiratory and skeletal muscles without affecting consciousness. It must only be used by clinicians skilled in airway management where facilities for endotracheal intubation and positive pressure ventilation are immediately available [Australian].
- **Histamine Release:** Has the potential to cause histamine release, which may lead to life-threatening anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions. Use with caution in patients with a history of asthma or increased sensitivity to histamine [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Patients with significant cardiovascular disease may be more sensitive to hypotensive episodes. Initial doses (0.3–0.4 mg/kg) should be administered slowly or in divided doses over one minute [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Disease:** Use with extreme caution and reduced dosage in patients with myasthenia gravis, Eaton-Lambert syndrome, or other neuromuscular disorders. Use of a peripheral nerve stimulator is essential [Australian].
- **Burn Patients:** Resistance to non-depolarising agents may develop; increased doses may be required based on the time since the injury and the extent of the burn [Australian].
- **Malignant Hyperthermia (MH):** While rare with atracurium, clinicians should remain prepared to treat MH in any patient undergoing general anaesthesia [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Histamine-Related Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** Skin flushing (frequency increases with dose: 1% at <0.3 mg/kg up to 29% at ≥0.6 mg/kg), transient hypotension (1% to 14% depending on dose) [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Bronchospasm (approx. 0.4%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, bradycardia, and hypertension (incidence approx. 1%) [Australian].
- **Integumentary:** Rash, urticaria, and injection site reactions [Australian].
- **Respiratory:** Dyspnoea, laryngospasm [Australian].
- **Neurological/ICU Specific:** Seizures have been reported in ICU patients receiving atracurium; however, these patients often had predisposing factors (e.g., cranial trauma, uraemia). A causal link to atracurium or its metabolite, laudanosine, has not been established [Australian].
- **General:** Rare instances of severe anaphylactoid reactions, including cardiac arrest [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Potentiating Agents:** 
    - Inhalation anaesthetics (isoflurane, enflurane increase potency by ~35%; halothane ~20%).
    - Antibiotics (aminoglycosides, polymyxins, tetracyclines, clindamycin).
    - Antiarrhythmics (propranolol, calcium channel blockers, lidocaine, procainamide, quinidine).
    - Diuretics (furosemide, mannitol, thiazides).
    - Others: Magnesium sulfate, lithium salts, ketamine, and ganglion blocking agents (trimetaphan) [Australian].
- **Anticonvulsants:** Chronic therapy may result in a longer onset time and shorter duration of blockade [Australian].
- **Other Muscle Relaxants:** Prior suxamethonium quickens the onset and increases the depth of atracurium block but does not prolong its duration [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category C. Atracurium crosses the placenta to a limited degree. It should only be used if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the foetus.
- **Obstetrics:** Used in Caesarean sections (0.3 mg/kg) without harmful effects on newborns; however, the potential for respiratory depression in the neonate must be considered [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if atracurium or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - ≥2 years: Adult dosing applies.
    - 1 month to 2 years: 0.4 mg/kg initial dose (under halothane). Maintenance doses may be required more frequently than in adults.
    - <1 month: Safety and efficacy not established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Standard doses may be used, though it is recommended to use the lower end of the dosing range and administer slowly [Australian].
- **Renal and Hepatic Impairment:** Atracurium can be used at standard doses at all levels of renal or hepatic function, including end-stage failure, due to its non-organ dependent elimination [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive antagonism of acetylcholine at the cholinergic receptor sites on the motor end-plate [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via Hofmann elimination (non-enzymatic chemical process at physiological pH and temperature) and ester hydrolysis by non-specific esterases. 
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 20 minutes. 
    - **Protein Binding:** Approximately 82%.
    - **Metabolism:** Laudanosine is a major metabolite; it possesses no neuromuscular blocking activity but has shown CNS excitatory effects in animal studies at high doses [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** ED95 averages 0.23 mg/kg. Onset of maximum block occurs within 3 to 5 minutes. Duration is approximately one-third to one-half that of d-tubocurarine or pancuronium at equipotent doses [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **General Surgery:** Trials in 875 patients established the dose-response relationship for flushing and hypotension [Australian].
- **ICU Use:** Limited safety/efficacy data (n=58) for long-term infusion (median 90 hours). Recovery to a train-of-four ratio >0.75 occurred in approximately 60 minutes (range 32–108 min) regardless of infusion duration [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Do not freeze. Protect from light.**
- **Shelf Life Extension:** Once removed from refrigeration, ampoules may be stored below 25°C for up to 1 week; discard if not used within this period [Australian].
- **Dilution Stability:** When diluted to ≥0.5 mg/mL, it is stable in daylight at up to 25°C in 0.9% saline (24 hours), 5% glucose (8 hours), or Ringer's solution (8 hours) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine – Schedule 4 [Australian].
