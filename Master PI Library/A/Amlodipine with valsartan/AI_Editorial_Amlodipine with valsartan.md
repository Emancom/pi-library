# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a structured format for quick reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection, solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - Dosing interval: Every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - Maximum recorded dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Titration: Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC plastic bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
- Single-dose vials: Discard any unused portion [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with pre-existing conditions, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **TMA Accumulation:** Chronic high-dose oral administration (not specific to IV) in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD Trials)
The following events were reported in clinical trials of ESRD patients, though causality was not necessarily established [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), accidental injury (10%), asthenia (9%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), nausea (8%), vomiting (15%).
- **Respiratory:** Cough increased (9%), dyspnea (7%), pharyngitis (23%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%).

### Post-Marketing and Serious Effects
- **Seizures:** Reported in both oral and IV administration [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when co-administered with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in the provided source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
    - **Clinical Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are supported by the general dosage recommendations; however, specific age-range exclusions were not noted in the source [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** The drug is primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. High doses of oral formulations should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis), trough concentrations increased from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While carnitine levels increase significantly, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].

- **Legal Classification:**
