# Editorial Product Information: Albendazole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of albendazole for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines. Prescribers must consult the current, locally approved PI for definitive guidance in their specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Albendazole (a benzimidazole derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** Eskazole [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** P02CA03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 400 mg chewable tablets [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hydatid Disease (Echinococcosis):** Treatment of hydatid cysts caused by *Echinococcus granulosus* in adults and children over 6 years of age when surgery is not feasible. Also indicated as an adjunct to surgery (pre-operatively or post-operatively) [Australian].
- **Larval Taeniasis (Neurocysticercosis):** Treatment of *Taenia solium* infection with neurological involvement [Australian].
- **Capillaria philippinensis:** Treatment of infection caused by *C. philippinensis* [Australian].
- **Intestinal Helminth Infections:** Short-term treatment of various intestinal helminths [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Tablets may be crushed, chewed, or swallowed whole. 
- **Food Effect:** Albendazole **must be taken with a meal** to increase systemic bioavailability for the treatment of tissue helminth infections (approximately 5-fold increase in absorption) [Australian].

### Hydatid Disease
- **Adults (≥60 kg):** 800 mg daily in two divided doses (400 mg twice daily) for 28 days.
- **Patients <60 kg and Children (≥6 years):** 15 mg/kg/day in two divided doses (maximum 800 mg/day).
- **Cycle Structure:** A 28-day treatment course followed by a 14-day drug-free interval. This may be repeated for up to three cycles. Longer treatment may be required for bone or brain cysts [Australian].
- **Surgical Adjunct:** Two 28-day cycles are recommended pre-operatively. If emergency surgery is required, a post-operative course of two 28-day cycles (separated by 14 drug-free days) should be administered [Australian].

### Neurocysticercosis
- **Adults (≥60 kg):** 800 mg daily in two divided doses for 7 to 28 days, depending on clinical response.
- **Patients <60 kg and Children (≥6 years):** 15 mg/kg/day in two divided doses.
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Corticosteroids (oral or IV) and anticonvulsants should be administered to prevent cerebral hypertensive episodes and inflammatory reactions during the first week of treatment [Australian].

### Capillaria philippinensis
- **Adults:** 400 mg once daily for 10 days [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Albendazole is contraindicated during pregnancy or in women suspected to be pregnant [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to albendazole, other benzimidazoles, or any excipients [Australian].
- **Age:** Not recommended for children under 6 years of age due to limited clinical experience [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Approximately 16% of patients experience mild-to-moderate liver enzyme elevations. Severe, potentially irreversible hepatocellular damage and jaundice have occurred. **Monitoring:** Liver function tests (LFTs) must be performed at the start of each cycle and every two weeks during treatment. Discontinue if enzymes exceed twice the upper limit of normal [Australian].
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** Albendazole can cause leucopenia, pancytopenia, and aplastic anaemia. **Monitoring:** Complete blood counts should be performed at the start and every two weeks during each 28-day cycle. Patients with liver disease are at higher risk [Australian].
- **Neurological Effects:** Treatment of neurocysticercosis may exacerbate symptoms (e.g., seizures, headache, increased intracranial pressure) due to inflammatory reactions following parasite death. Pre-existing neurocysticercosis may be "unmasked" when treating other conditions [Australian].
- **Hydatid Monitoring:** Long-term follow-up is necessary as recurrence occurs in approximately 14% of patients within 2 years [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Headache, mild-to-moderate hepatic enzyme elevations |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Leucopenia, dizziness, gastrointestinal disturbances (abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting), reversible alopecia, fever/hyperpyrexia |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Hepatitis |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Pancytopenia, thrombocytopenia, ocular maculopathy, pancreatitis, jaundice |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Aplastic anaemia, agranulocytosis, Erythema multiforme, Stevens-Johnson syndrome |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Metabolic Inducers/Inhibitors:** 
    - **Cimetidine, Praziquantel, and Dexamethasone:** Reported to increase plasma levels of the active metabolite (albendazole sulfoxide).
    - **Ritonavir, Phenytoin, Carbamazepine, and Phenobarbital:** May reduce plasma concentrations of the active metabolite, potentially reducing clinical efficacy [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Teratogenic and embryotoxic in animal studies (rats/rabbits). Females of childbearing age must have a negative pregnancy test before starting treatment and use effective contraception during and for one month after completing therapy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if albendazole or its metabolites enter human milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment and for at least one month following the final dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is not recommended in children under 6 years of age. For children ≥6 years, dosing is weight-based (15 mg/kg/day) [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No specific data available in provided sources [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use in patients with impaired renal function has not been studied [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. Patients with liver disease require more frequent blood count monitoring due to increased susceptibility to bone marrow suppression [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Albendazole exhibits larvicidal, ovicidal, and vermicidal activity. It inhibits tubulin polymerisation, disrupting helminth metabolism and leading to energy depletion, immobilisation, and death [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed from the GI tract due to low solubility. Absorption is enhanced 5-fold by fatty meals.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapid first-pass metabolism in the liver to albendazole sulfoxide (the active moiety for systemic infections).
    - **Elimination:** Albendazole sulfoxide has a plasma half-life of 8.5 hours. It is primarily eliminated in bile, with a small amount in urine. Sulfoxide levels may persist in plasma for several weeks following prolonged high-dose therapy [Australian].
