# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosage recommendations, and safety information.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their specific region.
- *Note: While the user instructions mentioned alfacalcidol, the provided source document pertains exclusively to **levocarnitine**. This editorial reflects the provided source.*

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. 
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations is recommended.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products visually for particulate matter or discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL. Stored in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C), the solution is stable for 24 hours [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of IV administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may trigger seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### General Precautions
- **Renal Impairment/ESRD:** While this product is IV, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of plasma carnitine levels is essential to prevent overtreatment or sub-therapeutic dosing.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of **≥5%** in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (regardless of causality) include [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), asthenia (8–12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).

### Post-Marketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Integumentary:** Body odor [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but noted as follows]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, no adequate studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. Risks to the child must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as in adults for metabolic disorders [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 5. Dosing in ESRD is specific to post-dialysis administration to replace dialytic losses [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria. This provides the necessary substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8. Trough concentrations remained stable or increased over 12 to 24 weeks in dose-ranging studies (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx only). NDC 36000-368-10 [US].
