## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, provided for clinical reference).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Clear, colorless sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]; Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (target: 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L) and may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at 25°C when mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].

### Precautions
- **Oral Formulation in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting. Less frequent effects include body odor and gastritis.
- **Trial-Derived Adverse Events (ESRD, ≥5% incidence):**
    - **General:** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20% vs 14% placebo), hypotension, tachycardia.
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16% vs 19% placebo), vomiting (15% vs 16% placebo), nausea (8%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis.
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitor INR levels following treatment initiation or dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but described as "Category B" equivalent]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. Weigh the risk to the child of excess carnitine intake against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight as described in Section 3.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in source; clinical experience suggests no major differences in response compared to younger adults.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. Use caution with oral formulations in this population due to metabolite accumulation (see Section 5) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Intravenous administration to ESRD patients results in significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations rose from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While efficacy in increasing carnitine levels is proven, the effect on clinical outcomes in ESRD remains to be determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat. Discard any unused portion as the formulation is preservative-free [US].
