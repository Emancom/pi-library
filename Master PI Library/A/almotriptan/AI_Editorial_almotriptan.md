# Editorial Product Information: Almotriptan

## Editorial Note
*   This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction:
    *   **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of almotriptan for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Almotriptan malate (selective 5-HT$_{1B/1D}$ receptor agonist).
*   **Brand Names:** Information not provided in source.
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **Film-coated tablets:** 6.25 mg and 12.5 mg [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ajanta Pharma USA Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Acute Treatment of Migraine (Adults):** For the treatment of migraine attacks with or without aura in patients with a history of migraine [US].
*   **Acute Treatment of Migraine (Adolescents 12–17 years):** For the treatment of migraine headache pain in patients with a history of migraine with or without aura, specifically for attacks typically lasting 4 hours or more when untreated [US].
*   **Limitations of Use:**
    *   A clear diagnosis of migraine must be established.
    *   Not indicated for the prophylactic therapy of migraine.
    *   Not indicated for the treatment of cluster headaches.
    *   Efficacy for migraine-associated symptoms (nausea, photophobia, phonophobia) has not been established in adolescents [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*   **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents 12–17 years):**
    *   **Single Dose:** 6.25 mg or 12.5 mg. The 12.5 mg dose is generally more effective in adults [US].
    *   **Repeat Dosing:** If the headache returns after initial relief, the dose may be repeated after 2 hours.
    *   **Maximum Daily Dose:** 25 mg within a 24-hour period.
*   **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be swallowed with fluids. Effectiveness of a second dose for patients who fail to respond to the first dose has not been established.
*   **Renal and Hepatic Dosing:**
    *   **Initial Dose:** 6.25 mg.
    *   **Maximum Daily Dose:** 12.5 mg [US].
*   **Treatment Frequency:** The safety of treating more than four migraines per month (30-day period) on average has not been established [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Ischemic Heart Disease (IHD):** History of or documented silent ischemia, myocardial infarction, angina pectoris, or coronary artery vasospasm (including Prinzmetal’s variant angina) [US].
*   **Cerebrovascular Syndromes:** History of stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA) [US].
*   **Peripheral Vascular Disease:** Including, but not limited to, ischemic bowel disease [US].
*   **Uncontrolled Hypertension.**
*   **Specific Migraine Types:** Hemiplegic or basilar migraine [US].
*   **Drug Interactions:**
    *   Use within 24 hours of ergotamine-containing or ergot-type medications (e.g., dihydroergotamine, methysergide).
    *   Use within 24 hours of another 5-HT$_1$ agonist (e.g., other triptans).
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to almotriptan or any inactive tablet components.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Myocardial Ischemia and Cardiac Events:** Serious cardiac events, including myocardial infarction and life-threatening arrhythmias, have been reported with triptans. Evaluation is strongly recommended for patients with cardiac risk factors (hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, smoking, obesity, diabetes, family history, post-menopausal females, or males >40 years). If evaluated as satisfactory, the first dose should be administered in a supervised medical setting [US].
*   **Chest, Throat, Neck, and Jaw Sensations:** Pain, pressure, or heaviness may occur; these are usually not of cardiac origin but must be evaluated for coronary artery disease (CAD) if symptoms suggest angina.
*   **Cerebrovascular Events:** Cerebral/subarachnoid hemorrhage and stroke (some fatal) have been reported. Triptans should not be used if the headache is atypical or suggests a serious neurological condition.
*   **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening, especially when combined with SSRIs or SNRIs. Symptoms include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular hyperactivity.
*   **Medication Overuse Headache (MOH):** Overuse (>10 days/month) may exacerbate headaches; detoxification may be required.
*   **Blood Pressure Increases:** Significant elevations in systemic blood pressure have been reported rarely.
*   **Sulfonamide Hypersensitivity:** Use with caution in patients allergic to sulfonamides due to potential cross-reactivity (almotriptan contains a sulfonyl group).
*   **Ophthalmic Effects:** Accumulation in melanin-rich tissues (e.g., the eye) has been noted in animal models. Clinical trials did not show adverse retinal effects in humans, but clinicians should be aware of potential long-term effects [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild and transient.

| Adverse Reaction | Adult Frequency (≥1% vs Placebo) | Adolescent Frequency (≥1% vs Placebo) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nausea** | 1–2% | 1–3% |
| **Paresthesia** | 1% | <1–1% |
| **Dry Mouth** | 1% | Not specified |
| **Dizziness** | <1% (infrequent) | 3–4% |
| **Somnolence** | <1% (infrequent) | <1–5% |
| **Headache** | <1% (infrequent) | 1–2% |
| **Vomiting** | <1% (infrequent) | 0–2% |

*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** Myocardial infarction, coronary artery vasospasm, ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, stroke, and peripheral/gastrointestinal vascular ischemia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Ergot-type Meds & Other Triptans:** Additive vasospastic effects; contraindicated within 24 hours of each other.
*   **Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs) / SNRIs:** Risk of serotonin syndrome.
*   **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Ketoconazole):** Co-administration with ketoconazole increases almotriptan exposure by approximately 60%. Dosing should be limited to a 6.25 mg starting dose and a 12.5 mg daily maximum when used with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors [US].
*   **MAO-A Inhibitors (e.g., Moclobemide):** Increases almotriptan C$_{max}$ and decreases clearance; however, the source states no dose adjustment is necessary [US].
*   **Other:** No significant interactions were observed with propranolol, fluoxetine (CYP2D6 inhibitor), or verapamil.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category C:** Animal studies show developmental toxicity (increased embryolethality and skeletal variations) at doses higher than clinical levels. Use only if potential benefit justifies potential fetal risk [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if almotriptan is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised. Rat studies showed milk levels up to 7 times higher than plasma [US].
*   **Fertility:** In rats, high doses (400 mg/kg/day) resulted in prolonged estrous cycles and impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:**
    *   **12–17 years:** Safety and efficacy established for migraine treatment.
    *   **Under 12 years:** Safety and efficacy not established; use not recommended [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Clearance is approximately 25% lower in healthy elderly subjects compared to younger adults. Start at the low end of the dosing range (6.25 mg) due to higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:**
    *   **Severe (Cl$_{cr}$ 10–30 mL/min):** Clearance decreases by 65%; C$_{max}$ increases by 80%.
    *   **Dose:** Start at 6.25 mg; max 12.5 mg/day [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    *   Pharmacokinetics not directly assessed, but a 60% decrease in clearance is expected based on known metabolic pathways. 
    *   **Dose:** Start at 6.25 mg; max 12.5 mg/day [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Selective agonist at 5-HT$_{1B}$, 5-HT$_{1D}$, and 5-HT$_{1F}$ receptors. It induces cranial vessel constriction, inhibits neuropeptide release, and reduces transmission in trigeminal pain pathways [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Bioavailability:** ~70%; T$_{max}$ is 1–3 hours. Food does not affect absorption.
    *   **Distribution:** Minimally protein bound (~35%).
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily via MAO-A (27%) and CYP3A4/2D6 (12%). Metabolites are inactive.
    *   **Elimination:** Mean half-life is 3–4 hours. Renal excretion accounts for 75% (40% as unchanged drug) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Adult Efficacy:** Established in three multi-center, randomised, double-blind trials. Both 6.25 mg and 12.5 mg doses showed significantly higher 2-hour pain relief response (55–65%) compared to placebo (33–40%) [US].
*   **Adolescent Efficacy:** Established in one randomised, placebo-controlled study (n=720). Pain relief at 2 hours was significantly higher for 6.25 mg (71.8%) and 12.5 mg (72.9%) compared to placebo (55.3%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F).
*   **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
*   **Packaging:** Keep out of reach of children.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
