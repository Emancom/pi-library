# Editorial Product Information: Acarbose

**Editorial Note:**  
This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from official regulatory source documents provided by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It combines clinical data and safety guidelines to provide a comprehensive reference for healthcare professionals. Where regulatory requirements or safety thresholds differ between agencies, the most conservative or comprehensive data has been included and noted accordingly.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Acarbose  
**Active Ingredient:** Acarbose  
**Formulation:** Oral tablets (25 mg [FDA], 50 mg, and 100 mg)

Acarbose is a complex oligosaccharide obtained through the fermentation of the microorganism *Actinoplanes utahensis*. It functions as an oral alpha-glucosidase inhibitor.

## 2. Approved Indications
Acarbose is indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus (non-insulin dependent diabetes mellitus). It may be used as monotherapy or in combination with other oral hypoglycemic agents or insulin.

## 3. Contraindications
Acarbose is contraindicated in the following patient populations:
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to acarbose or any inactive constituents.
*   **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** 
    *   Inflammatory bowel disease (e.g., ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease).
    *   Colonic ulceration or partial intestinal obstruction.
    *   Patients predisposed to intestinal obstruction or ileus.
    *   Chronic intestinal diseases associated with marked disorders of digestion or absorption.
    *   Conditions that may deteriorate due to increased gas formation in the intestine (e.g., Roemheld’s syndrome, major hernias, intestinal ulcers).
*   **Metabolic States:** Diabetic ketoacidosis.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Cirrhosis [FDA] or severe hepatic impairment [TGA].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Severe renal dysfunction with a creatinine clearance < 25 mL/min [TGA].
*   **Pediatrics:** Use in patients under 18 years of age is not recommended due to insufficient safety and efficacy data [TGA].
*   **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Acarbose is contraindicated during pregnancy and breastfeeding [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Hepatic Effects
Acarbose may cause dose-related elevations in serum transaminases (ALT/AST). While typically asymptomatic and reversible upon discontinuation, rare cases of fulminant hepatitis have been reported. 
*   **Monitoring:** Liver enzymes should be monitored every 3 months for the first year of treatment [FDA] or monthly for the first 6 to 12 months [TGA]. If elevations occur, dose reduction or withdrawal may be necessary.
*   **Weight Consideration:** Transaminase elevations are more common in patients weighing less than 60 kg [FDA].

### Hypoglycemia Management
Acarbose does not induce hypoglycemia when used as monotherapy. However, if used in combination with sulfonylureas or insulin, the risk of hypoglycemia increases. 
*   **Critical Note:** Because acarbose delays the breakdown of table sugar (sucrose/cane sugar), **glucose (dextrose)** must be used to treat acute hypoglycemic episodes. Sucrose is ineffective for rapid glucose elevation during acarbose therapy.

### Gastrointestinal Symptoms
Increased carbohydrate fermentation in the colon often leads to gastrointestinal side effects (flatulence, diarrhea, abdominal pain). These are dose-dependent and exacerbated by the ingestion of sucrose (cane sugar). Most symptoms diminish in frequency and intensity over time as treatment continues.

### Loss of Blood Glucose Control
During periods of physiological stress (fever, trauma, infection, surgery), blood glucose control may fail. Temporary insulin therapy may be required in these instances.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
Tablets should be taken orally three times daily at the start (with the first bite) of each main meal. They may be swallowed whole with liquid or chewed with the first few mouthfuls of food.

### Titration Schedule
To minimize gastrointestinal side effects, dosage must be individualized and increased slowly:
*   **Initial Dose:** 25 mg three times daily [FDA] or a ramp-up starting at 50 mg once daily for the first week, increasing to 50 mg twice daily in the second week, and 50 mg three times daily in the third week [TGA].
*   **Maintenance:** Dose adjustments should occur at 4–8 week intervals based on one-hour postprandial glucose levels and tolerance.

### Maximum Recommended Doses [FDA]
*   **Patients ≤ 60 kg:** 50 mg three times daily.
*   **Patients > 60 kg:** 100 mg three times daily.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequent adverse reactions are gastrointestinal, resulting from the drug's mechanism of action:
*   **Very Common (>10%):** Flatulence.
*   **Common (1%–10%):** Diarrhea, gastrointestinal and abdominal pain.
*   **Uncommon (0.1%–1%):** Nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, and increased hepatic enzymes.
*   **Rare (<0.1%):** Edema and jaundice.
*   **Post-Marketing Reports:** Thrombocytopenia, allergic skin reactions (rash, erythema, urticaria), subileus/ileus, hepatitis, and pneumatosis cystoides intestinalis (rare gas-filled cysts in the bowel wall) [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Hyperglycemic Agents:** Thiazides, diuretics, corticosteroids, phenothiazines, thyroid products, estrogens, and oral contraceptives may antagonize the effects of acarbose, leading to a loss of glycemic control.
*   **Intestinal Adsorbents and Enzymes:** Activated charcoal and digestive enzyme preparations (e.g., amylase, pancreatin) may reduce the efficacy of acarbose and should not be taken concomitantly.
*   **Digoxin:** Acarbose may interfere with digoxin absorption, necessitating dose adjustments of digoxin.
*   **Neomycin and Colestyramine:** Concomitant use may enhance the glucose-lowering effect of acarbose and increase the severity of gastrointestinal side effects.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
**Pregnancy:** Category B3 [TGA] / Category B [FDA]. While animal studies showed no teratogenic effects, there are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Acarbose is generally not recommended for use during pregnancy; insulin is preferred for maintaining blood glucose levels in pregnant patients.

**Breastfeeding:** Acarbose is secreted into the milk of lactating rats. Because it is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk, acarbose should not be administered to nursing mothers [TGA/FDA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Systemic bioavailability of the parent drug is extremely low (<2%). Most activity occurs locally within the gastrointestinal tract.
*   **Metabolism:** Acarbose is metabolized exclusively in the gut by intestinal bacteria and digestive enzymes. Approximately 34% of the dose is absorbed as metabolites.
*   **Excretion:** Absorbed drug and metabolites are excreted by the kidneys. Approximately 51% of an oral dose is eliminated in the feces as unabsorbed drug within 96 hours.
*   **Special Populations:** In patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl < 25 mL/min), peak plasma concentrations are 5 times higher than in healthy volunteers [FDA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Acarbose is a competitive, reversible inhibitor of pancreatic alpha-amylase and membrane-bound intestinal alpha-glucoside hydrolase enzymes. This inhibition delays the digestion of complex carbohydrates (disaccharides, oligosaccharides, and polysaccharides) into absorbable monosaccharides (glucose). By slowing the rate of glucose absorption, acarbose reduces the postprandial rise in blood glucose and smoothes daily glucose fluctuations. It does not stimulate insulin secretion.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 25°C [TGA] or between 20°C and 25°C [FDA].
*   Protect from moisture. 
*   Keep the container tightly closed or store in original blister packaging.
