# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine (Intravenous)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in a specific region. Prescribers should consult their local regulatory documents for full compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for both acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). 
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to initiating therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels is advised [US].
- **Target Range:** Free carnitine levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose Adjustments (ESRD):** Adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. In the event of a severe reaction, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures may occur [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and body odor have been reported [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate that levocarnitine may increase the International Normalized Ratio (INR) in patients taking warfarin. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter, but animal reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. 
    - **Recommendation:** The risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug should be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). See Section 3 for specific details [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for patients with ESRD on dialysis. However, oral levocarnitine is not recommended for this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a carrier molecule essential for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD PK Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients undergoing hemodialysis for ≥6 months showed that 20 mg/kg administered three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** In studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg, trough concentrations increased in a dose-proportional manner over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Special Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC plastic bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].
