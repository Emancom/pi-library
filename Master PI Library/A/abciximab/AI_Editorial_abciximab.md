# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory information into a single reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in the transport of long-chain fatty acids.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or via infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and not less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustments:** Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels [US].

### Monitoring
- **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain baseline carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** None known at the time of publication, though hypersensitivity reactions have been reported during therapy [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While the injection bypasses the gut, the safety of chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in renal impairment is compromised by the potential accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO). The clinical impact of this in long-term IV use in ESRD is not fully established [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discolouration prior to use [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Common Adverse Effects (Frequency ≥5% in ESRD Trials)
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), infection (15%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcaemia (9%), hypervolaemia (5%).
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (13%), paraesthesia (5%), depression (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).

### Serious and Notable Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (noted in post-marketing reports).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal oedema.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; distinct "fishy" body odour [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    - **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned, but text states "only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled human studies exist.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine after administration. The benefits of maternal therapy must be weighed against potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as described in metabolic disorders. Safety and efficacy appear consistent with adult populations for the indicated metabolic conditions [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. High doses of the *oral* form are cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is an endogenous substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for beta-oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also assists in removing excess organic and fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters that are excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces).
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, the distribution half-life is approximately 0.6 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations and corrects the ratio of acylcarnitine to free carnitine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine levels rose from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 12 and 24 weeks demonstrated dose-proportionate increases in trough carnitine concentrations. 
- **Note:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine levels in ESRD, the definitive impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local jurisdiction) [US].
