# Editorial Product Information: aprotinin + factor + fibrinogen + thrombin + calcium

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **aprotinin + factor + fibrinogen + thrombin + calcium**
- Chemical class: Human plasma–derived two‑component fibrin sealant with synthetic aprotinin (fibrinolysis inhibitor), human factor XIII, human fibrinogen and human thrombin; contains calcium (as calcium chloride) and human albumin as excipients.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Topical application only (adhesive/sealant). Injection into tissue or intravascular administration is contraindicated.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated for topical adherence of:
  - autologous skin grafts in burn patients.
  - tissue flaps during facial rhytidectomy (face‑lift) surgery.
- Not indicated for haemostasis.

(If approvals differ by jurisdiction in the full regulatory dossiers, those differences are not specified in the available sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Apply topically only. Do not inject.
- Recommended thin application: approximately 2 mL per 100 cm² to avoid excessive clot thickness and potential interference with wound healing.
- Approximate surface areas covered by package sizes when applied by spray:
  - 2 mL → ~100 cm²
  - 4 mL → ~200 cm²
  - 10 mL → ~500 cm²

### Dosing by indication
- Skin graft adherence and tissue flap fixation: apply a thin layer over the recipient surface and immediately position graft/flap onto the wound bed. Specific dosing is based on surface area as above.

### Administration instructions
- The product is delivered as two separate components that must be mixed immediately prior to application so equal volumes of sealer protein solution (contains fibrinogen, factor XIII, aprotinin) and thrombin solution (contains thrombin and calcium) are applied and blended.
- Dry the recipient site prior to application using standard techniques (compresses, swabs, suction). Do not use pressurised air or gas for drying.
- Apply the mixed solution thinly to the surface to be adhered.
- The clinician has up to 60 seconds to manipulate and position the graft prior to polymerisation.
- After positioning, hold the graft/flap with gentle compression for at least 3 minutes to ensure set and firm adhesion.
- For small areas, use the provided cannula/application tip. For larger areas, spray application (with an approved device) is recommended using recommended pressure and distance settings.
- If using spray application: use a pressure and distance according to manufacturer’s spray set instructions; recommended spray distance generally 10–15 cm and recommended spray pressure generally up to 1.5–2.0 bar (21.8–29.0 psi). To reduce embolic risk, avoid pressures above the device manufacturer recommendations; where no device‑specific recommendation exists, avoid pressures above 1.4–1.7 bar (20–25 psi) and avoid spraying closer than 10–15 cm.
- If application is interrupted, expect rapid clogging of cannula; replace cannula immediately before resuming application.
- Expel air from syringe prior to attaching application device; do not expel air in joining piece/cannula until start of application to avoid clogging.

### Preparation and thawing/warming (handling)
- Product should be completely thawed and warmed to liquid consistency (33–37 °C) prior to use. Do not exceed 37 °C and do not microwave.
- Quick thaw/warm and room temperature/ incubator methods with minimum times for various pack sizes are provided by the manufacturer (not reproduced here); follow manufacturer instructions for thaw/warm technique and minimum times.
- Do not use solutions that are cloudy, discoloured or contain deposits.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to aprotinin (including previous hypersensitivity to aprotinin‑containing products) or to any component of the product (including human albumin or other excipients).
- Injection (intravascular or soft tissue injection) of the product is contraindicated. Intravascular injection may cause life‑threatening thromboembolic events. Soft tissue injection is associated with risk of anaphylactic reactions and local tissue damage.
- Application into highly vascularised tissue (eg. nasal mucosa) is contraindicated because of increased risk of intravascular administration and severe hypersensitivity.
- Known allergy to bovine proteins: use with caution because synthetic aprotinin is structurally identical to bovine aprotinin; careful assessment required.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- **Hypersensitivity and anaphylactic reactions, including fatal anaphylaxis, have been reported.** Previous exposure to aprotinin increases risk of immunisation; repeat exposure within six months carries greater immunogenic risk.
- **Air/gas embolism risk with spray application:** Life‑threatening or fatal air/gas embolism has occurred when fibrin sealants were applied using spray devices at higher than recommended pressures and/or at close distances to tissue. Exercise extreme caution with spray devices.

### General warnings and precautions
- Products are derived from pooled human plasma. There is potential risk of transmission of infectious agents (viruses, theoretical risk of prion disease). Donor screening and manufacturing viral inactivation/removal reduce but do not eliminate risk. Parvovirus B19 may be of particular concern (risk to pregnant women, immunocompromised patients, those with increased RBC turnover).
- Assess risk–benefit in patients with known allergic diathesis, history of hypersensitivity to medical products, prior exposure to aprotinin‑containing products. Consider pre‑treatment with antihistamines when proceeding.
- Avoid application intravascularly or into highly vascularised tissue to reduce risk of thromboembolic complications and severe hypersensitivity.
- Do not use in enclosed body cavities with spray application.
- Monitor patient when spraying for changes in blood pressure, pulse, oxygen saturation and end‑tidal CO2 (monitoring recommended because of potential for air/gas embolism).
- Keep adjacent tissues covered where possible to avoid unintended tissue adhesion.
- Apply product in a thin layer to avoid excessive clot thickness which may impair wound healing.
- Sealer protein and thrombin solutions may be denatured by contact with alcohol, iodine or heavy metals (eg. disinfectants). Remove such substances from the site before application.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis during and after application.
- When spray application is used, monitor haemodynamic and respiratory parameters (blood pressure, pulse, oxygen saturation, end tidal CO2) due to risk of gas embolism.
- Report any suspected transmission of infection to the manufacturer/ appropriate authorities.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Take care with repeat exposure to product within six months due to increased risk of immune response.
- If treating pregnant patients, carefully consider potential risks — see Section 9.
- Use only application devices that are licensed/CE marked/approved for use with this product and follow their instructions for pressure and distance.

## 7. Adverse Effects

- Frequency categorisation used: very common (≥1/10), common (≥1/100 to <1/10), uncommon (≥1/1,000 to <1/100), rare (≥1/10,000 to <1/1,000), very rare (<1/10,000), unknown (cannot be estimated from available data).

### Summary of adverse reactions reported
- Clinical trial (phase 3; split‑thickness skin grafts in burns): 8 non‑serious adverse reactions in 6 patients (no serious reactions). Reported events included graft failure (detachment/non‑adherence, necrosis), pruritus and dermal cyst.
- Post‑marketing reports (limited data): hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis and anaphylactic shock (including fatal outcomes); bradycardia; tachycardia; hypotension; haematoma; air/gas embolism associated with spray misapplication.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Phase 3 clinical trial (multi‑centre, prospective, evaluator‑blinded, randomised) in which the product was used to affix split‑thickness skin grafts to excised burn wounds:
  - Reported safety data: eight non‑serious adverse reactions in six patients; no serious reactions in that study.
  - Event details: five instances of skin graft failure (including detachment/non‑adherence), one graft necrosis, two reports of pruritus, one dermal cyst.
- Facial rhytidectomy clinical studies:
  - No reports of serious associated adverse reactions above 1% incidence in the available summaries.
