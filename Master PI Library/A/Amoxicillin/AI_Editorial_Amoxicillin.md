# Editorial Product Information: Amoxicillin and Clavulanic Acid

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Amoxicillin (as trihydrate) and Clavulanic Acid (as potassium clavulanate).
- **Chemical Class:** Amoxicillin is a semi-synthetic antibiotic (aminopenicillin); Clavulanic acid is a $\beta$-lactamase inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** **Alphaclav Duo Viatris**, **Alphaclav Duo Forte Viatris** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01CR02 (Amoxicillin and $\beta$-lactamase inhibitor).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablet: 500 mg amoxicillin / 125 mg clavulanic acid.
    - Film-coated tablet: 875 mg amoxicillin / 125 mg clavulanic acid.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd trading as Viatris [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Amoxicillin/Clavulanic acid** is indicated for the short-term treatment of bacterial infections caused by sensitive organisms at the following sites [Australian]:
- **Urinary Tract Infections:** Including both complicated and uncomplicated infections.
- **Lower Respiratory Tract Infections:** Including community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) and acute exacerbations of chronic bronchitis.
- **Upper Respiratory Tract Infections:** Including sinusitis, otitis media, and recurrent tonsillitis.
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections.**

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Usual dose:** One 500/125 mg tablet every 12 hours.
- **Severe infections:** One 875/125 mg tablet every 12 hours.
- **Important Dosing Note:** The 500/125 mg and 875/125 mg tablets both contain the same amount of clavulanic acid (125 mg). Consequently, two 500/125 mg tablets are **not** bioequivalent to one 875/125 mg tablet and should not be substituted as such [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be taken at the start of a meal (or with the first mouthful of food) to minimize gastrointestinal intolerance and optimize the absorption of clavulanic acid.
- Therapy should typically continue for 48 to 72 hours beyond the resolution of symptoms or evidence of bacterial eradication.
- Treatment duration should not exceed 14 days without clinical review.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of allergic reactions to $\beta$-lactams (e.g., penicillins, cephalosporins).
- **Hepatic History:** Previous history of amoxicillin/clavulanic acid-associated jaundice or hepatic dysfunction [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal anaphylactoid reactions have been reported. If an allergic reaction occurs, discontinue immediately. 
- **Kounis Syndrome:** A serious allergic reaction that can result in myocardial infarction, potentially presenting as chest pain during an allergic response.
- **Drug-Induced Enterocolitis Syndrome (DIES):** Reported primarily in children; characterized by protracted vomiting (1–4 hours post-dose) without skin or respiratory symptoms.
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhoea (CDAD):** Pseudomembranous colitis ranging from mild to life-threatening. Avoid drugs that delay peristalsis (e.g., opiates) if CDAD is suspected.
- **Hepatic Effects:** Cholestatic hepatitis and jaundice may occur, typically several weeks after treatment. Risk is higher in males and elderly patients, particularly with prolonged use.
- **Infectious Mononucleosis:** Avoid use if mononucleosis is suspected, as a high incidence of morbilliform rash is associated with amoxicillin use in these patients.
- **Crystalluria:** Predominantly observed with high doses or parenteral therapy; maintain adequate fluid intake to reduce the risk of amoxicillin crystals in the urine.
- **Laboratory Interferences:** High urine concentrations of amoxicillin may produce false-positive glucose reactions with copper sulfate tests (e.g., Benedict’s Solution); enzymatic glucose oxidase tests should be used instead.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are generally mild and transient. 

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, vomiting, mucocutaneous candidiasis |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Dizziness, headache, indigestion, moderate rise in AST/ALT, thrombocytosis |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Gastritis, stomatitis, glossitis, black "hairy" tongue, interstitial nephritis, exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, tooth discolouration (superficial; removable by brushing) |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Reversible hyperactivity, convulsions (especially in renal impairment or high doses) |
| **Not Known** | Kounis syndrome, DIES, aseptic meningitis, crystalluria, acute pancreatitis |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, DRESS syndrome, AGEP, and SDRIFE (baboon syndrome) have been reported rarely.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** Decreases renal tubular secretion of amoxicillin, leading to increased and prolonged amoxicillin blood levels.
- **Allopurinol:** Concurrent use may increase the incidence of skin rashes.
- **Oral Anticoagulants:** Rare reports of increased INR. Monitor prothrombin time/INR closely during co-administration.
- **Methotrexate:** Penicillins may reduce methotrexate excretion, increasing the risk of toxicity.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** May affect gut flora, potentially reducing the efficacy of combined oral contraceptives.
- **Alcohol:** Avoid during and for several days after treatment due to potential disulfiram-like reactions observed with other $\beta$-lactams.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Animal studies show no teratogenic effects. Prophylactic use in women with preterm, premature rupture of the foetal membrane (pPROM) may increase the risk of necrotising enterocolitis in neonates. Use should be avoided during the first trimester unless deemed essential.
- **Breastfeeding:** Amoxicillin is excreted in breast milk. There are no data on clavulanic acid excretion in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) using a 2:1 ratio at doses up to 1200 mg/kg/day showed no effect on fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Children weighing 40 kg or more should receive adult dosing. For children <40 kg, suspensions are recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of hepatic events; otherwise, no specific data available.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **CrCl >30 mL/min:** No adjustment required.
    - **CrCl 10–30 mL/min:** One 500/125 mg tablet every 12 hours. **Do not use the 875/125 mg strength.**
    - **CrCl <10 mL/min:** One 500/125 mg tablet every 24 hours.
    - **Haemodialysis:** Administer an additional dose at the end of dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dose with caution and monitor hepatic function at regular intervals.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Amoxicillin provides bactericidal activity by inhibiting cell wall synthesis. Clavulanic acid irreversibly inhibits $\beta$-lactamase enzymes, protecting amoxicillin from degradation and extending its spectrum to include many $\beta$-lactamase-producing bacteria.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; clavulanic acid absorption is optimized when taken at the start of a meal.
    - **Distribution:** Diffuses into pus, bile, synovial fluid, and peritoneal fluid. Poor CSF penetration unless meninges are inflamed. Amoxicillin is ~20% protein-bound; Clavulanic acid is 9–30% protein-bound.
    - **Elimination:** Amoxicillin is primarily excreted renally (~70% unchanged in urine). Clavulanic acid undergoes both renal and non-renal elimination.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Comparative Efficacy:** Pivotal studies (n=1,361) compared 875/125 mg q12h to 500/125 mg q8h. Results showed comparable efficacy across RTIs and UTIs. While overall adverse event rates were similar, the q12h regimen (875/125 mg) was associated with significantly lower rates of severe diarrhoea (1.0%) compared to the q8h regimen (2.5%).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C. 
- Protect from moisture [Australian].
