# Editorial Product Information: Argatroban

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Argatroban product information for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the regulatory and clinical data provided in the available source documentation.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for the most current and jurisdiction-specific requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Argatroban (synthetic direct thrombin inhibitor).
- **Chemical Class:** L-arginine derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Argatroban Injection (Plano Pharmaceuticals Inc.) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 125 mg per 125 mL (1 mg/mL) aqueous sodium chloride solution [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Plano Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT):** Indicated for the prophylaxis or treatment of thrombosis in adult patients with HIT [US].
- **Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI):** Indicated as an anticoagulant in adult patients with or at risk for HIT who are undergoing PCI [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- All parenteral anticoagulants must be discontinued prior to initiating **Argatroban**.
- The 125 mg/125 mL formulation is ready-to-use and does not require dilution [US].
- Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration; the solution should be clear and colorless to pale yellow.

### Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT)
- **Initial Dosage (No Hepatic Impairment):** 2 mcg/kg/min administered as a continuous intravenous infusion.
- **Monitoring:** Monitor therapy using activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT). 
    - Obtain baseline aPTT. 
    - Check aPTT 2 hours after initiation or any dose change.
- **Target Range:** 1.5 to 3 times the initial baseline value (not to exceed 100 seconds).
- **Titration:** Adjust dose (maximum 10 mcg/kg/min) until a steady-state aPTT in the target range is achieved.

### Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI)
- **Initial Dosage:** 
    - **Bolus:** 350 mcg/kg administered over 3 to 5 minutes via a large-bore intravenous line.
    - **Infusion:** Start at 25 mcg/kg/min.
- **Monitoring:** Monitor via activated clotting time (ACT). Check ACT 5 to 10 minutes after the bolus is completed.
- **Procedure Threshold:** The procedure may proceed if the ACT is >300 seconds.
- **Dose Adjustments during PCI:**
    - If ACT <300 seconds: Give an additional 150 mcg/kg bolus and increase infusion to 30 mcg/kg/min.
    - If ACT >450 seconds: Decrease infusion to 15 mcg/kg/min.
    - For complications (dissection, thrombus): Additional 150 mcg/kg bolus and increase infusion to 40 mcg/kg/min may be required.

### Conversion to Oral Anticoagulation (Warfarin)
- **Overlap Therapy:** Maintain continuous anticoagulation by overlapping **Argatroban** and warfarin. Do not use a loading dose of warfarin.
- **Doses ≤2 mcg/kg/min:** Measure INR daily. **Argatroban** can typically be discontinued when the INR is >4 on combined therapy. Repeat INR measurement 4 to 6 hours after stopping **Argatroban**.
- **Doses >2 mcg/kg/min:** Reduce the **Argatroban** dose to 2 mcg/kg/min and follow the procedure above.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Major Bleeding:** Use is contraindicated in patients with active major bleeding.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to **Argatroban**.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Risk of Hemorrhage:** Hemorrhage can occur at any site. Use with extreme caution in conditions with increased bleeding risk, including severe hypertension, recent major surgery (brain, eye, spinal cord), lumbar puncture, spinal anesthesia, or gastrointestinal ulcerations.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is significantly reduced (approx. 4-fold) in moderate to severe hepatic impairment. Use a reduced starting dose in HIT and avoid use during PCI in patients with significant hepatic disease [US].
- **Laboratory Monitoring:** Anticoagulant effects correlate with aPTT (for HIT) and ACT (for PCI). **Argatroban** also prolongs PT and INR, which complicates the transition to warfarin.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Hemorrhagic Reactions
- **HIT Patients:** Overall bleeding was 5.3% in clinical trials (Major: GI 2.3%, Genitourinary/hematuria 0.9%) [US].
- **PCI Patients:** Major bleeding rate was 1.8% (Retroperitoneal 0.9%, GI 0.9%) [US].

### Non-Hemorrhagic Reactions
| Frequency | HIT Patients | PCI Patients |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Not specified | Chest pain (15.2%), Hypotension (10.7%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dyspnea, Hypotension, Fever, Diarrhea, Sepsis, Cardiac arrest, Nausea, Ventricular tachycardia, Pain, UTI, Vomiting | Back pain, Nausea, Vomiting, Headache, Bradycardia, Abdominal pain, Fever, Myocardial infarction |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Intracranial Hemorrhage:** Observed in investigational studies for other indications (e.g., acute MI with thrombolytics) at 1%.
- **Anaphylaxis:** Reported in postmarketing surveillance.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Concomitant use increases PT and INR prolongation significantly.
- **Heparin:** Allow sufficient time for heparin’s effect on aPTT to subside before starting **Argatroban**.
- **Thrombolytics/Antiplatelet Agents:** Safety and efficacy of concurrent use with thrombolytics or Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa antagonists have not been established; risk of bleeding is increased.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No demonstrated interaction with digoxin, erythromycin, aspirin, or acetaminophen [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified as a letter grade, but data suggests no evidence of adverse developmental outcomes in animal studies at 0.2–0.3 times the maximum human dose.
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Monitor neonates for bleeding if used during pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of the drug in human milk. It is detected in rat milk. Discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the mother's clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effect on fertility or reproductive function at 0.3 times the maximum human dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established. PK data shows clearance in seriously ill children (0.16 L/hr/kg) is 50% lower than in adults.
- **Elderly Use:** Effectiveness is not affected by age, though older patients may have an increased risk of adverse events due to underlying conditions.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is necessary. Approximately 20% of the drug is cleared through dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **HIT Patients:** For moderate/severe impairment (Child-Pugh), reduce initial dose to 0.5 mcg/kg/min.
    - **PCI Patients:** Avoid use in clinically significant hepatic disease or AST/ALT ≥3x ULN.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A direct thrombin inhibitor that reversibly binds to the thrombin active site. It does not require antithrombin III and inhibits both free and clot-associated thrombin.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Anticoagulant effects occur immediately upon starting infusion. Steady-state is reached within 1–3 hours. It dose-dependently increases aPTT, ACT, PT, INR, and TT.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Distributes primarily in extracellular fluid (Vd 174 mL/kg). Protein binding is 54% (albumin 20%, α1-acid glycoprotein 34%).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via hepatic hydroxylation and aromatisation (CYP3A4/5). The main metabolite (M1) is 3–5 times less potent.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in feces (65%) via biliary secretion. Terminal half-life is 39–51 minutes.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **HIT Studies:** Two historically controlled studies (n=568) demonstrated that **Argatroban** significantly reduced the composite endpoint of death, amputation, and new thrombosis compared to historical controls (34.2% vs 43.0%) [US].
- **PCI Studies:** In patients with HIT/at risk for HIT undergoing PCI, acute procedural success (absence of death, emergency CABG, or Q-wave MI) was achieved in 98.2% of patients [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from light. Retain in the original carton. Do not freeze.
- **Stability:** Discard if solution is cloudy or contains precipitates.
