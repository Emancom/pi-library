  - Nonaqueous otic solution: 2% (20.65 mg/mL) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Superficial Ear Infections:** Treatment of superficial infections of the external auditory canal caused by organisms susceptible to the action of the antimicrobial agent [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- Prior to administration, all cerumen and debris should be carefully removed to ensure the solution contacts the infected surfaces directly [US].

### Adult Dosing
- **Initial Therapy (Wick Method):** To ensure continuous contact, a cotton wick saturated with the solution should be inserted into the ear canal. The wick may also be saturated after insertion. The patient should keep the wick in place for at least 24 hours and maintain moisture by adding 3 to 5 drops of the solution every 4 to 6 hours [US].
- **Subsequent Therapy:** Once the wick is removed after 24 hours, the patient should instill 5 drops of the solution into the ear canal 3 to 4 times daily for the remaining duration of treatment [US].

### Pediatric Dosing (Aged 3 Years and Older)
- Dosage may be reduced due to the smaller capacity of the ear canal. A dose of 3 to 4 drops may be sufficient [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to acetic acid or any of the excipients in the formulation (e.g., propylene glycol, benzethonium chloride, sodium acetate, citric acid) [US].
- **Tympanic Membrane Perforation:** Perforated tympanic membrane is a contraindication for the use of any medication in the external ear canal [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Sensitization and Irritation:** Treatment should be discontinued promptly if the patient develops signs of sensitization or local irritation [US].
- **Local Reaction:** Transient stinging or burning may occur occasionally, particularly when the solution is first instilled into an acutely inflamed ear [US].
- **Monitoring:** Clinical response should be monitored; if infection does not improve, alternative antimicrobial therapy should be considered.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- Acetic acid provides both antibacterial and antifungal activity [US].
- The solution is buffered to a pH of 3, which creates an acidic environment unfavourable for the growth of many pathogenic organisms in the external ear canal [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
- **Propylene Glycol:** Hydrophilic agent that provides low surface tension to aid distribution [US].
- **Benzethonium Chloride:** A surface-active agent (surfactant) that promotes contact between the solution and the ear tissues [US].
