# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**. 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals based on the provided regulatory source.
- This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI or local prescribing guidelines in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/generic name).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose may be followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - Highest recorded dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** Recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Plasma Monitoring:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to parenteral therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations is recommended.
- **Target Levels:** Plasma free carnitine levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose Adjustment (ESRD):** Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) levels. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be required if levels are high [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Precaution:** While this document focuses on the injection, note that chronic high-dose **oral** levocarnitine is not recommended in renal impairment due to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects listed were reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Frequency data reflects events occurring at ≥5% [US].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations. |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia. |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes. |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, depression, paresthesia. |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis. |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash. |

- **Serious/Specific Reactions:** Body odor, gastritis, and seizures (post-marketing).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed. Monitor INR levels closely upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not explicitly assigned a letter, but described as follows]: Reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the child against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders follows the mg/kg weight-based protocol (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in source; clinical trials focused on ESRD and metabolic patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** This is a primary indication (ESRD); however, caution is advised regarding the potential accumulation of metabolites if oral forms are used in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Clears toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life: 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life: 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Bioavailability:** Oral forms (tablets/solution) have an absolute bioavailability of approximately 15% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels rose from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** A study of ~90 patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg 3 times per week showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local jurisdiction).
