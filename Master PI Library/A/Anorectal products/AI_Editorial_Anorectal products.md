# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, pharmacological profiles, and regulatory guidance into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for information purposes only. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing and dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients diagnosed with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency.
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
Administration must be via the intravenous route.

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Maintenance Dosing:** Should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours.
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are supratherapeutic.

### Monitoring
- Pre-therapy plasma carnitine levels should be established.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations is recommended.
- Target plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally range between 35 and 60 µmol/L.

## 4. Contraindications
- There are no known contraindications for levocarnitine injection listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, occasionally within minutes of administration. These are reported most frequently in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately and initiate standard medical care.
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution):** While this PI concerns the injectable form, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### General Reactions
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
- **Less Frequent:** Gastritis and a distinct "fishy" body odor.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine therapy and following any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labeling, but previously aligned with Category B]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Due to the lack of adequate human studies, use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Weight-based dosing is appropriate for children (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document; clinical discretion is advised regarding renal function and comorbidities.
- **Renal Impairment:** The primary target population for this product is ESRD patients. No specific dose adjustment for renal stage is provided, as dosing is performed post-dialysis to replace losses.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This provides the necessary substrate for beta-oxidation and subsequent energy production in skeletal and cardiac muscle.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose, approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal data was derived from two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials involving patients on chronic hemodialysis. The trials demonstrated that doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels remained stable or increased over 24 weeks of therapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL).
