# Editorial Product Information: Aclidinium bromide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details and legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Aclidinium bromide.
- **Chemical Class:** Long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA); competitive muscarinic receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Bretaris Genuair** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R03BB05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Inhalation powder delivered via a breath-actuated, multi-dose dry powder inhaler (Genuair). 
    - **Metered Dose:** 400 micrograms aclidinium bromide (equivalent to 343 mcg aclidinium). 
    - **Delivered Dose (from mouthpiece):** 322 micrograms aclidinium bromide (equivalent to 275 mcg aclidinium) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral inhalation.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** A. Menarini Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD):** Indicated as a long-term maintenance bronchodilator treatment to relieve symptoms in adult patients [Australian].
- **Note:** Not indicated for the treatment of asthma or for the relief of acute bronchospasm (rescue therapy) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** One inhalation (322 mcg delivered dose) twice daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Administer at the same time each day (morning and night), approximately 12 hours apart.
    - For oral inhalation only.
    - The Genuair inhaler includes a color-coded indicator (green to red) and an audible "click" to confirm successful inhalation.
- **Missed Dose:** Take as soon as possible unless it is nearly time for the next scheduled dose. Do not take a double dose to compensate for a missed one.
- **Titration:** No titration schedule is specified; the maximum dose is one inhalation twice daily.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to aclidinium bromide, atropine or its derivatives (e.g., ipratropium, oxitropium, tiotropium), or the excipient **lactose monohydrate** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Paradoxical Bronchospasm:** As with other inhalation therapies, aclidinium may cause life-threatening paradoxical bronchospasm. If this occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and institute alternative therapy.
- **Deterioration of Disease:** Should not be initiated in patients with acutely deteriorating COPD. Not to be used as rescue therapy for acute symptoms.
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use with caution in patients with:
    - Myocardial infarction within the previous 6 months.
    - Unstable angina.
    - New York Heart Association (NYHA) Class III or IV heart failure hospitalisation within the previous 12 months.
    - Known cardiac arrhythmias (e.g., atrial fibrillation, paroxysmal tachycardia) or risk factors for arrhythmias.
- **Anticholinergic Effects:** Use with caution in patients with:
    - **Narrow-angle glaucoma:** Potential for increased intraocular pressure.
    - **Prostatic hyperplasia or bladder-neck obstruction:** Potential for urinary retention.
- **Dental Hygiene:** Long-term dry mouth associated with anticholinergic therapy may increase the risk of dental caries.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticholinergic Agents:** Co-administration with other inhaled anticholinergic-containing medications (e.g., tiotropium, glycopyrronium) has not been studied and is not recommended due to potential additive effects.
- **Metabolic Interactions:** 
    - **CYP450:** In vitro studies indicate aclidinium and its metabolites are not expected to interact with drugs metabolised by CYP450 enzymes.
    - **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** Aclidinium is not an inhibitor of P-gp; metabolites are not expected to cause significant P-gp interactions.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3:** Animal studies have shown limited evidence of increased occurrence of fetal damage (delayed ossification and decreased weight at maternotoxic doses). 
    - **Recommendation:** Use only if the expected benefit to the mother justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if aclidinium or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. A decision must be made whether to discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, weighing the benefit of breastfeeding for the child against the benefit of therapy for the mother.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed slight reductions in fertility only at exposures significantly higher (>62 times) than the recommended human dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for use in patients under 18 years of age (safety and efficacy not established) [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustments required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustments required for patients with renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustments required. Aclidinium is primarily metabolised by chemical and enzymatic cleavage in plasma rather than the liver.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Aclidinium bromide is a long-acting, competitive muscarinic receptor antagonist with subnanomolar affinity for all five muscarinic subtypes (M1–M5). It induces bronchodilation by antagonising M3 receptors in the airway smooth muscle. It has a longer residence time at M3 receptors than M2 receptors.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Rapid, dose-dependent, and long-lasting inhibition of acetylcholine-induced bronchoconstriction. No clinically significant effects on QT interval or cardiac rhythm were observed at therapeutic doses.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed from the lungs (Tmax <15 minutes in COPD patients). Systemic bioavailability of unchanged drug is <5%.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is approximately 87% for the carboxylic acid metabolite and 15% for the alcohol metabolite.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly and extensively hydrolysed into inactive alcohol and carboxylic acid derivatives by plasma esterases (primarily butyrylcholinesterase). 
    - **Elimination:** Effective half-life is approximately 10 hours. Primarily excreted as metabolites in the urine (65%) and faeces (33%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** In 3-month and 6-month Phase III trials, aclidinium 322 mcg twice daily provided clinically meaningful improvements in lung function (FEV1), with bronchodilation evident within 30 minutes of the first dose.
- **Symptom Control:** Significant improvements were noted in breathlessness (Transition Dyspnoea Index) and health status (St George’s Respiratory Questionnaire).
- **Long-term Safety (ASCLEPIOS):** A 3-year study in 3,630 patients with cardiovascular risk factors confirmed that aclidinium did not increase the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) and significantly reduced the rate of moderate-to-severe COPD exacerbations and hospitalisations [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C.
- **In-use Shelf Life:** Use within 90 days of opening the sealed pouch.
- **Handling:** Keep the inhaler protected inside the pouch until the administration period begins.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription only.
