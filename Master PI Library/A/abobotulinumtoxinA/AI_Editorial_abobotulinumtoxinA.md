# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following official Product Information (PI) document: **United States (FDA)**: "Levocarnitine Injection, USP — Baxter Healthcare Corporation".
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory information into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN/USP), a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Chemical Class**: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt (L-carnitine).
- **Brand Names**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration**: Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Crisis**: A loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance**: Subsequent daily doses are recommended at 50 mg/kg, adjusted based on clinical response. Maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration**: Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment**: Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) can be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].
- Discard unused portions; the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity**: Serious reactions (anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm) have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue treatment if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures)**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].

### Specific Precautions
- **Oral vs. IV use**: Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. High doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally cleared by the kidneys [US].
- **Monitoring**: It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitoring should include weekly/monthly blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in clinical trials (ESRD patients) with a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain (9%), Accidental injury (10%), Asthenia (9%), Chest pain (12%), Fever (7%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Nausea (8%), Vomiting (15%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Depression (5%), Paresthesia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Pharyngitis (23%), Rhinitis (9%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].
- **Other reported effects**: Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis (less frequent) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concurrently. Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: [US]: No adequate or well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal reproductive studies (rats/rabbits at 3.8x human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding**: Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk. Weigh the risks to the infant against the benefit of supplementation to the mother; consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Clinical use is covered under the "Dosage and Administration" sections for metabolic disorders and ESRD [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specifically detailed in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: Primary indication includes ESRD patients on dialysis. Note the warning regarding accumulation of metabolites with *oral* levocarnitine in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normal free carnitine levels are typically >20 µmol/L [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**: 
    - **Distribution**: Not protein-bound; mean distribution half-life of 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination**: Terminal half-life of 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg thrice weekly post-dialysis) increased mean trough concentrations from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Precautions**: Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility**: Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at 25°C when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States**: Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
