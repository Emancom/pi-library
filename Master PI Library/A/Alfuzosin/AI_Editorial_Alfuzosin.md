# Editorial Product Information: Alfuzosin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian source was available for analysis; therefore, jurisdiction-specific details primarily reflect Australian regulatory positions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Alfuzosin hydrochloride (a quinazoline derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** Xatral SR [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G04CA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 10 mg modified-release (MR) tablets. The Australian formulation is a three-layered, round, biconvex tablet featuring a white active matrix between two inactive yellow layers.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH):** Treatment of functional symptoms associated with BPH [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The standard regimen is one 10 mg modified-release tablet taken once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole. 
    - **Caution:** Patients must not crush, chew, grind, or pound the tablets to powder, as this may lead to inappropriate rapid release and absorption, increasing the risk of adverse effects.
    - The dose should be administered immediately following a meal.
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to alfuzosin hydrochloride or any excipients.
- History of orthostatic hypotension.
- **Hepatic Insufficiency:** Use is strictly contraindicated in patients with impaired liver function [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** 
    - Contraindicated in combination with other alpha-1 blockers.
    - Contraindicated in combination with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Postural Hypotension:** May develop within a few hours of administration, with or without symptoms (e.g., dizziness, fatigue, sweating). This is most common at the start of therapy. Patients should be advised to lie down until symptoms resolve.
- **Cardiac Conditions:** 
    - Use with caution in patients with pre-existing cardiac disease or those on antihypertensive therapy, as the risk of a pronounced blood pressure drop is higher. 
    - **Coronary Artery Disease:** Alfuzosin should not be prescribed as monotherapy; specific treatment for coronary insufficiency should continue. Discontinue alfuzosin if angina worsens or recurs.
    - Caution is advised in patients with congenital or acquired QT prolongation.
- **Priapism:** Like other alpha-blockers, alfuzosin is associated with priapism. Patients must seek immediate medical attention for persistent, painful erections, as untreated priapism can lead to permanent impotence.
- **Intraoperative Floppy Iris Syndrome (IFIS):** Observed during cataract surgery in patients currently or previously treated with alpha-1 blockers. Surgeons should be informed of current or past alfuzosin use prior to surgery.
- **Prostate Carcinoma:** Must be excluded prior to initiating therapy for presumed BPH.
- **Monitoring:** Blood pressure should be monitored regularly, particularly in the elderly and at the initiation of treatment.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (CYP3A4):** Alfuzosin is a substrate of CYP3A4. 
    - **Potent Inhibitors:** Ketoconazole (200-400 mg daily) significantly increases alfuzosin $C_{max}$ (2.1 to 2.3-fold) and AUC (2.5 to 3.2-fold). Concomitant use is contraindicated.
    - **Grapefruit Juice:** May increase alfuzosin levels; caution is advised.
- **Pharmacodynamic:**
    - **Alpha-blockers:** Combination with other alpha-1 blockers (e.g., prazosin, tamsulosin) is contraindicated. Avoid non-selective alpha-blockers (e.g., phentolamine).
    - **Antihypertensives and Nitrates:** Increased risk of hypotension.
    - **General Anesthetics:** May lead to blood pressure instability. It is recommended to stop alfuzosin 24 hours prior to surgery [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B2:** Alfuzosin is not intended for use in women. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) show no evidence of teratogenicity at high exposures, though slight prolongation of gestation was noted in rats.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not intended for use in women. It is unknown if alfuzosin is excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of reproductive organ toxicity or impaired fertility in male rats at high doses.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Efficacy not demonstrated in children <16 years; not indicated for pediatric use.
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetic parameters ($C_{max}$, AUC) are not increased compared to younger volunteers. However, there is a higher clinical risk of postural hypotension.
- **Renal Impairment:** $C_{max}$ and AUC are moderately increased, but these changes are not considered clinically relevant. No dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated due to increased systemic exposure risk [Australian].
- **Other:** Safety has not been formally assessed in patients with Parkinson’s disease, multiple sclerosis, or severe heart failure.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A selective, postsynaptic alpha-1 adrenoreceptor antagonist. It relaxes smooth muscle in the bladder neck, urethra, and prostatic stroma, thereby reducing resistance to urinary flow.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits functional uroselectivity. It reduces standing blood pressure and may increase heart rate, particularly at peak plasma concentrations.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Mean bioavailability is ~104% relative to immediate-release formulations. $T_{max}$ is reached 9 hours post-dose in the fed state.
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 90% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized by the liver, primarily via CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is approximately 9.1 hours. Only 11% is excreted unchanged in the urine; the majority (75–91%) is excreted as inactive metabolites in the feces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BPH Efficacy:** A 3-month placebo-controlled study (n=297) showed a significant reduction in the International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) and a significant increase in peak flow rate ($Q_{max}$) starting as early as day 14.
- **Acute Urinary Retention (AUR):** 
    - The **ALFAUR** study demonstrated that alfuzosin 10 mg increased the success of spontaneous voiding after catheter removal (61.9% vs 47.9% for placebo) and reduced the need for surgery over 6 months. 
    - Benefits for spontaneous voiding were most pronounced in men $\ge$ 65 years.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Packaging:** Polyvinyl chloride (PVC)/Aluminum foil blister packs.
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be returned to a pharmacy [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **US/European/Other:** Not specified in available sources.
