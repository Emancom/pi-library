# Editorial Product Information: Abacavir and Lamivudine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **European Union (EMA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Abacavir (as sulfate) and Lamivudine.
- **Chemical Class:** Nucleoside analogue reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs).
- **Brand Name:** **Kivexa** [European].
- **ATC Code:** J05AR02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated tablets containing 600 mg of abacavir (as sulfate) and 300 mg of lamivudine [European].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ViiV Healthcare BV [European].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV Infection:** Indicated in antiretroviral combination therapy for the treatment of Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) infection in adults, adolescents, and children weighing at least 25 kg [European].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents $\ge$ 25 kg):** The recommended dose is one tablet once daily [European].
- **Paediatric Dosing:** Not recommended for children weighing less than 25 kg, as the fixed-dose combination prevents the necessary dose reductions [European].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets may be taken with or without food. Tablets should ideally be swallowed whole to ensure the full dose is received [European].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered. If it is almost time for the next dose, the missed dose should be skipped. Do not take a double dose [European].
- **Discontinuation:** Because it is a fixed-dose combination, **Kivexa** should be discontinued if dose adjustment or cessation of one of the individual components is required. In such cases, separate preparations of abacavir and lamivudine must be used [European].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to abacavir, lamivudine, or any excipients (e.g., sunset yellow FCF) [European].
- **HLA-B*5701 Status:** Must not be used in patients known to carry the HLA-B*5701 allele due to the high risk of a hypersensitivity reaction [European].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions (HSR):**
    - **Screening:** HLA-B*5701 screening is mandatory for all patients prior to initiation, regardless of racial origin [European].
    - **Clinical Presentation:** Characterised by fever and/or rash. Other symptoms include dyspnoea, cough, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and lethargy. Symptoms usually appear within the first six weeks (median onset 11 days) [European].
    - **Management:** If HSR is suspected, **Kivexa** must be stopped immediately. **Never re-initiate abacavir** in patients who have experienced a suspected HSR, as life-threatening hypotension and death may occur within hours [European].
- **Cardiovascular Risk:** Observational studies suggest an association between abacavir use and an increased risk of myocardial infarction. Modifiable risk factors (e.g., smoking, hypertension) should be managed [European].
- **Lactic Acidosis and Severe Hepatomegaly:** Reported with NRTIs. Treatment should be suspended if clinical or laboratory findings suggest lactic acidosis or pronounced hepatotoxicity [European].
- **Chronic Hepatitis B/C Co-infection:** Patients are at increased risk for severe hepatic adverse reactions. If **Kivexa** is discontinued in HBV co-infected patients, clinical and laboratory monitoring is required, as lamivudine withdrawal may cause an acute exacerbation of hepatitis [European].
- **Immune Reactivation Syndrome:** Inflammatory responses to asymptomatic opportunistic pathogens may occur during the initial phase of treatment [European].
- **Mitochondrial Dysfunction:** Exposure in utero may result in haematological or metabolic disorders in infants [European].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trial data for the individual components.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** ($\ge$ 1/100 to < 1/10) | Hypersensitivity, headache, insomnia, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, rash, alopecia, arthralgia, muscle disorders, fever, lethargy, fatigue [European]. |
| **Uncommon** ($\ge$ 1/1000 to < 1/100) | Neutropenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, transient rises in liver enzymes (AST, ALT) [European]. |
| **Rare** ($\ge$ 1/10,000 to < 1/1000) | Pancreatitis, hepatitis, angioedema, rhabdomyolysis, rises in serum amylase [European]. |
| **Very Rare** (< 1/10,000) | Lactic acidosis, peripheral neuropathy, pure red cell aplasia, Erythema multiforme, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, Toxic epidermal necrolysis [European]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Abacavir is metabolised by alcohol dehydrogenase and UGT; lamivudine is cleared renally via organic cation transporters (OCT) [European].
- **Contraindicated/Avoided Combinations:**
    - **Emtricitabine:** Avoid due to intracellular competition and similarity to lamivudine [European].
    - **Cladribine:** Concomitant use is not recommended due to potential loss of cladribine efficacy [European].
- **Clinically Significant Interactions:**
    - **Riociguat:** Abacavir may increase riociguat exposure. Dose reduction of riociguat may be required [European].
    - **Methadone:** Abacavir increases methadone clearance by 22%; occasionally, methadone re-titration is necessary [European].
    - **Sorbitol:** Chronic co-administration with sorbitol-containing medicines should be avoided as it decreases lamivudine exposure [European].
    - **Trimethoprim/Sulfamethoxazole:** Increases lamivudine exposure by 40%. No dose adjustment is needed unless renal impairment is present [European].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not recommended during the first trimester. A large volume of data (over 1000 outcomes for each component) indicates no malformative or foetal/neonatal toxicity [European].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Abacavir is excreted in human milk; lamivudine is excreted at low levels. HIV-infected women should not breastfeed to avoid virus transmission [European].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effect on male or female fertility [European].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children $\ge$ 25 kg. Safety and efficacy in children < 25 kg not established for this fixed-dose tablet [European].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific PK data for patients > 65 years. Caution is advised due to age-related declines in renal and haematological function [European].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **CrCl $\ge$ 50 mL/min:** No dose adjustment required.
    - **CrCl 30–49 mL/min:** Use with caution; monitor for lamivudine-related toxicities (anaemia/neutropenia).
    - **CrCl < 30 mL/min:** **Not recommended** as the required lamivudine dose reduction cannot be achieved with the fixed-dose tablet [European].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh 5–6):** Close monitoring required; use separate components if dose reduction is needed.
    - **Moderate to Severe:** **Not recommended** [European].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Both drugs are NRTIs. They are intracellularly converted to active triphosphates (carbovir-TP and lamivudine-TP) which terminate the viral DNA chain by competing with natural dNTPs for incorporation via HIV reverse transcriptase [European].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; bioavailability $\approx$ 83% (abacavir) and 80–85% (lamivudine). No significant food effect [European].
    - **Distribution:** Both penetrate the CSF (Abacavir CSF/plasma ratio 30–44%) [European].
    - **Elimination:** Abacavir half-life $\approx$ 1.5 hours (primarily hepatic metabolism). Lamivudine half-life $\approx$ 18–19 hours (primarily renal excretion) [European].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CNA30021:** A pivotal 48-week study in 770 therapy-naïve adults demonstrated the non-inferiority of abacavir 600 mg once daily compared to 300 mg twice daily (both with lamivudine and efavirenz) [European].
- **ACTG 5202:** Demonstrated that in patients with high baseline viral loads ($\ge$ 100,000 copies/mL), abacavir/lamivudine had a higher risk of virological failure compared to tenofovir/emtricitabine [European].
- **ARROW Trial:** Supported once-daily dosing in paediatric patients weighing $\ge$ 25 kg [European].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Do not store above 30°C [European].
- **Shelf Life:** 3 years [European].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Legal Classification:** Medicinal product subject to restricted medical prescription [European].
