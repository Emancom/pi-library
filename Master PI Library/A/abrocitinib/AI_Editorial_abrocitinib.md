# Unified Editorial Product Information: Abrocitinib (CIBINQO)

**Editorial Note:** This document represents a unified synthesis of Product Information (PI) for abrocitinib, incorporating regulatory data and safety requirements from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). In instances of divergence between these authorities, the most comprehensive or conservative guidance has been selected and cited accordingly.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** CIBINQO  
**Active Ingredient:** Abrocitinib  
**Formulation:** Film-coated tablets (50 mg, 100 mg, and 200 mg).

---

## 2. Approved Indications
CIBINQO is indicated for the treatment of moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis in:
*   **Adults** who are candidates for systemic therapy [TGA/FDA].
*   **Pediatric patients 12 years of age and older** whose disease is not adequately controlled with other systemic medicinal products (including biologics) or when use of those therapies is inadvisable [FDA].

**Limitations of Use:** Use in combination with other JAK inhibitors, biological immunomodulators, or other potent immunosuppressants is not recommended [FDA/TGA].

---

## 3. Contraindications
Treatment with CIBINQO is contraindicated in patients with:
*   Hypersensitivity to abrocitinib or any of its excipients.
*   Concomitant use of antiplatelet therapies (except low-dose aspirin ≤81 mg daily) during the initial 3 months of treatment [TGA/FDA].
*   Active serious systemic infections, including tuberculosis (TB) [TGA].
*   Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) [TGA].
*   **Pregnancy and lactation** [TGA—*Note: FDA provides precautionary guidance rather than a strict contraindication*].
*   Combination with biological disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (bDMARDs) [TGA].

---

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### JAK Inhibitor Class Warnings (Serious Infections, Mortality, Malignancy, MACE, and Thrombosis)
Consistent with the JAK inhibitor class, CIBINQO carries significant risks:
*   **Serious Infections:** Increased risk of bacterial, fungal, viral, and opportunistic infections (e.g., herpes zoster, pneumonia) which may lead to hospitalization or death. Treatment should be withheld if a serious infection develops until it is controlled.
*   **Mortality:** Higher rates of all-cause mortality, including sudden cardiovascular death, have been observed in older patients (≥50 years) with cardiovascular risk factors taking JAK inhibitors compared to TNF blockers.
*   **Malignancy:** Lymphomas, lung cancers, and non-melanoma skin cancers (NMSC) have been reported. Current or past smokers are at increased risk. Periodic skin examinations are required.
*   **Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events (MACE):** Increased risk of myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular death.
*   **Thrombosis:** Serious and sometimes fatal events, including pulmonary embolism (PE), deep vein thrombosis (DVT), and arterial thrombosis, have occurred. Avoid use in patients at high risk for thrombosis.

### Laboratory Abnormalities
Treatment is associated with dose-related decreases in platelet count and absolute lymphocyte count (ALC). 
*   **Monitoring:** Perform a complete blood count (CBC) and lipid panel at baseline, 4 weeks after initiation, and 4 weeks after any dose increase. 
*   **Initiation Thresholds:** Do not initiate if platelets <150,000/mm³, ALC <500/mm³, ANC <1,000/mm³, or Hemoglobin <8 g/dL.

### Hypoglycemia in Diabetes
Physicians should monitor blood glucose more frequently in patients with diabetes, as hypoglycemia has been reported following the initiation of JAK inhibitors [FDA].

---

## 5. Dosage and Administration
CIBINQO is administered orally once daily, with or without food. Tablets must be swallowed whole and should not be crushed or split.

### Recommended Dosing
*   **Standard Starting Dose:** 100 mg once daily.
*   **Dose Escalation:** If an adequate response is not achieved after 12 weeks, the dose may be increased to 200 mg once daily. Upon achieving disease control, clinicians should consider tapering back to 100 mg [TGA].
*   **Elderly (≥65 years):** The recommended starting dose is 100 mg once daily [TGA].

### Renal and Hepatic Impairment
*   **Mild Renal Impairment (eGFR 60–89 mL/min):** No adjustment.
*   **Moderate Renal Impairment (eGFR 30–59 mL/min):** Reduce dose to 50 mg once daily.
*   **Severe Renal Impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min):** Reduce dose to 50 mg once daily. 100 mg may be considered only if there is no response to 50 mg [TGA].
*   **Hepatic:** No adjustment for mild/moderate (Child-Pugh A/B); contraindicated in severe impairment [TGA].

---

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequently reported adverse reactions (≥1% of patients) include:
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (most common, often transient), vomiting, and upper abdominal pain.
*   **Nervous System:** Headache and dizziness.
*   **Dermatological:** Acne and dermatitis contact.
*   **Infections:** Nasopharyngitis, herpes simplex, and urinary tract infections.
*   **Investigations:** Dose-dependent increases in blood creatine phosphokinase (CPK) and lipids.

---

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Strong CYP2C19 Inhibitors (e.g., fluvoxamine, fluconazole):** Reduce CIBINQO dose to 50 mg once daily.
*   **Strong CYP2C19/2C9 Inducers (e.g., rifampin):** Concomitant use is not recommended as it significantly decreases abrocitinib exposure.
*   **P-gp Substrates:** Coadministration with P-gp substrates (e.g., digoxin) may increase their concentrations; monitor appropriately.
*   **Gastric pH:** Products that increase gastric pH (e.g., famotidine, antacids) may reduce absorption; the 200 mg dose may be considered if used concomitantly [TGA].

---

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** CIBINQO is contraindicated during pregnancy [TGA]. Animal studies have demonstrated embryofetal lethality and skeletal variations at clinically relevant exposures. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for one month after treatment.
*   **Breastfeeding:** CIBINQO is contraindicated during lactation [TGA]. It is recommended that breastfeeding be discontinued during treatment and for at least one day after the final dose [FDA].

---

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed with peak plasma concentrations reached within 1 hour. Absolute bioavailability is approximately 60%.
*   **Metabolism:** Primarily mediated by CYP2C19 (~53%) and CYP2C9 (~30%). Two active metabolites (M1 and M2) contribute to the overall pharmacological activity.
*   **Elimination:** The mean elimination half-life is approximately 5 hours. Excretion is primarily via the kidneys as metabolites.

---

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Abrocitinib is a selective inhibitor of Janus kinase 1 (JAK1). By reversibly blocking the ATP binding site, it inhibits JAK1-mediated signaling pathways for multiple cytokines involved in the pathogenesis of atopic dermatitis, including IL-4, IL-13, IL-31, and interferon-gamma. This modulation reduces inflammatory markers such as C-reactive protein (hsCRP) and various chemokines.

---

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [TGA] or at 20°C to 25°C with excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [FDA].
*   **Packaging:** Keep tablets in the original package to protect from moisture. The container system is child-resistant.
