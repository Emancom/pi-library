# Editorial Product Information: Liposomal Amphotericin B

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesises information from the following jurisdiction-specific Product Information (PI) documents: 
    - **Australian (TGA)**: AmBisome (liposomal amphotericin B) Injection.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical use in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Amphotericin B (as a liposomal formulation).
- **Chemical Class**: Polyene antifungal antibiotic.
- **Brand Names**: **AmBisome** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: J02AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Sterile, lyophilised powder for intravenous infusion. Each vial contains 50 mg of amphotericin B encapsulated in a liposome bilayer. 
- **Excipients of Note**: Contains approximately 900 mg of sucrose per vial [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Gilead Sciences [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**AmBisome** is indicated for:
- **Systemic Fungal Infections**: Treatment of infections caused by susceptible organisms [Australian].
- **Empirical Treatment**: Treatment of presumed fungal infections in febrile neutropenic patients who have failed to respond to broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis**: Prevention of systemic *Candida*, *Aspergillus*, and *Cryptococcus* infections in liver transplant patients at high risk [Australian].
- **Visceral Leishmaniasis**: Treatment in both immunocompetent and immunocompromised patients (efficacy primarily demonstrated against *L. infantum*) [Australian].
- **Cryptococcal Meningitis**: Treatment of systemic mycoses and high-dose induction therapy in HIV-associated cryptococcal meningitis [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Systemic Mycoses**: Initially 3.0 mg/kg/day, which may be increased stepwise to 5.0 mg/kg/day. Therapy should continue for a minimum of 14 days [Australian].
- **Mucormycosis**: Recommended dose is 5 to 10 mg/kg/day. In cases of brain involvement or solid-organ transplant, 10 mg/kg/day is recommended. Rapid escalation is advised over slow titration [Australian].
- **Cryptococcal Meningitis**: 3 to 6 mg/kg/day for 14 days [Australian].
- **HIV-associated Cryptococcal Meningitis (High-dose Induction)**: A single dose of 10 mg/kg on Day 1, administered in combination with flucytosine and fluconazole as part of a 14-day regimen [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis (Liver Transplant)**: 1 mg/kg/day for five consecutive days post-transplantation [Australian].
- **Visceral Leishmaniasis**: 
    - Immunocompetent: 1.0 to 1.5 mg/kg/day for 21 days OR 3.0 mg/kg/day for 10 days. 
    - Immunocompromised (e.g., HIV+): 1.0 to 1.5 mg/kg/day for 21 days [Australian].
- **Febrile Neutropenia**: 3 mg/kg/day for 10 to 14 days, or until neutropenia resolves [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution**: Vials must be reconstituted with 12 mL Sterile Water for Injection to achieve 4 mg/mL. Vials must be shaken vigorously for at least 30 seconds [Australian].
- **Dilution**: The reconstituted concentrate must be further diluted with 5% Glucose Injection to a final concentration of 0.20 mg/mL to 2.00 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Filtration**: Use the provided 5-micron filter during preparation. In-line filters may be used, provided the mean pore diameter is **not less than 1.0 micron** [Australian].
- **Compatibility**: **AmBisome** is physically incompatible with saline and must not be mixed with other drugs or electrolytes. Flush existing IV lines with 5% Glucose prior to administration [Australian].
- **Infusion Rate**: Administer over 30 to 60 minutes. For doses greater than 5 mg/kg, a 2-hour infusion is recommended [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of the excipients (phospholipids, cholesterol, sucrose, tocopherol) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Nephrotoxicity**: While significantly less toxic than conventional amphotericin B, **AmBisome** remains potentially nephrotoxic. Monitor renal function closely, particularly in patients with pre-existing renal impairment or those on concomitant nephrotoxic agents [Australian].
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs)**: Severe anaphylactic and anaphylactoid reactions have been reported. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue the infusion immediately [Australian].
- **Back Pain Syndrome**: A specific infusion-related reaction characterised by severe low back pain (sometimes with chest tightness) can occur within minutes of starting the infusion. This typically resolves rapidly upon stopping the infusion and may not recur with slower infusion rates [Australian].
- **Electrolyte Monitoring**: Regular monitoring (at least weekly) of serum potassium and magnesium is required. Supplementation may be necessary due to the risk of hypokalaemia [Australian].
- **Sucrose Content**: Diabetic patients should be alerted to the ~900 mg of sucrose contained in each vial [Australian].
- **Leucocyte Transfusions**: Acute pulmonary toxicity has been reported when amphotericin B is given during or shortly after leucocyte transfusions. Space these infusions as far apart as possible and monitor pulmonary function [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are based on clinical trial data (N=834) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Pyrexia (fever), rigors, chills, hypokalaemia |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, headache, tachycardia, vasodilation (flushing), dyspnoea, rash, back pain, increased serum creatinine/BUN, increased alkaline phosphatase, hyponatraemia, hyperbilirubinaemia |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anaphylactoid reactions, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, convulsions, cardiac failure, hypotension, respiratory failure |
| **Rare/Not Known** | Cardiac arrest, arrhythmia, rhabdomyolysis (associated with hypokalaemia), renal failure, angioneurotic oedema |

- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Nephrotoxicity and anaphylaxis are the most significant risks. Liposomal amphotericin B is generally better tolerated than conventional amphotericin B deoxycholate regarding acute infusion toxicity [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Nephrotoxic Agents**: Concomitant use with cyclosporin, aminoglycosides, and pentamidine may increase the risk of renal damage. Use with caution and monitor renal function frequently [Australian].
- **Antineoplastics**: Cisplatin and nitrogen mustards may enhance renal toxicity and the risk of bronchospasm or hypotension [Australian].
- **Corticosteroids/ACTH**: May exacerbate hypokalaemia induced by amphotericin B [Australian].
- **Digitalis Glycosides**: Amphotericin B-induced hypokalaemia may potentiate digitalis toxicity [Australian].
- **Skeletal Muscle Relaxants**: Hypokalaemia may enhance the effect of non-depolarising muscle relaxants (e.g., tubocurarine) [Australian].
- **Flucytosine**: While often used synergistically, amphotericin B may increase flucytosine toxicity by slowing its renal excretion [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category B3** [Australian]. Safety in pregnant women has not been established. Use only if the potential benefits outweigh the risks. Animal studies showed no teratogenic potential, though some visceral variations occurred at maternally toxic doses [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. It is recommended that breastfeeding be discontinued during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility**: Animal studies showed a small reduction in the fertility index at maternally toxic doses (15 mg/kg/day in rats) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safe and effective use has been reported in children (from >30 days old) at doses comparable to adults on a mg/kg basis. No unique paediatric adverse events were reported in trials [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No systematic studies have been conducted in patients over 65, but the drug has been used in this population without reports of unique problems [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: **AmBisome** has been used successfully in patients with pre-existing renal impairment. For dialysis patients, administration should only commence **after** the dialysis session is completed [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No specific data are available to make dosage recommendations [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Amphotericin B binds to ergosterol in the fungal cell membrane, leading to increased permeability, leakage of intracellular components, and cell death. The liposomal formulation facilitates higher plasma concentrations and targeted delivery to the liver and spleen while reducing exposure to the kidneys [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Highly protein-bound. Volume of distribution (Vd) is significantly lower than conventional amphotericin B (approx. 24–30 L).
    - **Metabolism**: Metabolic pathways are not fully understood.
    - **Elimination**: Terminal half-life is approximately 26–38 hours. Clearance ranges from 0.5 to 1.3 L/hr. Dialysis does not significantly affect elimination [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **AmBiLoad Study**: Compared standard (3 mg/kg) vs. loading dose (10 mg/kg) for invasive filamentous fungal infections. Standard dosing showed 50% response and 72% survival at 12 weeks; loading doses showed higher toxicity without improved efficacy [Australian].
- **Study 94-0-002**: Demonstrated that **AmBisome** (3 mg/kg) had equivalent efficacy to conventional amphotericin B (0.6 mg/kg) for empirical treatment of febrile neutropenia but with significantly lower rates of chills, hypokalaemia, and nephrotoxicity [Australian].
- **Cryptococcal Meningitis**: A single 10 mg/kg dose was shown to be non-inferior to 7 days of amphotericin B deoxycholate in terms of 10-week mortality for HIV-associated meningitis [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials**: Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Product**: Contains no preservatives; should ideally be used immediately. If necessary, it may be stored at 2-8°C for up to 24 hours. Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Handling**: Aseptic technique is mandatory. Discard any partially used vials [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification**: Rx-only [Australian].
