# Editorial Product Information: Amivantamab

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for **amivantamab**. It is designed as a structured clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
*   **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved documentation for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Amivantamab
*   **Chemical Class:** Fully-human immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1)-based bispecific antibody targeting epidermal growth factor (EGF) and mesenchymal-epidermal transition (MET) receptors.
*   **Brand Name:** **Rybrevant** [Australian]
*   **ATC Code:** L01FX18
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrate for solution for infusion; 350 mg per 7 mL single-use vial (50 mg/mL).
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion after dilution.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Janssen-Cilag Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
**Amivantamab** is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) in the following settings [Australian]:
*   **Combination with Lazertinib:** First-line treatment for patients with EGFR exon 19 deletions or exon 21 L858R substitution mutations.
*   **Combination with Carboplatin and Pemetrexed:** 
    *   Treatment of patients with EGFR exon 19 deletions or exon 21 L858R substitution mutations following progression on or after an EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI).
    *   First-line treatment for patients with activating EGFR exon 20 insertion mutations.
*   **Monotherapy:** Treatment of patients with activating EGFR exon 20 insertion mutations whose disease has progressed on or after platinum-based chemotherapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults ≥ 18 years)
Dosing is weight-based using baseline body weight. The initial dose is administered as a split infusion over two days (Week 1, Day 1 and Day 2) to mitigate infusion-related reactions (IRRs).

*   **Weight < 80 kg:** 1050 mg
*   **Weight ≥ 80 kg:** 1400 mg

### Dosing Schedule [Australian]
*   **Monotherapy or with Lazertinib:** Once weekly for 4 weeks, followed by once every two weeks (Q2W) starting at Week 5.
*   **With Carboplatin and Pemetrexed:** Once weekly for 4 weeks, followed by once every three weeks (Q3W) starting at Week 7. Maintenance doses are higher in this regimen (e.g., 1750 mg for <80 kg and 2100 mg for ≥80 kg starting at Week 7).

### Pre-infusion Medications
To reduce the risk of IRRs, the following must be administered prior to the initial infusion:
*   **Antihistamines:** (e.g., diphenhydramine 25–50 mg or equivalent).
*   **Antipyretics:** (e.g., paracetamol 500–1000 mg).
*   **Glucocorticoids:** Dexamethasone (8 mg orally) twice daily for two days before the first dose, plus 8 mg orally one hour before infusion and 10–20 mg IV 45–120 minutes before infusion.
*   **VTE Prophylaxis:** When used with lazertinib, anticoagulant prophylaxis is recommended for the first four months of therapy. Vitamin K antagonists are not recommended.

### Administration Instructions
*   **Preparation:** Dilute in 250 mL of 5% dextrose or 0.9% sodium chloride. Use an in-line, low protein-binding 0.2-micrometre filter.
*   **Rate:** Initial Day 1 rate is 50 mL/hr. Subsequent rates may increase if tolerated.
*   **Access:** Use a peripheral vein for the first two weeks to manage IRRs; central access may be used thereafter.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Hypersensitivity to **amivantamab** or any of its excipients (disodium edetate, histidine, methionine, polysorbate 80, sucrose).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs):** Highly common (61%), primarily occurring during the first infusion. Symptoms include chills, dyspnoea, flushing, and nausea. Management requires split dosing, premedication, and potential rate reduction or permanent discontinuation for Grade 4/anaphylactic reactions.
*   **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** Reported in 2.7% of patients, including fatal cases. Withhold if suspected; permanently discontinue if confirmed.
*   **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Significant risk (36%) when used with lazertinib, including deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism. Prophylactic anticoagulation is strongly recommended for the first 4 months.
*   **Dermatological Toxicity:** Rash (including acneiform dermatitis) and nail toxicity (paronychia) are frequent. Severe reactions, including Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), require permanent discontinuation.
*   **Ocular Toxicity:** Keratitis and uveitis have occurred. Patients with worsening eye symptoms should be referred to an ophthalmologist.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are presented by frequency based on clinical trial data [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Rash, nail toxicity, infusion-related reactions, fatigue, oedema, stomatitis, nausea, constipation, diarrhoea, musculoskeletal pain, paraesthesia, decreased appetite, and cough. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | VTE, pneumonia, ILD/pneumonitis, COVID-19, conjunctivitis, dizziness, headache, and visual impairment. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Uveitis, toxic epidermal necrolysis. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** VTE (11%), pneumonia (4%), and ILD (2.9%) were frequently reported serious reactions in combination trials.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted. As a monoclonal antibody, **amivantamab** is not expected to be cleared via cytochrome P450 enzymes.
*   **Combinations:** When used with lazertinib, carboplatin, or pemetrexed, consult the specific Product Information for those agents regarding their respective interaction profiles.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Based on its mechanism, **amivantamab** may cause fetal harm. Female and male patients of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 3 months following the final dose.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 3 months after the last dose due to the risk of serious adverse reactions in the infant.
*   **Fertility:** Data are not available regarding the effect on human fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients aged 17 years and younger.
*   **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between patients ≥ 65 years and younger patients. No dose adjustment is required.
*   **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is necessary for patients with mild or moderate renal impairment. Data are unavailable for severe impairment.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is necessary for patients with mild hepatic impairment. Data are unavailable for moderate or severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** A bispecific antibody that binds the extracellular domains of EGFR and MET. It inhibits ligand binding, promotes receptor degradation, and induces immune effector cell-mediated destruction of tumour cells via antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and trogocytosis.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Mean volume of distribution is 5.34 L.
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily via catabolic pathways. Terminal half-life is approximately 13.7 days.
    *   **Steady State:** Reached by Week 13.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **MARIPOSA (1st-line EGFR-mutant NSCLC):** Combination with lazertinib showed a significant improvement in progression-free survival (PFS) compared to osimertinib (23.7 vs 16.6 months; HR 0.70).
*   **MARIPOSA-2 (Post-osimertinib EGFR-mutant NSCLC):** Combination with chemotherapy improved PFS compared to chemotherapy alone (6.3 vs 4.2 months; HR 0.48).
*   **PAPILLON (1st-line EGFR Exon 20 insertion):** Combination with chemotherapy improved PFS compared to chemotherapy alone (11.4 vs 6.7 months; HR 0.40).
*   **CHRYSALIS (Post-platinum EGFR Exon 20 insertion):** Monotherapy demonstrated an overall response rate (ORR) of 43%.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Protect from light by storing in the original carton.
*   **Diluted Solution:** Use immediately. If necessary, diluted solution may be stored for up to 10 hours (including infusion time) at room temperature (15°C to 25°C) in room light.
