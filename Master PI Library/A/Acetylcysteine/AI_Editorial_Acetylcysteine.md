# Editorial Product Information: Acetylcysteine (Intravenous)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory and clinical data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Acetylcysteine (N-acetyl-mercapto-alanine; the N-acetyl derivative of L-cysteine).
- **Brand Names:** Acetadote Concentrated Injection [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** V03AB23 (Antidotes, paracetamol).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated injection for intravenous (IV) infusion, 200 mg/mL (6 g in 30 mL vials) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion (must be diluted prior to use).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Phebra Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Paracetamol Overdose:** Indicated as an antidote for paracetamol poisoning to prevent or minimize hepatotoxicity [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
The standard treatment course consists of three consecutive intravenous infusions, delivering a total dose of 300 mg/kg body weight over approximately 20 hours.

1.  **Initial Infusion:** 150 mg/kg diluted in 200 mL of 5% glucose, infused over 15 to 60 minutes.
2.  **Second Infusion:** 50 mg/kg diluted in 500 mL of 5% glucose, infused over the following 4 hours.
3.  **Third Infusion:** 100 mg/kg diluted in 1000 mL of 5% glucose, infused over the final 16 hours.

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** Therapy is most effective when initiated within 10 hours of paracetamol ingestion. It may be considered in high-risk patients presenting later than 15 hours after ingestion, though its role is less established in late presentations.
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted in 5% glucose solution. 
- **Monitoring:** Do not delay treatment while awaiting paracetamol plasma assay results. If the initial level is below the treatment nomogram, therapy may be discontinued. If the level is above the line, the full course should be completed even if subsequent levels fall below the reference line.

### Treatment Guide (Volume calculation)
- **Initial:** 0.75x mL of **acetylcysteine** (where x = weight in kg).
- **Second:** 0.25x mL of **acetylcysteine**.
- **Third:** 0.5x mL of **acetylcysteine**.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **acetylcysteine** or any excipients.
- **Previous Reactions:** History of previous anaphylactic reactions to **acetylcysteine** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylactoid Reactions:** Commonly occur during or at the end of the loading dose. Symptoms include airway obstruction (bronchospasm), angioedema, hypotension, tachycardia, and urticaria. If reactions occur, the infusion should be temporarily stopped and may be restarted at a slower rate after administration of an antihistamine.
- **Asthma:** Use with extreme caution in patients with asthma or a history of bronchospasm.
- **Gastrointestinal Bleeding:** Use caution in patients with a history of oesophageal varices or peptic ulceration, as **acetylcysteine**-induced vomiting may increase the risk of haemorrhage.
- **Fluid Overload:** Risk of hyponatraemia and seizures in patients weighing less than 40 kg or those on fluid restriction; fluid volumes should be adjusted accordingly.
- **Monitoring:** Daily liver function tests (LFTs), electrolytes, urea, creatinine, glucose, and coagulation studies (prothrombin time) are required. Monitor for metabolic acidosis and cardiac toxicity (ST changes).
- **Incompatibilities:** **Acetylcysteine** is incompatible with rubber and certain metals (iron, copper, nickel). It is compatible with silicone rubber and plastic.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are most frequent during the initial loading dose and are often dose-related.

| System / Organ | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Immune System** | Anaphylactoid reaction (common, approx. 17%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting |
| **Respiratory** | Bronchospasm, dyspnoea, stridor, respiratory arrest (rare) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Tachycardia, bradycardia, hypotension, hypertension, flushing, cardiac arrest |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | Urticaria, rash, angioedema, sweating, periorbital oedema |
| **General** | Injection site reaction, rigors, malaise, chest pain, face oedema |
| **Laboratory** | Hypokalaemia, acidosis, thrombocytopenia, deterioration of liver function |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Laboratory Tests:** May cause a false-positive reaction with reagent dipstick tests for urinary ketones [Australian].
- **General Interactions:** No specific drug-drug interaction data is available in the provided source.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B2. There is no evidence of teratogenicity in animal studies. While well-controlled studies in pregnant women are lacking, clinical experience has not demonstrated positive evidence of adverse fetal effects [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if **acetylcysteine** or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed some effects on male rat fertility at 60% of the maximum clinical dose, but no effects were observed at 15% of the clinical dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use the same mg/kg dosing regimen as adults. However, fluid volumes must be modified to account for weight and age to avoid potentially fatal fluid overload/hyponatraemia.
- **Elderly:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients over 65 years in controlled trials [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. Limited data exists for these populations; a risk-benefit assessment is required.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Paracetamol is metabolised into a toxic reactive metabolite (NAPQI) which is normally detoxified by glutathione. In overdose, glutathione stores are depleted. **Acetylcysteine** acts as a sulfhydryl (SH) group donor, restoring hepatic glutathione levels and acting as an alternative substrate for the detoxification of the toxic metabolite.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Deacetylated in the liver to cysteine; also oxidised to N-acetylcystine and N,N-diacetylcystine.
    - **Excretion:** Renal clearance accounts for approximately 30% of total body clearance. 
    - **Half-life:** Mean terminal half-life is 1.95 hours (reduced **acetylcysteine**) and 5.58 hours (total **acetylcysteine**).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Infusion Rate Safety Study:** An Australian multicentre study compared 15-minute vs. 60-minute loading dose infusions. Anaphylactoid reactions occurred in 18% of the 15-minute group vs. 14% of the 60-minute group. No hepatotoxicity occurred in patients treated within 8 hours of ingestion.
- **Paediatric Safety:** An observational study of 148 paediatric patients showed that 13% of those treated with IV **acetylcysteine** experienced adverse reactions (rash, flushing, anaphylactoid symptoms), with no deaths reported.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store unopened vials below 25°C and protect from light.
- **Diluted Solution:** To reduce microbiological hazard, use immediately. If necessary, store at 2°C to 8°C for no more than 24 hours.
- **Appearance:** The solution may turn light pink or purple after the stopper is punctured; this does not affect product quality.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
