# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines and the specific manufacturer’s PI before clinical application.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic name); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders (Adult and Paediatric)
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US]. 
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Reported Dose:** 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal insufficiency has not been evaluated. Chronic high doses of the oral form in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, targeting a free carnitine concentration between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Very Common Effects
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials for ESRD patients (incidence ≥5% regardless of causality):
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), asthenia (8–12%), chest pain (6–15%), fever (5–12%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase (10–18%), pharyngitis (15–27%).

### Secondary and Rare Effects
- Body odor, gastritis, anemia, hypercalcemia, and hypervolemia have been noted [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Anaphylaxis:** As part of hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when administered with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels following treatment initiation or dose adjustment in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate human studies exist; use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in cows indicate increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (mg/kg) as described in Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources; however, generally requires caution regarding renal function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis. Use caution with oral formulations due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine may promote the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).
- **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed pre-dialysis trough concentrations increased from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to ~190 µmol/L by week 8 [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed a dose-proportional increase in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
