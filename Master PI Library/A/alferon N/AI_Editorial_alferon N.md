# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA). 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with a primary or secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or infusion.
    - Acute Crisis: A loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and not less than every 6 hours.
    - Subsequent maintenance doses: 50 mg/kg/day or as clinically indicated. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Adjustments: Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Monitoring and Administration Instructions
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before parenteral therapy.
- Monitor weekly and monthly; target free carnitine levels are between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These can occur within minutes of intravenous administration, primarily in ESRD patients. Discontinue immediately if reactions occur [US].
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **INR Monitoring:** Increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) has been reported when used concomitantly with warfarin. Monitor INR levels at initiation and following dose adjustments [US].
- **Renal Function:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event frequency in clinical trials of ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% regardless of causality):

| Body System | Adverse Event | Frequency (Levocarnitine 10–40 mg/kg) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction, Pain, Flu syndrome, Headache, Abdominal pain, Fever | Common (>10%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, Hypotension, Tachycardia, Chest pain | Common (>10%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, Vomiting, Nausea | Common (>10%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, Cough increase, Dyspnea, Rhinitis | Common (>10%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, Paresthesia | Common (>10%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, Hypervolemia | Common (5-10%) |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity [US].
- Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Concomitant use may result in an increased INR. Management: Frequent monitoring of prothrombin time/INR is recommended [US].
- **Other Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal therapy against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is consistent with adult weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. However, oral levocarnitine is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities resulting from the accumulation of toxic acylCoA compounds [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, ~76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to trough levels between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies at 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative.
- **Stability (Admixture):** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

- **United States:** Rx-only [US].
