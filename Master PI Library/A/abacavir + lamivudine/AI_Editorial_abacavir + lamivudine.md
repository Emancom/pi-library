## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) infection in adults and adolescents from 12 years of age as part of antiretroviral combination therapy [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.1].
- Note: This fixed-dose product is indicated only for patients weighing ≥40 kg because it is not dose-adjustable [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].

## 4. Dosage and Administration
General dosing (adult and adolescent)
- Recommended dose: one tablet once daily in adults and adolescents (from 12 years) who weigh ≥40 kg [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].

Specific administration notes
- Can be taken with or without food [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].
- Do not administer this fixed-dose tablet to patients who weigh less than 40 kg because dose reduction is not possible with the single formulation [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].
- The product should not be prescribed for patients requiring lamivudine dose adjustments (e.g. creatinine clearance <30 mL/min); use separate abacavir and lamivudine preparations when dose adjustment or discontinuation of one component may be required [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].

Renal impairment
- Not recommended in patients with creatinine clearance <30 mL/min because lamivudine dose reduction is required in renal impairment and the fixed-dose tablet cannot be adjusted [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].
- Patients with creatinine clearance 30–49 mL/min receiving the fixed-dose tablet may experience 1.6–3.3-fold higher lamivudine AUC compared with patients with CrCl ≥50 mL/min; such patients should be monitored for lamivudine-related adverse events (notably neutropenia and anaemia). If dose adjustment is required, discontinue the fixed-dose tablet and use individual components to allow lamivudine dose reduction [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].

Hepatic impairment
- A dose reduction of abacavir may be required in mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A); because dose adjustment cannot be performed with the fixed-dose tablet, consider using separate abacavir and lamivudine products when dose reduction is necessary. The fixed-dose tablet is not recommended in moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C) [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].

Elderly
- Pharmacokinetics have not been studied in patients over 65 years; consider age-related decreases in hepatic, renal, or cardiac function and concomitant medicines [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].

Paediatric
- Not recommended for children <12 years or for those weighing <40 kg due to inability to adjust dose with this fixed combination; refer to individual product information for dosing of separate components [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].

## 5. Contraindications
(All contraindications listed in the provided source are below — jurisdiction indicated where relevant.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
Boxed/major warnings
- **Hypersensitivity reaction to abacavir can be life‑threatening and, in rare cases, fatal. The drug must never be restarted following a hypersensitivity reaction to abacavir.** Testing for HLA‑B*5701 is recommended before initiating abacavir and before re‑starting in patients of unknown HLA‑B*5701 status who previously tolerated abacavir. Abacavir-related hypersensitivity can occur at any time but most commonly within the first 6 weeks of therapy and frequently involves 2 or more organ systems (fever, rash, gastrointestinal, constitutional, or respiratory symptoms). If hypersensitivity is suspected, stop abacavir immediately; do not restart until hypersensitivity is excluded, and if confirmed do not re-challenge with any abacavir-containing product [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- Lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis, including fatal cases, have been reported with nucleoside analogues including abacavir and lamivudine; suspend therapy if clinical or laboratory findings suggest lactic acidosis with or without hepatitis [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

General warnings and precautions
- Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome may occur after initiating antiretroviral therapy; evaluate inflammatory symptoms promptly and treat as appropriate. Autoimmune disorders (e.g. Graves’ disease, polymyositis, Guillain‑Barré syndrome) have been reported in this context [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- Post‑treatment exacerbations of hepatitis B have been observed after stopping lamivudine in patients with chronic hepatitis B; monitor liver function tests and HBV markers when stopping therapy in coinfected patients [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- Fat redistribution (fat loss or fat gain) has been reported during combination antiretroviral therapy; long‑term consequences are unknown [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- Serum lipids and blood glucose may increase during antiretroviral therapy; consider monitoring and manage clinically as appropriate [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- Potential association between abacavir use and myocardial infarction has been observed in some observational studies, while meta-analyses of randomized trials have not confirmed excess risk; the evidence is inconsistent and inconclusive. Consider and minimise modifiable coronary risk factors when prescribing [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- Therapy-experienced patients with prolonged prior NRTI exposure or virus with multiple NRTI resistance mutations may have limited response to abacavir; consider potential cross‑resistance when selecting regimens [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- Do not use the fixed‑dose tablet as the sole backbone with other NRTIs in triple nucleoside regimens without consideration: randomized trials showed higher early virological failure for some triple‑nucleoside regimens when compared with regimens combining two nucleosides plus a different pharmacological class agent [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- Patients should be counselled to read the consumer information and to retain any alert card supplied; patients who have experienced an abacavir hypersensitivity reaction should dispose of remaining product to avoid inadvertent re‑challenge [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity throughout therapy and particularly during the first 6 weeks [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- In patients with CrCl 30–49 mL/min receiving the fixed-dose tablet, monitor for lamivudine-related adverse events (notably neutropenia and anaemia); if these occur consider switching to individual components to allow lamivudine dose adjustment [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].
- Consider periodic monitoring of liver function tests and HBV markers when stopping therapy in patients with chronic hepatitis B [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid chronic coadministration of sorbitol-containing medicines with lamivudine oral solution where possible because sorbitol reduces lamivudine exposure; if chronic coadministration cannot be avoided, consider more frequent HIV‑1 viral load monitoring [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- If reintroduction of abacavir is contemplated after a non‑hypersensitivity discontinuation and HLA‑B*5701 status is unknown, reintroduction should occur only where medical care is readily available [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

## 8. Drug Interactions
Pharmacokinetic interactions and clinically relevant points (as reported)
- Abacavir and lamivudine show no clinically significant interaction with each other in clinical studies [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- Abacavir is not significantly metabolised by major CYP enzymes (CYP3A4, CYP2C9, CYP2D6) and shows limited potential to inhibit these enzymes; abacavir has potential to inhibit CYP1A1 in vitro. Overall, there is little potential for interactions via major P450 enzymes with abacavir [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- Lamivudine has limited metabolism and low plasma protein binding and is predominantly cleared renally by active organic cation secretion; therefore metabolic interactions are unlikely, but interactions affecting renal elimination should be considered [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- Ethanol: concomitant ethanol alters abacavir metabolism and increases abacavir AUC by about 41%; this change is not considered clinically significant by the source [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- Methadone: co‑administration altered abacavir Cmax and tmax but not AUC; abacavir increased mean methadone clearance by ~22% in one study — usually not clinically relevant but occasional methadone dose re‑titration may be required [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- Riociguat: in vitro CYP1A1 inhibition by abacavir and a clinical observation with abacavir/dolutegravir/lamivudine showed approximately three‑fold higher riociguat AUC compared with historical controls; riociguat dose may need adjustment when co‑administered with abacavir‑containing regimens — consult riociguat labelling [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- Sorbitol (oral solution): coadministration with single‑dose lamivudine oral solution produced dose‑dependent decreases in lamivudine exposure (AUC and Cmax); avoid chronic coadministration of sorbitol‑containing medicines with lamivudine where possible [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- Trimethoprim: co‑trimoxazole (160/800 mg) increases lamivudine exposure by ~40% due to trimethoprim; unless the patient has renal impairment, no lamivudine dose adjustment is required, but caution is advised in renal impairment [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- Emtricitabine: lamivudine may inhibit intracellular phosphorylation of emtricitabine and both share the same resistance mutation (M184V); lamivudine is not recommended for use in combination with emtricitabine or emtricitabine‑containing fixed‑dose combinations [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- Transporter effects: abacavir and lamivudine show no or weak inhibition of several transporters in vitro (OATP1B1/OATP1B3, BCRP, P‑gp) and are not expected to substantially affect substrates of these transporters at therapeutic concentrations; details and exceptions cited in source [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].

Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Co‑administration with other abacavir‑ or lamivudine‑containing products is contraindicated [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- Combining lamivudine with emtricitabine is not recommended due to potential antagonism at phosphorylation and shared resistance pathway (M184V) [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].

Management notes
- Avoid chronic sorbitol coadministration with lamivudine oral solution and monitor viral load if unavoidable [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- In renal impairment, consider lamivudine dose adjustment and use separate components if required [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].
- Check riociguat labelling and consider dose reduction if combining with abacavir‑containing regimens [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].

## 10. Use in Special Populations
Paediatric use
- The fixed‑dose tablet is not recommended for children <12 years or for those weighing <40 kg because dose adjustment is required for younger/smaller children and cannot be achieved with this product; refer to the individual components for paediatric dosing [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].

Elderly
- Pharmacokinetics not studied in patients >65 years; exercise caution due to potential age‑related organ function decline and concomitant medicines [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].

Renal impairment
- Not recommended in patients with creatinine clearance <30 mL/min because lamivudine dose reduction is necessary and cannot be effected with the fixed‑dose formulation [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].
- Patients with CrCl 30–49 mL/min may have 1.6–3.3× higher lamivudine AUC; monitor for lamivudine‑related adverse events and switch to individual components to permit lamivudine dose adjustment if toxicity develops [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].

Hepatic impairment
- Dose reduction of abacavir may be required in mild hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh A); because the fixed combination cannot be dose adjusted, use separate components if needed. The fixed‑dose tablet is not recommended in moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh B or C) [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].

## 11. Pharmacology
Mechanism of action
- The combination contains two nucleoside analogue reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs) active against HIV; both components act as nucleoside analogues interfering with viral reverse transcriptase [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.1].

Pharmacokinetics (selected points reported)
- Abacavir: not significantly metabolised by major CYP450 enzymes (CYP3A4, CYP2C9, CYP2D6); limited potential to inhibit CYP3A4; in vitro potential to inhibit CYP1A1. Ethanol increases abacavir AUC ~41% [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- Lamivudine: limited metabolism, low plasma protein binding, predominantly eliminated renally by active organic cation secretion (MATE/OCT pathways implicated); renal function affects lamivudine clearance and dosing [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5; Section 4.2].
- Drug‑transporter interactions: abacavir/lamivudine exhibit low or weak effects on several drug transporters in vitro and are generally not expected to substantially alter plasma concentrations of transporter substrates at therapeutic exposures; specific in vitro findings and select clinical exceptions are described in source [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].

Pharmacodynamics
- Cross‑resistance: resistance mutations to NRTIs can limit response to abacavir and lamivudine; consider potential cross‑resistance in therapy‑experienced patients [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

## 12. Clinical Trials
- The source PI refers to randomized, double‑blind, controlled studies that demonstrated higher rates of early virological failure with some triple nucleoside regimens (e.g. tenofovir, lamivudine and abacavir; tenofovir, lamivudine and didanosine) compared with regimens combining two nucleosides plus a different pharmacological class agent — this underpins guidance to use abacavir/lamivudine with agents from different classes rather than as part of triple‑nucleoside regimens alone [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- Therapy‑experienced patients with prior prolonged NRTI exposure or viruses with multiple NRTI resistance mutations had limited response to abacavir in clinical trials referenced by the source [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
