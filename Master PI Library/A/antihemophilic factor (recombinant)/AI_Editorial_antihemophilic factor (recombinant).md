# Editorial Product Information: antihemophilic factor (recombinant)

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **antihemophilic factor (recombinant)**
- Chemical class: Recombinant coagulation factor VIII (rFVIII) protein
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous (IV) infusion only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2008 [US]
- Last revision/update of source prescribing information: July 2022 [US]
- Withdrawal/suspension/restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Other jurisdictions' registration dates or revision dates: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment and control of bleeding episodes in persons with congenital hemophilia A (factor VIII deficiency).
- Perioperative (surgical) management requiring factor VIII replacement.
- Routine prophylaxis to reduce the frequency of bleeding episodes in persons with hemophilia A.
- Not indicated for treatment of von Willebrand's disease (product does not contain von Willebrand factor).

(Where jurisdictional variation exists in the provided sources, the above indications reflect the position stated in the available source information [US]. No alternative regional-only indications were specified in the provided sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General principles
- Administer intravenously after proper reconstitution only.
- Calculate dose using the empirical recovery: on average, 1 IU of factor VIII per kg body weight raises plasma factor VIII activity by approximately 2 IU/dL (or 2% of normal).
- Dose calculation:
  - Required units (IU) = body weight (kg) × desired factor VIII rise (IU/dL or % of normal) × 0.5 (IU/kg per IU/dL)
  - Alternatively: IU/dL (or % of normal) = Total dose (IU) / body weight (kg) × 2 [IU/dL]/[IU/kg]

### Dosing — Routine prophylaxis (starting regimens)
- Adults and adolescents (≥12 years): recommended starting regimen 30 IU/kg administered three times weekly.
- Children (<12 years): recommended starting regimen 25 IU/kg administered every other day.
- Children <12 years often have higher clearance (shorter half-life, larger volume of distribution, lower recovery) and may require more frequent or higher dosing; adjust based on clinical response and, if available, individual pharmacokinetic parameters.

### Dosing — On‑demand treatment and control of bleeding
- Titrate dose and frequency to the severity and location of bleed, severity of factor VIII deficiency, and clinical response. Maintain plasma factor VIII activity at or above target levels for the specified duration:
  - Minor bleeding (e.g., hemarthrosis, minor muscle or oral bleeds): Target 20–40 IU/dL (20–40%); repeat every 12–24 hours; at least 1 day or as clinically indicated.
  - Moderate bleeding (e.g., bleeding into muscles, mild head trauma, oral cavity bleeds): Target 30–60 IU/dL (30–60%); repeat every 12–24 hours; typically 3–4 days or until local hemostasis achieved.
  - Major bleeding (e.g., gastrointestinal, intracranial, intra-abdominal, intrathoracic bleeding, fractures): Target 60–100 IU/dL (60–100%); repeat every 8–24 hours; continue until bleeding resolved.

### Dosing — Perioperative management (surgery)
- Maintain plasma factor VIII activity at or above target levels perioperatively:
  - Minor procedures (including tooth extraction): Target 30–60 IU/dL; repeat every 12–24 hours; usually 3–4 days or until local hemostasis/wound healing achieved. For tooth extraction, a single infusion plus oral antifibrinolytic therapy within 1 hour may be sufficient.
  - Major surgery: Target 60–100 IU/dL; repeat every 8–24 hours; continue until threat resolved and adequate hemostasis and wound healing achieved. Individualize per surgical and patient factors.

### Administration instructions and reconstitution (summary)
- Use aseptic technique.
- Reconstitute lyophilized product with the supplied diluent according to the manufacturer’s reconstitution instructions for the specific kit provided with the product (product is available in kits that contain a diluent chamber and a powder chamber or vials and diluent).
- Transfer diluent into the powder chamber (or reconstitute vial per kit instructions), mix gently (do not shake vigorously) until powder is fully dissolved. Final solution should be clear to slightly opalescent and colorless; discard if particulate matter or discoloration is present.
- Remove most, but not all, air from the drug chamber prior to infusion as per reconstitution instructions.
- Administer reconstituted product using infusion set supplied with the kit or as directed. Do not mix reconstituted product in the same tubing or container with other medicinal products.
- If not used immediately, store reconstituted product upright with protective cap in place and use within 3 hours after reconstitution or after removal of the protective tip cap (manufacturer guidance).
- Polysorbate 80 is present in the reconstituted product and is known to increase extraction of di-(2-ethylhexyl) phthalate (DEHP) from polyvinyl chloride (PVC); consider this during preparation and administration. The infusion tubing included with the kit does not contain DEHP.
- For administrations requiring multiple vials or multiple prefilled syringes: combine reconstituted product into a larger sterile luer-lock syringe using aseptic technique and appropriate luer-to-luer connectors; connectors may not be supplied with all kits—obtain as needed.

### Titration and individualization
- Adjust dose or frequency based on:
  - Clinical response (control of bleeding),
  - Measured plasma factor VIII activity and recovery,
  - Individual pharmacokinetics (recovery, half-life) when clinically indicated.
- Use individual factor VIII recovery values and, if indicated, other pharmacokinetic characteristics to guide dosing when available.

### Missed dose
- Specific missed-dose instructions are not provided in available sources. Follow the individualized prophylaxis schedule established by the treating clinician; if a dose is missed, administer as soon as possible and resume the regular dosing schedule. For clinical guidance when a dose is missed, consult the treating haemophilia specialist. (Not specified in available sources for formal instructions.)

## 5. Contraindications
- Known life‑threatening immediate hypersensitivity reactions to the product or any component of the product, including anaphylaxis.
- Known hypersensitivity to non‑human mammalian proteins contained as trace impurities (e.g., hamster proteins) — do not administer to patients with known life-threatening immediate hypersensitivity to such components.

(These contraindications are stated in the available source [US]. No additional, region‑specific contraindications were provided in the available sources.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed/major warnings
- **Hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis:** Severe allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, have occurred with factor VIII products. **Discontinue use immediately and institute appropriate therapy** if hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis is suspected.
- **Inhibitor development:** Neutralizing antibodies (inhibitors) to factor VIII may develop and reduce efficacy. Monitor and investigate inadequate clinical or laboratory responses.

### General warnings and precautions
- Inform patients and caregivers about signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity (e.g., hives, pruritus, generalized urticaria, chest tightness, wheezing, hypotension) and instruct them to seek immediate medical attention if these occur.
- The product contains trace amounts of hamster (CHO-derived) proteins; patients may develop hypersensitivity to these non-human proteins.
- If expected plasma factor VIII levels are not attained or bleeding is not controlled with appropriate dosing, perform an assay that measures factor VIII inhibitor concentration.
- Reconstituted product contains polysorbate 80; consider DEHP extraction risk from PVC containers and tubing during preparation and storage.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor plasma factor VIII activity to confirm adequate levels and to guide dose and frequency when clinically indicated. Use the one‑stage clotting assay for measuring plasma factor VIII activity when indicated.
- Monitor patients for development of factor VIII inhibitors. If clinical/laboratory response is inadequate, perform inhibitor testing (Bethesda assay with Nijmegen modification recommended) and express results in Bethesda Units (BU). A positive test criterion in the clinical studies was ≥0.6 BU/mL (laboratory-dependent).
- Use individual recovery and/or pharmacokinetic data to guide dosing when clinically appropriate.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Pediatric pharmacokinetics: children have shorter half-lives, larger volumes of distribution, and lower recovery than adults; dosing may need to be increased or given more frequently in patients <12 years of age.
- Do not administer reconstituted product in the same tubing or container with other medicinal products.

Frequencies are classified as: very common (>10%), common (1–10%), uncommon (0.1–1%), rare (<0.1%), very rare (<0.01%). Where exact frequency was reported in sources, that numerical frequency is provided.

### Summary of adverse reactions observed in clinical trials and postmarketing
- Across clinical studies in previously treated patients (PTPs), the most commonly reported adverse reactions (≥10%) were:
  - Headache — 24% (very common)
  - Arthralgia — 23% (very common)
  - Pyrexia (fever) — 23% (very common)
  - Cough — 12% (very common)
- Other adverse reactions reported in ≥5% of subjects:
  - Diarrhea — 8% (common)
  - Vomiting — 8% (common)
  - Asthenia — 6% (common)
- Immunogenicity:
  - Development of factor VIII inhibitors occurred in 4 subjects across studies (overall 2.4%) — falls within the "common" range (1–10%) based on the provided dataset. Results varied by study; both transient and persistent low titer inhibitors were reported in surgical subjects; pediatric subjects accounted for some cases.
  - Anti‑CHO (Chinese hamster ovary) and anti‑TN8.2 antibodies were not detected in PTPs in the cited studies; isolated anti‑CHO cell antibodies were reported in one surgical subject without associated allergic reaction in one study.
- Postmarketing reports:
  - Inadequate therapeutic response has been reported (frequency not quantifiable from postmarketing data).

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- **antihemophilic factor (recombinant)** is a recombinant form of coagulation factor VIII that replaces deficient or dysfunctional endogenous factor VIII in patients with congenital hemophilia A, thereby facilitating the intrinsic pathway of blood coagulation and assisting in formation of a stable fibrin clot.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Empirical recovery: on average, 1 IU/kg raises plasma factor VIII activity by approximately 2 IU/dL.
- Pharmacokinetic differences by age:
  - Children display shorter half-lives, larger volumes of distribution, and lower recovery relative to adults; this necessitates consideration of higher or more frequent dosing in younger children.
- Specific numeric values for absorption, distribution volumes, clearance rates, and elimination half‑lives are not specified in the available source excerpts for inclusion beyond the noted pediatric differences.
- Measurement: plasma factor VIII activity is monitored using the one‑stage clotting assay when clinically indicated.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Increase in plasma factor VIII activity is proportional to administered dose according to the empirical recovery formula; target factor VIII activity levels are used to guide therapy for bleeding episodes and perioperative management.

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Summary of clinical development in previously treated patients (PTPs)
- Clinical evaluation included five completed studies (total N = 178), including four studies in adult and pediatric previously treated patients and two pivotal studies:
  - Pivotal study 1: n = 94 PTPs with severe to moderately severe hemophilia A (FVIII:C ≤2%) evaluated for routine prophylaxis and on‑demand treatment. These subjects received a total of 6,775 infusions in the study cohort.
  - Pivotal study 2 (surgical prophylaxis): n = 30 PTPs undergoing elective major surgery and expected to receive postoperative replacement for at least 6 days; resulted in 1,161 infusions.
- Overall exposure in clinical trials comprised 13,109 infusions with a median dose per infusion of 28 IU/kg (range 6–108 IU/kg).
- Pediatric exposure included 72 pediatric PTPs <17 years of age across studies (with 46 subjects <6 years, including 4 subjects 0 to <2 years; 4 subjects 6 to <12 years; and 22 adolescents 12 to <17 years).
- Immunogenicity/pivotal safety findings:
  - Immunogenicity (FVIII inhibitors) was prospectively assessed in 167 adult and pediatric PTPs with ≥50 exposure days. Across studies, 4 subjects developed factor VIII inhibitors (2.4%). Study‑specific and historical comparisons were used in Bayesian analyses to estimate population inhibitor rates; an upper 95% bound estimate for inhibitor rate was reported in source analyses (numerical estimate provided in the source text of the original PI).
  - Other antibody findings included isolated reports of anti‑CHO cell antibodies in single surgical subjects without allergic reactions; anti‑FVIII and anti‑CHO antibodies otherwise were largely not detected in PTP cohorts.
- Efficacy outcomes:
  - The dosing tables and perioperative targets described in Section 4 reflect the clinical regimens used and guidance established in the clinical trials and product labeling to achieve hemostatic control for bleeding episodes and surgical procedures.
