# Editorial Product Information: Amisulpride

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of amisulpride for use by healthcare professionals. It is designed to consolidate clinical data into a structured format for quick reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) mandated for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulatory guidelines for final prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Amisulpride (a substituted benzamide antipsychotic).
- **Brand Names:** **Amipride 400** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 400 mg film-coated, capsule-shaped tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrow Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenic Disorders:** Treatment of both acute and chronic schizophrenic disorders. This includes the management of prominent positive symptoms (e.g., hallucinations, delusions, thought disorders) and/or prominent negative symptoms (e.g., social and emotional withdrawal, blunted affect) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):**
    - **Acute Psychotic Episodes:** Recommended oral range is 400 mg/day to 800 mg/day.
    - **Maximum Dose:** In individual cases, the daily dose may be escalated to a maximum of 1,200 mg/day. Doses exceeding 1,200 mg/day have not been extensively safety-evaluated and are not recommended [Australian].
    - **Negative Symptoms:** Doses between 50 mg/day and 300 mg/day are noted for lower extrapyramidal symptom (EPS) incidence [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets should preferably be taken before meals [Australian].
    - Daily doses exceeding 400 mg must be administered in two divided doses (BID) [Australian].
- **Titration:** No specific titration schedule is required upon initiation; dosage should be tailored to the individual clinical response [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to amisulpride or any tablet excipients.
- **Prolactin-Dependent Tumors:** Concomitant pituitary gland prolactinomas or breast cancer [Australian].
- **Phaeochromocytoma:** Known or suspected catecholamine-secreting tumor [Australian].
- **Paediatrics:** Children up to the age of puberty [Australian].
- **Lactation:** Breastfeeding is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Drug Combinations:** 
    - Concurrent use of medications that induce *torsades de pointes*, including Class Ia antiarrhythmics (e.g., quinidine, disopyramide) and Class III antiarrhythmics (e.g., amiodarone, sotalol) [Australian].
    - Concurrent use of bepridil, cisapride, sultopride, thioridazine, methadone, IV erythromycin, IV vincamine, halofantrine, pentamidine, or sparfloxacin [Australian].
    - Combination with levodopa due to reciprocal antagonism [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** May be contraindicated in some cases of hepatic disease to prevent potential metabolic interference [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal; characterized by muscle rigidity, hyperthermia, autonomic instability, and elevated CPK. Discontinue therapy immediately if NMS is suspected [Australian].
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** Amisulpride causes dose-dependent QT prolongation, increasing the risk of serious ventricular arrhythmias (e.g., *torsades de pointes*). Monitor for bradycardia (<55 bpm), hypokalaemia, and congenital long QT syndrome before and during therapy [Australian].
- **Seizure Disorders:** Amisulpride lowers the seizure threshold; close monitoring is required for patients with a history of epilepsy [Australian].
- **Metabolic Effects:** 
    - **Hyperglycaemia/Diabetes:** Significant increases in blood glucose have been reported. Monitor fasting glucose in patients with risk factors (obesity, family history) or established diabetes [Australian].
    - **Hyperprolactinaemia:** Amisulpride increases plasma prolactin, which may lead to galactorrhoea, amenorrhoea, gynaecomastia, or impotence. Caution is advised in patients with a history of breast cancer [Australian].
- **Cerebrovascular Events:** A 3-fold increase in stroke risk has been observed in elderly patients with dementia treated with atypical antipsychotics [Australian].
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Cases of VTE, sometimes fatal, have been reported; identify and manage VTE risk factors before treatment [Australian].
- **Withdrawal:** Abrupt cessation of high doses may cause withdrawal symptoms or involuntary movement disorders; gradual tapering is recommended [Australian].
- **Other Precautions:** Use with caution in Parkinson’s disease (may worsen symptoms) and in patients with sleep apnoea or those using CNS depressants [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are categorized as: Very common (≥10%), Common (1–10%), Uncommon (0.1–1%), Rare (<0.1%).

| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous System** | Very Common | Extrapyramidal symptoms (tremor, rigidity, hypokinesia, akathisia, hypersalivation, dyskinesia) |
| | Common | Acute dystonia, somnolence, insomnia, anxiety, agitation |
| | Uncommon | Tardive dyskinesia, seizures, confusion |
| | Rare | Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS) |
| **Endocrine** | Common | Hyperprolactinaemia (reversible upon discontinuation) |
| | Rare | Benign pituitary tumors (e.g., prolactinoma) |
| **Metabolic** | Uncommon | Hyperglycaemia, hypertriglyceridemia, hypercholesterolaemia |
| | Rare | Hyponatraemia, SIADH |
| **Cardiac/Vascular** | Common | QT prolongation, hypotension |
| | Uncommon | Bradycardia, hypertension |
| | Rare | Ventricular arrhythmias (*torsades de pointes*), VTE |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Common | Constipation, nausea, vomiting, dry mouth |
| | Uncommon | Elevated hepatic enzymes (transaminases), hepatocellular injury |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Agranulocytosis (rare), *torsades de pointes*, NMS, and fatal VTE [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Contraindicated Combinations:** Levodopa (antagonism); QT-prolonging agents (e.g., Class Ia and III antiarrhythmics, methadone, IV erythromycin) [Australian].
- **Not Recommended Combinations:** 
    - **Alcohol:** Potentiation of sedative effects.
    - **QT-Prolonging/Bradycardic Agents:** Beta-blockers, diltiazem, verapamil, clonidine, digitalis.
    - **Hypokalaemic Agents:** Diuretics, stimulant laxatives, IV amphotericin B, glucocorticoids [Australian].
- **Combinations Requiring Caution:**
    - **CNS Depressants:** Narcotics, anaesthetics, benzodiazepines, and barbiturates (increased sedation).
    - **Antihypertensives:** Potential for additive hypotensive effects.
    - **Clozapine:** May lead to increased plasma levels of amisulpride [Australian].
    - **Lithium:** While a low-dose study showed no PK interaction, a small trend in QT prolongation was noted [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category C** [Australian].
- **Pregnancy Clinical Considerations:** Amisulpride crosses the placenta. Neonates exposed during the third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal symptoms and/or withdrawal symptoms (e.g., agitation, respiratory distress, feeding disorders). Use is not recommended unless the clinical benefit justifies the potential fetal risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Amisulpride has been detected in breast milk. Use is **contraindicated** during lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, male fertility was unaffected, but female fertility showed a temporary reduction in mating behavior [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children up to puberty. Efficacy and safety in adolescents (puberty to 18 years) are not established; use in this age group is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** High risk of hypotension and sedation. Use with extreme caution. There is an increased risk of death in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Amisulpride is renally eliminated. 
    - **CrCl 30–60 mL/min:** Reduce dose to one-half.
    - **CrCl 10–30 mL/min:** Reduce dose to one-third.
    - **CrCl <10 mL/min:** Use with extreme caution; experience is very limited [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosage reduction is generally not required as the drug is weakly metabolised, though caution is advised in moderate to severe cases [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Amisulpride is a selective antagonist with high affinity for human dopaminergic D2 and D3 receptor subtypes. It lacks affinity for D1, D4, D5, serotonin, alpha-adrenergic, histamine, or cholinergic receptors. At low doses, it preferentially blocks presynaptic D2/D3 receptors (improving negative symptoms); at higher doses, it blocks postsynaptic receptors in limbic structures (improving positive symptoms) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 48%. Peak plasma levels occur at 3.5 hours post-dose. High-carbohydrate meals significantly decrease AUC and Cmax.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 5.8 L/kg. Plasma protein binding is low (16%).
    - **Metabolism:** Weakly metabolised into two inactive metabolites (~4% of dose).
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal (50% of an IV dose excreted unchanged in urine). Elimination half-life is approximately 12 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Data:** Efficacy was established in eleven Phase II and III trials (n=1,933). In acute schizophrenia, amisulpride was as effective as haloperidol, flupenthixol, and risperidone. 
- **Dose-Response:** A 4-week trial (n=319) demonstrated that doses of 400 mg and 800 mg/day were significantly superior to 100 mg/day (subtherapeutic control) in improving positive symptoms (BPRS and PANSS scores) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
- **Special Handling:** Unused medicine should be disposed of via local pharmacy programs [Australian].
