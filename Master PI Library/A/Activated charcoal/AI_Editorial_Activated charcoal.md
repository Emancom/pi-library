# Editorial Product Information: Activated Charcoal

## Editorial Note

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Activated charcoal (Physical adsorbent).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Carbosorb X** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Viscous suspension: 0.2 g/mL (50 g per 250 mL bottle) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral or via nasogastric/orogastric tube.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Phebra Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Poisoning and Drug Overdosage:** Treatment of toxic substances or medications ingested orally [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
- Administration is most effective when performed as soon as possible, ideally within one hour of poison ingestion [Australian].
- **Method:** Shake vigorously for at least 30 seconds before use. May be administered orally or via tube (dilute with water if necessary for tube administration) [Australian].

### Adult and Pediatric (12 Years and Over) Dosing
- **Single Dose:** 1 g/kg bodyweight, up to a maximum of 50 g [Australian]. 
- **Repeat Dose Regimens:** Indicated for substances with enterohepatic recirculation, slow-release formulations, or "gastrointestinal dialysis" (e.g., carbamazepine, dapsone, phenobarbitone, quinine, or theophylline).
    - Initial dose: 1 g/kg bodyweight.
    - Subsequent doses: 12.5 g per hour or equivalent doses every 2–6 hours until the risk of toxicity has subsided [Australian].

### Pediatric (1 Month to 11 Years) Dosing
- **Single Dose:** 1 g/kg bodyweight, up to a maximum of 50 g [Australian].
- **Repeat Doses:** Administered only when necessary and requires strict monitoring of fluid and electrolyte status [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Ingestion of Corrosives:** Contraindicated in poisoning involving strong acids or alkalis [Australian].
- **Low Adsorptive Capacity:** Contraindicated for substances where charcoal has poor affinity, including:
    - Cyanides
    - Alcohols (ethanol, methanol, ethylene glycol)
    - Hydrocarbons
    - Metals/Salts (ferrous sulfate and other iron salts, lithium)
    - Specific medications (tolbutamide, other sulfonylureas, malathion, dicophane) [Australian].
- **Anatomical/Clinical Factors:** 
    - Patients with an unprotected airway [Australian].
    - Gastrointestinal tract that is not anatomically intact [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Airway Protection:** Aspiration of charcoal is a severe complication and may be fatal. Patients with impaired gag reflexes, drowsiness, or those at risk of seizures require airway protection (e.g., cuffed endotracheal tube) before administration [Australian].
- **Emetics:** Should not be administered concurrently with systemically active emetics (e.g., ipecacuanha) as charcoal adsorbs the emetic agent. Induced emesis should be completed before charcoal administration [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Motility:** Use with extreme caution in patients with ileus or diminished bowel sounds. Avoid concurrent use of drugs that decrease gut motility (e.g., opioids, atropine, verapamil), especially during repeat-dose charcoal regimens, due to the risk of obstruction [Australian].
- **Antidotes:** Specific oral antidotes should not be given with charcoal as they may be inactivated. Any concurrent necessary medication should be administered parenterally [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Contains sucrose (0.33 g/mL); use clinical judgment in diabetic patients [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Frequent** | Faecal discolouration (black stools) [Australian] |
| **Common** | Vomiting [Australian] |
| **Rare/Serious** | Aspiration pneumonia, bronchiolitis obliterans, respiratory failure [Australian] |
| **Serious (Repeat Doses)** | Intestinal obstruction, charcoal bezoar formation [Australian] |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic:** Adsorbs and inactivates systemically active emetics and specific oral antidotes [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic:** As a non-selective adsorbent, **activated charcoal** may reduce the absorption of almost any concurrently administered oral medication.
- **Nutritional:** May adsorb minerals, vitamins, enzymes, and amino acids from the GI tract, particularly during repeat-dose therapy [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Activated charcoal** is not absorbed systemically and is not expected to pose a direct risk to the fetus.
    - Use during pregnancy only when the clinical benefit of detoxification outweighs the risk of poisoning to the mother and fetus [Australian].
    - Pregnancy category: Not specified in available sources.
- **Breastfeeding:** Systemic absorption does not occur; therefore, excretion into breast milk is not expected [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in infants under 1 month is not established. Monitoring of electrolytes and fluids is mandatory for children on repeat-dose regimens [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in sources [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources. (Note: Systemic absorption is negligible, limiting the impact of renal or hepatic clearance).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A physical adsorbent that binds drugs and toxins to its surface within the gastrointestinal tract, preventing their systemic absorption [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Not absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract [Australian].
    - **Distribution/Metabolism:** Not applicable.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted entirely unchanged in the faeces [Australian].
- **Adsorption Profile:** Effective for aspirin, barbiturates, TCAs, digoxin, amphetamines, morphine, cocaine, and phenothiazines. Ineffective for alcohols, corrosives, and most metals [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Do not refrigerate. Single-use only; discard any unused portion [Australian].
