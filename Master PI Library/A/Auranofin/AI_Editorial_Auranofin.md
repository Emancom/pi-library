# Editorial Product Information: Auranofin

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of auranofin for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidelines before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Auranofin (an organogold compound containing 29% gold).
- **Chemical Class:** Chrysotherapy (Gold salts).
- **Brand Names:** **Ridaura** [US].
- **ATC Code:** M01CB03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Oral capsules containing 3 mg of auranofin [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sebela Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Management of adults with active classical or definite rheumatoid arthritis (ARA criteria) who have had an insufficient therapeutic response to, or are intolerant of, an adequate trial of full doses of one or more nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs). Auranofin should be part of a comprehensive program including non-drug therapies [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Standard Dosage:** 6 mg daily, administered either as 3 mg twice daily or 6 mg once daily [US].
- **Dose Escalation:** If an inadequate response is observed after six months, the dose may be increased to 9 mg daily (3 mg three times daily).
- **Maximum Dose:** 9 mg daily. Safety at dosages exceeding 9 mg/day has not been studied.
- **Discontinuation:** Therapy should be discontinued if an inadequate response persists after a three-month trial of 9 mg daily.

### Administration Instructions
- Unlike NSAIDs, auranofin does not produce an immediate response. Therapeutic effects are typically seen after 3 to 4 months, though some patients may require 6 months to show improvement.
- **Transferring from Injectable Gold:** Patients may be transitioned by discontinuing the injectable agent and starting oral auranofin at 6 mg daily. No significant difference in disease control has been noted at six months post-transfer [US].

## 4. Contraindications
Auranofin is contraindicated in patients with a history of any of the following gold-induced disorders:
- Anaphylactic reactions.
- Necrotizing enterocolitis.
- Pulmonary fibrosis.
- Exfoliative dermatitis.
- Bone marrow aplasia or other severe hematologic disorders.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Gold Toxicity
Auranofin can cause serious gold toxicity. Danger signs include:
- **Hematologic:** Fall in hemoglobin, leukopenia (<4,000 WBC/mm³), granulocytopenia (<1,500/mm³), or thrombocytopenia (<150,000/mm³).
- **Renal:** Proteinuria or hematuria.
- **Mucocutaneous:** Pruritus, rash, or stomatitis.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Persistent diarrhea.

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Baseline:** CBC with differential, platelet count, urinalysis, and renal/liver function tests must be performed prior to initiating therapy.
- **Ongoing:** CBC with differential, platelet counts, and urinalysis should be monitored at least monthly.

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Occurs in 1–3% of patients. A precipitous decline in platelets or a count below 100,000/mm³ requires immediate withdrawal of auranofin.
- **Proteinuria:** Developed in 3–9% of patients. Clinically significant proteinuria or microscopic hematuria requires immediate cessation of therapy.
- **Gastrointestinal Effects:** Diarrhea/loose stools occur in approximately 50% of patients. This is often manageable by dose reduction (e.g., from 6 mg to 3 mg daily).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are most frequent during the first six months of treatment.

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Gastrointestinal | Diarrhea/Loose stools (47%), Abdominal pain (14%), Nausea/Vomiting (10%) |
| | Dermatological | Rash (24%), Pruritus (17%) |
| | Mucous Membrane | Stomatitis (13%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Gastrointestinal | Constipation, Anorexia, Flatulence, Dyspepsia, Dysgeusia |
| | Dermatological | Hair loss, Urticaria |
| | Hematological | Anemia, Leukopenia, Thrombocytopenia, Eosinophilia |
| | Renal | Proteinuria, Hematuria |
| | Hepatic | Elevated liver enzymes |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Gastrointestinal | Gastrointestinal bleeding, Melena, Ulcerative enterocolitis |
| | Dermatological | Angioedema |
| | Respiratory | Interstitial pneumonitis |
| | Neurological | Peripheral neuropathy |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Aplastic anemia, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, and pure red cell aplasia have been reported (incidence <0.1%). Gold deposits in the lens or cornea (chrysiasis) have been observed but are generally not associated with visual impairment [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Phenytoin:** One case report suggests that concurrent administration may increase phenytoin blood levels [US].
- **Combination Therapy:** The safety of using auranofin concurrently with injectable gold, hydroxychloroquine, penicillamine, or immunosuppressants (e.g., methotrexate, azathioprine) has not been established.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [US]. Auranofin has shown teratogenic effects in animal studies (e.g., gastroschisis and umbilical hernia in rabbits). Use in pregnant women is not recommended.
- **Breastfeeding:** Nursing during auranofin therapy is not recommended. While human data on auranofin are unavailable, gold has been detected in the milk of women receiving injectable gold and in the milk of treated rats/mice.
- **Fertility:** Women of childbearing potential should be warned of the potential risks to the fetus.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments are not provided in available sources, though general caution regarding organ reserve is advised.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Potential benefits must be weighed against the risks of gold toxicity in patients with progressive renal disease or significant hepatocellular disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The exact mechanism is not fully understood. In rheumatoid arthritis, it appears to modify disease activity as shown by reduced synovitis and lower ESR levels. It does not induce complete remission.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 25% of the gold in auranofin is absorbed.
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 40% of gold is associated with red blood cells and 60% with serum proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Auranofin is rapidly metabolized; the intact drug has not been detected in the blood.
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal plasma half-life is 26 days (range 21–31). Approximately 60% of absorbed gold is excreted in the urine, with the remainder in the feces.
- **Steady State:** Achieved in approximately three months.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Comparative Trials:** In controlled trials comparing oral auranofin to injectable gold, auranofin was associated with fewer dropouts due to adverse reactions, whereas injectable gold had fewer dropouts due to inadequate therapeutic effect [US].
- **Safety Data:** Incidence rates were derived from 4,784 patients, with over 500 patients treated for more than three years.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Special Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** **Rx-only** (Prescription only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (Refer to local jurisdiction).
