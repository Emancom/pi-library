# Editorial Product Information: Armodafinil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document is synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Armodafinil. It is the (R)-enantiomer of modafinil and belongs to the chemical class of benzhydryl sulfinyl compounds.
- **Brand Names:** Nuvigil [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06BA13.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral Tablets: 50 mg, 150 mg, and 250 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Neuraxpharm Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Narcolepsy:** Indicated to improve wakefulness in adult patients experiencing excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS).
- **Obstructive Sleep Apnoea/Hypopnoea Syndrome (OSAHS):** Used as an adjunct to Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) to improve wakefulness. **Note:** It is not a treatment for the underlying obstruction [Australian].
- **Shift Work Sleep Disorder (SWSD):** Indicated for the treatment of excessive sleepiness associated with moderate to severe chronic SWSD in instances where non-pharmacological interventions are ineffective or inappropriate [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Narcolepsy and OSAHS:** The recommended dose is 150 mg or 250 mg taken once daily in the morning. While doses up to 250 mg are generally well-tolerated in OSAHS, there is no consistent evidence of additional clinical benefit beyond the 150 mg dose [Australian].
- **Shift Work Sleep Disorder (SWSD):** 150 mg taken daily approximately one hour before the start of the work shift [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be taken with or without food.
- **Food Effect:** Administration with food may delay the onset of action and prolong the clinical effect. To avoid delayed onset, it is recommended to take the dose on an empty stomach [Australian].

### Special Considerations
- **Diagnosis:** Use should only follow a comprehensive evaluation of sleepiness (including history, physical exam, and laboratory testing if necessary) using ICSD or DSM criteria.
- **CPAP Compliance:** In OSAHS, clinicians must ensure maximal effort and periodic assessment of CPAP compliance before and during armodafinil therapy.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to modafinil, armodafinil, or any excipients (e.g., lactose monohydrate).
- **Pregnancy:** Armodafinil is strictly contraindicated during pregnancy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Skin Reactions
- **Life-threatening Rashes:** Rare cases of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Rash with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) have been reported. 
- **Management:** Discontinue armodafinil immediately at the first sign of a rash unless the rash is clearly not drug-related. Clinical duration of therapy does not predict risk, as cases have occurred within 1 day to 2 months of initiation [Australian].

### Hypersensitivity and Multi-organ Reactions
- **Angioedema/Anaphylaxis:** Patients should report swelling of the face, eyes, lips, or larynx, or difficulty swallowing/breathing.
- **Multi-organ Hypersensitivity:** Fatal cases have occurred with modafinil/armodafinil. Signs include fever, rash, myocarditis, hepatitis, and haematological abnormalities [Australian].

### Psychiatric Symptoms
- **Risk:** Anxiety, agitation, nervousness, irritability, and depression were reported at higher rates than placebo.
- **Suicidality:** Cases of suicidal ideation, aggression, and mania have been observed in clinical trials and post-marketing settings.
- **Precaution:** Exercise caution in patients with a history of psychosis, depression, or mania. Discontinuation should be considered if psychiatric symptoms emerge [Australian].

### Cardiovascular System
- **Monitoring:** Small increases in mean systolic and diastolic blood pressure (1.2 to 4.3 mmHg) and pulse rate (0.9 to 3.5 BPM) have been observed. Regular monitoring of heart rate and blood pressure is recommended [Australian].
- **Existing Disease:** Use with caution in patients with a recent history of myocardial infarction or unstable angina.
- **Valvular Risks:** Not recommended for patients with a history of left ventricular hypertrophy or mitral valve prolapse syndrome who have previously experienced CNS stimulant-related symptoms [Australian].

### Reduced Contraceptive Efficacy
- **Interaction:** Armodafinil reduces the effectiveness of steroidal contraceptives (including pills, implants, and IUDs).
- **Requirement:** Patients must use alternative or concomitant non-hormonal contraception during therapy and for two months following discontinuation [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Post-Marketing:** Supraventricular arrhythmias, myocardial infarction, convulsions, hallucinations, suicidal ideation, and alopecia have been reported rarely or very rarely [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4/5 Induction (Weak):** Armodafinil may reduce levels of CYP3A substrates. Dose adjustments may be necessary for midazolam, triazolam, cyclosporine, and steroidal contraceptives [Australian].
- **CYP2C19 Inhibition (Reversible):** May increase levels of substrates such as omeprazole (40% increase observed), phenytoin, diazepam, and clomipramine.
- **CYP1A2/CYP2B6:** Weak induction observed in vitro; however, no significant effect on CYP1A2 activity was seen clinically with caffeine [Australian].

### Management Notes
- **Warfarin:** No significant change in PK was seen with a single dose, but frequent prothrombin time/INR monitoring is recommended during co-administration [Australian].
- **CNS Actives:** Reduced systemic exposure to quetiapine (approx. 29%) has been noted; however, no dose adjustment is typically required for quetiapine.
- **Diagnostic Imaging:** Armodafinil may interfere with dopamine-binding radiopharmaceuticals (e.g., ioflupane/DaTSCAN). Discontinuation for at least five half-lives prior to imaging should be considered [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Suspected to cause congenital malformations (including cardiac anomalies and microcephaly) and spontaneous abortion. A negative pregnancy test is required within one week before starting treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. While excretion in human milk is unknown, infant growth retardation has been reported in association with armodafinil use [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were seen in animal studies at twice the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established. Not approved for use in patients under 18 years of age. Serious rash (0.8%) was noted in paediatric trials of the related compound, modafinil [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance may be reduced. Consideration should be given to lower doses and increased monitoring, particularly in patients >75 years of age [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy have not been established. In modafinil studies, the metabolite modafinil acid was increased 9-fold in severe renal failure (CrCl ≤20 mL/min) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing must be reduced in patients with severe hepatic impairment (with or without cirrhosis). Specific dosing based on the degree of impairment is not established due to lack of data [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Precise mechanism is unknown. It acts as an indirect dopamine receptor agonist by binding to the dopamine transporter (DAT) and inhibiting dopamine reuptake. Wake-promoting effects are DAT-dependent.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed; Tmax is 2 hours (fasted) but delayed by 2–4 hours with food.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is ~42 L. Protein binding is ~60% (mostly to albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via hydrolytic deamidation and S-oxidation (CYP3A4/5). Two major metabolites: armodafinil acid and modafinil sulfone.
    - **Elimination:** Apparent terminal half-life is ~15 hours. Steady state is reached within 7 days. Eliminated >80% in urine (as metabolites) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **OSAHS:** Two 12-week studies (N=395 and N=263) demonstrated statistically significant improvements in sleep latency (MWT) and overall clinical condition (CGI-C) for 150 mg and 250 mg doses compared to placebo.
- **Narcolepsy:** A 12-week study (N=196) showed significantly enhanced MWT scores and CGI-C improvement for both 150 mg and 250 mg doses.
- **SWSD:** A 12-week study (N=254) using 150 mg demonstrated prolonged time to sleep onset (MSLT) during simulated night shifts [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from light and moisture.
- **Packaging:** PVC blisters with aluminium foil lidding [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
