# Editorial Product Information: Adenosine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Adenosine (endogenous purine nucleoside).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Adenocor** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C01EB10.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Clear, colourless sterile solution for injection. Each 2 mL vial contains 6 mg of adenosine (3 mg/mL) in 0.9% sodium chloride [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Rapid intravenous (IV) bolus injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi-aventis Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Therapeutic:** Rapid conversion of paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardias (PSVT) to normal sinus rhythm, including those involving accessory bypass tracts (e.g., Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome) [Australian].
- **Diagnostic:** 
    - Aid in the diagnosis of broad or narrow QRS complex supraventricular tachycardias [Australian].
    - Improvement of diagnostic sensitivity during intracavity electrophysiological investigations [Australian].
- **Note:** Adenosine is not effective for converting atrial flutter, atrial fibrillation, or ventricular tachycardia to sinus rhythm [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Adenosine must be administered as a rapid IV bolus in a hospital setting with continuous ECG monitoring and resuscitation equipment immediately available [Australian].
- **Initial Dose:** 3 mg administered over 2 seconds.
- **Second Dose:** If the first dose does not eliminate the SVT within 1–2 minutes, administer 6 mg as a rapid IV bolus.
- **Third Dose:** If the second dose does not result in elimination within 1–2 minutes, administer 12 mg as a rapid IV bolus.

### Administration Instructions
- Administer either directly into a vein or into an IV line as proximally as possible.
- Follow immediately with a rapid saline flush to ensure the drug reaches systemic circulation [Australian].
- Stop dose escalation if a high-level atrioventricular (AV) block develops at any dose [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not applicable as this medication is administered by a healthcare professional in an emergency/diagnostic setting.

## 4. Contraindications
Adenosine is contraindicated in patients with:
- Known hypersensitivity to adenosine [Australian].
- Sick sinus syndrome or second/third-degree AV block (unless a functional artificial pacemaker is present) [Australian].
- Chronic obstructive lung disease, including asthma [Australian].
- Long QT syndrome [Australian].
- Severe hypotension or decompensated heart failure [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Risk:** May cause transient asystole, cardiac arrest (potentially fatal), severe bradycardia, or significant hypotension. Use with extreme caution in patients with left main coronary stenosis, uncorrected hypovolaemia, or stenotic valvular heart disease [Australian].
- **Arrhythmias at Conversion:** Conversion to sinus rhythm often results in new ECG rhythms (e.g., PVCs, PACs, sinus bradycardia, sinus pause) in approximately 55% of patients; these are typically self-limiting and last only a few seconds [Australian].
- **Post-Transplant Sensitivity:** Patients with a recent heart transplant (<1 year) show increased sensitivity; use with caution [Australian].
- **Bronchospasm:** Adenosine may precipitate or aggravate bronchospasm. Discontinue immediately if severe respiratory difficulties occur [Australian].
- **Cerebrovascular Risk:** Transient ischemic attacks/strokes may occur secondary to hemodynamic effects [Australian].
- **Convulsions:** Carefully monitor patients with a history of seizures/convulsions [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally transient due to the very short half-life of adenosine.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Bradycardia, sinus pause, skipped beats, AV block, atrial extrasystoles; dyspnoea (urge to breathe deeply); flushing, chest pressure/pain. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Headache, dizziness, light-headedness; nausea; apprehension; burning sensation. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Sinus tachycardia, palpitations; hyperventilation; head pressure; metallic taste; blurred vision, sweating, general discomfort. |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Atrial fibrillation, ventricular fibrillation, torsades de pointes; bronchospasm; worsening of intracranial hypertension; injection site reactions. |
| **Not Known** | Cardiac arrest/asystole (sometimes fatal), MI, hypotension; respiratory failure/apnoea; loss of consciousness, convulsions; anaphylaxis. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Xanthines (e.g., Aminophylline, Theophylline):** These are competitive antagonists. Avoid for 24 hours prior to adenosine administration. Avoid caffeine-containing food/drinks for at least 12 hours prior [Australian].
- **Dipyridamole:** Inhibits adenosine uptake and can potentiate its effect four-fold. Dipyridamole should be discontinued 24 hours before adenosine bolus use or the adenosine dose significantly reduced [Australian].
- **Carbamazepine:** May increase the degree of heart block produced by adenosine [Australian].
- **Cardioactive Drugs:** Has been safely administered with digitalis, quinidine, beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, and ACE inhibitors without altering the adverse reaction profile [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. There are no controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefits outweigh potential risks to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available in humans or animals. Adenosine should not be used during lactation; consider alternative infant feeding if treatment is essential [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats/mice) showed decreased spermatogenesis at high doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No controlled studies have been undertaken; evidence is insufficient to recommend a specific posology [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Follow adult dosing recommendations [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Renal or hepatic failure is expected to have no effect on the activity or metabolism of an adenosine bolus, as the drug's action is direct and rapid [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Adenosine slows conduction through the AV node. This interrupts re-entry circuits involving the AV node, restoring normal sinus rhythm in PSVT [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Transiently slows AV conduction to help evaluate atrial activity on ECG [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption/Distribution:** Rapidly removed from circulation.
    - **Metabolism:** Taken up by erythrocytes and vascular endothelial cells; metabolised to inosine and adenosine monophosphate (AMP).
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is estimated to be less than 10 seconds [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C. 
- **Do not refrigerate.** 
- Vials are for single use only; discard any unused portion [Australian].
