# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administered via intravenous injection only [US].
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Monitoring
- For metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy, with weekly and monthly monitoring thereafter. Monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug and initiate medical treatment [US].

### General Precautions
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - General: Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), flu syndrome (15–29%), headache (3–37%) [US].
    - Cardiovascular: Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%) [US].
    - Digestive: Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
    - Respiratory: Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, fever, chest pain, back pain, infection [US].
    - Arrhythmia, tachycardia, palpitations, vascular disorder [US].
    - Anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia, gastrointestinal disorder [US].
    - Anemia, hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes [US].
    - Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, anxiety, depression [US].
    - Pruritus, rash, rhinitis, dyspnea [US].
    - Eye disorder, taste perversion, urinary tract infection, kidney failure [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
    - Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].
    - Seizures [US].
- **Specific Clinical Observation:** Transient nausea and vomiting, as well as body odor, have been observed, though frequency is difficult to estimate due to underlying pathology [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Jurisdiction Classification:** Not specified by letter category in source, but stated as "use only if clearly needed" [US].
    - **Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess intake must be weighed against maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, yeast) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are weight-based (50 mg/kg) and applicable to infants and children. In premature infants and newborns, secondary deficiency is defined by concentrations below age-related normals [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in source documents [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The injection is indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. High-dose **oral** levocarnitine should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients on hemodialysis results in significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioavailability Studies:** In healthy volunteers, tablets and oral solution were found to be bioequivalent, with an absolute bioavailability of approximately 15% [US].
- **Dialysis Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly post-dialysis), trough levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. A dose-ranging study (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing. Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
- **Stability in Infusion:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
