# Editorial Product Information: Avatrombopag

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided for this specific review; as such, clinical content primarily reflects the Australian regulatory position.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a synthesis for clinical reference and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Avatrombopag (as maleate).
- **Chemical Class:** Thrombopoietin (TPO) receptor agonist; small molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** **DOPTELET** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B02BX08.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Film-coated tablets containing 20 mg of avatrombopag (as 23.6 mg avatrombopag maleate) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Swedish Orphan Biovitrum Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Thrombocytopenia in Chronic Liver Disease (CLD):** Treatment of adult patients with CLD who are scheduled to undergo a procedure [Australian].
- **Chronic Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP):** Treatment of adult patients with chronic ITP who have had an insufficient response to a previous treatment [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Chronic Liver Disease (Pre-procedure)
- **General Dosing:** A single 5-day course taken once daily with food.
- **Timing:** Begin dosing 10 to 13 days prior to the procedure. The procedure should occur 5 to 8 days after the final dose.
- **Dose Tiering (Based on Platelet Count):**
    - Platelet count <40 x $10^9$/L: 60 mg (3 tablets) daily for 5 days.
    - Platelet count 40 to <50 x $10^9$/L: 40 mg (2 tablets) daily for 5 days.
- **Note:** Do not use to normalize platelet counts; use only to reach an adequate count for the procedure.

### Chronic Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)
- **Initial Dose:** 20 mg (1 tablet) once daily with food [Australian].
- **Titration:** Adjust the dose to maintain a platelet count $\ge 50 \times 10^9$/L. The maximum daily dose is 40 mg.
- **Dose Levels for Titration:** Ranges from 20 mg once weekly (Level 1) to 40 mg once daily (Level 6).
- **Discontinuation:** Stop treatment if the platelet count does not reach $\ge 50 \times 10^9$/L after 4 weeks at the maximum dose (40 mg daily), or if the count exceeds 400 x $10^9$/L after 2 weeks at the minimum dose (20 mg weekly).

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be taken with food.
- **Missed Dose:** Take as soon as remembered. Do not take two doses at once. For CLD, all 5 days of dosing must be completed.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to avatrombopag or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Thrombotic/Thromboembolic Events:** TPO receptor agonists are associated with thrombotic risks (e.g., portal vein thrombosis in CLD; arterial/venous events in ITP). Risk is increased in patients with known genetic or acquired prothrombotic conditions. Monitor for signs of thromboembolism.
- **Bone Marrow Reticulin:** Stimulation of TPO receptors may increase bone marrow reticulin. Monitor for cellular morphological abnormalities via peripheral blood smears and CBC before and during treatment.
- **Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS) Progression:** There is a theoretical risk that TPO-R agonists may stimulate the progression of existing haematological malignancies like MDS. Confirm ITP diagnosis by excluding MDS, especially in older patients (>60 years).
- **Invasive Procedures (CLD):** Efficacy/safety not established for major surgeries (e.g., laparotomy, craniotomy, organ resection).
- **Monitoring:** Obtain platelet counts prior to administration (CLD) and weekly during ITP titration until stable, then monthly.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Chronic Liver Disease (CLD) Population
- **Common (1–10%):** Pyrexia (10%), abdominal pain (7%), nausea (7%), headache (6%), fatigue (4%), peripheral oedema (3%).
- **Serious:** Hyponatremia (0.7%).

### Chronic Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP) Population
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (31%), fatigue (28%), contusion (26%), epistaxis (19%), upper respiratory tract infection (15%), arthralgia (13%), gingival bleeding (13%), petechiae (11%), nasopharyngitis (10%).
- **Serious:** Headache (1.6%).

### Post-marketing Reports
- **Hypersensitivity:** Pruritus, rash, choking sensation, facial/tongue swelling, and pharyngeal oedema [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 and CYP2C9:** Avatrombopag is a substrate of these enzymes.
    - **Dual Inhibitors (e.g., fluconazole):** Increases avatrombopag exposure. For ITP, reduce the starting dose to 20 mg three times a week [Australian].
    - **Dual Inducers (e.g., rifampicin):** Decreases avatrombopag exposure. For ITP, increase the starting dose to 40 mg daily [Australian].
- **Transporters:** Avatrombopag inhibits OAT1, OAT3, OCT1, BCRP, MRP2, OATP1B3, and P-gp in vitro, though clinical significance varies.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Interferon preparations:** May reduce platelet counts; consider during co-administration.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. (Studies in animals have shown evidence of increased fetal damage, but human significance is uncertain). Use only if benefits outweigh potential risks to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for at least 2 weeks after the last dose. Lactating women receiving short-term therapy (CLD) should pump and discard milk during this period.
- **Fertility:** Human data unavailable; animal studies showed no effect on fertility at exposures significantly higher than clinical doses.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or response observed between elderly (>65 years) and younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment (CLcr $\ge 30$ mL/min). Data unavailable for severe impairment or haemodialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Use only if benefits outweigh risks. Monitor closely for hepatic encephalopathy, ascites, and thrombotic tendencies.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Orally bioavailable TPO receptor agonist. It stimulates the proliferation and differentiation of megakaryocytes from bone marrow progenitor cells, increasing platelet production. It does not compete with endogenous TPO.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ at 5–6 hours (fasted). Taking with food reduces exposure variability.
    - **Distribution:** >96% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily CYP2C9 and CYP3A4.
    - **Excretion:** Primarily faecal (88%); 34% as unchanged drug. Elimination half-life $\approx 19$ hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CLD (ADAPT-1 & ADAPT-2):** Two Phase 3 trials showed avatrombopag was superior to placebo in reducing the need for platelet transfusions or rescue procedures for bleeding in patients with CLD undergoing procedures. Response rates were 66-88% for avatrombopag vs 23-38% for placebo [Australian].
- **ITP:** A Phase 3 trial demonstrated a significantly higher cumulative number of weeks with platelet counts $\ge 50 \times 10^9$/L (median 12.4 weeks) compared to placebo (0 weeks).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below $25^\circ C$ [Australian].
- **Packaging:** PA/Al/PVC/Al blister packs.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring (Black Triangle Scheme) [Australian].
