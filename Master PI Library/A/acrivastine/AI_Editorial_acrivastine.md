# Editorial Product Information: Acrivastine and Pseudoephedrine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States** (Food and Drug Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current, locally approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Acrivastine and Pseudoephedrine Hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Acrivastine is an alkylamine derivative (H1-antihistamine); Pseudoephedrine is a sympathomimetic amine (decongestant).
- **Brand Names:** **Semprex-D** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral capsule containing 8 mg acrivastine and 60 mg pseudoephedrine hydrochloride [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Marketed by UCB, Inc.; manufactured by DSM Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Seasonal Allergic Rhinitis:** For the relief of symptoms including sneezing, rhinorrhea, pruritus, lacrimation, and nasal congestion. Use is indicated when both antihistaminic and nasal decongestant activities are desired [United States].
- **Note:** Effectiveness for the relief of common cold symptoms has not been adequately established [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents $\ge$ 12 years):** One capsule administered orally every 4 to 6 hours, four times daily [United States].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Efficacy beyond 14 days of continuous treatment has not been adequately investigated in clinical trials [United States].
- **Administration Instructions:** May be taken without regard to food.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **acrivastine**, **triprolidine**, **pseudoephedrine**, or other sympathomimetic amines (e.g., phenylpropanolamine) [United States].
- Severe hypertension or severe coronary artery disease [United States].
- Concurrent use of monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors, or use within 14 days of discontinuing an MAO inhibitor [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [United States].
- **General Cardiovascular and Endocrine Cautions:** Use with caution in patients with hypertension, diabetes mellitus, ischemic heart disease, hyperthyroidism, or increased intraocular pressure [United States].
- **Genitourinary and Gastrointestinal Cautions:** Use with caution in patients with prostatic hypertrophy, stenosing peptic ulcer, or pyloroduodenal obstruction [United States].
- **CNS Effects:** Overdosage of sympathomimetics may produce CNS stimulation, convulsions, or cardiovascular collapse with hypotension. Acrivastine may cause sedation in some patients [United States].
- **Mental Alertness:** Patients should assess their response to the medication before engaging in activities requiring mental alertness, such as driving or operating machinery [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects reported with a frequency >1% in clinical trials [United States]:

| Adverse Event | Placebo (n=1767) | Acrivastine + Pseudoephedrine (n=1650) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Headache** | 18% | 19% |
| **Somnolence** | 6% | 12% |
| **Dry Mouth** | 2% | 7% |
| **Insomnia** | 1% | 4% |
| **Dizziness** | 2% | 3% |
| **Nervousness** | 1% | 3% |
| **Pharyngitis** | 2% | 3% |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Rare post-marketing reports include anaphylaxis, angioedema, bronchospasm, and erythema multiforme [United States].
- **Sympathomimetic Reactions:** Pseudoephedrine may cause ephedrine-like reactions, including tachycardia and palpitations [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAO Inhibitors:** Concomitant use can result in a hypertensive crisis. Contraindicated within 14 days of MAOI therapy [United States].
- **CNS Depressants and Alcohol:** Concurrent use may lead to additive reductions in alertness and impairment of CNS performance; avoid concomitant use [United States].
- **Antihypertensive Agents:** Pseudoephedrine may reduce the efficacy of drugs that interfere with sympathetic activity (e.g., certain antihypertensives) [United States].
- **Sympathomimetic Amines:** Combined cardiovascular effects may be harmful; use caution [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category B]. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no evidence of teratogenicity at doses many times the human daily dose. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if acrivastine is excreted in human milk; pseudoephedrine is excreted in human milk. Use in nursing mothers only if the benefit outweighs the risk to the infant [United States].
- **Fertility:** No effect on male or female fertility was seen in rats at doses significantly exceeding the recommended human dose [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children under 12 years of age have not been established [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** More likely to experience adverse reactions to sympathomimetics. Monitor renal function due to the risk of toxic reactions from decreased drug clearance [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Both components are primarily excreted by the kidneys. **Semprex-D** is NOT recommended for patients with a creatinine clearance $\le$ 48 mL/min [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Acrivastine:** A potent H1-receptor antagonist that reduces histamine-induced wheal and flare.
    - **Pseudoephedrine:** An indirect sympathomimetic amine that releases norepinephrine from adrenergic nerves, resulting in vasoconstriction and nasal decongestion [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Both components are rapidly absorbed. Peak plasma levels: ~1.14 hours (acrivastine) and standard levels for pseudoephedrine [United States].
    - **Distribution:** Acrivastine is ~50% protein bound; pseudoephedrine does not significantly bind to plasma proteins [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Acrivastine is minimally metabolised to a propionic acid derivative.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal. Acrivastine terminal half-life: ~1.9 to 3.5 hours. Pseudoephedrine terminal half-life: ~6.2 hours (highly pH-dependent) [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Wheal and Flare Studies:** In healthy volunteers, acrivastine 8 mg significantly reduced histamine-induced wheal and flare areas within 30 minutes, with peak effects at 1-2 hours [United States].
- **Rhinitis Trials:** Five randomized, controlled trials in 696 patients demonstrated that the combination was more effective than either component alone in reducing sneezing, rhinorrhea, pruritus, lacrimation, and nasal congestion over a 14-day period [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 15°C to 25°C (59°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Keep in a dry place and protect from light [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a Human Prescription Drug (Rx-only).
