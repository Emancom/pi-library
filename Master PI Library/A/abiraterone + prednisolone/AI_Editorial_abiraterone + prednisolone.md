# Editorial Product Information: abiraterone + prednisolone

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **abiraterone + prednisolone**
- Chemical class:
  - Abiraterone acetate: inhibitor of CYP17 (17α-hydroxylase/C17,20-lyase) — steroidogenesis inhibitor (antiandrogen via androgen biosynthesis blockade)
  - Prednisolone: systemic glucocorticoid (corticosteroid)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of metastatic prostate cancer in combination with androgen deprivation therapy; specific indications described:
  - Newly diagnosed high‑risk metastatic hormone‑sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) — used with ADT (prednisolone 5 mg daily in the combined pack).
  - Metastatic castration‑resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) in patients who are asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic after failure of ADT.
  - mCRPC in patients who have received prior taxane chemotherapy.
- Jurisdictional note: Specific indication wording and approvals by region were not provided beyond the above; where an indication was specified only in a region, the region was not separately identified in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- **Abiraterone acetate:** 1000 mg once daily (equivalent to two 500 mg tablets) as a single daily dose.
- **Prednisolone:** given concomitantly:
  - 5 mg once daily when used for hormone‑sensitive metastatic prostate cancer (mHSPC).
  - 10 mg once daily when used for metastatic castration‑resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC).

### Administration instructions
- Abiraterone **must NOT be taken with food**. Take on an empty stomach:
  - At least 2 hours after eating, and
  - Do not eat for at least 1 hour after dosing.
- Swallow abiraterone tablets whole with water.
- Prednisolone dosing instructions: Not further specified beyond daily dose.

### Dose modifications for hepatotoxicity
- If ALT or AST rises > 5 × ULN or bilirubin > 3 × ULN: interrupt abiraterone immediately until LFTs return to baseline.
- Re‑treatment after LFT normalization: may resume at reduced abiraterone dose 500 mg once daily.
  - On re‑treatment, monitor LFTs at least every 2 weeks for 3 months, then monthly.
  - If hepatotoxicity recurs at reduced dose, permanently discontinue abiraterone.
- If ALT or AST reaches 20 × ULN at any time: discontinue abiraterone and do not re‑treat.

### Special populations — dosing adjustments
- Renal impairment: no dosage adjustment necessary.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - No adjustment for mild hepatic impairment stated.
  - Use with caution in moderate hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh B) — only if benefit clearly outweighs risk.
  - **Do not use** in severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C).

### Titration, missed dose
- Titration schedules: Not specified in available sources.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- **Abiraterone acetate:** contraindicated in women who are or may potentially be pregnant.
- **Abiraterone acetate:** contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh Class C).
- **Abiraterone acetate + prednisolone:** contraindicated in combination with radium‑223 dichloride (radium‑223) (co‑administration not recommended due to increased mortality and fracture risk reported in a randomized trial).
- **Prednisolone:** contraindicated in uncontrolled infections and in patients with known hypersensitivity to prednisolone or any excipient.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/major warnings
- **Combination with radium‑223 dichloride is contraindicated**; combined use was associated with increased mortality and fracture in a randomized study and is not recommended.

### General warnings and precautions
- Mineralocorticoid excess (abiraterone mechanism) — monitor and manage:
  - Hypertension, hypokalaemia and fluid retention can occur due to excess mineralocorticoids from CYP17 inhibition.
  - Use with caution in patients with a history of cardiovascular disease, heart failure, recent myocardial infarction, or ventricular arrhythmia.
  - Correct hypokalaemia and control hypertension before starting therapy.
  - QT prolongation and Torsades de Pointes have been observed in patients who develop hypokalaemia or who have underlying cardiac conditions while receiving abiraterone.
- Hepatotoxicity:
  - Marked increases in liver enzymes have occurred, sometimes requiring dose modification or discontinuation; rare cases of fulminant hepatitis and liver failure reported.
  - Measure ALT, AST and bilirubin prior to start, every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then monthly thereafter. If symptoms suggest hepatotoxicity, measure immediately.
- Hypoglycaemia:
  - Isolated cases reported when given with pioglitazone or repaglinide in patients with diabetes; monitor blood glucose.
- Use with chemotherapy:
  - Safety and efficacy of concomitant cytotoxic chemotherapy not established.
- Corticosteroid‑related precautions (prednisolone):
  - Scleroderma renal crisis risk: caution in systemic sclerosis; blood pressure and renal function monitoring recommended.
  - Adrenal suppression and corticosteroid withdrawal effects: avoid abrupt cessation after prolonged therapy; increase steroid dose in stress situations.
  - Increased susceptibility to infection; corticosteroids may mask infection signs; avoid live vaccines while on therapy.
  - Visual disturbances (including cataract, glaucoma, central serous chorioretinopathy) have been reported with systemic corticosteroids.
  - Monitor blood glucose in patients with diabetes (glucocorticoids can increase hyperglycaemia).
  - Osteoporosis risk with long‑term use; consider bone protection where appropriate.
  - Gastrointestinal: increased risk of ulceration/bleeding, particularly with concurrent alcohol use.
- Paediatric use: not for use in children.
- Occupational/handling safety: women who are pregnant or may be pregnant should not handle abiraterone tablets without protection (e.g., gloves). Male patients should use condoms if engaging in sexual activity with a pregnant woman; if sexual partner is of child‑bearing potential use condom plus additional effective contraception.

### Monitoring requirements
- Liver function tests (ALT, AST, bilirubin): baseline, every 2 weeks for first 3 months, then monthly.
- Blood pressure, serum potassium, and assessment for fluid retention: at least monthly.
- Blood glucose in patients with diabetes: monitor frequently.
- Renal function and blood pressure in patients with systemic sclerosis: monitor routinely.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Pre‑existing moderate or severe hepatic impairment: limited data; exercise caution and avoid use in severe impairment.
- Cardiac history: safety not established in patients with LVEF <50% or certain NYHA heart failure classes in clinical studies referenced.
- Avoid concomitant strong CYP3A4 inducers unless necessary — impact on abiraterone exposure may be substantial.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Abiraterone‑related pharmacokinetic interactions
- In vitro:
  - Abiraterone is a strong inhibitor of CYP1A2, CYP2D6 and CYP2C8; moderate inhibitor of CYP2C9, CYP2C19 and CYP3A4/5.
  - Abiraterone and metabolites inhibit OATP1B1 in vitro.
- Clinical interaction data:
  - CYP2D6 substrates: co‑administration increased dextromethorphan AUC ≈ 200% (dextromethorphan used as probe); caution with drugs metabolized by CYP2D6, especially narrow therapeutic index substrates — consider dose reduction.
  - CYP3A4 inducers: rifampicin (strong inducer) reduced abiraterone Cmax and AUC by ~55%; avoid strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampicin, rifabutin, rifapentine, phenobarbital) or evaluate clinical efficacy if no alternative.
  - CYP3A4 inhibitors: ketoconazole (strong inhibitor) had no clinically meaningful effect on abiraterone pharmacokinetics in a reported study.
  - CYP2C8 substrates: co‑administration with abiraterone increased pioglitazone AUC by 46%; active metabolites of pioglitazone decreased ~10%. Monitor for toxicity with CYP2C8 substrates with narrow therapeutic index (examples: pioglitazone, repaglinide).
  - CYP1A2 substrates: co‑administration with theophylline showed no increase in theophylline exposure in a study.
- Clinical relevance:
  - Avoid or use with caution concomitant medications that are sensitive substrates of CYP2D6 or CYP2C8, and strong inducers of CYP3A4; adjust doses where appropriate.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Mineralocorticoid excess risk (abiraterone) mitigated by concomitant corticosteroid (prednisolone).
- Spironolactone: binds androgen receptor and may increase PSA levels; use with abiraterone not recommended.
- Prednisolone interactions:
  - Numerous interactions typical for systemic corticosteroids: antacids; antidiabetic agents (oral/insulin); digitalis glycosides; diuretics (risk of hypokalaemia); enzyme‑inducing drugs (barbiturates, phenytoin, rifampicin); potassium supplements; ritodrine; sodium‑containing medications; somatrem; vaccines (live vaccines contraindicated); live viruses or other immunisations — consider diminished vaccine response and infection risk.

### Management notes
- Monitor glucose closely when combining with antidiabetic agents and when corticosteroids are used.
- Monitor for signs of toxicity when co‑administering CYP2C8 substrates (e.g., repaglinide, pioglitazone); consider alternative antidiabetic agents if hypoglycaemia risk is high.
- Avoid combining with radium‑223 (see contraindications).
- Do not start live vaccines while on prednisolone therapy; consider immunisation timing and competency.

- Jurisdictional notes on interactions: Not specified in available sources beyond the clinical interaction data provided.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Abiraterone:** contraindicated in women who are or may potentially be pregnant. Maternal CYP17 inhibition is expected to alter fetal hormone levels and may affect fetal development.
- Animal data: embryofetal developmental toxicity in rats at ≥10 mg/kg/day (reduced fetal weight and survival, delayed ossification, increased resorptions and post‑implantation loss; decreased anogenital distance). Not teratogenic but reproductive system effects consistent with antiandrogenic activity were observed in animals.
- Male patients: condom use required if engaging in sexual activity with a pregnant woman; if partner is of child‑bearing potential use condom plus effective contraception.
- Women who are pregnant or may be pregnant should not handle abiraterone tablets without protection (e.g., gloves).
- Pregnancy category (Australian): Category D (as stated in available sources).
- Pregnancy category/status in other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Abiraterone: it is not known whether abiraterone or metabolites are excreted in human milk. Abiraterone is not for use in women; further guidance not specified.
- Prednisolone: data not specified in available sources for the combination.

### Fertility
- Animal studies: abiraterone reduced fertility in male and female rats; effects were reversible after drug cessation (4–16 weeks). Reproductive organ changes and decreased testosterone were observed in multiple species at exposures similar to or lower than human clinical exposures.

### Elderly

### Renal impairment

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Abiraterone acetate:** irreversible inhibitor of CYP17 (17α‑hydroxylase/C17,20‑lyase), reducing androgen biosynthesis in testes, adrenals and prostate tumor tissue; inhibition of CYP17 can lead to increased mineralocorticoid precursors causing hypertension, hypokalaemia and fluid retention.
- **Prednisolone:** systemic glucocorticoid that suppresses ACTH release, thereby mitigating the mineralocorticoid excess caused by CYP17 inhibition and reducing related adverse effects.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Abiraterone should be taken on an empty stomach; food increases systemic exposure — therefore dosing instructions require fasting conditions (at least 2 hours after food and no food for at least 1 hour following dosing).
- Metabolism:
  - Abiraterone is a substrate of CYP3A4.
  - In vitro, abiraterone and its major metabolites inhibit multiple cytochrome P450 enzymes (strong: CYP1A2, CYP2D6, CYP2C8; moderate: CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP3A4/5) and OATP1B1 transport.
- Drug interaction study highlights:
  - Rifampicin (strong CYP3A4 inducer) decreased abiraterone Cmax and AUC by ~55%.
  - Ketoconazole (strong CYP3A4 inhibitor) had no clinically meaningful effect in one study.
  - Abiraterone increased dextromethorphan exposure (CYP2D6 probe) approximately 2‑fold.
  - Abiraterone increased pioglitazone AUC by ~46% in a CYP2C8 interaction study.
- Elimination:
  - Specific clearance, half‑life, excretion routes and volumes of distribution were not specified in the available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- By inhibiting CYP17 enzymatic activity, abiraterone decreases androgen production and can increase upstream mineralocorticoid precursors; prednisolone reduces ACTH drive thereby decreasing incidence/severity of mineralocorticoid excess.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Safety observations referenced in clinical trials:
  - The safety of abiraterone was not established in some patients with heart failure or reduced left ventricular ejection fraction in clinical studies referenced (specific study identifiers mentioned in source but detailed results not provided).
  - A randomized trial in patients with asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic bone‑predominant mCRPC showed increased mortality and increased rate of fracture when radium‑223 dichloride was added to abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone; this finding led to recommendation against combining the agents.
