# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following regulatory authority:
    *   United States (Food and Drug Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing, and safety information into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians should consult the current local regulatory documentation before prescribing. As only the United States PI was provided for this synthesis, regional variations for other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) are not included.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport; amino acid derivative.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
*   **Metabolic Disorders:**
    *   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
    *   **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    *   **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    *   **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and never less than every 6 hours (q6h).
    *   **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    *   **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
*   **Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable when mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL. Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before re-administration [US].
*   **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
*   **General Precautions (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

### Serious and Postmarketing Adverse Effects
*   **Neurologic:** Seizures.
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm.
*   **Digestive:** Transient nausea, vomiting, gastritis, and body odor have been observed with chronic use.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
*   **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned, but data indicates reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
*   **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, S. cerevisiae) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg), applicable across paediatric ranges as directed in Section 3 [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** The injection is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Monitoring of trough levels is required to guide downward dose adjustments (see Section 3) [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
*   **Elimination:** Following intravenous bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
*   **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L by week 8.
*   **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
*   **Stability:** Vials are single-dose; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
