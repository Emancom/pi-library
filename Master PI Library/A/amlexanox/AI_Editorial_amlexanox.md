# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in the provided source (Generic: Levocarnitine Injection, USP) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Injection (Solution): 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing chronic hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The standard dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
  - **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses administered every 3 or 4 hours. Dosing intervals should not exceed 6 hours.
  - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
  - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
  - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment if levels are adequate [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical intervention initiated [US].

### General Precautions
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Caution):** While this document describes the injection, it is noted that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely impaired renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** For patients with metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD Trials)
The following events were reported in clinical trials of dialysis patients, though causality was not always established [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%), chest pain (6–15%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).

### Serious and Other Reported Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in both patients with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and transient nausea/vomiting.
- **Other:** Body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate that levocarnitine may increase the International Normalised Ratio (INR). Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended in patients receiving concomitant warfarin therapy, especially after initiating levocarnitine or adjusting the dose [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned in modern labeling, but historically aligned with Category B]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use in pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The benefits to the mother should be weighed against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing and safety in children are addressed under general metabolic disorder dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral therapy should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, where they undergo beta-oxidation for energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
  - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
  - **Bioavailability (Oral reference):** Approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased plasma carnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to trough levels of 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While increases in carnitine concentrations are well-documented, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial, as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
