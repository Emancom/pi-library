# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from the United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory information into a clinically relevant format.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is intended for informational purposes only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Metabolic Disorders
*   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
*   **Initial Loading:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
*   **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
*   **Daily Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required. The maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg/day [US].

### 3.2 ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
*   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
*   **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are supratherapeutic [US].

### 3.3 Monitoring and Administration
*   **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations. Weekly and monthly monitoring of plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs is recommended [US].
*   **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) stored at 25°C in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **None Known:** No contraindications are currently specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase seizure frequency or severity in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history [US].
*   **Renal Impairment (Oral Metabolites):** While the injection bypasses initial GI metabolism, the safety of chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in renal failure is not established due to the potential accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed. In ESRD clinical trials (regardless of causality), the following were reported at ≥5%:
    *   **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), headache (22–37%), chest pain, fever, flu syndrome.
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia.
    *   **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), abdominal pain.
    *   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase, dyspnea.
*   **Other Noted Effects:** Body odor and gastritis.
*   **Serious Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitor INR levels at initiation and after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound; therefore, it is unlikely to interact with drugs through protein-binding displacement [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category:** Not assigned (Regulatory text indicates Category C equivalent). 
    *   **Data:** Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following administration. Risks to the infant of excess carnitine must be weighed against maternal benefit; consideration may be given to interrupting nursing or treatment [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** See dosage and administration. Safety and efficacy are established for the indicated metabolic and ESRD populations [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects over 65 to determine if they respond differently. Dose selection should be cautious, starting at the low end of the range [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Primarily used in ESRD populations. Note the risk of metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) with chronic oral dosing in severe renal impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for β-oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it corrects the increased ratio of acylcarnitine to carnitine and increases plasma free carnitine levels [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis increased trough carnitine concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. 
*   **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough levels over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increase, the specific effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in ESRD has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
*   **Integrity:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **US:** Prescription Only (Rx Only). Scheduled as a Human Prescription Drug.
