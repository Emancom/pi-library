# Editorial Product Information: anti-thymocyte globulin (rabbit)

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **anti-thymocyte globulin (rabbit)**
- Chemical class: Immunoglobulin G polyclonal anti-thymocyte globulin derived from rabbit sources (polyclonal rabbit IgG)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous use only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 1998 [US]
- Date of most recent revision/update in provided source PI: Revised 6/2026 [US]
- Withdrawals, suspensions, or restrictions: Not specified in available sources for any jurisdiction other than the revision date above
- Registration/approval dates for jurisdictions other than the United States: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prophylaxis of acute rejection in adult and pediatric patients receiving a kidney transplant, used in conjunction with concomitant immunosuppression. [US-approved]
- Treatment of acute rejection in adult and pediatric patients receiving a kidney transplant, used in conjunction with concomitant immunosuppression. [US-approved]
- Indications in other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult and pediatric patients by weight)
- Prophylaxis of acute rejection: **1.5 mg/kg** of body weight administered once daily. Usual duration: **4 to 7 days**.
- Treatment of acute rejection: **1.5 mg/kg** of body weight administered once daily. Usual duration: **7 to 14 days**.
- Note: Dosing is product-specific; ensure prescribed dose corresponds to **anti-thymocyte globulin (rabbit)** and not to other ATG preparations whose concentrations/compositions differ.

### Administration instructions
- First infusion: administer over a minimum of **6 hours**.
- Subsequent infusions: administer over at least **4 hours**.
- Premedication: give corticosteroid, acetaminophen, and/or an antihistamine approximately **1 hour** prior to each infusion to reduce infusion-related reactions.
- Reconstitution (aseptic technique):
  - Reconstitute each vial with **5 mL** Sterile Water for Injection; each reconstituted vial contains 25 mg (5 mg/mL).
  - Allow vial to reach room temperature before reconstitution; clean stopper with alcohol; rotate gently until dissolved; discard vial if persistent particulate matter.
- Dilution:
  - Transfer reconstituted contents into an infusion bag of compatible infusion solution (saline or dextrose).
  - Recommended dilution: **use approximately 50 mL infusion solution per vial** (total infusion volume typically 50–500 mL depending on number of vials and clinical judgement).
  - Mix the infusion bag gently by inversion no more than once or twice.
- Infusion set and venous access:
  - Infuse through a **0.22 micrometer** filter into a high‑flow vein.
  - Administer under strict medical supervision in a hospital setting; monitor closely during infusion.

### Dose modifications / monitoring-based adjustments
- Monitor total white blood cell (WBC) and platelet counts during and after therapy.
  - If WBC count is **2,000–3,000 cells/mm³**, reduce the dose by **one-half**.
  - If platelet count is **50,000–75,000 cells/mm³**, reduce the dose by **one-half**.
  - Consider stopping therapy if WBC count is **<2,000 cells/mm³** or platelet count is **<50,000 cells/mm³**.

### Concomitant medications recommended
- Use with standard concomitant immunosuppression regimens.
- Administer prophylactic antifungal and antibacterial therapy if clinically indicated.
- Antiviral prophylaxis:
  - Recommended for patients who are seropositive for cytomegalovirus (CMV) at time of transplant and for CMV‑seronegative recipients of kidneys from CMV‑seropositive donors; typical prophylaxis duration in trials was 3 months post‑transplant [US].
- Immunizations with live attenuated vaccines are **not recommended** for patients who have recently received therapy.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
- History of allergy or anaphylactic reaction to rabbit proteins or to any product excipients.
- Active acute or chronic infections that contraindicate any additional immunosuppression.
- Any additional jurisdiction‑specific contraindications: Not specified in available sources beyond the items above.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed warnings: No boxed warning explicitly specified in available sources. (The product carries multiple serious warnings; see below.)
- Use only by physicians experienced in immunosuppressive therapy in transplantation.
- Hypersensitivity and infusion‑related reactions:
  - Severe hypersensitivity, including **anaphylaxis**, and severe **cytokine release syndrome (CRS)** including fatal events have been reported.
  - Infusion‑related reactions can be reduced by adhering to recommended infusion rates and premedication; slow or stop infusion if severe reaction occurs and provide supportive treatment.
  - If hypersensitivity or infusion‑related reaction occurs, **terminate the infusion immediately** and manage clinically.
- Cytopenias:
  - **Leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia and anemia** have occurred; monitor blood counts and adjust or stop therapy per dose modification guidance.
  - **Thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA)** has been reported, particularly when administered concurrently with calcineurin inhibitors; monitor for signs of TMA.
- Infection and reactivation:
  - Increased susceptibility to bacterial, fungal, viral and protozoal infections and to reactivation of infections (notably CMV); infections can be severe or fatal.
  - Monitor and treat infections promptly; consider appropriate prophylaxis.
- Malignancy:
  - Use of immunosuppressive agents, including this product, may increase the risk of malignancies, including lymphoma and lymphoproliferative disorders.
  - Malignancies (including fatal outcomes and PTLD) have been reported in clinical studies.
- Immunizations:
  - Safety of live attenuated vaccines following therapy has not been studied; live attenuated vaccines are not recommended for recently treated patients.
- Laboratory test interference:
  - Product may interfere with rabbit antibody–based immunoassays and with cross‑match or panel‑reactive antibody cytotoxicity assays (may persist until rabbit antibodies clear).
  - No interference demonstrated with routine clinical laboratory tests that do not use immunoglobulin reagents.
- Other precautions:
  - To minimize risk of over‑immunosuppression, consider decreasing maintenance immunosuppression during the period of anti‑thymocyte globulin (rabbit) use.

### Monitoring requirements
- Frequent monitoring of WBC, differential, platelets during and after therapy.
- Monitor for signs and symptoms of infusion reactions during infusion and for delayed reactions (e.g., serum sickness).
- Monitor for infection (including CMV) and malignancy during follow‑up.
- Monitor renal function, electrolytes (notably potassium), and other relevant labs as clinically indicated.
- Recognize potential interference with serologic assays and cross‑match testing.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Formal drug interaction studies have not been performed with anti‑thymocyte globulin (rabbit).
- Pharmacodynamic interaction:
  - Increased risk of thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) when used concomitantly with calcineurin inhibitors (e.g., tacrolimus or cyclosporine); monitor closely when co‑administered.
  - Additive immunosuppressive effect with other immunosuppressants; consider decreasing maintenance immunosuppression to avoid over‑immunosuppression.
- Immunogenicity / assay interference:
  - Therapy can stimulate production of antibodies that cross‑react with rabbit immune globulins.
  - May interfere with rabbit antibody–based immunoassays and with cross‑match or panel‑reactive antibody cytotoxicity assays; this can affect serologic testing and transplant compatibility testing.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key trials described in the provided materials:

1) Prophylaxis trial (Study 1)
- Design: randomized, open‑label, international, multicenter trial in adult solitary deceased‑donor kidney transplant patients.
- Population: total n = 278 (THYMOGLOBULIN n = 141; Active Comparator n = 137).
- Major safety findings:
  - Higher rates of overall infections in the THYMOGLOBULIN group (76%; severe in 23%) compared with Active Comparator (63%; severe in 15%).
  - Urinary tract infection was the most frequent infection (approx. 39–42%).
  - Leukopenia and hyperkalemia were frequent laboratory abnormalities (leukopenia ~63%; hyperkalemia ~57% in THYMOGLOBULIN group).
  - Malignancies: six patients in THYMOGLOBULIN group developed malignancies (including EBV‑related lymphomas and other cancers); one patient in comparator developed renal cancer.
  - Infusion‑related reactions, serum sickness (reported in 6 of 405 combined patients across studies), and anaphylactic shock (reported in 2 of 405) were observed.

2) Treatment trial (US Phase 3; Study 3)
- Design: randomized controlled trial comparing anti‑thymocyte globulin (rabbit) to an active comparator for treatment of acute rejection.
- Population: total n = 163 (THYMOGLOBULIN n = 82; Active Comparator n = 81).
- Major safety findings:
  - Adverse reactions more frequent in THYMOGLOBULIN group included chills, leukopenia, headache, abdominal pain, hypertension, nausea, dyspnea, hyperkalemia.
  - Treatment‑emergent thrombocytopenia and infections (including CMV and sepsis) were reported.
  - Malignancies including PTLD occurred in both groups during follow‑up (3 cases THYMOGLOBULIN; 3 cases Active Comparator).

- Trial outcomes for efficacy (e.g., primary efficacy endpoints and comparative rejection rates) were not available in the provided excerpt; the provided source focused largely on safety and dosing.
