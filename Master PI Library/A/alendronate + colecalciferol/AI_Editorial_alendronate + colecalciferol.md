# Editorial Product Information: alendronate + colecalciferol

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **alendronate + colecalciferol**
- Chemical class:
  - Alendronate: bisphosphonate (antiresorptive agent)
  - Colecalciferol: vitamin D3 (secosteroid)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of osteoporosis in select patients where vitamin D supplementation is recommended.
  - Prior to treatment, osteoporosis should be confirmed by either:
    - low bone mass at least 2 standard deviations below the gender-specific young adult mean, or
    - the presence of an osteoporotic fracture.
- Not specified for other indications in the provided sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dose: one tablet once weekly containing alendronate 70 mg (molar equivalent) co-formulated with colecalciferol (either 70 µg = 2,800 IU weekly or 140 µg = 5,600 IU weekly).
  - The 70 µg weekly colecalciferol dose is equivalent to seven daily doses of 400 IU (10 µg).
  - The 140 µg weekly colecalciferol dose is equivalent to seven daily doses of 800 IU (20 µg).

### Dosing by indication
- Treatment of osteoporosis where vitamin D3 supplementation is recommended: one tablet once weekly as above.
- Optimal duration of therapy: not determined; need for continued therapy should be periodically re-evaluated.

### Administration instructions
- Take on arising for the day, at least 30 minutes before the first food, beverage or other medication.
- Swallow the tablet with a full glass of plain water to facilitate delivery to the stomach and reduce oesophageal irritation.
- Do not lie down for at least 30 minutes after taking and until after the first food of the day.
- Do not take at bedtime or before arising for the day.
- Do not chew or suck the tablet (risk of oropharyngeal ulceration).
- Wait at least 30 minutes after dosing before taking any other oral medications or supplements (see Interactions).

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- If a dose is missed, take one tablet on the morning after remembering.
- Do not take two tablets on the same day; return to the once-weekly schedule on the originally chosen day.

## 5. Contraindications
List of contraindications (all items from available sources):
- Abnormalities of the oesophagus which delay oesophageal emptying (e.g., stricture, achalasia).
- Inability to stand or sit upright for at least 30 minutes.
- Known hypersensitivity to alendronate, colecalciferol, or any component of the product.
- Hypocalcaemia.
- Use in children: not studied; should not be given to children (contraindicated for paediatric use in provided sources).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- **Severe oesophageal ulceration and other serious oesophageal adverse reactions have been reported.** Patients must receive full dosing and administration instructions and must be advised to discontinue the product and seek medical attention if they develop dysphagia, odynophagia, retrosternal pain, or new or worsening heartburn.

### General warnings and precautions
- Correct hypocalcaemia and treat vitamin D deficiency before initiating therapy. The vitamin D content of the combination is not suitable to correct established vitamin D deficiency or osteomalacia.
- Alendronate, as other bisphosphonates, may cause irritation of the upper gastrointestinal (GI) mucosa. Exercise caution when treating patients with active upper GI disease (e.g., dysphagia, known Barrett's oesophagus, gastritis, duodenitis, or ulcers).
- Remain upright and take with a full glass of water to reduce risk of oesophageal irritation. Failure to follow dosing instructions increases risk of oesophageal adverse experiences.
- Vitamin D3 (colecalciferol) may worsen hypercalcaemia or hypercalciuria in patients with diseases associated with unregulated calcitriol production (e.g., lymphoma, sarcoidosis, certain leukemias). Monitor serum and urine calcium in these patients.
- Patients with malabsorption may have inadequate absorption of vitamin D3.
- Localised osteonecrosis of the jaw (ONJ) has been reported rarely with oral bisphosphonates, generally in association with tooth extraction and/or local infection. Consider dental examination with preventive dentistry in patients with risk factors prior to initiating therapy. For invasive dental procedures, clinical judgment should guide management and whether to discontinue bisphosphonate therapy on an individual risk/benefit basis.
- Atypical stress fractures of the proximal femoral shaft (insufficiency fractures), generally in patients treated long-term (usually >3 years), have been reported. Patients may have prodromal thigh/groin pain. Evaluate suspected cases and consider discontinuation pending evaluation based on individual benefit/risk.
- Bone, joint, and/or muscle pain has been reported; in rare cases this has been severe and/or incapacitating. Symptoms may begin days to months after starting therapy and may improve after stopping therapy.
- Small, asymptomatic decreases in serum calcium and phosphate may occur; monitor where clinically indicated (see Laboratory effects).
- Ensure adequate calcium and vitamin D intake, especially in patients with Paget’s disease or those receiving glucocorticoids.
- Use in renal impairment: not recommended in patients with creatinine clearance < 35 mL/min. No dosage adjustment necessary for elderly or for patients with mild-to-moderate renal insufficiency (CrCl 35–60 mL/min).
- Use in breastfeeding women: not studied; should not be given to women who are breast-feeding.
- Paediatric use: not studied; should not be given to children.
- Monitor patients for signs of oesophageal reaction; discontinue if symptoms occur.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor serum calcium in patients at risk of hypocalcaemia or with disorders of mineral metabolism; correct hypocalcaemia before starting therapy.
- Monitor serum and urine calcium in patients with conditions associated with increased calcitriol production.
- Consider dental assessment in patients with risk factors for ONJ and monitor for dental symptoms during therapy.
- Evaluate patients with unexplained thigh, hip, or groin pain for possible femoral stress fracture; image contralateral femur if fracture identified.
- Monitor renal function when clinically indicated (see renal impairment guidance).
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of oesophageal irritation (dysphagia, odynophagia, retrosternal pain, persistent heartburn).

### Specific clinical precautions
- Do not co-administer calcium-containing supplements or antacids at the same time as alendronate; these impair absorption.
- Do not co-administer other oral medications at the same time; wait at least 30 minutes after dosing.
- Consider total vitamin D intake from concomitant supplements to avoid excessive dosing; additional supplements should not be taken at the same time of day as the combination product.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Calcium supplements, antacids, and other oral medications (if taken concomitantly) reduce absorption of alendronate; patients must wait at least 30 minutes after taking the product before taking any other oral medication.
- For colecalciferol:
  - Olestra, mineral oils, orlistat, and bile acid sequestrants (e.g., cholestyramine, colestipol) may impair absorption of vitamin D.
  - Anticonvulsants, cimetidine, and thiazides may increase the catabolism of vitamin D.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant use of hormone replacement therapy (oestrogen ± progestin) with alendronate was studied; combined use produced greater increases in bone mass and greater decreases in bone turnover than either alone. Safety profile of combination consistent with individual treatments in the studies described.
- NSAIDs: since NSAID use is associated with GI irritation, exercise caution during concomitant use with alendronate.

### Clinically significant interactions / management notes
- Do not administer calcium-containing supplements or antacids at the same time as the alendronate + colecalciferol tablet.
- Wait at least 30 minutes after taking alendronate + colecalciferol before taking any other oral medication.
- Consider timing of additional vitamin D supplementation because the product already provides substantial weekly vitamin D; avoid taking additional vitamin D supplements at the same time of day as the combination product.
- Clinically significant interactions with other drugs not detailed beyond the above in the available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
- Australian pregnancy classification: Category B3 [Australian].
- Use in pregnancy:
  - Alendronate: has not been studied in pregnant women and should not be given to them.
  - Animal data (rats): oral alendronate doses ≥2 mg/kg/day resulted in dystocia due to maternal hypocalcaemia; fetal weight reduced at maternal doses >5 mg/kg/day. No teratogenic effects seen in rats or rabbits at oral doses up to 25 and 35 mg/kg/day respectively.
  - Colecalciferol: clinical human pregnancy data not available in sources; animal data with ergocalciferol (vitamin D2) indicate developmental toxicity at high doses in rabbits and rats.
  - No reproductive toxicity studies of the alendronate + colecalciferol combination were reported in the provided sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Not studied in breast-feeding women; the combination should not be given to breast-feeding women (per available sources).
- No data on presence in human milk or effects on the breast-fed infant provided.

### Fertility
- Alendronate: no effect on fertility in male and female rats at oral doses up to 9 and 15 mg/kg/day respectively.
- Alendronate + colecalciferol combination: no fertility data available in the provided sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials

- Two three-year, placebo-controlled, double-blind, multicentre studies in postmenopausal women: safety data reported (see Adverse Effects); discontinuation rates due to any clinical adverse experience were 4.1% for alendronate 10 mg/day (n=196) and 6.0% for placebo (n=397) in one presentation of study data. Specific efficacy endpoints (e.g., fracture reduction rates) were not provided in the available excerpts.
- Fracture Intervention Trial: reported discontinuation rates due to any clinical adverse experience — alendronate 5–10 mg/day group 9.1% (n=3236) versus placebo 10.1% (n=3223); discontinuations due to upper GI adverse experiences were 3.2% (alendronate) vs 2.7% (placebo). Trial design in the available text indicates randomized, controlled structure but detailed primary outcomes were not provided in the provided sources.
- One-year double-blind multicentre study comparing once-weekly 70 mg alendronate to daily 10 mg showed overall similar safety and tolerability profiles; adverse event rates for common GI and musculoskeletal events were similar between regimens (see Adverse Effects for study-specific frequencies).
- Other clinical efficacy details and primary outcome data were not included in the provided source excerpts.
