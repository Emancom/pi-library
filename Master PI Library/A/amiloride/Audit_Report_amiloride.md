# Originality Audit Report: amiloride

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 92/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 2
- **Overall Assessment:** Pass

## Verbatim Copied Passages
The following passages are identical or near-identical to the source text, though they primarily concern technical parameters where variation is limited:

1.  **Text:** "BUN >10.7 mmol/L (30 mg/100 mL) or serum creatinine >0.13 mmol/L (1.5 mg/100 mL)"
    *   **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 4.3 (Contraindications).
    *   **Location:** Editorial Section 3, Impaired Renal Function.
2.  **Text:** "eliminated unchanged by the kidneys (approx. 50%) and in the feces (approx. 40%) within 72 hours"
    *   **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 5.2 (Pharmacokinetic Properties) and Document 2 (FDA), Pharmacokinetics.
    *   **Location:** Editorial Section 9, Excretion.

## Close Paraphrases
1.  **Mechanism of Action:** The editorial describes the mechanism as "inhibiting sodium reabsorption in the distal convoluted tubule and the cortical collecting duct. By reducing the net negative potential of the tubular lumen, it inhibits the secretion of potassium and hydrogen ions..." This is a very close paraphrase of the TGA/FDA text: "exerts its potassium sparing effect through the inhibition of sodium reabsorption at the distal convoluted tubule, cortical collecting tubule and collecting duct; this decreases the net negative potential of the tubular lumen and reduces both potassium and hydrogen secretion..." (Editorial Section 10).
2.  **Metabolic Acidosis Warning:** "Shifts in acid-base balance can cause rapid increases in extracellular potassium" is a condensed version of the source's "Shifts in acid-base balance alter the ratio of extracellular/intracellular potassium and the development of acidosis may be associated with rapid increases in serum potassium levels." (Editorial Section 4).

## Originality Strengths
- **Structural Synthesis:** The editorial successfully creates a new hierarchical structure that does not exist in either source, particularly in how it separates "Monotherapy" and "Combination Therapy" clinical data.
- **Introductory Context:** The "Editorial Note" is entirely original and provides necessary regulatory context for the harmonization of the two documents.
- **Summarization:** The sections on Thiazide-specific warnings (NMSC, ARDS) and Pregnancy/Breastfeeding are excellent summaries that distill several paragraphs of source text into clear, bulleted takeaways without relying on the source's sentence structure.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Harmonization:** The editorial explicitly identifies which information comes from which agency (using [FDA, TGA] tags), which is not present in the sources.
- **Metric Conversion:** The editorial helpfully provides both mmol/L and mg/100 mL for lab values, whereas Document 2 (FDA) uses only US units.
- **Omission of Redundancy:** The editorial strips away repetitive brand-name mentions (Moduretic) and replaces them with generic active ingredient descriptions.
- **Categorization:** It provides a dual-category view for pregnancy (Category B vs Category C), acknowledging the differences in regulatory classifications between the US and Australia for monotherapy vs. combination therapy.

## Recommendation
**PASS.** 

The editorial is a high-quality, original synthesis. While technical descriptions of the mechanism of action and specific laboratory thresholds remain close to the source text for the sake of medical accuracy and regulatory compliance, the prose and overall arrangement of the document are the author's own. No revision is required.
