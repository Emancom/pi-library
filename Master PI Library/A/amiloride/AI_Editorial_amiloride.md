It should be noted that while the FDA source focuses on amiloride hydrochloride as a monotherapy, the TGA source describes a fixed-dose combination of amiloride hydrochloride and hydrochlorothiazide. # Unified Product Information: Amiloride Hydrochloride

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Amiloride Hydrochloride (available as monotherapy or in combination with Hydrochlorothiazide).
**Active Ingredient:** Amiloride hydrochloride (as the dihydrate).

## 2. Approved Indications
Amiloride is a potassium-conserving (antikaliuretic) diuretic indicated for the following:

*   **Edema of Cardiac Origin:** Management of congestive heart failure, typically as an adjunct to thiazide diuretics or other kaliuretic agents to maintain serum potassium levels [FDA, TGA].
*   **Hepatic Cirrhosis with Ascites:** Treatment of fluid retention associated with liver disease [TGA].
*   **Hypertension:** As adjunctive therapy to prevent potassium depletion in hypertensive patients where such loss is anticipated or where maintaining electrolyte balance is critical (e.g., patients on digitalis or those with significant cardiac arrhythmias) [FDA, TGA].

Note: Monotherapy is generally reserved for patients with documented, persistent hypokalemia despite titrated therapy [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
Amiloride is contraindicated in the following conditions:
*   **Hyperkalemia:** In the presence of elevated serum potassium levels, defined as >5.5 mmol/L (mEq/L) [FDA, TGA].
*   **Concurrent Antikaliuretic Therapy:** Use with other potassium-sparing agents (e.g., spironolactone, triamterene) or potassium supplementation is strictly contraindicated due to the risk of rapid, fatal increases in serum potassium [FDA, TGA].
*   **Impaired Renal Function:**
    *   Anuria, acute or chronic renal failure, or evidence of diabetic nephropathy [FDA, TGA].
    *   Patients with a Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN) >10.7 mmol/L (30 mg/100 mL) or serum creatinine >0.13 mmol/L (1.5 mg/100 mL) should not receive this medication without intensive electrolyte monitoring [FDA, TGA].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known sensitivity to amiloride or, in the case of combination products, sulfonamide-derived drugs [TGA, FDA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Hyperkalemia
Amiloride can cause severe, potentially fatal hyperkalemia. Risk factors include renal impairment, diabetes mellitus (even without recognized renal disease), and advanced age [FDA, TGA]. Serum potassium must be monitored closely, especially upon initiation, during dosage adjustments, or during any illness that may impair renal function.

### Metabolic or Respiratory Acidosis
Therapy should be initiated with extreme caution in severely ill patients predisposed to acidosis, such as those with cardiopulmonary disease or decompensated diabetes. Shifts in acid-base balance can cause rapid increases in extracellular potassium [FDA, TGA].

### Thiazide-Specific Warnings (Combination Therapy)
When used in combination with hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ), the following risks apply:
*   **Non-melanoma Skin Cancer (NMSC):** Cumulative exposure to HCTZ is associated with an increased risk of basal cell and squamous cell carcinoma. Patients should be advised to limit sun exposure and report new skin lesions [TGA].
*   **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS):** Severe pulmonary toxicity has been reported post-HCTZ intake. If ARDS is suspected, the drug must be discontinued [TGA].
*   **Ocular Effects:** Idiosyncratic reactions may lead to choroidal effusion, acute myopia, or secondary angle-closure glaucoma [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Amiloride should be administered with food to improve gastrointestinal tolerance.

### Monotherapy (Amiloride HCl)
*   **Initial Dose:** 5 mg once daily.
*   **Adjustment:** May be increased to 10 mg/day if required. Doses exceeding 10 mg are rarely necessary, though up to 20 mg may be used with strict electrolyte monitoring in cases of persistent hypokalemia [FDA].

### Combination Therapy (Amiloride/HCTZ)
*   **Standard Dose:** 1 to 2 tablets daily (usually 5 mg amiloride/50 mg HCTZ per tablet).
*   **Maximum Dose:** Should not exceed 4 tablets per day.
*   **Maintenance:** Once diuresis is achieved, the dose should be reduced for maintenance therapy [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Amiloride is generally well-tolerated, but the following may occur:
*   **General:** Headache, weakness, fatigue, chest pain [FDA, TGA].
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, anorexia, diarrhea, vomiting, abdominal pain, appetite changes, and rare activation of peptic ulcers [FDA, TGA].
*   **Metabolic:** Hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, and gout. Combination therapy may cause hypokalemia or hyperglycaemia due to the thiazide component [TGA].
*   **Nervous System:** Dizziness, encephalopathy (in patients with pre-existing liver disease), paresthesia, and tremors [FDA, TGA].
*   **Dermatological:** Rash, pruritus, alopecia [FDA].
*   **Urogenital/Other:** Impotence, dysuria, and rare cases of interstitial nephritis [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **ACE Inhibitors/ARBs:** Concomitant use significantly increases the risk of hyperkalemia [FDA, TGA].
*   **NSAIDs:** Use with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (including COX-2 inhibitors) may reduce the diuretic and antihypertensive effects and increase the risk of renal failure and hyperkalemia, particularly in the elderly [TGA, FDA].
*   **Lithium:** Diuretics reduce the renal clearance of lithium, significantly increasing the risk of lithium toxicity [FDA, TGA].
*   **Potassium Supplements:** Strictly contraindicated due to the risk of life-threatening hyperkalemia [FDA, TGA].
*   **Resins (Cholestyramine/Colestipol):** May impair the absorption of the thiazide component in combination products [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **Amiloride Monotherapy:** FDA Category B. Animal studies show no evidence of fetal harm, but human data are lacking. Use only if clearly needed [FDA].
*   **Combination Therapy:** TGA Category C. Thiazides cross the placenta and may cause neonatal thrombocytopenia or electrolyte imbalances. Use is not recommended for routine edema in healthy pregnant women [TGA].

### Breastfeeding
Amiloride is excreted in animal milk; however, its presence in human milk is unconfirmed. Thiazides do appear in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug [FDA, TGA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Onset of action occurs within 2 hours of oral administration [FDA, TGA].
*   **Peak Effect:** Electrolyte excretion peaks between 6 and 10 hours.
*   **Duration:** Approximately 24 hours.
*   **Metabolism:** Amiloride is not metabolized by the liver.
*   **Excretion:** Eliminated unchanged by the kidneys (approx. 50%) and in the feces (approx. 40%) within 72 hours [FDA, TGA].
*   **Half-life:** Plasma half-life ranges from 6 to 9 hours [FDA, TGA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Amiloride is a potassium-sparing agent with relatively weak natriuretic and antihypertensive properties compared to thiazides. It exerts its effect by inhibiting sodium reabsorption in the distal convoluted tubule and the cortical collecting duct. By reducing the net negative potential of the tubular lumen, it inhibits the secretion of potassium and hydrogen ions, thereby conserving these ions while promoting sodium excretion [FDA, TGA]. It is not an aldosterone antagonist.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Amiloride (FDA):** Store at controlled room temperature between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) in a tight, light-resistant container.
*   **Combination Product (TGA):** Store below 30°C.
