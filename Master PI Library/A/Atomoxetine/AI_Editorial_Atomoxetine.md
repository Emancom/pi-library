# Editorial Product Information: Atomoxetine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **Atomoxetine** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data to support quality use of medicines.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource. It is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Atomoxetine (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor (SNRI).
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Atomoxetine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06BA09.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Hard gelatin capsules in 10 mg, 18 mg, 25 mg, 40 mg, 60 mg, 80 mg, and 100 mg strengths [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD):** Indicated for the treatment of ADHD as defined by DSM-IV criteria in children aged 6 years and older, adolescents, and adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
**Atomoxetine** capsules are for oral administration and may be taken with or without food. Capsules must not be opened as the powder is an ocular irritant [Australian].

### Paediatric and Adolescent (up to 70 kg Body Weight)
- **Initial Dose:** Approximately 0.5 mg/kg/day.
- **Titration:** Increase after a minimum of 3 days to a target dose of approximately 1.2 mg/kg/day.
- **Maximum Dose:** 1.4 mg/kg/day or 100 mg, whichever is less.
- **Administration:** Administered as a single daily dose in the morning or as two evenly divided doses (morning and late afternoon/early evening).

### Paediatric and Adolescent (over 70 kg Body Weight) and Adults
- **Initial Dose:** 40 mg/day.
- **Titration:** Increase after a minimum of 3 days to a target dose of approximately 80 mg/day.
- **Maintenance:** After 2 to 4 weeks, the dose may be increased to a maximum of 100 mg/day in patients who have not achieved an optimal response.
- **Maximum Recommended Total Daily Dose:** 100 mg.

### Missed Doses and Interruption
- **Interrupted Treatment:** If therapy is paused for more than one week, it should be restarted at the recommended initial starting dose.
- **Discontinuation:** The drug may be discontinued without tapering the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
**Atomoxetine** is contraindicated in patients with:
- Known hypersensitivity to the drug or its excipients.
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use or use within 2 weeks of discontinuing an MAOI. MAOI therapy should not start within 2 weeks of stopping **Atomoxetine**.
- **Narrow-Angle Glaucoma:** Due to the increased risk of mydriasis.
- **Phaeochromocytoma:** Including a history of phaeochromocytoma.
- **Severe Cardiovascular Disorders:** Conditions where clinical deterioration is expected with increases in heart rate (20 bpm) or blood pressure (15–20 mmHg).
- **Symptomatic Cardiovascular Disease:** Moderate to severe hypertension, atrial fibrillation/flutter, ventricular arrhythmias, or advanced arteriosclerosis.
- **Uncontrolled Hyperthyroidism.**

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Suicidality in Children and Adolescents [Australian]
Pooled analyses of short-term trials in children and adolescents with ADHD showed a greater risk of suicidal ideation (0.44%) compared to placebo (0%). One suicide attempt occurred in the atomoxetine group; no completed suicides were reported. Clinicians must balance clinical need with the risk of suicidality. All paediatric patients must be monitored closely for suicidal thinking, behaviour, clinical worsening, or unusual changes in mood (anxiety, agitation, irritability, hostility).

### Cardiovascular Effects
- **Monitoring:** Heart rate and blood pressure should be measured at baseline, after dose adjustments, and periodically during treatment.
- **Structural Cardiac Abnormalities:** **Atomoxetine** should generally not be used in children or adults with known structural cardiac defects, cardiomyopathy, or serious rhythm abnormalities due to the risk of sudden death.
- **QT Prolongation:** Use caution in patients with congenital or acquired QT prolongation or a family history thereof.

### Hepatic Injury
**Atomoxetine** can cause severe liver injury, including rare cases of acute liver failure. The drug should be discontinued and not restarted in patients who develop jaundice or laboratory evidence of liver injury.

### Psychiatric Effects
- **Psychosis/Mania:** Treatment-emergent psychotic or manic symptoms (e.g., hallucinations, delusional thinking) can occur in patients without a prior history.
- **Aggressive Behaviour:** Hostility and emotional lability have been observed; patients should be monitored for the appearance or worsening of these behaviours.
- **Bipolar Disorder:** Patients should be screened for bipolar disorder before initiation to avoid inducing a mixed/manic episode.

### Other Precautions
- **Seizures:** Use with caution in patients with a history of seizures.
- **Priapism:** Reported in post-marketing cases; requires prompt medical attention.
- **Growth:** Height and weight should be monitored in children; data suggests a slight decrease in expected height/weight gain during the first year of treatment, which generally normalises over time.
- **Urinary Effects:** Potential for urinary retention or hesitation, particularly in patients with bladder outlet obstruction or prostatic enlargement.
- **Peripheral Vasculopathy:** Includes Raynaud’s phenomenon; monitor for digital changes.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Paediatric Population
- **Very Common ($\ge$10%):** Abdominal pain, vomiting, nausea, decreased appetite (14%), increased blood pressure/heart rate, somnolence.
- **Common (1–10%):** Constipation, dyspepsia, influenza, dizziness, mood swings, dermatitis, anorexia, insomnia, irritability, pruritus, rash, weight decreased, depression.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Palpitations, sinus tachycardia, conjunctivitis, syncope, tremor.

### Adult Population
- **Very Common ($\ge$10%):** Dry mouth (21%), nausea (12%), constipation (10%), appetite decreased (10%), insomnia (13%), increased blood pressure/heart rate.
- **Common (1–10%):** Palpitations, flatulence, rigors, dizziness, headache, libido decreased, erectile disturbance (7%), dysmenorrhoea (7%), urinary hesitation/retention, hyperhidrosis, hot flushes.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Suicidal ideation/behaviour (Paediatric) [Australian].
- Severe liver injury.
- Sudden cardiac death.
- Anaphylactic reactions (uncommon).
- Priapism (very rare).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., fluoxetine, paroxetine, quinidine) significantly increase atomoxetine plasma concentrations (6- to 8-fold AUC increase in Extensive Metabolisers). Slower titration is required.
- **Cytochrome P450:** Atomoxetine does not clinically inhibit or induce CYP1A2, CYP3A, CYP2D6, or CYP2C9.
- **Midazolam:** Co-administration results in a small increase in midazolam plasma concentration (no dose adjustment needed for CYP3A4 substrates).
- **Protein Binding:** Atomoxetine is highly bound to albumin (98.5%). It increases the unbound fraction of diazepam, paroxetine, and phenytoin in vitro.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Risk of fatal reactions (hyperthermia, myoclonus, autonomic instability). (See Contraindications).
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Risk of serotonin syndrome when combined with SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, or tramadol.
- **Pressor Agents:** Potential for additive effects on blood pressure; use cautiously with drugs like salbutamol.
- **Antihypertensives:** Atomoxetine may reduce the effectiveness of antihypertensive therapy.
- **Seizure Threshold:** Caution with drugs that lower the seizure threshold (e.g., tricyclic antidepressants, SSRIs, neuroleptics, bupropion).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B3. No adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal studies showed reduced foetal survival and skeletal anomalies at maternally toxic doses. Use only if potential benefits justify potential risks to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Atomoxetine is excreted in rat milk. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk; caution is advised [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment of fertility or reproductive performance at doses up to 50 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 6 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients over 65 years of age have not been evaluated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Reduce initial and target doses to 50% of the normal dose.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Reduce initial and target doses to 25% of the normal dose.
- **Renal Impairment:** ADHD patients with end-stage renal disease should undergo cautious titration to the desired response; the drug may exacerbate hypertension in this population.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Atomoxetine is a highly selective and potent inhibitor of the presynaptic noradrenaline transporter (Ki 4.5 nM). It has minimal affinity for other noradrenergic receptors or other neurotransmitter transporters.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapidly and almost completely absorbed (Tmax ~1–2 hours). High-fat meals decrease Cmax by 37% but do not affect AUC.
- **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is 0.85 L/kg. 98.5% plasma protein bound.
- **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by the CYP2D6 pathway. The main metabolite, 4-hydroxyatomoxetine, is equipotent to the parent drug. 
    - **Extensive Metabolisers (EM):** Half-life ~3.6 hours.
    - **Poor Metabolisers (PM):** Half-life ~21 hours. PMs have 10-fold higher AUC compared to EMs.
- **Excretion:** Excreted primarily as 4-hydroxyatomoxetine-O-glucuronide in the urine (>80%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Paediatric Efficacy:** Established in four acute (6–9 week) placebo-controlled trials. Primary outcome was the ADHDRS-IV-Parent Version. Significant improvements were seen at doses of 1.2 mg/kg/day and 1.8 mg/kg/day [Australian].
- **Adult Efficacy:** Established in two 10-week trials (LYAA, LYAO) using the CAARS scale. Mean final doses were ~95 mg/day, showing statistically significant symptom reduction [Australian].
- **Relapse Prevention:** Study LYAF showed a relapse rate of 18.7% for atomoxetine vs 31.4% for placebo over 40 weeks [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
