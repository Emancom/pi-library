# Editorial Product Information: adrenaline (epinephrine)

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **adrenaline (epinephrine)**
- Chemical class: sympathomimetic catecholamine (alpha- and beta-adrenergic agonist)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration:
  - Recommended: intramuscular injection (anterolateral thigh)
  - Additional PK context: subcutaneous administration is discussed in pharmacokinetic data (not specified as an intended route for the auto-injector)
  - Intravenous administration: **contraindicated for auto-injector use** (see Warnings)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of source product information: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Emergency treatment of anaphylaxis (acute severe allergic reactions) due to insect stings or bites, foods, drugs or other allergens.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing
- Adults (≥ 30 kg): single intramuscular dose of 0.3 mg (0.3 mL) adrenaline.
- Children (15 to 30 kg): single intramuscular dose of 0.15 mg (0.3 mL) adrenaline.
- Selection of dose should be based on patient body weight and clinical assessment of the life‑threatening reaction.

### Repeat dosing / titration
- If symptoms recur or have not subsided, the adrenaline dose may be repeated every 5 to 15 minutes.
- More than two sequential doses should only be given under direct medical supervision.

### Practical administration instructions
- Intended for immediate self- or caregiver-administration as emergency supportive therapy; not a substitute for emergency medical care — patients must seek medical evaluation after use.
- Inspect the solution through the device viewing window prior to use; use only if clear, colourless and free of particles.
- Administer intramuscularly into the anterolateral aspect of the mid-thigh (not the buttock). The device is designed to deliver through clothing or directly through skin.
- Deploy device firmly against the outer mid-thigh until an activation click is felt/heard; maintain firm pressure for approximately 3 seconds to ensure delivery.
- For young/uncooperative children, restrain the leg to minimise movement or kicking during injection.
- Each auto-injector is single-use only. Dispose of used devices in an appropriate sharps container.
- Do not inject intravenously with an auto-injector; every effort should be made to avoid inadvertent intravascular administration.

### Special administration considerations
- In patients with a thick subcutaneous fat layer (> 20 mm skin-to-muscle distance under maximum compression) there is risk that IM injection may not reach muscle tissue and effect may be suboptimal; an additional IM injection may be required.
- Avoid injection into hands, fingers, feet, ears, nose, buttocks or genitalia — such injections may cause loss of blood flow to the affected area and may not treat anaphylaxis effectively.
- Do not use the auto-injector for demonstration purposes; patients/caregivers should be instructed in correct use from the package leaflet and by a healthcare professional.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
(Contraindications are relative given the life‑threatening indication)
- Known contraindications / conditions where adrenaline should not be used unless the benefit outweighs risk:
  - Certain types of arrhythmia
  - Cerebral arteriosclerosis
  - Situations where vasopressor drugs are contraindicated, e.g. thyrotoxicosis
  - Shock other than anaphylactic shock
  - During general anaesthesia with halogenated hydrocarbons or cyclopropane
- Note: the product information emphasises that contraindications are relative because adrenaline is used for life‑threatening emergencies.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Critical warnings
- **DO NOT INJECT INTRAVENOUSLY** with the auto-injector; inadvertent intravascular administration can cause a marked rise in blood pressure and may precipitate cerebral haemorrhage, pulmonary oedema and death.
- Administration of adrenaline via an auto-injector is emergency supportive therapy and not a substitute for prompt medical or hospital care.

### General warnings and precautions
- Confirm presence of anaphylactic shock when possible before administration since the product is indicated for anaphylaxis.
- Patients/carers should be informed about the risk of biphasic anaphylaxis (initial recovery followed by a recurrence of symptoms).
- Prescribe two auto-injectors and advise patients to carry them at all times in situations of potential risk.
- More than two sequential doses should only be administered under direct medical supervision.
- The product contains sodium metabisulfite (a sulfite) which may cause allergic-type reactions including bronchospasm in susceptible individuals (particularly those with asthma); however, presence of a sulfite should not prevent use in life‑threatening allergic reactions.
- Cleansing skin with alcohol does not remove Clostridium spores; injection into the buttock has been associated with Clostridial soft tissue infections (including gas gangrene). Do not inject into the buttock.
- Hold the leg firmly during injection in young children to reduce risk of lacerations, bent or embedded needles caused by kicking or movement.
- Use caution in patients with: ventricular fibrillation, prefibrillatory rhythm, tachycardia, recent or current myocardial infarction, phenothiazine-induced circulatory collapse, cardiac dilation, cardiac arrhythmias, coronary artery disease, hypertension, organic brain damage, severe renal impairment, prostatic adenoma with residual urine, hypercalcaemia, hypokalaemia, hyperthyroidism, Parkinsonism (may increase rigidity and tremor), psychoneurosis and in the elderly.
- In patients with high intraocular pressure associated with angle-closure glaucoma, adrenaline-induced mydriasis may precipitate an acute angle-closure episode; in open-angle glaucoma adrenaline may reduce intraocular pressure.
- Syncope has been reported following administration to asthmatic children.
- Advise patients to seek medical care if they develop signs of infection (persistent redness, warmth, swelling or tenderness) at the injection site.

### Monitoring requirements
- Immediate medical assessment after use is required; monitor cardiovascular and respiratory status as clinically indicated.
- Specific laboratory monitoring recommendations are not specified in available sources.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- The following interactions are documented in the available product information.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions / potentiation
- Effects of adrenaline may be potentiated by:
  - Tricyclic antidepressants
  - Levothyroxine sodium and other thyroid hormones
  - Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAO inhibitors)
  - Catechol-O-methyl transferase (COMT) inhibitors
  - Theophylline
  - Oxytocin
  - Parasympatholytics (anticholinergics)
  - Certain antihistamines (e.g. diphenhydramine, dexchlorpheniramine, chlorpheniramine, tripelennamine)
  - Levodopa
  - Alcohol
- Concomitant use with other sympathomimetic agents is not recommended because of additive effects and increased toxicity.

### Antagonists / altered response
- Alpha-adrenergic blocking agents (e.g. ergot alkaloids, phentolamine) antagonise adrenaline's vasoconstrictor and hypertensive effects.
- Beta-adrenergic blocking drugs (particularly non‑selective agents) may:
  - Antagonise the cardiostimulatory and bronchodilatory effects of adrenaline
  - When adrenaline is given to patients on non‑selective beta‑blockers, severe hypertension and bradycardia may occur

### Other clinically relevant interactions
- Digoxin or quinidine: risk of cardiac arrhythmias may be higher when adrenaline is administered concomitantly.
- General anaesthetics (e.g. halothane, cyclopropane, trichlorethylene): increase risk of adrenaline‑induced ventricular arrhythmias and acute pulmonary oedema if hypoxia is present.
- Hypoglycaemic agents: adrenaline-induced hyperglycaemia may impair glycaemic control in diabetic patients; insulin or oral hypoglycaemic dose adjustments may be required.

### Interaction management notes
- Avoid or use caution with the above concomitant agents; monitor cardiovascular status and blood glucose as clinically indicated.
- Specific dosing adjustments for interactions are not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category: [Australian] Category A.
- Available information indicates adrenaline has been administered to many pregnant women without proven increased risk of malformations or other harmful foetal effects when used for its indicated emergency purpose.
- Adrenaline may delay the second stage of labour by inhibiting uterine contractions.
- Use with caution in pregnant women whose maternal blood pressure exceeds 130/80 mmHg.
- Other jurisdictions' pregnancy category/classification: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Adrenaline is excreted in breast milk but is not orally bioavailable and would not be expected to have adverse effects on the nursing infant.
- Recommendation: use in breastfeeding mothers when clinically indicated for anaphylaxis.

### Fertility
- Studies evaluating effects on fertility after repeated exposure in animals have not been conducted. Given that adrenaline occurs naturally in the body, detrimental effects on fertility are considered unlikely. No specific fertility data are provided.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **adrenaline (epinephrine)** is a sympathomimetic catecholamine acting on both alpha and beta adrenergic receptors.
  - Alpha stimulation: reduces vasodilatation and vascular permeability associated with anaphylaxis, supporting blood pressure.
  - Beta-1 stimulation: increases heart rate, cardiac output and coronary perfusion.
  - Beta-2 stimulation: causes bronchial smooth muscle relaxation, relieving bronchospasm, wheeze and dyspnoea.
  - Additional effects: antihistaminic action, bronchodilation, smooth muscle relaxation in gastrointestinal, uterine and urinary tracts.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: onset of action is rapid; duration is short. Plasma half‑life approximately 2.5 minutes. After subcutaneous or intramuscular administration, local vasoconstriction may retard absorption so clinical effects last longer than the plasma half‑life would predict.
- Distribution: rapidly distributed to heart, spleen, glandular tissues and adrenergic nerves; approximately 50% plasma protein bound.
- Metabolism: rapidly inactivated by COMT and MAO in liver and other tissues.
- Elimination: up to 90% of an intravenous dose is excreted as metabolites in urine.
- Placental transfer: crosses the placenta; excreted in breast milk.
- Pharmacokinetic study data: in a crossover study of 35 healthy subjects varying by thigh subcutaneous fat thickness, female subjects with skin-to-muscle distance >20 mm had slower absorption and a trend toward lower plasma exposure in the first 10 minutes after injection from an auto-injector. Overall exposure from 0–30 min with auto-injector exceeded that from syringe delivery in this study, but inter- and intra-subject variability was high.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Major clinical effects include increased systolic blood pressure with reduced diastolic pressure, tachycardia, hyperglycaemia and hypokalaemia. Potent cardiac stimulant and vasopressor.
