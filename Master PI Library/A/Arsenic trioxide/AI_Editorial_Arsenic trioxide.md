# Editorial Product Information: Arsenic Trioxide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of arsenic trioxide, synthesized from official Product Information (PI) sources to assist healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for the most current and region-specific requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Arsenic trioxide (As₂O₃)
- **Chemical Class:** Inorganic arsenic compound
- **Brand Names:** Arsenic Trioxide - AFT [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01XX27
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Concentrated solution for injection: 10 mg in 10 mL glass ampoules and 12 mg in 12 mL glass vials [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion (after dilution).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsed/Refractory Acute Promyelocytic Leukaemia (APL):** Induction of remission and consolidation in patients who have relapsed from or are refractory to retinoid and anthracycline therapy. The disease must be characterized by the t(15:17) translocation or PML/RAR-alpha gene expression [Australian].
- **Newly Diagnosed APL:** Induction of remission and consolidation in previously untreated patients, used in combination with all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA) and/or chemotherapy. The disease must be characterized by the t(15:17) translocation or PML/RAR-alpha gene expression [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Dose:** 0.15 mg/kg/day.
- **Administration:** Administered via intravenous (IV) infusion over 2 hours.
- **Dilution:** Dilute with 100–250 mL of 5% glucose injection or 0.9% sodium chloride injection [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Newly Diagnosed (High-Risk, WBC > 10 x 10⁹/L):** 
    - **Induction:** 0.15 mg/kg/day from Day 9 to Day 36.
    - **Consolidation Cycle 2:** 0.15 mg/kg/day for 28 days.
    - **Consolidation Cycle 3:** 0.15 mg/kg/day, 5 days per week for 5 weeks [Australian].
- **Newly Diagnosed (Low/Intermediate Risk, WBC ≤ 10 x 10⁹/L):**
    - **Induction:** 0.15 mg/kg/day until complete remission (CR) or max 60 days.
    - **Consolidation:** 0.15 mg/kg/day, 5 days per week (4 weeks on, 4 weeks off) for 4 cycles [Australian].
- **Relapsed/Refractory APL:**
    - **Induction:** Daily infusion of 0.15 mg/kg until bone marrow remission (max 60 days).
    - **Consolidation:** 25 daily doses of 0.15 mg/kg over 5 weeks, commencing 3–4 weeks after induction [Australian].

### Dose Modification
- **Toxicity:** If Grade 3 or higher toxicity (NCI-CTC) occurs, interrupt therapy. Resume at 50% of the previous dose only after resolution. If no recurrence occurs within 3 days, escalate back to 100% [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to arsenic or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **APL Differentiation Syndrome:** Characterized by fever, dyspnoea, weight gain, and pulmonary infiltrates. Can be fatal. Treat with high-dose steroids (e.g., dexamethasone 10 mg IV every 12 hours) and consider temporary cessation of arsenic trioxide [Australian].
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** Can lead to Torsade de Pointes or complete AV block. 
    - **Monitoring:** Perform baseline 12-lead ECG; assess electrolytes (potassium, calcium, magnesium) and creatinine.
    - **Targets:** Maintain potassium > 4 mmol/L and magnesium > 0.8 mmol/L. 
    - **Management:** If QTc > 500 msec, corrective measures must be taken. Discontinue if syncope or arrhythmias occur [Australian].
- **Hyperleukocytosis:** Common during induction; usually managed with hydroxyurea if WBC counts exceed specific thresholds (e.g., > 10 x 10⁹/L) [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Monitor liver function tests (LFTs). Dose reduction or interruption is required for Grade 3/4 elevations [Australian].
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Monitor for symptoms; risk may increase with prolonged treatment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Fever, infection, hepatotoxicity, arrhythmia (including QTc prolongation), leukocytosis, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Fatigue, APL differentiation syndrome, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, headache, insomnia, peripheral neuropathy, hypokalaemia, hyperglycaemia, rash, pruritus, bone/joint pain |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Chest pain, pulmonary haemorrhage, pleural effusion, hypoxia, seizures, hypernatraemia, renal failure, CVA, pericardial effusion |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Immune suppression leading to herpes zoster |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Sudden death (sometimes early in treatment), cerebral haemorrhage, and Torsade de Pointes have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Avoid concomitant use with ziprasidone or pimozide. Use with caution alongside other antiarrhythmics or thioridazine [Australian].
- **Electrolyte-Depleting Drugs:** Caution with diuretics or amphotericin B, as they may exacerbate electrolyte imbalances and increase arrhythmia risk [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Based on *in vitro* data, arsenic trioxide does not inhibit major CYP450 enzymes [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X [Australian]**. Highly likely to cause permanent foetal damage. Must not be used in pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown. Breastfeeding is contraindicated during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Not systematically studied; however, animal data suggests potential for malformations and tumorigenic responses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited data available for patients aged 5–18. Safety and efficacy in children under 5 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data. Caution is required due to a higher frequency of decreased renal function in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied. Since renal excretion is the primary elimination route, "particular caution" is advised [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied. As the liver is the primary site of detoxification, caution is advised [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Arsenic trioxide induces apoptosis and partial differentiation of leukaemic cells. It also causes the degradation of the PML/RAR-alpha fusion protein [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributed; 95–97% bound to haemoglobin. Accumulates in sulphur-rich tissues (hair, nails, skin).
    - **Metabolism:** Reduction and methylation, primarily in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (1–8% of daily dose excreted daily). Mean plasma elimination half-life is approximately 92 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Relapsed/Refractory APL:** Multiple studies (e.g., Soignet et al.) demonstrate complete remission (CR) rates generally exceeding 85% [Australian].
- **APML4 Study (Newly Diagnosed):** Open-label Phase 2 trial using arsenic trioxide with ATRA and idarubicin. Reported a CR rate of 93.5% and 5-year overall survival of 94.3% [Australian].
- **Lo-Coco Trial (Newly Diagnosed):** Phase 3 study in low-to-intermediate risk APL. ATRA + arsenic trioxide was shown to be non-inferior (and superior in 2-year event-free survival) to ATRA + chemotherapy [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Concentrate:** Store below 30°C. Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Diluted Solution:** Contains no antimicrobial preservative. Use immediately. If necessary, store at 2°C–8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].
- **Handling:** Arsenic trioxide is a cytotoxic agent; standard safe handling and disposal procedures for cytostatics must be followed [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
