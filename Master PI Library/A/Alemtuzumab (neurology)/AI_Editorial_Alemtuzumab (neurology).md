# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory information into a clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for review. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) is "Not specified in available sources" unless otherwise noted.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule; amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Sterile solution for intravenous injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]; Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism leading to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Dialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours. Daily maintenance doses typically range around 50 mg/kg but may reach up to 300 mg/kg if clinically required [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** Administer as a 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are supranormal [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Not specified as an absolute contraindication in available sources, though serious reactions are warned against.
- **General:** None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without a strict risk-benefit assessment and close monitoring [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity of events has been observed [US].
- **Thromboembolic/INR Monitoring:** (See Drug Interactions).
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While primarily a concern with high-dose oral therapy, chronic administration in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect profiles differ significantly based on the underlying pathology (metabolic vs. ESRD).

### Very Common (>10%) [US-reported in ESRD trials]
- **General:** Pain (30%), Injection site reaction (33%), Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%).

### Common (1–10%) [US-reported in ESRD trials]
- **General:** Abdominal pain (9%), Fever (7%), Asthenia (9%), Infection (15%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia (6%), Palpitations (5%), Arrhythmia (2%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%), Peripheral edema (5%).
- **Nervous System:** Insomnia (4%), Paresthesia (5%), Depression (5%).
- **Other:** Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%), Pruritus (5%), Rash (3%), Taste perversion (3%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and Angioedema**
- **Seizures**
- **Severe hypotension** [US]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Narrative format used (corresponds to historical Category B). Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women; use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the maternal benefits of treatment. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have been established for the primary and secondary carnitine deficiency indications. Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources; clinical trials typically focus on ESRD populations regardless of age.
- **Renal Impairment:** This is a primary target population for the ESRD indication. However, caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) with chronic high-dose therapy [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis, IV administration of 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough concentrations, with levels remaining stable or increasing over 24 weeks of therapy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
- **Admixture Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) at room temperature [US].
