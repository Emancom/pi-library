# Editorial Product Information: Apixaban

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the clinical and regulatory information for **apixaban**, intended for use by healthcare professionals as a concise clinical reference.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Apixaban
- **Chemical Class:** Selective inhibitor of coagulation Factor Xa (FXa).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Apexa** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B01AF02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 2.5 mg (yellow, round) and 5 mg (pink, oval) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prevention of Venous Thromboembolic Events (VTE):** Indicated for adult patients following elective total hip or total knee replacement surgery [Australian].
- **Prevention of Stroke and Systemic Embolism:** Indicated for adult patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF) who possess at least one additional stroke risk factor [Australian].
- **Treatment of Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) and Pulmonary Embolism (PE):** Approved for use in adult patients [Australian].
- **Prevention of Recurrent DVT and PE:** Approved for use in adult patients [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **VTE Prevention (Elective Hip/Knee Surgery):** 2.5 mg twice daily. The first dose should be administered 12 to 24 hours post-operatively.
    - **Duration (Knee):** 10 to 14 days [Australian].
    - **Duration (Hip):** 32 to 38 days [Australian].
- **Stroke/Systemic Embolism Prevention (NVAF):** 5 mg twice daily.
    - **Dose Reduction (2.5 mg twice daily):** Indicated if the patient meets at least two of the following: age $\ge$80 years, body weight $\le$60 kg, or serum creatinine $\ge$133 $\mu$mol/L [Australian].
- **Treatment of DVT and PE:** 10 mg twice daily for the first 7 days, followed by 5 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Prevention of Recurrent DVT and PE:** 2.5 mg twice daily, initiated following at least 6 months of treatment for DVT or PE [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food.
- **Swallowing Difficulties:** Tablets may be crushed and suspended in water, 5% glucose in water (D5W), or apple juice for oral administration. Alternatively, crushed tablets may be delivered via a nasogastric tube in 60 mL of water or D5W. Crushed preparations are stable for up to 4 hours [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- The dose should be taken as soon as remembered on the same day. Resume twice-daily administration thereafter. Do not double the dose to compensate for a missed one [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to apixaban or any tablet excipients.
- Clinically significant active bleeding (e.g., gastrointestinal, intracranial).
- Hepatic disease associated with coagulopathy and clinically relevant bleeding risk, including severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C).
- Severe renal impairment with a creatinine clearance <25 mL/min [Australian].
- Spontaneous or pharmacological impairment of haemostasis.
- Lesions or conditions posing a significant risk of major bleeding (e.g., recent brain/spinal injury or surgery, malignant neoplasms, oesophageal varices, vascular aneurysms).
- Concomitant use of other anticoagulants (e.g., UFH, LMWH, warfarin, rivaroxaban), except during treatment switching or when UFH is used at doses to maintain a patent central venous or arterial catheter.
- Concomitant systemic treatment with strong inhibitors of both CYP3A4 and P-gp (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haemorrhage Risk:** Apixaban increases the risk of serious, potentially fatal bleeding. Use with extreme caution in patients with congenital or acquired bleeding disorders, thrombocytopenia, or severe uncontrolled hypertension.
- **Spinal/Epidural Haematoma:** When neuraxial anaesthesia or spinal/epidural puncture is used, patients are at risk of developing a haematoma that can cause permanent paralysis. Indwelling catheters must be removed at least 5 hours before the first apixaban dose. The next dose should not be given for at least 5 hours after catheter removal.
- **Premature Discontinuation:** Discontinuing any oral anticoagulant without adequate alternative anticoagulation increases the risk of thrombotic events, including stroke.
- **Renal Impairment:** Close monitoring of renal function is advised. [Australian] PI contraindicates use if CrCl is <25 mL/min.
- **Prosthetic Heart Valves:** Not recommended, as safety and efficacy have not been established in patients with prosthetic valves or significant mitral stenosis [Australian].
- **Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS):** Not recommended, particularly in "triple positive" patients, due to a potential increase in recurrent thrombotic events compared to Vitamin K antagonists.
- **Surgery/Invasive Procedures:** Discontinue at least 48 hours prior to elective procedures with moderate/high bleeding risk and at least 24 hours prior for low-risk procedures.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (1–10%)
- **Haematological:** Anaemia (including post-operative and haemorrhagic).
- **Vascular:** Haemorrhage (haematoma, vaginal, urethral), hypotension.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, gastrointestinal haemorrhage (including haematemesis, melaena).
- **Other:** Contusion, epistaxis, haematuria, procedural pain [Australian].

### Uncommon (0.1–1%)
- **Haematological:** Thrombocytopenia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Haematochezia, gingival bleeding, mouth haemorrhage.
- **Respiratory:** Haemoptysis.
- **Other:** Hypersensitivity, ocular haemorrhage, skin haemorrhage [Australian].

### Rare (<0.1%)
- **Serious:** Intracranial/intraspinal haemorrhage, intra-abdominal haemorrhage, retroperitoneal haemorrhage, pulmonary alveolar haemorrhage.
- **Other:** Muscle haemorrhage [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 and P-gp Inhibitors:** Concomitant use (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir) can double apixaban exposure and is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Moderate CYP3A4/P-gp Inhibitors:** (e.g., diltiazem, clarithromycin) increase exposure but do not require dose adjustment.
- **Strong CYP3A4 and P-gp Inducers:** (e.g., rifampin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St. John’s Wort) decrease apixaban exposure. Apixaban is not recommended for DVT/PE treatment when used with these agents and should be used with caution for other indications [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants and Antiplatelets:** Concomitant use with NSAIDs, ASA, or P2Y12 inhibitors significantly increases bleeding risk. Dual antiplatelet therapy combined with apixaban further increases this risk.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C** [Australian]. 
    - Limited data in humans. Animal studies show no direct harmful effects on embryo-fetal development, but placental haemorrhage is a known risk for this drug class. Apixaban is not recommended during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Apixaban is a substrate for BCRP and is excreted in animal milk (rat). A decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the medication [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on male or female fertility observed in animal studies at doses significantly higher than human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 18 years; safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher plasma concentrations observed (approx. 32% increase). No dose adjustment required unless the patient meets dose-reduction criteria for NVAF [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild/Moderate:** No dose adjustment necessary.
    - **Severe (CrCl 15–25 mL/min):** Contraindicated due to limited clinical experience [Australian].
    - **Dialysis (CrCl <15 mL/min):** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild/Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** No dose adjustment required; use with caution.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Apixaban is a reversible, direct, and highly selective inhibitor of Factor Xa. It inhibits free and clot-bound FXa and prothrombinase activity, thereby preventing thrombin generation and thrombus formation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability ~50% for doses up to 10 mg. $T_{max}$ is 3 to 4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is ~87%. $V_{ss}$ is ~21 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4/5; minor contributions from CYP1A2, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, and 2J2.
    - **Elimination:** Multiple routes. Renal excretion accounts for ~27% of total clearance. Total clearance is ~3.3 L/h; half-life is ~12 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ADVANCE-2 & 3:** Demonstrated superiority of apixaban 2.5 mg BID over enoxaparin 40 mg QD in preventing the composite of VTE/all-cause death following knee and hip replacement, respectively.
- **ARISTOTLE:** In NVAF patients, apixaban 5 mg BID was superior to warfarin in preventing stroke/systemic embolism, caused less major bleeding, and reduced all-cause mortality.
- **AVERROES:** In NVAF patients unsuitable for VKA, apixaban was superior to aspirin in preventing stroke/systemic embolism without a significant increase in major bleeding.
- **AMPLIFY:** Demonstrated non-inferiority of apixaban compared to enoxaparin/warfarin for the treatment of DVT/PE, with a significant reduction in major bleeding [Australian].
