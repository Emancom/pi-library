# Editorial Product Information: acrivastine + pseudoephedrine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **acrivastine + pseudoephedrine**
- Chemical class:
  - acrivastine: alkylamine H1-antihistamine (triprolidine analogue)
  - pseudoephedrine: indirect sympathomimetic amine (decongestant; releases norepinephrine)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Relief of symptoms associated with seasonal allergic rhinitis, including:
  - sneezing
  - rhinorrhoea
  - pruritus
  - lacrimation
  - nasal congestion
- Use should be limited to situations where both an H1-antihistamine effect (acrivastine) and a nasal decongestant effect (pseudoephedrine) are desired.
- Efficacy beyond 14 days of continuous treatment: not adequately investigated in clinical trials.
- Efficacy for relief of common cold symptoms: not adequately studied.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
- Typical clinical trial dose (adults):
  - acrivastine 8 mg + pseudoephedrine 60 mg per dose.
  - In clinical trials, this combination was administered three or four times daily (regimens studied included three or four daily doses); many efficacy trials used four daily doses for up to 14 days.
- Maximum daily dose: Not explicitly specified in available sources for the fixed combination. (Note: acrivastine alone has been studied at various dose ranges in pharmacokinetic trials, but no unified approved maximum daily dose for the combination is specified in available sources.)
- Duration of therapy: Clinical efficacy evaluated up to 14 days; continued use beyond 14 days has not been adequately investigated.
- Administration instructions:
  - Take only as prescribed.
  - Avoid concomitant use of additional over-the-counter antihistamines or sympathomimetic decongestants unless instructed by a clinician.
  - Patients should assess individual response for drowsiness/sedation before driving or operating machinery.
  - Avoid concurrent alcohol or other CNS depressants because of additive sedation.
- Titration schedule: Not specified in available sources.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to acrivastine, other alkylamine antihistamines (e.g., triprolidine), pseudoephedrine, other sympathomimetic amines (e.g., phenylpropanolamine), or to any components of the formulation.
- Severe hypertension.
- Severe coronary artery disease.
- Concomitant use of monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors, and for 14 days after stopping an MAO inhibitor (risk of hypertensive crisis).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None reported in the available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Sedation: Acrivastine produces sedation in some patients. Patients should evaluate individual tolerance prior to engaging in tasks that require mental alertness (e.g., driving, operating machinery).
- CNS depressants: Concurrent use with alcohol or other CNS depressants may increase sedation and impair performance; avoid co-use.
- Cardiovascular effects (pseudoephedrine): Use with caution in patients with hypertension, ischemic heart disease, or other cardiovascular disease; sympathomimetic overdosage may cause severe cardiovascular events including arrhythmias and cardiovascular collapse.
- Endocrine/metabolic: Use with caution in diabetes mellitus; sympathomimetics may affect glycaemic control.
- Ophthalmic: Use with caution in patients with increased intraocular pressure or narrow-angle glaucoma.
- Thyroid disease: Use with caution in patients with hyperthyroidism.
- Urinary/urogenic: Use with caution in patients with prostatic hypertrophy or bladder-neck obstruction (risk of urinary retention).
- Gastrointestinal: Use with caution in patients with stenosing peptic ulcer or pyloroduodenal obstruction.
- Elderly patients: Greater sensitivity to both antihistamines and sympathomimetics may occur; monitor closely and consider renal function when selecting dose. Elderly patients are more likely to experience dizziness, sedation, bladder outlet obstruction and hypotension.
- Renal impairment:
  - Acrivastine and pseudoephedrine are primarily renally excreted and accumulate in renal impairment.
  - Use of the fixed combination product in patients with creatinine clearance ≤ 48 mL/min is **not recommended**.
- Dialysis: Total body load removed by dialysis is limited (approx. 20% acrivastine, 27% acrivastine metabolite, 38% pseudoephedrine); supplemental dose after dialysis is not required.
- Pregnancy and lactation: Use in pregnancy or during lactation only if potential benefit justifies potential risk to fetus or infant (see Section 9).
- Paediatric use: Safety and effectiveness in children under 12 years of age have not been established.
- Monitoring:
  - Monitor blood pressure and heart rate in patients with cardiovascular risk.
  - Monitor renal function in patients at risk of impaired renal clearance (including elderly).
  - Monitor for signs of excessive sedation, anticholinergic effects, or sympathomimetic adverse effects.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors:
  - Concomitant use may precipitate hypertensive crisis; concomitant use is contraindicated and avoid for 14 days after stopping an MAO inhibitor.
- Other sympathomimetics:
  - Additive cardiovascular stimulation may occur with other sympathomimetic amines; coadministration should be avoided or used with caution.
- Beta-adrenergic agonists:
  - May increase effects of sympathomimetic amines.
- Antihypertensive drugs:
  - Sympathomimetic components may reduce effectiveness of antihypertensive agents that rely on sympathetic inhibition.
- CNS depressants and alcohol:
  - Concomitant use may enhance sedative effects of acrivastine; avoid coadministration.
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Acrivastine is ~50% bound to plasma proteins (mainly albumin) and binding is concentration-independent over the tested range; no displacement with phenytoin or theophylline was observed in studies.
- Pseudoephedrine is not significantly protein bound and is renally excreted; its elimination is highly dependent on urinary pH (shorter half-life with acidic urine, prolonged with alkaline urine).
- No formal interaction studies with many concomitant medications have been performed for the combination product; clinicians should consider potential interactions based on pharmacology of the components.
### Interaction management
- Avoid combining with MAO inhibitors; if recent MAOI use, wait 14 days after stopping MAOI before initiating the combination.
- Use caution when prescribing with other sympathomimetics or drugs that may increase sympathetic tone or affect blood pressure/heart rate.
- Monitor blood pressure and heart rate when used with antihypertensives or drugs affecting cardiac conduction.
- Avoid alcohol and CNS depressants due to additive sedation.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - Pregnancy category: **[US] Category B** (no evidence of teratogenicity in animal studies at high multiples of human dose; however, adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women are not available).
  - Animal data indicate no teratogenicity for acrivastine alone or in combination with pseudoephedrine at multiple times the human dose, but high doses in some perinatal studies were associated with maternal and neonatal mortality in rats.
  - Clinical recommendation: Use during pregnancy only if potential benefit justifies potential risk to the fetus.
- Breastfeeding:
  - Acrivastine: presence in human milk not specified / unknown.
  - Pseudoephedrine: excreted in human milk.
  - Recommendation: Use in nursing mothers only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the nursing infant.
- Fertility:
  - Reproductive/fertility studies in animals: no adverse effects on fertility reported for acrivastine alone at high multiples of human dose; combination rat studies did not show effects on fertility at tested doses.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Safety and effectiveness in children under 12 years have not been established.
- Elderly:
  - Pharmacokinetic studies: apparent volume of distribution for acrivastine is lower in elderly; pseudoephedrine elimination half-life modestly prolonged (approx. 18% in elderly). No appreciable differences in plasma concentrations were demonstrated that required dosage adjustment in healthy elderly subjects; however, elderly patients may have increased sensitivity and reduced renal function—monitor renal function and use caution.
- Renal impairment:
  - Both acrivastine and pseudoephedrine accumulate with renal impairment.
  - Pharmacokinetic changes by degree of renal impairment (single-dose studies reported):
    - Acrivastine elimination half-life increased ≈50% in mild renal impairment (CrCl 26–48 mL/min), ≈130% in moderate/severe impairment (CrCl 6–17 mL/min).
    - Pseudoephedrine elimination half-life increased ≈3-fold in mild, ≈7-fold in moderate, and ≈10-fold in severe renal impairment.
  - Oral clearance diminished commensurately with half-life increases.
  - Use of the fixed combination product in patients with creatinine clearance ≤48 mL/min is **not recommended**.
  - Dialysis removes only a minority of the total body load (see Section 6); supplemental dosing post-dialysis is not required.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Acrivastine: H1-receptor antagonist (alkylamine class) that reduces histamine-mediated symptoms of allergic rhinitis; may cause sedation in some patients.
- Pseudoephedrine: Indirect sympathomimetic amine that promotes release of norepinephrine from adrenergic nerve terminals to produce nasal decongestion via vasoconstriction of nasal mucosal blood vessels.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Both acrivastine and pseudoephedrine are rapidly absorbed after oral administration of the combination; the combination formulation is bioequivalent to solutions of the individual components.
  - Time to maximum plasma concentration (Tmax) for acrivastine: approximately 1.14 ± 0.23 hours.
- Distribution:
  - Acrivastine: apparent volume of distribution ~0.46 L/kg after single dose, increasing to ~0.82 L/kg at steady state; about 50% plasma protein binding (mainly albumin).
  - Pseudoephedrine: apparent volume of distribution ~3.0 L/kg; negligible protein binding.
- Metabolism and elimination:
  - Acrivastine:
    - Primarily renally excreted.
    - In mass balance studies, ~84% of administered radioactivity recovered in urine over 72 hours (67% as unchanged acrivastine, 11% as propionic acid metabolite, 6% as other metabolites); ~13% recovered in feces.
    - Terminal half-life: ~1.9 ± 0.3 hours (single dose); increased to ~3.5 ± 1.9 hours at steady state.
    - Linear kinetics across acrivastine doses studied.
  - Pseudoephedrine:
    - Primarily renally excreted; 55–75% of dose appears unchanged in urine.
    - Terminal half-life: ~6.2 ± 1.8 hours (varies substantially with urinary pH — shorter with acidic urine [~4 h at urine pH 5], prolonged with alkaline urine [~13 h at urine pH 8]).
    - Steady-state Cmax reported ~498 ± 129 ng/mL in combination dosing.
- Special populations:
  - Renal impairment prolongs half-lives and reduces clearance for both components (see Section 10).
  - Dialysis removes limited amounts (approx. 20% acrivastine, 27% acrivastine metabolite, 38% pseudoephedrine).

### Pharmacodynamics
- Acrivastine reduced histamine-induced wheal and flare responses in healthy volunteers; significant reduction vs placebo was seen within 30 minutes, with maximal effect at 1–2 hours and persistence up to 6 hours after a single 8 mg oral dose.
- The combination produces both antihistaminic and nasal decongestant effects, with onset of action typically within 1–2 hours of dosing.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Healthy volunteer pharmacodynamic studies:
  - Single-dose acrivastine 8 mg reduced histamine-induced wheal and flare versus placebo; onset within 30 minutes, maximum 1–2 hours, effect up to 6 hours.
- Pivotal and supportive clinical efficacy trials:
  - Five randomized, placebo- and/or active-controlled trials evaluated the fixed combination versus its individual components in seasonal allergic rhinitis.
    - Approximately 696 patients received four doses daily of acrivastine 8 mg + pseudoephedrine 60 mg (or equivalent concurrent individual components) for 14 days in these trials.
    - The combination reduced sneezing, rhinorrhoea, pruritus, and lacrimation more than pseudoephedrine alone and reduced nasal congestion more than acrivastine alone, demonstrating contribution of both components.
    - Onset of antihistaminic and decongestant effects occurred within 1–2 hours after the first dose.
    - Somnolence was more common with the combination (~12%) versus placebo (~6%).
  - Additional clinical trial experience:
    - Across controlled and uncontrolled studies summarized in available sources, thousands of patients were exposed to acrivastine alone or in combination for periods ranging from one day to up to one year; the majority were exposed for <90 days.
    - Typical clinical trial exposure to the combination included three or four daily doses of acrivastine 8 mg + pseudoephedrine 60 mg.
- Limitations:
  - Efficacy beyond 14 days has not been adequately investigated.
  - The combination has not been adequately studied for symptomatic relief of the common cold.
