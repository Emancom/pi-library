# Editorial Product Information: amlodipine + valsartan

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **amlodipine + valsartan**
- Chemical class:
  - Amlodipine: dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker
  - Valsartan: angiotensin II receptor antagonist (ARB)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of hypertension.
- Note: Treatment should not be initiated with the fixed-dose combination product; patients should be started on appropriate monotherapy or separate component tablets and only switched to the fixed-dose combination when clinically appropriate or when patients are adequately controlled on the same component doses given separately.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Available fixed component strengths: 5 mg amlodipine / 80 mg valsartan; 5 mg / 160 mg; 10 mg / 160 mg; 5 mg / 320 mg; 10 mg / 320 mg.
- Recommended dose: one tablet once daily (choose the strength that provides the required component doses).

### Dosing by indication
- Hypertension: use one tablet daily at the strength required to achieve blood pressure control (select from available fixed doses).

### Administration instructions
- Route: oral.
- Can be taken with or without food; however, keep administration consistent with respect to meals.
- It is recommended to take with some water.
- For convenience, patients adequately controlled on separate valsartan and amlodipine tablets may be switched to an equivalent fixed-dose combination tablet containing the same component doses.
- A patient whose blood pressure is not adequately controlled on amlodipine or valsartan monotherapy may be switched to combination therapy with appropriate fixed-dose strengths. Direct change from monotherapy to the fixed-dose combination may be considered when clinically appropriate.

### Titration schedules
- Treatment should not be initiated with the fixed-dose combination product; titration is performed using monotherapy or separate components prior to switching to an appropriate fixed-dose tablet.
- Specific stepwise titration schedules for the fixed-dose product are not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
Include all contraindications reported in available sources:
- Hypersensitivity to the active substances (amlodipine or valsartan), to other dihydropyridine derivatives, or to any excipients.
- Severe hepatic impairment, biliary cirrhosis, and cholestasis.
- Severe renal impairment (GFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2) and patients undergoing dialysis.
- Pregnancy — the product must not be used during pregnancy. **(see Section 9)** [Australian: Pregnancy Category D]
- Concomitant use with aliskiren in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus.
- Any other contraindications specific to components or excipients present in full regional PIs (not specified in available sources).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed or highlighted warnings
- **Pregnancy:** Use of agents acting on the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (including valsartan) during pregnancy can cause fetal morbidity and mortality. The product must not be used during pregnancy and should be discontinued as soon as pregnancy is detected. [Australian: Pregnancy Category D]

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypotension: symptomatic hypotension may occur, particularly in volume- or salt-depleted patients (e.g., patients receiving high-dose diuretics). Excessive hypotension was observed in clinical placebo-controlled studies (0.4% of patients with uncomplicated hypertension).
  - Correction of volume depletion prior to initiating therapy or close medical supervision at treatment start is recommended.
  - If symptomatic hypotension occurs, place the patient supine and administer intravenous normal saline if necessary; therapy may be resumed once blood pressure stabilizes.
- Renal artery stenosis: use with caution in patients with unilateral or bilateral renal artery stenosis; monitor renal function (serum creatinine, BUN).
- Heart failure / post-myocardial infarction: use caution in patients with severe heart failure or post-MI; initiation may cause hypotension and/or renal dysfunction. Monitor renal function.
- Concomitant RAS blockade: co-administration of ARBs with ACE inhibitors or aliskiren increases risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia, and renal dysfunction; dual blockade requires caution and monitoring.
- Aortic/mitral valve stenosis and hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy: exercise special caution (as with other vasodilators).
- Beta-blocker withdrawal: amlodipine is not a beta-blocker and does not protect against adverse effects of abrupt beta-blocker withdrawal; taper beta-blockers gradually if indicated.
- Angioedema: angioedema (including laryngeal/glottic involvement and airway obstruction) has been reported with valsartan; discontinue immediately and do not re-administer if angioedema occurs.
- Hepatic injury: clinically significant liver disease, including hepatitis, has been reported with ARBs; monitor liver function when clinically indicated.
- Primary hyperaldosteronism: limited efficacy expected due to mechanism; use not recommended.
- Kidney transplantation: no experience with use in recent kidney transplant recipients.
- Elderly: exercise caution when increasing dose; pharmacokinetics may be altered.
- Pediatric use: safety and efficacy in patients < 18 years have not been established; not recommended.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor blood pressure periodically to assess effectiveness.
- Renal function (serum creatinine, BUN) and electrolytes (potassium) should be monitored:
  - Prior to initiation and during therapy in patients at risk (e.g., elderly, renal impairment, heart failure, concomitant nephrotoxic agents, or concomitant RAS blockers).
  - Monitoring of creatinine and potassium is specifically advised for patients with moderate renal impairment.
- Monitor liver function tests in patients with hepatic impairment or when clinically indicated.
- Monitor for signs of angioedema and hypersensitivity.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Concomitant use with potassium supplements, potassium-sparing diuretics, or potassium-containing salt substitutes requires caution and frequent potassium monitoring due to risk of hyperkalaemia.
- Concomitant NSAIDs, particularly in elderly, volume-depleted, or renally impaired patients, may increase risk of renal dysfunction when combined with ARBs and thiazide diuretics; monitor renal function closely.
- Use with CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers may alter amlodipine exposure—exercise caution and monitor for increased or reduced calcium-channel blocker effects.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions (selected, as specified)
- Amlodipine-related:
  - Simvastatin: co-administration with multiple doses of amlodipine increases exposure to simvastatin; consider reducing simvastatin dose per its product information.
  - CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., diltiazem): diltiazem increased amlodipine plasma concentrations by ~50% in an elderly study; more potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir) may increase amlodipine exposure to a greater extent — exercise caution.
  - CYP3A4 inducers (carbamazepine, phenobarbital, phenytoin, rifampicin, St John’s Wort): may reduce amlodipine levels; clinical monitoring and possible dose adjustment are indicated.
  - Grapefruit juice: may increase bioavailability of calcium channel blockers; administration with grapefruit or grapefruit juice is not recommended.
  - Cyclosporin: in renal transplant patients coadministration did not alter cyclosporin pharmacokinetics in the study described.
  - Sildenafil: a single 100 mg sildenafil dose did not affect amlodipine pharmacokinetics; combined use produced additive blood pressure-lowering effects without pharmacokinetic interaction in the reported study.
  - Dantrolene (IV infusion): concomitant IV dantrolene and calcium channel blockers may increase hyperkalaemia risk; avoid in susceptible patients.
- Valsartan-related:
  - Hepatic transporters: co-administration with inhibitors of hepatic uptake transporter OATP1B1 (e.g., rifampicin, cyclosporin) or hepatic efflux transporter MRP2 (e.g., ritonavir) may increase systemic exposure to valsartan.
  - Valsartan is not extensively metabolized by CYP450; clinically relevant CYP-mediated interactions are not expected.
  - Protein binding: despite high plasma protein binding, in vitro studies did not show clinically relevant displacement interactions with diclofenac, frusemide, or warfarin.
  - No clinically significant interactions were found in monotherapy studies with: cimetidine, warfarin, frusemide, digoxin, atenolol, indomethacin, hydrochlorothiazide, amlodipine, or glibenclamide (as reported).

### Pharmacodynamic interactions (selected, as specified)
- Dual blockade of the renin-angiotensin system:
  - Concomitant use of ARBs (including valsartan) with ACE inhibitors or aliskiren increases risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia, and renal dysfunction. Concomitant use with aliskiren is contraindicated in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus.
  - Combination of an ARB or ACE inhibitor with a thiazide diuretic and an NSAID/COX-2 inhibitor increases the risk of renal impairment; monitor renal function.
- Lithium: reversible increases in serum lithium concentrations and lithium toxicity have been reported with concomitant administration of lithium and ACE inhibitors or ARBs. Monitor serum lithium closely and adjust lithium dose as necessary.
- Potassium-elevating agents: concomitant use with potassium supplements, potassium-sparing diuretics, or potassium-containing salt substitutes increases risk of hyperkalaemia; use caution and monitor potassium.
- Other antihypertensive agents: additive blood pressure-lowering effects can occur when combined with other antihypertensives (alpha-blockers, diuretics, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors); monitor blood pressure.

### Clinically significant interactions with management notes
- Simvastatin: consider reducing simvastatin dose when co-administered with amlodipine.
- CYP3A4 inhibitors/inducers: exercise caution; monitor for increased or reduced effects of amlodipine; adjust dose if required.
- Lithium: monitor serum lithium concentrations frequently when co-administered; consider avoiding the combination when possible.
- NSAIDs + ARB ± thiazide: monitor renal function; consider avoiding the combination in high-risk patients.
- Potassium supplements/potassium-sparing diuretics: avoid or monitor potassium frequently.
- Hepatic transporter inhibitors: be aware potential for increased valsartan exposure; monitor for adverse effects.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Contraindicated in pregnancy.** Agents that act on the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system can cause fetal and neonatal morbidity and death when administered to pregnant women.
- Documented fetal/neonatal risks associated with in utero exposure to ARBs (including valsartan) include hypotension, neonatal skull hypoplasia, anuria, renal failure (reversible or irreversible), oligohydramnios, and death. Cases of spontaneous abortion have been reported.
- If pregnancy is detected, discontinue the product as soon as possible.
- Calcium channel blockers (amlodipine) may cause fetal hypoxia secondary to maternal hypotension; use only if potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
- [Australian: Pregnancy Category D]

### Breastfeeding
- It is not known whether valsartan and/or amlodipine are excreted in human milk.
- Valsartan was excreted in the milk of lactating rats; animal data showed reduced postnatal growth and survival at high doses.
- Recommendation from available sources: breastfeeding is not advisable for women taking the combination product.

### Fertility
- Valsartan: fertility of male and female rats was not affected at studied doses.
- Amlodipine: reversible biochemical changes in human sperm have been reported; animal data indicate potential effects on male fertility in some studies.
- Combination product: no specific fertility studies; testes, ovaries and secondary sex organs were not affected in short-term animal toxicity studies described.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Not recommended for patients < 18 years due to lack of established safety and efficacy.

### Elderly
- Caution is advised when increasing the dose in elderly patients; pharmacokinetics may be altered. Monitor blood pressure, renal function, and potential adverse effects.

### Renal impairment
- Mild to moderate renal impairment: no dosage adjustment required; monitoring of creatinine and potassium is advised for patients with moderate renal impairment.
- Severe renal impairment (GFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2) and patients undergoing dialysis: contraindicated.

### Hepatic impairment
- Valsartan is mainly eliminated unchanged via the bile; amlodipine is extensively metabolized by the liver.
- Mild to moderate hepatic impairment (without cholestasis): use with caution; monitor liver function tests. The daily dose should not exceed 5 mg amlodipine / 80 mg valsartan in mild to moderate hepatic impairment without cholestasis.
- Severe hepatic impairment, biliary cirrhosis, or cholestasis: contraindicated.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Amlodipine**: dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker that inhibits transmembrane influx of calcium ions into vascular smooth muscle and cardiac muscle, producing peripheral arterial vasodilation and lowering blood pressure.
- **Valsartan**: selective angiotensin II receptor (AT1) antagonist that blocks the vasoconstrictor and aldosterone-secreting effects of angiotensin II, leading to vasodilation and reduced aldosterone-mediated sodium and water retention.

### Pharmacokinetics (selected points from available sources)
- Absorption: Not specified in available sources for the combination product.
- Distribution:
  - Valsartan: highly bound to plasma proteins (specific percentage not specified in available sources).
  - Amlodipine: distribution characteristics not fully specified in available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Amlodipine: extensively metabolized in the liver (CYP3A4 implicated).
  - Valsartan: not metabolised to a significant extent (eliminated predominantly unchanged).
- Elimination:
  - Valsartan: mostly eliminated unchanged via the bile.
  - Amlodipine: elimination via hepatic metabolism; specifics not provided.
- Special pathways:
  - Hepatic transporters: inhibitors of OATP1B1 or MRP2 may increase valsartan systemic exposure.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Combined therapy produces additive antihypertensive effects through complementary mechanisms: arterial vasodilation (amlodipine) and blockade of angiotensin II-mediated vasoconstriction and aldosterone effects (valsartan).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Excessive hypotension was observed in 0.4% of patients with uncomplicated hypertension treated with the combination in placebo-controlled studies.
- Small short-term study: valsartan administration to twelve patients with renovascular hypertension secondary to unilateral renal artery stenosis did not produce significant changes in renal haemodynamics, serum creatinine, or BUN in that small study; however, other RAS-acting agents may increase blood urea and serum creatinine in bilateral renal artery stenosis—monitoring is recommended.
- In clinical trials involving patients with heart failure or post-myocardial infarction, valsartan commonly produced reductions in blood pressure; initiation may cause symptomatic hypotension in patients with complicated post-MI courses. An increase in mortality was observed among some patients receiving combinations of valsartan, ACE inhibitors and beta blockers in clinical trials; concurrent administration of all three is not recommended.
- Reproductive and developmental animal studies: reported foetotoxicity and postnatal effects at high doses in animals when valsartan and/or amlodipine were given during gestation or lactation (details and dose levels provided in regional toxicology summaries). No teratogenic effects were observed in some species at specified doses in available sources, but foetal/neonatal toxicity and decreased postnatal survival have been reported in animal studies at high exposure levels.
