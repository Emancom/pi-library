# Editorial Product Information: Alclometasone Dipropionate

## Editorial Note
*   This editorial synthesis incorporates information from the following jurisdiction: United States (Food and Drug Administration).
*   The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
*   This document is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Alclometasone dipropionate (synthetic corticosteroid).
*   **Chemical Class:** Group of synthetic steroids used topically for anti-inflammatory and antipruritic effects.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Alclometasone Dipropionate Ointment USP [US].
*   **ATC Code:** D07AB10 (Alclometasone).
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Topical ointment, 0.05% (0.5 mg alclometasone dipropionate per gram) [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical (Dermatologic use only).
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharmaceutical Industries, Inc. (Distributor) [US]; Sun Pharma Canada Inc. (Manufacturer) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Corticosteroid-responsive dermatoses:** Indicated for the relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations [US-approved].
*   **Pediatric dermatoses:** Indicated for use in patients aged 1 year or older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   Apply a thin film of the 0.05% ointment to the affected areas two or three times daily.
*   Massage the medication gently until it disappears.

### Administration Instructions
*   For external use only; avoid contact with eyes.
*   Treated areas should not be bandaged or wrapped in an occlusive manner unless specifically directed by a clinician.
*   **Diaper Area:** Should not be applied to the diaper area if the patient requires diapers or plastic pants, as these function as occlusive dressings [US].

### Duration of Therapy
*   Therapy should be discontinued once clinical control is achieved. 
*   If no improvement is observed within 2 weeks, the diagnosis should be reassessed.

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to alclometasone dipropionate or any other component of the preparation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
*   **HPA Axis Suppression:** Systemic absorption of topical corticosteroids can produce reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, potentially leading to glucocorticosteroid insufficiency. Risk factors include application to large surface areas, prolonged use, and the use of occlusive dressings. 
*   **Systemic Effects:** Manifestations of Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycemia, and glucosuria may occur due to systemic absorption. 
*   **Local Irritation:** If irritation develops, treatment should be discontinued and appropriate therapy initiated.
*   **Allergic Contact Dermatitis:** Typically diagnosed by a failure to heal rather than clinical exacerbation; diagnosis should be confirmed with patch testing.
*   **Concomitant Infections:** If a skin infection is present or develops, an appropriate antifungal or antibacterial agent must be used. If the infection does not respond promptly, the corticosteroid should be discontinued until the infection is controlled.
*   **Ophthalmic Use:** This product is not for ophthalmic use; avoid contact with eyes.

### Monitoring Requirements
*   Patients applying the steroid to large surface areas or under occlusion should be evaluated periodically for HPA axis suppression using the ACTH stimulation test, A.M. plasma cortisol test, or urinary free cortisol test.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (1–10%)
*   Itching, burning, and erythema (reported in approximately 1% of patients) [US].

### Infrequent/Rare (<1%)
The following local adverse reactions are reported infrequently but may increase with occlusive dressings (listed in decreasing order of occurrence):
*   Folliculitis
*   Acneiform eruptions
*   Hypopigmentation
*   Perioral dermatitis
*   Allergic contact dermatitis
*   Secondary infection
*   Skin atrophy
*   Striae
*   Miliaria

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Corticosteroids are teratogenic in animals following systemic and dermal application. There are no adequate or well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if topical administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable quantities in human milk. Caution should be exercised when administered to a nursing woman. 
*   **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies to evaluate the effect of alclometasone dipropionate on fertility have not been performed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients 1 year of age and older. Safety and efficacy for longer than 3 weeks have not been established. Use in patients under 1 year is not recommended. Children are at higher risk for HPA axis suppression and Cushing's syndrome due to a higher skin surface area to body mass ratio. Manifestations include linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, and intracranial hypertension (e.g., bulging fontanelles, headaches).
*   **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials included a limited number of patients aged 65 and over; no significant differences in safety or efficacy were observed compared to younger patients. No dosage adjustment is warranted [US].
*   **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Alclometasone dipropionate is a corticosteroid that induces phospholipase A2 inhibitory proteins (lipocortins). These proteins control the biosynthesis of inflammatory mediators (prostaglandins, leukotrienes) by inhibiting the release of their precursor, arachidonic acid.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    *   **Absorption:** Approximately 3% of the steroid is absorbed during 8 hours of contact with intact skin in healthy volunteers [US]. Absorption increases with inflammation, skin disease, or occlusion.
    *   **Distribution/Metabolism/Elimination:** Once absorbed systemically, topical corticosteroids are handled through pharmacokinetic pathways similar to systemically administered corticosteroids (metabolised primarily in the liver and excreted by the kidneys and in bile).
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. It is classified as being in the low to medium range of potency [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **HPA Axis Studies:** In healthy subjects, application to 30% of the body twice daily for 7 days (with occlusion) or to 80% of the body for 21 days (with occlusion) resulted in a approximately 10% decrease in plasma and urinary free cortisol levels, suggesting HPA axis suppression under these conditions.
*   **Carcinogenicity:** A 26-week dermal study in transgenic (Tg.AC) mice showed an increased incidence of benign skin neoplasms at the treatment site; however, this effect may be associated with the vehicle. The clinical relevance to humans is unclear.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Storage Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Keep tubes tightly closed. Professional samples may be supplied in 5 g tubes; commercial sizes include 15 g, 45 g, and 60 g tubes.
