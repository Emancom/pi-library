# Editorial Product Information: Aminocaproic Acid

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a structured format for quick reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, full Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Aminocaproic acid (6-aminohexanoic acid).
- **Chemical Class:** Fibrinolysis inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** Aminocaproic Acid Tablets USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 500 mg and 1000 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Distributed by American Health Packaging; manufactured by Vitruvias Therapeutics [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Aminocaproic acid** is indicated for the enhancement of hemostasis in clinical scenarios where fibrinolysis contributes significantly to bleeding [US]. 

Specific approved applications include:
- **Surgical complications:** Bleeding following cardiac surgery (with or without bypass) and portacaval shunt procedures [US].
- **Hematological disorders:** Bleeding associated with amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia (typically accompanying aplastic anemia) [US].
- **Obstetric emergencies:** Acute and life-threatening abruptio placentae [US].
- **Hepatic disease:** Bleeding associated with hepatic cirrhosis [US].
- **Neoplastic disease:** Carcinoma of the prostate, lung, stomach, and cervix [US].
- **Urinary fibrinolysis:** Excessive bleeding of the urinary tract, including surgical hematuria (post-prostatectomy or nephrectomy) and non-surgical hematuria (associated with polycystic or neoplastic diseases of the genitourinary system) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Acute Bleeding Syndromes:** A loading dose of 5 g (either five 1000 mg tablets or ten 500 mg tablets) is administered during the first hour of treatment [US].
- **Maintenance Dose:** Following the loading dose, a continuous rate of 1 g per hour (one 1000 mg tablet or two 500 mg tablets) is recommended [US].
- **Duration:** Treatment typically continues for approximately 8 hours or until the bleeding is successfully controlled [US].

### Administration Instructions
- The oral tablets are scored and may be administered as part of the loading and maintenance regimen described above [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Active Intravascular Clotting:** Must not be used when there is evidence of an active intravascular clotting process [US].
- **Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC):** Aminocaproic acid is contraindicated in the presence of DIC unless administered concurrently with heparin [US].
- **Diagnostic Requirement:** Before administration, clinicians must differentiate primary fibrinolysis from DIC using appropriate laboratory tests (e.g., platelet count, protamine paracoagulation test, or euglobulin clot lysis test) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Intrarenal Obstruction:** In patients with upper urinary tract bleeding, administration may cause glomerular capillary thrombosis or clots in the renal pelvis and ureters. Usage in hematuria of upper urinary tract origin should be avoided unless benefits outweigh these risks [US].
- **Myopathy and Rhabdomyolysis:** Rare reports of skeletal muscle weakness with fiber necrosis have occurred following prolonged administration. Symptoms range from mild myalgia to severe proximal myopathy and rhabdomyolysis with acute renal failure.
    - **Monitoring:** Creatine phosphokinase (CPK) levels should be monitored in patients receiving long-term therapy [US].
    - **Management:** Discontinue the drug if CPK levels rise. Note that the syndrome may recur upon rechallenge [US].
- **Cardiac Damage:** Animal studies have shown subendocardial hemorrhages and fatty degeneration of the myocardium. One human case of cardiac and hepatic necrotic lesions was reported following a cumulative dose of 26 g [US].
- **Neurological Deficits:** Use in subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) has been associated with hydrocephalus, cerebral ischemia, or cerebral vasospasm, though a causal drug relationship is not definitively established [US].
- **Hyperfibrinolysis Requirement:** The drug should only be administered when a definite diagnosis of hyperfibrinolysis (hyperplasminemia) is established [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Aminocaproic acid is generally well-tolerated. Reported adverse events include:
- **General:** Edema, headache, malaise.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Allergic reactions, anaphylactoid reactions, anaphylaxis.
- **Cardiovascular:** Bradycardia, hypotension, peripheral ischemia, thrombosis.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting.
- **Hematologic:** Agranulocytosis, coagulation disorder, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Increased CPK, muscle weakness, myalgia, myopathy, myositis, rhabdomyolysis.
- **Neurologic:** Confusion, convulsions, delirium, dizziness, hallucinations, intracranial hypertension, stroke, syncope.
- **Respiratory:** Dyspnea, nasal congestion, pulmonary embolism.
- **Skin:** Pruritus, rash.
- **Special Senses:** Tinnitus, vision decreased, watery eyes.
- **Urogenital:** Increased BUN, renal failure. Dry ejaculation has been reported in hemophilia patients post-dental surgery, resolving within 24–48 hours of treatment completion [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Clotting Factors:** Concurrent administration with Factor IX Complex concentrates or Anti-Inhibitor Coagulant concentrates is not recommended due to an increased risk of thrombosis [US].
- **Laboratory Test Interactions:** Prolongation of template bleeding time may occur with high-dose intravenous infusion (>24 g/day). In vitro, high concentrations of the drug inhibit platelet aggregation induced by ADP and collagen [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C]. Animal reproduction studies have not been performed. It is unknown if the drug causes fetal harm or affects reproductive capacity. Administer to pregnant women only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing mothers [US].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies (rats), dietary administration of maximum human therapeutic doses impaired fertility (decreased implantations and litter sizes) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in provided sources, though renal considerations apply.
- **Renal Impairment:** Total body clearance is markedly decreased in patients with severe renal failure. Higher plasma concentrations may occur; caution is advised [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Aminocaproic acid inhibits fibrinolysis principally by inhibiting plasminogen activators and, to a lesser extent, via antiplasmin activity [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Oral absorption follows a zero-order process at a rate of 5.2 g/hr. Absorption is complete (F=1) after a 5 g dose, with peak plasma concentrations (164 ± 28 mcg/mL) reached in approximately 1.2 hours [US].
- **Distribution:** Distributes throughout intravascular and extravascular compartments; penetrates red blood cells and other tissue cells. Apparent volume of distribution (oral) is 23.1 ± 6.6 L [US].
- **Metabolism:** Approximately 11% of the dose is converted to the metabolite adipic acid [US].
- **Elimination:** Primarily renal. 65% of the dose is recovered unchanged in urine. Renal clearance is 116 mL/min (approximating creatinine clearance), and total body clearance is 169 mL/min. The terminal elimination half-life is approximately 2 hours [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
Fibrinolysis inhibition is maintained at plasma levels of 0.13 mg/mL. A regimen of a 5 g bolus followed by 1–1.25 g/hr maintenance sustains these therapeutic levels in patients with normal renal function [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** Unit dose blisters. Do not use if the blister is torn or broken [US].
