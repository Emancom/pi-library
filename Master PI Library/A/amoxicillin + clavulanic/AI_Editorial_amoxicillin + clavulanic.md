# Editorial Product Information: amoxicillin + clavulanic

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **amoxicillin + clavulanic**
- Chemical class: Aminopenicillin (amoxicillin) combined with a beta‑lactamase inhibitor (clavulanic acid)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
Indications are for short‑term treatment of bacterial infections caused by susceptible organisms. Where specific sites are listed in the available source, they include:
- Urinary tract infections (uncomplicated and complicated)
- Lower respiratory tract infections, including community‑acquired pneumonia and acute exacerbations of chronic bronchitis
- Upper respiratory tract infections, such as sinusitis, otitis media and recurrent tonsillitis
- Skin and skin‑structure infections

Appropriate culture and susceptibility testing is recommended; empirical therapy may be started when clinical circumstances suggest probable β‑lactamase–producing organisms, but therapy should be adjusted when results are available.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
General principles:
- Take immediately before or with the first mouthful of food to reduce gastrointestinal intolerance and optimize absorption.
- Continue treatment for 48–72 hours beyond clinical resolution or until bacteriological eradication has been documented. Do not exceed 14 days without review.

Adult dosing (as provided in available source):
- Typical adult dose for mild–moderate infections: one tablet containing 500 mg amoxicillin with 125 mg clavulanic acid every 12 hours.
- For more severe infections: one tablet containing 875 mg amoxicillin with 125 mg clavulanic acid every 12 hours.
- Important: tablets with different amoxicillin:clavulanate ratios are not interchangeable based on amoxicillin content alone; clavulanic acid content may be identical while amoxicillin differs—do not substitute two lower‑strength tablets for one higher‑strength tablet unless equivalence is confirmed.

Pediatrics:
- Children weighing ≥ 40 kg: dose as for adults.
- Children weighing < 40 kg: use oral suspension formulations (specific dosing for suspensions not specified in available sources).

Renal impairment:
- Both components are renally excreted; serum half‑lives increase with renal impairment.
- For formulations containing 875/125 mg: should not be used in patients with moderate to severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance ≤ 30 mL/min) [Australian].
- For the 500/125 mg formulation (recommended adjustments provided in source):
  - Mild impairment (CrCl > 30 mL/min): no change.
  - Moderate impairment (CrCl 10–30 mL/min): one 500/125 tablet every 12 hours.
  - Severe impairment (CrCl < 10 mL/min): one 500/125 tablet every 24 hours.
- Hemodialysis: dialysis reduces serum concentrations of both agents; administer an additional dose at end of dialysis.

Hepatic impairment:
- Data are insufficient for specific dose recommendations. Use with caution and monitor hepatic function at regular intervals.

Titration and missed dose:
- Titration schedules: Not specified in available sources.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to penicillins or cephalosporins (history of allergic reaction to β‑lactams).
- Previous history of amoxicillin/clavulanic acid–associated jaundice or hepatic dysfunction.
- Any other contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
Boxed / critical warnings:
- **Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity (anaphylactoid) reactions have been reported with penicillins. Discontinue immediately if an allergic reaction occurs and institute appropriate emergency treatment (including adrenaline for anaphylaxis).** [Australian]
- **Avoid use when infectious mononucleosis is suspected** because of frequent occurrence of a morbilliform rash with aminopenicillins.

General warnings and precautions:
- Take a careful allergy history for penicillins, cephalosporins, and other allergens before initiating therapy.
- Hypersensitivity reactions may progress to Kounis syndrome (allergic coronary syndrome) presenting with chest pain during an allergic reaction.
- Drug‑induced enterocolitis syndrome (DIES): reported mainly in children receiving amoxicillin/clavulanic acid — characterised by protracted vomiting 1–4 hours after administration, possibly with abdominal pain, diarrhoea, hypotension or leucocytosis; severe cases including shock have been reported.
- Clostridioides difficile–associated diarrhoea/pseudomembranous colitis has been reported with many antibiotics including amoxicillin; consider this in any patient who develops diarrhoea during or after antibiotic therapy.
- Monitor organ system function (renal, hepatic, haematopoietic) during prolonged therapy.
- Patients with lymphatic leukaemia are especially susceptible to amoxicillin‑induced skin rashes — use with caution.
- Superinfections, including fungal overgrowth, may occur with prolonged use.
- Rare reports of cholestatic hepatitis (sometimes severe but usually reversible), most commonly in males and elderly and sometimes associated with prolonged therapy; signs may appear several weeks after cessation.
- Abnormal prolongation of prothrombin time/INR has been reported rarely in patients taking oral anticoagulants; monitor coagulation parameters when co‑administered and adjust anticoagulant dose as needed.
- Crystalluria (including acute renal injury) has been observed rarely (predominantly with parenteral high doses); maintain adequate hydration and urinary output with high doses.
- Effects on laboratory tests: high urine concentrations of amoxicillin can produce false‑positive reducing sugar tests (e.g., Clinitest/Benedict’s/Fehling’s); use glucose oxidase–based urine tests instead.
- Effects on oestrogen levels: transient decreases in certain conjugated oestrogens have been observed following ampicillin; similar effects may occur with amoxicillin.

Monitoring requirements:
- Renal function: baseline and during prolonged therapy or in renal impairment; adjust dosing interval per renal function.
- Hepatic function: monitor regularly in patients with hepatic dysfunction or when prolonged therapy is required.
- Haematologic parameters: monitor with prolonged therapy (risk of reversible leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia).
- Coagulation (INR/prothrombin time) when co‑administered with vitamin K antagonists.

Specific clinical precautions:
- Avoid use in suspected infectious mononucleosis.
- Use caution in patients with a history of multiple allergies or prior severe hypersensitivity reactions.
- Avoid drugs that delay peristalsis in cases of antibiotic‑associated colitis (e.g., opiates, diphenoxylate with atropine).

## 8. Drug Interactions
Pharmacokinetic interactions:
- Probenecid: decreases renal tubular secretion of amoxicillin (but not clavulanic acid), resulting in increased and prolonged amoxicillin plasma concentrations.
- Penicillins may reduce excretion of methotrexate, potentially increasing methotrexate toxicity.

Pharmacodynamic and other interactions:
- Allopurinol: co‑administration with ampicillin has been associated with a substantially increased incidence of rash; no specific data available for amoxicillin/clavulanic acid, but caution is advised.
- Oral anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin, acenocoumarol): rare increases in INR reported with amoxicillin; monitor prothrombin time/INR closely when starting or stopping amoxicillin/clavulanic acid and adjust anticoagulant dose as necessary.
- Combined oral contraceptives: as with other antibiotics that alter gut flora, reduced enterohepatic recirculation of oestrogens may reduce contraceptive efficacy; advise patients accordingly.
- Alcohol: no direct data available for amoxicillin/clavulanic acid; ingestion of alcohol during treatment with some other beta‑lactams has produced disulfiram‑like reactions; avoid alcohol during and for several days after therapy as a precaution.

Management notes:
- Monitor drug levels or effects where clinically indicated (e.g., INR for anticoagulants; methotrexate toxicity signs; renal function for agents cleared renally).
- When co‑administering with probenecid or in renal impairment, be aware of prolonged amoxicillin exposure.

Jurisdictional differences:
- No jurisdiction‑specific interaction differences were provided in the available source. If region‑specific interaction guidance is required, consult the regional PI.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
Pregnancy:
- Pregnancy category reported as B1 [Australian]: animal studies with oral and parenteral administration have not shown teratogenicity; limited human data available.
- In preterm premature rupture of membranes (pPROM): prophylactic amoxicillin/clavulanic acid may be associated with an increased risk of necrotising enterocolitis in neonates; use caution.
- Use in pregnancy should be avoided unless considered essential by the physician, particularly in the first trimester.
- Effects in labour/delivery: animal data (guinea pig) suggest IV ampicillin may reduce uterine tone and contractions; clinical significance in humans is unknown.

Breastfeeding:
- Amoxicillin is excreted into human milk; data on clavulanic acid excretion into human milk are not available. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women.

Fertility:
- Animal data: amoxicillin/clavulanic acid up to 1200 mg/kg/day (2:1 ratio formulation) produced no effects on fertility or reproductive performance in rats.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
Paediatric use:
- Children ≥ 40 kg: dose as adults.
- Children < 40 kg: use oral suspension formulations (specific suspension dosing not specified in available sources).
- DIES has been reported mainly in children receiving amoxicillin/clavulanic acid.

Elderly:
- No specific data were provided for elderly dosing; exercise caution because hepatic events have been reported predominantly in older patients. Monitor renal and hepatic function and adjust dosing interval in renal impairment.

Renal impairment:
- See section 4 for dosing adjustments.
- 875/125 mg formulation: not to be used in patients with CrCl ≤ 30 mL/min [Australian].
- 500/125 mg formulation: adjust dosing interval per degree of renal impairment (see section 4).

Hepatic impairment:
- Data insufficient to provide dosage recommendations. Use with caution and monitor liver function tests at regular intervals.

## 11. Pharmacology
Mechanism of action:
- **Amoxicillin**: bactericidal agent that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis during active multiplication.
- **Clavulanic acid**: a β‑lactamase inhibitor that extends amoxicillin’s activity to β‑lactamase–producing organisms by inhibiting β‑lactamase enzymes.

Pharmacokinetics (information available in sources):
- Excretion: Both amoxicillin and clavulanic acid are primarily excreted by the kidneys. Serum half‑lives of both increase in renal impairment.
- Hemodialysis reduces circulating concentrations of both agents; an additional dose is recommended after dialysis.
- Urinary concentrations of amoxicillin are high following oral administration.
- Other pharmacokinetic parameters (absorption rates, bioavailability, volume of distribution, plasma protein binding, metabolic pathways, precise half‑life values) are Not specified in available sources.

Pharmacodynamics:
- In vitro susceptibility data cited in the source indicate activity against many strains of common respiratory and urinary pathogens, and clavulanic acid restores activity against β‑lactamase producers. Specific MIC distributions and resistance percentages were reported in the source for certain organisms (e.g., Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae) but full data tables are not reproduced here in detail.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Clinical trial safety data presented in the available source include two cohorts:
  - 875/125 mg twice‑daily regimen: total patients N = 584. Most frequently reported adverse events (related or possibly related) included diarrhoea (14.9%), nausea (7.9%), headache (6.8%), abdominal pain (4.5%), vomiting (3.8%), genital moniliasis (3.6%), and vaginitis (3.4%).
  - 500/125 mg twice‑daily regimen: total patients N = 462. Most frequently reported adverse events included diarrhoea (12.8%), nausea (5.2%), headache (4.8%), upper respiratory infection (1.9%), and genital moniliasis (1.9%).
- Trial design details (randomisation, blinding, primary efficacy endpoints, comparator arms, microbiological eradication rates, clinical cure rates, statistical outcomes) are Not specified in available sources.
