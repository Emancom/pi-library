# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine (Intravenous)

## Editorial Note
- The information contained in this editorial was synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information when prescribing or administering this medication in a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Daily doses generally remain at 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustments:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels, starting as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended. Free carnitine levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
- **ESRD:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough plasma concentrations below the normal range of 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in patients with ESRD on dialysis.
- **Management:** If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical care.

### General Precautions
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. In ESRD patients, chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) which are usually renally excreted.
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (ESRD Clinical Trials)
Based on double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in patients on chronic hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5%:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), chest pain (12%), headache (22%), infection (15%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Other:** Dizziness (13%), asthenia (9%).

### Serious and Less Common Effects
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, body odor.
- **Neurological:** Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Classified as safe for use only if clearly needed. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women.
- **Breastfeeding:** The effect of supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight as described in Section 3.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 5. Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral formulations are used in patients with severe renal compromise. Levocarnitine is easily removed by hemodialysis.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, dose adjustments in ESRD are based on plasma trough concentrations.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are then excreted.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces).
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability for oral formulations is low (approx. 15–16%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis 3x weekly), mean plasma trough levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in ESRD patients showed dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcome Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the specific effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness) has not been definitively determined.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL.
