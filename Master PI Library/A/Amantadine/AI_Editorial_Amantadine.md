# Editorial Product Information: Amantadine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals to provide a single, coherent clinical reference.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Amantadine hydrochloride (a tricyclic amino-derivative).
- **Brand Names:** **AMANTAMED** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N04BB01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 100 mg hard gelatin capsules [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Medsurge Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Parkinson’s Disease:** Treatment of idiopathic Parkinson’s disease, post-encephalitic parkinsonism, symptomatic parkinsonism (e.g., following carbon monoxide poisoning injury), and arteriosclerotic parkinsonism [Australian].
- **Drug-Induced Extrapyramidal Reactions:** Treatment of extrapyramidal symptoms in adults [Australian].
- **Influenza Type A:** Prophylaxis of respiratory tract illness caused by Influenza Type A, particularly for individuals at high risk for whom infection may have serious consequences [Australian].
- **Note:** Amantadine is **not** indicated for the treatment of tardive dyskinesia [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Parkinson’s Disease
- **Adults (to 65 years):** 100 mg daily for the first week, increased in the second week to 100 mg twice daily. Doses exceeding 200 mg/day may offer additional relief but are associated with increased toxicity. Dose escalations should occur at intervals of at least one week [Australian].
- **Adults (>65 years):** 100 mg once daily is the recommended dose [Australian].
- **Combined Treatment:** When used with other anti-parkinsonian agents, the existing therapy should be continued while starting **amantadine**. In patients on high-dose anticholinergics or L-dopa, the initial low-dose period should be extended to two weeks [Australian].

### Drug-induced Extrapyramidal Reactions
- **Adults:** The primary intervention is reducing the dose of the causative agent. If not practical, the usual dose of **amantadine** is 100 mg twice daily, up to 100 mg three times daily if required for optimal response [Australian].

### Type A Virus Influenza
- **Prophylaxis (Children 5–9 years):** 100 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis (Age 10–65 years):** 100 mg twice daily. A reduced dose of 100 mg daily may be used if intolerance to the higher dose occurs [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis (>65 years):** 100 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Timing:** Prophylaxis should start immediately after exposure and continue for at least 10 days [Australian].

### Administration and Titration
- **Missed Doses:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Discontinuation:** Therapy must be tapered gradually. Abrupt withdrawal can exacerbate parkinsonian symptoms or trigger a neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS)-like condition or catatonia [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Pregnancy [Australian].
- Lactation [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to **amantadine** or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Narrow Therapeutic Index:** The margin between the effective and toxic dose is small (only 100–200 mg/day). The maximum recommended daily dose should not be exceeded [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Disorders:** Use with caution in patients with a history of confusional, hallucinatory, or psychotic states. Underlying psychiatric disorders may be exacerbated [Australian].
- **Seizure Disorders:** Increased frequency of major motor seizures has been reported; close monitoring and potential dose reduction are required [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use caution in patients with heart failure (due to peripheral oedema) or orthostatic hypotension [Australian].
- **Anticholinergic Effects:** **Amantadine** possesses anticholinergic activity. It is contraindicated in untreated angle-closure glaucoma and should be used cautiously in patients with prostatic enlargement [Australian].
- **Ocular Effects:** Isolated cases of corneal lesions (e.g., epithelial oedema, reduced visual acuity) have been reported. If visual symptoms occur, discontinue the drug and consult an ophthalmologist [Australian].
- **Impulse Control Disorders:** Patients should be monitored for pathological gambling, increased libido, hypersexuality, and compulsive eating/spending. Consider dose reduction if these develop [Australian].
- **Suicide Risk:** Caution is advised due to reports of suicide attempts involving **amantadine** overdose [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Undesirable effects typically appear within the first 1–4 days of treatment and usually resolve within 24–48 hours of discontinuation.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Livedo reticularis, peripheral oedema |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Dizziness, light-headedness, headache, lethargy, ataxia, dysarthria, depression, anxiety, insomnia, palpitations, orthostatic hypotension, indigestion, dry mouth, nausea, constipation, hyperhidrosis, fatigue |
| **Rare** (0.1–0.01%) | Tremor, dyskinesia, convulsions, confusional state, psychosis, diarrhoea, rash, corneal lesions, urinary retention/incontinence |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)-like symptoms, cardiac failure, leukopenia, neutropenia, elevated liver enzymes, photosensitivity |
| **Not Known** | Delirium, hypomania, mania, hair loss, sudden loss of vision |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** NMS-like symptoms (upon withdrawal), cardiac arrest/sudden death (reported in overdose), and severe corneal oedema [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticholinergic Agents:** Concomitant use increases the risk of confusion, hallucinations, and atropine-like side effects [Australian].
- **Antipsychotics/Levodopa:** Risk of psychotic decompensation [Australian].
- **Hydrochlorothiazide + Triamterene:** May reduce **amantadine** clearance, leading to toxic plasma levels (ataxia, myoclonus, confusion) [Australian].
- **CNS Stimulants/Alcohol:** May result in additive CNS toxicity and should be avoided [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Contraindicated. Complications during pregnancy have been reported. Women of childbearing potential must use highly effective contraception during treatment and for 5 days after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. **Amantadine** is excreted into breast milk, and adverse reactions have been reported in breastfed infants [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Rat studies at maximum human-equivalent doses showed impaired fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 5 years of age. Hypothermia has been observed in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** More susceptible to adverse effects. Dosing should be limited to 100 mg daily in the absence of renal disease; further adjustments are required if renal function is impaired [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** **Amantadine** accumulates significantly in renal failure. Dosage must be adjusted by increasing the dosing interval [Australian].

**Renal Dosing Schedule (100 mg Dose Interval):**
- CrCl >75 mL/min: Every 12 hours
- CrCl 36–75 mL/min: Every 1 day
- CrCl 26–35 mL/min: Every 2 days
- CrCl 15–25 mL/min: Every 3 days
- CrCl <15 mL/min: Every 7 days [Australian].

- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; specific dosing adjustments are not provided in the primary source [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Antiparkinsonian:** Acts as an indirect dopamine agonist. It enhances the synthesis and release of dopamine and delays reuptake. It may also alter striatal D2 receptors and exert anticholinergic effects [Australian].
    - **Antiviral:** Specifically inhibits the replication of Influenza A viruses, likely by interfering with viral protein processing [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Completely absorbed; Tmax 1–4 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 67% protein bound. Crosses the blood-brain barrier; CSF concentrations are ~60% of plasma levels [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Minor metabolism via N-acetylation [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted unchanged in urine (65–85%). Elimination half-life is ~15 hours in healthy young adults but doubles in the elderly and increases significantly (3–5 fold) in renal impairment [Australian].
- **Haemodialysis:** Ineffective at removing **amantadine** (<5% removed) [Australian].
