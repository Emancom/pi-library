# Editorial Product Information: Adapalene

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of product information for **adapalene** (topical cream), intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory guidance to assist in therapeutic decision-making.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Adapalene
- **Chemical Class:** Naphthoic acid derivative with retinoid-like activity.
- **Brand Name(s):** Differin [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Topical Cream: 1 mg/g (0.1%) [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Galderma Australia [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acne Vulgaris:** Topical treatment of comedo, papular, and pustular acne involving the face, chest, or back [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents ≥12 years):** Apply a thin film to the affected areas once daily before bedtime.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Wash and dry the affected areas thoroughly before application.
    - Avoid contact with the eyes, lips, and mucous membranes.
- **Clinical Timeline:** Improvement is typically observed within 4 to 8 weeks. Continued use is generally required for further improvement.
- **Duration of Therapy:** Safety has been demonstrated for up to 12 weeks of treatment. It is recommended that a physician reassess the patient’s progress after 3 months of use [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to adapalene or any excipients, including methyl hydroxybenzoate and propyl hydroxybenzoate [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **External Use Only:** Not for ophthalmic, oral, or intravaginal use.
- **Tissue Integrity:** Avoid application to broken skin (cuts/abrasions), eczematous skin, or sunburned skin.
- **Severe Acne:** Use with caution in patients with severe acne involving large areas of the body; efficacy and safety have not been established for acne conglobulata (severe pustular or deep cystic acne).
- **Photosensitivity:** Adapalene may increase sensitivity to UV light. Patients should avoid or minimise exposure to sunlight and artificial UV radiation (e.g., sunlamps). The use of sunscreen and protective clothing is recommended.
- **Local Irritation:** If severe irritation occurs, the medication should be discontinued. For mild to moderate irritation, clinicians may suggest reduced frequency of application or temporary cessation until symptoms resolve.
- **Excipient Warnings:** Contains hydroxybenzoates, which may cause allergic reactions (potentially delayed) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily local and typically occur within the first month of therapy, often resolving with continued use.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Redness, dry skin, burning sensation at application site, scaling. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Skin irritation, pruritus, erythema, sunburn. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Contact dermatitis, eczema, skin exfoliation, acne flare, facial/eyelid oedema, skin discomfort, vesiculobullous eruptions, conjunctivitis. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Allergic reaction, lack of drug effect. |
| **Unknown (Post-marketing)** | Anaphylactic reaction, angioedema, application site burn, skin hypopigmentation, skin hyperpigmentation. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Topical Retinoids:** Concurrent use with other retinoids or drugs with a similar mode of action should be avoided [Australian].
- **Irritant Products:** Caution is advised when used alongside abrasive cleansers, strong drying agents, astringents, or peeling agents (e.g., sulfur, resorcinol, salicylic acid, or benzoyl peroxide) due to potential additive irritant effects.
- **Standard Acne Therapy:** Adapalene does not cause mutual degradation when used with topical erythromycin, clindamycin phosphate, or benzoyl peroxide.
- **Systemic Interactions:** Low systemic absorption makes interactions with systemic medications unlikely [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Adapalene has been shown to induce foetal abnormalities in animal studies at high oral doses. While topical administration at high multiples of human exposure was not associated with teratogenicity in animals, some skeletal variations were noted.
    - **Recommendation:** Adapalene should **not** be used by women who are pregnant or planning to become pregnant. If pregnancy occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if adapalene is excreted in human milk. Use with caution; do not apply to the chest area to avoid direct infant exposure.
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children under 12 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available; clinical trials generally focused on younger populations.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, systemic absorption is negligible.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Adapalene binds to specific retinoic acid nuclear receptors (RAR-beta and RAR-gamma) but does not bind to cytosolic receptor proteins. It normalises the differentiation of follicular epithelial cells, leading to decreased microcomedone formation.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits potent modulator activity on cellular differentiation, keratinisation, and inflammatory processes. It inhibits polymorphonuclear leukocyte chemotaxis and the metabolism of arachidonic acid.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Very low through human skin; often undetectable in plasma (limit of quantification 0.25 ng/mL).
    - **Distribution:** Radioactivity distributes to the liver, spleen, adrenals, and ovaries in animal models.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily O-demethylation, hydroxylation, and conjugation in animals.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via the biliary route.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Comparative Efficacy:** In a trial of 350 subjects, adapalene cream significantly reduced total lesions (32% vs 15% for vehicle) and non-inflammatory lesions (37% vs 15% for vehicle) over 12 weeks.
- **Comparison with Tretinoin:** A trial of 277 subjects compared adapalene 0.1% cream to tretinoin 0.05% cream. Both showed significant reductions in total lesions (60% and 67% respectively) and inflammatory lesions (43% and 56% respectively) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Avoid exposure to excessive heat. Keep the tube tightly closed when not in use. Keep out of reach of children.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
