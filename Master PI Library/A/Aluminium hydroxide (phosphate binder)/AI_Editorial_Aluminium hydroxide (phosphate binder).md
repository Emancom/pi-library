# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) source documents provided for the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory approved labeling for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (L-carnitine).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule/amino acid derivative required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) clear, colorless sterile solution in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Errors:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer intravenously as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Initial Crisis Management:** A loading dose is often administered in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest recorded administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Timing:** Administer into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Monitoring and Titration:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration [US].
- **Management:** Discontinue treatment immediately upon sign of severe hypersensitivity. Re-administration should only be considered after a thorough risk-benefit analysis and under close monitoring [US].

### Seizures
- Seizures have been reported in patients receiving levocarnitine, including those without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### General Precautions
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation):** The safety of oral levocarnitine has not been established in patients with renal impairment. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% frequency, regardless of causality) include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Serious/Postmarketing Effects:** Seizures, anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].
- **Body Odor:** Reported as a less frequent adverse reaction [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Warfarin
- **Interaction:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin [US].
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Risk Summary:** No adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal reproductive studies (rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].

### Breastfeeding
- **Risk Summary:** Carnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration [US].
- **Recommendation:** The risks to the child from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric Use
- Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg); however, specific age-related safety profiles are not detailed beyond standard dosing instructions [US].

### Elderly Use
- Not specified in available sources.

### Renal Impairment
- Levocarnitine is easily removed by dialysis. See **Section 3** for specific ESRD dosing [US].
- Accumulation of TMA/TMAO metabolites is a concern with high-dose *oral* therapy in renal impairment [US].

### Hepatic Impairment
- Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This provides the necessary substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in tissues (excluding the brain), particularly skeletal and cardiac muscle [US].
- It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are quickly eliminated, thereby alleviating autointoxication from acylCoA accumulation [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations from a pre-dialysis baseline of ~20 µmol/L to trough levels of ~190 µmol/L by week 8. Doses of 10 mg/kg, 20 mg/kg, and 40 mg/kg all showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Clinical Outcome Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations in ESRD is demonstrated, the effect of supplementation on clinical outcomes (signs and symptoms) in this population is not determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of opened vials as the formulation is preservative-free [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
