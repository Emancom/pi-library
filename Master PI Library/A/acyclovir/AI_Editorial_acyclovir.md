# Editorial Product Information: acyclovir

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): acyclovir
- Chemical class: Synthetic purine deoxynucleoside analogue
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Topical (cutaneous) application to external lips and face only
- Strength cited in available sources: 5% w/w (50 mg per gram) topical cream

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2002 [US]
- Date of last revision/update of provided U.S. product information: February 2021 [US]
- Withdrawals/suspensions/restrictions: Not specified in available sources
- Other jurisdictions' first registration/approval dates or PI revision dates: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of recurrent herpes labialis (cold sores) in immunocompetent adults and adolescents 12 years of age and older. [US]
- Use for other indications (e.g., genital herpes, varicella zoster, systemic infections) is not specified in the available topical-cream product information.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult and adolescent)
- Apply topical acyclovir cream five (5) times per day for four (4) days.
- For adolescents 12 years of age and older, the same dosing regimen as adults is recommended. [US]

### Dosing by indication
- Recurrent herpes labialis: initiate therapy as early as possible following onset of signs or symptoms (prodrome or appearance of lesions). Apply 5 times daily for 4 days. [US]

### Administration instructions
- Intended for cutaneous use only on the external aspects of the lips and face. Do not apply on mucous membranes, in the eye, inside the mouth or nose, or on the genitals. [US]
- Apply a quantity sufficient to cover the affected area including the outer margin. Initiate at earliest sign/symptom of recurrence.
- Wash hands prior to application and ensure face/lips are clean and dry; wash hands after application to avoid inadvertent spread.
- Avoid applying other topical products to the affected area while using acyclovir cream.
- Avoid unnecessary rubbing of the affected area to prevent aggravation or transfer of infection.

### Titration schedules
- Not applicable / Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to acyclovir, valacyclovir, or any component of the formulation. [All sources provided]
- No additional contraindications are specified in the provided product information.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Cutaneous use only:** Use only on external aspects of the lips and face; application to mucous membranes, eyes, inside the mouth or nose, or genitals is not recommended due to lack of data and potential for irritation. [US]
- **Contact sensitization and irritation:** Topical acyclovir cream has demonstrated potential for local irritation and contact sensitization. Patients should discontinue use and seek medical advice if signs of hypersensitivity or severe local irritation occur. [US]
- **Immunocompromised patients:** The effect of topical acyclovir cream in immunocompromised patients has not been established; efficacy and safety are not defined in that population. [US]

### Monitoring requirements
- Specific laboratory monitoring requirements are not specified in the available sources.
- Monitor for local hypersensitivity reactions and signs of systemic allergic response (e.g., angioedema, anaphylaxis) and manage appropriately.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Counsel patients to avoid contact with eyes; if contact occurs, rinse thoroughly with water and seek medical advice if irritation persists. [US]
- Keep out of reach of children. [US]

## 7. Adverse Effects
The following adverse reactions were reported in clinical trials and postmarketing experience for topical acyclovir cream provided in the available sources.

Notes on frequency classification used:
- Very common: >10%
- Common: 1–10%
- Uncommon: 0.1–1%
- Rare: <0.1%
- Very rare: <0.01%
- “Frequency unknown” denotes events reported postmarketing where incidence cannot be estimated from spontaneous reports.

Clinical-trials summary:
- In pooled double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, local application-site reactions were reported by 5% of patients receiving topical acyclovir cream versus 4% receiving placebo (vehicle).
- Specific local reactions (each reported in <1% of patients in trials): dry lips, desquamation, dryness of skin, cracked lips, burning skin, pruritus, flakiness of skin, and stinging on skin.
- Dermal irritation and cumulative irritation potential were observed in separate dermal tolerance testing.
- Contact sensitization testing: in a repeat-insult patch study, possible cutaneous sensitization reactions were observed in 4/202 evaluable subjects (2%) for both acyclovir cream and placebo; reactions were confirmed on rechallenge in 3 subjects.

Postmarketing:
- Reports include angioedema and anaphylaxis (general), and contact dermatitis and eczema (skin). These events were reported voluntarily; frequency estimates cannot be made.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Risk summary: Systemic absorption of topical acyclovir cream is minimal; maternal topical use is not expected to result in meaningful fetal exposure. Published observational literature over several decades has not identified a drug‑associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes with topical acyclovir use. [US]
- Background population risks (for context): In the U.S. general population the estimated background risk of major birth defects is 2–4% and the estimated background risk of miscarriage in clinically recognized pregnancies is 15–20%. These are general population estimates and not attributable to acyclovir exposure. [US]
- Specific pregnancy-category classification systems (e.g., Australian categories) are not provided in the available sources for topical acyclovir cream.

### Breastfeeding
- Risk summary: Minimal systemic absorption following topical use; breastfeeding is not expected to result in exposure of the infant to topical acyclovir cream. There are no data on effects of topical acyclovir cream on the breastfed infant or on milk production. Consider benefits of breastfeeding and maternal clinical need when deciding to use topical acyclovir cream. [US]

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **acyclovir** is a synthetic purine deoxynucleoside analogue active against α‑herpesviruses (HSV‑1 and HSV‑2).
- Selective activation by herpesvirus-encoded thymidine kinase (TK) converts acyclovir to acyclovir monophosphate; cellular kinases convert it to the active triphosphate form.
- Acyclovir triphosphate inhibits viral DNA replication by (1) competitive inhibition of viral DNA polymerase, (2) incorporation into and termination of the growing viral DNA chain, and (3) direct inactivation of viral DNA polymerase.

### Pharmacokinetics (topical cream)
- Percutaneous absorption study (6 adult male volunteers): cream applied to 710 cm2 on back, 5 times daily for 4 days. Average daily urinary excretion of acyclovir was approximately 0.04% of the daily applied dose. Plasma concentrations were below the limit of detection (0.01 μM) in 5 subjects and 0.014 μM in 1 subject. These results indicate minimal systemic absorption in adults. [US]
- Systemic absorption after topical application has not been evaluated in patients <18 years of age in the provided sources. [US]
- Distribution, metabolism and elimination specifics following topical administration are not described in detail in the topical-cream product information beyond urinary excretion findings.

### Pharmacodynamics / Antiviral activity
- In vitro activity: EC50 values (plaque‑reduction assays on Vero cells) for acyclovir against clinical HSV isolates vary:
  - HSV‑1: 0.09 to 59.9 μM (0.02 to 13.5 μg/mL)
  - HSV‑2: 0.04 to 44.0 μM (0.01 to 9.9 μg/mL)
- The quantitative relationship between in vitro susceptibility and clinical response has not been established and virus sensitivity testing is not standardized.

### Resistance
- Resistance to acyclovir can result from mutations in viral thymidine kinase (TK; pUL23) and DNA polymerase (POL; pUL30) genes, including frameshifts and substitutions that reduce or abolish TK activity (TK‑negative, TK‑partial, TK‑altered).
- A variety of resistance‑associated amino acid substitutions and frameshifts have been observed in cell culture and in clinical isolates from treated patients. Cross‑resistance to related antivirals (penciclovir, famciclovir) and to foscarnet may occur depending on the mutation.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Adult pivotal studies (efficacy)
- Two double-blind, randomized, placebo (vehicle)-controlled trials evaluated topical acyclovir cream for recurrent herpes labialis.
- Patient population: average of approximately five episodes in prior 12 months. In one trial median age 37 years (range 18–81), 74% female, 94% Caucasian; in the other median age 38 years (range 18–87), 73% female, 94% Caucasian.
- Intervention: subjects instructed to begin treatment within 1 hour of noticing signs or symptoms and to apply medication 5 times daily for 4 days.
- Primary/clinical outcome summary: Mean duration of recurrent herpes labialis episode was approximately 0.5 day shorter with topical acyclovir cream versus placebo (approximately 4.5 days vs 5.0 days). No significant difference was observed in prevention of progression of cold-sore lesions between active treatment and placebo in the trials described. [US]

### Safety pooled data
- Across five double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (pooled for safety), 1,124 patients received topical acyclovir cream and 1,161 received placebo (vehicle). Local application-site reactions were reported in 5% of patients receiving acyclovir cream and 4% receiving placebo.
- Additional dermal tolerance and sensitization studies:
  - Single occluded and semi-occluded patch testing in 22 healthy adults showed both acyclovir cream and vehicle exhibited high and cumulative irritation potential.
  - Repeat-insult patch testing in 251 healthy adults (202 evaluable) showed possible cutaneous sensitization reactions in 4 (2%) subjects with both active and placebo; confirmed on rechallenge in 3 subjects.

### Pediatric studies
- Open-label, uncontrolled study in 113 patients aged 12–17 years used same dosing as adults; safety profile similar to adults. Effectiveness in <12 years not established.

## 15. Patient Counselling Information (key points for healthcare professionals to convey)
- Indication and expectations: topical acyclovir cream is indicated for treatment of recurrent cold sores of the lips and face in immunocompetent adults and adolescents ≥12 years; it is not a cure but may modestly shorten episode duration when started early.
- Administration: instruct patients to begin at earliest sign or symptom (prodrome or lesion appearance) and to apply the cream five times daily for four days, covering the affected area including outer margins.
- Restrictions of use: for external use only on lips/face; do not apply to eyes, inside mouth or nose, mucous membranes, or genitals.
- Adverse effects and allergic reactions: advise patients of potential for local irritation, contact sensitization, and rare but serious allergic reactions (angioedema, anaphylaxis); discontinue use and seek medical attention for signs of severe hypersensitivity.
- General hygiene: wash hands before and after application; avoid touching or rubbing area; avoid use of other topical products on the lesion during treatment.
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding: systemic absorption after topical use is minimal and fetal or infant exposure is not expected; discuss maternal clinical needs and breastfeeding benefits with patients as data on effects in breastfed infants are lacking.
- Pediatric counselling: not established for children <12 years; safety data available for ages 12–17 indicate similar adverse-event profile to adults.
