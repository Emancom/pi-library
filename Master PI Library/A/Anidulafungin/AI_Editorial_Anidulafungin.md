# Editorial Product Information: Anidulafungin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source material; therefore, clinical recommendations and regulatory statuses for those regions are noted as "Not specified in available sources."
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Anidulafungin (Echinocandin class).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Anidulafungin Accord** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Powder for injection, 100 mg per vial [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Accord Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Invasive Candidiasis:** Treatment of invasive candidiasis, including candidaemia, in adult and paediatric patients aged one month and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Adult Patients:** A single 200 mg loading dose on Day 1, followed by a maintenance dose of 100 mg daily thereafter [Australian]. 
- **Paediatric Patients (1 month to <18 years):** A loading dose of 3.0 mg/kg (maximum 200 mg) on Day 1, followed by a maintenance dose of 1.5 mg/kg (maximum 100 mg) daily thereafter [Australian].

### Duration of Treatment
- Treatment should continue for at least 14 days after the last positive culture. Total duration should generally not exceed one month [Australian].
- In paediatric patients, therapy may be switched to an oral antifungal after a minimum of 10 days of IV treatment [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Do not administer as a bolus injection.**
- **Infusion Rate:** The rate must not exceed 1.1 mg/minute (equivalent to 1.4 mL/minute or 84 mL/hour) to minimise infusion-related reactions [Australian].
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute each 100 mg vial with 30 mL water for injections to achieve 3.33 mg/mL.
- **Dilution:** Further dilute with either 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose to reach a final concentration of 0.77 mg/mL.
- **Minimum Infusion Duration:** 90 minutes for a 100 mg dose; 180 minutes for a 200 mg dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to anidulafungin or any of the product excipients [Australian].
- Hypersensitivity to other medicinal products of the echinocandin class (e.g., caspofungin) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylactic Reactions:** Anaphylaxis and shock have been reported. Discontinue immediately if these occur [Australian].
- **Infusion-Related Reactions:** Symptoms include rash, urticaria, flushing, pruritus, dyspnoea, bronchospasm, and hypotension. These are infrequent if the infusion rate is maintained $\le$ 1.1 mg/min [Australian].
- **Hepatic Effects:** Laboratory abnormalities in liver function tests (LFTs) have been observed. Isolated cases of significant hepatic dysfunction, hepatitis, or failure have occurred in patients with serious underlying conditions. Monitor LFTs if abnormalities develop [Australian].
- **Hereditary Fructose Intolerance (HFI):** This product contains fructose. It should not be administered to patients with HFI unless strictly necessary. Risk is highest in infants and children under 2 years old, where HFI may not yet be diagnosed [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are typically mild to moderate.
- **Common (1–10%):** Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, hypokalaemia, increased ALT/AST/alkaline phosphatase, rash, and pruritus [Australian].
- **Infusion-Related:** Flushing, hot flush, and urticaria [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Anaphylactic shock and reaction (post-marketing) [Australian].
    - Convulsions and bronchospasm [Australian].
    - Potential for hepatic failure [Australian].
    - **Paediatric Considerations:** Hepatobiliary adverse events (specifically increased ALT and AST) appear to occur at a higher frequency in paediatric patients compared to adults [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
Anidulafungin has negligible renal clearance and is not a clinically relevant substrate, inducer, or inhibitor of CYP450 isoenzymes.
- **No Dose Adjustments Required:** 
    - Ciclosporin (AUC increased by 22%, but not clinically significant).
    - Voriconazole, Tacrolimus, Liposomal Amphotericin B, and Rifampicin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Use only if clearly indicated. Effective contraception should be used in women of childbearing potential. Animal studies showed skeletal changes in rat fetuses, though these were not dose-related [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Use with caution. Anidulafungin is excreted in the milk of lactating rats; human data are unavailable. The clinician should consider the benefit of breastfeeding against potential risks to the child [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal studies at 2 times the human maintenance dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients $\ge$ 1 month old. Use in neonates (< 1 month) is not recommended due to high polysorbate 80 content in the formulation, which has been associated with life-threatening toxicities in this age group [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients $\ge$ 65 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for any degree of renal insufficiency, including patients on dialysis. Anidulafungin is not dialysable [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for mild, moderate, or severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective inhibition of 1,3-$\beta$-D-glucan synthase, an enzyme essential for fungal cell wall synthesis. It is fungicidal against *Candida* species [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapid distribution half-life (0.5–1 hour); volume of distribution (30–50 L) similar to total body fluid; >99% protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Does not undergo hepatic metabolism. It undergoes slow chemical degradation at physiologic pH to an inactive ring-opened peptide [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily eliminated via biliary excretion of degradants. Less than 1% is excreted in urine [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Displays linear pharmacokinetics across a dose range of 15–130 mg daily [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Invasive Candidiasis/Candidaemia (Adults):** In a pivotal Phase 3 trial, anidulafungin (200 mg loading/100 mg maintenance) was superior to fluconazole. Global success at the end of IV therapy was 75.6% for anidulafungin versus 60.2% for fluconazole [Australian].
- **Neutropenic Patients:** Pooled analysis of adult neutropenic patients showed a successful global response rate of 56.5% at the end of IV treatment [Australian].
- **Paediatric Study:** A prospective study of 68 patients (1 month to <18 years) showed an overall successful global response rate of 70.3% at the end of IV therapy [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze). Excursions up to 25°C for up to 96 hours are permitted [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Should be used as soon as possible. If necessary, store at 2-8°C for up to 24 hours (do not freeze) or at room temperature for up to 6 hours [Australian].

- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Not specified for other jurisdictions in available sources.
