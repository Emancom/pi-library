# Editorial Product Information: Acitretin

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for **acitretin**, designed as a coherent reference for healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a clinical summary and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Acitretin (a synthetic aromatic analogue of retinoic acid).
*   **Chemical Class:** Retinoid.
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Neotigason** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   Hard gelatin capsules: 10 mg and 25 mg [Australian].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Teva Pharma Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Acitretin is indicated for the treatment of:
*   Severe, intractable psoriasis in all clinical forms [Australian].
*   Severe disorders of keratinisation, including:
    *   Ichthyosis [Australian].
    *   Keratosis follicularis (Darier's disease) [Australian].
    *   Hyperkeratosis palmaris et plantaris [Australian].
    *   Pustulosis palmaris et plantaris [Australian].
    *   Lichen planus affecting the skin or mucosae [Australian].
    *   Pityriasis rubra pilaris [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Due to inter-individual variations in absorption and metabolism, dosage must be personalised based on clinical efficacy and tolerability.
*   **Administration:** Capsules should be taken once daily, preferably with a main meal or with milk to optimise absorption [Australian].
*   **Initial Dose:** 25 mg or 30 mg daily for approximately two to four weeks [Australian].
*   **Maintenance Dose:** Generally 25 mg to 50 mg daily for an additional six to eight weeks to achieve optimal results. 
*   **Psoriasis:** Therapy may be ceased once lesions have sufficiently resolved [Australian].
*   **Keratinisation Disorders:** Continuous maintenance is typically required at the lowest effective dose (often <20 mg daily; maximum 50 mg daily) [Australian].

### Pediatric Dosing
Acitretin should only be used in children when alternative therapies have failed. 
*   **Standard Dose:** 0.5 mg/kg daily [Australian].
*   **Maximum Dose:** 1 mg/kg or 35 mg daily for limited periods [Australian].
*   **Maintenance:** Must be kept at the lowest possible level due to long-term safety concerns regarding bone development [Australian].

### Missed Dose
Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
**Acitretin** is strictly contraindicated in the following:
*   **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Highly teratogenic. Contraindicated in pregnant women and nursing mothers [Australian].
*   **Women of Childbearing Potential (WOCBP):** Contraindicated unless all conditions of the Pregnancy Prevention Programme (PPP) are met [Australian].
*   **Organ Impairment:** Patients with severe hepatic or renal impairment [Australian].
*   **Hyperlipidemia:** Patients with chronic, abnormally elevated blood lipid values [Australian].
*   **Drug Combinations:** 
    *   Concomitant use with **tetracyclines** (risk of benign intracranial hypertension) [Australian].
    *   Concomitant use with **methotrexate** (increased risk of hepatitis) [Australian].
    *   Concomitant use with **Vitamin A** or other retinoids (risk of hypervitaminosis A) [Australian].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to acitretin, other retinoids, or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
*   **Teratogenicity:** **Acitretin** is highly teratogenic, causing a high frequency of severe, life-threatening birth defects. It must not be used by women who are pregnant or who intend to become pregnant during therapy or for **3 years** following cessation of treatment [Australian].

### Pregnancy Prevention Programme (PPP)
Prescribers must ensure WOCBP understand the following:
*   Use of at least one highly effective (user-independent) or two complementary forms of contraception for 1 month before, during, and for 3 years after treatment [Australian].
*   Strict avoidance of alcohol during treatment and for 2 months after (alcohol facilitates the conversion of acitretin to etretinate, which has a much longer half-life) [Australian].
*   Mandatory negative pregnancy tests (minimum sensitivity 25 mIU/mL) monthly during treatment and every 1–3 months post-therapy for 3 years [Australian].

### General Warnings
*   **Blood Donation:** Patients must not donate blood during therapy and for 3 years after treatment ends to avoid potential exposure to pregnant transfusion recipients [Australian].
*   **Psychiatric Disorders:** Reports of depression, anxiety, mood alterations, and psychotic disorders. Patients with a history of depression require careful monitoring [Australian].
*   **Pseudotumour Cerebri:** Benign intracranial hypertension has been associated with retinoid use. Discontinue immediately if symptoms (headache, nausea, vomiting, visual disturbances, papilloedema) occur [Australian].
*   **Hyperostosis:** Long-term treatment may lead to bone changes, including spinal hyperostosis and extraskeletal calcification. Musculoskeletal symptoms should be investigated promptly [Australian].
*   **Lipid and Hepatic Monitoring:** Serum lipids and liver function tests must be monitored before treatment, every 1–2 weeks for the first 2 months, and every 3 months thereafter [Australian].
*   **Diabetes:** Retinoids may affect glucose tolerance; blood sugar should be checked more frequently in the early stages of treatment [Australian].
*   **UV Exposure:** Retinoids enhance the effects of UV light; patients should avoid excessive sunlight and use SPF 15+ sunscreen [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse effects are dose-dependent and resemble hypervitaminosis A symptoms. They typically resolve upon dose reduction or withdrawal.

| System Organ Class | Very Common (>10%) | Common (1–10%) | Uncommon to Rare (<1%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Skin & Appendages** | Dry skin/lips, pruritus, erythema, scaling, alopecia, nail fragility | Bullous eruption, dermatitis, abnormal hair texture | Photosensitivity, skin fissures, pyogenic granuloma |
| **Ocular** | Dry eyes, conjunctivitis, contact lens intolerance | Blurred vision, impaired night vision | Keratitis, corneal ulceration, papilloedema |
| **Musculoskeletal** | | Arthralgia, arthritis, bone pain | Hyperostosis, exostosis, elevated CK |
| **Metabolic/Liver** | Elevated triglycerides/cholesterol, elevated transaminases/ALP | | Jaundice, hepatitis, pancreatitis (rare) |
| **Neurological** | | Headache, fatigue, somnolence | Dizziness, pseudotumour cerebri |
| **Respiratory** | Dry/inflamed mucous membranes (epistaxis, rhinitis) | | Dysphonia |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Capillary leak syndrome (retinoic acid syndrome), exfoliative dermatitis, and severe psychiatric disturbances have been reported in post-marketing experience [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Ethanol:** Consumption leads to the formation of etretinate, which is eliminated much more slowly than acitretin (half-life ~120 days). Alcohol must be avoided during treatment and for 2 months after stopping [Australian].
*   **Contraceptives:** Progestogen-only "minipills" should be avoided as retinoids may reduce their efficacy [Australian].
*   **Phenytoin:** Acitretin may partially reduce the protein binding of phenytoin [Australian].
*   **Tetracyclines/Methotrexate/Vitamin A:** Combined use is contraindicated (see Section 4) [Australian].
*   **No Significant Interactions:** Digoxin, cimetidine, and coumarin-type anticoagulants (warfarin) showed no significant pharmacokinetic interaction with acitretin in specific studies [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy Category:** **Category X** [Australian]. Acitretin is strictly contraindicated. Exposure always involves a risk of congenital malformations (e.g., craniofacial, cardiac, CNS, and skeletal defects) [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Acitretin is lipophilic and is assumed to pass into breast milk in significant quantities [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** Full PPP information must be provided to both males and females. Semen data suggest minimal risk of teratogenic effects from male paternal exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Use only in life-threatening or severe cases where other therapies are exhausted. Closely monitor growth parameters and bone development (X-ray) [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** Appropriate examinations for ossification abnormalities are recommended during long-term treatment [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. In end-stage renal failure, plasma concentrations are significantly lower, but the drug is not removed by haemodialysis [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment due to risk of hepatotoxicity [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Acitretin reverses epidermal proliferation and increased keratinisation. The exact cellular mechanism is not fully defined but involves the modulation of skin cell growth and inflammation [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations reached in 1–5 hours. Bioavailability is approximately 60% (range 36–95%) and is significantly enhanced when taken with food [Australian].
*   **Distribution:** Highly lipophilic; protein binding exceeds 99% [Australian].
*   **Metabolism:** Metabolised via isomerisation to 13-cis acitretin, glucuronidation, and side-chain cleavage. Ethanol consumption induces the formation of etretinate [Australian].
*   **Elimination:** Excreted in approximately equal parts via bile and urine. The elimination half-life is ~50 hours for acitretin and ~60 hours for 13-cis acitretin. However, if etretinate is formed, its half-life is approximately 120 days [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Available sources describe that acitretin use in psoriatic patients results in clinical improvement characterized by reduced scaling, erythema, and lesion thickness, alongside decreased epidermal inflammation [Australian]. Specific pivotal trial data were not detailed in the provided documentation.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
*   **Precautions:** The product is sensitive to moisture; store in the original package [Australian].
*   **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources (refer to packaging) [Australian].
