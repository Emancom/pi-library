# Editorial Product Information: Alosetron

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. No other regional documents were provided for comparison.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory requirements into a structured, concise format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulatory guidelines and the current official PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Alosetron hydrochloride (selective serotonin 5-HT₃ antagonist).
- **Brand Name(s):** Lotronex [US].
- **ATC Code:** A03AE01 (Serotonin receptor antagonists).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 0.5 mg tablets (white, oval, film-coated, debossed with "GX EX1").
    - 1 mg tablets (blue, oval, film-coated, debossed with "GX CT1") [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sebela Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Diarrhea-Predominant Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS-D):** Indicated only for women with chronic symptoms (typically ≥6 months) who have failed to respond adequately to conventional therapy. [US-approved]
    - **Criteria for "Severe" IBS-D:** Includes diarrhea plus one or more of the following: frequent/severe abdominal pain, frequent bowel urgency/fecal incontinence, or significant disability/restriction of daily activities.
    - **Note:** Safety and efficacy have not been adequately established in men [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Initial Adult Dose:** 0.5 mg twice daily for 4 weeks. This starting dose is intended to minimise the risk of constipation.
- **Dose Titration:** If the initial dose is well-tolerated but does not adequately control symptoms after 4 weeks, the dose may be increased to 1 mg twice daily.
- **Discontinuation Criteria:** 
    - Discontinue immediately if the patient develops constipation or signs of ischemic colitis.
    - If symptoms are not adequately controlled after 4 weeks of treatment at 1 mg twice daily, alosetron is unlikely to provide benefit and should be discontinued.
- **Administration Instructions:** May be taken with or without food.
- **Missed Dose:** Not explicitly detailed; follow standard practice of taking the next dose as scheduled without doubling.

## 4. Contraindications
Alosetron is contraindicated in patients with:
- **Constipation:** Current constipation or a history of chronic/severe constipation or its sequelae.
- **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** History of intestinal obstruction, stricture, toxic megacolon, GI perforation, and/or adhesions.
- **Vascular/Inflammatory Conditions:** History of ischemic colitis, impaired intestinal circulation, thrombophlebitis, or hypercoagulable states.
- **Inflammatory Bowel Disease:** Crohn’s disease, ulcerative colitis, or diverticulitis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh score >9).
- **Drug Interactions:** Concomitant use of fluvoxamine [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Serious Gastrointestinal Adverse Reactions [US]:**
    - **Ischemic Colitis:** May occur at any time during treatment without warning. Can be life-threatening.
    - **Serious Complications of Constipation:** Including obstruction, ileus, impaction, toxic megacolon, and secondary bowel ischemia. Rare cases of perforation and death have been reported.
    - **Immediate Action:** Discontinue alosetron immediately in any patient who develops constipation or symptoms of ischemic colitis (e.g., rectal bleeding, bloody diarrhea, new/worsening abdominal pain).
- **Monitoring:** Prompt clinical evaluation and diagnostic testing are required for suspected ischemic colitis.
- **Vulnerable Populations:** The risk of serious constipation complications is increased in the elderly, the debilitated, or those taking concurrent medications that decrease bowel motility.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data [US]
| Adverse Effect | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Constipation** | Very Common (>10%) — Reported in 29% at 1 mg BID |
| **Abdominal Pain/Discomfort** | Common (1–10%) |
| **Nausea** | Common (1–10%) |
| **Gastrointestinal discomfort** | Common (1–10%) |
| **Abdominal distention** | Common (1–10%) |
| **Hemorrhoids** | Common (1–10%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Ischemic colitis (cumulative incidence of 0.3% over 6 months), toxic megacolon, GI perforation, and small bowel mesenteric ischemia.
- **Hepatic:** Occasional elevations in ALT (>2-fold upper limit of normal) have been observed (1%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** 
    - **Fluvoxamine:** Contraindicated. Increases alosetron AUC by ~6-fold and prolongs half-life by ~3-fold.
    - **Moderate Inhibitors (e.g., quinolones, cimetidine):** Should be avoided unless clinically necessary due to expected increases in alosetron exposure.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** 
    - **Ketoconazole:** Increases alosetron AUC by ~29%. Use caution when co-administering with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., clarithromycin, voriconazole) [US].
- **Other Enzymes:** Alosetron does not significantly inhibit or induce CYP3A4, 2C9, or 2C19. It may inhibit N-acetyltransferase and CYP1A2 at high concentrations.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Data in pregnant women are insufficient to determine drug-associated risks. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at high multiples of the human dose showed no adverse developmental effects [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Alosetron is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk. Caution is advised; infants exposed via breast milk should be monitored for severe constipation or blood in stools.
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility or reproductive performance were observed in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established. Use is not recommended [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Patients ≥65 years may be at higher risk for complications of constipation. Use with appropriate caution.
- **Renal Impairment:** No significant effect on the elimination of alosetron. Data for end-stage renal disease are unavailable.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate:** Use with caution; exposure may be increased.
    - **Severe:** Contraindicated due to risk of significantly increased exposure.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent, selective 5-HT₃ receptor antagonist. These receptors are ligand-gated cation channels on enteric neurons. By blocking these receptors, alosetron reduces visceral pain, slows colonic transit time, and modulates GI secretions.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; absolute bioavailability ~50–60%. Food decreases absorption by ~25%.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution 65–95 L. Plasma protein binding is 82%.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by the liver, primarily via CYP1A2, with minor contributions from CYP3A4 and CYP2C9.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~1.5 hours. Renal clearance is ~112 mL/min; only 13% is excreted unchanged in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy in Women:** Five 12-week US multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials confirmed efficacy in women with IBS-D.
- **Key Outcomes:** Alosetron significantly improved abdominal pain and discomfort, reduced bowel urgency, and improved stool consistency compared to placebo.
- **Dose-Response:** 0.5 mg twice daily showed a lower incidence of constipation compared to 1 mg twice daily while still providing clinical benefit [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from light and moisture [US].
- **Packaging:** Typically supplied in bottles of 30 tablets with child-resistant closures.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only. Prescribing was historically restricted under a specific Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) program due to GI risks.
