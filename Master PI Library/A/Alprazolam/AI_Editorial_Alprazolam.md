# Editorial Product Information: Alprazolam

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information regarding other major jurisdictions (US, European, UK, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided for this specific synthesis.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Alprazolam, a triazolo-benzodiazepine derivative.
- **Chemical Name:** 8-chloro-1-methyl-6-phenyl-4H-s-triazolo (4,3-α)(1,4) benzodiazepine.
- **Brand Names:** Alprax [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05BA12 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for alprazolam).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 0.25 mg, 0.5 mg, 1.0 mg, and 2.0 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anxiety:** Short-term symptomatic relief of anxiety, including cases where patients present with concurrent symptoms of depression [Australian].
- **Panic Disorder:** Treatment of panic disorder, with or without phobic avoidance. Indicated for the attenuation or blocking of panic attacks and phobias in patients experiencing agoraphobia with panic attacks [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Dosage must be individualized based on symptom severity and patient response. The lowest effective dose should be utilized to minimize the risk of oversedation or ataxia. If dose escalation is required, the evening dose should be increased before daytime doses [Australian].

### Adult Dosing [Australian]
*   **Anxiety:** Start with 0.5 mg to 1.5 mg daily in divided doses. The usual maintenance range is 0.5 mg to 4.0 mg daily.
*   **Anxiety with Depressive Symptoms:** Start with 1.5 mg daily in divided doses. Maintenance range is 1.5 mg to 4.5 mg daily.
*   **Panic Related Disorders:** Start with 0.5 mg to 1.0 mg at bedtime. Titrate by no more than 1 mg every 3–4 days. The mean dose in clinical trials was 5.7 ± 2.27 mg, with rare requirements up to 10 mg daily.

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food. Administration after meals may delay the onset of absorption and decrease the intensity of side effects such as somnolence [Australian].

### Discontinuation and Titration
- **Tapering:** Withdrawal must be gradual. It is recommended that the daily dose be reduced by no more than 0.25 mg to 0.5 mg every three days. Some patients may require even slower tapering [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to alprazolam or other benzodiazepines.
- Myasthenia gravis.
- Chronic obstructive airways disease with incipient respiratory failure [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **CNS Depression:** Patients must be cautioned against operating heavy machinery or driving, as impairment may persist the day following use. Tolerance to alcohol and other CNS depressants is reduced [Australian].
- **Dependence and Withdrawal:** Physical and psychological dependence can occur at recommended doses. Withdrawal symptoms (including seizures) can manifest following rapid dose reduction or abrupt cessation.
- **Depression and Suicide:** Alprazolam is not recommended as primary therapy for depression or psychosis. In panic disorder, which is often comorbid with depression, clinicians should exercise caution due to increased suicide risk in untreated patients [Australian].
- **Paradoxical Reactions:** Acute rage, stimulation, or excitement may occur; therapy should be discontinued if these manifest [Australian].
- **Specific Medical Conditions:** Use with caution in patients with acute narrow-angle glaucoma, respiratory depression, or epilepsy (abrupt withdrawal may trigger seizures) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Side effects are typically observed at the start of therapy and usually resolve with continued use or dose adjustment.

### Very Common (>10%) [Australian]
- Drowsiness, lightheadedness, ataxia, coordination disorders, fatigue, slurred speech.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Respiratory:** Respiratory depression, especially in patients with compromised lung function.
- **Psychiatric:** Confusion, hallucinations, mania/hypomania, and suicidal ideation.
- **Hepatic:** Rare instances of jaundice or elevated liver enzymes [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP3A4) [Australian]
- **Potent Inhibitors (Contraindicated/Not Recommended):** Ketoconazole and itraconazole significantly increase alprazolam concentrations.
- **Other Inhibitors (Caution/Dose reduction):** Fluvoxamine, cimetidine, fluoxetine, oral contraceptives (20% increase in steady-state), diltiazem, macrolide antibiotics (erythromycin, clarithromycin), and grapefruit juice.
- **Protease Inhibitors:** Ritonavir interactions are complex; initial inhibition of clearance is followed by induction upon extended exposure, requiring dose adjustments.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with alcohol, barbiturates, antipsychotics, sedative-hypnotics, tricyclic antidepressants, and narcotic analgesics [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category C**. Benzodiazepines cross the placenta and may cause fetal harm (increased risk of congenital malformations) if used in the first trimester. Use in the third trimester or during labor may result in "floppy infant syndrome" (hypotonia, respiratory depression, hypothermia) or neonatal withdrawal [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Alprazolam is excreted in breast milk. It may cause lethargy and weight loss in infants. Use is generally not recommended while breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly and Debilitated Patients:** Increased sensitivity to sedative effects, giddiness, and ataxia; high risk of falls. Initial dosing should be limited to 0.5 mg to 0.75 mg daily in divided doses [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; dose reduction may be necessary [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; dose reduction may be necessary [Australian].
- **Ethnicity:** Peak concentrations and half-life are approximately 15% and 25% higher, respectively, in Asians compared to Caucasians [Australian].
- **Smoking:** Alprazolam concentrations may be reduced by up to 50% in smokers [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Acts as a selective depressant within the central nervous system. Similar to other benzodiazepines, it exhibits anxiolytic, muscle relaxant, and anticonvulsant properties [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics [Australian]:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed with nearly complete bioavailability. Peak levels vary 2- to 3-fold between individuals.
    - **Distribution:** 80% bound to human serum protein (primarily albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized via CYP3A4 to α-hydroxyalprazolam and 4-hydroxyalprazolam (both have low clinical activity).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal excretion. Mean half-life is 10–14 hours (range 6–25 hours). Steady state is reached within 7 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Panic Disorder:** An 8-week comparative study of 543 patients demonstrated that alprazolam was significantly more effective than placebo in reducing panic attacks. By week 4, 46.8% of patients were attack-free compared to 27.1% on placebo. Long-term efficacy has been observed for up to 8 months [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Bottles:** Store below 30°C.
- **Blister Packs:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
