# Editorial Product Information: Apomorphine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for full prescribing details and legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Apomorphine hydrochloride hemihydrate.
- **Chemical Class:** Directly acting dopamine receptor agonist (aporphine derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Apomine** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N04BC07.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 100 mg/20 mL (5 mg/mL) sterile solution for infusion in a clear glass vial [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) infusion only. **Contraindicated for intravenous (IV) use.**
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** InterPharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Parkinson’s Disease:** Indicated for the reduction of the number and severity of "off" phases in patients with Parkinson's disease who are severely disabled by motor fluctuations refractory to conventional therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initiation:** Must be undertaken in a specialist hospital unit. Patients must be established on the antiemetic **domperidone** (usually 20 mg three times daily) for at least 48–72 hours prior to the first dose [Australian].
- **Titration (Threshold Dose Determination):**
    - Start with a 1.5 mg SC bolus; observe for 30 minutes.
    - If response is inadequate, administer 3 mg SC 40 minutes later.
    - Increase in increments (5 mg, 7 mg) every 40 minutes until an unequivocal motor response is achieved.
    - Maximum single bolus dose during titration is 10 mg [Australian].
- **Continuous Infusion:** Once a threshold dose is established, continuous SC infusion is administered via a portable syringe driver.
    - **Initial rate:** 1 mg/hour.
    - **Maintenance range:** Typically 1.25–5.5 mg/hour (0.02–0.08 mg/kg/hour). Most patients require 2–4 mg/hour [Australian].
    - **Maximum daily dose:** Generally should not exceed 200 mg/day [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Waking Hours Only:** Infusions should typically run during waking hours. 24-hour infusions are not advised unless the patient has severe night-time symptoms. A treatment-free period of at least 4 hours overnight is recommended to avoid tolerance [Australian].
- **Site Rotation:** The infusion site must be changed every 24 hours to minimize local reactions [Australian].
- **Visual Inspection:** Solution must be clear, colourless to slightly yellow. **Discard if the solution has turned green** [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Concomitant 5HT3 Antagonists:** Use with ondansetron, granisetron, or palonosetron is contraindicated due to reports of profound hypotension and loss of consciousness [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to apomorphine, morphine (cross-sensitivity risk), sodium metabisulfite, or any excipients [Australian].
- **Specific Pathologies:** 
    - Respiratory or CNS depression [Australian].
    - Pre-existing neuropsychiatric problems or dementias (e.g., Alzheimer’s disease) [Australian].
    - Inadequate renal or liver function [Australian].
    - Unstable coronary vascular disease or cerebrovascular disease [Australian].
- **Clinical Response:** Patients whose "on" response to levodopa is marred by severe dyskinesia or psychotoxicity [Australian].
- **Pediatrics:** Contraindicated in children and adolescents under 18 years of age [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Local SC Effects:** Very common; includes itchy nodule lesions, induration, and erythema. Rotation of sites and potential use of ultrasound can mitigate these [Australian].
- **Neuropsychiatric Disturbances:** May exacerbate pre-existing disturbances. Patients should be monitored for impulse control disorders (e.g., pathological gambling, hypersexuality, binge eating) and Dopamine Dysregulation Syndrome (DDS) [Australian].
- **Somnolence and Sudden Sleep Onset:** Patients must be cautioned regarding driving and operating machinery [Australian].
- **Postural Hypotension:** Extra caution is required in patients with pre-existing cardiac disease or those taking antihypertensives [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** Caution in patients at risk for Torsades de pointes. Baseline and periodic ECG monitoring is advisable [Australian].
- **Hemolytic Anemia:** Use with levodopa may cause Coombs’ positive hemolytic anemia. Screen prior to treatment and every 6 months [Australian].
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** Contains sodium metabisulfite, which may cause anaphylactic or asthmatic reactions in susceptible individuals [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reactions (nodules, induration, erythema), Hallucinations [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea/vomiting (prevalent but tolerance develops), transient sedation, yawning, neuropsychiatric disturbances (confusion, visual hallucinations) [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Dyskinesias during "on" periods, sudden sleep onset, postural hypotension (usually transient), breathing difficulties, rashes, injection site necrosis/ulceration, hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia [Australian]. |
| **Rare (0.01–0.1%)** | Peripheral eosinophilia (reversible) [Australian]. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis (due to metabisulfites), profound hypotension (if used with 5HT3 antagonists), and thrombus formation/pulmonary embolism if administered intravenously (IV) in error [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **5HT3 Antagonists (e.g., Ondansetron):** **Contraindicated.** Leads to severe hypotension and loss of consciousness [Australian].
- **Dopamine Antagonists:** Avoid drugs that interfere with central amine mechanisms (e.g., metoclopramide, antipsychotics like phenothiazines or butyrophenones) as they may antagonise effect [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Apomorphine may potentiate the hypotensive effects of cardiac and antihypertensive medications [Australian].
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Avoid concomitant use with medications known to prolong the QT interval [Australian].
- **Clozapine:** Potential interaction may exist; use with caution [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Safety is not established; should not be used in women who are pregnant or likely to become pregnant. Animal studies show decreased fertility in males and potential fetal damage [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if apomorphine is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; contraindicated for those under 18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Well-represented in clinical trials. No specific dose adjustment is required other than standard individual titration, though extra caution is advised due to the higher risk of postural hypotension [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in cases of "inadequate" renal function [Australian]. Severe renal insufficiency requires reducing the dosing frequency of the necessary co-prescribed domperidone [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in cases of "inadequate" liver function [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Directly acting dopamine receptor agonist. Antiparkinsonian effects are primarily due to stimulation of postsynaptic D2 receptors, though D4, D5, and alpha-adrenergic/serotonergic antagonism may contribute [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** High affinity for D4 and D5; moderate affinity for D2, D3, alpha-1D, alpha-2B, alpha-2C, and various 5HT receptors. No affinity for beta-adrenergic or H1 receptors [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and completely absorbed from SC tissue. Peak plasma levels in ~3 minutes.
    - **Distribution:** Highly lipid-soluble; brain concentrations are up to 8 times higher than plasma. Distribution half-life is 5 minutes.
    - **Metabolism:** Hepatic; routes include sulfation, N-demethylation, and glucuronidation via CYP 2B6, 2C8, and 3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Rapidly cleared; elimination half-life is approximately 33 minutes [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C. Do not refrigerate or freeze [Australian].
- **Protection:** Protect from light; keep the vial in the outer carton [Australian].
- **In-use Stability:** After opening the vial, chemical and physical stability is maintained for 7 days at <25°C. However, because it contains no antimicrobial agent, it should be used within 24 hours of opening [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (S4)** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
