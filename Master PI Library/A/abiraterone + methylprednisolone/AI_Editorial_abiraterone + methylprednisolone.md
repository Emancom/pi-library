# Editorial Product Information: abiraterone + methylprednisolone

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **abiraterone + methylprednisolone**
- Chemical class:
  - Abiraterone (as abiraterone acetate): selective inhibitor of CYP17 (17α‑hydroxylase/17,20‑lyase) — androgen biosynthesis inhibitor / steroidogenesis inhibitor.
  - Methylprednisolone: synthetic glucocorticoid (systemic corticosteroid).
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of patients with:
  - Newly diagnosed high‑risk metastatic hormone sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) in combination with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT).
  - Metastatic castration‑resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) who are asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic after failure of ADT.
  - mCRPC in patients who have received prior chemotherapy containing a taxane.
- Jurisdictional note: Indications above are those provided in available sources. If an indication differs by region it is not specified in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- **Abiraterone**: Recommended dose is 500 mg once daily (administered as four 125 mg tablets in the source formulation).
- **Methylprednisolone**:
  - For metastatic hormone sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC): 4 mg once daily.
  - For metastatic castration resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC): 4 mg twice daily.

### Dosing by indication
- mHSPC: Abiraterone 500 mg once daily + methylprednisolone 4 mg once daily, given with ADT.
- mCRPC (asymptomatic/mildly symptomatic after ADT failure): Abiraterone 500 mg once daily + methylprednisolone 4 mg twice daily.
- mCRPC post‑taxane chemotherapy: Abiraterone 500 mg once daily + methylprednisolone 4 mg twice daily.

### Re‑treatment and dose modification for hepatotoxicity
- If ALT or AST rise above 5× upper limit of normal (ULN) or bilirubin rises above 3× ULN: withhold abiraterone immediately and monitor liver function.
- Re‑treatment after return of liver function tests to baseline may be given at a reduced abiraterone dose of 250 mg once daily.
- If hepatotoxicity recurs at 250 mg once daily, discontinue abiraterone permanently.
- If ALT or AST reach 20× ULN at any time while on therapy, discontinue abiraterone and do not re‑treat.

### Administration instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed whole with water. Do not crush or chew tablets.
- Be aware that specific abiraterone tablet formulations may differ in food‑effect and dosing from other abiraterone products; follow the specific product instructions in the local region.
- Patients started on abiraterone + methylprednisolone who are receiving an LHRH (GnRH) agonist should continue LHRH therapy.

### Missed dose and titration instructions
- Specific instructions for missed doses: Not specified in available sources.
- General titration schedules beyond the hepatotoxicity re‑treatment guidance above: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- **Abiraterone**:
  - Known hypersensitivity to abiraterone acetate or any excipient in the formulation.
  - Women who are or may potentially be pregnant (contraindicated).
  - Severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh Class C).
  - Concomitant use with radium‑223 dichloride (radium‑223) — combination not recommended due to increased mortality and fractures observed in a clinical trial.
- **Methylprednisolone**:
  - Known hypersensitivity to methylprednisolone or any excipient in the formulation.
- Excipients with known effects:
  - Product contains lactose and (in some formulations) sulfites — contraindicated or should be avoided in patients with known lactose intolerance, rare hereditary galactose intolerance, total lactase deficiency, glucose‑galactose malabsorption, or sulfite sensitivity as appropriate.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Mineralocorticoid excess (abiraterone)**:
  - Abiraterone causes increased mineralocorticoid levels through CYP17 inhibition and can result in hypertension, hypokalaemia and fluid retention.
  - Co‑administration of a corticosteroid (methylprednisolone) suppresses ACTH and reduces the incidence and severity of these reactions.
  - Use abiraterone with caution in patients with underlying cardiovascular disease; safety not established in patients with LVEF <50% or NYHA Class III–IV heart failure in some trials.
  - Monitor blood pressure, serum potassium and for fluid retention at least monthly.
  - Post‑marketing reports include QT prolongation and Torsades de Pointes in patients who develop hypokalaemia or have underlying cardiovascular conditions.

- **Hepatotoxicity (abiraterone)**:
  - Marked increases in liver enzymes occurred in controlled studies; very rarely fulminant hepatitis and hepatic failure have been reported.
  - Measure serum transaminases (ALT, AST) and bilirubin prior to starting therapy, every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then monthly thereafter.
  - Interrupt treatment immediately for ALT/AST >5× ULN or bilirubin >3× ULN; follow re‑treatment guidance in Section 4.
  - Abiraterone should be used cautiously in patients with moderate hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh B); not to be used in severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C).

- **Glycaemic effects**:
  - Hypoglycaemia may occur when abiraterone + methylprednisolone is given to diabetic patients receiving pioglitazone or repaglinide — monitor blood glucose closely.
  - Methylprednisolone may worsen hyperglycaemia; monitor blood glucose in diabetic patients.

- **Concomitant therapies**:
  - Safety and efficacy of concomitant cytotoxic chemotherapy with abiraterone not established.
  - Concomitant use of radium‑223 with abiraterone plus a corticosteroid increased mortality and fractures in a randomised trial; radium‑223 is not recommended in combination. It is recommended that subsequent treatment with radium‑223 is not initiated for at least 5 days after the last administration of abiraterone + methylprednisolone.

- **Corticosteroid‑specific precautions (methylprednisolone)**:
  - Abrupt withdrawal may precipitate adrenal insufficiency; monitor when tapering or withdrawing.
  - In stress situations, increased corticosteroid dosing may be required.
  - Immunosuppression: increased risk of new, worsening, disseminated, or reactivated infections (viral, bacterial, fungal, protozoal, helminthic); monitor for infection, consider dose reduction/withdrawal as needed.
  - Screen for latent tuberculosis before prolonged corticosteroid therapy and consider chemoprophylaxis where indicated.
  - Varicella or measles exposure may warrant prophylaxis (VZIG or IG) in non‑immune corticosteroid‑treated patients; active disease may require antiviral therapy.
  - Hepatitis B reactivation has occurred with immunosuppressive corticosteroid doses—screen and consult specialists for carriers.
  - Avoid corticosteroids in systemic fungal infections unless needed to control drug reactions.
  - Use with great caution in patients with known or suspected Strongyloides infection; corticosteroid‑induced immunosuppression may precipitate hyperinfection.
  - Avoid in cerebral malaria.
  - Live vaccines are contraindicated during immunosuppressive corticosteroid therapy; inactivated vaccines may be less effective.

- **Cardiovascular risks (corticosteroids and abiraterone-related effects)**:
  - Corticosteroids may worsen hypertension, dyslipidaemia and increase thromboembolic risk.
  - Use with caution in congestive heart failure; systemic corticosteroids are not recommended in patients with congestive heart failure.
  - Particular caution after recent myocardial infarction; rare cases of myocardial rupture reported after corticosteroid use.
  - Careful monitoring when used with cardioactive drugs (e.g., digoxin) due to steroid‑induced electrolyte disturbances.

- **Endocrine and metabolic effects**:
  - Corticosteroids can induce or worsen Cushing's syndrome, exacerbate hypothyroidism effects on corticosteroid action, and precipitate thyrotoxic periodic paralysis in susceptible hyperthyroid patients with steroid‑induced hypokalaemia.
  - Pheochromocytoma crisis has been reported with systemic corticosteroid administration.

- **Ocular effects**:
  - Prolonged corticosteroid use may cause posterior subcapsular cataracts, increased intraocular pressure/glaucoma, and may enhance secondary ocular infections.
  - Central serous chorioretinopathy (which can lead to retinal detachment) has been associated with systemic corticosteroids — refer to ophthalmology for visual symptoms.

- **Psychiatric effects**:
  - Corticosteroids may cause psychiatric disturbances ranging from mood changes to severe depression and psychosis; onset typically within days to weeks; monitor and treat as required.

- **Gastrointestinal effects**:
  - Corticosteroids may mask signs of peptic ulceration, peritonitis or pancreatitis; use with caution in patients at risk and with NSAIDs.

- **Musculoskeletal effects**:
  - High‑dose corticosteroids may cause acute myopathy (may involve respiratory muscles), rhabdomyolysis, and chronic use increases risk of osteoporosis.
  - Tumour lysis syndrome has been reported in post‑marketing experience following systemic corticosteroid use alone or with cytotoxic therapy.

- **Renal effects**:
  - Increased risk of scleroderma renal crisis in systemic sclerosis patients receiving corticosteroids.

### Monitoring requirements
- Liver function (ALT, AST, bilirubin): baseline, every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then monthly.
- Blood pressure, serum potassium, and assessment for fluid retention: at least monthly.
- Blood glucose: monitor frequently in patients with diabetes and when combined with hypoglycaemia‑inducing antidiabetic agents (e.g., pioglitazone, repaglinide).
- Screen for hepatitis B prior to prolonged corticosteroid therapy; screen for latent TB where relevant.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of infection, psychiatric disturbances, visual changes, muscle weakness, and signs of adrenal insufficiency when tapering corticosteroids.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Correct hypertension and hypokalaemia prior to starting abiraterone.
- Do not co‑administer radium‑223 with abiraterone + methylprednisolone; if radium‑223 is to be started after abiraterone, allow a minimum interval of 5 days after the last dose of abiraterone.
- Use caution in moderate hepatic impairment; do not use in severe hepatic impairment.
- No dosage adjustment required for renal impairment for abiraterone per available information, but methylprednisolone use in renal impairment requires caution.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions (abiraterone)
- In vitro data indicate abiraterone acetate is:
  - Strong inhibitor of CYP1A2, CYP2D6 and CYP2C8.
  - Moderate inhibitor of CYP2C9, CYP2C19 and CYP3A4/5.
  - Inhibitor of OATP1B1 (clinical relevance unclear).
- Major abiraterone metabolites (abiraterone sulphate and N‑oxide abiraterone sulphate) are strong inhibitors of CYP2C8 in vitro.
- Clinical interaction example:
  - Abiraterone (with a corticosteroid coadministered in the clinical study cited) increased systemic exposure (AUC) of the CYP2D6 substrate dextromethorphan by ~200% and increased AUC24 of dextrorphan (active metabolite) by ~33%.
  - Clinical implication: Use caution and consider dose reduction when abiraterone is administered with drugs that are substrates of CYP2D6, particularly narrow therapeutic index drugs metabolised by CYP2D6.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions and clinically significant interactions
- Hypoglycaemia: Concomitant use of abiraterone + methylprednisolone with pioglitazone or repaglinide in patients with diabetes has been associated with hypoglycaemia — monitor blood glucose and adjust antidiabetic therapy as needed.
- Radium‑223 dichloride: Combination with abiraterone plus a corticosteroid increased mortality and fractures in a randomised trial — radium‑223 is not recommended in combination. If radium‑223 is to be used after abiraterone, delay initiation for at least 5 days after the last abiraterone dose.
- Potential for increased cardiac adverse effects if abiraterone induces hypokalaemia — caution when combined with drugs that predispose to QT prolongation.
- Corticosteroid interactions:
  - Corticosteroids increase infection risk and may interact with live vaccines (live vaccines contraindicated during immunosuppressive corticosteroid dosing).
  - Concomitant amphotericin B and hydrocortisone (reported) followed by cardiac enlargement and congestive heart failure — exercise caution with potassium‑depleting agents and cardioactive drugs (e.g., digoxin).
  - NSAIDs: concurrent use with corticosteroids may increase risk of gastrointestinal ulceration/bleeding.

### Management notes
- Review and adjust doses of concomitant medications metabolised by CYP2D6 (and potentially other CYPs inhibited by abiraterone) when coadministered with abiraterone.
- Monitor electrolytes (especially potassium) and ECG in patients at risk for QT prolongation.
- Screen for hepatitis B before prolonged corticosteroid therapy; monitor and manage per specialist advice.
- Avoid live vaccines in patients receiving immunosuppressive corticosteroid doses.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric use**:
  - This medicine is not for use in children. No paediatric dosing data available in the sources.
- **Elderly**:
  - Caution recommended with prolonged corticosteroid treatment in the elderly due to increased risks (osteoporosis, diabetes, infection susceptibility, skin thinning, fluid retention and hypertension). No specific dose adjustment provided.
- **Renal impairment**:
  - Abiraterone: No dosage adjustment necessary for renal impairment per available information.
  - Methylprednisolone: Use with caution in patients with renal insufficiency; specific dosing adjustments not provided.
- **Hepatic impairment**:
  - Abiraterone:
    - No dosage adjustment necessary for pre‑existing mild hepatic impairment.
    - No clinical data for moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh B or C); use with caution in moderate impairment only if benefit clearly outweighs risk.
    - Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C).
  - Re‑treatment and dose reduction rules for hepatotoxicity are given in Section 4.
- **Cardiac impairment**:
  - Safety of abiraterone has not been established in patients with LVEF <50% or NYHA Class III–IV heart failure in certain studies; use caution in patients with significant cardiac disease.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Abiraterone**: selective inhibitor of CYP17 (17α‑hydroxylase/17,20‑lyase), blocking androgen biosynthesis in adrenal glands, testes and tumour tissue; inhibition of CYP17 decreases glucocorticoid production and increases ACTH, which can lead to increased mineralocorticoid levels (hypertension, hypokalaemia, fluid retention).
- **Methylprednisolone**: synthetic glucocorticoid that provides glucocorticoid activity, suppresses ACTH, reduces mineralocorticoid excess induced by abiraterone, and exerts anti‑inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Specific numeric parameters (Cmax, Tmax, bioavailability) are Not specified in available sources.
  - The abiraterone tablet formulation described in the source is stated to be taken with or without food; note that food‑effect and dosing may differ between abiraterone formulations — follow local product instructions.
- Distribution, metabolism, elimination:
  - Detailed distribution, metabolism and elimination parameters (e.g., protein binding, half‑life, clearance routes) are Not specified in available sources.
  - In vitro metabolism: abiraterone and its metabolites inhibit multiple CYP enzymes (see Section 8).
- Pharmacodynamic effects:
  - Reduction in androgen production results in decreased tumour androgen stimulation but may provoke mineralocorticoid excess without concomitant corticosteroid.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key trial findings described in available sources:
  - Randomised trial evidence indicated that addition of radium‑223 dichloride to abiraterone plus a corticosteroid in patients with asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic bone‑predominant mCRPC increased mortality and fracture rate; consequently, radium‑223 is not recommended in combination with abiraterone + methylprednisolone. It is recommended to delay radium‑223 initiation for at least 5 days after the last administration of abiraterone + methylprednisolone if sequential therapy is considered.
  - Safety in certain cardiac populations: safety of abiraterone was not established in patients with LVEF <50% or NYHA Class III–IV heart failure in some clinical studies cited in the sources.
