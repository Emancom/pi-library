# Editorial Product Information: **Aciclovir** (Intravenous)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Aciclovir** (as aciclovir sodium).
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic acyclic purine nucleoside analogue.
- **Brand Names:** Aciclovir Accord [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection/infusion containing 25 mg/mL of **aciclovir**. Available in 250 mg/10 mL, 500 mg/20 mL, and 1000 mg/40 mL vials [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only. 
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Accord Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
The following indications are approved [Australian]:
- Resolution of acute clinical manifestations of mucocutaneous *Herpes simplex* virus (HSV) infections in patients who are immunocompromised.
- Management of severe first-episode genital herpes (primary or non-primary) in immunocompetent patients.
- Management of acute manifestations of *Varicella zoster* virus (VZV) infection in patients who are immunocompromised.
- Treatment of shingles (*Varicella zoster*) in immunocompetent patients exhibiting very severe local or systemic symptoms, particularly if initiated within 72 hours of rash onset.
- Treatment of *Herpes simplex* encephalitis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Dosage is determined by the patient's immune status and the specific infection [Australian]:
- **HSV Infections (Normal or Immunocompromised):** 5 mg/kg every 8 hours.
- **Severe Herpes Zoster (Immunocompetent):** 5 mg/kg every 8 hours.
- **VZV Infection (Immunocompromised):** 10 mg/kg every 8 hours.
- **HSV Encephalitis (Normal or Immunocompromised):** 10 mg/kg every 8 hours.

### Administration Instructions
- **Strict Requirement:** Must be administered by **slow intravenous infusion over at least one hour**. Rapid, bolus, intramuscular, or subcutaneous administration must be avoided to prevent renal damage [Australian].
- **Hydration:** Patients must maintain adequate hydration. High urine flow should be established, especially during the first few hours post-infusion [Australian].
- **Obese Patients:** Dosing based on actual body weight may lead to elevated plasma concentrations; consider dose reduction in this population [Australian].
- **Preparation:** The solution has a pH of approximately 11.0. For infusion, dilute to a concentration not exceeding 5 mg/mL (0.5% w/v). Compatible fluids include 0.9% or 0.45% Sodium Chloride, 5% Glucose, and Lactated Ringers Solution [Australian].

### Duration of Treatment
- Typically 5 to 7 days for most infections.
- At least 10 days for HSV encephalitis [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **aciclovir**, valaciclovir, or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Renal Tubular Damage:** Precipitation of **aciclovir** crystals in renal tubules can occur if the maximum solubility of free **aciclovir** (2.5 mg/mL at 37°C) is exceeded. This risk is mitigated by slow infusion and adequate hydration [Australian].
- **Neurological Effects:** Reversible encephalopathic changes (e.g., confusion, tremors, hallucinations, seizures, coma) have been reported. Use with caution in patients with pre-existing neurological abnormalities, hypoxia, or significant renal/hepatic/electrolyte disturbances [Australian].
- **Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP)/Haemolytic Uraemic Syndrome (HUS):** Cases, including fatalities, have occurred in immunocompromised patients [Australian].
- **Viral Resistance:** Development of resistant HSV strains should be considered, particularly in severely immunocompromised patients receiving prolonged or repeated courses [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Closely monitor renal function, especially when using high doses or co-administering other nephrotoxic agents [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequency categories are estimated as follows [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (≥1/100 to <1/10) | Nausea, vomiting, phlebitis, pruritus, urticaria, rashes (including photosensitivity), increases in blood urea and creatinine. |
| **Uncommon** (≥1/1,000 to <1/100) | Decreases in haematological indices (anaemia, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia). |
| **Rare** (≥1/10,000 to <1/1,000) | Anaphylaxis, dyspnoea, reversible increases in bilirubin and liver enzymes, angioedema. |
| **Very Rare** (<1/10,000) | Headache, dizziness, agitation, tremor, ataxia, dysarthria, hallucinations, psychotic symptoms, convulsions, somnolence, encephalopathy, coma, neutropenia, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, jaundice, hepatitis, renal impairment, acute renal failure, renal pain, fatigue, fever, local inflammatory reactions. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Encephalopathy, TTP/HUS in immunocompromised patients, and Stevens-Johnson Syndrome/Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid/Cimetidine:** Increase the AUC and reduce the renal clearance of **aciclovir**. No dose adjustment is generally required due to the wide therapeutic index [Australian].
- **Mycophenolate Mofetil:** Co-administration increases plasma AUCs for both **aciclovir** and the inactive metabolite of mycophenolate [Australian].
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Use with caution when combined with drugs affecting renal physiology (e.g., ciclosporin, tacrolimus) due to additive nephrotoxicity risk [Australian].
- **Lithium:** High-dose IV **aciclovir** may increase the risk of lithium toxicity; serum lithium levels must be monitored [Australian].
- **Theophylline:** **Aciclovir** may increase theophylline AUC by approximately 50%. Monitoring of theophylline plasma levels is recommended [Australian].
- **Zidovudine:** One report noted profound lethargy with concomitant use, though most patients show no significant increase in toxicity [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3**. Animal studies show **aciclovir** crosses the placenta. While a post-marketing registry has not shown an increase in birth defects compared to the general population, use should only be considered when potential benefits outweigh hazards [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Aciclovir** is excreted in human milk. Administration to nursing mothers should only occur if benefits to the mother outweigh risks to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of clinical effects on human sperm count, morphology, or motility at oral doses up to 1 g/day for 6 months [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use (1 to 12 years):** Dosing is based on Body Surface Area (BSA). HSV infections (excluding encephalitis) and VZV: 250 mg/m² every 8 hours. VZV (immunocompromised) and HSV encephalitis: 500 mg/m² every 8 hours [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Increased risk of neurological side effects due to age-related decline in renal function. Monitor hydration and creatinine clearance; dose reduction may be necessary [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Adjust dose or frequency based on Creatinine Clearance (CrCl) [Australian]:
    - **CrCl 25–50 mL/min:** Recommended dose every 12 hours.
    - **CrCl 10–25 mL/min:** Recommended dose every 24 hours.
    - **CrCl 0 (anuric)–10 mL/min:** Half of the recommended dose every 24 hours and after dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though caution is advised in patients with serious hepatic abnormalities [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Aciclovir** is converted to **aciclovir** monophosphate by viral-coded thymidine kinase, and subsequently to the active triphosphate form by cellular enzymes. The triphosphate acts as a substrate for and inhibitor of viral DNA polymerase, terminating viral DNA synthesis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Low plasma protein binding (9% to 33%) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily excreted unchanged; the major metabolite is 9-carboxymethoxymethylguanine (10-15% of the dose) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via renal glomerular filtration and tubular secretion. Terminal half-life is ~2.9 hours in adults, ~4 hours in neonates (0-3 months), and ~19.5 hours in chronic renal failure [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- **Do not refrigerate or freeze**, as this causes precipitation of crystals that may not redissolve [Australian].
