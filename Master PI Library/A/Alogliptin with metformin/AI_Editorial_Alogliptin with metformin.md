# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)** via Baxter Healthcare Corporation. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (also known as L-carnitine).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is **50 mg/kg** administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h).
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].

- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustments:** Dosing should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on clinical response and levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- **Baseline:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating therapy.
- **Routine:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of plasma carnitine levels, blood chemistries, and vital signs is recommended.
- **Target Range:** Free carnitine plasma concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management. The risks and benefits of re-administration must be carefully weighed [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients both with and without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been reported [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** While the injection is indicated for ESRD, the safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine in renal impairment has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology (ESRD or metabolic disease). Frequencies below are derived from clinical trials in ESRD patients [US].

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].
- **Other:** A "fishy" body odor has been reported with levocarnitine use [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned in provided source; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. While studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk after administration, the risk to the infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). Clinical trials in ESRD populations included paediatric patients, though specific age-stratified data was not highlighted [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not provided in the source; however, caution is generally advised due to the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). The intravenous route avoids the accumulation of metabolites associated with the oral route in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are then eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trough Levels:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly increased pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels. While carnitine concentrations increase, the definitive effect on clinical outcomes (malaise, muscle weakness) in the ESRD population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not applicable for US jurisdiction; generally classified as a prescription-only medicine.
