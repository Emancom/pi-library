# Editorial Product Information: Aluminum Acetate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) and labeling provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory and clinical data into a single, concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Aluminum acetate (0.2%)
- **Chemical Class:** Astringent [US]
- **Brand Name(s):** **Dermeleve Anti Itch Scalp Serum** [US]
- **ATC Code:** D02AB (Aluminum-containing preparations, suggested classification)
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Topical solution: 0.2 g aluminum acetate per 100 mL (0.2%) [US]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical (Scalp)
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Advanced Derm Solutions LLC [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- Relief of scalp itch [US]
- Relief of rashes caused by external irritants, including soaps, detergents, or cosmetics [US]
- Clinical labeling indicates utility in relieving scalp itch associated with:
    - Psoriasis [US]
    - Dermatitis [US]
    - Eczema [US]
    - Neuropathic itch [US]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children $\ge$ 2 years)
- Apply the solution to the affected area as needed or as directed by a healthcare professional [US].
- If using after bathing, the hair should be dried first prior to application [US].

### Administration Instructions
- The product features a flow-control cap; twist to open.
- Apply the serum directly to the scalp and rub in if necessary [US].
- Application is recommended at the first sign of itching [US].

### Pediatrics
- **Children $\ge$ 2 years:** Follow adult dosing [US].
- **Children < 2 years:** Consult a physician before use [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Application to open wounds or broken skin [US].
- Hypersensitivity to aluminum acetate or any of the inactive ingredients (see Section 1).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Precautions:** For external use only [US].
- **Ocular Exposure:** Avoid contact with the eyes [US].
- **Duration of Use:** Patients should discontinue use and consult a physician if the condition worsens or if symptoms persist for more than seven days [US].
- **Safety:** Keep out of reach of children [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for use in children aged 2 years and older. Use in children under 2 years requires medical consultation [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources. 
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, systemic absorption of topical aluminum acetate 0.2% is generally considered minimal.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Aluminum acetate acts as an astringent, which precipitates proteins and constricts tissues, thereby reducing inflammation and oozing while providing relief from itching [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The proprietary formulation (SrX-38™) includes Strontium and Calcium salts along with Niacinamide (Vitamin B3), which are intended to reduce irritation and enhance the skin's natural defenses against inflammation cycles [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available sources. Topical astringents typically act locally with negligible systemic absorption.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Source documentation refers to the formulation being "clinically shown" to reduce irritation and itch, though specific pivotal trial data and primary outcomes are not detailed in the provided labeling [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20-25°C (68-77°F) [US]. 
- **Secondary Reference:** Labeling also notes to store below 24°C (70°F) in specific sections [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Revised date of labeling is October 2025 [US].
