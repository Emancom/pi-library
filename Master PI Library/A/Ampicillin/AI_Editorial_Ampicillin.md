# Editorial Product Information: Ampicillin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Austrapen (ampicillin sodium)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing decisions. [US, European, Canadian, Japanese, and UK sources were not provided for this synthesis and are therefore not represented.]

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ampicillin (as sodium)
- **Chemical Class:** Aminopenicillin; beta-lactam antibiotic
- **Brand Names:** Austrapen [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** J01CA01
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Powder for injection, 1000 mg per vial [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection, slow intravenous (IV) injection, or IV infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd trading as Viatris [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
Ampicillin is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by susceptible strains of Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms [Australian]:
- Respiratory tract infections
- Chronic bronchitis
- Urinary tract infections (UTI)
- Gastrointestinal tract infections
- Bacterial meningitis
- Septicaemia
- Intraperitoneal, intrapleural, and intra-articular infections (via specific local administration) [Australian]

*Note: Bacteriological studies should be performed to determine organism sensitivity.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Respiratory Tract Infections:** 250 mg to 500 mg every six hours.
- **Chronic Bronchitis:** 500 mg every six hours (High dose: 1 g every six hours).
- **Urinary Tract Infections:** 500 mg every six hours.
- **Gastrointestinal Tract Infections:** 500 mg to 750 mg every six hours.
- **Bacterial Meningitis and Septicaemia:** 200 mg/kg/day in equally divided doses, every four to six hours intravenously (Maximum: 12 g daily).

### Paediatric Dosing
For children weighing more than 20 kg, adult dosing should be followed [Australian].
- **Respiratory Tract:** 25 to 50 mg/kg/day in divided doses every six hours.
- **Urinary Tract:** 50 mg/kg/day in divided doses every six hours.
- **Gastrointestinal:** 50 to 70 mg/kg/day in divided doses every six hours.

### Administration Instructions
- **IM Injection:** Reconstitute 1 g with 1.5 mL Water for Injections.
- **Direct IV Injection:** Reconstitute in 10 to 20 mL Water for Injections; inject **SLOWLY** over 3 to 5 minutes. Rapid administration may result in convulsive seizures [Australian].
- **IV Infusion:** Reconstitute as for IM, then dilute further in compatible IV fluid. Administer as a rapid infusion over 30 to 40 minutes.
- **Local Administration:** 500 mg daily for intraperitoneal, intrapleural, or intra-articular use. **Intrathecal use is not recommended.**

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to beta-lactam antibiotics (e.g., penicillins, cephalosporins).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue immediately if SCAR is suspected.
- **Anaphylaxis:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity reactions have occurred, more frequently with parenteral therapy. Immediate emergency treatment with adrenaline is required for anaphylactoid reactions.
- **Pseudomembranous Colitis:** *Clostridium difficile*-associated diarrhoea ranges from mild to life-threatening. Avoid drugs that delay peristalsis (e.g., opiates).
- **Coring:** To avoid rubber stopper fragments (coring), use a 21G (0.8 mm) needle for reconstitution. Slice the bung with the bevelled edge facing upwards rather than using a stabbing motion [Australian].
- **Mononucleosis and Leukaemia:** High incidence of skin rash occurs if ampicillin is administered to patients with infectious mononucleosis or lymphatic leukaemia.
- **Sodium Content:** Contains 66 mg (2.9 mmol) of sodium per 1 g vial.
- **Crystalluria:** Maintain high urinary output to prevent ampicillin precipitation, especially in patients with indwelling catheters.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>10%)** | Rash (the most common reaction) [Australian] |
| **Frequent (1-10%)** | Erythematous maculopapular rash, exanthema (especially in mononucleosis/leukaemia) |
| **Occasional/Uncommon** | Urticaria, erythema multiforme, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, glossitis, stomatitis |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN, hepatitis, cholestatic jaundice, nephropathy, exfoliative dermatitis |

- **Haematological:** Anaemia, thrombocytopenia, eosinophilia, and agranulocytosis (usually reversible upon discontinuation).
- **Neurological:** Encephalopathy may occur at high blood levels (800 mg/L) or in patients with renal failure/meningitis. Symptoms include convulsions, hyper-reflexia, and coma.
- **Local:** Pain at IM site; phlebitis at IV site.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** Reduces renal tubular secretion, leading to increased and prolonged ampicillin blood levels.
- **Bacteriostatic Antibiotics:** Tetracyclines, erythromycin, and chloramphenicol may antagonise the bactericidal action of ampicillin.
- **Gentamicin:** Inactivation occurs if mixed in the same parenteral solution; do not mix [Australian].
- **Allopurinol:** Substantially increases the incidence of skin rashes.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** May reduce the effectiveness of hormonal contraceptives.
- **Methotrexate:** Ampicillin may inhibit renal clearance, increasing the risk of methotrexate toxicity.
- **Laboratory Tests:** High urine concentrations may cause false-positive glucose reactions with Clinitest or Benedict's solution. Enzymatic glucose oxidase tests (e.g., Testape) should be used instead.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category A** [Australian]. Ampicillin diffuses across the placenta. It has been used extensively for over 30 years without evidence of untoward effects. 
- **Labour/Delivery:** Animal studies show decreased uterine tone; the effect on human labour/delivery is unknown.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in breast milk. Alternative feeding is recommended to avoid potential sensitisation of the newborn [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric Use
- **Neonatal Dosage:** Half-life varies inversely with age. Recommended dose is 25 mg/kg (50 mg/kg for meningitis) at the following intervals:
    - < 2000 g and 0-7 days: every 12 hours.
    - < 2000 g and > 7 days: every 8 hours.
    - \> 2000 g and 0-7 days: every 8 hours.
    - \> 2000 g and > 7 days: every 6 hours.

### Renal Impairment
Excretion is delayed. Reduce total daily dosage based on GFR:
- **GFR 10 to 50 mL/min:** Normal unit dose every 6 to 12 hours.
- **GFR < 10 mL/min:** Normal unit dose every 12 to 16 hours.

### Hepatic Impairment
- Periodic assessment of hepatic function is advised during prolonged therapy [Australian]. No specific dose adjustments provided in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis during active multiplication.
- **Microbiology:** Active against susceptible *Streptococcus pyogenes*, *S. pneumoniae*, *S. viridans*, *Enterococcus faecalis*, and non-penicillinase producing *Staphylococci*. Gram-negative activity includes *H. influenzae*, *N. meningitidis*, *P. mirabilis*, and *Salmonella* spp.
- **Resistance:** Ineffective against penicillinase-producing bacteria (e.g., resistant *Staphylococci*, *Pseudomonas*, *Klebsiella*, and *Serratia*).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Diffuses into most tissues; limited penetration into CSF unless meninges are inflamed. Protein binding is approximately 29%.
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal (66-73% excreted unchanged/penicilloic acid in 6 hours). Half-life is ~1 hour (increases to 20 hours in end-stage renal failure).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data provided in the available source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Dry Powder:** Store below 25°C, protected from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Ampicillin is unstable in concentrated solutions. Use immediately after reconstitution to reduce microbiological hazard.
- **IV Stability (2-8°C):** If necessary, diluted solutions in Normal Saline or Ringer's Solution remain stable (potency loss <10%) for up to 24 hours when refrigerated. Stability is significantly lower in Sodium Bicarbonate or Dextran solutions (approx. 6 hours) [Australian].
