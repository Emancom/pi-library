# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) document:
    - **United States (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI or SmPC required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name**: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism)**: For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose**: In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency**: Administer every 3 to 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Dose Range**: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration**: Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment**: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions**: The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal impairment or ESRD on dialysis have not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring**: It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs prior to and during therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity**: Anaphylaxis and respiratory distress [US].
- **Neurological**: Seizures [US].

### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in Dialysis Trials)
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients regardless of causality [US]:
- **Body as a Whole**: Abdominal pain (9%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), headache (22%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%).
- **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive**: Diarrhea (16%), nausea (8%), vomiting (15%).
- **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Neurological**: Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Transient GI effects**: Nausea and vomiting.
- **Specific**: Body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: [US: Category not specified in narrative but labeled as "use only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women exist [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess intake must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Refer to Dosage and Administration for weight-based dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment**: IV administration is indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. Oral formulations should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic/fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination**: Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. In a single IV dose study, 76% of the dose was excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Carnitine deficiency is biochemically defined as plasma free carnitine <20 µmol/L at one week post-term [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: Clinical trials demonstrated that 20 mg/kg IV three times per week after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. In a 9-week study (n=12), mean pre-dialysis trough levels increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Metabolic Outcomes**: While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility**: Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
