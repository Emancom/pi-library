## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated for the treatment of patients with:  
  - Newly diagnosed high‑risk metastatic hormone sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) in combination with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.1].  
  - Metastatic advanced prostate cancer (castration resistant prostate cancer, mCRPC) who are asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic after failure of ADT [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.1].  
  - mCRPC patients who have received prior chemotherapy containing a taxane [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.1].

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- **Abiraterone acetate**: 500 mg once daily (four 125 mg tablets) orally, administered in combination with methylprednisolone [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].  
- **Methylprednisolone**:  
  - For metastatic hormone sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC): 4 mg once daily [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].  
  - For metastatic castration resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC): 4 mg twice daily [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].

### Administration instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed whole with water; do not crush or chew [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].  
- Abiraterone tablets in this PI can be taken with or without food (see pharmacokinetics reference in PI) — note that abiraterone formulations can differ in food effect; avoid medication errors and overdose by ensuring correct product and dosing [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].  
- Patients on luteinising hormone‑releasing hormone (LHRH) agonists should continue LHRH therapy when started on this combination [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].  

### Titration / dose modification
- For hepatotoxicity: if ALT or AST >5× ULN or bilirubin >3× ULN, withhold abiraterone immediately until liver tests normalise. Re‑treatment may be considered at a reduced dose of 250 mg once daily (two 125 mg tablets) after return to baseline; if hepatotoxicity recurs at the reduced dose, discontinue permanently. If severe hepatotoxicity (ALT or AST ≥20× ULN) occurs at any time, discontinue and do not re‑treat [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4].  

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources [Source: TGA PI — no missed dose instructions in provided excerpts].

## 5. Contraindications
(All contraindications below are taken from the available source and shown with jurisdiction attribution when region‑specific.)
- **Abiraterone acetate**:  
  - Women who are or may potentially be pregnant (contraindicated) [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.3].

  - Concomitant use with radium‑223 dichloride (radium 223) — contraindicated (see warnings) [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.3].

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified as boxed/black‑box in available sources. If jurisdictions issue boxed warnings, they were not present in the provided PI excerpt [Source: TGA PI — boxed warning not shown in excerpt].

### General warnings and precautions
- **Mineralocorticoid excess** (abiraterone): May cause hypertension, hypokalaemia and fluid retention due to CYP17 inhibition leading to increased mineralocorticoid levels; use caution in patients with cardiovascular disease. Ensure pre‑existing hypertension is controlled and hypokalaemia corrected prior to initiation [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- Co‑administration of a corticosteroid (methylprednisolone) reduces ACTH drive and lowers incidence/severity of mineralocorticoid excess manifestations [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- QT prolongation and Torsades de Pointes have been reported postmarketing in patients who develop hypokalaemia or have underlying cardiovascular conditions while taking abiraterone [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- **Hepatotoxicity**: Marked increases in liver enzymes occurred in clinical studies and very rarely fulminant hepatitis and hepatic failure reported. Measure serum transaminases and bilirubin prior to starting treatment, every two weeks for the first three months, then monthly thereafter. Interrupt therapy for ALT/AST >5× ULN or bilirubin >3× ULN; severe hepatotoxicity (ALT/AST ≥20× ULN) requires permanent discontinuation [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- Hypoglycaemia risk noted when abiraterone plus methylprednisolone is administered to patients with pre‑existing diabetes receiving pioglitazone or repaglinide; monitor blood glucose [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- Safety and efficacy of concomitant cytotoxic chemotherapy with abiraterone not established [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- **Concomitant use with radium‑223 dichloride**: Addition of radium‑223 to abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone in a randomised trial increased mortality and fracture rate; radium‑223 is not recommended in combination with this regimen. If using radium‑223 after stopping this combination, wait at least 5 days following the last dose before initiating radium‑223 [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  

### Methylprednisolone‑specific warnings
- Monitor for adrenocortical insufficiency if corticosteroids withdrawn; if abiraterone continued after steroid withdrawal, monitor for mineralocorticoid excess signs [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- Hyperglycaemia possible with corticosteroid use — monitor blood glucose in patients with diabetes [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- Immunosuppression and increased risk of infection (viral, bacterial, fungal, protozoan, helminthic) with corticosteroids; may exacerbate or mask infections. Screen and monitor as clinically indicated [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- Hepatitis B reactivation can occur with immunosuppressive corticosteroid dosages — screen for hepatitis B before prolonged methylprednisolone therapy and consult specialist for carriers [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- Avoid use of live vaccines in patients receiving immunosuppressive corticosteroid doses [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- Caution in patients with congestive heart failure, recent myocardial infarction, hypertension, osteoporosis, seizure disorders, myasthenia gravis, ocular herpes simplex, and other conditions where systemic steroids are hazardous — follow monitoring recommendations in PI [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- Potential for psychiatric adverse reactions with systemic steroids; monitor and counsel patients/caregivers regarding mood changes and suicidal ideation [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- Thrombosis including venous thromboembolism has been reported with corticosteroids; use with caution in patients predisposed to thromboembolic disorders [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- Risk of Strongyloides hyperinfection with corticosteroids in patients with known or suspected infestation — use caution and consider screening or empiric therapy where appropriate [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- Tumour lysis syndrome has been reported postmarketing with systemic corticosteroids in patients with malignancies — monitor high‑risk patients [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  

### Monitoring requirements
- Liver function tests (ALT, AST, bilirubin): baseline, every 2 weeks for first 3 months, then monthly [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4].  
- Blood pressure, serum potassium and assessment for fluid retention: monthly [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4].  
- Blood glucose monitoring in patients with diabetes, particularly when combined with pioglitazone/repaglinide or due to steroid use [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- Monitor for signs of infection, psychiatric changes, ocular symptoms, muscular weakness, and other steroid‑related effects as clinically indicated [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- **CYP inhibition (in vitro):** Abiraterone acetate is a strong inhibitor of CYP1A2, CYP2D6 and CYP2C8, and a moderate inhibitor of CYP2C9, CYP2C19 and CYP3A4/5 in vitro. Major metabolites (abiraterone sulphate and N‑oxide abiraterone sulphate) are strong inhibitors of CYP2C8 in vitro. Abiraterone and metabolites inhibit OATP1B1 in vitro; clinical relevance unclear [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].  
- **Clinical interaction (CYP2D6):** Abiraterone (with prednisone in the study) increased dextromethorphan AUC ≈200% and increased AUC24 for dextrorphan ≈33%; caution with drugs metabolised by CYP2D6, particularly narrow therapeutic index substrates — consider dose reductions [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].  
- **CYP3A4 inducers:** Abiraterone is a CYP3A4 substrate. In a study with a strong CYP3A4 inducer (rifampicin) pre‑treatment, abiraterone Cmax and AUC∞ were reduced by ~55% (study used another abiraterone product) — strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampicin, rifabutin, rifapentine, phenobarbital) should be avoided or used with careful clinical evaluation [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].  

### Pharmacodynamic interactions / clinical considerations
- **Hypokalaemia risk potentiation:** Concomitant use of drugs that decrease potassium (e.g., potassium‑wasting diuretics, certain cardioactive drugs) may increase risk of adverse cardiac events (QT prolongation, arrhythmia) with abiraterone; monitor electrolytes and adjust therapy as needed [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.5].  
- **Pioglitazone / repaglinide:** Combination with abiraterone plus methylprednisolone may cause hypoglycaemia in diabetic patients on pioglitazone or repaglinide — monitor blood glucose [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- **Radium‑223:** Combination is not recommended due to increased mortality and fracture risk in a randomised trial [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- **Vaccinations:** Live vaccines contraindicated in patients receiving immunosuppressive corticosteroid doses; killed/inactivated vaccines may be less effective [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

### Management notes
- Review concomitant medications for CYP2D6 substrates with narrow therapeutic indices and consider dose adjustment or monitoring [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].  
- Avoid strong CYP3A4 inducers when possible; if unavoidable, closely evaluate clinical efficacy of abiraterone [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].  
- Monitor electrolytes (especially potassium), blood pressure, liver function and blood glucose as per monitoring recommendations [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4].

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- **Abiraterone acetate:** Not for use in children [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- **Methylprednisolone in combination:** No data available for paediatric use in combination with abiraterone acetate [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

### Elderly
- Caution recommended with prolonged corticosteroid treatment in the elderly due to increased risks (osteoporosis, diabetes, infection susceptibility, skin thinning, fluid retention causing hypertension) [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

### Renal impairment
- **Abiraterone acetate:** No dosage adjustment necessary for patients with renal impairment (per provided source) [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].  
- **Methylprednisolone:** Use with caution in patients with renal insufficiency; specific dosing adjustments not provided [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

### Hepatic impairment
- **Abiraterone acetate:**  
  - No dose adjustment necessary for mild hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh A) [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4].  
  - No clinical data for multiple doses in moderate or severe impairment (Child‑Pugh B or C); use with caution in moderate impairment only if benefit outweighs risk; do not use in severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C) — contraindicated [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.3; Section 4.4].  
  - Management of hepatotoxicity: withhold for ALT/AST >5× ULN or bilirubin >3× ULN and consider re‑treatment at reduced dose (250 mg daily) after return to baseline; discontinue if severe hepatotoxicity recurs or if ALT/AST ≥20× ULN [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4].  
- **Methylprednisolone:** Enhanced effects in patients with cirrhosis; close monitoring advised; specific dose modifications not provided [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Abiraterone acetate:** Inhibits CYP17 (17α‑hydroxylase/C17,20‑lyase), decreasing androgen biosynthesis from testicular, adrenal and intra‑tumoural sources which reduces androgen‑driven prostate cancer growth; inhibition leads to increased mineralocorticoid levels causing hypertension, hypokalaemia and fluid retention [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4; Section 5.1 referenced].  
- **Methylprednisolone:** Systemic glucocorticoid with anti‑inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects; used to suppress ACTH drive thereby reducing mineralocorticoid excess from abiraterone therapy [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption / food effect:** The formulation referenced in the source can be taken with or without food [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Section 5.2 referenced].  
- **Metabolism:** Abiraterone is a substrate of CYP3A4; abiraterone and metabolites inhibit multiple CYP enzymes (1A2, 2D6, 2C8 strongly in vitro; moderate inhibition of 2C9, 2C19, 3A4/5 in vitro). Major metabolites include abiraterone sulphate and N‑oxide abiraterone sulphate [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].  
- **Elimination:** Specific elimination (half‑life, routes, clearance) parameters are not provided in the available source excerpt [Source: TGA PI, Section 5.x not provided in excerpt].

### Pharmacodynamics
- Abiraterone reduces androgen production resulting in therapeutic effects in prostate cancer; inhibition of steroidogenesis increases mineralocorticoid precursors causing characteristic adverse effects (hypertension, hypokalaemia, fluid retention) that are mitigated by concomitant corticosteroid therapy [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4; Section 5.1 referenced].

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Safety not established in patients with left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) <50% or New York Heart Association (NYHA) Class III or IV heart failure in Study 301; similar safety not established in NYHA Class II–IV in other studies referenced (study identifiers cited in PI) [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].  
- A randomised clinical trial in asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic bone‑predominant mCRPC patients showed that adding radium‑223 dichloride to abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone increased mortality and fracture rate; this finding underlies the recommendation not to combine radium‑223 with this regimen [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
