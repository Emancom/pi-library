# Editorial Product Information: Alendronate Sodium and Colecalciferol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Alendronate sodium (a bisphosphonate) and Colecalciferol (Vitamin D3).
- **Chemical Class:** Bisphosphonate and Vitamin D analogue.
- **Brand Names:** **Alendronate Plus D3-DRLA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M05BB03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Modified capsule-shaped uncoated tablets:** 
        - 70 mg alendronic acid (as 91.37 mg alendronate sodium) / 70 mcg colecalciferol (2800 IU).
        - 70 mg alendronic acid (as 91.37 mg alendronate sodium) / 140 mcg colecalciferol (5600 IU).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr Reddy’s Laboratories (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Treatment of Osteoporosis:** Indicated for use in specific patients where vitamin D supplementation is recommended. [Australian].
- **Diagnostic Criteria [Australian]:** Prior to treatment, osteoporosis must be confirmed by either:
    - Bone mass measurement of at least 2 standard deviations below the gender-specific mean for young adults; or
    - The presence of a prior osteoporotic fracture.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One tablet (either strength) taken once weekly.
- **Administration Instructions (Critical for Safety):**
    - **Timing:** Take at least 30 minutes before the first food, beverage, or medication of the day.
    - **Fluid:** Swallow only with a full glass of plain water. Mineral water, coffee, or juice significantly reduce absorption.
    - **Posture:** Patients must remain upright (standing or sitting) for at least 30 minutes and until after their first food of the day.
    - **Restrictions:** Do not take at bedtime or before arising for the day. Do not chew or suck the tablet due to the risk of oropharyngeal ulceration.
- **Calcium/Vitamin D Supplementation:** Patients should receive supplemental calcium if dietary intake is inadequate. The 70 mcg (2800 IU) dose provides the equivalent of 400 IU/day; the 140 mcg (5600 IU) dose provides the equivalent of 800 IU/day.
- **Missed Dose:** Take one tablet the morning after the omission is remembered. Do not take two tablets on the same day. Resume the original weekly schedule on the chosen day.
- **Duration of Use:** The optimal duration of bisphosphonate therapy is not established. Periodically re-evaluate the need for continued therapy.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Oesophageal Abnormalities:** Conditions that delay emptying, such as stricture or achalasia.
- **Postural Limitations:** Inability to stand or sit upright for at least 30 minutes.
- **Hypocalcaemia:** Must be corrected before starting therapy.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to any component of the formulation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Severe Oesophageal Adverse Reactions:** Alendronate may cause severe local irritation of the upper GI mucosa. Oesophagitis, ulcers, and erosions (rarely leading to stricture or perforation) have been reported. **Instruct patients to discontinue use and seek medical attention if they develop dysphagia, odynophagia, or retrosternal pain.**
- **Upper GI Disorders:** Use with caution in patients with active GI problems (e.g., Barrett’s oesophagus, gastritis, duodenitis, or ulcers).
- **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ):** Reported rarely with oral bisphosphonates. Risk factors include cancer, concomitant corticosteroids/chemotherapy, poor oral hygiene, and invasive dental procedures (e.g., tooth extraction). A dental examination is recommended before treatment in patients with risk factors.
- **Atypical Femoral Fractures:** Low-energy stress fractures of the femoral shaft have been reported with long-term use. Evaluate patients presenting with new thigh, hip, or groin pain.
- **Musculoskeletal Pain:** Severe and occasionally incapacitating bone, joint, and/or muscle pain may occur.
- **Vitamin D Metabolism:** Colecalciferol may exacerbate hypercalcaemia/hypercalciuria in patients with unregulated calcitriol production (e.g., leukaemia, lymphoma, sarcoidosis). Monitor serum and urine calcium in these populations.
- **Mineral Metabolism:** Ensure adequate treatment of vitamin D deficiency and other mineral disturbances before initiation. Note that the vitamin D content in this product is for maintenance and not suitable for correcting severe deficiency.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (1–10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, dyspepsia, acid regurgitation, nausea, diarrhoea, constipation, flatulence, abdominal distension.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Bone, muscle, or joint pain (4.1% in trials), muscle cramps.
- **Nervous System:** Headache.

### Uncommon (0.1–1%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Oesophageal ulcer, gastritis, vomiting, dysphagia, gastric ulcer.
- **Special Senses:** Taste perversion.

### Rare (<0.1%) and Post-Marketing
- **Gastrointestinal:** Oesophagitis, oesophageal erosions, oropharyngeal ulceration, melena.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Urticaria, angioedema, rash, photosensitivity.
- **Severe Skin Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis.
- **Special Senses:** Uveitis, scleritis, cholesteatoma of the external auditory canal.
- **Metabolic:** Symptomatic hypocalcaemia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Calcium, Antacids, and Oral Medications:** Likely to interfere with alendronate absorption. Wait at least 30 minutes after taking alendronate before taking any other oral medicine.
- **NSAIDs/Aspirin:** Increased risk of upper GI adverse events when used with daily alendronate (>10 mg); caution is advised.
- **Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT):** Concomitant use with oestrogen/progestin in studies showed no safety concerns and additive increases in bone mass.
- **Vitamin D Absorption Interference:** Olestra, mineral oils, orlistat, and bile acid sequestrants (e.g., cholestyramine) may impair colecalciferol absorption.
- **Vitamin D Catabolism:** Anticonvulsants, cimetidine, and thiazides may increase vitamin D catabolism.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3 [Australian].** Alendronate has not been studied in pregnant women and should not be used. Animal studies showed dystocia related to hypocalcaemia and reduced foetal weight at high doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. It is not known if alendronate is excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** No data available for the combination. Alendronate sodium alone showed no effect on rat fertility at oral doses up to 15 mg/kg/day.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and efficacy have not been established in children.
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required. Efficacy and safety profiles are similar to younger adults.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild-to-moderate (CrCl 35–60 mL/min):** No dosage adjustment required.
    - **Severe (CrCl < 35 mL/min):** **Not recommended** due to lack of clinical experience and potential for drug accumulation in bone.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (alendronate is not metabolised).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Alendronate:** A bisphosphonate that binds to hydroxyapatite and inhibits osteoclast-mediated bone resorption. It reduces bone turnover to premenopausal levels in postmenopausal women.
    - **Colecalciferol:** Converted to calcitriol (1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3), which increases intestinal absorption of calcium and phosphate and is essential for normal bone formation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Alendronate has very low oral bioavailability (0.64% in women, 0.6% in men) and is highly sensitive to food/drink. Colecalciferol is absorbed via chylomicrons.
    - **Distribution:** Alendronate transiently distributes to soft tissues before sequestering in bone or being excreted. Plasma protein binding is ~78%.
    - **Metabolism:** No evidence of alendronate metabolism. Colecalciferol is hydroxylated in the liver (to 25-OH D3) and kidney (to 1,25-(OH)2 D3).
    - **Elimination:** Alendronate is excreted in urine. The terminal half-life exceeds 10 years, reflecting release from the skeleton.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Vitamin D Status Study:** A 15-week study of 717 patients showed that the 70 mg/70 mcg weekly dose significantly increased serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D levels compared to alendronate alone. 89% of patients achieved levels ≥ 15 ng/mL vs. 68% in the control group.
- **Extension Study:** A 24-week extension showed that an additional 70 mcg (totaling 140 mcg/5600 IU weekly) further reduced the proportion of patients with vitamin D insufficiency (< 37.5 nmol/L) to 3.1% compared to 5.6% in the 70 mcg group.

## 12. Storage Conditions

- Protect from light and moisture.
- Shelf life is specified on the product packaging [Australian].
