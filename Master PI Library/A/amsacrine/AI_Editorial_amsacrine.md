# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Jurisdictions Contributed:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) currently available from the United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesizing clinical data and regulatory requirements.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
*   **Method:** Administered intravenously as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
*   **Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration before use.

### Metabolic Disorders
*   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
*   **Loading Dose:** Frequently administered in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
*   **Frequency:** Doses should be given every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
*   **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized based on clinical requirements.
*   **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
*   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
*   **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
*   **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range 40–50 µmol/L). Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and assess the risks/benefits before any attempt at re-administration [US].
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of seizure activity [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
*   **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation):** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
*   **Plasma Carnitine:** Monitor pre-dialysis (trough) levels in ESRD and regular levels in metabolic patients to ensure levels remain within the therapeutic range (35–60 µmol/L) [US].
*   **Coagulation:** Monitor INR in patients concurrently receiving warfarin [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Anaphylaxis and hypersensitivity reactions.
*   Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    *   **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Classification:** Not specifically assigned a letter category in the available source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. 
    *   **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration.
    *   **Recommendation:** Weigh the risk of excess carnitine intake by the infant against the benefit of supplementation to the mother. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders; 10–20 mg/kg for ESRD). See Section 3 for specific instructions [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Dosing adjustments for IV use in ESRD are based on pre-dialysis carnitine levels. Note the warning regarding oral levocarnitine in renal impairment (Section 5) [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces).
*   **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis), mean plasma levocarnitine concentrations increased from ~20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Similar increases were noted in dose-ranging studies (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg) [US].
*   **Clinical Outcomes:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations in ESRD is proven, the effects on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of opened vials as the formulation is preservative-free [US].
*   **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].
