# Editorial Product Information: Amphetamine Sulfate

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of amphetamine sulfate clinical information, synthesized for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Only one jurisdiction (United States) was provided for the compilation of this specific document.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Amphetamine sulfate (non-catecholamine, sympathomimetic amine).
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available source [Generic provided by Granules Pharmaceuticals Inc.].
- **ATC Code:** N06BA01 (Amphetamine).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Tablet:** 5 mg (white to off-white, round, scored) and 10 mg (light blue, round, double scored) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Granules Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Narcolepsy:** [US-approved].
- **Attention Deficit Disorder with Hyperactivity (ADHD):** Indicated as an integral part of a total treatment program (including psychological, educational, and social measures) for children with a behavioral syndrome characterized by distractibility, short attention span, hyperactivity, emotional lability, and impulsivity [US-approved].
- **Exogenous Obesity:** Indicated as a short-term (a few weeks) adjunct in a weight reduction regimen based on caloric restriction for patients refractory to alternative therapy (e.g., repeated diets, group programs) [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- Administer at the lowest effective dosage; adjust individually [US].
- Avoid late evening doses due to the risk of insomnia [US].
- **Obesity:** Administer 30 to 60 minutes before meals [US].

### ADHD (Pediatric)
- **Ages 3 to 5 years:** Start with 2.5 mg daily; increase in increments of 2.5 mg at weekly intervals until optimal response is achieved [US].
- **Ages 6 years and older:** Start with 5 mg once or twice daily; increase in increments of 5 mg at weekly intervals. Maximum dose is rarely required to exceed 40 mg/day [US].

### Narcolepsy
- **Usual dose:** 5 mg to 60 mg per day in divided doses [US].
- **Ages 6 to 12 years:** Start with 5 mg daily; increase by 5 mg weekly [US].
- **Ages 12 years and older:** Start with 10 mg daily; increase by 10 mg weekly [US].
- **Administration:** Give first dose on awakening; additional doses (5 or 10 mg) at 4 to 6-hour intervals [US].

### Exogenous Obesity
- **Adults/Pediatrics (>12 years):** Usual dosage is up to 30 mg daily, taken in divided doses of 5 to 10 mg [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to amphetamine products [US].
- Concomitant use or use within 14 days of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs), including linezolid or intravenous methylene blue, due to risk of hypertensive crisis [US].
- Patients with pheochromocytoma or a history of pheochromocytoma [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Abuse, Misuse, and Addiction
- **Amphetamine sulfate has a high potential for abuse and misuse, which can lead to substance use disorder and addiction. Misuse/abuse can result in overdose and death. Risk is higher with non-approved routes (e.g., snorting, injection). Assess risk before prescribing and monitor throughout treatment [US].**

### Cardiovascular Warnings
- **Serious Cardiac Disease:** Sudden death has been reported in patients with structural cardiac abnormalities or other serious heart problems. Avoid use in patients with known structural abnormalities, cardiomyopathy, serious arrhythmias, or coronary artery disease [US].
- **Blood Pressure/Heart Rate:** CNS stimulants cause a mean increase in blood pressure (2–4 mmHg) and heart rate (3–6 bpm). Monitor all patients for hypertension and tachycardia [US].

### Psychiatric Warnings
- **Pre-existing Psychosis:** May exacerbate symptoms of behavior disturbance and thought disorder [US].
- **Bipolar Disorder:** May induce mixed/manic episodes; screen for risk factors (family history of suicide, bipolar, or depression) before initiation [US].
- **New Psychotic/Manic Symptoms:** Occurred in ~0.1% of patients at recommended doses. Consider discontinuation if hallucinations or mania occur [US].

### Other Precautions
- **Growth Suppression:** Monitor height and weight in pediatric patients; treatment interruption may be necessary if growth is not as expected [US].
- **Seizures:** May lower the convulsive threshold. Discontinue in the presence of seizures [US].
- **Peripheral Vasculopathy (Raynaud’s Phenomenon):** Associated with digital ulceration and soft tissue breakdown. Monitor for digital changes (numbness, color changes) [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Increased risk when used with other serotonergic drugs or CYP2D6 inhibitors. Discontinue immediately if symptoms (agitation, hallucinations, autonomic instability) occur [US].
- **Tics:** May onset or exacerbate motor and verbal tics or Tourette’s syndrome [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Cardiovascular
- Palpitations, tachycardia, hypertension. Isolated reports of cardiomyopathy with chronic use [US].

### Central Nervous System
- Overstimulation, restlessness, dizziness, insomnia, euphoria, dyskinesia, dysphoria, tremor, headache, exacerbation of tics/Tourette’s, and rare psychotic episodes at recommended doses [US].

### Gastrointestinal
- Dry mouth, unpleasant taste, diarrhea, constipation, anorexia, weight loss, and intestinal ischemia [US].

### Other Systems
- **Allergic:** Urticaria [US].
- **Endocrine:** Impotence, libido changes, frequent/prolonged erections [US].
- **Musculoskeletal:** Rhabdomyolysis [US].

### Most Common (Reported by Patients)
- Headache, nervousness, itching, stomachache, dizziness, mood swings, and vomiting [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** (e.g., paroxetine, fluoxetine, quinidine, ritonavir) May increase amphetamine exposure and risk of serotonin syndrome [US].
- **Acidifying Agents:** (Gastrointestinal: ascorbic acid, fruit juices; Urinary: ammonium chloride) Lower blood levels and efficacy by decreasing absorption or increasing excretion [US].
- **Alkalinizing Agents:** (Gastrointestinal: sodium bicarbonate; Urinary: acetazolamide, some thiazides) Increase blood levels and potentiate action [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Slow amphetamine metabolism, potentiating the release of norepinephrine; risk of fatal hypertensive crisis and malignant hyperpyrexia [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** (SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, fentanyl, lithium, St. John’s Wort) Increase risk of serotonin syndrome [US].
- **Antihypertensives:** Amphetamines may antagonize the hypotensive effects [US].
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants:** May enhance activity and cardiovascular effects [US].
- **Chlorpromazine/Haloperidol:** Block dopamine/norepinephrine reuptake, inhibiting stimulant effects [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but detailed data provided]. Animal studies (mice) showed embryotoxic and teratogenic effects at 41 times the maximum human dose. Infants born to dependent mothers have increased risk of premature delivery, low birth weight, and withdrawal symptoms (dysphoria, agitation, lassitude). Use only if benefit outweighs risk [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Amphetamines are excreted in human milk. Mothers should be advised to refrain from nursing while taking amphetamine sulfate [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity and long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - ADHD: Not recommended under 3 years of age [US].
    - Obesity: Not recommended under 12 years of age [US].
    - Long-term effects in children are not well established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available source; general caution regarding cardiovascular status and lower effective dosing is standard for the drug class.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Sympathomimetic amine with CNS stimulant activity. Peripheral actions include increased systolic/diastolic blood pressure and weak bronchodilator/respiratory stimulant effects. The specific mechanism for behavioral effects in children or appetite suppression is not fully established [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Isomer potency:** The l-isomer is more potent in cardiovascular activity; the d-isomer is more potent in CNS excitatory effects [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Partially metabolized by CYP2D6 [US].
    - **Excretion:** Urinary excretion is pH-dependent (enhanced by acidification, reduced by alkalinization) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Elevation of plasma corticosteroid levels, particularly in the evening [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Obesity:** Short-term clinical trials show drug-treated obese adults lose more weight than those on placebo and diet, though the magnitude is described as "only a fraction of a pound a week" over placebo. Weight loss is greatest in the first few weeks [US].
- **ADHD:** Trials support its use as part of a "total treatment program" for stabilization in children with developmentally inappropriate symptoms [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a well-closed container as defined in the USP. Store in a secure, preferably locked area to prevent theft/misuse [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Schedule II (CII) Controlled Substance. Rx-only [US].
