# Editorial Product Information: Asenapine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** The information in this editorial is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
*   **Purpose:** This document serves as a harmonised clinical synthesis intended for healthcare professionals. It provides a structured overview of the drug’s profile, safety, and administration.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information when prescribing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** **Asenapine** (as maleate).
*   **Chemical Class:** Dibenzo-oxepino pyrrole; atypical antipsychotic.
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Saphris** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 5 mg and 10 mg sublingual wafers [Australian].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Sublingual.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Organon Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Schizophrenia:** Treatment in adults [Australian].
*   **Bipolar 1 Disorder:** 
    *   Treatment of acute manic or mixed episodes in adults (as monotherapy or in combination with lithium or sodium valproate) [Australian].
    *   Prevention of relapse of manic or mixed episodes in adults (as monotherapy or in combination with lithium or sodium valproate) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Schizophrenia:** The recommended initial dose is 5 mg twice daily. An increase to 10 mg twice daily should only occur after clinical assessment, as controlled trials showed no added benefit at the higher dose but demonstrated increased adverse effects [Australian].
*   **Bipolar 1 Disorder (Monotherapy):** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg twice daily. This may be reduced to 5 mg twice daily based on clinical assessment [Australian].
*   **Bipolar 1 Disorder (Combination Therapy):** The recommended starting dose is 5 mg twice daily. Depending on clinical response and tolerability, the dose may be increased to 10 mg twice daily [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Wafers:** Must be handled with dry hands. The wafer should be removed by peeling back the blister tab (not pushed through the foil). The wafer must be placed under the tongue to dissolve completely. It should not be crushed, chewed, or swallowed [Australian].
*   **Post-Administration:** Patients should not eat or drink for 10 minutes following administration to ensure optimal absorption [Australian].
*   **Combination Use:** When taken with other medications, **Saphris** should be administered last [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
*   Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to asenapine or any excipients (gelatin, mannitol). Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis and angioedema, have been reported [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Elderly Patients with Dementia-Related Psychosis:** Increased risk of death compared to placebo. **Asenapine** is not approved for this indication [Australian].
*   **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal syndrome characterized by hyperthermia, muscle rigidity, and autonomic instability. **Asenapine** must be discontinued immediately if symptoms occur [Australian].
*   **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Risk of involuntary, rhythmical movements. If symptoms appear, discontinuation should be considered [Australian].
*   **Metabolic Changes:** Associated with weight gain, hyperglycemia, diabetes mellitus, and dyslipidaemia. Monitoring of glucose and lipids is recommended [Australian].
*   **Orthostatic Hypotension and Syncope:** Risk is higher early in treatment due to $\alpha_1$-adrenergic antagonism. Use with caution in patients with known cardiovascular disease [Australian].
*   **QT Prolongation:** While clinically relevant prolongation was not observed in dedicated studies (increases of 2 to 5 msec), caution is advised in patients with known cardiovascular disease or when used with other QT-prolonging agents [Australian].
*   **Leukopenia, Neutropenia, and Agranulocytosis:** Monitoring of complete blood count (CBC) is recommended for patients with a history of low white blood cell counts. Treatment should be discontinued if severe neutropenia occurs (ANC < 1000/mm³) [Australian].
*   **Seizures:** Use with caution in patients with a history of seizure disorders [Australian].
*   **Suicide:** Close supervision of high-risk patients is required; prescriptions should be for the smallest quantity consistent with good management [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorized by frequency based on clinical trial data (5-10 mg BID) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Events |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Somnolence |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, oral hypoaesthesia (numbness), dry mouth, fatigue, weight increase, increased appetite, sedation, akathisia, dizziness, headache, insomnia, anxiety, tremor, parkinsonism |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Not specifically tabulated, but dysphagia and syncope are noted as occasional [Australian]. |

*   **Oral Local Anaesthetic Effect:** Oral hypoaesthesia or paraesthesia may occur immediately after administration and usually resolves within one hour [Australian].
*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** NMS, anaphylaxis, angioedema, agranulocytosis, and severe extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    *   **Fluvoxamine (CYP1A2 Inhibitor):** Increases asenapine exposure (AUC increased by 29%); co-administer with caution [Australian].
    *   **Paroxetine (CYP2D6 Inhibitor/Substrate):** Asenapine may enhance the inhibitory effects of paroxetine on its own metabolism. Co-administration of 5 mg BID asenapine with 20 mg paroxetine resulted in a 2-fold increase in paroxetine exposure [Australian].
    *   **Antihypertensives:** Asenapine may enhance the effects of certain antihypertensive agents via $\alpha_1$-adrenergic antagonism [Australian].
*   **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    *   **Centrally Acting Drugs and Alcohol:** Caution is advised due to additive CNS effects [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Neonates exposed to antipsychotics during the third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal or withdrawal symptoms post-delivery. Use only if benefits outweigh risks [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Asenapine is excreted in rat milk; human data is unavailable. Breastfeeding is not recommended while receiving **Saphris** [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in rat studies at exposures 11 times the human dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children or adolescents under 18 years of age. Paediatric trials showed a higher frequency of metabolic syndrome, weight gain, and dystonia compared to adults [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** Use with care. Exposure is approximately 30% higher in subjects aged 65–85 compared to younger adults [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. **Not recommended** for patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) due to a 7-fold increase in exposure [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Antagonist at D₂ and 5-HT₂\textsubscript{A} receptors. It also exhibits activity at 5-HT\textsubscript{1A}, 5-HT\textsubscript{1B}, 5-HT\textsubscript{2C}, 5-HT₆, 5-HT₇, D₃, and $\alpha_2$-adrenergic receptors [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Rapid sublingual absorption; peak plasma levels (C\textsubscript{max}) in 0.5 to 1.5 hours. Absolute bioavailability is 35% (sublingual) vs <2% (oral/swallowed) [Australian].
    *   **Distribution:** Extensive extravascular distribution (Volume of distribution ~1700 L). Highly protein bound (95-97%) [Australian].
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily via direct glucuronidation (UGT1A4) and oxidative metabolism (CYP1A2) [Australian].
    *   **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 24 hours. Excreted via urine (~50%) and faeces (~40%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Schizophrenia:** Efficacy demonstrated in three 6-week fixed-dose trials using PANSS scores. Maintenance of effect was confirmed in a 26-week trial where asenapine significantly reduced relapse rates (12%) compared to placebo (47%) [Australian].
*   **Bipolar 1 Disorder:** Efficacy established in two 3-week monotherapy trials and one 12-week combination trial with lithium or valproate, using the YMRS scale [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 30°C [Australian].
*   Protect from light and moisture [Australian].
*   Store in the original container [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
