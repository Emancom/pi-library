# Editorial Product Information: Alglucosidase alfa

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **alglucosidase alfa** Product Information for healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory data into a single clinically relevant resource.
- **Prescriber Notice:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Alglucosidase alfa (rch). It is a purified form of the lysosomal enzyme acid $\alpha$-glucosidase (GAA), produced by recombinant DNA technology in a Chinese hamster ovary cell line.
- **Chemical Class:** Enzyme replacement therapy (lysosomal $\alpha$-glucosidase).
- **Brand Names:** **Myozyme** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AB07.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Powder for concentrate for solution for infusion. Each vial contains 50 mg of alglucosidase alfa. Upon reconstitution, each vial contains 5 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi-aventis Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pompe Disease:** Indicated for the long-term treatment of patients with a confirmed diagnosis of Pompe disease (acid $\alpha$-glucosidase deficiency) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric):** The recommended dose is 20 mg/kg of body weight administered once every 2 weeks [Australian].
- **Reconstitution Instructions:**
    - Reconstitute with 10.3 mL of Sterile Water for Injection, injecting slowly against the vial wall to avoid foaming.
    - Yields 5 mg/mL solution (10 mL extractable volume).
    - Vials should be at room temperature before reconstitution (approx. 30 minutes).
    - Tilt and roll gently; do not shake or invert [Australian].
- **Dilution Instructions:**
    - Dilute in 0.9% Sodium Chloride to a final concentration of 0.5 mg/mL to 4 mg/mL.
    - Remove air from the infusion bag to minimise protein interaction with the air-liquid interface [Australian].
- **Administration:**
    - Must be administered via an infusion pump using an in-line, low protein-binding 0.2 $\mu$m filter.
    - **Incremental Infusion Rate:** Initial rate should not exceed 1 mg/kg/hr. If tolerated, the rate may be increased by 2 mg/kg/hr every 30 minutes to a maximum rate of 7 mg/kg/hr [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Life-threatening hypersensitivity (anaphylactic reaction) to alglucosidase alfa or any of the excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis and Hypersensitivity:** Serious and life-threatening anaphylactic reactions, including anaphylactic shock and cardiac arrest, have occurred in both infantile-onset (IOPD) and late-onset (LOPD) patients. Some reactions were IgE-mediated. Medical support, including resuscitation equipment, must be available during infusion [Australian].
- **Infusion Associated Reactions (IARs):** Reported in approximately 14% to 51% of patients. If a reaction occurs, the infusion should be slowed or temporarily stopped. Pre-treatment with antihistamines, antipyretics, or corticosteroids may be considered, though IARs may still occur [Australian].
- **Immune-Mediated Reactions:** Severe cutaneous reactions (ulcerative and necrotizing skin lesions) and nephrotic syndrome (due to immune complex deposition) have been reported. Monitor for systemic immune complex-mediated reactions and perform periodic urinalysis in patients with high IgG titres [Australian].
- **Immunogenicity:** Most patients (approx. 89%) develop IgG antibodies, typically within 3 months. High sustained antibody titres (HSAT) may reduce clinical efficacy, particularly in CRIM-negative (Cross Reactive Immunologic Material) patients. Monitoring IgG titres every 3 months is recommended [Australian].
- **Acute Cardiorespiratory Failure:** Patients with underlying cardiac hypertrophy or compromised respiratory function are at risk of acute failure due to fluid overload during infusion [Australian].
- **General Anaesthesia Risk:** Infantile-onset patients with cardiac hypertrophy are at risk of cardiac arrhythmia and sudden death during general anaesthesia (e.g., for central venous catheter placement) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - **General:** Pyrexia, irritability, chills, fatigue.
    - **Respiratory:** Cough, tachypnoea, respiratory distress.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea, vomiting, nausea.
    - **Skin:** Rash, urticaria.
    - **Vascular:** Flushing, hypertension.
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness, headache, tremor.
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Tachycardia, bradycardia, cyanosis, peripheral oedema, hyperhidrosis, muscle spasms, myalgia, arthralgia.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
    - Anaphylactic shock, cardiac arrest, cardiorespiratory failure, respiratory arrest, apnoea, and nephrotic syndrome [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted [Australian].
- **Management:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category:** B1 (Drugs taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the human fetus having been observed). 
    - Use only if clearly necessary. The risks of untreated Pompe disease to the mother must be weighed against potential fetal risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Alglucosidase alfa may be excreted in breast milk. A 24-hour interruption of breastfeeding (pumping and discarding milk) following infusion may be considered to minimise infant exposure [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal studies (mice) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Evaluated in patients from 1 month to 16 years of age. There is no evidence for special considerations based on age in the paediatric population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include patients aged 65 and older; it is unknown if they respond differently than younger subjects [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and efficacy have not been evaluated in these populations. No specific dosing recommendations are available [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Pompe disease is caused by a deficiency of lysosomal GAA, leading to glycogen accumulation in cardiac and skeletal muscles. Alglucosidase alfa provides an exogenous source of GAA. It binds to mannose-6-phosphate receptors on the cell surface, is internalised, and transported to lysosomes where it degrades accumulated glycogen [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Vss ranges from 66.9 to 914 mL/kg depending on age and study.
    - **Elimination:** Plasma elimination half-life is approximately 2 to 3 hours. 
    - **Clearance:** Patients with high antibody titres ($\ge$ 12,800) may show an increase in clearance (approx. 50%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Infantile-Onset (Study AGLU01602):** A pivotal trial of 18 non-ventilated patients ($\le$ 7 months old). 89% (16/18) were alive at the end of the study compared to a historical cohort. 13 of 18 were alive and free of invasive ventilation at 18 months of age [Australian].
- **Late-Onset (Study AGLU02704):** A randomised, placebo-controlled trial of 90 patients. Treated patients showed a statistically significant improvement in the 6-Minute Walk Test (mean increase of 28.12 metres vs placebo) and stabilisation of pulmonary function (FVC) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze or shake. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Use as soon as practicable. If necessary, the solution is stable for up to 24 hours when stored at 2°C to 8°C [Australian].
