# Editorial Product Information: acetaminophen + chlorpheniramine + dextromethorphan

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **acetaminophen + chlorpheniramine + dextromethorphan**
- Chemical class:
  - Acetaminophen — analgesic / antipyretic
  - Chlorpheniramine maleate — antihistamine (first-generation)
  - Dextromethorphan hydrobromide — cough suppressant (antitussive)
  (Purposes listed in source: pain reliever/fever reducer; antihistamine; cough suppressant)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Marketing start date (as stated in source): 07/01/2026 [US]
- Date of last revision/update of source PI (as stated): Revised: 7/2026 [US]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
Provides temporary relief of symptoms associated with the common cold or influenza,
including:
  - headache
  - minor aches and pains
  - cough due to minor throat and bronchial irritation
  - minor sore throat pain
  - runny nose
  - temporary reduction of fever

(Indications above are those listed in the available source. No other indications are
specified.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Adults and adolescents 12 years of age and over: one packet taken every 6 hours
  while symptoms persist.
- Do not exceed 3 packets in 24 hours unless directed by a doctor.

### Maximum acetaminophen guidance
- The product's own dosing instructions limit use to 3 packets per 24-hour period.
- The source includes a liver safety statement indicating that severe liver damage may
  occur if more than 4,000 mg of acetaminophen is taken within 24 hours, when used
  alongside other acetaminophen-containing products, or when consuming 3 or more
  alcoholic drinks daily while using this product.

### Administration instructions
- Dissolve the contents of one packet into 8 oz of hot water; sip while hot. The entire
  drink should be consumed within 10–15 minutes.
- If using a microwave: add packet contents to 8 oz of cool water; stir briskly before
  and after heating. Do not overheat.

### Titration / missed dose
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
- Do not use in children under 12 years of age.
- Do not use in patients who are allergic to acetaminophen.
- Do not use concurrently with any other product containing acetaminophen
  (prescription or nonprescription).
- Do not use in patients who are currently taking a prescription monoamine oxidase
  inhibitor (MAOI), or within 2 weeks of discontinuing an MAOI. If it is unclear
  whether a concurrent prescription contains a MAOI, a doctor or pharmacist should
  be consulted before use.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Critical warnings
- **Liver warning:** This product contains acetaminophen. Severe liver damage may
  result if more than 4,000 mg of acetaminophen is taken within 24 hours, if taken
  together with other acetaminophen-containing products, or if 3 or more alcoholic
  drinks are consumed every day during use.
- **Allergy alert:** Acetaminophen may cause severe skin reactions, which may
  include skin reddening, blisters, or rash. If a skin reaction occurs, use should be
  discontinued and medical attention sought immediately.

### General warnings and precautions
- Sore throat: if a sore throat is severe, persists for more than 2 days, or is
  accompanied or followed by fever, headache, rash, nausea, or vomiting, a doctor
  should be consulted promptly.
- Alcoholic drinks should be avoided while using this product.
- Marked drowsiness may occur. Concurrent use of alcohol, sedatives, or tranquilizers
  may further increase drowsiness.
- Caution is required when driving or operating machinery while taking this product.
- Excitability may occur, particularly in children.
- Stop use and consult a doctor if:
  - pain or cough worsens or persists beyond 7 days
  - fever worsens or persists beyond 3 days
  - redness or swelling is present
  - new symptoms develop
  - cough returns or is accompanied by rash or a persistent headache
  (These may indicate a serious underlying condition.)

### Conditions requiring pre-use medical consultation
- A doctor should be consulted before use in patients with:
  - liver disease
  - glaucoma
  - difficulty urinating due to an enlarged prostate gland
  - a breathing problem such as emphysema or chronic bronchitis
  - a cough associated with excessive phlegm (mucus)
  - a chronic or persistent cough (e.g., associated with smoking, asthma, or
    emphysema)
- A doctor or pharmacist should be consulted before use in patients who are taking:
  - the blood-thinning drug warfarin
  - sedatives or tranquilizers
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding: a health professional should be consulted before use.

### Overdose
- In the event of overdose, medical help should be obtained or a Poison Control
  Center contacted immediately. Prompt medical attention is critical for both adults and
  children, even in the absence of signs or symptoms.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- **Acetaminophen-containing products:** concurrent use increases the risk of
  acetaminophen overdose and severe liver damage — combined use should be avoided.
- **Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs):** concurrent use with a prescription MAOI
  is contraindicated; at least 2 weeks should elapse after stopping an MAOI before
  use of this product.
- **Alcohol:** concurrent alcohol use increases the risk of hepatotoxicity; alcoholic
  drinks should be avoided during use.
- **Sedatives, tranquilizers, and alcohol:** concurrent use may increase drowsiness;
  caution and appropriate counselling regarding activities requiring alertness are
  recommended.
- **Warfarin:** patients taking warfarin should consult a doctor or pharmacist before
  use.
- Other pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic interactions beyond those listed above:
  Not specified in available sources.
