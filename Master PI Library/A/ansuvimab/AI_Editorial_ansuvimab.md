# Editorial Product Information: Ansuvimab

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ansuvimab, synthesized for healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current PI for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Ansuvimab-zykl.
*   **Chemical Class:** Recombinant human IgG1$\kappa$ monoclonal antibody directed against the glycoprotein of *Orthoebolavirus zairense*.
*   **Brand Name(s):** **EBANGA** [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   For injection: 400 mg of lyophilized powder in a single-dose vial for reconstitution and further dilution [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Emergent Manufacturing Operations Baltimore, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Treatment of Ebola Virus Disease:** Indicated for the treatment of infection caused by *Orthoebolavirus zairense* (formerly Zaire ebolavirus) in adult and pediatric patients, including neonates born to a mother who is RT-PCR positive for the infection [US-approved].
*   **Limitations of Use:**
    *   Efficacy has not been established for species other than *Orthoebolavirus zairense* or for the *Orthomarburgvirus* genus [US].
    *   Emergence of resistance or changes in viral virulence could diminish clinical benefit; clinicians should consider local susceptibility patterns [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
*   **Recommended Dose:** A single intravenous infusion of **50 mg/kg** [US].

### 3.2 Reconstitution and Dilution Instructions
*   **Reconstitution:** Vials must reach ambient temperature (15°C to 27°C) for ~20 minutes before use. Reconstitute with 7.7 mL of Sterile Water for Injection, USP. The resulting solution provides 50 mg/mL of ansuvimab [US].
*   **Dilution:** Further dilute the reconstituted solution in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, USP, or 5% Dextrose Injection, USP.
    *   **Patients $\ge$ 2 kg:** Use an infusion bag. Total volume is weight-dependent (e.g., 250 mL bag for 51–100 kg weight range) [US].
    *   **Patients 0.5 kg to < 2 kg:** Use a 10 mL syringe compatible with an IV infusion pump [US].

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
*   **Infusion Rate:** Administer over 60 minutes.
*   **Equipment:** Use a 1.2-micron in-line filter extension set.
*   **Co-administration:** Do not mix with or administer simultaneously through the same line with other medicinal products.
*   **Post-Infusion:** Flush the line with 2–5 mL of diluent (syringe pump) or at least 25 mL of diluent (infusion bag) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions Including Infusion-Associated Events:** Acute, life-threatening reactions have been reported during and after infusion. 
    *   **Symptoms:** Hypotension, chills, fever elevation, tachycardia, and tachypnea.
    *   **Management:** Monitor all patients. If a severe or life-threatening reaction occurs, discontinue administration immediately and provide emergency care. For mild to moderate reactions, the infusion rate may be slowed or interrupted [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event evaluation in clinical trials is often confounded by the underlying *Orthoebolavirus zairense* infection.
*   **Very Common (>10%):** Pyrexia (17%) [US].
*   **Common (1–10%):** Tachycardia (9%), diarrhea (9%), vomiting (8%), hypotension (8%), tachypnea (6%), chills (5%), and hypoxia (3%) [US].
*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** Infusion-associated events led to discontinuation in 1% of clinical trial participants [US].
*   **Laboratory Abnormalities (Grade 3 or 4):** Significant elevations in creatinine (27%), high potassium (15%), high aspartate aminotransferase (13%), and high alanine aminotransferase (12%) were reported, though these may reflect disease progression [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Live Vaccines:** No formal interaction studies have been conducted. Ansuvimab may inhibit the replication of live vaccine viruses (e.g., rVSV-ZEBOV) and reduce their efficacy. 
*   **Management:** Concurrent administration of live vaccines for Ebola should be avoided. The interval between ansuvimab therapy and live vaccination should follow current vaccination guidelines [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Classification:** No specific letter category (PLLR).
    *   **Summary:** Ebola virus infection is life-threatening for mother and fetus. Clinical data from the PALM trial show high maternal/neonatal morbidity consistent with the disease. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. Treatment should not be withheld due to pregnancy [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Patients infected with *Orthoebolavirus zairense* are instructed not to breastfeed to avoid postnatal transmission of the virus. It is unknown if ansuvimab is excreted in human milk [US].
*   **Fertility:** Data on human fertility are not available; nonclinical studies have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness are established for patients from birth to <18 years. In the PALM trial, 28-day mortality and safety were similar between pediatric and adult participants [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Limited data (only 3% of trial participants were $\ge$65 years). No specific differences in response compared to younger patients have been identified [US].
*   **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** The effect of renal or hepatic impairment on the pharmacokinetics of ansuvimab is unknown [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### 10.1 Mechanism of Action
Ansuvimab-zykl binds to the glycan cap and inner chalice of the Ebola virus GP1 subunit (residues 111–119). This blocks the binding of the virus to the Niemann-Pick cell receptor 1 (NPC1) in host cells, thereby inhibiting viral entry [US].

### 10.2 Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Consistent with other human IgG1 monoclonal antibodies.
*   **Metabolism/Elimination:** Not specified; likely degraded by proteolytic enzymes into small peptides and amino acids.
*   **Half-life:** Not specified in available sources for infected patients [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **The PALM Trial:** A multi-center, open-label, randomized controlled trial in the Democratic Republic of Congo. 
    *   **Population:** 174 participants received EBANGA (50 mg/kg) alongside optimized standard of care.
    *   **Primary Outcome:** 28-day mortality was **35%** for the ansuvimab group compared to **49%** in the investigational control group (p=0.008) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze or shake [US].
*   **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** 
    *   If stored refrigerated (2°C to 8°C), the total time (vial + IV bag) must not exceed **4 hours**. 
    *   Allow approximately 20 minutes for refrigerated bags to reach ambient temperature before administration [US].
