# Editorial Product Information: antithymocyte globulin (rabbit)

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **antithymocyte globulin (rabbit)**
- Chemical class: Polyclonal rabbit-derived immunoglobulin preparation directed against human thymocyte/T‑lymphocyte antigens
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous infusion only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prophylaxis of graft rejection in renal transplantation.
- Treatment of steroid‑resistant or moderate to severe renal transplant rejection.
- Treatment of refractory or relapsing aplastic anaemia.
- If indications vary by jurisdiction, this unified document reflects the indications reported in the available product information. Specific regional approvals or restrictions beyond these indications are not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adults)
- Dosing varies by indication and clinical regimen; use only under specialist supervision and as part of established immunosuppressive protocols.
- Dose should be calculated on body weight; for obese patients dose should be based on ideal body weight rather than actual body weight.
- Paediatric and elderly dosing: the available sources indicate the same dosing recommendations as adults, unless specific clinical circumstances require adjustment.

### Dosing by indication
- Renal transplantation — prophylaxis of graft rejection:
  - 1 to 1.5 mg/kg/day for 3 to 9 days (cumulative dose 3 to 13.5 mg/kg).
- Renal transplantation — treatment of steroid‑resistant or moderate to severe rejection:
  - 1.5 mg/kg/day for 7 to 14 days (cumulative dose 10.5 to 21 mg/kg).
- Aplastic anaemia — treatment of relapsing or refractory disease:
  - 2.5 to 3.75 mg/kg/day for 5 consecutive days (cumulative dose 12.5 to 18.75 mg/kg).

### Administration instructions
- Intravenous infusion using a high‑flow vein is the recommended route; peripheral vein administration is permitted with precautions.
- Premedication: give corticosteroid, antihistamine and/or antipyretic approximately 1 hour prior to infusion to reduce infusion‑related reactions.
- Reconstitution and dilution (procedural summary):
  - Reconstitute each vial with 5 mL sterile water for injection to yield a nominal concentration of 5 mg/mL (follow local aseptic technique).
  - Further dilute into 0.9% sodium chloride injection (USP/EP) or 5% glucose solution prior to infusion.
  - Recommended dilution volume: use approximately 50 mL of infusion solution per vial (total infusion volumes typically 50–500 mL depending on dose).
  - Mix gently; avoid shaking. Inspect reconstituted solution for particulate matter or discoloration; do not use if present.
  - Use as soon as practicable after dilution; if delay is necessary, hold at 2–8°C for no more than 24 hours and protect from light (storage specifics limited in source).
- Infusion rate:
  - First dose: deliver over a minimum of 6 hours.
  - Subsequent doses: deliver over at least 4 hours.
  - Adjust infusion rate downward if infusion‑associated reactions occur.
- Filters and compatibility:
  - Administration through a 0.22 μm in‑line filter is recommended to reduce particulate delivery.
  - Do not infuse in the same intravenous line with other products.
  - When administered via peripheral vein, concurrent addition of heparin and hydrocortisone in 0.9% sodium chloride may help reduce superficial thrombophlebitis and deep vein thrombosis.
  - Do not combine heparin and hydrocortisone with this product in dextrose (glucose) infusion solution because precipitation has been reported.
- Concomitant therapy:
  - Antiviral prophylaxis is recommended where clinically appropriate (e.g., to reduce CMV risk in transplant recipients).
- Titration / dose modification:
  - Monitor lymphocyte/T‑cell counts and adjust dose according to clinical protocol and degree of T‑cell depletion.
  - Haematological dose modification guidance (see Warnings & Precautions and sections below): consider dose reduction if platelet count 50,000–75,000 cells/mm3 or white cell count 2,000–3,000 cells/mm3; consider stopping therapy for persistent severe thrombocytopenia <50,000 cells/mm3 or leukopenia <2,000 cells/mm3.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to rabbit proteins or to any excipient contained in the product.
- Presence of acute or chronic infections that would contraindicate further immunosuppression.
- Any additional jurisdiction‑specific contraindications are not specified in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources. (Critical safety information is provided below under General warnings and precautions.)

### General warnings and precautions
- **Serious immune‑mediated reactions**: anaphylaxis and severe cytokine release syndrome (CRS) have been reported; fatal anaphylaxis has occurred rarely. If anaphylaxis occurs, stop infusion immediately and institute emergency management; further exposure is not recommended in patients with prior anaphylaxis.
- **Infusion‑associated reactions**: IARs can occur with the first or subsequent infusions. Rapid administration increases risk of severe CRS and related cardiorespiratory events; adhere to recommended infusion durations and slow the rate for intolerance.
- **Infections**: increased susceptibility to bacterial, fungal, viral, and protozoal infections including reactivation (notably cytomegalovirus). Fatal infections have been reported. Use appropriate prophylaxis and monitor closely.
- **Haematological effects**: thrombocytopenia and leukopenia (including lymphopenia and neutropenia) are common and generally reversible. Monitor full blood counts frequently; adjust or stop therapy as per thresholds.
- **Malignancy**: prolonged or combined immunosuppression may increase risk of malignancies including lymphoma and post‑transplant lymphoproliferative disease (PTLD).
- **Thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA)**: may occur, particularly when administered concomitantly with calcineurin inhibitors; monitor for clinical and laboratory evidence.
- **Immunisations**: safety of live attenuated vaccines after therapy has not been studied; live vaccines are not recommended for recently treated patients.
- **Effects on laboratory tests / immunogenicity**:
  - The product may interfere with assays using rabbit antibodies (e.g., rabbit antibody‑based immunoassays), cross‑match or panel‑reactive antibody cytotoxicity assays, and some ELISAs.
  - A substantial proportion of patients develop anti‑rabbit antibodies after treatment (see Clinical Trials / Immunogenicity).
- **Use in nursing mothers / pregnancy**:
  - Pregnancy: product should not be used during pregnancy unless the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. [Australian: Category C]
  - Breastfeeding: discontinue breastfeeding during therapy due to potential excretion of immunoglobulins in human milk.
- **Use in elderly and paediatric patients**: dosing recommendations are the same as for adults in available sources; exercise caution and monitor closely, particularly in older patients with comorbidities.
- **Driving and operating machinery**: due to potential for acute infusion reactions and CRS, patients should not drive or operate machinery during treatment.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for infusion reactions continuously during and after administration.
- Regular monitoring of:
  - T‑cell counts (absolute and/or subsets) to assess degree of T‑cell depletion.
  - Full blood count including white blood cell differential and platelets (daily during initial therapy in some study protocols; at clinician discretion thereafter).
  - Clinical signs of infection; consider viral surveillance (e.g., CMV) per institutional protocols.
  - Renal and hepatic function as clinically indicated (no routine dose adjustment required based on available PK information, see Section 10).
  - Coagulation parameters if clinically indicated because transient abnormalities have been reported.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Ensure experienced physicians prescribe and administer this agent in appropriate inpatient settings with resuscitation facilities available.
- Be alert for serum sickness (typically 5–15 days after therapy onset) presenting with fever, rash, arthralgia, and myalgia.
- Adjust or discontinue concomitant immunosuppression per institutional protocols when infections or severe cytopenias occur.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Formal drug‑drug interaction studies have not been performed; specific pharmacokinetic interaction data are not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamic / clinical interactions
- Concomitant immunosuppressive agents: additive immunosuppression increases risk of infections, reactivation of latent infections, febrile neutropenia, sepsis and malignancy (including PTLD). Monitor and manage accordingly.
- Calcineurin inhibitors: concomitant administration has been associated with reports of thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA). Exercise caution and monitor for TMA.

### Assay and laboratory interactions
- Interference with laboratory assays that use rabbit antibodies: may interfere with rabbit antibody‑based immunoassays, cross‑match or panel‑reactive antibody cytotoxicity assays, and some ELISA formats. Interpret such tests with caution in treated patients.

### Compatibility and admixture
- When administered via a peripheral vein, combining this product with heparin and hydrocortisone in 0.9% sodium chloride may reduce local venous irritation; however, combining heparin and hydrocortisone with this product in a dextrose infusion solution (5% glucose) has been reported to cause precipitation and is not recommended.

### Management notes
- Monitor for additive immunosuppression when using other agents that depress cellular or humoral immunity.
- Use caution when interpreting assays that may be falsely affected by circulating rabbit immunoglobulins.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - Not recommended in pregnancy unless potential benefits justify potential fetal risks. Drugs of this class are known to cross the placenta and may produce immunosuppressive effects in the fetus.
  - [Australian] Pregnancy category: C.
- Breastfeeding:
  - It is not known whether rabbit-derived immunoglobulins are excreted in human milk; because other immunoglobulins are excreted in human milk, breastfeeding should be discontinued during therapy.
- Fertility:
  - Effects on fertility have not been studied in the available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Controlled data are limited. The safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established in randomized controlled trials according to the available sources; however, limited clinical use suggests dosing, efficacy and adverse event profiles similar to adults. Use under specialist supervision.

### Elderly use
- Available data do not demonstrate a clear difference in response between older and younger patients; caution and careful monitoring are advised in older patients. A retrospective analysis in aplastic anaemia suggested similar response rates in patients <60 and >60 years, though closer monitoring is recommended.

### Renal impairment
- No dose adjustment is required based on renal impairment according to the pharmacokinetic and metabolic data reported in the available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dose adjustment is required based on hepatic impairment according to the pharmacokinetic and metabolic data reported in the available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- **antithymocyte globulin (rabbit)** is a polyclonal rabbit immunoglobulin preparation directed against human thymocyte/T‑lymphocyte antigens. It depletes circulating T lymphocytes and modulates immune responses, producing profound and prolonged T‑cell depletion which reduces the risk of graft rejection and treats certain immune‑mediated conditions.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Route of administration: intravenous infusion.
- Absorption: Not applicable (IV).
- Distribution, metabolism, elimination: Detailed pharmacokinetic parameters (volume of distribution, clearance, half‑life) are not specified in the available sources. The product is metabolized/degraded as a protein; available sources indicate no recommended dose adjustment in hepatic or renal impairment based on PK/metabolism findings.
- Immunogenicity: A substantial proportion of patients develop anti‑rabbit antibodies after treatment (e.g., 68% in one multicentre trial), with many developing antibodies by day 7 of therapy.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Produces T‑cell depletion measurable by decreased absolute T‑cell counts and subsets; clinical immunosuppressive effects correlate with the degree and duration of T‑cell depletion.

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Key trial summaries described in available sources
- Multicentre double‑blind randomized Phase III trial (renal acute rejection):
  - Population: Patients with biopsy‑proven acute rejection of a first or second renal allograft.
  - Comparator: Rabbit antithymocyte globulin vs. equine antithymocyte globulin (comparator product).
  - Size: 163 patients randomized; 82 received rabbit product in one report.
  - Outcomes: Safety profiles between treatments were broadly comparable; certain adverse events (e.g., leukopenia) were more frequently reported with the rabbit product in that dataset. Both groups reported three malignancies each during one‑year follow‑up, including two PTLDs in each arm.
  - Immunogenicity: Antibodies to rabbit proteins detected in approximately 68% of treated patients; many patients developed antibodies within 7 days.
- French post‑marketing study (00PTF01):
  - Population: 240 patients followed daily for 2 weeks, at day 30 and at 1 year.
  - Findings: Haematological events were common: neutropenia (<2.5 x10^9/L) occurred in 50% of patients, severe neutropenia (<0.8 x10^9/L) in 2%; thrombocytopenia <80 x10^9/L in 14% and <50 x10^9/L in 3%. Infusion‑associated reactions were common but transient in many patients.
- Additional clinical experience and post‑marketing reports document serious immune‑mediated reactions (anaphylaxis, severe CRS), opportunistic and reactivated infections (including CMV), thrombotic microangiopathy in certain contexts (notably with calcineurin inhibitors), and reports of malignancies including lymphomas/PTLD.
