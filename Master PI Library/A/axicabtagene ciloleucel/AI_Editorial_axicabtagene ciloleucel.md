# Unified Product Information: YESCARTA (axicabtagene ciloleucel)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** YESCARTA  
**Active Ingredient:** Axicabtagene ciloleucel  
**Formulation:** Suspension for intravenous infusion.  

YESCARTA is a CD19-directed, genetically modified autologous T-cell immunotherapy. It consists of the patient’s own T cells, which are harvested via leukapheresis and engineered ex vivo to express a chimeric antigen receptor (CAR). This CAR includes a murine anti-CD19 single-chain variable fragment (scFv) coupled with CD28 and CD3-zeta co-stimulatory domains.

## 2. Approved Indications
YESCARTA is indicated for the treatment of:
*   **Large B-cell Lymphoma (LBCL):** 
    *   Adult patients with relapsed or refractory LBCL after two or more lines of systemic therapy, including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) not otherwise specified, primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma, high-grade B-cell lymphoma, and DLBCL arising from follicular lymphoma [FDA].
    *   Adult patients with LBCL that is refractory to first-line chemoimmunotherapy or relapses within 12 months of first-line chemoimmunotherapy [FDA].
    *   *Note:* YESCARTA is not indicated for patients with primary central nervous system lymphoma [TGA].
*   **Follicular Lymphoma (FL):** Adult patients with relapsed or refractory FL after two or more lines of systemic therapy.

## 3. Contraindications
YESCARTA is contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of the excipients (including dimethyl sulfoxide [DMSO] and human albumin) [TGA]. Clinicians must also consider the contraindications associated with the required lymphodepleting chemotherapy regimen.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
*   **Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS):** Fatal or life-threatening CRS has occurred. Do not treat patients with active infections or inflammatory disorders.
*   **Neurologic Toxicities (ICANS):** Fatal or life-threatening Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome (ICANS) has occurred, sometimes concurrently with or following CRS resolution.
*   **Secondary Malignancies:** T-cell malignancies have been reported following treatment with CD19-directed genetically modified therapies [FDA].

### Clinical Precautions
*   **Monitoring and Proximity:** Patients must be monitored daily for at least 7 days at a qualified clinical facility. Following the initial week, patients should be instructed to remain within close proximity of the facility for at least 4 weeks [TGA] (Note: FDA recommends at least 2 weeks).
*   **Infection Risk:** Severe and opportunistic infections have been reported. Prophylactic antimicrobials should be administered according to local guidelines. Screening for HIV, HBV, and HCV must be completed before cell collection.
*   **Cytopenias and Hypogammaglobulinemia:** Prolonged Grade 3 or higher cytopenias (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anemia) may persist beyond Day 30 post-infusion. B-cell aplasia and hypogammaglobulinemia are expected; monitor immunoglobulin levels and manage with replacement therapy if recurrent infections occur.
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Allergic reactions may occur during infusion, potentially due to DMSO or residual gentamicin.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
**For Autologous Use Only.** YESCARTA must be administered in a qualified clinical setting by experienced healthcare professionals.

*   **Target Dose:** $2 \times 10^6$ CAR-positive viable T cells per kg of body weight.
*   **Maximum Dose:** $2 \times 10^8$ CAR-positive viable T cells.
*   **Required Lymphodepleting Regimen:** Cyclophosphamide (500 $mg/m^2$ IV) and Fludarabine (30 $mg/m^2$ IV) on the 5th, 4th, and 3rd day prior to YESCARTA infusion.
*   **Premedication:** Administer paracetamol/acetaminophen (500–1000 mg) and an H1-antihistamine (e.g., diphenhydramine 12.5 mg) approximately 60 minutes before infusion.
*   **Tocilizumab Availability:** At least 4 doses of tocilizumab should be available [TGA]. At minimum, 1 dose must be on-site at the time of infusion, with access to additional doses within 8 hours [TGA]. (Note: FDA requires 2 doses available prior to infusion).

### Preparation and Handling
*   Thaw the product at approximately $37^\circ C$ using a water bath or dry thaw method. 
*   Do NOT use a leukodepleting filter.
*   Infuse the entire contents within 30 minutes via gravity or peristaltic pump.
*   Once thawed, the product is stable for up to 3 hours at room temperature ($20^\circ C$ to $25^\circ C$).

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common non-laboratory adverse reactions (incidence $\ge 30\%$) include:
*   Pyrexia, CRS, hypotension, encephalopathy, and tachycardia.
*   Fatigue, headache, chills, nausea, and febrile neutropenia.
*   Musculoskeletal pain and decreased appetite.

**Severe Toxicities:**
*   **CRS (90–94% of patients):** Manifests as fever, hypotension, and hypoxia. Management requires tocilizumab and/or corticosteroids based on grading.
*   **Neurologic Toxicities (up to 87%):** Manifests as tremors, confusion, aphasia, and encephalopathy. Fatal cerebral edema has been reported.

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted. 
*   **Corticosteroids:** Avoid use as pre-infusion medication, as they may interfere with CAR-T activity, unless required for life-threatening emergencies.
*   **Vaccinations:** The safety of live viral vaccines has not been established. Vaccination with live vaccines is not recommended for at least 6 weeks before lymphodepletion, during treatment, and until immune recovery.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy (Category C [TGA]):** There are no data on YESCARTA use in pregnant women. It is not recommended for women who are pregnant. Sexually active females of reproductive potential should undergo pregnancy testing prior to treatment.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if YESCARTA is excreted in human milk. The potential risk to the breastfed infant should be weighed against the clinical need for the mother.
*   **Contraception:** There is insufficient data to recommend a specific duration of contraception following treatment.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
Following intravenous infusion, YESCARTA cells undergo rapid initial expansion, typically peaking within 7 to 14 days. After the peak, levels decline and generally reach baseline levels by 3 months. In clinical trials, higher Cmax and AUC (0–28 days) were positively associated with objective clinical responses in LBCL and FL patients. Neither age (21–80 years) nor gender significantly impacted the pharmacokinetic profile.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
YESCARTA binds to CD19-expressing cells (including malignant and normal B cells). Upon binding, the CD28 and CD3-zeta domains trigger a signaling cascade that results in T-cell activation, proliferation, and the secretion of inflammatory cytokines. This process leads to the targeted destruction of CD19-positive cells.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Long-term Storage:** YESCARTA must be stored in the vapor phase of liquid nitrogen at a temperature $\le -150^\circ C$ [TGA] (Note: FDA specifies $\le -120^\circ C$). 
*   The product must remain frozen in its patient-specific infusion bag and metal cassette until the patient is ready for treatment.
*   **Thawed Product:** Once thawed, do NOT refreeze. The product may be held at room temperature for a maximum of 3 hours.
