# Editorial Product Information: Ambrisentan

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ambrisentan for use by healthcare professionals. It is designed to synthesise clinical data into a coherent, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information. Clinicians should consult the local PI for prescribing within their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ambrisentan, an orally active propanoic acid-class endothelin receptor antagonist (ERA).
- **Brand Names:** **AMBRISENTAN VIATRIS** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C02KX02 (Endothelin receptor antagonists).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - 5 mg film-coated tablets (pink, round, biconvex, debossed with ‘M’ and ‘AN’) [Australian].
  - 10 mg film-coated tablets (pink, capsule-shaped, biconvex, debossed with ‘M’ and ‘AN1’) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd trading as Viatris [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH):** Indicated for the treatment of idiopathic PAH and PAH associated with connective tissue disease (PAH-CTD) in patients presenting with WHO functional class II, III, or IV symptoms [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended starting dose is 5 mg once daily.
- **Dose Escalation:** Additional clinical benefit may be achieved by increasing the dose to 10 mg once daily, depending on individual tolerability and clinical response [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
  - Tablets should be swallowed whole; they must not be split, crushed, or chewed.
  - Ambrisentan may be administered with or without food.
- **Dose Modification (Co-administration):** When used concurrently with **ciclosporin A**, the ambrisentan dose should be limited to 5 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Discontinuation:** Limited data suggest that abrupt cessation is not associated with rebound worsening of PAH [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Highly contraindicated due to teratogenic potential [Australian].
- **Women of Child-bearing Potential:** Contraindicated in women of child-bearing potential who are not utilising reliable contraception. Pregnancy must be avoided during treatment and for at least 3 months following discontinuation [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment (with or without cirrhosis) and those with baseline aminotransferases (ALT or AST) >3 times the Upper Limit of Normal (ULN) [Australian].
- **Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF):** Contraindicated in patients with IPF, regardless of the presence of secondary pulmonary hypertension [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ambrisentan or any excipients (including lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Teratogenicity [Australian]:** Ambrisentan is likely to cause serious birth defects if used during pregnancy. It is a Category X medication.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** While ambrisentan is associated with a lower incidence of liver enzyme elevations compared to other ERAs, hepatic injury and autoimmune hepatitis have been reported. 
  - **Monitoring:** Evaluate hepatic function prior to initiation. Monthly monitoring of ALT/AST is warranted for the first 6 months of therapy. 
  - **Discontinuation:** Therapy should be stopped if ALT/AST >5x ULN, or if elevations are accompanied by bilirubin >2x ULN or signs of clinical hepatic injury (e.g., jaundice) [Australian].
- **Haematological Changes:** Reductions in haemoglobin and haematocrit often occur within the first few weeks of therapy. 
  - **Monitoring:** Measure haemoglobin prior to initiation, at 1 month, and periodically thereafter. Treatment is not recommended in patients with clinically significant pre-existing anaemia [Australian].
- **Fluid Retention:** Peripheral oedema is a known effect of ERAs and may also indicate underlying PAH progression or heart failure. It is more frequent and severe in elderly patients and when used in combination with tadalafil [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Veno-Occlusive Disease (PVOD):** If acute pulmonary oedema develops, the possibility of associated PVOD should be considered; vasodilators may be life-threatening in these patients [Australian].
- **Hypotension:** Exercise caution in patients with pre-existing low blood pressure [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
  - Peripheral oedema and fluid retention (highest incidence within the first month).
  - Headache (including sinus headache and migraine).
  - Anaemia (when used in combination therapy) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** 
  - Gastrointestinal: Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, constipation, diarrhoea.
  - Respiratory: Nasal congestion, epistaxis, dyspnoea, cough, upper respiratory tract infections, sinusitis, nasopharyngitis.
  - Cardiovascular: Palpitations, flushing, hypotension, chest pain.
  - Systemic: Fatigue, asthenia, dizziness.
  - Laboratory: Increased hepatic transaminases.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** 
  - Hypersensitivity reactions (angioedema, rash, pruritus).
  - Syncope.
  - Hepatic injury and autoimmune hepatitis [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Cardiac failure (often associated with fluid retention), severe anaemia requiring transfusion, and hepatotoxicity [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Ciclosporin A:** Inhibits OATP and P-gp, leading to a 2-fold increase in ambrisentan exposure. Limit ambrisentan dose to 5 mg/day [Australian].
- **Ketoconazole:** Strong CYP3A4 inhibitor; increases ambrisentan exposure by approximately 35%. Monitor closely if co-administered with 10 mg ambrisentan [Australian].
- **Rifampin:** Transiently increases ambrisentan exposure (approx. 2-fold) upon initiation due to OATP inhibition. No long-term dose adjustment is required as levels stabilise by day 7 [Australian].
- **Phosphodiesterase Inhibitors (Sildenafil, Tadalafil):** No clinically significant pharmacokinetic interactions, though the combination with tadalafil increases the risk of peripheral oedema and anaemia [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** No clinically significant effect on PT or INR [Australian].
- **Digoxin:** Minor increase in digoxin $C_{max}$ (29%), generally not requiring dose adjustment [Australian].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** No significant effect on ethinyl estradiol or norethindrone pharmacokinetics [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X** [Australian]. Teratogenicity is a class effect of ERAs. Animal studies demonstrated abnormalities of the jaw, tongue, and palate.
- **Contraception:** Two reliable methods must be used. Monthly pregnancy tests are recommended. Patients must not become pregnant for 3 months after stopping therapy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established; breastfeeding is not recommended. Animal data showed reduced survival of pups and impaired reproductive capacity in offspring exposed during lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed testicular tubular atrophy and impaired fertility. Human data are inadequate to determine the risk to male or female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients <18 years of age. Safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian]. Animal studies in juvenile rats showed a decrease in brain weight and potential risks to the developing oropharynx [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is required, but caution is advised due to a higher incidence of peripheral oedema [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment required for mild-to-moderate impairment. Use with caution in severe renal impairment ($CrCl$ <30 mL/min) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
  - **Mild:** Use with caution; dose reduction may be necessary. 
  - **Moderate:** Not recommended.
  - **Severe:** Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ambrisentan is a selective endothelin type A ($ET_A$) receptor antagonist. By blocking $ET_A$, it inhibits phospholipase C-mediated vasoconstriction and protein kinase C-mediated cell proliferation, while preserving $ET_B$-mediated nitric oxide and prostacyclin production and endothelin-1 clearance [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; $T_{max}$ ~1.5 hours. Food does not significantly affect AUC.
  - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (98.8%), mainly to albumin.
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily glucuronidated via UGT1A9S, 2B7S, and 1A3S. Oxidative metabolism occurs via CYP3A4 and CYP2C19.
  - **Elimination:** Primarily biliary/faecal (66%); 22% recovered in urine. Terminal half-life is 13.6 to 16.5 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ARIES-1 and ARIES-2:** Two pivotal Phase 3, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies.
  - **Primary Endpoint:** Significant improvement in exercise capacity measured by the 6-minute walk distance (6MWD) at 12 weeks.
  - **Outcomes:** Placebo-adjusted mean increases in 6MWD were 30.6 m to 59.4 m depending on dose.
  - **Clinical Worsening:** Ambrisentan significantly delayed the time to clinical worsening (death, hospitalisation for PAH, or lung transplantation) compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Handling:** Keep in the original container (PVC/PVDC/aluminium foil blisters) [Australian].
