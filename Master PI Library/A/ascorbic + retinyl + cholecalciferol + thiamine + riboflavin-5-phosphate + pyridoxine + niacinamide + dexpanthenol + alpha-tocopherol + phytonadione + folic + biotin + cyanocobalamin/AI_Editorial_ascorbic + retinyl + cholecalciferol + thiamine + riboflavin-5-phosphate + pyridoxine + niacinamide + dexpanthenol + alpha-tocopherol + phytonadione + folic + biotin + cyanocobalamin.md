### Dosing by indication / pediatric dosing
- Recommended daily dosage is weight-based for pediatric patients receiving parenteral nutrition (combined volume of the two supplied vials added to IV fluid):
  - Weight < 1 kg: 1.5 mL daily
  - Weight 1 kg to < 3 kg: 3.25 mL daily
  - Weight ≥ 3 kg: 5 mL daily
- The above daily doses provide the individual vitamin amounts per administration (examples from mixed preparation):
  - Ascorbic acid (vitamin C): 24 mg (<1 kg), 52 mg (1–<3 kg), 80 mg (≥3 kg)
  - Vitamin A (as palmitate): 690 IU (0.2 mg), 1,495 IU (0.5 mg), 2,300 IU (0.7 mg)
  - Vitamin D (cholecalciferol): 120 IU (3 mcg), 260 IU (7 mcg), 400 IU (10 mcg)
  - Thiamine (B1; HCl): 0.4 mg, 0.8 mg, 1.2 mg
  - Riboflavin (B2; riboflavin-5-phosphate): 0.4 mg, 0.9 mg, 1.4 mg
  - Pyridoxine (B6; HCl): 0.3 mg, 0.7 mg, 1.0 mg
  - Niacinamide: 5.1 mg, 11.1 mg, 17 mg
  - Dexpanthenol (pantothenyl alcohol): 1.5 mg, 3.3 mg, 5 mg
  - Vitamin E (dl-α-tocopheryl acetate): 2.1 IU (2 mg), 4.6 IU (5 mg), 7 IU (7 mg)
  - Vitamin K (phytonadione): 0.1 mg, 0.1 mg, 0.2 mg
  - Folic acid: 42 mcg, 91 mcg, 140 mcg
  - Biotin: 6 mcg, 13 mcg, 20 mcg

### Preparation and administration instructions
- Do not administer as a direct, undiluted intravenous injection.
- Preparation steps (pharmacy admixture program use):
  - Combine contents of the two vials (transfer Vial 2 into Vial 1) to create the mixed solution.
  - After first vial closure puncture, complete dispensing from the pharmacy bulk vial within 4 hours.
  - Withdraw single-dose volumes (1.5 mL, 3.25 mL or 5 mL) from the mixed solution and add each dose to at least 100 mL of intravenous dextrose or saline prior to administration.
  - After dilution into an intravenous infusion, refrigerate the resulting solution unless used immediately and use within 24 hours after dilution.
  - Minimize exposure to light during handling and administration (some components are light-sensitive).
  - Visually inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before administration.
  - Do not add directly to intravenous lipid emulsions.

## 5. Contraindications

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Risk of Aluminum Toxicity:** The product contains aluminum that may accumulate and be toxic with prolonged parenteral administration, particularly in patients with renal impairment and in premature neonates. Periodic monitoring of aluminum levels should be considered in at-risk patients. Tissue loading can occur at low exposure rates; patients with impaired kidney function are at higher risk.
- **Allergic Reactions to Thiamine:** Intravenous thiamine may cause allergic reactions ranging from urticaria and bronchospasm to rare anaphylaxis. Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity.
- **Hypervitaminosis A:** Patients with renal failure or liver disease may be at increased risk of vitamin A toxicity; monitor vitamin A levels and use caution when supplementing these patients. Supplemental vitamin A may be required for low-birth-weight infants — monitor serum levels.
- **Decreased Anticoagulant Effect of Warfarin:** Contains vitamin K which can antagonize warfarin; monitor prothrombin time/INR and adjust anticoagulant dose as necessary.
- **Interference with Diagnosis of Megaloblastic Anemia:** Contains folic acid and cyanocobalamin which can mask deficiencies; avoid administration prior to diagnostic blood sampling for megaloblastic anemia detection.
- **Potential to Develop Vitamin Deficiencies or Excesses:** Periodically monitor blood vitamin concentrations, particularly when parenteral multivitamins are the sole vitamin source for prolonged periods. Additional doses of specific vitamins may be required if deficiencies persist.
- **Interference with Urine Glucose Testing:** Ascorbic acid (vitamin C) may produce false-negative urine glucose results.
- **Vitamin E Overdose in Infants:** Avoid additional oral or parenteral vitamin E supplementation in infants receiving this product because it may raise vitamin E blood concentrations and risk toxicity.
- **Risk of Low Vitamin A Levels Due to Adherence to Plastic:** Vitamin A may adhere to plastics leading to lower delivered concentrations; monitor vitamin A levels and consider additional therapeutic dosing if indicated, especially in low-birth-weight infants.
- **Risk of E-Ferol Syndrome in Low-Birth-Weight Infants:** Polysorbates present in the formulation have been associated with E-Ferol syndrome in low-birth-weight infants (manifestations reported with polysorbates include thrombocytopenia, renal dysfunction, hepatomegaly, cholestasis, ascites, hypotension and metabolic acidosis). No cases directly attributable to this product were reported in the available source, but exercise caution.
- Monitoring requirements (summary):
  - Periodic blood vitamin concentrations to detect deficiency or excess.
  - Aluminum levels for patients at risk of accumulation (e.g., renal impairment, premature neonates).
  - Renal function, calcium, phosphorus and vitamin A levels in patients with renal impairment.
  - Vitamin A levels in patients with hepatic impairment.
  - Prothrombin/INR in patients receiving warfarin.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Warfarin: Vitamin K in the product may reduce the anticoagulant effect of warfarin. Monitor prothrombin time/INR and adjust warfarin dose as necessary.
- Antibiotics: Thiamine, riboflavin, pyridoxine, niacinamide and ascorbic acid have been reported to decrease activity of certain antibiotics including erythromycin, kanamycin, streptomycin, doxycycline and lincomycin.
- Bleomycin: Ascorbic acid and riboflavin may inactivate bleomycin in vitro and could reduce its activity.
- Levodopa: Pyridoxine (vitamin B6) may increase metabolism of levodopa, lowering levodopa blood levels and reducing efficacy.
- Phenytoin:
  - Folic acid may increase phenytoin metabolism and lower serum phenytoin concentrations, potentially increasing seizure activity.
  - Phenytoin may decrease serum folic acid concentrations.
- Methotrexate: Folic acid may decrease response to methotrexate therapy.
- Hydralazine and isoniazid: Concomitant use may increase pyridoxine (vitamin B6) requirements.
- Clinical management notes:
  - Monitor relevant drug levels and clinical response when co-administering interacting agents.
  - Adjust doses of concomitant medicines (e.g., warfarin, phenytoin, levodopa) as clinically indicated and monitor laboratory parameters (INR, drug serum concentrations, vitamin blood levels) where appropriate.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Risk summary: Administration of the approved recommended dose in parenteral nutrition is not expected to cause major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal/fetal outcomes when used as indicated. Pregnant patients should follow recommended daily allowances for pregnancy because requirements may be higher.
- Clinical considerations: Maternal deficiency of key vitamins (e.g., folic acid) is associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes; parenteral multivitamin supplementation should be considered if oral/enteral intake is inadequate.
- Jurisdictional pregnancy category systems: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Risk summary: Vitamins contained in the product are present in human milk. Administration of the recommended dose in parenteral nutrition is not expected to cause harm to a breastfed infant.
- Recommendations: Lactating patients should follow RDA for lactation; weigh benefits of breastfeeding versus maternal clinical need for parenteral multivitamins and potential effects on breastfed child.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Approved for prevention of vitamin deficiency in pediatric patients up to 11 years of age receiving parenteral nutrition. [US]
- Premature neonates: product contains aluminum and premature neonates are at particular risk of aluminum toxicity; monitor aluminum levels during prolonged administration. Additional caution regarding polysorbates and E-Ferol syndrome in low-birth-weight infants; avoid additional vitamin E supplementation in infants receiving this product.
- Dosing: See section 4 (pediatric weight-based dosing).

### Elderly use
- Not specified in available sources.

### Renal impairment
- The product has not been formally studied in renal impairment. Monitor renal function, calcium, phosphorus and vitamin A levels. Patients with renal impairment may be at increased risk of aluminum accumulation and hypervitaminosis A.

### Hepatic impairment
- The product has not been formally studied in hepatic impairment. Monitor vitamin A levels in patients with liver disease.
