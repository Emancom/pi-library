# Editorial Product Information: Amoxapine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States PI was available for review; clinicians in other regions should consult their local regulatory authority (e.g., Australian TGA, European EMA).

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Amoxapine (a dibenzoxazepine class antidepressant).
- **Brand Names:** Asendin (historical reference; current provided source lists generic **Amoxapine Tablets, USP**). [US]
- **ATC Code:** N06AA17 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for amoxapine).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Oral tablets: 25 mg, 50 mg, 100 mg, and 150 mg. [US]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Actavis Pharma, Inc. (distributed for Teva Pharmaceuticals). [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- Relief of symptoms of depression in patients with neurotic or reactive depressive disorders. [US]
- Endogenous and psychotic depressions. [US]
- Depression accompanied by anxiety or agitation. [US]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** 50 mg two or three times daily. [US]
- **Titration:** Depending on tolerance, dosage may be increased to 100 mg two or three times daily by the end of the first week. [US]
- **Usual Effective Dose:** 200 mg to 300 mg daily. [US]
- **Maximum Dose (Outpatient):** If no response is seen at 300 mg, the dose may be increased up to 400 mg daily. [US]
- **Maximum Dose (Inpatient):** In hospitalized patients refractory to therapy with no history of seizures, dosage may be cautiously raised to 600 mg daily in divided doses. [US]
- **Administration:** Once an effective dose is established, the drug may be given as a single bedtime dose (not to exceed 300 mg). If the total daily dose exceeds 300 mg, it must be given in divided doses. [US]

### Maintenance Therapy
- Use the lowest dose that maintains remission. If symptoms return, increase to the previously effective level. [US]

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known prior hypersensitivity to dibenzoxazepine compounds. [US]
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Do not administer concurrently. A minimum of 14 days must elapse after discontinuing an MAOI before starting amoxapine. [US]
- **Myocardial Infarction:** Use is not recommended during the acute recovery phase following a myocardial infarction. [US]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Suicidality and Antidepressant Drugs [US]
- Antidepressants increase the risk of suicidal thinking and behavior (suicidality) in children, adolescents, and young adults (ages 18–24) during short-term studies of Major Depressive Disorder (MDD). 
- Amoxapine is **not approved** for use in pediatric patients. [US]
- Monitor all patients closely for clinical worsening and emergence of suicidal thoughts, especially during the first few months of therapy or during dose adjustments.

### Neuroleptic-Like Malignant Effects
- **Tardive Dyskinesia (TD):** Amoxapine has substantive neuroleptic activity. TD, a potentially irreversible syndrome of involuntary movements, may develop. Risk increases with duration of treatment and total cumulative dose. [US]
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** A potentially fatal complex (hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, autonomic instability) has been reported with amoxapine. Immediate discontinuation is required. [US]

### General Precautions
- **Seizure Disorders:** Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of convulsive disorders or overt/latent seizure disorders. [US]
- **Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Pupillary dilation may trigger an acute attack in patients with anatomically narrow angles. [US]
- **Cardiovascular Disorders:** Use with caution; tricyclic-class drugs can induce sinus tachycardia, conduction changes, and arrhythmias. Myocardial infarction and stroke have been reported. [US]
- **Urinary Retention:** Use with caution in patients with a history of urinary retention. [US]
- **Bipolar Disorder:** Screen patients for bipolar risk; antidepressants may precipitate a mixed/manic episode. [US]

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequent (>10%)
- Drowsiness (14%) [US]
- Dry mouth (14%) [US]
- Constipation (12%) [US]

### Common (1–10%)
- Blurred vision (7%) [US]
- Anxiety, insomnia, restlessness, nervousness, palpitations, tremors, confusion, excitement, nightmares, ataxia. [US]
- Edema, skin rash. [US]
- Elevation of prolactin levels. [US]
- Nausea. [US]
- Dizziness, headache, fatigue, weakness, excessive appetite, increased perspiration. [US]

### Uncommon/Rare (<1%)
- **Anticholinergic:** Mydriasis, delayed micturition, urinary retention, nasal stuffiness, paralytic ileus. [US]
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension, hypertension, syncope, tachycardia, arrhythmias, heart block. [US]
- **CNS/Neuromuscular:** Seizures, hypomania, extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS), tardive dyskinesia, NMS, tingling/paresthesias, tinnitus. [US]
- **Hematologic:** Leukopenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia. [US]
- **Allergic:** Drug fever, urticaria, photosensitization, hepatitis. [US]
- **Endocrine/Other:** Galactorrhea, breast enlargement, SIADH, change in blood glucose, painful ejaculation, weight gain/loss. [US]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Risk of hyperpyretic crises, severe convulsions, and death (see Contraindications). [US]
- **Anticholinergics:** Risk of paralytic ileus when combined with tricyclic-class drugs. [US]
- **CNS Depressants:** Amoxapine may enhance the response to alcohol, barbiturates, and other CNS depressants. [US]
- **Cimetidine:** May increase serum levels of tricyclic antidepressants; specific interaction with amoxapine is possible. [US]
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Drugs that inhibit cytochrome P450 2D6 (e.g., quinidine, SSRIs like fluoxetine/sertraline) can significantly increase amoxapine plasma concentrations, potentially leading to toxicity. [US]
- **Electroshock Therapy:** Concurrent administration may increase hazards. [US]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned by letter, but text-based]. Amoxapine should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Studies in animals showed fetotoxicity (intrauterine death, decreased birth weight) at 3–10 times the human dose. [US]
- **Breastfeeding:** Amoxapine is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women as the effects on infants are unknown. [US]
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed a slight decrease in male fertile matings and a reversible increase in female estrous cycle length at high doses. [US]

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established. Not approved for use in children. [US]
- **Elderly Use:** Lower starting dosages are recommended (25 mg 2–3 times daily). The elderly may be more sensitive to side effects such as tardive dyskinesia and sedation. [US]
- **Renal Impairment:** Amoxapine is substantially excreted by the kidney; monitor closely, particularly in elderly patients. [US]
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Consider potential for impairment in elderly populations; specific dose adjustments for hepatic failure are not provided in the source. [US]

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Not fully understood in humans. In animals, it reduces the uptake of norepinephrine and serotonin and blocks dopamine receptors. [US]
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits antidepressant activity with a mild sedative component and substantive neuroleptic activity. [US]
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak blood levels in approximately 90 minutes. [US]
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 90% serum protein bound. [US]
    - **Metabolism:** Almost completely metabolized; major active metabolite is 8-hydroxy-amoxapine. [US]
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal. Serum half-life is 8 hours (parent drug) and 30 hours (8-hydroxy metabolite). [US]

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Clinical studies demonstrate a more rapid onset of action compared to amitriptyline or imipramine. [US]
- Initial clinical effect may occur within 4 to 7 days; over 80% of responders show effect within 2 weeks. [US]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F). [US]
- **Packaging:** Dispense in a tight container with a child-resistant closure. [US]

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison schedule not specified (standard legend drug).
