## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated in combination with a corticosteroid (prednisone or prednisolone) for treatment of:
  - Newly diagnosed high‑risk metastatic hormone‑sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) in combination with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.1].
  - Metastatic advanced prostate cancer (metastatic castration‑resistant prostate cancer, mCRPC) in patients who are asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic after failure of ADT [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.1].
  - mCRPC in patients who have received prior chemotherapy containing a taxane [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.1].

## 4. Dosage and Administration
- General adult dosing:
  - Recommended dose: 1000 mg once daily (four 250 mg tablets) administered as a single daily oral dose on an empty stomach. Tablets must be swallowed whole with water. Patients should take the dose at least two hours after eating and should not eat for at least one hour after dosing. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]
- Corticosteroid co‑therapy (by indication):
  - For hormone‑sensitive metastatic prostate cancer (mHSPC): co‑administer prednisolone 5 mg once daily. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]
  - For metastatic castration‑resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC): co‑administer prednisolone 10 mg once daily (prednisone equivalent data presented in clinical studies). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]
- Administration instructions and food effects:
  - Must not be taken with food; dosing on an empty stomach is required (take at least two hours after a meal and do not eat for at least one hour after dosing). Tablets must be swallowed whole with water. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 5.2]
- Dose modifications for hepatotoxicity:
  - If ALT or AST rises to > 5 × ULN or bilirubin > 3 × ULN while on treatment, withhold abiraterone immediately until LFTs return to baseline; on return to baseline the patient may be re‑treated at a reduced dose of 500 mg once daily (two 250 mg tablets) provided benefit outweighs risk. If hepatotoxicity recurs at 500 mg daily, discontinue permanently. If ALT or AST rises to 20 × ULN at any time, discontinue and do not re‑treat. Reduced doses must not be taken with food. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4]
- Renal impairment:
  - No dosage adjustment is necessary for patients with renal impairment. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]
- Hepatic impairment:
  - No dose adjustment for pre‑existing mild hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh A). There are no data for multiple doses in moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh B or C). Use with caution in moderate hepatic impairment only if benefit clearly outweighs risk; do not use in severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.3; Section 5.2]
- Concomitant ADT:
  - Patients receiving an LHRH agonist should continue on that therapy. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]
- Missed dose instructions:
  - Not specified in available sources. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, no missed dose guidance in provided excerpt]

## 5. Contraindications
(Listed exactly as stated in the available source)
- Contraindicated in women who are or may potentially be pregnant. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3]
- Contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh Class C). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3]
- Combination contraindication: abiraterone plus prednisolone/prednisone is contraindicated in combination with radium‑223 dichloride (radium 223) (specific combination contraindication listed). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3; Section 4.4]
- Any other contraindications: Not specified beyond the above in available sources. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed warnings: Not specified in available sources as a boxed (highlighted) warning; however, multiple serious safety risks requiring monitoring are described. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- General warnings and precautions:
  - Mineralocorticoid excess effects: Abiraterone inhibits CYP17, leading to increased mineralocorticoid levels which can cause hypertension, hypokalaemia and fluid retention. Co‑administration of a corticosteroid reduces ACTH drive and lowers incidence/severity of these effects. Correct hypertension and hypokalaemia before initiating treatment. Use caution in patients with cardiovascular disease (including heart failure, recent myocardial infarction, ventricular arrhythmia). QT prolongation and Torsades de Pointes have been observed postmarketing in patients who develop hypokalaemia or have underlying cardiovascular disease while taking abiraterone. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
  - Hepatotoxicity: Marked transaminase elevations have occurred and may require drug interruption, dose reduction, or discontinuation. Monitor ALT/AST and bilirubin before treatment, every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then monthly thereafter; measure immediately if symptoms or signs suggest hepatic injury. For ALT/AST > 5 × ULN or bilirubin > 3 × ULN, interrupt treatment immediately until tests normalise; see dosing re‑introduction guidance above. Severe hepatotoxicity (ALT or AST 20 × ULN) requires permanent discontinuation and no re‑treatment. Patients with active or symptomatic viral hepatitis were excluded from clinical trials; safety in this group is unknown. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.2]
  - Corticosteroid withdrawal and adrenal insufficiency: Withdrawal of the concomitant corticosteroid may precipitate adrenocortical insufficiency; monitor for signs and consider corticosteroid coverage in stress situations. Abiraterone reduces glucocorticoid production via 17α‑hydroxylase inhibition. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
  - Metabolic effects: Use of concomitant glucocorticoids can increase hyperglycaemia—monitor blood glucose frequently in patients with diabetes. Isolated cases of hypoglycaemia were reported when abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone were used in patients with diabetes receiving pioglitazone or repaglinide—monitor blood glucose. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.5]
  - Use with chemotherapy: Safety and efficacy of concomitant cytotoxic chemotherapy have not been established. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
  - Use with radium‑223 dichloride: In a randomized clinical trial the addition of radium‑223 to abiraterone plus prednisolone showed increased mortality and fracture rate; radium‑223 is not recommended in combination with abiraterone plus prednisolone. If switching, do not initiate radium‑223 for at least 5 days after the last abiraterone dose when given with prednisolone. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Monitoring requirements:
  - Liver function tests (ALT, AST, bilirubin): prior to starting therapy, every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then monthly thereafter. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4]
  - Blood pressure, serum potassium and signs of fluid retention: monitor at least monthly. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4]
  - Blood glucose in patients with diabetes (due to corticosteroid co‑therapy): monitor frequently. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Specific clinical precautions:
  - Caution in patients with left ventricular ejection fraction < 50% or NYHA Class II–IV heart failure (safety not established in trial populations with such conditions). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
  - Avoid co‑administration with spironolactone because of potential androgen receptor interactions and PSA effects (use not recommended). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Summary of clinically relevant pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Enzyme inhibition (in vitro): Abiraterone is a strong inhibitor of CYP1A2, CYP2D6 and CYP2C8 and a moderate inhibitor of CYP2C9, CYP2C19 and CYP3A4/5 in vitro. Major metabolites (abiraterone sulfate and N‑oxide abiraterone sulfate) are strong inhibitors of CYP2C8 in vitro. Abiraterone and metabolites inhibit OATP1B1 in vitro. Clinical relevance is not fully defined for all in vitro observations. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
  - CYP2D6 substrates: Co‑administration increased dextromethorphan AUC ≈200% (with abiraterone plus prednisone). Caution with drugs metabolised by CYP2D6, especially narrow therapeutic index drugs; consider dose reduction. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
  - CYP3A4 interactions: Abiraterone is a CYP3A4 substrate. Co‑treatment with a strong CYP3A4 inducer (rifampicin 600 mg daily for 6 days) reduced abiraterone Cmax and AUC by ≈55% in a healthy volunteer study — strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampicin, rifabutin, rifapentine, phenobarbital) should be avoided or used with careful evaluation of clinical efficacy. Co‑administration with a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor (ketoconazole 400 mg for 6 days) had no clinically meaningful effect on abiraterone PK in a small study. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
  - CYP2C8 substrates: In a CYP2C8 interaction trial, pioglitazone AUC was increased by 46% when co‑administered with a single 1000 mg dose of abiraterone acetate. Monitor for toxicity of CYP2C8 substrates with narrow therapeutic indices (examples: pioglitazone, repaglinide). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
  - CYP1A2 substrates: A single‑dose theophylline study showed no increase in systemic exposure when co‑administered with abiraterone plus prednisone. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
  - Spironolactone: Use not recommended—spironolactone binds the androgen receptor and may increase PSA levels when used with abiraterone. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
- Management notes:
  - Avoid strong CYP3A4 inducers where possible; if unavoidable, carefully evaluate clinical response. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
  - Monitor for increased exposure/toxicity of CYP2D6 and CYP2C8 substrates; consider dose adjustments for narrow therapeutic index drugs metabolised by these enzymes. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
  - Consider blood glucose monitoring when co‑administered with antidiabetic agents, especially where interactions (pioglitazone, repaglinide) have been reported. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.5]

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - **Contraindicated in women who are or may potentially be pregnant.** Pregnancy category: Australian Category D. Maternal CYP17 inhibition is expected to alter hormone levels and could affect fetal development. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3; Section 4.6]
  - Animal data: Fertility reductions and reproductive system changes were observed in animal studies (rats, mice, monkeys) consistent with antiandrogenic activity; embryofetal developmental effects (reduced fetal weight and survival, delayed ossification, increased resorptions, decreased ano‑genital distance) were observed in rats at ≥10 mg/kg/day. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.6]
  - Semen precautions: It is not known whether abiraterone or metabolites are present in human semen. Condom use is required if the patient engages in sexual activity with a pregnant woman; if partner is a woman of child‑bearing potential, use condom plus another effective contraceptive method. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.6]
  - Handling precautions: Pregnant women should not handle tablets without protection (e.g., gloves) to avoid inadvertent exposure. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.6]
- Breastfeeding:
  - Abiraterone is not for use in women; it is unknown whether abiraterone or metabolites are excreted in human breast milk. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.6]
- Fertility:
  - Animal fertility studies show reversible reductions in fertility and effects on male reproductive organs consistent with pharmacologic antiandrogenic activity; reversibility observed in animal models. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.6]

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Not for use in children; paediatric use not approved. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Elderly:
  - No specific dose adjustment provided; safety data: in Phase 3 studies 70% of patients were ≥ 65 years and 27% were ≥ 75 years; adverse effects were more common in patients ≥ 75 years in both active and placebo groups. No formal dose adjustment guidance beyond general caution. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.8; Section 4.4]
- Renal impairment:
  - No dosage adjustment necessary for renal impairment (no specific eGFR/stage‑based dosing provided). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Mild (Child‑Pugh A): no dose adjustment specified. Moderate (Child‑Pugh B): no clinical data for multiple dosing; use only if benefit clearly outweighs risk and with caution. Severe (Child‑Pugh C): contraindicated (do not use). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.3; Section 5.2]

## 11. Pharmacology
- Mechanism of action:
  - Abiraterone is an inhibitor of CYP17 (17α‑hydroxylase/C17,20‑lyase), decreasing androgen biosynthesis, which may result in increased mineralocorticoid levels leading to hypertension, hypokalaemia and fluid retention (mineralocorticoid excess). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 5.1; Section 4.4]
- Pharmacokinetics:
  - Absorption: Oral tablets; significant food effect—must be taken on an empty stomach (at least 2 hours after food and avoid food for at least 1 hour after dosing). Tablets should be swallowed whole with water. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 5.2]
  - Metabolism: Abiraterone is a substrate of CYP3A4; major metabolites include abiraterone sulfate and N‑oxide abiraterone sulfate. In vitro data show inhibitory activity on multiple CYP enzymes and OATP1B1 by parent and metabolites. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5; Section 5.2]
  - Drug interaction PK data: Rifampicin (strong CYP3A4 inducer) reduced abiraterone Cmax and AUC by ~55% in a healthy volunteer study; ketoconazole (strong CYP3A4 inhibitor) had no clinically meaningful effect in a small study. Pioglitazone AUC increased ~46% when co‑administered with a single dose of abiraterone. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
  - Elimination: Specific clearance, half‑life numerical values and detailed distribution parameters are not specified in the provided excerpt. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 5.2—numerical PK parameters not provided in excerpt]
- Pharmacodynamics:
  - Clinical pharmacodynamic consequence of CYP17 inhibition is reduced androgen production and compensatory increase in mineralocorticoid activity (see warnings on hypertension/hypokalaemia/fluid retention). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 5.1; Section 4.4]

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key pivotal trials described in the available PI (design and context):
  - Study 3011: Phase 3 study in newly diagnosed high‑risk metastatic hormone‑sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) in patients continuing ADT (LHRH agonist or orchiectomy). Active arm received abiraterone acetate 1000 mg daily plus low‑dose prednisone 5 mg daily and ADT; control arm ADT plus placebo. Median duration of treatment with abiraterone ~24 months. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.8; Section 4.1]
  - Study 301: Phase 3 study in metastatic castration‑resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) patients who had received prior chemotherapy (including taxane). Active arm: abiraterone 1000 mg daily plus low‑dose prednisone/prednisolone 10 mg daily; placebo arm received prednisone/prednisolone. Average duration of abiraterone treatment ~8 months. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.8; Section 4.1]
  - Study 302: Phase 3 study in asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic, chemotherapy‑naïve mCRPC patients. Active arm: abiraterone 1000 mg daily plus low‑dose prednisone/prednisolone 10 mg daily; placebo arm received prednisone/prednisolone. Average duration of abiraterone treatment ~13.8 months. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.8; Section 4.1]
