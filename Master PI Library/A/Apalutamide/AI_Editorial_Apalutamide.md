# Editorial Product Information: Apalutamide

## Editorial Note
- Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for **apalutamide**, intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local regulatory documentation before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Apalutamide (Androgen receptor inhibitor).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ERLYAND** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L02BB05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Film-coated tablets: 60 mg and 240 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Janssen-Cilag Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic castration-sensitive prostate cancer (mCSPC):** Approved for use in combination with androgen deprivation therapy (ADT).
- **Non-metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (nmCRPC):** Approved for the treatment of patients at high risk of developing metastatic disease.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard recommended dose is **240 mg administered orally once daily**.
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Patients must also receive a gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) analogue concurrently unless they have undergone a bilateral orchiectomy.
- **Administration Instructions:**
  - Tablets should be swallowed whole; they may be taken with or without food.
  - **Alternative for Swallowing Difficulties:** Tablets (one 240 mg or four 60 mg) may be dispersed in 10–20 mL of non-fizzy water (do not crush or split). After 2 minutes, the mixture should be stirred and combined with 30 mL of orange juice, green tea, applesauce, drinkable yogurt, or additional water. The mixture must be consumed immediately.
  - **Feeding Tube Administration:** Tablets may be administered via a nasogastric (NG) tube (8 French or greater). Disperse the dose in a syringe with 10–20 mL of non-fizzy water, shake vigorously after 10 minutes, and administer immediately.
- **Dosage Adjustments (Toxicity):** 
  - For Grade $\geq$ 3 toxicity or intolerable adverse effects, withhold the dose until symptoms return to $\leq$ Grade 1 (or baseline). 
  - Resume at the original dose or, if warranted, a reduced dose of 180 mg or 120 mg.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible on the same day. The patient should return to the normal schedule the following day. Do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** **Apalutamide** is contraindicated in women who are or may become pregnant [Australian: Category D].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to the active ingredient or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ischaemic Cardiovascular and Cerebrovascular Events:** Significant events, including fatalities, have occurred. Monitor for signs of ischaemic heart disease and stroke. Optimise management of risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidaemia). 
- **Seizure:** Permanently discontinue **apalutamide** in any patient who experiences a seizure during treatment. Use with caution in patients with a history of seizures or predisposing factors.
- **Falls and Fractures:** **Apalutamide** increases the risk of falls and fractures. Evaluate fracture risk and manage according to established guidelines (e.g., bone-targeted agents). Falls were more frequent in elderly populations.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** Rare post-marketing reports of DRESS, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), which can be fatal. Discontinue immediately if signs appear.
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Cases (some fatal) have been reported. Interrupt treatment for unexplained worsening of pulmonary symptoms; discontinue if ILD is confirmed.
- **Hypothyroidism:** Reported in patients receiving **apalutamide**, particularly those already on thyroid replacement therapy. Monitor TSH levels and adjust levothyroxine as needed.
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** Consider ECG and electrolyte monitoring in patients with a history of QT prolongation or those taking medications that prolong the QT interval.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Effect of Other Drugs on Apalutamide:**
  - **Strong CYP2C8 Inhibitors:** (e.g., gemfibrozil) Increase **apalutamide** exposure (AUC increased by 68%). Use caution and consider dose reduction based on tolerability.
  - **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole) Increase **apalutamide** exposure (AUC increased by 51%). No initial dose adjustment is required.
- **Effect of Apalutamide on Other Drugs (Induction):**
  - **Strong Induction:** **Apalutamide** is a strong inducer of **CYP3A4** (e.g., midazolam AUC decreased by 92%) and **CYP2C19** (e.g., omeprazole AUC decreased by 85%).
  - **Weak Induction:** **Apalutamide** is a weak inducer of **CYP2C9** (e.g., S-warfarin AUC decreased by 46%) and transporters **P-gp, BCRP, and OATP1B1**.
  - **Management:** Avoid co-administration with sensitive substrates of these enzymes if substitution is possible. Monitor for loss of efficacy (e.g., monitor INR if co-administered with warfarin).
- **Digoxin Assay Interference:** **Apalutamide** may cause falsely elevated digoxin levels in chemiluminescent microparticle immunoassays (CMIA). Use alternative assays to confirm levels.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Apalutamide** is not indicated for use in women. It is classified as **Category D** in Australia. Potential for fetal harm due to mechanism of action.
- **Contraception:** Male patients with female partners of reproductive potential must use a condom and another highly effective contraceptive method during treatment and for **3 months** after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available; however, **apalutamide** is not indicated for use in females.
- **Fertility:** Based on animal data, male fertility may be impaired.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in effectiveness were observed between younger and older patients. However, those $\geq$ 75 years experienced a higher frequency of adverse events, including falls.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is needed for mild to moderate renal impairment. No data are available for severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease (eGFR $\leq$ 29 mL/min/1.73m²).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is needed for mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B) impairment. Safety in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) has not been studied.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Apalutamide is an Androgen Receptor (AR) inhibitor. It binds directly to the ligand-binding domain, prevents AR nuclear translocation, inhibits DNA binding, and impedes AR-mediated transcription. It lacks agonist activity.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Absolute oral bioavailability is ~100%; $t_{max}$ is 2 hours.
  - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (96%). $V_d$ is ~276 L.
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by **CYP2C8** and **CYP3A4** to the active metabolite, *N-desmethyl apalutamide*.
  - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in urine (65%) and faeces (24%). Half-life is approximately 3 days at steady state.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **TITAN (mCSPC):** A Phase 3 trial in 1,052 patients showing significant improvement in Overall Survival (OS) and radiographic Progression-Free Survival (rPFS) when **apalutamide** was added to ADT compared to placebo plus ADT. The hazard ratio (HR) for OS was 0.65 (p < 0.0001).
- **SPARTAN (nmCRPC):** A Phase 3 trial in 1,207 patients showing significant improvement in Metastasis-Free Survival (MFS). Median MFS was 40.5 months for **apalutamide** vs 16.2 months for placebo (HR 0.28; p < 0.0001). OS was also significantly improved (73.9 vs 59.9 months).
