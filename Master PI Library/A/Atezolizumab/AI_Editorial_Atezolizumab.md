# Editorial Product Information: Atezolizumab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **atezolizumab** for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulations and the most current official PI before administration.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Atezolizumab** (an Fc-engineered, humanised IgG1 monoclonal antibody).
- **Brand Name:** **Tecentriq**.
- **ATC Code:** L01FF05.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated injection for intravenous (IV) infusion (60 mg/mL). Available in 840 mg/14 mL and 1200 mg/20 mL single-use vials [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
[Note: All indications below are Australian-approved.]

- **Early-Stage Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** Adjuvant monotherapy following complete resection and platinum-based chemotherapy for adult patients with Stage II to IIIA (7th ed. UICC/AJCC) NSCLC whose tumours express PD-L1 on ≥ 50% of tumour cells.
- **Metastatic NSCLC:**
  - First-line treatment in combination with **bevacizumab**, **paclitaxel**, and **carboplatin** for non-squamous NSCLC. (In EGFR/ALK-positive patients, use only after failure of targeted therapies).
  - First-line treatment in combination with **nab-paclitaxel** and **carboplatin** for non-squamous NSCLC (EGFR/ALK-negative).
  - Monotherapy for locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC following prior chemotherapy.
- **Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC):** First-line treatment in combination with **carboplatin** and **etoposide** for extensive-stage SCLC (ES-SCLC).
- **Urothelial Carcinoma:** Monotherapy for locally advanced or metastatic disease in patients who are cisplatin-ineligible and whose tumours express PD-L1 (IC ≥ 5% of tumour area).
- **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC):** In combination with **bevacizumab** for patients with unresectable or metastatic HCC who have not received prior systemic therapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
**Atezolizumab** may be administered as a fixed dose using one of three regimens [Australian]:
- 840 mg every 2 weeks.
- 1200 mg every 3 weeks.
- 1680 mg every 4 weeks.

### Administration Instructions
- **Initial Infusion:** Administer over 60 minutes.
- **Subsequent Infusions:** If the first is tolerated, subsequent doses may be given over 30 minutes.
- **Precaution:** Do not administer as an IV push or bolus. Do not co-administer other drugs through the same line.
- **Preparation:** Dilute in 0.9% sodium chloride to a final concentration between 3.2 mg/mL and 16.8 mg/mL. Do not shake.

### Dose Modifications
- **Dose Reductions:** Not recommended.
- **Dose Withholding/Discontinuation:** Required for immune-mediated adverse reactions based on severity (Grade 1–4). Generally, Grade 2 requires withholding; Grade 3–4 requires permanent discontinuation (except for certain endocrinopathies).

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **atezolizumab** or any of the excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Immune-Mediated Adverse Reactions (IMARs):** Can occur in any organ system and may be fatal. Common IMARs include pneumonitis, hepatitis, colitis, and endocrinopathies.
- **Specific Overlap Syndrome:** Cases of concurrent myocarditis, myositis, and myasthenia gravis have been reported. These have a high mortality risk and require urgent, stringent management.
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs):** Monitor for hypersensitivity. Grade 3–4 reactions require permanent discontinuation.
- **Haemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis (HLH):** Reported in patients receiving **atezolizumab**. Discontinue if HLH is suspected.

### Monitoring Requirements
- Monitor liver function tests (ALT, AST, bilirubin) and thyroid function prior to and periodically during treatment.
- Monitor for signs of respiratory distress (pneumonitis) and changes in renal function.
- Evaluate HCC patients for the presence of varices within 6 months prior to starting combination therapy with **bevacizumab** due to bleeding risks.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect profiles vary by whether the drug is used as monotherapy or in combination.

### Monotherapy Frequency [Australian]
- **Very Common (>10%):** Fatigue (35.9%), decreased appetite, nausea, cough, dyspnoea, pyrexia, diarrhoea, rash, musculoskeletal pain, back pain, vomiting, asthenia, arthralgia, pruritus, headache, and urinary tract infection.
- **Common (1–10%):** Anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hypothyroidism, ALT/AST increased, infusion-related reactions, hypotension.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Hyperthyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, pancreatitis, Guillain-Barré syndrome, meningoencephalitis.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Myocarditis, hypophysitis, uveitis, facial paresis, myelitis, HLH.

### Combination Therapy Highlights
- Rates of anaemia (36.8%), neutropenia (35.8%), and thrombocytopenia (27.7%) are significantly higher when combined with chemotherapy.
- Alopecia is very common (26.4%) in combination regimens.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies performed. **Atezolizumab** is cleared via catabolism; metabolic interactions are not expected.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Use of systemic corticosteroids or immunosuppressants before starting treatment should be avoided as they may interfere with the PD-L1 inhibitory activity. However, they are essential for treating IMARs after therapy has commenced.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian: Category D]. Likely to cause foetal harm or death based on its mechanism of action. Animal models show an increased risk of immune-mediated rejection of the foetus. 
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use highly effective contraception during and for 5 months after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Unknown if excreted in human milk. Advise discontinuation of breastfeeding or the drug, considering the importance of the drug to the mother.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed reversible effects on the female menstrual cycle (lack of corpora lutea); no effect on male reproductive organs.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients <18 years. Early phase studies failed to demonstrate clinical benefit in this population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients ≥65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Data in severe impairment (eGFR 15–29 mL/min/1.73 m²) are too limited for recommendations.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Not studied in severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Atezolizumab** binds directly to PD-L1 and blocks interactions with the PD-1 and B7.1 receptors. This releases PD-L1/PD-1 mediated inhibition of the immune response, reactivating anti-tumour T-cell activity. It does not bind PD-L2, preserving some immune homeostasis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
  - **Steady State:** Achieved after multiple doses.
  - **Elimination Half-life:** Approximately 27 days.
  - **Clearance:** 0.200 L/day.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **IMpower010:** Demonstrated significant disease-free survival (DFS) improvement in adjuvant Stage II–IIIA NSCLC with PD-L1 ≥ 1%.
- **IMpower150:** Showed improved overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) in metastatic non-squamous NSCLC when **atezolizumab** was added to **bevacizumab** and chemotherapy.
- **IMpower133:** Showed OS and PFS benefit in ES-SCLC when added to carboplatin/etoposide.
- **IMbrave150:** Demonstrated superior OS and PFS compared to sorafenib in first-line HCC.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate).
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Do not shake. Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Diluted solution should be used immediately. If not used, it may be stored for up to 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C, or 8 hours at ambient temperature (≤ 25°C) [Australian].
