# Editorial Product Information: Anagrelide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and administration guidelines into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidance before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Anagrelide hydrochloride (an orally active quinazoline derivative).
- **Brand Name:** **Agrylin** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XX35 (Not explicitly in source, but standard for anagrelide).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 0.5 mg capsules [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Takeda Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Essential Thrombocythaemia:** Indicated for the treatment of patients with essential thrombocythaemia to reduce elevated platelet counts and the risk of associated clinical symptoms [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Starting Dose:** 1 mg per day, administered in two divided doses of 0.5 mg [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** The dose should be titrated to the lowest effective level required to maintain a platelet count between 150 x 10⁹/L and 400 x 10⁹/L (ideally) or at least below 600 x 10⁹/L [Australian].
- **Titration:** Dosage increases should not exceed 0.5 mg per day in any single week [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** 10 mg per day or 2.5 mg in a single dose [Australian]. *Note: Dose limitation is strictly advised due to potential hypotensive effects.*

### Administration Instructions
- Treatment must be initiated under close medical supervision.
- **Food Effect:** Food has no clinically significant effect on bioavailability, though it may slightly decrease Cmax and increase AUC [Australian].

### Hepatic Impairment Dosing
- **Moderate Impairment:** Start at 0.5 mg/day and maintain for at least one week with intensive cardiovascular monitoring [Australian].
- **Severe Impairment:** Use is contraindicated [Australian].

### Missed Dose and Discontinuation
- **Discontinuation:** Abrupt withdrawal should be avoided. Platelet counts typically rise within 4 days of cessation and may rebound to levels higher than pre-treatment values. Frequent monitoring is required during withdrawal to mitigate the risk of potentially fatal thrombotic complications, such as cerebral infarction [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to anagrelide hydrochloride or any capsule excipients [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Therapeutic doses can cause vasodilation, tachycardia, palpitations, and congestive heart failure. A pre-treatment cardiovascular assessment (ECG and echocardiogram) is recommended. Electrolyte imbalances (hypokalaemia or hypomagnesaemia) must be corrected before starting therapy [Australian].
- **QTc Prolongation:** Anagrelide may increase the heart rate and prolong the QTc interval. Caution is required in patients with congenital long QT syndrome or those taking other QTc-prolonging medications [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Hypertension:** Cases have been reported; patients should be assessed for underlying cardiopulmonary disease before and during treatment [Australian].
- **Bleeding Risk:** Increased risk of major haemorrhage when used concomitantly with aspirin [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Monitoring:** Close monitoring is required for patients with renal insufficiency (creatinine ≥ 0.18 mmol/L) or hepatic dysfunction (bilirubin/AST > 1.5x ULN) [Australian].
- **Lactose Intolerance:** Capsules contain lactose; not suitable for patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are most frequent at the start of therapy and generally decrease with continued treatment.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache (44.5%), palpitations (27.2%), diarrhoea (24.3%), asthenia (22.1%), oedema (19.8%), abdominal pain (17.4%), nausea (15.1%), dizziness (14.5%), dyspnoea (10.5%), flatulence (10.5%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Anaemia, thrombocytopenia, arrhythmia, tachycardia, angina pectoris, hypertension, pruritus, alopecia, rash, myalgia, arthralgia, paraesthesia, depression, insomnia, epistaxis. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pancreatitis, ventricular tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, pleural effusion, pulmonary hypertension, hepatic enzyme increase, confusional state. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Myocardial infarction, cardiomyopathy, cardiomegaly, renal failure, lung infiltration. |
| **Post-marketing (Unknown)** | Torsade de pointes, Prinzmetal angina, hepatitis, tubulointerstitial nephritis, cerebral infarction, interstitial lung disease (pneumonitis and allergic alveolitis). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Aspirin:** Potentiates anti-platelet effects. Concomitant use increases the risk of major haemorrhagic events compared to anagrelide alone [Australian].
- **PDE3 Inhibitors:** Anagrelide inhibits phosphodiesterase III; may exacerbate the effects of other PDE3 inhibitors (e.g., milrinone) [Australian].
- **CYP1A2 Interactions:**
    - **Inhibitors (e.g., fluvoxamine, ciprofloxacin):** May increase anagrelide exposure [Australian].
    - **Inducers (e.g., omeprazole):** May decrease anagrelide exposure, requiring dose adjustment [Australian].
- **Sucralfate:** May interfere with the absorption of anagrelide [Australian].
- **Warfarin/Digoxin:** No significant pharmacokinetic interactions observed [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3.** Anagrelide is not recommended for women who are or may become pregnant. Animal studies show disrupted implantation and increased embryonic death. If used, patients must be informed of potential fetal harm. Contraception is mandatory for women of child-bearing potential [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if anagrelide is excreted in human milk. Due to potential risks to the infant, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggests disrupted implantation in females at high doses; no effect on male fertility was observed [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 16 years of age have not been established. Pharmacokinetic data indicates lower exposure (Cmax and AUC) in children (7–14 years) compared to adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetic differences (higher exposure) in the elderly do not necessitate a different starting regimen or titration schedule [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) does not significantly alter the parent drug's PK but increases exposure to the active metabolite (3-hydroxy anagrelide) two-fold [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Moderate impairment results in an 8-fold increase in exposure. Use is contraindicated in severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
The exact mechanism is not fully elucidated. Anagrelide appears to suppress the expression of transcription factors (including GATA-1 and FOG-1) required for megakaryocytopoiesis, thereby reducing platelet production. It disrupts the postmitotic phase of megakaryocyte development, reducing size and ploidy [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapid; Tmax ~0.9 hours.
- **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via CYP1A2. The primary active metabolite is **3-hydroxy anagrelide**, which is more potent as a PDE3 inhibitor than the parent drug [Australian].
- **Elimination:** Half-life of parent drug is ~1.3 hours; active metabolite half-life is ~3 hours. >97% is excreted within 5 days, primarily in urine (79%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ET Studies:** In a study of 551 patients with essential thrombocythaemia, 73% of those receiving at least one dose responded (reduction in platelets to ≤600 x 10⁹/L or ≥50% reduction from baseline). Response typically occurs within 7 to 14 days [Australian].
- **Comparison with Hydroxyurea:** An investigator-led trial in high-risk ET patients compared anagrelide + aspirin to hydroxyurea + aspirin. The anagrelide group showed a higher incidence of arterial thrombosis (9.1% vs 4.2%), serious haemorrhage (5.4% vs 2.0%), and transformation to myelofibrosis (4.0% vs 1.2%) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Container:** Available in bottles of 100 capsules [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (S4)** - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
