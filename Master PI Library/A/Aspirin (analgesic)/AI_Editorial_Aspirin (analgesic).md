# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic name); a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over 24 hours [US].
    - Frequency: Every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - Maximum reported dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - Administration: Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to initiating therapy [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended; targets for free carnitine concentrations should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine):** While this PI covers the injection, it notes that the safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

### Other Serious/Notable Effects
- **Metabolic:** Body odor has been observed [US].
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), and Seizures (post-marketing) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Although bovine studies show increased carnitine in milk, the risks of excess intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, etc.) were negative. No long-term animal studies for carcinogenic potential have been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations are provided for metabolic disorders. Safe use in infants and children has been established for these indications [US].
- **Elderly:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The injection is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Oral formulations should be avoided in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioequivalence:** A study of 15 healthy volunteers confirmed bioequivalence between 330 mg tablets and oral solution (Cmax ~80 µmol/L) [US].
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed a significant increase in trough carnitine levels (from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks). A separate dose-ranging study (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) confirmed dose-proportional increases in plasma concentrations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Preservative:** The formulation contains no preservatives; discard unused portions [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
