# Editorial Product Information: Anakinra

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information provided by the Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **anakinra** for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulations and the current approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Anakinra (recombinant DNA origin). It is a recombinant, non-glycosylated form of the human interleukin-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1Ra) with an additional N-terminal methionine.
- **Brand Name:** **Kineret**
- **ATC Code:** L04AC03
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile, preservative-free solution for injection in a graduated pre-filled syringe. Each syringe contains 100 mg of anakinra in 0.67 mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Swedish Orphan Biovitrum [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):** Treatment of active adult RA in patients with an inadequate response to one or more disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (DMARDs); must be used in combination with methotrexate [Australian].
- **Cryopyrin-Associated Periodic Syndromes (CAPS):** Treatment of CAPS in adults and paediatric patients (aged 8 months and older, weighing ≥10 kg), including:
    - Neonatal-Onset Multisystem Inflammatory Disease (NOMID) / Chronic Infantile Neurological, Cutaneous, Articular Syndrome (CINCA)
    - Muckle-Wells Syndrome (MWS)
    - Familial Cold Autoinflammatory Syndrome (FCAS) [Australian].
- **Systemic Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (SJIA):** Treatment of active SJIA in patients aged 2 years and older who have failed to respond adequately to non-biological DMARDs [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** 100 mg once daily via SC injection [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **CAPS (Adults and Children ≥8 months/10 kg):**
    - *Starting dose:* 1–2 mg/kg/day. 
    - *Maintenance (Mild CAPS):* Usually maintained at 1–2 mg/kg/day.
    - *Maintenance (Severe CAPS):* May require titration to 3–4 mg/kg/day, with a maximum allowable dose of 8 mg/kg/day [Australian].
- **SJIA:** 2 mg/kg/day up to a maximum of 100 mg/day [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer at approximately the same time each day.
- Alternate injection sites to minimize discomfort.
- The syringe is graduated to allow doses between 20 mg and 100 mg. It is not suitable for patients weighing <10 kg [Australian].
- Prior to administration, allow the syringe to reach room temperature (approx. 30 minutes) and inspect for particulate matter (small translucent-to-white protein particles are acceptable; discard if discoloured or contains large clumps) [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to *E. coli*-derived proteins, **anakinra**, or any excipients [Australian].
- Concurrent use with TNF-alpha antagonists (e.g., etanercept, adalimumab) [Australian].
- **Anakinra** must NOT be initiated in patients with neutropenia (Absolute Neutrophil Count [ANC] <1.5 x 10⁹/L) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serious Infections:** Increased incidence compared to placebo (1.8% vs 0.7%). Do not initiate in patients with active, chronic, or localised infections. Exercise extreme caution in patients with a history of recurring infections or underlying conditions like poorly controlled diabetes [Australian].
- **Neutropenia:** Commonly associated with treatment. Monitor ANC before initiation, monthly for the first 6 months, and quarterly thereafter. Discontinue if ANC falls below 1.5 x 10⁹/L [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylactic reactions and angioedema have been reported uncommonly. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Events:** Reports of interstitial lung disease and pulmonary hypertension have occurred, predominantly in paediatric SJIA patients (particularly those with Trisomy 21). A causal link is not established, but monitoring is advised [Australian].
- **DRESS (Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms):** Rarely reported, primarily in SJIA. This can be fatal; discontinue if signs appear [Australian].
- **Immunisations:** Do not administer live vaccines concurrently. Complete all necessary vaccinations prior to starting therapy where possible [Australian].
- **Malignancies:** RA patients are at a higher baseline risk for lymphoma. While cases occurred in trials, the rate was consistent with general RA populations [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Table (General Population)
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (≥10%)** | Injection site reactions (ISRs), Headache, Total cholesterol increase |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Serious infections, Neutropenia, Thrombocytopenia |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Allergic reactions (anaphylaxis, angioedema, urticaria), Rash, Hepatic enzyme elevation |
| **Not Known** | Non-infectious hepatitis, Injection site amyloid deposits, DRESS |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Injection Site Reactions (ISRs):** Most common reason for therapy-related discomfort (71% in RA). Typically mild-to-moderate, occurring within the first 4 weeks and resolving within 14–28 days [Australian].
- **Serious Infections:** Including sepsis, pneumonia, and cellulitis. Four deaths were reported in long-term RA safety studies due to infection [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **TNF-alpha Antagonists:** Concurrent use with etanercept (and likely others) increases the risk of serious infections and neutropenia without added clinical benefit; this combination is contraindicated [Australian].
- **CYP450 Substrates:** Chronic inflammation suppresses CYP450 enzymes. By antagonising IL-1, **anakinra** may normalise CYP450 levels. This is clinically relevant for drugs with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., warfarin), which may require dose adjustment upon starting or stopping **anakinra** [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian: Category B1]. Reproductive studies in animals showed no harm to the foetus at doses 15–30 times the human dose. However, there are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Unknown if excreted in human milk. Naturally occurring IL-1Ra is present in breast milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on male or female fertility were observed in animal models [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for CAPS (≥8 months and ≥10 kg) and SJIA (≥2 years). Not recommended for paediatric RA (JRA) due to insufficient efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Caution is advised due to a higher baseline incidence of infections in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - *Severe/End-stage (<30 mL/min):* Consider dosing 100 mg every other day.
    - *Moderate (30–50 mL/min):* Use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - *Moderate (Child-Pugh Class B):* No dose adjustment required. 
    - *Severe:* Use with caution (not specifically studied) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive antagonist of the Interleukin-1 (IL-1) type I receptor. It blocks the biologic activity of both IL-1α and IL-1β, which are key mediators of inflammation, cartilage degradation, and bone resorption [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In CAPS, treatment reduces acute phase reactants (CRP, SAA) and IL-6 expression within the first weeks [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - *Bioavailability:* >80% (SC injection).
    - *Tmax:* 3 to 7 hours.
    - *Elimination:* Predominantly metabolised in proximal renal tubules.
    - *Half-life:* 4 to 6 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** A Phase 3 study (n=501) showed that **anakinra** (100 mg/day) + methotrexate achieved a 38% ACR20 response vs 22% for placebo at 6 months [Australian].
- **CAPS:** An open-label study (n=43) demonstrated significant and sustained reduction in disease symptoms (fever, rash, joint pain) and inflammatory markers (SAA, CRP) over 5 years [Australian].
- **SJIA:** Evidence from a small randomized trial (n=11) and a retrospective registry (n=306) supported efficacy, showing rapid decreases in CRP and ferritin and sustained clinical response [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Light:** Protect from light; store in the original carton.
- **Handling:** Avoid vigorous shaking.
- **Room Temperature Excursion:** May be kept at room temperature (up to 25°C) for a single period of up to 72 hours. Do not return to the refrigerator after it has reached room temperature [Australian].
