# Editorial Product Information: Acebutolol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Acebutolol hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Selective, hydrophilic beta-1 adrenoreceptor blocking agent with mild intrinsic sympathomimetic activity (ISA).
- **Brand Name(s):** Acebutolol Hydrochloride Capsules, USP (Amneal) [US].
- **ATC Code:** C07AB04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral capsules: 200 mg and 400 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amneal Pharmaceuticals LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Management of hypertension in adults. It may be used as monotherapy or in combination with other agents, particularly thiazide diuretics [US].
- **Ventricular Arrhythmias:** Management of ventricular premature beats. It is indicated to reduce the total frequency of premature beats, as well as paired and multiform ventricular ectopic beats, and R-on-T beats [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Hypertension:** 
  - Initial: 400 mg once daily. Some patients may require twice-daily dosing for 24-hour control.
  - Maintenance: 400 mg to 800 mg per day. Some patients are maintained on 200 mg per day.
  - Severe Hypertension/Inadequate Control: May respond to 1200 mg daily (administered as 600 mg BID).
- **Ventricular Arrhythmia:**
  - Initial: 400 mg daily (administered as 200 mg BID).
  - Titration: Increase gradually until optimal response is achieved, generally between 600 mg and 1200 mg per day.

### Administration Instructions
- Food intake does not significantly affect the area under the curve (AUC), though it may slightly decrease the rate of absorption and peak concentration.

### Titration and Withdrawal
- **Withdrawal:** If treatment is to be discontinued, the dose should be reduced gradually over approximately two weeks to avoid exacerbation of ischemic heart disease [US].

## 4. Contraindications
Acebutolol is contraindicated in patients with [US]:
- Persistently severe bradycardia.
- Second- and third-degree heart block.
- Overt cardiac failure.
- Cardiogenic shock.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiac Failure:** Beta-blockade may precipitate more severe failure in patients with diminished myocardial contractility. In patients with a history of heart failure controlled by digitalis/diuretics, use with caution; both agents impair AV conduction.
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Sudden cessation in patients with coronary artery disease can exacerbate angina pectoris or lead to myocardial infarction. Patients should be advised not to interrupt therapy without medical supervision.
- **Peripheral Vascular Disease:** Beta-antagonists can aggravate symptoms of arterial insufficiency in peripheral or mesenteric vascular disease.
- **Bronchospastic Disease:** Patients with bronchospastic disease should generally not receive beta-blockers. However, due to its relative beta-1 selectivity, low doses of acebutolol may be used with caution in patients who do not respond to or tolerate alternative treatments. A bronchodilator (e.g., theophylline or beta-2 agonist) should be available.
- **Major Surgery:** Routine withdrawal prior to surgery is not recommended, but the heart’s impaired ability to respond to reflex stimuli may increase risks associated with general anesthesia.
- **Diabetes and Hypoglycemia:** Beta-blockers may mask tachycardia associated with hypoglycemia; however, dizziness and sweating are typically unaffected.
- **Thyrotoxicosis:** Beta-blockade may mask clinical signs of hyperthyroidism (e.g., tachycardia). Abrupt withdrawal may precipitate thyroid storm.
- **Anaphylactic Reaction:** Patients with a history of severe anaphylaxis may be more reactive to repeated allergen challenge and may be less responsive to standard doses of epinephrine.

### Serious/Potentially Important Effects (Incidence <2%)
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension, bradycardia, heart failure, intensification of AV block.
- **CNS:** Anxiety, hyper/hypoesthesia, impotence.
- **Dermatological:** Pruritus, reversible alopecia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Vomiting, abdominal pain, mesenteric arterial thrombosis, ischemic colitis.
- **Hematologic:** Agranulocytosis, nonthrombocytopenic/thrombocytopenic purpura.
- **Autoimmune:** Reversible development of antinuclear antibodies (ANA) (infrequent symptoms of arthralgia/myalgia <1%); extremely rare systemic lupus erythematosus.
- **Liver/Biliary:** Increased SGOT, SGPT, LDH, bilirubin, or alkaline phosphatase. These are typically reversible upon discontinuation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Catecholamine-depleting drugs (e.g., reserpine):** May have additive effects, leading to marked bradycardia or hypotension (vertigo, syncope, or orthostatic changes).
- **Alpha-adrenergic stimulants:** Concomitant use (including OTC nasal decongestants) may result in exaggerated hypertensive responses.
- **NSAIDs:** May blunt the antihypertensive effect of beta-adrenoreceptor blocking agents.
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** Both agents slow AV conduction and heart rate; combined use increases bradycardia risk.
- **Absence of Interaction:** No significant interactions observed with digoxin, hydrochlorothiazide, hydralazine, sulfinpyrazone, oral contraceptives, tolbutamide, or warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category B [US]:** No evidence of teratogenicity in rats or rabbits. However, rabbit studies showed fetal growth retardation at maternally toxic doses. Acebutolol and its metabolite cross the placenta.
  - **Clinical Considerations:** Neonates of mothers treated with acebutolol may exhibit reduced birth weight, decreased blood pressure, and decreased heart rate. Monitoring of infants at birth is recommended.
- **Breastfeeding:** Acebutolol and diacetolol are secreted in breast milk with high milk-to-plasma ratios (7.1 and 12.2, respectively). Use in nursing mothers is **not recommended** [US].
- **Fertility:** No significant impact on reproductive performance or fertility was observed in rat studies at doses up to 240 mg/kg/day.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Bioavailability of acebutolol and its metabolite is approximately doubled in geriatric patients, likely due to reduced first-pass metabolism and renal function. 
  - **Dose Adjustment:** Start at the low end of the dosing range. Doses above 800 mg/day should be avoided [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Elimination of the active metabolite diacetolol is significantly reduced. 
  - **CrCl <50 mL/min:** Reduce daily dose by 50%.
  - **CrCl <25 mL/min:** Reduce daily dose by 75%.
  - Acebutolol and its metabolite are dialyzable.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Acebutolol is a cardioselective beta-1 adrenoceptor blocker. It possesses mild intrinsic sympathomimetic activity (ISA) and exhibits membrane-stabilizing effects at high concentrations (though not at clinical doses).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Well absorbed from the GI tract; absolute bioavailability is ~40% due to extensive first-pass metabolism.
  - **Metabolism:** Converted to an active N-acetyl derivative, **diacetolol**, which is equipotent to the parent drug but more cardioselective.
  - **Distribution:** Low protein binding (~26%). Minimal penetration of the blood-brain barrier.
  - **Elimination:** Acebutolol half-life is 3–4 hours; diacetolol half-life is 8–13 hours. Renal excretion accounts for 30–40%; nonrenal mechanisms (bile and intestinal wall passage) account for 50–60%.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hypertension:** Double-blind controlled studies showed efficacy superior to placebo and similar to propranolol and hydrochlorothiazide. Response was similar regardless of once-daily or twice-daily dosing regimens for most patients.
- **Arrhythmia:** Comparisons with placebo, propranolol, and quinidine demonstrated significant reduction in mean total ventricular ectopic beats (VEB), paired VEB, multiform VEB, and ventricular tachycardia.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from light. Keep container tightly closed. Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription drug).
