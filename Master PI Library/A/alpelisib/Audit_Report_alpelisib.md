# Originality Audit Report: alpelisib

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 88/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 2
- **Overall Assessment:** Pass

## Verbatim Copied Passages
1.  **Section 5 (Recommended Dosing):**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "300 mg (two 150 mg tablets) taken orally once daily. PIQRAY must be taken immediately following food at approximately the same time each day."
    *   **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 4.2, Page 2: "The recommended dose of PIQRAY is 300 mg (two 150 mg film-coated tablets) taken orally, once daily. PIQRAY should be taken immediately following food, at approximately the same time each day."
    *   **Location:** Editorial Section 5, Paragraph 1.

2.  **Section 5 (Dose Modifications):**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "Only one dose reduction is permitted for cases of pancreatitis"
    *   **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA), Table 1 footnote, Page 2: "Only one dose reduction is permitted for pancreatitis."
    *   **Location:** Editorial Section 5, Footnote/Note.

## Close Paraphrases
1.  **Approved Indications (Section 2):** The phrasing "treatment of adults (postmenopausal women and men) with hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, advanced or metastatic breast cancer" closely mirrors the formal regulatory definition in both Document 1 (Section 4.1) and Document 2 (Section 1). However, this is expected for regulatory compliance to ensure clinical accuracy.
2.  **Missed Dose/Vomiting (Section 5):** The "9-hour rule" and the instruction not to take an additional dose after vomiting are technical instructions that remain structurally similar to the TGA source (Section 4.2, Page 2), though the editorial has streamlined the wording significantly.
3.  **Mechanism of Action (Section 10):** The description of the "p110α subunit of PI3K" and "hyperactivation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway" uses the same technical sequence as Document 1 (Section 5.1), but synthesizes the information into a more concise narrative.

## Originality Strengths
The editorial excels in **Synthesis and Comparison**. It does not simply copy a single PI; it actively compares the two. A major strength is the **Harmonization of Safety Data**. For example, in Section 8 (Use in Pregnancy), the writer identifies the Australian "Category D" classification while simultaneously adopting the more conservative FDA "1 week" contraception window (the TGA source only requires 4 days). This demonstrates a high level of active editorial intervention and cross-referencing rather than passive copying.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Consolidated Lab Data:** The "Adverse Effects" section (Section 6) merges percentages and laboratory findings from various tables in the sources into a single, high-level summary.
- **Regulatory Attribution:** The editorial explicitly cites which agency (FDA or TGA) provided specific terms, such as the inclusion of "Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN)" which is highlighted in the FDA Highlights but not the primary TGA warning headers.
- **Streamlined Storage:** It combines the FDA's specific temperature range with the TGA's moisture protection requirement into a single cohesive instruction.
- **Simplified Excipients:** The contraindications list (Section 3) cleans up the chemical nomenclature found in TGA Section 6.1 (e.g., changing "iron oxide red CI77491" to "iron oxides").

## Recommendation
**PASS**

The editorial is a genuine synthesis. While two short technical phrases regarding the dose and pancreatitis are verbatim, these are standard clinical instructions where precision is required for safety. The rest of the document shows clear evidence of original drafting, intelligent merging of differing international standards, and a cohesive "editorial voice" that provides added value beyond the source documents. No revision is required.
