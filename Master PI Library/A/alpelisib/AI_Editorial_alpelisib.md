# Product Information: Alpelisib (PIQRAY)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Brand Name:** PIQRAY  
**Active Ingredient:** Alpelisib  

Alpelisib is available as film-coated tablets in three strengths:
*   **50 mg:** Light pink, round, curved with beveled edges; imprinted with “L7” and “NVR”.
*   **150 mg:** Pale red, ovaloid, curved with beveled edges; imprinted with “UL7” and “NVR”.
*   **200 mg:** Light red, ovaloid, curved with beveled edges; imprinted with “YL7” and “NVR”.

## 2. Approved Indications
Alpelisib is indicated in combination with fulvestrant for the treatment of adults (postmenopausal women and men) with hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, advanced or metastatic breast cancer. 

Patients must have a PIK3CA mutation as detected by a validated test (using tumor or plasma specimens) following disease progression on or after an endocrine-based regimen.

## 3. Contraindications
PIQRAY is contraindicated in patients with a history of severe hypersensitivity to alpelisib or any of its inactive excipients (including microcrystalline cellulose, mannitol, sodium starch glycolate, hypromellose, magnesium stearate, macrogol, talc, titanium dioxide, and iron oxides).

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Severe Hypersensitivity
Serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis and anaphylactic shock, have been reported. Symptoms may include dyspnea, flushing, rash, fever, or tachycardia. If severe hypersensitivity occurs, PIQRAY must be permanently discontinued.

### Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs)
SCARs, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), erythema multiforme (EM), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) [FDA], and drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS), have been reported. PIQRAY should be interrupted if SCARs are suspected and permanently discontinued if confirmed.

### Hyperglycaemia
Severe hyperglycaemia, occasionally associated with hyperglycaemic hyperosmolar nonketotic syndrome (HHNKS) or ketoacidosis (including fatal cases), has been observed. 
*   **Monitoring:** Test fasting plasma glucose (FPG) and HbA1c prior to initiation. Monitor FPG weekly for the first 2 weeks, then every 4 weeks thereafter.
*   **Management:** Consider metformin premedication in high-risk patients. Dose interruption, reduction, or discontinuation may be required based on the severity of the glucose elevation.

### Pneumonitis
Severe pneumonitis and interstitial lung disease (ILD) have occurred. Patients presenting with new or worsening non-specific respiratory symptoms (e.g., hypoxia, cough, dyspnea) should be evaluated immediately. Confirming a diagnosis of non-infectious pneumonitis requires permanent discontinuation.

### Diarrhoea and Colitis
Diarrhoea is a common adverse effect and may lead to severe dehydration and acute kidney injury. Colitis has also been reported in the post-marketing setting. Patients should be advised to initiate antidiarrheal treatment and increase fluid intake at the first sign of loose stools.

## 5. Dosage and Administration

### Recommended Dosing
The recommended dose is **300 mg** (two 150 mg tablets) taken orally once daily. PIQRAY must be taken **immediately following food** at approximately the same time each day. 

When used in combination, the recommended dose of fulvestrant is 500 mg administered intramuscularly on Days 1, 15, and 29, and once monthly thereafter.

### Dose Modifications
Management of severe toxicities may require dose reduction (maximum of two reductions allowed).
*   **Starting Dose:** 300 mg/day (two 150 mg tablets)
*   **First Reduction:** 250 mg/day (one 200 mg and one 50 mg tablet)
*   **Second Reduction:** 200 mg/day (one 200 mg tablet)
*   **Discontinuation:** If further reduction below 200 mg/day is required.

*Note: Only one dose reduction is permitted for cases of pancreatitis [TGA/FDA].*

### Missed Dose and Vomiting
*   **Missed Dose:** If within 9 hours of the usual time, take the dose with food. If more than 9 hours have passed, skip the dose for that day.
*   **Vomiting:** If the patient vomits after taking a dose, an additional dose should not be taken that day. Resume the normal schedule the following day.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is based on the SOLAR-1 trial. Very common adverse drug reactions (ADRs) (≥10%) include:
*   **Metabolic:** Hyperglycaemia (66%), decreased appetite, weight loss.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea (58%), nausea, stomatitis, vomiting, abdominal pain, dyspepsia.
*   **Skin:** Rash (52%), alopecia, pruritus, dry skin.
*   **General:** Fatigue, mucosal inflammation, pyrexia, peripheral oedema.
*   **Nervous System:** Dysgeusia, headache.
*   **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Increased creatinine, increased ALT/GGT, increased lipase, decreased lymphocyte count, decreased haemoglobin, and decreased calcium.

## 7. Drug Interactions

### Effect of Other Drugs on Alpelisib
*   **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** Co-administration significantly reduces alpelisib exposure (AUC decreased by ~74%). Avoid concurrent use of strong inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine, St. John’s wort).
*   **BCRP Inhibitors:** Alpelisib is a substrate of Breast Cancer Resistance Protein (BCRP). Caution is advised as inhibitors (e.g., lapatinib, pantoprazole) may increase alpelisib plasma concentrations.
*   **Acid-Reducing Agents:** While H2-antagonists and PPIs can decrease alpelisib solubility, the effect is mitigated when PIQRAY is taken with food.

### Effect of Alpelisib on Other Drugs
*   **CYP2C9 Substrates:** Alpelisib is a mild inhibitor of CYP2C9. Caution should be exercised when co-administered with sensitive substrates (e.g., warfarin).
*   **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Alpelisib is a time-dependent inhibitor and inducer of CYP3A4; however, no dose adjustments are currently required for co-administered substrates like midazolam.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
PIQRAY is classified as Pregnancy Category D [TGA] and can cause fetal harm based on its mechanism of action and animal data. 
*   **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for **1 week** after the final dose [FDA]. Male patients with pregnant or potentially pregnant partners should use condoms during treatment and for **1 week** after the final dose [FDA].

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if alpelisib is excreted in human milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child, women should not breastfeed during treatment and for **1 week** after the last dose [FDA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations (Tmax) are reached within 2 to 4 hours. Bioavailability is significantly enhanced by food (AUC increases by 73-77% with a high-fat meal).
*   **Distribution:** Alpelisib is 89% protein-bound. The volume of distribution at steady state is approximately 114 L.
*   **Metabolism:** Primary clearance occurs through non-hepatic hydrolysis (mediated by esterases and amidases), with CYP3A4 contributing to a lesser extent (~15%).
*   **Elimination:** The mean half-life is 8 to 9 hours. Clearance is approximately 9.2 L/hr under fed conditions. Excretion is primarily faecal (81%).

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Alpelisib is an orally bioavailable, phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K) inhibitor. it demonstrates higher inhibitory activity against the Class I PI3Kα isoform than other class members. 

Gain-of-function mutations in the *PIK3CA* gene (encoding the p110α subunit of PI3K) lead to hyperactivation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, driving cellular transformation and tumor growth. Alpelisib inhibits this pathway, reducing AKT phosphorylation. When combined with fulvestrant, alpelisib demonstrates synergistic anti-tumor activity in HR+ breast cancer cell lines.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store at 20°C to 25°C [FDA]; excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C. 
*   Keep the tablets in the original package to protect from moisture [TGA].
*   Ensure the medicine is kept out of reach of children.
