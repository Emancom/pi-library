# Editorial Product Information: acamprosate calcium

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **acamprosate calcium**
- Chemical class: Not specified in available sources
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval:
  - [Australian] First approval recorded: 06/03/2018
- Date of last revision/update of source PI:
  - [Australian] Last revision recorded: 18/07/2023
- Withdrawals, suspensions or restrictions: Not specified in available sources for any jurisdiction other than the contraindications below.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Maintenance of abstinence in alcohol-dependent patients as an adjunct to psychosocial support/counselling.
  - [Australian] Indicated to maintain abstinence in alcohol-dependent patients; should be combined with counselling.
- No other specific indications are described in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended treatment duration: up to 1 year.
- Treatment should be started as soon as possible after the alcohol withdrawal period and may be continued if relapse occurs.
- Dosage by body weight (based on source PI):
  - Adults weighing ≥ 60 kg: total daily dose ≈ 1998 mg (two 333 mg doses three times daily; expressed as 666 mg three times daily).
  - Adults weighing < 60 kg: total daily dose ≈ 1332 mg (two 333 mg in the morning, and one 333 mg at midday and at night).
- Lower doses may be ineffective. Efficacy and safety of higher doses have not been established.

### Administration instructions
- Administer with meals.
- Tablets should be swallowed whole.
- Food reduces the bioavailability compared with fasting administration.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to acamprosate calcium or any excipient.
- Pregnancy.  
  - [Australian] Specifically contraindicated in pregnancy (pregnancy category: B2).
- Breastfeeding/lactation — not to be administered to breastfeeding women.
- Renal insufficiency defined as serum creatinine > 120 µmol/L.
- Severe hepatic failure (Child–Pugh Classification C).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Acamprosate is not a treatment for the acute alcohol withdrawal period; initiation is recommended after withdrawal.
- Alcohol dependence can be associated with depression and suicidality; patients (including those receiving acamprosate) should be monitored for mood changes and suicidal ideation.
- Each dose unit contains calcium (reported as 33.3 mg of calcium per 333 mg tablet in the available source) — consider total calcium intake where relevant.
- Preclinical toxicity observations (see Preclinical Safety) included gastrointestinal and soft tissue effects in animal studies; relevance to humans is uncertain.
- The safety and efficacy have not been established in:
  - Severe hepatic impairment (Child–Pugh C).
  - Patients older than 65 years: safety and efficacy not established; the product should not be administered to the elderly [Australian].
  - Children and adolescents under 18 years: safety and efficacy not established; should not be administered to children [Australian].

### Monitoring requirements
- Renal function: use is contraindicated if serum creatinine > 120 µmol/L; pharmacokinetics relate to renal function (see Pharmacokinetics). Monitor renal function as clinically appropriate.
- Monitor for psychiatric symptoms, including depression and suicidality.
- Effects on routine laboratory tests: no specific data available in the sources.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Caution when co-prescribing with other psychotropic medicines; interaction studies have not been performed for all such drugs — monitor patients closely.
- Consider risk of hypercalcaemia in cases of prolonged/ chronic overdose.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Alcohol: Concomitant intake of alcohol and acamprosate does not affect the pharmacokinetics of either substance.
- Food: Administration with food reduces the oral absorption (bioavailability) of acamprosate.
- Naltrexone: Co‑administration produced an approximate 25% increase in AUC and a 33% increase in Cmax of acamprosate; this was considered of limited clinical impact and dose adjustment was not deemed necessary in the available data. Pharmacokinetics of naltrexone and 6‑beta‑naltrexol were not affected by co‑administration with acamprosate.
- Diazepam, disulfiram, imipramine: Pharmacokinetic studies showed no interaction with acamprosate.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Not specifically detailed beyond the lack of pharmacokinetic interaction with alcohol and the recommendation to monitor when combining with psychotropic medicines.

### Clinical management notes
- When initiating or adjusting co‑therapy with psychotropic medications for which interaction data are lacking, monitor for clinical signs of interaction and adverse effects.
- No routine dose adjustments required when co‑administering with naltrexone based on available data.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- [Australian] Pregnancy category: B2.
- Acamprosate crosses the placenta and distributes to fetal tissue in animal studies. Teratogenicity was not observed in mice, rats or rabbits at the doses studied in animals. Safety in pregnant women has not been established; acamprosate is contraindicated in pregnancy.

### Breastfeeding
- Excreted in milk of lactating animals. Safe use in breastfeeding women has not been demonstrated; acamprosate must not be administered to breastfeeding women. If a breastfeeding woman requires acamprosate treatment and cannot abstain from alcohol, a decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or discontinue acamprosate, taking into account the importance of the medicinal product to the woman.

### Fertility
- Animal studies (mice and rats) showed no adverse effects on fertility or reproductive performance at doses up to levels several times higher than human exposure based on body surface area estimates.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients younger than 18 years. Not recommended for children.

### Elderly use
- Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients older than 65 years. Not recommended for the elderly [Australian].

### Renal impairment
- Use is contraindicated in patients with renal insufficiency defined as serum creatinine > 120 µmol/L.
- Acamprosate is primarily renally excreted and pharmacokinetic parameters (apparent plasma clearance, renal clearance and half‑life) correlate linearly with creatinine clearance.
- Specific dose adjustments by eGFR or creatinine clearance staging (other than the stated contraindication threshold) are not specified in the available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Pharmacokinetics are not significantly altered by hepatic dysfunction generally; however, safety and efficacy have not been established in severe hepatic failure (Child–Pugh C) and use is contraindicated in that group.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Acamprosate has structural similarity to some amino acid neuromediators (e.g., taurine and GABA‑like structures) and includes an acetylation that may facilitate blood‑brain barrier passage. Pharmacodynamic studies have not consistently confirmed a specific neuromodulatory action at amino acid receptors.
- In animal models of ethanol dependence, administration during the induction phase reduced the withdrawal‑associated rise in extracellular brain glutamate concentrations.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Animal experiments indicate acamprosate reduces voluntary alcohol intake in dependent rodents without reducing food or total fluid intake. The precise mechanism in humans remains unclear.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Oral absorption is moderate, slow, sustained and markedly variable between individuals. Food reduces oral bioavailability. Steady state is reached by approximately day 7 of continuous dosing. Reported oral absorption in the first 24 hours is usually less than 10% of the administered dose.
- Distribution: Acamprosate is not protein bound. Following a 15‑minute IV infusion of 666 mg, the volume of distribution averaged about 72 L. Evidence for significant brain penetration after oral dosing is inconclusive in animal studies.
- Metabolism: Not metabolised to any appreciable extent.
- Elimination: Excreted primarily unchanged in urine. Apparent elimination half‑life after an oral 666 mg dose ranged from approximately 13 to 28.4 hours. Renal function (creatinine clearance) is linearly related to plasma and renal clearance and half‑life.

### Preclinical safety
- Genotoxicity: No evidence of genotoxicity in assays for gene mutations and chromosomal damage.
- Carcinogenicity: In long‑term dietary studies, no increased tumour incidence in mice up to doses approximating human exposure; in male rats at high dose (approx. twice human exposure by BSA) an increased incidence of adrenal pheochromocytomas was observed. Dietary calcium has been associated with adrenal medullary proliferative disease in rats; there is no evidence this poses a risk for humans.
- Other preclinical toxicology findings in animals included diarrhoea, hyperkeratosis and dysplasia of the stomach, soft tissue calcification, and increased deaths due to renal and cardiac lesions at some tested doses.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Pooled analysis and clinical programme: In a combined efficacy analysis pooling data from 3,338 detoxified alcohol‑dependent patients enrolled in multiple trials up to one year in duration:
  - Absolute abstinence at 1 year approximately doubled versus placebo: 22% for acamprosate‑treated patients versus 12% for placebo when abstinence was defined as no alcohol intake at all.
  - Treatment retention was higher with acamprosate: 50% retention in acamprosate group versus 40% in placebo at one year (statistically significant difference from Day 90 onward).
  - Cumulative Abstinent Duration (fraction of total abstinent days over total treatment exposure) was longer with acamprosate in 11 of 12 comparative trials (across studies: 53.3 ± 38.5% vs 40 ± 35.4% for placebo).
  - No evidence of rebound drinking or signs of medication withdrawal after stopping acamprosate were observed in the available clinical data.
- Trial details (individual trial designs, dosing regimens beyond those stated in dosing section, population characteristics and exact statistical measures beyond pooled outcomes) are not specified in the available sources.

## 13. Overdose
- Acute overdose: Reported cases generally benign; diarrhoea is the primary symptom attributable to acute overdose.
- Chronic overdosage: Risk of hypercalcaemia should be considered.
- Management: Supportive and symptomatic treatment. For specific management advice contact local poison information services as appropriate.
- Specific antidotes or extracorporeal removal recommendations are not specified in the available sources.
