# Editorial Product Information: articaine + epinephrine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **articaine + epinephrine**
- Chemical class:
  - Articaine: amide-type local anaesthetic (racemic mixture)
  - Epinephrine: sympathomimetic vasoconstrictor
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: submucosal (dental) injection
- Strengths/Concentrations referenced in available sources: articaine 4% (40 mg/mL) with epinephrine (as acid tartrate) at 1:100,000

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Local or regional anaesthesia for simple and complex dental procedures in:
  - adults
  - adolescents
  - children 4 years of age and older
- Indication limitation: indicated only for dental procedures

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Maximum recommended dose (adults, healthy): 7 mg articaine/kg body weight.
  - Example provided: for a 60 kg adult this corresponds to five (5) standard 2.2 mL cartridges (note: cartridge size described in source; cartridge pack details omitted per instructions).
- Typical onset: rapid (approximately 1–6 minutes).
- Typical duration for procedures (pulpal analgesia): approximately one hour (dependent on technique and procedure).

### Recommended volumes for common dental procedures (adult)
- Infiltration: 0.5 – 2.5 mL (corresponding to ~20 – 100 mg articaine)
- Nerve block: 0.5 – 3.4 mL (~20 – 136 mg articaine)
- Oral surgery: 1.0 – 5.1 mL (~40 – 204 mg articaine)
- These suggested volumes are guidance only; do not exceed the maximum recommended total dose (7 mg/kg).

### Paediatric dosing (children ≥ 4 years)
- Maximum recommended dose (children): 7 mg/kg body weight.
- Example dosing (from tabulated guidance):
  - For a 20 kg child:
    - Maximum dose (volume): 0.175 mL/kg → 3.5 mL total
    - Recommended dose for simple procedure: 0.06 mL/kg → 1.2 mL
    - Recommended dose for complex procedure: 0.07 mL/kg → 1.4 mL
  - For a 40 kg child:
    - Maximum dose (volume): 7.0 mL total
    - Recommended dose for simple procedure: 2.4 mL
    - Recommended dose for complex procedure: 2.8 mL
- Use in children under 4 years of age: not recommended (safety and effectiveness not established).

### Administration instructions
- Administer by submucosal injection only for dental procedures.
- Inject slowly (approximately 1 mL/min) and with frequent aspiration to reduce the risk of inadvertent intravascular administration.
- Use one or more cartridges on a single patient during a single treatment session only. If a cartridge is partially used, discard the remainder — do not reuse.
- An intravenous (IV) injection or intentional intravascular injection is contraindicated.

### Titration and missed doses
- Titration schedules: Not specified in available sources.
- Missed dose instructions: Not applicable / Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications stated in source material:
- Known hypersensitivity to articaine or to other amide-type local anaesthetics.
- Hypersensitivity to any component of the product (including excipients).
- Known hypersensitivity to sulfites (sodium metabisulfite present as antioxidant).
- Injection by the intravenous route.
- Inflammation or sepsis in the region of the proposed injection.
- Patients who have experienced bronchospasm after administration of any product containing sulfites.
- Patients known or suspected to have a deficiency in plasma cholinesterase activity.
- Patients receiving monoamine oxidase inhibitors (or who have received such agents within two weeks).
- Patients receiving tricyclic antidepressants.
- Patients in whom there is a possibility that general anaesthesia might be required to complete the procedure.
- Arterial hypertension (contraindication to the vasoconstrictor component).
- Coronary disease (contraindication to the vasoconstrictor component).
- Valvular cardiac disease (particularly sequelae to acute rheumatic fever) (contraindication to vasoconstrictor).
- Untreated thyrotoxicosis (contraindication to vasoconstrictor).
- Known sensitivity to sympathomimetic amines (contraindication to vasoconstrictor).
- Do not use in children under 4 years of age.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed / prominent warnings
- **Resuscitation preparedness:** When any local anaesthetic is used, resuscitative equipment and resuscitative drugs, including oxygen, must be immediately available to manage possible cardiovascular, respiratory or central nervous system adverse reactions.
- **Intravascular injection risk:** Injection must always be made slowly with frequent aspirations; inadvertent intravascular injection can produce systemic adverse effects even at low doses.

### General warnings and precautions
- Insert an intravenous cannula before administering major blocks because of the potential for hypotension and bradycardia following major nerve blocks.
- Delay in recognition and management of dose-related toxicity and inadequate ventilation can progress to acidosis, cardiac arrest, and death.
- The absence of blood on aspiration does not guarantee that intravascular injection has been avoided; continuous monitoring is required.
- Use with caution in patients with hepatic disease — articaine is partly metabolised by the liver and severe hepatic disease may impair normal metabolism leading to accumulation and potential toxicity.
- Because the product contains epinephrine, it is not advised for diabetic patients and for patients with thyrotoxicosis.
- Use the lowest effective dose to avoid high plasma levels; repeated doses may increase blood levels due to accumulation of drug or metabolites.
- Reduce doses in debilitated, elderly, acutely ill patients and children in proportion to age and condition.
- Use with caution in patients with heart block or abnormalities of cardiac conduction.
- Avoid use of solutions containing vasoconstrictors in areas supplied by end arteries or compromised blood supply; patients with peripheral vascular disease or hypersensitive vascular disease may have exaggerated vasoconstrictor response — ischaemic injury or necrosis may occur.
- Use with caution in patients during or following administration of potent inhalational general anaesthetic agents (risk of cardiac arrhythmias).
- Familial malignant hyperthermia: it is unknown whether amide-type local anaesthetics might trigger this reaction; ensure standard management protocol is available if general anaesthesia may become necessary.
- Use with caution in patients with prostatic hypertrophy and in patients with epilepsy.
- Not recommended in patients with deficiency of plasma cholinesterase activity.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor cardiovascular and respiratory vital signs and level of consciousness after each injection.
- Observe patients receiving dental blocks constantly for signs of central nervous system or cardiovascular toxicity.
- Watch for early neurological signs of toxicity: restlessness, anxiety, tinnitus, dizziness, blurred vision, tremors, depression, drowsiness.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Solutions containing epinephrine may produce transient increases in blood glucose and may reduce blood sugar control in diabetes; monitor accordingly.
- Avoid concurrent use with certain drugs that interact with sympathomimetics or that alter the cardiovascular response to epinephrine (see Section 8).
- Patients should be informed pre-procedure of temporary sensory and motor loss following injections.

### Serious adverse effects and toxicity signs (described in source)
- Systemic local anaesthetic toxicity may occur immediately (with inadvertent intravascular injection) or later (overdose). Early central nervous system symptoms described include:
  - nervousness, shaking, yawning, trembling, apprehension, nystagmus, logorrhoea, headache, nausea, buzzing in the ears
- Progression of toxicity can lead to confusion, convulsions, respiratory depression/arrest, cardiovascular stimulation or depression, atrioventricular block, ventricular arrhythmias, cardiac arrest, and death.
- Cardiac effects described: changes in conduction, excitability, refractoriness and contractility; severe toxic blood concentrations depress conduction and excitability leading to arrhythmias and cardiac arrest.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacodynamic interactions (noted cautionary combinations)
- Concurrent use with agents that have sympathomimetic properties — increased circulating epinephrine concentrations may produce clinically significant effects.
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) and tricyclic antidepressants: administration of solutions containing epinephrine may produce severe, prolonged hypotension or hypertension; use with caution or avoid concurrent use.
- Phenothiazines and butyrophenones: may reduce or reverse the pressor effect of epinephrine.
- Vasopressor drugs and ergot-type oxytocic drugs: concurrent use may cause severe, persistent hypertension or cerebrovascular accidents.
- Hypoglycaemic agents: epinephrine-induced hyperglycaemia may reduce glycaemic control.
- Beta-adrenoceptor antagonists (e.g., propranolol, metoprolol, timolol): epinephrine administration may result in dose-dependent hypertension with reflex bradycardia and possible heart block.
- Adrenergic neuron blocking agents (e.g., guanethidine): interaction expected because of epinephrine content.
- Quinidine: combination with epinephrine may precipitate cardiac arrhythmias.
- Cardiac glycosides (e.g., digoxin): epinephrine may interact resulting in cardiac arrhythmias.
- Thyroid hormones: may potentiate the actions of epinephrine.
- Inhalational anaesthetics: risk of serious cardiac arrhythmias if epinephrine-containing local anaesthetics are used in patients following inhalational anaesthetics.

### Other drugs to use with caution (as listed)
- Anti-arrhythmic agents (e.g., procainamide, mexiletine, disopyramide)
- Amiodarone
- Cimetidine
- Antiepileptic drugs (phenytoin, phenobarbitone, primidone, carbamazepine) — source lists phenytoin and other antiepileptics
- Phenytoin and other antiepileptic agents may interact; use with caution
- Antiepileptic skeletal muscle relaxant: Not specified in available sources beyond the phrase present in source material
- Management notes: when concurrent therapy is necessary, careful monitoring is essential; concurrent use of some combinations should generally be avoided.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- No formal drug interaction studies have been performed per source material.
- Articaine metabolism: in vitro data indicate approximately 5–10% of articaine is metabolised by human liver microsomal P450 enzymes; implications for interactions with P450-modulating drugs are not quantified in the available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
- Australian pregnancy categorisation: Category B3 [Australian].
- Clinical experience in pregnancy: no clinical data available in pregnant women in the source material.
- Use in pregnancy: should be used only when benefits outweigh risks.
- Animal data (summarised from source):
  - Subcutaneous administration of articaine with epinephrine during organogenesis produced no embryofetal effects in rabbits up to 40 mg/kg/day and in rats up to 80 mg/kg/day (approximately 2× maximum recommended human dose on a mg/m2 basis). Fetal death and increased skeletal variations occurred in rabbits at a maternotoxic dose of 80 mg/kg.
  - Articaine alone given to rats throughout gestation and lactation at 80 mg/kg/day increased stillbirths, delayed eye opening and impaired learning in pups, with maternal toxicity; 40 mg/kg/day did not produce these effects. A comparable study using articaine with epinephrine produced maternal toxicity without offspring effects.
- Fertility: In rats, no effects on male or female fertility were observed up to 80 mg/kg/day (approx. 2× maximum recommended human dose on a mg/m2 basis).

### Breastfeeding
- Excretion into human milk: Not specified in available sources.
- Recommendation from source: exercise caution when administering to nursing women; if administered, nursing mothers should not breastfeed for at least 48 hours following anaesthesia with articaine + epinephrine.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Approved/indicated for children aged ≥4 years (safety and effectiveness not established in children <4 years).
- Paediatric dosing guidance provided (see Section 4); maximum recommended dose 7 mg/kg.
- Use in children <4 years: not recommended.

### Elderly
- No overall differences in safety or effectiveness observed between elderly and younger subjects in the reported clinical experience, but increased sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be excluded.
- Clinical trials: small numbers reported some differences in need for additional injections (see Clinical Trials). Dose reduction should be considered for debilitated or elderly patients as clinically indicated.

### Renal impairment
- Specific dosing adjustments by eGFR or renal stage: Not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Use with caution in patients with hepatic disease because amide-type local anaesthetics are metabolised by the liver and severe hepatic disease increases risk of accumulation and toxic plasma concentrations.
- Specific dosing adjustments by severity: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- Articaine: amide local anaesthetic that blocks nerve impulse conduction by decreasing neuronal membrane sodium permeability, thereby inhibiting depolarisation and conduction of nerve impulses.
- Epinephrine: vasoconstrictor (sympathomimetic) added to reduce systemic absorption of local anaesthetic, prolong anaesthetic effect, and reduce bleeding.

### Pharmacokinetics (as reported)
- Absorption: onset of anaesthesia is rapid (approx. 1–6 minutes) following submucosal injection.
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Articaine is partly metabolised by plasma ester hydrolysis and to a lesser extent by hepatic metabolism; in vitro studies indicate approximately 5–10% is metabolised by human liver microsomal P450 enzymes.
  - Presence of plasma cholinesterase deficiency may alter metabolism (use contraindicated in known deficiency).
- Elimination: Not specified in available sources (e.g., half-life, renal excretion percentages not provided).

### Pharmacodynamics
- Onset and duration: rapid onset; pulpal analgesia duration about one hour depending on technique.
- Systemic effects at high plasma concentrations include CNS excitation followed by depression, and cardiovascular depression including conduction disturbances and arrhythmias.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal/primary clinical trial information summarized:
  - The adverse event dataset reported derived from clinical trials in the US and UK (as stated in source material).
  - Patient exposure: of 1,325 patients treated in the primary clinical trials, 882 were exposed to articaine 4% with epinephrine 1:100,000.
  - Efficacy observations: anaesthesia achieved rapidly (1–6 minutes); duration sufficient for most dental procedures (~1 hour pulpal analgesia); for most common procedures a single infiltration is sufficient.
  - Elderly: no overall differences in safety or effectiveness versus younger patients in the reported clinical data; a small proportion of older patients required additional injections for complete anaesthesia.
  - Paediatric trials: safety and effectiveness described for children and adolescents 4–16 years in trial summaries in source material (details not enumerated beyond dosing guidance and statement that doses of 7 mg/kg were not exceeded in clinical trials).

- Trial designs and primary outcomes: Not specified in detail in available sources (e.g., randomisation, comparator agents, primary endpoints beyond onset/duration and safety reporting are not provided).
