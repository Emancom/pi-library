# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources; listed as **Levocarnitine Injection, USP**.
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile injection solution, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours (by infusion or IV injection).
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough carnitine concentrations.

### Monitoring
- It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L).

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately.
- **Seizures:** Post-marketing reports indicate that seizures may occur in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Note):** While this document concerns the injection, the PI notes that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]).
- **Particulate Matter:** Parenteral products must be inspected visually for discoloration or particulate matter prior to administration.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations (uncommon), arrhythmia. |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia. |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional**| Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes. |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression. |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis. |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash. |

### Post-marketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **General:** Body odor, gastritis, and transient nausea/vomiting have been observed.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when **levocarnitine** is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating **levocarnitine** or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned in text, but animal studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm]. There are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women; use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. It is not known if it is excreted in human milk. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation for the mother. Consideration should be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 for weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) in metabolic disorders.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in the provided source.
- **Renal Impairment:** **Levocarnitine** is supplemental in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Levocarnitine** is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, **levocarnitine** injection increases plasma carnitine concentrations.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; contains no preservative.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only.
