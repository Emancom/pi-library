# Editorial Product Information: Anastrozole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult local regulatory guidelines for final prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Anastrozole (a potent and highly selective non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor).
- **Brand Names:** Anastrozole-GH [Australian].
- **Dosage Form:** Film-coated tablet.
- **Strength:** 1 mg.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Generic Health Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Early Breast Cancer:** Adjuvant treatment of postmenopausal women with estrogen/progesterone-receptor-positive early breast cancer [Australian].
- **Advanced Breast Cancer (First-line):** Treatment of postmenopausal women with estrogen/progesterone-receptor-positive advanced breast cancer [Australian].
- **Advanced Breast Cancer (Second-line):** Treatment of advanced breast cancer in postmenopausal women whose disease has progressed following tamoxifen therapy [Australian]. 
  - *Note:* Patients with estrogen-receptor-negative disease or those who failed to respond to prior tamoxifen therapy rarely show a response to anastrozole.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose for adults, including the elderly, is 1 mg taken orally once daily [Australian].
- **Early Breast Cancer Duration:** Recommended total duration of hormonal therapy is 5 years [Australian].
- **Switching Therapy:** For patients transitioning from tamoxifen, the switch should occur after 2 to 3 years of tamoxifen therapy [Australian].
- **Administration:** Tablets should be taken at the same time each day. Food may slightly decrease the rate of absorption, but it does not affect the overall extent of absorption to a clinically significant degree [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Anastrozole is strictly contraindicated during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Lactation:** Contraindicated in women who are breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to anastrozole or any excipient (e.g., lactose monohydrate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bone Mineral Density (BMD):** As anastrozole lowers circulating estrogen, it reduces BMD and increases fracture risk. Patients with or at risk of osteoporosis should have BMD assessed by bone densitometry at treatment initiation and regular intervals. Treatment or prophylaxis for osteoporosis should be initiated as appropriate [Australian].
- **Tendon Disorders:** Use of aromatase inhibitors is associated with tendonitis and tenosynovitis. Tendon rupture is a rare but potential risk. Patients should be monitored for signs of tendon disorders [Australian].
- **Menopausal Status:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in pre-menopausal women; use is limited to postmenopausal status [Australian].
- **Severe Impairment:** Anastrozole has not been investigated in patients with severe hepatic or severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min). Risk/benefit must be carefully weighed in these populations [Australian].
- **LHRH Agonists:** Safety and efficacy in combination with LHRH agonists have not been established; this combination should be avoided outside of clinical trials [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are generally mild to moderate.

| Frequency | Adverse Events |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Hot flushes, asthenia, arthralgia/joint stiffness, arthritis, osteoporosis, headache, nausea, rash, depression. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vaginal dryness, vaginal bleeding (usually in the first few weeks of therapy), hair thinning (alopecia), allergic reactions, vomiting, diarrhoea, somnolence, Carpal Tunnel Syndrome, sensory disturbances (paraesthesia, taste loss/perversion), increases in ALP, ALT, and AST, anorexia, hypercholesterolaemia, bone pain, myalgia. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypercalcaemia (with or without increased PTH), trigger finger, urticaria, increases in gamma-GT and bilirubin, hepatitis. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Erythema multiforme, anaphylactoid reaction, cutaneous vasculitis (including Henoch-Schönlein purpura). |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Stevens-Johnson syndrome, angioedema. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Ischaemic cardiovascular events (e.g., angina pectoris) have been reported, primarily in patients with pre-existing ischaemic heart disease. Tendon rupture has also been reported post-marketing [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Tamoxifen:** Must not be co-administered with anastrozole. Concurrent use reduces anastrozole plasma levels by approximately 27% and does not improve efficacy [Australian].
- **Estrogen-containing therapies:** Should be avoided as they negate the pharmacological action of anastrozole [Australian].
- **Excluded Interactions:** No clinically significant interactions were observed with bisphosphonates, warfarin, cimetidine, or antipyrine [Australian].
- **CYP450 Inhibition:** While anastrozole inhibits CYP1A2, 2C8/9, and 3A4 *in vitro*, clinically significant inhibition of metabolism at the 1 mg dose is unlikely [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. Anastrozole may cause fetal harm when administered to pregnant women. Animal studies indicate high rates of abortion and pre-implantation loss. It is contraindicated in pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if anastrozole is excreted in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data show reduced fertility and increased pre-implantation loss. Effects on human fertility are unknown [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended. Safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required. Pharmacokinetics are independent of age in postmenopausal women [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose change is recommended for mild to moderate impairment. Use with caution in severe renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose change is recommended for stable hepatic cirrhosis. Use with caution in severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Anastrozole is a potent, non-steroidal aromatase inhibitor. It blocks the conversion of androstenedione to estrone (and subsequently estradiol) in peripheral tissues, which is the primary source of estrogen in postmenopausal women.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** A 1 mg daily dose produces an estradiol suppression of greater than 80% [Australian]. It does not possess progestogenic, androgenic, or estrogenic activity.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak plasma levels typically occur within 2 hours (fasted).
    - **Distribution:** 40% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via N-dealkylation, hydroxylation, and glucuronidation. The major metabolite, triazole, is inactive.
    - **Elimination:** Slowly eliminated with a plasma half-life of 40 to 50 hours; primarily excreted via urine as metabolites [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ATAC Trial (Adjuvant Treatment Alone or in Combination):** A large phase III study (9366 patients) demonstrated that anastrozole was superior to tamoxifen in disease-free survival and time to recurrence for postmenopausal women with hormone-receptor-positive early breast cancer. It was associated with fewer hot flushes and vaginal events but higher rates of joint disorders and fractures compared to tamoxifen [Australian].
- **ABCSG 8 / ARNO 95:** These trials supported the strategy of switching to anastrozole after 2 years of tamoxifen, showing improved event-free survival compared to continuing tamoxifen for 5 years [Australian].
- **First-line Advanced Breast Cancer:** Pooled analysis of Trials 0027 and 0030 showed anastrozole 1 mg was at least as effective as tamoxifen regarding time to progression and objective response rate [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
