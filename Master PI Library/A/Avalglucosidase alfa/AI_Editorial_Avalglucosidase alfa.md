# Editorial Product Information: Avalglucosidase alfa

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesises information from the following jurisdiction's official Product Information: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of avalglucosidase alfa for healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local PI for legal and clinical compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Avalglucosidase alfa (a recombinant human acid $\alpha$-glucosidase produced in Chinese hamster ovary cells).
- **Chemical Class:** Alimentary tract and metabolism product; Enzyme.
- **Brand Name:** **Nexviazyme** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AB22.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Powder for injection, 100 mg per single-use vial. Upon reconstitution with 10 mL of sterile water, the solution contains 10 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi-aventis [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pompe Disease:** Long-term enzyme replacement therapy for the treatment of patients aged one year and older with Pompe disease (acid $\alpha$-glucosidase deficiency) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Standard Dose:** 20 mg/kg of body weight administered every other week.
- **Dose Escalation:** For patients with infantile-onset Pompe disease (IOPD) showing insufficient control or declining response, the dose may be increased to 40 mg/kg every other week [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute each 100 mg vial with 10 mL of Sterile Water for Injections. Inject the water slowly down the side of the vial to avoid foaming; tilt and roll gently without shaking.
- **Dilution:** Reconstituted solution must be diluted in 5% dextrose in water to a final concentration of 0.5 mg/mL to 4 mg/mL.
- **Infusion Setup:** Use an in-line, low protein-binding, 0.2 μm filter. Do not infuse with other products in the same line.
- **Rate of Infusion:**
  - Start at 1 mg/kg/hour.
  - Incrementally increase the rate every 30 minutes if no infusion-associated reactions (IARs) occur, following a 4-step or 5-step schedule depending on the patient type (LOPD vs. IOPD) [Australian].

### Pre-treatment and Management
- Pre-treatment with antihistamines, antipyretics, and/or corticosteroids may be considered to mitigate allergic reactions.
- If IARs occur, the infusion should be slowed or temporarily stopped. If severe hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate appropriate medical treatment [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Life-threatening hypersensitivity to avalglucosidase alfa or any excipient when re-challenge has previously been unsuccessful [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis:** Reported in approximately 60.6% of patients in clinical trials. Symptoms include respiratory distress, hypotension, hypoxia, and angioedema. Resuscitation equipment must be available during administration [Australian].
- **Infusion-Associated Reactions (IARs):** Reported in ~39.4% of patients. Higher risk is associated with higher infusion rates and acute underlying illness.
- **Acute Cardiorespiratory Failure:** Caution is required in patients susceptible to fluid volume overload or those with acute respiratory illness/compromised cardiac function [Australian].
- **Anesthesia Risks (IOPD):** Cardiac arrhythmia and sudden death have occurred during general anesthesia for central venous catheter placement in IOPD patients with cardiac hypertrophy. Extreme caution is required [Australian].
- **Immunogenicity:** Anti-drug antibodies (ADA) are common (95% in treatment-naïve patients). High ADA titres may correlate with a higher frequency of IARs [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are classified by frequency based on pooled clinical trial data (N=142).

| Frequency | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** ($\ge$10%) | Headache, Nausea, Pruritus, Rash, Hypersensitivity |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Anaphylaxis, Dizziness, Somnolence, Tremor, Tachycardia, Flushing, Hypertension, Cough, Dyspnea, Diarrhea, Vomiting, Urticaria, Erythema, Myalgia, Fatigue, Pyrexia, Chills, Chest discomfort, Increased blood pressure |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Conjunctivitis, Paresthesia, Lacrimation increased, Ventricular extrasystoles, Tachypnea, Laryngeal edema, Dysphagia, Angioedema, Infusion site pain/reaction |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory distress, chills, dyspnea, hypoxia, tongue edema, and hypotension [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No formal interaction studies have been conducted [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. There are no data on use in pregnant women. Animal studies in mice showed increased fetal loss at maternally toxic doses but no malformations. Use only if potential benefits outweigh potential risks to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available regarding presence in human milk or effects on the infant. Benefit-risk assessment is required before use during lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No clinical data available. Animal studies showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility in mice [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Evaluated in patients aged 1 to 12 years (IOPD) and 9 to 16 years (LOPD). No data available for patients younger than one year [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients over 65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild impairment. Not studied in moderate or severe renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and efficacy have not been studied in this population [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Avalglucosidase alfa is a recombinant human acid $\alpha$-glucosidase (rhGAA). It provides an exogenous source of GAA to degrade lysosomal glycogen. It is conjugated with hexamannose structures containing mannose-6-phosphate (M6P) to increase uptake into skeletal muscle via the cation-independent M6P receptor [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is ~3.4 L (LOPD) and 3.5–5.4 L (IOPD).
  - **Metabolism:** Expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids via non-saturable catabolic pathways.
  - **Elimination:** Mean plasma elimination half-life is ~1.55 hours in LOPD and 0.6–1.19 hours in IOPD [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **COMET Study (LOPD):** A double-blind, multinational study (n=100) compared avalglucosidase alfa to alglucosidase alfa. The primary endpoint was change in Forced Vital Capacity (FVC) % predicted in the upright position at 49 weeks. Avalglucosidase alfa demonstrated non-inferiority, with an LS mean improvement of 2.89% compared to 0.46% for alglucosidase alfa [Australian].
- **mini-COMET Study (IOPD):** Evaluated safety and efficacy in 22 paediatric patients. Data showed stabilization or improvement in motor function and cardiac mass in patients previously treated with alglucosidase alfa [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Should be used immediately. If not used immediately, it can be stored for up to 24 hours refrigerated (2°C to 8°C). Once at room temperature, it must be used within 9 hours (including infusion time) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
