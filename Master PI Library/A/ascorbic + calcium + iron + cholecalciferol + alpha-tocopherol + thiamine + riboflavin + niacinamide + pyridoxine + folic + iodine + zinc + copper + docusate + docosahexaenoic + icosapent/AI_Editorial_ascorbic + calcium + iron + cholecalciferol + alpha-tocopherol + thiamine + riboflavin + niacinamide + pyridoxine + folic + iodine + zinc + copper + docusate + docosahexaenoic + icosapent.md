## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
- Not specified as a boxed (black box) warning in available sources.

### General warnings
- **Accidental iron overdose:** Iron-containing products are a leading cause of fatal accidental poisoning in children under 6 years of age. This product must be kept out of the reach of children. In the event of accidental ingestion, a physician or poison control centre should be contacted without delay.
- **Omega-3 fatty acids and bleeding risk:** Daily intake of omega-3 fatty acids exceeding 3 grams has been shown to have potential antithrombotic effects, including prolonged bleeding time and elevated INR. Use of omega-3 fatty acids should be avoided in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy and in those with known inherited or acquired bleeding diatheses.
- **Folic acid and megaloblastic anaemia:** Folic acid used as sole therapy is not appropriate for pernicious anaemia or other megaloblastic anaemias arising from vitamin B12 deficiency. At doses above 0.1 mg daily, folic acid may obscure pernicious anaemia by producing haematological improvement while neurological deterioration continues.

### Precautions / specific clinical precautions
- The total daily DHA dose from all sources should not exceed 1 g/day; caution should be exercised when this product is co-prescribed with other omega-3-containing products.
- Patients on anticoagulant therapy should avoid omega-3 fatty acid supplementation due to the risk of enhanced antithrombotic effects.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- **Anticoagulants and omega-3 fatty acids:** High-dose omega-3 fatty acids (exceeding 3 g/day) are associated with antithrombotic effects including prolonged bleeding time and elevated INR. Co-administration with anticoagulants should be avoided.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Not specified in available sources.

### Interactions related to masking of disease
- Folic acid at doses above 0.1 mg/day may mask the haematological signs of pernicious anaemia due to vitamin B12 deficiency, allowing neurological deterioration to proceed unrecognised. This is clinically relevant when evaluating patients for megaloblastic anaemia.

### Management notes
- Administration of omega-3 fatty acids should be avoided in patients on anticoagulant therapy and in those with inherited or acquired bleeding diatheses, as stated in product warnings.
- The source specifies that the prescribed daily DHA dose should not exceed 1 g/day.
