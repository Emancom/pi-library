# Editorial Product Information: Amifampridine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, cohesive reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes for healthcare professionals. it is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Amifampridine (3,4-diaminopyridine).
- **Chemical Class:** Potassium channel blocker; quaternary ammonium compound.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Ruzurgi** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 10 mg tablets. The tablets are functionally scored, oval, white to off-white, debossed with “10 | 110” on one side and “LACUNA” on the other [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lacuna Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome (LEMS):** Indicated for the treatment of LEMS in adults and paediatric patients aged 6 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
Dosing must be individualised based on clinical response, weight, and tolerability. The objective is to titrate to the lowest effective dose. 

### Adult and Paediatric Dosing (Aged 6 Years and Older)
Dosage is determined by body weight [Australian]:

| Patient Weight | Initial Daily Dose | Titration Regimen | Max Single Dose | Max Daily Dose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **< 45 kg** | 5 mg to 10 mg (divided 2–3 times/day) | Increase by 2.5 mg to 5 mg increments daily (up to 5 doses/day) | 10 mg | 40 mg |
| **≥ 45 kg** | 10 mg to 20 mg (divided 2–3 times/day) | Increase by 5 mg to 10 mg increments daily (up to 5 doses/day) | 20 mg | 80 mg* |

*\*Note: Some patients may benefit from a total daily dose of up to 100 mg [Australian].*

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken without regard to food [Australian].
- Tablets should be swallowed with a glass of water.
- **Oral Suspension (1 mg/mL):** For patients requiring increments smaller than 5 mg, those with swallowing difficulties, or those using feeding tubes, a suspension can be prepared. Three 10 mg tablets are placed in 30 mL of sterile water and shaken for 30 seconds. The suspension must be refrigerated and is stable for 24 hours [Australian].

### Missed Dose
If a dose is missed by a few hours and the patient experiences weakness, the dose should be taken as soon as possible. If it is close to the next scheduled dose, the patient should skip the missed dose and resume the regular schedule. Doubling doses is prohibited [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- History of seizures [Australian].
- Hypersensitivity to amifampridine or other aminopyridines [Australian].
- Concurrent use of other forms of amifampridine or other aminopyridines [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Seizures:** **Amifampridine** can cause seizures. This risk may be dose-dependent and has been observed in patients with and without a history of epilepsy. Use with extreme caution in patients taking concomitant medications that lower the seizure threshold [Australian].
- **QTc Prolongation:** Supratherapeutic doses (e.g., 120 mg/day) have been associated with QTc interval prolongation. Caution is advised in patients with risk factors for Torsade de Pointes or those with a slow acetylator (NAT2) phenotype [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** While anaphylaxis was not reported in **Ruzurgi** clinical trials, it has occurred with other aminopyridines. Discontinue immediately if clinical signs of hypersensitivity appear [Australian].
- **NAT2 Poor Metabolisers:** Individuals who are N-acetyltransferase 2 (NAT2) poor metabolisers (slow acetylators) experience higher drug exposure. These patients require more conservative titration and lower starting doses [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on a thorough QT study (120 mg dose) and expanded access data [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dysaesthesia (48%), Oral dysaesthesia (29%), Abdominal pain (25%), Dyspepsia (17%), Dizziness (12%), Nausea (10%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Back pain (8%), Hypoaesthesia (6%), Muscle spasms (6%), Diarrhoea (2%), Paraesthesia (2%), Dysgeusia (2%). |

### Expanded Access Experience
Additional reactions reported in ≥5% of patients in long-term use include falls, pneumonia, small cell lung cancer (often associated with paraneoplastic LEMS), dyspnoea, arthralgia, depression, and headache [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Seizures.
- Cardiac arrhythmias (associated with QTc prolongation).
- Hypersensitivity/Anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Seizure Threshold Lowering Drugs:** Concomitant use increases the risk of seizures [Australian].
- **Cholinergic Drugs:** Concurrent use with cholinesterase inhibitors (e.g., pyridostigmine) may potentiate cholinergic effects and increase the risk of adverse reactions [Australian].
- **Cytochrome P450:** Amifampridine is not a substrate, inducer, or inhibitor of major CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4) [Australian].
- **Transporters:** Amifampridine is unlikely to be a substrate or inhibitor of P-gp, BCRP, OAT1, OAT3, OCT2, OATP1B1, or OATP1B3 [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal data is limited. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks to the fetus. Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data exists regarding the presence of amifampridine in human milk or its effects on the infant. The clinical need for the mother must be weighed against the potential risks to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal fertility studies have not been conducted [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for ages 6–17. Safety and efficacy for children under 6 have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical data showed no overall differences in safety/efficacy compared to younger adults. However, because elderly patients are more likely to have reduced renal function, dose selection should be cautious with frequent monitoring [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Amifampridine and its metabolites are cleared renally. Titrate more slowly in moderate or severe impairment; consider dose reduction or discontinuation based on clinical tolerance [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in patients with hepatic impairment. As it is extensively metabolised by NAT2, hepatic impairment may increase exposure. Use the lowest starting dose and monitor closely [Australian].
- **NAT2 Poor Metabolisers:** Starting dose should be 5 mg/day (for <45 kg) or 10 mg/day (for ≥45 kg) in known slow acetylators [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A broad-spectrum potassium channel blocker. By blocking presynaptic potassium channels, it prolongs the action potential and increases calcium influx into nerve terminals, thereby enhancing the release of acetylcholine into the neuromuscular junction [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Enhances muscle strength in LEMS.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; median $T_{max}$ is 0.5 hours (fasted). High-fat meals decrease $C_{max}$ by 41–52% and delay $T_{max}$ to 1 hour [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Moderate to high volume of distribution; plasma protein binding is 25.3% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by N-acetyltransferase 2 (NAT2) to the inactive metabolite 3-N-acetyl amifampridine [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (80–90% recovery in urine). Mean elimination half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is 3.6 to 4.2 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
The efficacy of **Ruzurgi** was established in the **DAPPER** study, a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled withdrawal trial. Patients on a stable dose (30–100 mg/day) were randomised to continue treatment or taper to placebo.
- **Primary Outcome:** 72% of patients tapered to placebo experienced >30% deterioration in the Triple Timed Up and Go (3TUG) test, compared to 0% in the continued treatment group ($p < 0.0001$) [Australian].
- **Secondary Outcome:** The self-assessment scale for LEMS-related weakness (W-SAS) showed significant worsening in the placebo group compared to the treatment group [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Prior to Dispensing:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Keep container tightly closed with desiccant [Australian].
- **After Dispensing:** Store below 25°C for up to 3 months in the original container. Protect from moisture and light [Australian].
- **Oral Suspension:** Stable under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C) for up to 24 hours [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
