# Editorial Product Information: Aflibercept (**Afqlir**)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of aflibercept (specifically the biosimilar **Afqlir**) for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and administration guidelines into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the local PI for the most current and legally binding information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Aflibercept.
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant fusion protein (human VEGF receptor 1 and 2 extracellular domains fused to the Fc portion of human IgG1).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Afqlir** [Australian].
- **Biosimilar Status:** **Afqlir** is a biosimilar to **Eylea** (aflibercept) 2 mg [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** S01LA05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Solution for intravitreal injection. 
    - 40 mg/mL available in 2 mg single-dose vials or pre-filled syringes [Australian].
    - **Note:** **Afqlir** is only available as a 2 mg dose; an 8 mg formulation of this brand is not available [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravitreal injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Afqlir** (aflibercept) 2 mg is indicated in adults for the treatment of:
- Neovascular (wet) age-related macular degeneration (wet AMD).
- Visual impairment due to macular oedema secondary to central retinal vein occlusion (CRVO).
- Visual impairment due to macular oedema secondary to branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO).
- Diabetic macular oedema (DME).
- Visual impairment due to myopic choroidal neovascularisation (myopic CNV).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 2 mg aflibercept (equivalent to 50 microlitres).
- **Injection Interval:** Not shorter than one month between injections in the same eye.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Wet AMD:** Initiate with one injection per month for three consecutive months, followed by one injection every two months. Intervals may be extended via a "treat-and-extend" regimen in 2- or 4-week increments [Australian].
- **Macular Oedema secondary to CRVO or BRVO:** Initiate with one injection per month for three consecutive months. Subsequent intervals are adjusted based on visual and anatomic outcomes [Australian].
- **DME:** Initiate with one injection per month for five consecutive months, followed by one injection every two months. Intervals may be individualised based on a treat-and-extend approach [Australian].
- **Myopic CNV:** Initiate with a single injection. Additional doses are administered only if disease activity persists or recurs [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Must be administered by a qualified ophthalmologist experienced in intravitreal injections.
- Aseptic technique and adequate anaesthesia are mandatory.
- **Vial/Syringe Preparation:** Both containers contain excess volume; the excess must be expelled to align the plunger with the 0.05 mL dosing line to ensure exactly 2 mg is delivered. Injecting the entire volume may lead to overdose.
- **Post-Injection Monitoring:** Monitor intraocular pressure (IOP) and optic nerve head perfusion immediately following the procedure.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to aflibercept or any excipients (histidine, trehalose dihydrate, polysorbate 20).
- Active or suspected ocular or periocular infection.
- Active severe intraocular inflammation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Injection-related Effects:** Intravitreal injections are associated with endophthalmitis, retinal detachment, and iatrogenic traumatic cataract. More rarely, retinal vasculitis and/or retinal occlusive vasculitis have been reported.
- **Intraocular Pressure:** Transient increases in IOP are common within 60 minutes of injection. Monitor patients with poorly controlled glaucoma closely.
- **Arterial Thromboembolic Events (ATEs):** There is a potential risk of systemic ATEs (e.g., non-fatal stroke, myocardial infarction, or vascular death) following VEGF inhibitor use.
- **Bilateral Treatment:** Should be avoided as it has not been systematically studied and may increase systemic exposure.
- **Retinal Pigment Epithelial (RPE) Tear:** Caution is advised in patients with risk factors for RPE tears, such as large or high pigment epithelial retinal detachments.
- **Treatment Withholding:** Withhold treatment in cases of rhegmatogenous retinal detachment, stage 3 or 4 macular holes, or retinal breaks. Also, withhold if there is a BCVA decrease of ≥30 letters or if intraocular surgery is planned within 28 days.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Distribution
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Conjunctival haemorrhage (25.0%), reduced visual acuity (11.1%), eye pain (10.2%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Retinal pigment epithelial tear, retinal degeneration, vitreous haemorrhage, cataract (various types), corneal erosion/abrasion, increased IOP, blurred vision, vitreous floaters/detachment, injection site pain/haemorrhage, eyelid oedema. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Endophthalmitis, retinal detachment/tear, iritis, uveitis, iridocyclitis, corneal oedema. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Blindness, traumatic cataract, vitritis, hypopyon. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Serious reactions related to the procedure (occurring in <1 in 2400 injections) include endophthalmitis, retinal detachment, and traumatic cataract.
- Systemic hypersensitivity (including anaphylaxis) has been reported in post-marketing experience.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted.
- Aflibercept must not be mixed with other medicinal products.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Limited data in humans. Animal studies demonstrate reproductive toxicity (malformations) after systemic administration. Aflibercept should not be used during pregnancy unless the benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus.
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 3 months after the final injection.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established; a risk to the breastfed child cannot be excluded. A decision must be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or abstain from therapy.
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggests reversible effects on male and female fertility (e.g., changes in sperm morphology, irregular menses) at systemic exposures significantly higher than human therapeutic doses.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been studied in children or adolescents.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required. Limited experience in DME patients ≥75 years.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Pharmacokinetic analysis suggests no dose adjustment is necessary for patients with renal or hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Aflibercept acts as a soluble decoy receptor ("VEGF Trap"). It binds to VEGF-A and Placental Growth Factor (PlGF) with higher affinity than their natural receptors, thereby inhibiting the activation of VEGFR-1 and VEGFR-2. This suppresses pathological neovascularisation and vascular permeability.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Slowly absorbed from the eye into systemic circulation. Systemic $C_{max}$ of free aflibercept is very low (approx. 0.02 µg/mL in wet AMD) and generally becomes undetectable within two weeks.
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** As a protein, it is expected to undergo proteolytic catabolism. It does not accumulate in plasma when dosed every 4 weeks.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Biosimilar Comparability:** Study CSOK583A12301 demonstrated that **Afqlir** is equivalent to the reference product (**Eylea**) in terms of change in BCVA at Week 8 in patients with wet AMD. The safety and immunogenicity profiles were broadly comparable [Australian].
- **Pivotal Data:** The efficacy of aflibercept 2 mg is supported by the VIEW 1 and 2 trials (wet AMD), COPERNICUS and GALILEO (CRVO), VIBRANT (BRVO), VIVID and VISTA (DME), and MYRROR (myopic CNV). These trials consistently showed superiority to sham or laser, or non-inferiority to ranibizumab.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Light Protection:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Room Temperature Stability:** Unopened vials or blisters may be stored below 30°C for a single period of up to 7 days [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Expiry as stated on the packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
