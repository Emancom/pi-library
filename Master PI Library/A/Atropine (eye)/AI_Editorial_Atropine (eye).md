# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis of the provided source and is not intended as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    - Dosing frequency: Every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours. 
    - Maximum reported dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** 
    - Starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight. 
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session. 
    - Dose adjustments: May be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for discoloration or particulate matter prior to administration.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before starting therapy and monitor weekly or monthly. Target free carnitine levels should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and laryngeal edema, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated. Re-administration should only be considered after a risk-benefit assessment [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine Caution:** The safety and efficacy of the *oral* formulation have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Less frequent effects include gastritis and a distinct body odor.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg body weight, similar to adults (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Note the specific warning regarding the accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) with the *oral* formulation in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is essential for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, levocarnitine administration increases plasma concentrations and may alleviate metabolic abnormalities related to carnitine deficiency (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness, arrhythmias) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x/week post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations increased from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging:** Trough concentrations remained stable or increased over 12- and 24-week periods with doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
