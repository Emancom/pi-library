# Editorial Product Information: Azathioprine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian** (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized, clinically accurate editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Azathioprine** (an imidazole derivative of 6-mercaptopurine).
- **Chemical Class:** Purine antimetabolite/immunosuppressant.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Azathioprine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AX01 (not explicitly stated in source, but standard for the active ingredient).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 25 mg film-coated tablets (orange, round, biconvex, embossed "AZA 25") [Australian].
    - 50 mg film-coated tablets (pale yellow, round, biconvex, embossed "AZA 50") [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral (Intravenous administration is reserved for patients unable to tolerate oral therapy) [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Azathioprine** is indicated as an immunosuppressant antimetabolite, often as a steroid-sparing agent in combination with corticosteroids or other immunosuppressive procedures.
- **Organ Transplantation:** Prophylaxis of graft rejection in patients receiving organ transplants [Australian].
- **Autoimmune and Inflammatory Conditions:** Management of patients suffering from:
    - Severe rheumatoid arthritis [Australian].
    - Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) [Australian].
    - Dermatomyositis and polymyositis [Australian].
    - Autoimmune chronic active hepatitis [Australian].
    - Pemphigus vulgaris [Australian].
    - Polyarteritis nodosa [Australian].
    - Autoimmune haemolytic anaemia [Australian].
    - Chronic refractory idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- **Administration:** Tablets should be taken at least 1 hour before or 3 hours after food or milk [Australian]. Tablets must not be divided, crushed, or broken [Australian].
- **Titration:** Dosing must be individualized based on clinical response and haematological tolerance.

### Adult and Paediatric Dosing
- **Transplantation:** 
    - **Loading Dose:** Up to 5 mg/kg/day orally on the first day [Australian].
    - **Maintenance:** 1 to 4 mg/kg/day, adjusted based on clinical requirements and haematological monitoring [Australian].
- **Other Conditions:**
    - **Initial Dose:** Generally 1.0 mg/kg/day (50 mg to 100 mg) [Australian].
    - **Titration:** Increase in increments of 0.5 mg/kg/day every several weeks, up to a maximum of 2.5 mg/kg/day [Australian].
    - **Maintenance:** Range from <1 mg/kg/day to 3 mg/kg/day. If no improvement is observed within three months, withdrawal should be considered [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **azathioprine** or any component of the formulation. Hypersensitivity to 6-mercaptopurine should alert the clinician to potential cross-reactivity [Australian].
- Patients with rheumatoid arthritis who were previously treated with alkylating agents (e.g., cyclophosphamide, chlorambucil, melphalan) due to a prohibitive risk of neoplasia [Australian].
- **Azathioprine** therapy should not be initiated in patients who are pregnant, likely to become pregnant, or known to be pregnant [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General Warnings
- **Myelotoxicity:** **Azathioprine** causes dose-related, reversible bone marrow suppression. Complete blood counts (CBC), including platelets, must be performed weekly for the first eight weeks of therapy, and at least monthly thereafter [Australian].
- **TPMT Deficiency:** Patients with inherited deficiency of thiopurine methyltransferase (TPMT) are at high risk for severe, life-threatening myelosuppression. TPMT testing is recommended before starting therapy [Australian].
- **NUDT15 Mutation:** Patients with inherited mutated NUDT15 genes (particularly those of Asian ethnicity) are at increased risk for severe thiopurine toxicity (leukopenia and alopecia). Substantial dose reductions are generally required [Australian].
- **Carcinogenicity:** Increased risk of lymphoproliferative disorders and other malignancies, notably skin cancers (melanoma and non-melanoma), sarcomas, and uterine cervical cancer in situ. Risk is related to the intensity and duration of immunosuppression [Australian].
- **Infections:** Increased susceptibility to viral, fungal, and bacterial infections, including reactivation of Hepatitis B and Cytomegalovirus (CMV). Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML) has been reported [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Routine monitoring of liver function tests (LFTs) is required. Therapy must be discontinued immediately if jaundice appears [Australian].
- **Macrophage Activation Syndrome (MAS):** A life-threatening disorder that may develop in patients with autoimmune conditions, particularly IBD. **Azathioprine** must be discontinued if MAS occurs or is suspected [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Viral, fungal, and bacterial infections in transplant patients [Australian]. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Leucopenia, nausea, vomiting, gastrointestinal discomfort (early in therapy) [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Anaemia, thrombocytopenia, hypersensitivity reactions, hepatotoxicity (in RA patients), pancreatitis (especially in IBD and transplant) [Australian]. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, aplastic anaemia, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, Veno-occlusive disease (VOD), lymphomas, skin cancers [Australian]. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** Most frequently expressed as leucopenia; may be delayed [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity Syndromes:** Malaise, dizziness, fever, rigors, exanthema, vasculitis, myalgia, arthralgia, hypotension, and renal dysfunction. If these occur, **azathioprine** must be permanently withdrawn [Australian].
- **Alopecia:** Reported in 50% of renal transplant recipients; usually reversible despite continued treatment [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Xanthine Oxidase Inhibitors (Allopurinol, Oxipurinol, Thiopurinol):** These inhibit the metabolism of **azathioprine**. The dose of **azathioprine** must be reduced to **one-quarter** (25%) of the original dose when used concomitantly [Australian].
- **Febuxostat:** Concomitant administration is not recommended due to insufficient data for dose reduction [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blocking Agents:** May potentiate the blockade of succinylcholine and reduce the effect of non-depolarising agents like tubocurarine [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants (Warfarin):** **Azathioprine** may inhibit the anticoagulant effect; higher doses of warfarin may be required [Australian].
- **Ribavirin:** Co-administration is not advised as it reduces efficacy and increases the toxicity of **azathioprine** [Australian].
- **Aminosalicylates (Olsalazine, Mesalazine, Sulfasalazine):** May inhibit TPMT, increasing the risk of myelosuppression [Australian].
- **Vaccines:** Live vaccines are contraindicated due to the risk of infection in immunocompromised hosts [Australian]. Reduced response to killed vaccines (e.g., Hepatitis B) may occur [Australian].
- **Infliximab:** May increase levels of active 6-TGN metabolites and decrease leucocyte counts [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category D** [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** **Azathioprine** should not be initiated in patients who are pregnant or intended to become pregnant. It should only be used if the maternal benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus. Premature birth and low birth weight have been reported. Neonatal leucopenia and thrombocytopenia may occur if maternal leucocyte counts are low at 32 weeks' gestation [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** 6-mercaptopurine is identified in breast milk. Nursing is not recommended due to the risk of adverse effects on the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Increased fertility has been noted in both male and female transplant recipients following the relief of renal failure [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Adequate contraceptive precautions must be used by either partner while receiving therapy [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing follows the mg/kg weight-based guidelines provided for adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available, but it is recommended that dosages be at the lower end of the adult range. Haematological monitoring must be stringent [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing should be at the lower end of the range. Monitor haematological response carefully and reduce dosage if toxicity occurs [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Metabolism may be impaired. Dosing should be at the lower end of the range with regular LFT and CBC monitoring [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Azathioprine** is a pro-drug of 6-mercaptopurine (6-MP). It acts as a purine antimetabolite, inhibits nucleic acid biosynthesis to prevent cell proliferation, and incorporates purine thioanalogues into DNA, leading to immunosuppression [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed from the upper GI tract [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 6-MP crosses cell membranes and is converted to active thioinosinic acid. It does not cross the blood-brain barrier significantly [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly broken down in vivo into 6-MP and a methylnitroimidazole moiety. 6-MP is oxidized by xanthine oxidase [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Eliminated primarily as the inactive metabolite thiouric acid in the urine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Provided the film coating is intact, there is no risk in handling tablets. However, they should not be crushed or broken [Australian].
