# PRODUCT INFORMATION: ARTICAINE HYDROCHLORIDE WITH ADRENALINE (EPINEPHRINE)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
*   **Active Ingredients:** Articaine hydrochloride and Adrenaline (as acid tartrate/bitartrate).
*   **Concentrations:** 
    *   Articaine hydrochloride 4% (40 mg/mL).
    *   Adrenaline (epinephrine) 1:100,000 or 1:200,000 [FDA].
*   **Physicochemical Properties:** Articaine is an amide-type local anesthetic. The solution is sterile, clear, and colorless. It contains sodium metabisulfite as an antioxidant.

## 2. Approved Indications
Articaine with adrenaline is indicated for the induction of local, infiltrative, or regional (conductive) anesthesia during both simple and complex dental procedures. It is approved for use in adults, adolescents, and pediatric patients aged 4 years and older.

## 3. Contraindications
Administration is contraindicated in patients with:
*   Known hypersensitivity to articaine, amide-type local anesthetics, or any other component of the formulation.
*   Known hypersensitivity to sulfites [TGA/FDA].
*   A history of asthma, as these patients may be more susceptible to sulfite-induced allergic reactions [FDA].
*   Severe arterial hypertension, coronary artery disease, or valvular cardiac disease (specifically sequelae to acute rheumatic fever) [TGA].
*   Untreated thyrotoxicosis [TGA].
*   Deficiency in plasma cholinesterase activity [TGA].
*   Inflammation or sepsis at the intended injection site [TGA].
*   Concurrent use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or tricyclic antidepressants, or use within the previous two weeks [TGA].

**Note:** Intravenous injection is strictly contraindicated [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
**Accidental Intravascular Injection:** To minimize the risk of intravascular injection, aspiration must be performed before the total dose is administered. However, the absence of blood in the syringe does not guarantee that a vessel has not been entered. Intravascular injection may lead to convulsions, CNS depression, and cardiac arrest.

**Systemic Toxicity:** Systemic absorption can affect the central nervous (CNS) and cardiovascular (CVS) systems. Early signs of toxicity include restlessness, anxiety, tinnitus, dizziness, blurred vision, or tremors. 

**Methemoglobinemia:** Local anesthetic use has been associated with methemoglobinemia. Patients with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency, congenital or idiopathic methemoglobinemia, or cardiac/pulmonary compromise are at increased risk. Signs include cyanotic skin discoloration and abnormal blood coloration [FDA].

**Hepatic and Renal Impairment:** Because amide-type anesthetics are metabolized by the liver, use with caution in patients with hepatic disease. Patients with severe liver impairment are at higher risk of achieving toxic plasma concentrations. Caution is also advised in patients with renal impairment [TGA/FDA].

**Malignant Hyperthermia:** While it is unknown if amide-type anesthetics trigger familial malignant hyperthermia, a standard management protocol should be available during anesthesia [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Articaine should be administered via submucosal infiltration or nerve block. The lowest effective dose should always be used.

*   **Adults:** The maximum recommended dose should not exceed 7 mg/kg of body weight. For a healthy 60 kg adult, this is approximately 5 cartridges (based on 2.2 mL [TGA]) or 0.175 mL/kg [FDA].
*   **Pediatric Patients (4–16 years):** Dosage should be reduced based on age, weight, and physical condition. The maximum dose is 7 mg/kg. Use is not recommended in children under 4 years due to insufficient data.
*   **Elderly/Debilitated Patients:** Reduced doses may be necessary commensurate with their physical status.

**Administration Instructions:**
*   Injection should be performed slowly (approximately 1 mL/min).
*   Visually inspect cartridges for particulate matter or discoloration.
*   Discard any unused portion of the cartridge immediately.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Common adverse reactions (incidence ≥1%) include:
*   **General:** Headache, facial edema, and postoperative pain.
*   **Nervous System:** Paresthesia (persistent numbness of the lips/tongue), dizziness, and hypoesthesia.
*   **Digestive:** Gingivitis and nausea.

**Serious or Post-marketing Reactions:**
*   **Cardiovascular:** Bradycardia, hypotension, tachycardia, and cardiovascular collapse.
*   **Neurological:** Facial paralysis, tremors, convulsions, and ocular muscle paralysis (diplopia, ptosis, or mydriasis) [FDA].
*   **Allergic:** Urticaria, rash, and anaphylactoid reactions.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Sympathomimetics:** Use with caution in patients receiving other sympathomimetic drugs, as adrenaline may increase the risk of hypertension and arrhythmias.
*   **MAOIs and Tricyclic Antidepressants:** May prolong and intensify the cardiovascular effects of adrenaline.
*   **Non-selective Beta-blockers:** May lead to severe hypertension followed by bradycardia.
*   **Phenothiazines and Butyrophenones:** May reduce or reverse the pressor effects of adrenaline.
*   **Hypoglycemics:** Adrenaline-induced hyperglycemia may necessitate dose adjustments in diabetic patients [TGA].
*   **Inhalational Anesthetics:** Potent general anesthetics (e.g., halothane) may sensitize the heart to adrenaline, increasing the risk of arrhythmias [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
**Pregnancy:** 
*   **TGA Category B3 / FDA Category C.** 
*   Animal studies have shown fetal death and skeletal variations at high doses. Articaine should only be used during pregnancy if the potential benefits outweigh the risks to the fetus.

**Breastfeeding:**
*   It is unknown if articaine is excreted in human milk. 
*   **Conservative recommendation (TGA):** Nursing women should refrain from breastfeeding for **48 hours** following anesthesia.
*   **Alternative recommendation (FDA):** Nursing mothers may choose to pump and discard milk for **4 hours** post-injection to minimize infant ingestion.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels are reached approximately 25 minutes after a single dose.
*   **Distribution:** 60% to 80% of articaine is bound to human serum albumin and gamma globulins.
*   **Metabolism:** Articaine is rapidly hydrolyzed by plasma carboxyesterases to its inactive metabolite, articainic acid. Only 5% to 10% is metabolized by the liver (microsomal P450 system).
*   **Excretion:** Primarily renal (53–57% within 24 hours). The elimination half-life is approximately 1.8 hours for articaine and 1.5 hours for articainic acid.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Articaine blocks the generation and conduction of nerve impulses by increasing the threshold for electrical excitation in the nerve and slowing the propagation of the nerve impulse. It specifically blocks sodium channels within the nerve membrane. Adrenaline is included as a vasoconstrictor to decrease local blood flow, thereby delaying systemic absorption and prolonging the duration of the anesthetic effect.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 25°C (77°F).
*   Protect from light.
*   Do not freeze.
*   Cartridges should not be used if the solution is discolored or contains particulate matter.
