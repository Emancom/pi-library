## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Dosage must be individualised according to energy expenditure, clinical condition, body weight, and metabolic capacity, and should account for additional enteral/oral intake.
- Average amino acid requirements (guidance):
  - Normal nutritional state or mild catabolic stress: 0.6–0.9 g amino acids/kg body weight/day (0.10–0.15 g nitrogen/kg/day).
  - Moderate to high metabolic stress (with or without malnutrition): 0.9–1.6 g amino acids/kg/day (0.15–0.25 g nitrogen/kg/day).
  - Special conditions (e.g., burns, marked anabolism): nitrogen needs may be higher.
- Example product-specific dosing ranges (reconstituted product):
  - The dosing range of 13–31 mL/kg body weight/day provides approximately 0.7–1.6 g amino acids/kg/day (0.11–0.26 g nitrogen/kg/day) and 14–33 kcal/kg/day total energy (11–27 kcal/kg/day non-protein energy).
  - Recommended maximum daily dose: 35 mL/kg body weight/day, providing ~1.8 g amino acids/kg/day (0.29 g nitrogen/kg/day), 4.5 g glucose/kg/day, 1.40 g lipids/kg/day and total energy ~38 kcal/kg/day (30 kcal/kg/day non-protein).
### Infusion rates and maximum rates
- Increase flow rate gradually during the first hour.
- Recommended infusion period: 14–24 hours (product-specific guidance).
- Maximum infusion rates:
  - Glucose: 0.25 g/kg/h
  - Amino acids: 0.1 g/kg/h
  - Lipids: 0.15 g/kg/h
- Maximum infusion rate by volume: should not exceed 2.0 mL/kg/h (corresponds to 0.10 g amino acids, 0.25 g glucose and 0.08 g lipids/kg/h in the referenced formulation).
### Additions and compatibility
- After mixing the compartments, additions (vitamins, trace elements, electrolytes or other medicinal products) may be made aseptically via the additive port only if compatibility with the complete admixture has been documented.
- Trace elements and vitamins are insufficient in the base product; supplementation should be added to meet individual requirements.
- Ceftriaxone must not be mixed or administered simultaneously with calcium-containing intravenous solutions (including this product) through the same infusion line due to the risk of precipitation (see Interactions).
- Do not administer simultaneously with blood through the same infusion tubing.
### Method of preparation and administration instructions (clinically relevant steps)
- Use on a single occasion for one patient only. Contains no antimicrobial preservative; after opening the bag, contents should be used immediately.
- Mix the three compartments at room temperature according to manufacturer instructions (manual rolling and inversion) and inspect for homogeneity (milky white emulsion) and absence of phase separation or visible particles prior to use.
- When performing additions, calculate final electrolyte/phosphate/calcium content and osmolarity, particularly for peripheral administration.
- Do not reconnect a partially used bag. Do not connect bags in series.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity or known allergy to any component including corn or corn products, fish, egg, soya, or peanut proteins, or to any active substance or excipient.
- Severe hyperlipidaemia.
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- Severe blood coagulation disorders.
- Congenital abnormalities of amino acid metabolism.
- Severe renal impairment without access to haemofiltration or dialysis.
- Uncontrolled hyperglycaemia.
- Pathologically elevated serum levels of any of the included electrolytes.
- General contraindications to infusion therapy: acute pulmonary oedema, hyperhydration, decompensated cardiac insufficiency.
- Unstable clinical conditions (examples): severe post‑traumatic conditions, uncompensated diabetes mellitus, acute myocardial infarction, stroke, embolism, metabolic acidosis, severe sepsis, hypotonic dehydration and hyperosmolar coma.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Administration route**: Must only be administered into a central vein (central venous administration).
- **Hypersensitivity / anaphylaxis**:
  - Infusion must be stopped immediately if any signs/symptoms of allergic reaction (fever, shivering, rash, dyspnoea) occur.
  - Contains soya oil, fish oil and egg phospholipids — risk of allergic reactions; cross-reactivity between soybean and peanut can occur.
  - Solutions containing dextrose should be used with caution in patients with known allergy to corn or corn products.
- **Pulmonary vascular precipitates**:
  - Pulmonary vascular precipitates and emboli have been reported in patients receiving parenteral nutrition; fatal outcomes have occurred.
  - Excessive addition of calcium and phosphate increases risk of calcium phosphate precipitate formation; precipitates have been reported even in absence of phosphate salt in the solution.
  - Inspect solution, infusion set and catheter periodically for precipitates; stop infusion and evaluate if pulmonary distress occurs.
- **Infection and sepsis**:
  - Strict aseptic technique is required during catheter insertion and handling. Immunosuppression, hyperglycaemia, malnutrition and underlying disease predispose to infections.
- **Fat overload syndrome**:
  - Has been reported with similar lipid emulsions; may occur with overdose or recommended administration but can also occur when given as instructed.
  - Characterised by sudden clinical deterioration with fever, anaemia, leucopoenia, thrombocytopenia, coagulation disorders, hyperlipidaemia, hepatomegaly/fatty liver, worsening liver function and central nervous system manifestations including coma.
  - Syndrome is usually reversible when lipid infusion is stopped.
- **Impaired lipid metabolism**:
  - Monitor triglycerides; serum triglyceride concentration should not exceed 4.6 mmol/L during infusion.
  - Use caution in patients with renal failure, diabetes mellitus, pancreatitis, impaired liver function, hypothyroidism and sepsis.
- **Refeeding syndrome**:
  - Severely malnourished patients are at risk for intracellular shifts of potassium, phosphorus and magnesium; thiamine deficiency and fluid retention may also develop.
  - Initiate nutrition slowly with close monitoring and appropriate supplementation to prevent complications.
- **Parenteral nutrition–associated liver disease**:
  - Hepatic complications (cholestasis, hepatic steatosis, fibrosis, cirrhosis, possible progression to hepatic failure; cholecystitis and cholelithiasis) may occur; monitor and evaluate abnormal liver tests.
  - Use with caution in patients with hepatic impairment including cholestasis and/or elevated liver enzymes.
  - Amino acid administration in hepatic insufficiency may lead to amino acid imbalances, hyperammonaemia, stupor and coma.
- **Glycaemic control**:
  - Hyperglycaemia should be treated clinically (insulin and/or infusion rate adjustment).
  - Sudden cessation of concentrated glucose infusion can cause hypoglycaemia due to continued endogenous insulin — parenteral nutrition should be withdrawn gradually.
- **Renal impairment**:
  - Use with caution; control phosphate, magnesium and potassium intake to prevent hyperphosphatemia, hypermagnesemia and hyperkalaemia.
  - Azotaemia may occur, particularly in renal impairment.
- **Electrolyte retention**:
  - Use cautiously in patients prone to electrolyte retention (e.g., those on corticosteroids or corticotrophin) — may lead to hypernatremia.
- **Bleeding disorders**:
  - Theoretical risk of bleeding disorders associated with fish oil content; exercise caution.
- **Elderly**:
  - Dose selection should be cautious given the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal or cardiac function and concomitant disease.
- **Paediatric use**:
  - No studies performed in the paediatric population. Paediatric use is not supported by available source information.
- **Laboratory interference**:
  - Lipids may interfere with laboratory tests (bilirubin, LDH, oxygen saturation, haemoglobin) if blood is sampled before lipids are cleared (generally 5–6 hours after last lipid administration).
- **Monitoring (throughout treatment)**:
  - Water and electrolyte balance (including K+, PO4 3−, Mg2+)
  - Serum osmolarity
  - Serum triglycerides
  - Acid‑base balance
  - Blood glucose
  - Liver function (including enzymes)
  - Renal function (including creatinine, urea)
  - Full blood count including platelets and coagulation parameters
  - Vitamins and trace elements (especially with long-term parenteral nutrition: monitor zinc and copper)
- **Lactic acidosis**:
  - Use with caution in patients with lactic acidosis, insufficient cellular oxygen supply and/or increased serum osmolarity; thiamine deficiency (e.g., chronic alcoholism) increases risk.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Ceftriaxone:
  - **Do not** mix or administer ceftriaxone simultaneously with calcium‑containing intravenous solutions (including this product) through the same infusion line because of the risk of precipitation of ceftriaxone‑calcium salt. If sequential administration is performed using the same line, flush thoroughly with a compatible fluid between infusions. [No regional variation stated; advice provided in available sources.]
- Blood:
  - Should not be administered simultaneously with blood through the same infusion tubing due to risk of pseudoagglutination and incompatibility.
- Vitamin K content (soy oil):
  - Soy oil contains vitamin K1, but the concentration in the product is low and not expected to significantly affect anticoagulant therapy with coumarin derivatives.
- Additions to admixture:
  - Additives must be checked for compatibility; incompatible additions can cause precipitation or destabilisation of the lipid emulsion leading to vascular occlusion.
- Other interactions:
  - No dedicated pharmacokinetic interaction studies reported in available sources.
- Management notes:
  - Avoid coadministration of incompatible drugs (especially ceftriaxone with calcium-containing solutions).
  - Flush lines between incompatible infusions.
  - Verify documented compatibility before adding drugs or supplements to admixture.
