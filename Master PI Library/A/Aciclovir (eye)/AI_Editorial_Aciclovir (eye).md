# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI or local prescribing guidelines in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States PI was provided for synthesis; as such, regional comparisons to other jurisdictions (e.g., Australia, Europe, Japan) are not included unless specified in the provided text.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for Levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Acute Management:** In patients with severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours).
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, adjusted based on clinical response and therapy requirements.
- **Max Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate medical treatment.
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, frequency or severity may increase.
- **General Precautions:** The safety/efficacy of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency and may result in the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Frequent Adverse Effects
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality):
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Fever (7%), Flu syndrome (25%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Chest pain (12%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), Nausea (8%), Vomiting (15%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%), Depression (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%).

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis (reported as less frequent).
- **Other:** Body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following administration. Risks to the child from excess carnitine must be weighed against maternal benefit; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients. Note that oral levocarnitine is not recommended for chronic high-dose use in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
    - **Bioavailability:** Oral tablets (~15.1%) and oral solution (~15.9%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations rose from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial; contains no preservative.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison schedule not applicable.
