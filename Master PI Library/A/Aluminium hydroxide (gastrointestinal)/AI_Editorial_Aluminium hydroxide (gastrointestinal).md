# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **US (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection, Baxter Healthcare Corporation.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis intended for reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 
- **Note**: At the time of this editorial, only the US-specific documentation was available for review. Information regarding other jurisdictions is listed as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class**: Carrier molecule (amino acid derivative).
- **Brand Name(s)**: Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01 (Not specified in provided source).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Intravenous [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation (US Sponsor); Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism leading to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders**: 
    - **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose**: In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency**: Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (minimum every 6 hours) [US].
    - **Maintenance**: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
    - **Recommended Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - **Administration**: Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Monitoring**: It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is advised [US].
- **Adjustment**: Dose reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) in ESRD may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These may occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients [US].
- **Seizure Activity**: Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with reports of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulations)**: While the safety of the *injection* is established in ESRD, chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements**: Regular monitoring of plasma carnitine levels and clinical response is essential to guide therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.
- **Common (≥5%) [US]**: 
    - **General**: Injection site reaction (33%–59%), pain (21%–49%), headache (3%–37%), fever (5%–12%), abdominal pain (5%–21%).
    - **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (18%–21%), hypotension (3%–19%), chest pain (6%–15%), tachycardia (5%–9%).
    - **Gastrointestinal**: Diarrhea (9%–35%), vomiting (9%–21%), nausea (5%–12%).
    - **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (15%–33%), cough increase (10%–18%), dyspnea (3%–19%).
- **Other Reported Effects**: 
    - Body odor, gastritis, and transient nausea/vomiting [US].
    - Seizures and serious hypersensitivity reactions (Postmarketing) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management**: Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustment in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Category**: Not formally assigned a letter grade in the source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA-based) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
    - **Recommendation**: Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: 
    - Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following exogenous administration [US].
    - Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impairment in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Note the precaution regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral forms are used in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters that are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination**: Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging**: Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions**: Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability**: Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentration) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling**: Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation lacks preservatives [US].

- **Other Jurisdictions**: Not specified in available sources.
