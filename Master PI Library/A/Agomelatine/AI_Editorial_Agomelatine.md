# Editorial Product Information: Agomelatine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authorities:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Agomelatine Lupin (Agomelatine-citric acid)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Agomelatine (administered as agomelatine-citric acid).
- **Chemical Class:** Melatonin receptor agonist and 5-HT$_{2C}$ receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** Agomelatine Lupin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06AX22.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 25 mg film-coated tablets: Yellow, oblong, biconvex tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Southern Cross Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** Treatment of major depression in adults, including the prevention of relapse [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard recommended dose is 25 mg once daily, taken orally at bedtime.
- **Dose Titration:** If clinical improvement is not observed after two weeks of therapy, the dose may be increased to 50 mg once daily (taken as two 25 mg tablets together at bedtime). 
- **Liver Function Assessment:** Dose escalation to 50 mg must be preceded by an assessment of the benefit-risk ratio and liver function testing.
- **Administration:** Tablets may be taken with or without food.
- **Treatment Duration:** For MDD, treatment should continue for at least six months to ensure symptomatic remission.
- **Switching Therapy:** When switching from SSRIs or SNRIs, agomelatine may be started immediately while tapering the previous antidepressant to avoid discontinuation symptoms [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Discontinuation:** No tapering is required upon cessation of agomelatine; the drug does not induce discontinuation symptoms.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to agomelatine or any of the product's excipients.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Active liver disease (e.g., cirrhosis) or baseline transaminase levels exceeding 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN).
- **Potent CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with drugs that strongly inhibit CYP1A2, such as fluvoxamine or ciprofloxacin [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Cases of liver injury, including hepatic failure (occasionally resulting in death or transplantation), hepatitis, and jaundice, have been reported, primarily within the first months of treatment. 
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Liver function tests (LFTs) are mandatory for all patients:
    - At baseline (before initiation).
    - Before a dose increase to 50 mg.
    - Periodically at approximately 3, 6, 12, and 24 weeks.
    - Thereafter when clinically indicated.
    - If transaminases increase, LFTs should be repeated within 48 hours.
- **Discontinuation Criteria:** Treatment must be stopped immediately if transaminases exceed 3x ULN or if signs/symptoms of liver injury (e.g., dark urine, jaundice, upper right abdominal pain, unexplained fatigue) develop.
- **Suicidality:** As with all antidepressants, patients (especially those under 25 years) should be closely monitored for worsening of depressive symptoms or emergent suicidal ideation, particularly during the first few months of therapy or following dose changes.
- **Bipolar Disorder:** Use with caution in patients with a history of mania or hypomania. Treatment should be discontinued if manic symptoms emerge.
- **Dementia:** Not recommended for treating depression in elderly patients with dementia [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typically mild to moderate and occur most frequently during the first two weeks of treatment.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, somnolence, insomnia, anxiety, nausea, diarrhoea, constipation, upper abdominal pain, vomiting, back pain, fatigue, weight increase, increased ALT/AST (>3x ULN) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Paraesthesia, migraine, blurred vision, tinnitus, eczema, hyperhidrosis, pruritus, urticaria, agitation, irritability, aggression, nightmares, abnormal dreams, restless leg syndrome |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hepatitis, hepatic failure, hallucinations, akathisia, urinary retention, face oedema, angioedema, weight decrease |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP1A2):** Agomelatine is primarily metabolised by CYP1A2 (90%) and to a lesser extent by CYP2C9/19 (10%).
    - **Contraindicated:** Fluvoxamine (potent CYP1A2/moderate CYP2C9 inhibitor) increases agomelatine exposure approximately 60-fold.
    - **Caution Recommended:** Moderate CYP1A2 inhibitors (e.g., propranolol, oestrogens, enoxacin) may increase agomelatine concentrations.
    - **Inducers:** Rifampicin (CYP inducer) may decrease bioavailability.
- **Cigarette Smoking:** Induction of CYP1A2 by heavy smoking (≥15 cigarettes/day) decreases agomelatine exposure but does not require dose adjustment.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** 
    - **Alcohol:** Consumption should be avoided.
    - **ECT:** No clinical experience; however, animal studies suggest no proconvulsant properties.
    - **Other Antidepressants:** No interactions noted with paroxetine, fluoxetine, or sertraline; however, caution is advised as combination data is limited.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. Animal studies do not indicate direct or indirect harmful effects. As a precautionary measure, use during pregnancy should be avoided.
- **Breastfeeding:** Animal data show agomelatine/metabolites are excreted in milk. It is unknown if they are excreted in human milk. A decision must be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or discontinue the drug, considering the benefit to the mother and the infant.
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility were observed in animal trials at high exposures.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children or adolescents (<18 years) due to lack of safety and efficacy data. 
- **Elderly Use:** 
    - **Age <75 years:** No dose adjustment required based on age alone.
    - **Age ≥75 years:** Not recommended; efficacy has not been demonstrated in this age group [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No significant pharmacokinetic changes in severe impairment. However, due to limited clinical data in MDD, caution is advised in patients with moderate to severe renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with cirrhosis or active liver disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Agomelatine acts as an agonist at melatonin MT$_1$ and MT$_2$ receptors and as an antagonist at serotonin 5-HT$_{2C}$ receptors. It increases dopamine and noradrenaline release specifically in the prefrontal cortex and has no effect on extracellular serotonin levels.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and well absorbed (≥80%) after oral administration. Peak plasma levels (C$_{max}$) reached in 1–2 hours. Absolute bioavailability is low (~1%) due to high first-pass metabolism.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (95%); volume of distribution is approximately 35 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP1A2 (90%) and CYP2C9/19 (10%). Metabolites are inactive.
    - **Elimination:** Rapid elimination with a half-life of 1–2 hours. Excretion is primarily urinary (80%) as metabolites.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Acute Efficacy:** Short-term (6–8 week) placebo-controlled trials demonstrated the efficacy of agomelatine 25–50 mg in reducing HAM-D total scores. Superiority over placebo was observed as early as two weeks [Australian].
- **Relapse Prevention:** A 44-week double-blind trial showed that agomelatine (25–50 mg) significantly reduced the risk of relapse (23.9% vs. 50.0% for placebo) in patients who had initially responded to acute treatment.
- **Sexual Function:** Clinical trials using the ASEX scale and SEX-FX scores showed no deleterious effect on sexual function compared to placebo or other antidepressants (e.g., paroxetine, venlafaxine).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in the original package to protect from moisture.
- Shelf life is specified on the external packaging [Australian].
