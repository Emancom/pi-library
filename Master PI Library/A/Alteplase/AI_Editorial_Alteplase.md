# Editorial Product Information: Alteplase

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the clinical properties, dosage, and regulatory status of **alteplase**, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Alteplase (rch)
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant human tissue-type plasminogen activator (t-PA); serine protease.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Actilyse** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** B01AD02
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Lyophilised powder for injection: 10 mg, 20 mg*, and 50 mg vials. 
    - *Note: The 20 mg presentation is not currently distributed in the Australian market.*
    - **Actilyse Cathflo** (2 mg) is a separate presentation not indicated for the systemic uses described herein [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion or bolus injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Boehringer Ingelheim Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI):** Lysis of suspected occlusive coronary artery thrombi associated with evolving transmural MI. Treatment should ideally begin within 12 hours of symptom onset [Australian].
- **Acute Massive Pulmonary Embolism (PE):** Treatment of patients with massive PE where thrombolytic therapy is clinically appropriate [Australian].
- **Acute Ischaemic Stroke (AIS):** Thrombolytic treatment of AIS. Treatment must be initiated within 4.5 hours of symptom onset and only after intracranial haemorrhage (ICH) has been excluded via imaging [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer as early as possible after symptom onset.
- Use a dedicated IV line with an infusion pump.
- Final concentration of the reconstituted solution is 1 mg/mL.

### Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI)
Two regimens are available; the comparative efficacy of these has not been formally evaluated [Australian].

**1. Accelerated Infusion (90-minute regimen):**
- **Patients $\ge$ 65 kg:** 100 mg total dose.
    - 15 mg IV bolus.
    - 50 mg IV infusion over 30 minutes.
    - 35 mg IV infusion over 60 minutes.
- **Patients < 65 kg:**
    - 15 mg IV bolus.
    - 0.75 mg/kg over 30 minutes (max 50 mg).
    - 0.5 mg/kg over 60 minutes (max 35 mg).

**2. 3-Hour Infusion:**
- **Patients $\ge$ 65 kg:** 100 mg total dose.
    - 10 mg IV bolus.
    - 50 mg IV infusion over the 1st hour.
    - 40 mg IV infusion over the next 2 hours.
- **Patients < 65 kg:**
    - 10 mg IV bolus.
    - Total infusion not to exceed 1.5 mg/kg over 3 hours.

### Acute Massive Pulmonary Embolism (PE)
- **Patients $\ge$ 65 kg:** 100 mg total dose over 2 hours.
    - 10 mg IV bolus (1–2 minutes).
    - 90 mg IV infusion over 2 hours.
- **Patients < 65 kg:**
    - 10 mg IV bolus (1–2 minutes).
    - Total infusion not to exceed 1.5 mg/kg over 2 hours.

### Acute Ischaemic Stroke (AIS)
- **Total Dose:** 0.9 mg/kg (maximum 90 mg).
- **Administration:** 10% of the total dose as an initial IV bolus, followed by the remaining 90% as an IV infusion over 60 minutes.

### Reconstitution and Dilution
- Reconstitute with the provided Water for Injections (WFI) to 1 mg/mL.
- Do not use bacteriostatic water.
- May be further diluted with sterile 0.9% Sodium Chloride to a minimum concentration of 0.2 mg/mL. Dilution below this or with glucose/WFI is not recommended due to turbidity risks.

## 4. Contraindications
**General Contraindications (All Indications):**
- Hypersensitivity to alteplase or excipients.
- Significant bleeding disorder (current or within 6 months).
- Known haemorrhagic diathesis.
- History of CNS damage (neoplasm, aneurysm, intracranial/spinal surgery).
- History or evidence of intracranial haemorrhage (ICH).
- Severe uncontrolled hypertension.
- Major surgery or significant trauma within the last 3 months.
- Prolonged or traumatic CPR (>2 mins), obstetrical delivery, or organ biopsy within 10 days.
- Recent (3 months) documented ulcerative GI disease.
- Arterial aneurysms or malformations; neoplasms with increased bleeding risk.
- Bacterial endocarditis, pericarditis, or acute pancreatitis.
- Severe hepatic disease (failure, cirrhosis, portal hypertension).
- Current use of oral anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin with INR > 1.3).

**Additional Contraindications for AMI/PE:**
- History of haemorrhagic stroke or stroke of unknown origin.
- Ischaemic stroke or TIA in the preceding 6 months (except for current AIS within 4.5 hours).

**Additional Contraindications for AIS:**
- Symptom onset > 4.5 hours or unknown.
- Minor neurological deficit or rapidly improving symptoms.
- Severe stroke (e.g., NIHSS > 25).
- Seizure at stroke onset.
- Platelet count < 100,000/mm³.
- Blood glucose < 2.8 mmol/L or > 22.2 mmol/L.
- History of prior stroke and concomitant diabetes.
- Patient age < 18 years [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bleeding:** The most common complication. Avoid rigid catheters, IM injections, and non-essential handling. If serious bleeding occurs (especially ICH), terminate infusion immediately.
- **Presentation Selection:** The 2 mg (Cathflo) presentation is unsuitable for systemic indications due to under-dosing risk.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Angio-oedema is the most frequent immune reaction. Risk increases with concomitant ACE inhibitor use.
- **Cholesterol Embolism:** Rarely reported; can be fatal.
- **Arrhythmias:** Reperfusion in AMI may cause coronary arrhythmias (e.g., bradycardia, VT). Conventional antiarrhythmic therapy should be available.
- **AIS Specific:** Treatment must be managed by a physician trained in neurological care. BP monitoring is required for 24 hours post-infusion; antihypertensives are recommended if systolic > 180 mmHg or diastolic > 105 mmHg.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Haemorrhage (any site), superficial bleeding (puncture sites), decreased blood pressure, reperfusion arrhythmias (AMI only), ICH (AIS only). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | ICH (AMI and PE indications), pharyngeal/respiratory tract haemorrhage, GI haemorrhage, urogenital haemorrhage, ecchymosis, increased body temperature. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pulmonary haemorrhage, retroperitoneal haemorrhage, pericardial haemorrhage, anaphylactoid reactions (rash, urticaria, bronchospasm, angio-oedema). |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Fat embolism, eye haemorrhage, parenchymatous organ haemorrhage. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Intracranial haemorrhage is the most significant risk, particularly in AIS (up to 15% of patients) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets:** Concomitant use of coumarin derivatives, heparin, or antiplatelet agents (e.g., aspirin, GP IIb/IIIa antagonists) increases bleeding risk. Avoid these within the first 24 hours following AIS treatment.
- **ACE Inhibitors:** May increase the risk of anaphylactoid reactions/angio-oedema [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** Do not mix alteplase with other medications in the same vial or IV line.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Studies show no teratogenicity in rats/rabbits, but dose-related increases in abortions were seen in rabbits. Use only if the benefit clearly outweighs the potential risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if alteplase is excreted in human milk. Nursing should be withheld for the first 24 hours after administration.
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Not recommended for use in children; not indicated for AIS in patients < 18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Patients > 80 years have an increased risk of ICH and mortality and may experience reduced efficacy compared to younger patients. Use must be based on an individual benefit-risk assessment [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic disease. Haemostatic defects secondary to severe renal/hepatic disease are contraindications due to increased bleeding risk.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Alteplase is a fibrin-specific plasminogen activator. It binds to fibrin in a thrombus, converting entrapped plasminogen to plasmin, which induces local fibrinolysis with minimal systemic effects.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** At 100 mg, circulating fibrinogen levels decrease by approximately 40–46% at 4 hours, typically recovering to 80% within 24 hours.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Clearance:** Rapidly cleared by the liver (500–700 mL/min).
    - **Half-life:** Alpha half-life is < 5 minutes; beta half-life is approximately 40 minutes.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **GUSTO Study (AMI):** Demonstrated that accelerated alteplase (100 mg over 90 mins) plus IV heparin resulted in lower 30-day mortality (6.3%) compared to streptokinase regimens (7.3%).
- **NINDS Stroke Study (AIS):** Established the benefit of treatment within 3 hours. Favourable outcomes (minimal/no disability) were significantly higher with alteplase vs placebo.
- **ECASS III (AIS):** Demonstrated efficacy in an extended time window of 3 to 4.5 hours, though the net benefit decreases as the time to treatment increases.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 30°C. Protect lyophilised powder from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Demonstrated stability for 24 hours at 2–8°C and 8 hours at 30°C. From a microbiological perspective, use immediately.
