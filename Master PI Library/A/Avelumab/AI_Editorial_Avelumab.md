# Editorial Product Information: Avelumab

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information when prescribing or dispensing in a specific region. Prescribers must consult their local approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Avelumab
- **Chemical Class:** Human monoclonal IgG1 antibody; programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) blocker.
- **Brand Name:** **BAVENCIO** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01FF04
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 200 mg/10 mL (20 mg/mL) sterile concentrate for solution for intravenous infusion. Clear, colourless to slightly yellow solution. [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Merkel Cell Carcinoma (mMCC):** Treatment of adults and paediatric patients aged 12 years and older. [Australian]
- **Urothelial Carcinoma (UC):** First-line maintenance treatment for patients with locally advanced or metastatic UC whose disease has not progressed following first-line platinum-based induction chemotherapy. [Australian]
- **Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC):** First-line treatment of patients with advanced RCC, administered in combination with **axitinib**. [Australian]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metastatic Merkel Cell Carcinoma:** 10 mg/kg body weight or 800 mg (fixed dose) IV every 2 weeks. [Australian]
- **Urothelial Carcinoma:** 800 mg IV every 2 weeks. [Australian]
- **Renal Cell Carcinoma:** 800 mg IV every 2 weeks in combination with **axitinib** 5 mg orally twice daily (12 hours apart) with or without food. [Australian]

### Premedication
Patients must be premedicated with an antihistamine and paracetamol prior to the first four infusions. If no infusion-related reactions occur during these doses, subsequent premedication is at the physician's discretion. [Australian]

### Administration Instructions
- **Duration:** Infuse over 60 minutes.
- **Filter:** Use a sterile, non-pyrogenic, low-protein binding 0.2 $\mu$m in-line or add-on filter.
- **Dilution:** Dilute with either 0.9% or 0.45% sodium chloride. 
- **Contraindication for delivery:** Do not administer as an IV push or bolus injection. [Australian]

### Treatment Modifications
Dose reductions or escalations of avelumab are not recommended. Dosing should be withheld or discontinued based on the severity of immune-mediated adverse reactions (IMARs) or infusion-related reactions (IRRs) (see Section 5). [Australian]

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to avelumab or any of the excipients (mannitol, glacial acetic acid, polysorbate 20, sodium hydroxide). [Australian]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs)
- Monitor for pyrexia, chills, flushing, hypotension, dyspnoea, wheezing, back pain, abdominal pain, and urticaria.
- **Management:** 
    - Grade 1: Reduce rate by 50%.
    - Grade 2: Withhold until Grade 0-1, then restart at a 50% slower rate.
    - Grade 3-4: Permanently discontinue. [Australian]

### Immune-Mediated Adverse Reactions (IMARs)
Avelumab can cause severe or fatal IMARs in any organ system. Early identification and management with corticosteroids (typically 1–2 mg/kg/day prednisone equivalent followed by a $\ge$1-month taper) are essential.
- **Pneumonitis:** Monitor for signs/symptoms; confirm with imaging. Permanently discontinue for Grade 3, 4, or recurrent Grade 2.
- **Hepatitis:** Monitor liver function tests (LFTs). Permanently discontinue for Grade 3 or 4.
- **Colitis:** Monitor for diarrhoea or abdominal pain. Permanently discontinue for Grade 4 or recurrent Grade 3.
- **Endocrinopathies:** Includes thyroid disorders (hypo/hyperthyroidism), adrenal insufficiency, and Type 1 diabetes mellitus. Manage with hormone replacement as indicated.
- **Nephritis and Renal Dysfunction:** Monitor serum creatinine.
- **Myocarditis & Pancreatitis:** Withhold for suspected cases; permanently discontinue if confirmed.
- **"Triple-M Syndrome":** Avelumab may cause simultaneous myositis, myocarditis, and myasthenia gravis. [Australian]

### Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events (MACE)
When used with axitinib, severe and fatal cardiovascular events have been reported. Monitor left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and signs of heart failure. [Australian]

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency as Monotherapy (mMCC and Solid Tumours)
- **Very Common ($\ge$10%):** Fatigue (32.4%), nausea (25.1%), diarrhoea (18.9%), constipation (18.4%), decreased appetite (18.4%), infusion-related reactions (17.1%), weight decrease (16.6%), vomiting (16.2%), anaemia (14.9%), cough (13.8%), pyrexia (13.6%), dyspnoea (13.2%), back pain (11.8%), and peripheral oedema (11.9%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Hypertension, headache, dizziness, arthralgia, pruritus, and hypothyroidism.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Immune-mediated pneumonitis, hepatitis, and colitis. Grade $\ge$3 anaemia (6.0%) and dyspnoea (3.9%). [Australian]

### Frequency in Combination with Axitinib (RCC)
- **Very Common ($\ge$10%):** Diarrhoea (62.2%), hypertension (50%), fatigue (35.5%), dysphonia (31.1%), musculoskeletal pain (37.1%), and nausea. [Australian]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted in humans.
- As a monoclonal antibody, avelumab is metabolised via catabolic pathways; therefore, pharmacokinetic interactions with cytochrome P450-mediated drugs are not expected. [Australian]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category D** [Australian].
- **Risk Summary:** Based on its mechanism (PD-L1 blockade), avelumab may cause foetal harm, including increased rates of abortion or stillbirth. IgG1 is known to cross the placenta.
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 1 month after the final dose. [Australian]
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if avelumab is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 1 month after the last dose. [Australian]

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for mMCC in patients $\ge$12 years. Safety/efficacy not established in children <12 years for mMCC, or in patients <18 years for UC and RCC. [Australian]
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy observed between patients $\ge$65 years and younger patients. [Australian]
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild or moderate impairment. Insufficient data for severe impairment (GFR 15 to 29 mL/min). [Australian]
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild hepatic impairment. Insufficient data for moderate or severe hepatic impairment. [Australian]

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Avelumab is a human IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds PD-L1 and blocks its interaction with PD-1 and B7.1 receptors. This inhibits the suppressive effects of PD-L1 on cytotoxic CD8+ T-cells, restoring anti-tumour immune responses. It also induces natural killer (NK) cell-mediated direct tumour cell lysis via antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC). [Australian]
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Small volume of distribution (4.12 L at steady state).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is approximately 6.1 days. Systemic clearance is 0.66 L/day. 
    - **Steady State:** Reached after 4 to 6 weeks of repeated dosing. [Australian]

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **mMCC (Study EMR100070-003):** A single-arm trial in two parts. Part A (previously treated) showed an Objective Response Rate (ORR) of 33%. Part B (treatment-naïve) showed an ORR of 39.7%. [Australian]
- **UC (Study B9991001):** Avelumab maintenance + BSC significantly improved overall survival (OS) compared to BSC alone (Median OS 21.4 vs 14.3 months; HR 0.69). [Australian]
- **RCC (Study B9991003):** Avelumab + axitinib significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS) compared to sunitinib (Median PFS 13.8 vs 8.4 months; HR 0.69). [Australian]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vial:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Store in the original package to protect from light. [Australian]
- **Diluted Solution:** 
    - Use immediately if possible.
    - May be stored for up to 8 hours at room temperature ($<25$°C).
    - May be stored for up to 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C. If refrigerated, allow to reach room temperature before administration. [Australian]

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
