# Editorial Product Information: Avanafil

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for **avanafil**, designed as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a synthesis of available regulatory data and does not replace the official Product Information mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis; information regarding other jurisdictions (US, European, Canadian, Japanese, etc.) is noted as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Avanafil**
- **Chemical Class:** Pyrimidine derivative; a selective and reversible inhibitor of cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP)-specific phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5).
- **Brand Names:** **SPEDRA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G04BE10
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets:** 50 mg, 100 mg, and 200 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** A. Menarini Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Erectile Dysfunction:** Treatment of erectile dysfunction in adult males [Australian]. 
    - *Note:* Sexual stimulation is required for the drug to be effective.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult Men):** The recommended dose is 100 mg taken approximately 15–30 minutes before anticipated sexual activity [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Based on efficacy and tolerability, the dose may be increased to a maximum of 200 mg or decreased to 50 mg. The lowest effective dose should be utilised.
- **Maximum Frequency:** Once per day.
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food. Onset of action may be delayed if taken with a high-fat meal [Australian].
- **Concomitant Alpha-blocker Therapy:** In patients stable on alpha-blocker therapy, **avanafil** should be initiated at the lowest dose of 50 mg [Australian].
- **Concomitant Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Dose should not exceed 100 mg, with a minimum interval of 48 hours between doses [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Nitrates:** Co-administration with any form of organic nitrates or nitric oxide donors (e.g., glyceryl trinitrate, isosorbide salts, sodium nitroprusside, amyl nitrite, nicorandil) [Australian].
- **Guanylate Cyclase Stimulators:** Concomitant use with riociguat (risk of symptomatic hypotension) [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Restrictions:**
    - Patients for whom sexual intercourse is inadvisable due to cardiovascular risk.
    - Unstable angina or angina occurring during intercourse.
    - Congestive heart failure (NYHA Class 2 or greater).
    - Myocardial infarction, stroke, or life-threatening arrhythmia/coronary revascularisation within the last 6 months.
    - Resting hypotension (< 90/50 mmHg) or hypertension (> 170/100 mmHg) [Australian].
- **Hepatic/Renal Impairment:**
    - Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C).
    - Severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) or end-stage renal disease on dialysis [Australian].
- **Ocular Conditions:**
    - Previous loss of vision in one eye due to non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy (NAION).
    - Known hereditary degenerative retinal disorders [Australian].
- **Potent CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with inhibitors such as ketoconazole, ritonavir, atazanavir, clarithromycin, and others [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **avanafil** or any tablet excipient.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Cardiovascular Status:** Physicians must consider cardiac risk during sexual activity in patients with pre-existing disease. **Avanafil** has vasodilatory properties that may augment the effects of antihypertensives.
- **Left Ventricular Outflow Obstruction:** Patients with aortic stenosis or idiopathic hypertrophic subaortic stenosis may be particularly sensitive to vasodilators.
- **Priapism:** Prolonged erections (>4 hours) require immediate medical attention to prevent penile tissue damage. Use with caution in patients with anatomical deformations (e.g., Peyronie's disease) or conditions predisposing to priapism (e.g., sickle cell anaemia).
- **Visual Effects:** Sudden visual defects or loss of vision may indicate NAION. Patients should discontinue PDE5 inhibitors and seek immediate care.
- **Sudden Hearing Loss:** Reported in association with PDE5 inhibitors; may be accompanied by tinnitus and dizziness.
- **Bleeding Disorders:** No safety data available for patients with bleeding disorders or active peptic ulceration; use only after careful risk-benefit assessment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (at 200 mg dose).
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Headache, Flushing, Nasal congestion [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** 
    - Dizziness, somnolence, sinus headache, vision blurred, palpitations, hot flush, dyspnoea exertional, dyspepsia, nausea, vomiting, stomach discomfort, back pain, muscle tightness, fatigue, increased hepatic enzymes, abnormal ECG [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** 
    - Influenza, nasopharyngitis, gout, insomnia, premature ejaculation, angina pectoris, tachycardia, hypertension, hypotension, epistaxis, dry mouth, gastritis, rash, myalgia, pollakiuria, and spontaneous penile erection [Australian].
- **Post-Marketing:** Central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR) (frequency unknown) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP3A4):**
    - **Potent Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir) Contraindicated; significantly increase **avanafil** exposure.
    - **Moderate Inhibitors:** (e.g., erythromycin, diltiazem, verapamil, fluconazole) Increase exposure; limit dose to 100 mg every 48 hours.
    - **Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine) Not recommended; may decrease efficacy [Australian].
    - **Grapefruit Juice:** Likely increases exposure; avoid within 24 hours of dosing.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Nitrates/GC Stimulators:** Potentiate hypotensive effects; contraindicated.
    - **Alpha-blockers:** Potential for additive blood pressure lowering and symptomatic hypotension.
    - **Alcohol:** Substantial consumption increases the risk of orthostatic hypotension, dizziness, and headache [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3:** Not indicated for use in women. Animal studies showed no direct harmful effects on development at standard doses, but fetal weight reduction and increased resorptions occurred at maternally toxic/supratherapeutic doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not indicated for use in women; no data available on excretion in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on sperm motility or morphology after single 200 mg doses in healthy volunteers. Daily 100 mg doses for 26 weeks showed no adverse effects on spermatogenesis [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available; not indicated for use in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required based on age alone (≥ 65 years); however, increased sensitivity in some older individuals should be considered. Data for patients > 70 years is limited [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate (CrCl ≥ 30 mL/min):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe/End-stage:** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** Initiate with the minimum effective dose and adjust based on tolerance.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective, reversible inhibitor of PDE5. It enhances the effect of nitric oxide by preventing the degradation of cGMP in the corpus cavernosum, leading to smooth muscle relaxation and inflow of blood.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits >100-fold selectivity for PDE5 over PDE6 (retina) and >20,000-fold selectivity over PDE3 (heart/blood vessels) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; $T_{max}$ of 30–45 minutes (fasted). 
    - **Distribution:** ~99% protein bound. Not found to accumulate with twice-daily dosing.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP3A4 (major) and CYP2C (minor). Major metabolites are M4 (18% potency of parent) and M16 (inactive).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (~63%) and to a lesser extent urinary (~21%). Terminal half-life is approximately 6–17 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Demonstrated in four randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials of up to 3 months. Significant improvements were seen in the International Index of Erectile Function (IIEF) score and successful intercourse (SEP3) across 50 mg, 100 mg, and 200 mg doses.
- **Onset of Action:** In a "time to onset" study, a significant percentage of sexual attempts were successful as early as 15 minutes after dosing with 100 mg and 200 mg compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Handling:** Keep in the original container (blister packs) to protect from light.
