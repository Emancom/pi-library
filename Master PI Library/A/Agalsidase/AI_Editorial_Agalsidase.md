# Editorial Product Information: Agalsidase Beta

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of official product information intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial serves as a clinical overview and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Agalsidase beta-rch (recombinant human $\alpha$-galactosidase A).
- **Chemical Class:** Alimentary tract and metabolism product; enzymes.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Fabrazyme**.
- **ATC Code:** A16AB04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile, non-pyrogenic, white to off-white lyophilised powder for injection. 
    - Strengths: 5 mg and 35 mg per vial [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Fabry Disease:** Indicated for long-term enzyme replacement therapy in patients with a confirmed diagnosis of Fabry disease ($\alpha$-galactosidase deficiency) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 1 mg/kg body weight administered every 2 weeks via intravenous infusion [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with Sterile Water for Injection to a concentration of 5 mg/mL (1.1 mL for the 5 mg vial; 7.2 mL for the 35 mg vial).
    - **Dilution:** Further dilute the reconstituted solution in 0.9% Sodium Chloride for Injection. The total volume is determined by the dose (e.g., <35 mg = 50 mL; >100 mg = 500 mL).
    - **Infusion Rate:** The initial rate must not exceed 0.25 mg/min (15 mg/hr). Once tolerance is established, the rate may be increased gradually.
    - **Filtration:** Use of an in-line, low protein-binding 0.2 $\mu$m filter is recommended.
    - **Line Management:** Do not infuse **Fabrazyme** in the same IV line with other products.
- **Home Infusion:** May be considered for patients tolerating infusions well, under the supervision of a trained healthcare professional [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Treatment is contraindicated in patients with clinical evidence of life-threatening hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis) to agalsidase beta or any of the excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Infusion-Associated Reactions (IARs):** Most patients develop IgG antibodies, which correlate with a higher risk of IARs. Symptoms include chills, fever, nausea, and headache. IARs are managed by slowing the infusion rate, temporary interruption, or administration of antipyretics, antihistamines, and/or corticosteroids.
- **Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis:** Severe allergic reactions require immediate discontinuation. Patients with positive IgE antibodies or skin tests have been successfully rechallenged using a reduced dose (0.5 mg/kg) and significantly slower initial rate (0.01 mg/min) [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Routine monitoring for IgG antibody formation is suggested (at baseline, then every 3 months for 18 months, then every 6 months until negative).
- **Specialist Oversight:** Therapy should be managed by physicians experienced in treating inherited metabolic diseases.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The majority of adverse events are mild to moderate and are categorised as infusion-associated reactions.

| Frequency | System Organ Class / Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Chills, pyrexia, feeling cold, nausea, vomiting, headache, paraesthesia. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Tachycardia, abdominal pain, fatigue, chest discomfort, hypertension, dizziness, somnolence, dyspnoea, pruritus, urticaria, flushing, pain in extremity. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Palpitations, lacrimation, asthenia, malaise, myalgia, back pain, cough, wheezing, rash, hypotension. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylactoid reactions, stroke/cerebrovascular accident, renal failure, cardiac arrest, and cardiac arrhythmia have been reported [Australian].
- **Post-Marketing Experience:** Leukocytoclastic vasculitis, hypoxia, and one case of membranous glomerulonephritis have been reported.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug/drug interaction studies have been performed. Metabolism-based cytochrome P450 interactions are unlikely [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** **Fabrazyme** should not be administered with **chloroquine**, **amiodarone**, **benoquin**, or **gentamicin** due to a theoretical risk of inhibited intracellular $\alpha$-galactosidase activity [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B2]. Data in pregnant women are limited. Animal studies (rats) at 45 times the human AUC showed no evidence of embryofetal harm. As a precaution, avoidance during pregnancy is preferred.
- **Breastfeeding:** Agalsidase beta is excreted in human milk. A decision must be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the maternal benefit against the infant risk.
- **Fertility:** No human studies available; no impairment noted in animal models.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Investigated in children aged 8–16 years. No dose adjustment is required for this age group. Safety/efficacy in children <8 years has not been evaluated, though treatment may be considered if clearly needed following risk/benefit analysis [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include patients aged 65 and over; differences in response compared to younger patients are unknown.
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific dose adjustment is suggested, though the drug's efficacy is primary in clearing renal vascular endothelium. 
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Caution should be exercised, though significant hepatic toxicity is not expected at clinical doses.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Recombinant human $\alpha$-galactosidase A provides an exogenous source of the enzyme to patients with Fabry disease. It catalyses the hydrolysis of glycosphingolipids, specifically globotriaosylceramide (GL-3), preventing its accumulation in the vascular endothelium and various organs.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment results in a dose-dependent clearance of GL-3 from the plasma and tissue vasculature (kidney, heart, and skin).
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - Displays non-linear pharmacokinetics.
    - **Half-life:** 45 to 102 minutes (dose-independent).
    - **Volume of Distribution:** 0.12 to 0.57 L/kg at steady state.
    - **Clearance:** Decreases after IgG seroconversion in paediatric patients, leading to increased exposure (2 to 4-fold) without a corresponding increase in adverse effects.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study:** A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=58) demonstrated that 1 mg/kg every 2 weeks for 5 months achieved the primary endpoint of GL-3 clearance (score of 0) from renal vascular endothelium in 69% of the treated group vs. 0% in placebo (p<0.0001) [Australian].
- **Phase 4 Study:** A trial (n=82) focused on clinical progression. While 27% of the agalsidase beta group met the composite clinical endpoint (renal, cardiac, cerebrovascular events or death) compared to 42% in the placebo group, the most pronounced benefits were observed in patients with less severe disease at baseline.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store under refrigeration between 2°C and 8°C. Do not use past the expiration date.
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Contains no preservatives; use immediately. If necessary, diluted solution is stable for up to 24 hours when stored at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Handling:** Vials are for single use in one patient only.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
