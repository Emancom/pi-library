# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) before and during therapy (weekly/monthly) alongside blood chemistries and vital signs [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2–3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history, frequency or severity may increase [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** (Note: specific to oral forms mentioned in the IV PI) High doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally renally excreted [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported with a frequency ≥5% in controlled trials (regardless of causality):
- **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain (21%), diarrhea (35%), nausea (12%), vomiting (21%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (21%), hypotension (19%), tachycardia (9%), arrhythmia (3%).
- **Neurological/Nervous:** Headache (37%), dizziness (18%), paresthesia (12%), insomnia (6%).
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (38%), pain (35%), fever (12%), chest pain (15%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (27%), cough increase (18%), dyspnea (11%).
- **Other:** Body odor, gastritis, and transient nausea/vomiting have been observed.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress (bronchospasm).
- Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category B (inferred)]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; however, supplementation has not been specifically studied in nursing mothers. Benefits to the mother should be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake for the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD includes paediatric patients as specified in the general dosing sections [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources beyond general precautions.
- **Renal Impairment:** IV levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients. Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral formulations are used in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed pre-dialysis (trough) levels increased from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses three times weekly demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison Schedule/Legal Classification: Prescription drug [US].
