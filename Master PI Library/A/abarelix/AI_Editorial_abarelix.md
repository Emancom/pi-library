# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (generic); [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (Standard: A16AA01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **ESRD-Related Carnitine Deficiency:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often administered in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Dosing should be q3h or q4h, and never less than q6h [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **Target Plasma Concentration:** Free carnitine levels should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are supranormal (>50 µmol/L) [US].

### General Instructions
- **Inspection:** Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of injection. Discontinue treatment immediately upon sign of severe reaction [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of epilepsy, the frequency or severity of seizures may increase [US].
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

| System Organ Class | Common (1–10%) | Very Common (>10%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Fever, infection, asthenia | Pain, headache, injection site reaction, flu syndrome, abdominal pain, chest pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Tachycardia, palpitations, arrhythmia | Hypertension, hypotension |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia | Diarrhea |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, paresthesia, depression | Not specified |
| **Respiratory** | Cough, rhinitis, dyspnea | Pharyngitis |
| **Other** | Body odor, gastritis, anemia | Hypervolemia, hypercalcemia |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure disorders) [US].
- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned, though reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8x human dose showed no harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after administration. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders (50 mg/kg) is applicable to paediatric patients. Specific age-range safety profiles are not detailed, but use is indicated for inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Dose adjustments are based on trough carnitine levels [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine can promote the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study in 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly) showed pre-dialysis carnitine levels rose from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Evaluated 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses. Trough concentrations increased proportionally with the dose over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on clinical outcomes in this specific population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only.
