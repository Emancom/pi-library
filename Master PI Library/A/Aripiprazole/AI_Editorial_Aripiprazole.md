# Editorial Product Information: Aripiprazole

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **aripiprazole** for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Aripiprazole
*   **Chemical Class:** Atypical antipsychotic (quinolone derivative).
*   **Brand Names:** **Abilify** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** N05AX12.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   Oral Tablets: 2 mg, 5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg, 20 mg, and 30 mg [Australian].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Otsuka Australia Pharmaceutical Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Schizophrenia:** Treatment of schizophrenia, including the maintenance of clinical improvement during continued therapy [Australian].
*   **Bipolar I Disorder:** 
    *   Acute treatment of manic or mixed episodes (as monotherapy or adjunctive therapy with lithium or valproate) [Australian].
    *   Maintenance treatment of manic or mixed episodes (as monotherapy) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
*   **Administration:** Administered once daily, without regard to meals [Australian].
*   **Schizophrenia:** 
    *   **Initial Dose:** 10 mg or 15 mg once daily.
    *   **Maintenance Dose:** 15 mg daily.
    *   **Range:** 10 mg to 30 mg daily. Doses above 15 mg have not demonstrated superior efficacy [Australian].
*   **Bipolar I Disorder:** 
    *   **Monotherapy/Adjunctive:** Start at 15 mg once daily.
    *   **Range:** May be increased to 30 mg daily based on clinical response [Australian].
*   **Maintenance (Bipolar I):** Patients responding to acute treatment may continue at 15 mg or 30 mg daily [Australian].

### Titration and Switching
*   **Titration:** Dosage increases should occur at intervals of no less than 2 weeks to allow for the achievement of steady-state concentrations [Australian].
*   **Switching from other Antipsychotics:** Can involve immediate discontinuation of the previous regimen or a cross-taper over 2 weeks [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **aripiprazole** or any excipients (e.g., lactose monohydrate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Elderly Patients with Dementia-Related Psychosis:** [Boxed Warning equivalent/Clinical Warning] Increased risk of death compared to placebo. Analysis of trials showed a 1.6 to 1.7 times higher risk of mortality, primarily due to cardiovascular or infectious (e.g., pneumonia) causes. **Aripiprazole** is not approved for dementia-related psychosis [Australian].
*   **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** A potentially fatal complex featuring hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, and autonomic instability. Discontinue all antipsychotics immediately if suspected [Australian].
*   **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Risk increases with long-term exposure. Consider discontinuation if signs/symptoms appear [Australian].
*   **Hyperglycemia and Diabetes Mellitus:** Severe cases (including ketoacidosis/coma) reported. Monitor glucose in patients with diabetes or risk factors (obesity, family history) [Australian].
*   **Pathological Gambling and Impulse-Control Disorders:** Patients may experience intense urges to gamble, binge eat, spend, or engage in sexual activity. Prescribers should specifically screen for new or increased urges [Australian].
*   **Suicide:** Risk is inherent in psychotic illnesses and Bipolar Disorder; close supervision of high-risk patients is required [Australian].
*   **Cerebrovascular Adverse Events:** Increased incidence of stroke and TIA observed in elderly patients with Alzheimer's-related psychosis [Australian].
*   **Orthostatic Hypotension:** May occur due to $\alpha_1$-adrenergic antagonism; use caution in patients with known cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease [Australian].
*   **Seizures:** Use cautiously in patients with a history of seizure disorders [Australian].
*   **Sleep Apnea:** Reported in patients with or without a prior history; use caution in those with risk factors (obesity, male gender) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency of Adverse Reactions (Adults)
Based on short-term, placebo-controlled trials [Australian]:

| System Organ Class | Very Common (>10%) | Common (1–10%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous System** | Headache, Akathisia | Dizziness, Sedation, Extrapyramidal disorder, Tremor, Somnolence |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea | Constipation, Vomiting, Dyspepsia, Dry mouth |
| **Psychiatric** | Agitation, Insomnia, Anxiety | Restlessness |
| **General** | - | Fatigue, Pain, Asthenia |
| **Respiratory** | - | Pharyngolaryngeal pain, Cough |
| **Eye** | - | Blurred vision |

*   **Weight Gain:** In short-term schizophrenia trials, 8% of **aripiprazole** patients had significant weight gain ($\ge$ 7% of body weight) vs 3% on placebo [Australian].
*   **Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS):** In Bipolar monotherapy, akathisia incidence was 13% vs 4% for placebo [Australian].
*   **Post-marketing Reports:** Rare instances of DRESS (Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms), restless legs syndrome, and oculogyric crisis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin) decrease **aripiprazole** clearance. The dose should be reduced (approximately half) [Australian].
*   **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., quinidine, fluoxetine, paroxetine) decrease clearance. The dose should be halved [Australian].
*   **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Potent inducers (e.g., carbamazepine, St John’s Wort) can decrease **aripiprazole** AUC by ~70%. The dose should be increased [Australian].
*   **CNS Agents and Alcohol:** Use caution due to additive sedative effects; patients should avoid alcohol [Australian].
*   **Antihypertensive Agents:** **Aripiprazole** may enhance the effects of certain antihypertensives via $\alpha_1$-adrenergic antagonism [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Neonates exposed during the third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks to the fetus.
*   **Breastfeeding:** **Aripiprazole** is excreted in human breast milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no direct impairment of fertility, though oestrous cyclicity was perturbed in females due to hyperprolactinaemia [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** Clearance is reduced by 20% in patients $\ge$ 65 years, but no specific dose adjustment is required based solely on age. However, caution is advised due to the risk of dementia-related mortality [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for Child-Pugh Class A, B, or C [Australian].
*   **Gender:** No adjustment required, though women show ~30-40% higher Cmax/AUC, primarily due to body weight differences [Australian].
*   **Poor Metabolizers of CYP2D6:** Approximately 8% of Caucasians; these individuals may have higher exposure and should be monitored for adverse effects [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Efficacy is proposed to be mediated via partial agonist activity at $D_2$ and $5HT_{1A}$ receptors, and antagonist activity at $5HT_{2A}$ receptors [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Bioavailability of 87%; peak plasma concentration in 3–5 hours.
    *   **Distribution:** Extensively distributed (Vd 4.9 L/kg); >99% protein bound.
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP3A4 and CYP2D6. Major active metabolite is dehydro-aripiprazole.
    *   **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 75 hours for **aripiprazole** and 100 hours for dehydro-aripiprazole. 27% excreted in urine, 60% in faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Schizophrenia:** Efficacy established in short-term (4-6 week) trials using PANSS and BPRS scales. Maintenance of effect was demonstrated in a 26-week placebo-controlled trial where the **aripiprazole** group had a significantly lower relapse rate (34% vs 57%) [Australian].
*   **Bipolar I Disorder:** Efficacy in acute mania established in 3-week trials using the Y-MRS scale. Maintenance was demonstrated in a 26-week randomised withdrawal study [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
*   **Packaging:** Aluminium blisters in cartons [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Poisons Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Medicine)** [Australian].
