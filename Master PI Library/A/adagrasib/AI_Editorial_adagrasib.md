# Editorial Product Information: Adagrasib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of adagrasib for healthcare professionals, synthesising data from the available regulatory source.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Adagrasib (as a crystalline solid).
- **Chemical Class:** Irreversible inhibitor of the RAS GTPase family (specifically KRAS G12C).
- **Brand Name:** **KRAZATI** [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01XX77.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - **Coated Tablet:** 200 mg (oval, white to off-white, immediate release, debossed with "200" and "M") [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mirati Therapeutics, Inc. (A Bristol Myers Squibb Company) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** As monotherapy for the treatment of adults with *KRAS G12C*-mutated locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC, as determined by an approved test, who have received at least one prior systemic therapy [US].
- **Colorectal Cancer (CRC):** In combination with cetuximab for the treatment of adults with *KRAS G12C*-mutated locally advanced or metastatic CRC, as determined by an approved test, following prior treatment with fluoropyrimidine-, oxaliplatin-, and irinotecan-based chemotherapy [US].

*Note: In the US, these indications are approved under accelerated approval based on objective response rate (ORR) and duration of response (DOR); continued approval may be contingent upon verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosage:** 600 mg (three 200 mg tablets) taken orally twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets should be swallowed whole; do not chew, crush, or split [US].
    - May be taken with or without food [US].
    - If vomiting occurs after administration, an additional dose should not be taken; resume at the next scheduled time [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed by more than 4 hours, the dose should be skipped. Resume dosing at the next scheduled time [US].
- **Dose Modifications for Adverse Reactions:**
    - **First reduction:** 400 mg twice daily [US].
    - **Second reduction:** 600 mg once daily [US].
    - **Discontinuation:** Permanently discontinue if the patient is unable to tolerate 600 mg once daily [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal Adverse Reactions:** Severe reactions (diarrhea, nausea, vomiting) are common. Monitor patients and provide supportive care (anti-emetics/antidiarrheals). Withhold or reduce dose based on severity [US].
- **QTc Interval Prolongation:** Adagrasib can cause concentration-dependent QTc prolongation. Avoid use in patients with congenital long QT syndrome or those with concurrent QTc prolongation. Monitor ECGs and electrolytes (potassium, magnesium) in at-risk patients [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Drug-induced liver injury (DILI) can occur. Monitor liver function tests (AST, ALT, bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase) prior to initiation, then monthly for the first 3 months, and as clinically indicated [US].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD) / Pneumonitis:** Can be fatal. Monitor for new or worsening respiratory symptoms (e.g., dyspnea, cough, fever). Withhold adagrasib for suspected ILD and permanently discontinue if confirmed [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions ($\ge$ 25%)
- **Monotherapy (NSCLC):** Nausea (70%), diarrhea (69%), vomiting (57%), fatigue (55%), musculoskeletal pain (38%), hepatotoxicity (37%), renal impairment (33%), edema (30%), dyspnea (26%), and decreased appetite (29%) [US].
- **Combination with Cetuximab (CRC):** Rash (84%), nausea (68%), diarrhea (65%), vomiting (57%), fatigue (57%), musculoskeletal pain (47%), hepatotoxicity (38%), headache (37%), and dry skin (36%) [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Serious reactions (NSCLC):** Occurred in 57% of patients; most common included pneumonia (17%), dyspnea (9%), and renal impairment (8%). Fatal reactions occurred in 11%, primarily due to pneumonia [US].
- **Serious reactions (CRC):** Occurred in 30% of patients; most common included pneumonia (4.3%) and pleural effusion (2.1%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use (e.g., rifampin), as adagrasib exposure is significantly reduced [US].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Avoid concomitant use until adagrasib reaches steady state (approx. 8 days) [US].
- **Adagrasib as a Perpetrator:** 
    - **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Avoid use with sensitive substrates (e.g., midazolam) [US].
    - **CYP2C9/CYP2D6 Substrates:** Avoid use where minimal concentration changes lead to serious toxicity [US].
    - **P-gp Substrates:** Avoid use with sensitive substrates (e.g., digoxin) [US].
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Avoid concomitant use with other drugs known to prolong the QTc interval [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in the US PI. 
    - **Risk Summary:** No human data available. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed skeletal malformations and variations at maternally toxic doses (approx. 2x human exposure) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of adagrasib in human milk. Advise women not to breastfeed during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Based on animal toxicology (phospholipidosis in reproductive organs), adagrasib may impair fertility in both males and females [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established [US].
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 65 years):** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed compared to younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment recommended for patients with mild to severe renal impairment ($CLcr$ 15 to $< 90$ mL/min) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment recommended for patients with mild to severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A to C) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Adagrasib is a potent, irreversible inhibitor of KRAS G12C. It covalently binds to the mutant cysteine, locking the protein in its inactive GDP-bound state, thereby inhibiting downstream signalling and tumor growth [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median $T_{max}$ is 6 hours. Steady state reached in 8 days with 6-fold accumulation [US].
    - **Distribution:** Apparent $V_d$ is 942 L; protein binding is 98% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4. It also undergoes auto-inhibition of CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 23 hours. Clearance is 37 L/h. Primary excretion route is faeces (75%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **KRYSTAL-1 (NSCLC):** A multicenter, single-arm study of 112 patients. Adagrasib 600 mg BID achieved an ORR of 43% (95% CI: 34, 53) and a median DOR of 8.5 months [US].
- **KRYSTAL-1 (CRC):** A study of 94 patients receiving adagrasib in combination with cetuximab. ORR was 34% (95% CI: 25, 45) with a median DOR of 5.8 months [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in original child-resistant container. Contains a desiccant; do not remove or eat [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx Only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
