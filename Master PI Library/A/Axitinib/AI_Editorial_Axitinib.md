# Editorial Product Information: Axitinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the provided official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- Information from other major jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, UK, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source material for this specific review. Where regional variations are standard in clinical practice but absent from the provided document, they are noted as "Not specified in available sources."
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Axitinib (a selective tyrosine kinase inhibitor).
- **Chemical Class:** Kinase inhibitor; vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR) antagonist.
- **Brand Name:** **INLYTA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EK01 (derived from international standards; not explicitly in source).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 1 mg (red, oval, debossed with "Pfizer" and "1 XNB") and 5 mg (red, triangular, debossed with "Pfizer" and "5 XNB") [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Advanced Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC):** Indicated for the treatment of patients with advanced renal cell carcinoma after the failure of one prior systemic therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Initial Dose:** 5 mg taken orally twice daily [Australian].
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food. Tablets should be swallowed whole with a glass of water. If a patient vomits or misses a dose, an additional dose should not be taken; the next dose should be taken at the scheduled time [Australian].
- **Dose Titration (Increase):** For patients who tolerate the 5 mg BID dose for two consecutive weeks without adverse reactions >Grade 2, are normotensive, and are not on antihypertensive medication:
    - Increase to 7 mg twice daily.
    - Using the same criteria, the dose may be further increased to a maximum of 10 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustment (Reduction):** Based on individual safety/tolerability, the dose may be reduced to 3 mg twice daily, and then further to 2 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Concomitant Strong CYP3A4/5 Inhibitors:** Avoid if possible. If required, reduce the axitinib dose by approximately half (e.g., 5 mg BID to 2 mg BID) [Australian].
- **Concomitant Strong CYP3A4/5 Inducers:** Avoid if possible. If required, a gradual dose increase of axitinib is recommended with close toxicity monitoring [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to axitinib or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypertension:** Very common; may occur as early as 4 days after initiation. Blood pressure must be well-controlled prior to starting therapy and monitored frequently during treatment [Australian].
- **Cardiac Failure:** Reported in 1.8% of patients. Monitor for signs/symptoms (e.g., dyspnea, edema) throughout treatment [Australian].
- **Thyroid Dysfunction:** Both hypothyroidism (24.6%) and hyperthyroidism (1.6%) have been reported. Monitor TSH levels before and during therapy [Australian].
- **Arterial Thromboembolic Events (ATE):** Use with caution in patients at risk for or with a history of ATE (e.g., TIA, MI, CVA). Axitinib has not been studied in patients with an ATE within the previous 12 months [Australian].
- **Venous Thromboembolic Events (VTE):** Reported including pulmonary embolism and DVT. Use with caution in high-risk patients; not studied in patients with a VTE within the previous 6 months [Australian].
- **Hemorrhage:** Fatal events have been reported. Axitinib has not been studied in patients with untreated brain metastases or recent active GI bleeding [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation and Fistula:** Periodically monitor for symptoms of GI perforation.
- **Reversible Posterior Leukoencephalopathy Syndrome (RPLS):** Rare neurological disorder; confirm with MRI. Discontinue if RPLS occurs [Australian].
- **Wound Healing:** Stop axitinib at least 24 hours prior to scheduled surgery due to potential interference with wound healing [Australian].
- **Proteinuria:** Monitor before and during treatment. Discontinue if nephrotic syndrome develops [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) periodically [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in pooled trials (N=672) [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhea (55.4%), Hypertension (51.2%), Fatigue (45.1%), Decreased appetite (39.0%), Nausea (33.0%), Dysphonia (32.7%), PPE/Hand-foot syndrome (32.1%), Weight decreased (32.7%), Hemorrhage (25.7%), Hypothyroidism (24.6%), Vomiting (23.7%), Proteinuria (21.1%), Cough (20.4%), Constipation (20.2%), Dyspnea (17.1%), Arthralgia (17.7%), Headache (16.2%), Stomatitis (15.5%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dehydration, Hyperkalemia, Hypercalcemia, Dizziness, Tinnitus, Cardiac failure, GI perforation/fistula, Rash, Alopecia, Myalgia, Renal failure |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | RPLS (0.3%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Hypertension (Grade 3/4: 24%), Diarrhea (Grade 3/4: 10.2%), Fatigue (Grade 3/4: 10.9%), and Palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia (PPE) (Grade 3/4: 7.6%) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4/5 Inhibitors (Strong):** Ketoconazole (strong inhibitor) increased axitinib AUC by 2-fold. Avoid strong inhibitors (e.g., clarithromycin, indinavir, itra/ketoconazole, ritonavir); if unavoidable, reduce axitinib dose [Australian].
- **CYP3A4/5 Inducers (Strong):** Rifampin (strong inducer) decreased axitinib AUC by 79%. Avoid strong inducers (e.g., carbamazepine, dexamethasone, phenytoin, rifampin, St. John's wort) [Australian].
- **CYP1A2 and CYP2C8:** Axitinib may inhibit these enzymes in vitro; however, clinical studies with paclitaxel (CYP2C8) showed no significant interaction [Australian].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** Axitinib inhibits P-gp in vitro but is not expected to affect the pharmacokinetics of digoxin at therapeutic concentrations [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Axitinib is embryotoxic and teratogenic in animals. It should not be used during pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 4 weeks after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety in human milk is unknown. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Based on animal findings, axitinib has the potential to impair reproductive function and fertility in humans [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required. In clinical trials, 34.3% of patients were ≥65 years; no overall differences in safety/efficacy were observed [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild to severe impairment (CrCL 15–89 mL/min). Use caution in end-stage renal disease (CrCL <15 mL/min) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Starting dose should be reduced to **2 mg twice daily**.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not studied; use is not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Axitinib is a potent and selective tyrosine kinase inhibitor of VEGFR-1, VEGFR-2, and VEGFR-3. It inhibits VEGF-mediated endothelial cell proliferation and survival, thereby inhibiting angiogenesis and tumor growth [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 58%. Peak plasma concentrations are reached within 4 hours (Tmax 2.5–4.1h). High-fat meals increase exposure by 19% [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (>99%), primarily to albumin [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised in the liver by CYP3A4/5; minor contributions from CYP1A2, CYP2C19, and UGT1A1 [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in feces (30-60%) and urine (23%). Plasma half-life is 2.5 to 6.1 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Phase 3 Study:** A randomised, open-label, multicenter study compared axitinib to sorafenib in 723 patients with advanced RCC after failure of one prior systemic therapy.
    - **Primary Endpoint:** Progression-free survival (PFS).
    - **Results:** Axitinib significantly improved median PFS (6.7 months) compared to sorafenib (4.7 months); HR 0.67 (95% CI 0.54, 0.81); p<0.0001 [Australian].
    - **Overall Survival (OS):** No statistically significant difference was observed between treatment arms (20.1 vs 19.2 months) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on packaging [Australian].
