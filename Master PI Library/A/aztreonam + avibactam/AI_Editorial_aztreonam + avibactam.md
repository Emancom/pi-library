# Editorial Product Information: aztreonam + avibactam

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **aztreonam + avibactam**
- Chemical class:
  - Aztreonam: monobactam antibacterial agent
  - Avibactam: non‑beta‑lactam beta‑lactamase inhibitor
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous infusion (IV)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Last revision noted in source material: June 2026 (date reported in available source; jurisdiction not separately itemised)
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated for treatment of complicated intra‑abdominal infections (cIAI) in adult patients (≥18 years) who have limited or no alternative therapeutic options, when used in combination with metronidazole.
- Approved/labelled pathogen coverage (when organism is susceptible): Escherichia coli; Klebsiella pneumoniae; Klebsiella oxytoca; Enterobacter cloacae complex; Citrobacter freundii complex; Serratia marcescens.
- Note: Approval is based on limited clinical safety and efficacy data.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Dosing is weight‑independent and is adjusted by estimated creatinine clearance (CLcr) calculated using the Cockcroft‑Gault formula.
- Administration: each dose is administered by intravenous infusion over 3 hours unless otherwise specified below.
- A single loading dose is given once, followed by maintenance doses beginning at the next scheduled dosing interval. Dosing interval is measured from the start of one infusion to the start of the next infusion.
- For treatment of cIAI, metronidazole should be administered concurrently.

### Recommended adult dosing by estimated CLcr
- CLcr > 50 mL/min
  - Loading: aztreonam + avibactam 2.67 g (aztreonam 2.0 g + avibactam 0.67 g) IV infusion over 3 hours, then
  - Maintenance: aztreonam + avibactam 2.0 g (aztreonam 1.5 g + avibactam 0.5 g) IV infusion over 3 hours every 6 hours
- CLcr > 30 to ≤ 50 mL/min
  - Loading: aztreonam + avibactam 2.67 g (aztreonam 2.0 g + avibactam 0.67 g) IV infusion over 3 hours, then
  - Maintenance: aztreonam + avibactam 1.0 g (aztreonam 0.75 g + avibactam 0.25 g) IV infusion over 3 hours every 6 hours
- CLcr > 15 to ≤ 30 mL/min
  - Loading: aztreonam + avibactam 1.8 g (aztreonam 1.35 g + avibactam 0.45 g) IV infusion over 3 hours, then
  - Maintenance: aztreonam + avibactam 0.9 g (aztreonam 0.675 g + avibactam 0.225 g) IV infusion over 3 hours every 8 hours
- CLcr ≤ 15 mL/min, including patients on hemodialysis
  - Loading: aztreonam + avibactam 1.33 g (aztreonam 1.0 g + avibactam 0.33 g) IV infusion over 3 hours, then
  - Maintenance: aztreonam + avibactam 0.9 g (aztreonam 0.675 g + avibactam 0.225 g) IV infusion over 3 hours every 12 hours
- Hemodialysis: both aztreonam and avibactam are hemodialyzable; administer dose after hemodialysis on dialysis days.

### Treatment duration
- Recommended duration for cIAI: 5 to 14 days.

### Preparation and administration instructions
- Reconstitution:
  - Reconstitute vial powder with 10 mL Sterile Water for Injection. Mix gently until dissolved.
  - The reconstituted vial yields approximately 12 mL total volume with aztreonam ≈127 mg/mL and avibactam ≈42.3 mg/mL.
  - The reconstituted solution is not for direct injection and must be further diluted.
- Withdrawal volumes (approximate) for preparation of doses (withdraw from reconstituted vial; discard unused portion):
  - Loading doses:
    - 2.67 g dose: withdraw ~15.7 mL (requires two vials)
    - 1.8 g dose: withdraw ~10.6 mL
    - 1.33 g dose: withdraw ~7.9 mL
  - Maintenance doses:
    - 2.0 g dose: use entire contents of one vial (≈12 mL)
    - 1.0 g dose: withdraw ~5.9 mL
    - 0.9 g dose: withdraw ~5.3 mL
- Dilution:
  - Further dilute withdrawn reconstituted volume to 50–250 mL infusion bag using one of: 0.9% sodium chloride, 5% dextrose, or lactated Ringer’s solution.
  - Target concentration ranges after dilution: aztreonam 2.7–40 mg/mL; avibactam 0.9–13.3 mg/mL.
  - Inspect solution visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration (colour may range from clear/colorless to yellow).
- Compatibility:
  - Compatible diluents for reconstitution and dilution: Sterile Water for Injection (reconstitution) and 0.9% sodium chloride, 5% dextrose, or lactated Ringer’s (dilution).
  - Do not mix with or physically add other medicinal products; compatibility with other drugs has not been established.
- Storage of prepared solutions:
  - Reconstituted solution: may be held up to 60 minutes at up to 30°C prior to transfer and dilution.
  - Diluted solutions stored in infusion bags:
    - If diluted in 0.9% sodium chloride or lactated Ringer’s: refrigerate 2–8°C up to 24 hours, followed by up to 12 hours at not more than 30°C (under ambient light).
    - If diluted in 5% dextrose: refrigerate 2–8°C up to 24 hours, followed by up to 4 hours at not more than 30°C (under ambient light).
  - Discard unused portions after these timeframes.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to aztreonam, avibactam, or any component of the formulation.
- No other contraindications are specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed warnings: None specified in available sources.
- General warnings and important precautions (critical):
  - **Hypersensitivity reactions**: Serious allergic reactions including rash, flushing, bronchospasm have been observed. Discontinue drug and provide appropriate treatment if hypersensitivity occurs.
  - **Serious skin reactions**: Reports of toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) have been associated with aztreonam in heavily pre‑treated patients (e.g., bone marrow transplant recipients with multiple risk factors). Discontinue immediately if serious skin reaction occurs.
  - **Hepatic adverse reactions**: Elevations in hepatic transaminases have been observed. Monitor liver function tests and consider discontinuation if clinically indicated. Transaminase elevations have resolved after discontinuation in reported cases.
  - **Clostridioides difficile–associated diarrhea (CDAD)**: Use of systemic antibacterials, including aztreonam + avibactam, may result in CDAD; consider diagnosis in patients with diarrhea.
  - **Development of drug‑resistant bacteria**: Use only to treat proven or strongly suspected bacterial infections to limit resistance emergence.
- Monitoring requirements:
  - Assess for history of hypersensitivity before initiation.
  - Monitor liver enzymes during therapy, particularly in patients with baseline liver disease or receiving other hepatotoxic agents.
  - Monitor renal function (CLcr) during therapy; dosing adjustments are required in renal impairment.
  - Monitor for signs of CDAD in patients with diarrhea.
  - On hemodialysis days, give dose after dialysis; both components are removed by hemodialysis.
- Specific clinical precautions:
  - For cIAI, concomitant administration of metronidazole is required.
  - Exercise caution in patients with multiple risk factors for severe skin reactions.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- Summary from clinical trial data (Trial 1) and reported adverse reactions with aztreonam component:
  - Most common adverse reactions (incidence >5% in Trial 1, aztreonam + avibactam arm):
    - Hepatic adverse reactions (14.5%)
    - Anemia (8.0%)
    - Diarrhea (5.8%)
    - Hypokalemia (5.8%)
    - Pyrexia (5.8%)
  - Additional adverse reactions observed (incidence <5% in Trial 1 or reported with aztreonam component); exact frequencies often not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Aztreonam: a monobactam antibacterial active against certain gram‑negative organisms by inhibiting cell wall synthesis.
- Avibactam: a beta‑lactamase inhibitor that restores or extends aztreonam activity against some beta‑lactamase–producing gram‑negative bacteria, including organisms expressing metallo‑beta‑lactamases when combined appropriately.
- The combination is used with metronidazole for mixed intra‑abdominal infections to cover anaerobic organisms.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Both aztreonam and avibactam are removed by hemodialysis.
- Reconstituted and diluted concentrations and infusion parameters are specified in the product preparation instructions (see Section 4).
- Other detailed absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination numeric parameters (e.g., half‑life, clearance) are not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Microbiologic activity: labeled organisms (see Section 3) are listed as susceptible pathogens in the approved indication.
- Detailed pharmacodynamic targets and PK/PD relationships are not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal clinical trial program included three trials (Trials 1, 2, and 3); Trial 1 provides the principal comparative safety dataset.
- Trial 1 (summary):
  - Design: randomized, comparative trial in patients with complicated intra‑abdominal infection (cIAI) and hospital‑acquired/ventilator‑associated bacterial pneumonia (HABP/VABP) (note: HABP/VABP is not an approved indication).
  - Participants: 275 patients received aztreonam + avibactam; 137 received comparator (meropenem ± colistin). Of the aztreonam + avibactam group, 203 had cIAI and 72 had HABP/VABP.
  - Dosing in trial: patients received an initial loading strategy (a short infusion followed by an extended infusion) then maintenance infusions every 6 hours (dose adjustment by CLcr); patients with cIAI in the aztreonam + avibactam arm also received metronidazole 500 mg IV every 8 hours.
  - Demographics: median age 58 years (range 18–87); majority male (66%) and White (59%); varying baseline severity including APACHE II > 10 in a proportion of patients.
  - Efficacy/safety outcomes:
    - Deaths: 6.9% (19/275) in aztreonam + avibactam group vs 8.0% (11/137) in comparator group overall. In cIAI subgroup: 3.0% (6/203) vs 2.9% (3/103).
    - Discontinuation due to adverse reaction: 3.6% in both treatment and comparator arms.
    - Hepatic adverse reactions occurred more frequently in the aztreonam + avibactam arm (15%) than comparator (12%); ALT ≥5× ULN occurred in 3.8% vs 3.1%; no Hy’s Law cases reported across Trials 1–3.
- Trials 2 and 3:
  - Smaller noncomparative studies conducted in patients with cIAI and other serious infections due to gram‑negative pathogens expressing metallo‑beta‑lactamases. Detailed trial design and outcomes for Trials 2 and 3 are not specified in available sources.
