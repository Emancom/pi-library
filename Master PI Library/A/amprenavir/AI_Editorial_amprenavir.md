# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory information for **levocarnitine** injection.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    *   *Severe Metabolic Crisis:* A loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours. Daily maintenance doses typically range from 50 mg/kg but may reach 300 mg/kg if required [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administration should occur as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
*   **Compatibility:** Stable when mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL. Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) [US].

### Monitoring
*   Plasma carnitine levels should be obtained prior to therapy.
*   Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries and vital signs.
*   **Target Concentration:** Plasma free carnitine should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These may occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
*   **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
*   **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events occurring at a frequency ≥5% in controlled trials (regardless of causality) include:
*   **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), flu syndrome (25–29%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), chest pain (6–15%), abdominal pain (5–21%) [US].
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), arrhythmia (3%) [US].
*   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
*   **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%) [US].

### Other Reported Reactions
*   **Neurologic:** Seizures.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, transient nausea/vomiting, and body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when co-administered with levocarnitine. INR levels should be monitored closely upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled human studies exist. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg), applicable across pediatric age ranges as clinically indicated [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided in source document; generally, dose selection for an elderly patient should be cautious [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** **Levocarnitine** is indicated for ESRD; however, oral formulations are cautioned due to metabolite accumulation [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is essential for energy metabolism. It acts as a carrier molecule, facilitating the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Following IV administration (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine levels from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
*   **Note:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation is preservative-free [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription drug).
