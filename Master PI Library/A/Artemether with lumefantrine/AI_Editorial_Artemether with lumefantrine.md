# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Note:** While the user context mentioned Artemether/Lumefantrine, the source document provided for analysis is the Levocarnitine Injection USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) PI. This editorial pertains exclusively to **Levocarnitine**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a synthesis of clinical data and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Administer as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - Loading Dose: Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
    - Maximum Dose: The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dose Adjustments: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may occur as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard unused portions as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations is recommended. 
- Target plasma free carnitine concentration: 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, often within minutes of administration. Most reports involve ESRD patients. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality) [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Effect | Frequency (Levocarnitine 10-40mg) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Injection site reaction, Pain, Flu syndrome, Chest pain, Headache, Infection, Abdominal pain, Fever | Very Common (>10%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, Hypotension, Tachycardia | Very Common (>10%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, Vomiting, Nausea | Very Common (>10%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, Paresthesia | Very Common (>10%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, Cough increase, Rhinitis, Dyspnea | Very Common (>10%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, seizures, and laryngeal edema [US].
- **Other Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned in current labeling]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk. The benefits to the mother must be weighed against potential risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); safety and efficacy follow adult parameters for indicated metabolic conditions [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific dosing provided for ESRD patients on hemodialysis (see Section 3). Cautions exist regarding the accumulation of metabolites if using oral formulations in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15-16% after correction for endogenous levels [US].
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), mean pre-dialysis trough levels increased from ~21 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature when mixed in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
