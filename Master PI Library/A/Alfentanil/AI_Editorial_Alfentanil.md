# Editorial Product Information: Alfentanil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was provided for review; therefore, regional variations from other jurisdictions (e.g., US, Europe, Canada) are not represented in this version.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Alfentanil hydrochloride (a synthetic opioid of the phenylpiperidine class, chemically related to fentanyl).
- **Brand Names:** **Alfentanil-hameln** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N01AH02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection: 0.5 mg/mL (as hydrochloride) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** hameln pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Alfentanil** is indicated for intravenous use by specialist anaesthetists and their trainees as [Australian]:
- An analgesic supplement administered via incremental intravenous boluses or continuous infusion.
- An anaesthetic induction agent for patients undergoing in-patient surgery where endotracheal intubation and controlled ventilation are required.

Due to its rapid onset and short duration, it is specifically suited for short procedures and outpatient surgery, provided appropriate post-operative surveillance is available [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Dosage must be individualized based on age, weight, physical status, pathology, concomitant medication use, and the type/duration of surgery. The lowest effective dose should be utilized [Australian].

### Adult Dosing by Technique [Australian]
*   **Spontaneous Ventilation:** 
    *   Initial dose: Up to 7 micrograms/kg (slow IV).
    *   Increments: 2–3 micrograms/kg every 10–15 minutes.
*   **Controlled Ventilation (Analgesic Supplement):**
    *   To attenuate hypertensive response to laryngoscopy: 20–50 micrograms/kg prior to intubation.
    *   Short procedures: Initial 7–15 micrograms/kg; increments of up to 15 micrograms/kg every 10–15 minutes.
    *   Procedures >60 minutes: Continuous infusion is preferred at 0.5 to 1 microgram/kg/min.
*   **Induction Agent:** 
    *   Bolus dose: ≥120 micrograms/kg (induces hypnosis and analgesia).

### Administration Instructions
- **The 10-Minute Rule:** Alfentanil should **not** be administered in the final 10 minutes of surgery to avoid post-operative respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Dilution:** Compatible with 0.9% sodium chloride, 5.0% glucose, and Ringer Lactate injection. Infusions should commence as soon as possible after preparation (or stored at 2°C–8°C for no more than 24 hours) [Australian].
- **Premedication:** A small IV dose of an anticholinergic (e.g., atropine) may be given just before induction to prevent bradycardia [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known intolerance to alfentanil, other opioid analgesics, or any formulation components [Australian].
- Use in post-operative analgesia [Australian].
- Patients with severe or acute respiratory disease and respiratory depression [Australian].
- Use in chronic (long-term) non-cancer pain (CNCP) [Australian].
- Administration via unapproved routes [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [Australian]
- **Limitations of Use:** Only for patients where alternative treatments (non-opioid analgesics) are inadequate.
- **Hazardous and Harmful Use:** Risk of addiction, abuse, and misuse, which can lead to overdose and death.
- **Life-threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious, fatal respiratory depression may occur; monitoring is required, especially during initiation or dose titration.
- **Concomitant CNS Depressant Use:** Use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or alcohol may result in profound sedation, coma, and death.

### General Precautions
- **Muscular Rigidity:** May cause truncal skeletal muscle rigidity (incidence increases with doses >20 mcg/kg). Management includes slow injection, benzodiazepine premedication, or muscle relaxants [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Bradycardia and hypotension may occur, particularly in hypovolaemic patients [Australian].
- **Cerebral Compliance:** Avoid rapid bolus injections in patients with compromised intracerebral compliance due to risks of transient decreases in mean arterial pressure and cerebral perfusion [Australian].
- **Endocrine Effects:** Long-term use may lead to adrenal insufficiency and androgen deficiency (e.g., low libido, erectile dysfunction, infertility) [Australian].
- **Sleep-Related Breathing Disorders:** Opioids can cause central sleep apnoea (CSA) in a dose-dependent fashion [Australian].
- **Hepatobiliary/Gastrointestinal:** May cause spasm of the sphincter of Oddi and increase the risk of pancreatitis [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Table [Australian]
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (17%), Vomiting (14%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Apnoea (8.6%), Movement disorder (7.9%), Bradycardia (5.4%), Hypotension (4.1%), Muscle rigidity (3.1%), Dizziness (2.4%), Hypertension (2.2%), Fatigue (2.0%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Agitation, Headache, Somnolence, Arrhythmia, Bronchospasm, Pruritus, Postoperative confusion |
| **Very Rare (<0.1%)** | Cardiac arrest, Respiratory arrest, Convulsion, Loss of consciousness, Miosis |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Central sleep apnoea, Pancreatitis, Spasm of sphincter of Oddi, Adrenal insufficiency |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Respiratory:** Life-threatening respiratory depression and arrest.
- **Neurological:** Toxic leukoencephalopathy has been observed with opioid overdose [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Barbiturates, benzodiazepines, antipsychotics, gabapentinoids, cannabis, and alcohol markedly potentiate respiratory and cardiovascular depression [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Co-administration with SSRIs, SNRIs, or MAOIs increases the risk of potentially life-threatening serotonin syndrome [Australian].
- **MAOIs:** Use is contraindicated within 2 weeks of receiving irreversible MAO inhibitors [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Alfentanil is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4. Inhibitors (e.g., fluconazole, voriconazole, erythromycin, diltiazem, cimetidine, ritonavir) may decrease clearance, increasing the risk of prolonged respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Effect on Other Drugs:** Alfentanil increases blood concentrations of **propofol** by approximately 17% [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Alfentanil crosses the placenta and may cause respiratory depression in the newborn. It is not recommended during childbirth (including caesarean section).
- **Breastfeeding:** Alfentanil may be excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding or using expressed milk is not recommended for 24 hours following administration [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available; animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed embryocidal effects at high doses (1.25 mg/kg) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy data for children under 12 years are not available [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Reduce initial dose by approximately one-third in patients >65 years due to reduced clearance [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose with caution; increased plasma free fraction may prolong clinical effects [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dose reduction required in cirrhotic patients due to reduced clearance and decreased protein binding [Australian].
- **Obese Patients:** Clearance is decreased; doses should be titrated based on clinical response [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent, short-acting $\mu$-opioid receptor agonist.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of action is rapid (within 1–2 minutes), significantly faster than fentanyl. Maximal analgesic and respiratory effects occur within 1–2 minutes [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Small volume of distribution (0.4–1.0 L/kg); high protein binding (92%), primarily to alpha1-acid-glycoprotein [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Hepatic via N- and O-dealkylation (primarily CYP3A4).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 90–111 minutes (range 50–150 minutes). Only 1% is excreted unchanged in the urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- The safety of alfentanil was evaluated in 18 clinical trials involving 1,157 subjects. Alfentanil was used as an induction agent or analgesic adjuvant for various surgical durations. Key data from these trials established the frequency of common adverse reactions such as nausea, vomiting, and respiratory depression [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C. Do not freeze.
- Protect from light; keep ampoules in the outer carton until use [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 8 (Controlled Drug) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine (Rx) [Australian].
