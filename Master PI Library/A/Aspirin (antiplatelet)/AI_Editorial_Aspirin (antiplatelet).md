## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 [Not specified in available sources; inferred from clinical class].
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Standard Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Initial Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range but may be adjusted based on clinical requirements.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- There are no known contraindications listed in the available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of injection. If a severe reaction occurs, therapy must be discontinued and not restarted without a careful risk-benefit analysis and close monitoring for reoccurrence [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to starting therapy.
    - Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels.
    - Target plasma free carnitine concentrations should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients undergoing chronic hemodialysis (incidence ≥5%) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus |

**Serious/Notable Adverse Effects:**
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (reported in postmarketing experience) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and transient nausea/vomiting.
- **General:** A distinctive "body odor" has been reported with levocarnitine use [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** [Not specified by letter, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm].
    - Recommendation: Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. The risks to the child must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, Saccharomyces, Schizosaccharomyces) were negative. Long-term animal studies for carcinogenic potential have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight; refer to Section 3 for metabolic disorder dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The injection is specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) if oral forms are used in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus, ~76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, levocarnitine injection increases plasma carnitine concentrations [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg administered three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L by week 8 [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability from oral tablets and solution (corrected for endogenous levels) is approximately 15% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].
