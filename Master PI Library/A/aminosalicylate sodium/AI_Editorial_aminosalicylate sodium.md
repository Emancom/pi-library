# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial harmonisation is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN). Chemically defined as (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with genetic metabolic defects resulting in secondary deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg given as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Max Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered in clinical practice [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - **Administration Timing:** Administer as a slow 2–3 minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations and may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions can occur within minutes of intravenous administration [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **Carnitine Levels:** Monitor plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
    - **General Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and clinical condition is recommended [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine Caution:** Safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following adverse effects were reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients on hemodialysis (incidence ≥5%).

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache, Hypertension, Hypotension, Diarrhea, Vomiting, Pharyngitis, Dizziness, Cough increased, Infection, Chest pain, Fever, Flu syndrome, Injection site reaction, Pain |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, Accidental injury, Asthenia, Back pain, Tachycardia, Arrhythmia, Palpitations, Nausea, Dyspepsia, Constipation, Anemia, Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Peripheral edema, Weight decrease/increase, Depression, Insomnia, Paresthesia, Dyspnea, Rhinitis, Pruritus, Rash, Eye disorder, Taste perversion |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (post-marketing reports) [US].
- **Immune:** Anaphylaxis and severe allergic reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred with concurrent use. **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely after initiating levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound, suggesting a low potential for protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows suggest increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. **Management:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg weight; specific safety profiles for paediatric patients are consistent with those in adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Injection:** Indicated for use in ESRD patients.
    - **Oral:** Accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) may occur with high-dose oral therapy in renal impairment; safety of oral forms not established in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US]. 
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal; approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly post-dialysis), trough levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8. Efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is established, though impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population remains to be fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified in available sources.
