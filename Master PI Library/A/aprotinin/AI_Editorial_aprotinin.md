# Editorial Product Information: Aprotinin-Containing Fibrin Sealants

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized by combining regulatory data from the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA, Australia) for the product ARTISS and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA, United States) for the product TISSEEL. While both products are human-plasma-derived fibrin sealants containing synthetic aprotinin, they differ in thrombin concentrations and approved clinical applications. Where discrepancies exist between regional guidelines, the most comprehensive or conservative guidance has been provided.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Fibrin Sealant (Human) with Synthetic Aprotinin  
**Active Ingredients:**
*   **Fibrinogen (Human):** The primary clottable protein.
*   **Thrombin (Human):** An enzyme that converts fibrinogen into fibrin.
*   **Aprotinin (Synthetic):** A fibrinolysis inhibitor used to prevent premature clot degradation.
*   **Factor XIII (Human):** Cross-links fibrin to stabilize the clot.
*   **Calcium Chloride Dihydrate:** Necessary for the activation of the coagulation components.

---

## 2. Approved Indications
Fibrin sealants containing aprotinin are used as adjuncts to standard surgical techniques. Approved uses include:

*   **Hemostasis:** An adjunct to achieve bleeding control in adult and pediatric patients when conventional methods (e.g., sutures, ligatures, or cautery) are impractical or ineffective [FDA].
*   **Tissue Sealing:** Used to prevent leakage from colonic anastomoses following the reversal of temporary colostomies [FDA].
*   **Tissue Adherence:** Indicated to adhere autologous skin grafts in burn patients and tissue flaps during facial rhytidectomy (face-lift) surgery [TGA, FDA].

**Note on Formulation Differences:** Formulations with low thrombin concentrations (e.g., 4 IU/mL) are specifically indicated for tissue adherence to allow time for graft manipulation and are **not** indicated for hemostasis [TGA].

---

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Aprotinin Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to synthetic aprotinin or any other component of the sealant.
*   **Intravascular Injection:** Administration into the circulatory system or highly vascularized tissues (e.g., nasal mucosa) is strictly contraindicated. Such use may lead to life-threatening thromboembolic events or anaphylaxis [TGA, FDA].
*   **Severe Arterial/Venous Bleeding:** Not indicated for the treatment of brisk, heavy hemorrhage where the sealant would be washed away before it can set [FDA].
*   **Inadequate Spray Distance:** Spray application is contraindicated if the minimum recommended distance from the applicator tip to the tissue cannot be maintained [FDA].

---

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis
Because these products contain aprotinin and human proteins, there is a risk of severe allergic reactions. The risk is elevated in patients with previous exposure to aprotinin-containing products (including previous sealant use) within six months. Clinicians should monitor for bradycardia, hypotension, and bronchospasm. Emergency equipment must be available during administration.

### Air or Gas Embolism
Life-threatening air or gas embolism has occurred with the use of spray devices. This risk is primarily associated with using higher-than-recommended pressures or improper distances from the tissue surface. 
*   **Pressure limits:** Use only the pressure range recommended by the device manufacturer. In the absence of specific guidance, avoid pressures above 1.4-1.7 bars (20-25 psi) [TGA].
*   **Monitoring:** Monitor blood pressure, pulse, and end-tidal CO₂ during spray application.

### Viral and Prion Risk
As products derived from pooled human plasma, the risk of transmitting infectious agents (e.g., HIV, HBV, HCV, and CJD) cannot be completely eliminated. Manufacturing includes viral inactivation steps (e.g., vapor heat and solvent/detergent treatment), but these may be less effective against small non-enveloped viruses such as Parvovirus B19.

### Application Site Precautions
*   **Denaturation:** Contact with solutions containing alcohol, iodine, or heavy metals (disinfectants) will denature the proteins. Ensure the wound site is thoroughly rinsed and dried before application.
*   **Neurosurgical Safety:** Safety and effectiveness in neurosurgical procedures or in confined spaces have not been established [FDA].

---

## 5. Dosage and Administration
**For Topical Use Only. Do Not Inject.**

### Preparation
*   **Thawing:** The product must be completely thawed and warmed to 33°C–37°C before use. 
*   **Methods:** Options include sterile water baths (fastest), non-sterile water baths (in pouches), or incubators. 
*   **Post-Thawing:** Once thawed, do not refreeze or refrigerate. Thawed solutions must be used within a limited window: 48 hours if kept at room temperature (15–25°C) [FDA] or 4 hours if quick-thawed [TGA].

### Application
1.  **Dry the Site:** Use standard techniques to dry the application area. Do not use pressurized gas for drying.
2.  **Apply Thinly:** Use a cannula or spray set to apply a thin layer (approx. 2 mL per 100 cm²). Excessive thickness may impede wound healing [TGA, FDA].
3.  **Timing:** 
    *   For **adherence** (low thrombin), the surgeon has up to 60 seconds to position the graft. Hold with gentle compression for at least 3 minutes [TGA].
    *   For **hemostasis** (high thrombin), allow at least 2 minutes for full polymerization [FDA].

---

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Common:** Pruritus (itching), skin graft failure (in adherence settings), and redness.
*   **Post-Marketing/Class Effects:** Anaphylactic shock, bradycardia, tachycardia, hypotension, dyspnea, and nausea.
*   **Application-Related:** Air embolism (associated with spray application), hematoma, and seroma.
*   **Uncommon:** Dermal cysts, impaired healing, and urticaria.

---

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **No Formal Studies:** No specific drug interaction studies have been conducted.
*   **Oxidized Cellulose:** Preparations containing oxidized cellulose may reduce the efficacy of the sealant and should not be used as carrier materials [FDA].
*   **Topical Disinfectants:** Alcohol and iodine-based solutions interfere with the product's protein structure (see Warnings).

---

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy (Category B2)
Safety has not been established in human pregnancy trials. Because Parvovirus B19 is potentially serious for a fetus, use should be restricted to cases where the clinical benefit clearly outweighs the risk.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown whether the components are excreted in human milk. The potential for adverse effects on the infant should be considered alongside the mother's clinical need for the sealant.

---

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
As a topical agent, systemic absorption is negligible when used as directed. Systemic exposure to aprotinin and its metabolites is minimal; they typically have a half-life of 30 to 60 minutes and are excreted via the kidneys [FDA]. The fibrin clot is gradually resorbed through the natural processes of fibrinolysis and phagocytosis, typically within 10 to 14 days.

---

## 10. Mechanism of Action
The product replicates the final stage of the blood coagulation cascade. When the Sealer Protein and Thrombin solutions are mixed:
1.  **Fibrin Formation:** Thrombin cleaves fibrinogen into fibrin.
2.  **Cross-linking:** In the presence of Calcium and Factor XIII, fibrin monomers polymerize to form a stable, insoluble matrix.
3.  **Antifibrinolysis:** Synthetic aprotinin inhibits plasmin, preventing the premature breakdown of the clot, which ensures the sealant remains in place during the initial healing or hemostatic phase.

---

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Frozen State:** Store at -18°C (-0.4°F) or colder. Keep in the outer carton to protect from light.
*   **Thawed State:**
    *   Unopened pouches may be stored for up to 48 hours at room temperature (15°C to 25°C) [FDA].
    *   **ARTISS specific:** TGA guidelines allow storage for up to 14 days at room temperature for unopened pouches, but they must be used within 4 hours if quick-thawed in a water bath.
*   **Disposal:** Discard any unused or expired product as biohazardous waste. Avoid re-sterilization.
