# Editorial Product Information: Afatinib

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial is derived from the following Product Information (PI) source: Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document serves as a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference by healthcare professionals and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Data from jurisdictions such as the US (FDA), Europe (EMA), or Canada (Health Canada) were not provided for this synthesis and are therefore not reflected.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Afatinib (present as afatinib dimaleate).
- **Chemical Class:** Irreversible ErbB Family Blocker (protein kinase inhibitor).
- **Brand Name(s):** **GIOTRIF** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EB03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 20 mg, 30 mg, 40 mg, and 50 mg strengths [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Boehringer Ingelheim Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) - Non-squamous:** Monotherapy for adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic non-squamous NSCLC with activating Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor (EGFR) mutations. This applies to both treatment-naïve patients and those who have failed cytotoxic chemotherapy [Australian].
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) - Squamous:** Monotherapy for adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic squamous NSCLC that has progressed during or following platinum-based chemotherapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard recommended dose is 40 mg taken orally once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets must be taken on an empty stomach. 
    - Food should be avoided for at least 3 hours before and 1 hour after dosing [Australian].
    - Tablets should be swallowed whole with water. 
    - **Alternative Administration:** For patients unable to swallow whole tablets, the dose may be dispersed in approximately 100 mL of non-carbonated water (no other liquids). Stir for up to 15 minutes until particles are very small and consume immediately. Rinse the glass with another 100 mL of water and consume to ensure the full dose is taken. This dispersion may also be delivered via a gastric tube [Australian].
- **Dose Escalation:** A maximum dose of 50 mg/day may be considered for patients who tolerate the 40 mg/day starting dose (i.e., minimal rash, diarrhoea, or stomatitis) during the first cycle [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustment for Toxicity:** If adverse reactions occur (CTCAE Grade ≥3 or prolonged/intolerable Grade 2), treatment should be paused until the reaction resolves to Grade 0 or 1. Therapy may then be resumed at a reduced dose in 10 mg decrements. If a patient cannot tolerate 20 mg/day, permanent discontinuation should be considered [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered on the same day. If the next dose is scheduled within 8 hours, the missed dose must be skipped [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to afatinib or any of the product excipients [Australian].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided Australian source.
- **Diarrhoea:** Very common and may lead to dehydration and renal impairment. Proactive management with antidiarrhoeal agents (e.g., loperamide) at the first sign of loose stools is essential. Treatment may require interruption or dose reduction [Australian].
- **Skin Reactions:** Rash and acneiform dermatitis are common. Sun protection and early use of emollients/antibiotics are recommended. Severe bullous or exfoliative conditions (including rare cases of SJS/TEN) require treatment interruption or discontinuation [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Reported in 0.7% of patients. Therapy must be interrupted for new or worsening respiratory symptoms (dyspnoea, cough, fever) pending evaluation. If ILD is confirmed, afatinib must be permanently discontinued [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Periodic liver function testing is required, especially in patients with pre-existing liver disease. Severe hepatic impairment developed during treatment requires discontinuation [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Reported in 0.2% of trials. Use with caution in patients taking concomitant corticosteroids or NSAIDs. Permanent discontinuation is required if perforation occurs [Australian].
- **Keratitis:** Patients with acute eye inflammation or blurred vision should be referred to an ophthalmologist. Treatment should be paused or stopped if ulcerative keratitis is diagnosed [Australian].
- **Left Ventricular Function:** Left ventricular dysfunction has been linked to HER2 inhibition. Consider LVEF monitoring in patients with cardiac risk factors [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Tablets contain lactose; patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption should not take this medication [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency below based on clinical trial data (LUX-Lung 3 and LUX-Lung 6) [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea (up to 96%), Rash/Acne (up to 89%), Stomatitis, Paronychia, Decreased appetite, Epistaxis, Pruritus, Nausea, Vomiting, Dry skin. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dehydration, Hypokalaemia, Dysgeusia, Conjunctivitis, Dry eye, Rhinorrhoea, Dyspepsia, Cheilitis, Elevated ALT/AST, Nail disorders, Palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia syndrome, Muscle spasms, Renal impairment/failure, Pyrexia, Weight loss. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Keratitis, Interstitial Lung Disease, Pancreatitis, Gastrointestinal perforation. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Stevens-Johnson syndrome, Toxic epidermal necrolysis. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** ILD, severe diarrhoea leading to renal failure, GI perforation, and hepatic failure have resulted in fatal outcomes in rare instances [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Interactions:** 
    - **Inhibitors:** Strong P-gp inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir, cyclosporin, ketoconazole, amiodarone) may increase afatinib exposure. They should be administered simultaneously with or after afatinib to minimise the effect [Australian].
    - **Inducers:** Strong P-gp inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, St. John’s Wort) may significantly decrease afatinib exposure [Australian].
- **Breast Cancer Resistance Protein (BCRP):** Afatinib may increase the bioavailability of BCRP substrates (e.g., rosuvastatin, sulfasalazine). Use with caution [Australian].
- **CYP450 Enzymes:** Afatinib is not a significant inhibitor or inducer of CYP450 enzymes; drug-drug interactions via this pathway are unlikely [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C.** 
    - Afatinib may cause foetal harm. Animal studies showed embryotoxic effects at maternally toxic doses. 
    - Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 2 weeks following the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is likely based on animal data. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no significant impact on fertility, but effects on reproductive organs were noted at higher doses; an effect on human fertility cannot be excluded [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established. Not recommended for children or adolescents [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment required. However, patients ≥70 years of age may experience a higher frequency of Grade 3 adverse events [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild or Moderate (eGFR 30-89 mL/min/1.73m²):** No starting dose adjustment needed.
    - **Severe (eGFR 15-29 mL/min/1.73m²):** Reduce starting dose to 30 mg once daily.
    - **ESRD (eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73m² or dialysis):** Not studied; no dosing recommendation provided [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild or Moderate (Child-Pugh A or B):** No starting dose adjustment needed.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not studied; treatment in this population is not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Afatinib is a potent, irreversible blocker of the ErbB family. It covalently binds to and inhibits signalling from EGFR (ErbB1), HER2 (ErbB2), ErbB3, and ErbB4 [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is 2–5 hours. Bioavailability is reduced by high-fat meals (50% decrease in $C_{max}$) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is ~95%.
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal enzyme-catalysed metabolism. Covalent adducts to proteins are the major circulating metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (85.4%); renal excretion is minimal (4.3%). The terminal half-life is approximately 37 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **LUX-Lung 3:** A phase III trial in EGFR mutation-positive NSCLC patients comparing afatinib to pemetrexed/cisplatin. Afatinib significantly improved median progression-free survival (PFS) (11.1 vs 6.9 months; HR 0.58). In patients with common mutations (Del19/L858R), the PFS was 13.6 months [Australian].
- **LUX-Lung 8:** A phase III trial in squamous NSCLC comparing afatinib to erlotinib. Afatinib showed superior PFS (2.4 vs 1.9 months; HR 0.81) and overall survival (OS) (7.9 vs 6.8 months; HR 0.81) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Keep in the original package to protect from moisture and light [Australian].
