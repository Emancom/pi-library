# Editorial Product Information: Acamprosate

## Editorial Note
*   This editorial document has been developed using official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of acamprosate for healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Acamprosate calcium (a structural analogue of GABA and taurine).
*   **Chemical Class:** Synthetic compound; 3-(Acetylamino)-1-propanesulfonic acid calcium salt (2:1).
*   **Brand Names:** Campral (original brand); various generic versions available [US].
*   **Dosage Form:** Enteric-coated, delayed-release tablets.
*   **Strengths:** 333 mg acamprosate calcium (equivalent to 300 mg of acamprosate) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mylan Pharmaceuticals; NCS HealthCare of KY (Vangard Labs) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Maintenance of Alcohol Abstinence:** Indicated for the maintenance of abstinence from alcohol in patients with alcohol dependence who are already abstinent at the initiation of treatment [US-approved].
*   **Comprehensive Treatment:** Acamprosate should be utilized as one component of a broader management strategy that includes psychosocial support [US-approved].
*   **Limitations of Use:** Efficacy has not been demonstrated in patients who have not completed detoxification or achieved initial abstinence. Efficacy in polysubstance abusers has not been adequately established [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*   **Standard Adult Dose:** 666 mg (two 333 mg tablets) administered three times daily [US].
*   **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole and not crushed or chewed. While the drug can be taken without regard to food, administering with meals may improve adherence in patients who eat three meals daily [US].
*   **Treatment Initiation:** Therapy should begin as soon as possible following the alcohol withdrawal period once abstinence is achieved.
*   **Duration of Therapy:** Treatment should be maintained even if the patient experiences a relapse [US].
*   **Missed Doses:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to acamprosate calcium or any components of the formulation [US].
*   **Severe Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with severe renal impairment, defined as a creatinine clearance (CrCl) of ≤ 30 mL/min [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Renal Impairment:** Dosing must be adjusted for moderate impairment and is contraindicated in severe cases (see Section 9) [US].
*   **Suicidality and Depression:** In controlled trials, adverse events of a suicidal nature (ideation, attempts, completed suicide) were infrequent but occurred more often in acamprosate-treated patients (1.4% to 2.4%) compared to placebo (0.5% to 0.8%). Patients should be monitored for new or worsening depression or suicidal thinking [US].
*   **Alcohol Withdrawal:** Acamprosate is not intended to treat or prevent the symptoms of acute alcohol withdrawal [US].
*   **Sulfite Sensitivity:** The formulation contains sodium sulfite, which may cause allergic-type or asthmatic reactions in susceptible individuals [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported at a rate of ≥3% and higher than placebo in clinical trials include:

| Body System | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea (10–17%), Nausea, Flatulence, Anorexia, Dry mouth |
| **Nervous System** | Insomnia, Anxiety, Depression, Dizziness, Paresthesia |
| **General** | Asthenia, Pain, Accidental injury |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, Sweating |

*   **Discontinuation Rates:** Approximately 7–8% of patients discontinued treatment due to adverse events. Diarrhea was the most common cause of discontinuation (2% of patients) [US].
*   **Postmarketing Experience:** Rare reports of acute kidney failure have been documented [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Alcohol:** Acamprosate does not affect the pharmacokinetics of ethanol, nor does it cause a disulfiram-like reaction [US].
*   **Naltrexone:** Co-administration leads to a 33% increase in acamprosate peak plasma concentration ($C_{max}$) and a 25% increase in AUC. However, no dosage adjustment is recommended [US].
*   **Other Medications:** No clinically significant interactions were observed with diazepam, disulfiram, imipramine, or desipramine [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Acamprosate does not inhibit or induce CYP450 isoenzymes (1A2, 3A4, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category C:** Animal studies (rats/rabbits) have shown teratogenic effects (hydronephrosis, retinal dysplasia, malformed iris) at doses approximately equal to or 3 times the maximum recommended human daily dose (MRHD) on a $mg/m^2$ basis.
    *   **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** In animal models, acamprosate is excreted in milk (milk-to-blood ratio of 1.3:1). Use with caution in nursing women, as human excretion data is unavailable [US].
*   **Fertility:** No effects on fertility were observed in animal studies at doses up to 4–5 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Higher risk of toxicity due to age-related decline in renal function. Renal monitoring and cautious dose selection are recommended [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:**
    *   **Moderate (CrCl 30–50 mL/min):** Reduce dose to 333 mg three times daily [US].
    *   **Severe (CrCl ≤ 30 mL/min):** Contraindicated [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with mild to moderate (Child-Pugh A and B) hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Not fully elucidated. It is hypothesized to interact with glutamate and GABA neurotransmitter systems to restore the balance between neuronal excitation and inhibition that is disrupted by chronic alcohol exposure [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Bioavailability is low (approximately 11%). Steady-state is reached within 5 days. Food decreases absorption ($C_{max}$ by 42%), but this is not considered clinically significant [US].
    *   **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 72–109 L (1 L/kg). Plasma protein binding is negligible [US].
    *   **Metabolism:** Acamprosate does not undergo metabolism [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily excreted unchanged via the kidneys. Terminal half-life ranges from 20 to 33 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Pivotal Trials:** Efficacy was supported by three double-blind, placebo-controlled trials involving 998 patients. Acamprosate was superior to placebo in maintaining continuous abstinence throughout the study periods (90 to 360 days) [US].
*   **Failed Studies:** A fourth study involving polysubstance users and patients who had not undergone prior detoxification failed to show superiority over placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
*   **Handling:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers with child-resistant closures [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only).
