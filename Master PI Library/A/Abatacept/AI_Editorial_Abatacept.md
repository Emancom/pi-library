# Editorial Product Information: Abatacept

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **Abatacept**
- Chemical class: Recombinant fusion protein (CTLA-4–immunoglobulin fusion protein) produced by recombinant DNA technology
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous (IV) infusion; Subcutaneous (SC) injection

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Moderate to severe active rheumatoid arthritis (RA) in adults with inadequate response or intolerance to other DMARDs (e.g., methotrexate or TNF blockers); used in combination with methotrexate for reduction in progression of joint damage and improvement in physical function.
- Severe, active and progressive RA in adults not previously treated with methotrexate (in combination with methotrexate).
- Reduction of signs and symptoms in paediatric patients (6 years of age and older) with moderately to severely active polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA) who have had an inadequate response to one or more DMARDs; may be used as monotherapy or with methotrexate.
  - Note: Subcutaneous formulation use in children is not recommended due to lack of clinical trial data. [Australian]
- Active psoriatic arthritis (PsA) in adults when response to previous DMARD therapy has been inadequate; may be used with or without non‑biologic DMARDs.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General principles
- IV infusions are administered over 30 minutes using weight‑range dosing for adults (see table below).
- SC injections: 125 mg once weekly for adults (see indication‑specific notes).
- Concomitant non‑biologic DMARDs (including methotrexate), corticosteroids, NSAIDs, salicylates, or analgesics may be continued during therapy.
- Do not administer concurrently with other biological DMARDs (e.g., TNF inhibitors, rituximab, anakinra). Concurrent use with TNF blockers is not recommended due to increased infections.

### Adult IV dosing (RA and PsA)
- Administer as a 30‑minute IV infusion using weight‑range dosing:
  - < 60 kg: 500 mg (2 × 250 mg vials)
  - 60–100 kg: 750 mg (3 × 250 mg vials)
  - > 100 kg: 1000 mg (4 × 250 mg vials)
- Schedule: initial infusion, repeat at 2 and 4 weeks after the first infusion, then every 4 weeks thereafter.

### Adult SC dosing
- Rheumatoid arthritis: 125 mg SC once weekly, regardless of body weight. May be initiated with or without an IV loading dose. If an IV loading dose is given, administer the first 125 mg SC dose within one day of the IV infusion.
- Psoriatic arthritis: 125 mg SC once weekly without an IV loading dose.

### Paediatric dosing (Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis; 6–17 years)
- Patients < 75 kg: 10 mg/kg IV based on body weight at each administration. Administer as a 30‑minute IV infusion. Schedule: initial infusion, then at 2 and 4 weeks, then every 4 weeks thereafter.
- Patients ≥ 75 kg: follow adult dosing regimen not to exceed 1000 mg per infusion.
- SC formulation: no clinical trial data in children; use in children is not recommended. [Australian]

### Special populations
- Elderly: No routine dose adjustment required. Observe for higher background rates of infection and malignancy in elderly patients.
- Renal impairment: Not studied — no dose recommendations can be made.
- Hepatic impairment: Not studied — no dose recommendations can be made.

### Administration instructions — IV (summary)
- Reconstitute and dilute per product instructions (aseptic technique). Inspect solution visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Final infusion administered over 30 minutes using a sterile, low‑protein‑binding filter (specified pore size in product instructions). Do not co‑administer other agents in the same IV line.
- Each vial is single‑use; discard any residue.

### Administration instructions — SC (summary)
- SC injections may be self‑administered after appropriate training and initial administration under medical supervision.
- Rotate injection sites; do not inject into tender, bruised, erythematous, or indurated skin.
- Pre‑use inspection for particulates and discoloration is required.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to abatacept or any of its components.
- Active severe infections such as sepsis, abscesses, active tuberculosis, or opportunistic infections.
- Concurrent administration with other biological disease‑modifying antirheumatic drugs (e.g., TNF inhibitors, rituximab, anakinra) is contraindicated/not recommended because of increased risk of infection.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed/major warnings
- **Risk of serious infections:** Serious and sometimes fatal infections (including sepsis and pneumonia) have occurred in patients receiving abatacept; discontinue abatacept if a patient develops a serious infection.
- **Hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis:** Anaphylaxis and serious allergic reactions, including fatal cases, have been reported; stop administration immediately if anaphylaxis occurs and permanently discontinue therapy.

### General warnings and precautions
- Infections
  - Exercise caution in patients with a history of recurrent infections, underlying conditions predisposing to infection, or chronic/latent/localised infections.
  - Screen for tuberculosis prior to initiation; patients with a positive screen should receive standard tuberculosis treatment prior to starting abatacept. Safety in patients with a positive TB screen has not been established.
  - Screen for viral hepatitis in accordance with published guidelines before starting therapy because of reported hepatitis B reactivation with anti‑rheumatic therapies.
- Concomitant biologic therapy
  - Concomitant use with TNF blockers increased overall and serious infection rates in clinical trials (see Section 7). Concurrent use with other biologic RA therapies (e.g., anakinra, rituximab) is not recommended due to insufficient safety data.
- Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML)
  - Cases of PML have been reported in patients receiving abatacept, mostly with concomitant immunosuppressants. Consider PML in the differential diagnosis for new or worsening neurological, psychiatric, or cognitive symptoms; if suspected, discontinue abatacept and initiate appropriate diagnostic evaluation.
- Malignancy
  - The potential role of abatacept in malignancy risk (including lymphoma) is unknown. In placebo‑controlled trials, malignancy frequencies were 1.2% for abatacept vs 0.9% for placebo in RA patients. Animal carcinogenicity studies demonstrated increases in lymphomas and mammary tumours in mice; clinical relevance unknown. Periodic skin examinations are recommended, particularly for patients at increased risk for skin cancer.
- Immunisations
  - Do not administer live vaccines concurrently with abatacept or within 3 months after discontinuation. Non‑live vaccines may be given; abatacept may blunt immunologic responses to some vaccines (see Section 7 — immunogenicity/vaccine response).
- Autoimmunity
  - A theoretical risk exists for exacerbation of autoimmune conditions (e.g., multiple sclerosis). In clinical trials, there was no increased autoantibody formation (e.g., ANA, anti‑dsDNA) versus placebo.
- Respiratory disease
  - Patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) treated with abatacept had increased frequencies of adverse events such as COPD exacerbations, cough, rhonchi, and dyspnoea. Use with caution in RA patients with COPD and monitor respiratory status.
- Sodium content
  - Product contains measurable sodium; consider in patients on a controlled sodium diet.
- Blood glucose monitoring interference
  - IV formulation contains maltose; maltose can interfere with certain blood glucose monitoring systems that use glucose dehydrogenase pyrroloquinolinequinone (GDH‑PQQ) test strips and produce falsely elevated glucose readings. Use alternative glucose monitoring systems or laboratory glucose assays not affected by maltose.
- Injection/infusion site and technique
  - The first SC dose should be administered under medical supervision. Train patients/caregivers on proper injection technique and maintain medical follow‑up.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic evaluation for infections (including TB/hepatitis as indicated).
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of infusion reactions during and after administration.
- Monitor for new neurological, psychiatric, or cognitive symptoms suggestive of PML.
- Consider periodic skin examination for malignancy surveillance.
- Monitor elderly patients closely for infection and malignancy.
- For patients receiving IV formulation, ensure correct reconstitution and inspect solution prior to dilution/administration.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concurrent use with TNF inhibitors: associated with increased overall infections (24% vs 19%) and increased serious infections (2.2% vs 0.8%) in placebo‑controlled clinical trials. Concurrent therapy with TNF blockers is not recommended.
- Concomitant use with other biologic RA therapies (e.g., anakinra, rituximab): insufficient experience; concurrent use not recommended.
- Concomitant use of immunosuppressive therapies may increase risk of infections and PML.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Not specified in available sources.

### Other interactions and admixture notes
- Do not infuse abatacept concomitantly in the same IV line with other agents; no co‑administration compatibility data available.
- Maltose in IV formulation interferes with blood glucose monitoring systems that use GDH‑PQQ test strips, producing falsely elevated glucose readings. Use alternative glucose monitoring methods.

### Management recommendations
- Avoid concurrent biological DMARDs.
- Screen for latent infections and viral hepatitis prior to initiation.
- Monitor for signs of infection when abatacept is used with other immunosuppressive therapies.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy category/system (regional categories): Not specified in available sources.
- Clinical data on use in human pregnancy: Not specified in available sources.
- Non‑clinical reproductive and developmental data:
  - Juvenile animal studies: Rats exposed to abatacept showed immune system abnormalities including increased susceptibility to infections leading to death in juvenile rats, and inflammation of thyroid and pancreas in juvenile and adult rats. Relevance to humans, particularly children ≥6 years, is unknown.
  - Carcinogenicity studies: In mice, increases in lymphomas and mammary tumours were observed; clinical significance for humans is unknown.
- Breastfeeding/safety: Not specified in available sources.
- Fertility data: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Indicated for polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis in children aged 6 years and older who have had an inadequate response to one or more DMARDs. Dose: 10 mg/kg IV for patients < 75 kg; patients ≥ 75 kg follow adult dosing up to 1000 mg IV.
- SC formulation: use in children is not recommended due to lack of clinical trial data. [Australian]
- Long‑term effects on growth, development, immune maturation in children are unknown.

### Elderly use
- No dose adjustment required solely on the basis of age.
- Clinical trial data: similar efficacy observed in patients ≥ 65 years, but higher frequencies of serious infection and malignancy were observed among abatacept‑treated patients > 65 compared with younger patients; exercise caution in elderly patients.

### Renal impairment
- Not studied; no dose recommendations available.

### Hepatic impairment
- Not studied; no dose recommendations available.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal and controlled trials demonstrated clinical benefit in adult RA: abatacept in combination with methotrexate reduced progression of joint damage and improved physical function compared with controls (specific trial names, designs, and numerical efficacy outcomes not detailed in available sources).
- In placebo‑controlled adult RA trials (combined dataset cited): 2,653 abatacept‑treated patients and 1,485 placebo patients were evaluated; malignancy frequencies were 1.2% (abatacept) vs 0.9% (placebo).
- Trials that included concomitant IV abatacept plus TNF blockers reported higher rates of infections (24%) and serious infections (2.2%) compared with TNF blocker therapy alone (19% and 0.8%, respectively).
- Vaccine response studies:
  - Pneumococcal polysaccharide (23‑valent) vaccine in healthy subjects suggested abatacept may blunt quantitative antibody response but did not fully inhibit clinically significant responses.
  - In RA patients vaccinated with pneumococcal 23‑valent vaccine, a majority (62/112) of abatacept‑treated patients mounted at least a 2‑fold increase in antibody titres.
  - In RA patients given seasonal trivalent influenza vaccine, 73/119 abatacept‑treated patients without protective baseline antibody levels achieved at least a 4‑fold rise in antibody titres.
  - A small study in healthy volunteers showed reduced quantitative response to tetanus toxoid, but the proportion achieving a 2‑fold rise was not altered.
- Other trial details (sample sizes, primary endpoints, statistical results, and additional safety data) are not specified in available sources.
