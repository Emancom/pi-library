# Editorial Product Information: Acetohydroxamic Acid

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of acetohydroxamic acid (AHA) for healthcare professionals, synthesised from official Product Information (PI) sources. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is intended as a clinical reference and does not replace the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this report, only the United States (FDA) documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Acetohydroxamic acid (AHA).
- **Chemical Class:** Urease inhibitor; synthetic compound derived from hydroxylamine and ethyl acetate.
- **Brand Names:** **Lithostat** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 250 mg oral tablets [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mission Pharmacal Company [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Urea-Splitting Urinary Infection:** Indicated as adjunctive therapy to reduce urinary ammonia and alkalinity. It is intended for use in patients with chronic infections that are recalcitrant to other medical or surgical management [US].
- **Clinical Limitation:** AHA should not be used as a substitute for curative surgical treatment (for urolithiasis) or appropriate antimicrobial therapy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended dose range is 10 to 15 mg/kg/day. 
- **Standard Regimen:** The typical starting dose is 12 mg/kg/day, administered in divided doses every 6 to 8 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** Regardless of body weight, the maximum daily dose is 1.5 grams [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** AHA should be taken on an empty stomach to facilitate absorption [US].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Long-term treatment may be necessary as long as the urea-splitting infection persists. Clinical experience currently extends to 7 years [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Renal Impairment:** Patients with a serum creatinine >2.5 mg/dL or a creatinine clearance <20 mL/min [US].
- **Pregnancy:** Use is strictly contraindicated due to potential fetal harm [US].
- **Contraception:** Female patients who are not using a satisfactory method of contraception [US].
- **Disease Status:** Patients whose condition is amenable to definitive surgery and appropriate antimicrobial therapy [US].
- **Non-Urease Infections:** Patients whose urine is infected by organisms that do not produce urease, or infections that can be controlled by culture-specific oral antimicrobials [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning:** None identified in provided sources.
- **Hematologic Effects (Hemolytic Anemia):** Coombs-negative hemolytic anemia has occurred. In approximately 15% of patients, laboratory evidence of anemia is present, often accompanied by mild reticulocytosis (5–6%). These effects are usually reversible upon discontinuation [US].
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** While seen in animal studies (leukopenia, anemia, thrombocytopenia), it has not been confirmed in humans; however, clinical monitoring is required [US].
- **Carcinogenesis Potential:** AHA is a known inhibitor of DNA synthesis and is cytotoxic. Its metabolite, acetamide, has been shown to cause hepatocellular carcinoma in rats at high doses. AHA is considered a potential human carcinogen [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **Hematology:** Complete blood count (CBC) including reticulocyte count is recommended after two weeks of treatment and every three months thereafter for the duration of therapy [US].
    - **Liver Function:** While not yet reported for AHA, close monitoring of liver function is recommended based on data from chemical derivatives [US].
    - **Renal Function:** Close monitoring is required in patients with existing renal insufficiency [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions occur in approximately 30% of patients. Risk is highest during the first year of treatment [US].

| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Effect Details |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Neurological** | Very Common (>10%) | Headache (approx. 30%), typically occurring in the first 48 hours; usually mild and responsive to salicylates [US]. |
| **Psychiatric** | Common (1–10%) | Depression, anxiety, nervousness, and tremulousness (approx. 20% total, but only 6% are severe) [US]. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Very Common (>10%) | Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, and malaise (20–25%) [US]. |
| **Hematological** | Very Common (>10%) | Laboratory findings of hemolytic anemia (15%) [US]. |
| **Dermatological** | Not specified | Nonpruritic macular rash (especially when taken with alcohol); Alopecia [US]. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Uncommon (<1%) | Palpitations; Superficial phlebitis (reported in early trials) [US]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary emboli (rarely reported in early trials), and severe hemolytic reactions requiring hospitalization [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Consumption of alcoholic beverages in association with AHA has resulted in a transient, nonpruritic macular rash appearing within 30–45 minutes and resolving spontaneously within 60 minutes [US].
- **Iron:** AHA chelates heavy metals, particularly iron. Concomitant oral administration reduces the absorption of both AHA and iron. If iron supplementation is required, intramuscular administration is preferred [US].
- **General Caution:** AHA should be used with caution when administered with other therapeutic agents until wider clinical experience is obtained [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Contraindicated.**
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered in text, but described as causing fetal harm. Teratogenic effects (exencephaly, encephalocele, and limb skeletal defects) were observed in rats at doses ≥750 mg/kg [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if AHA is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential for impairment, as AHA alters genetic material in tissue cultures [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Pediatric Use:** Initial dose of 10 mg/kg/day is recommended. Detailed dosage studies are limited, but children have tolerated this dose for up to one year. Mandatory close monitoring is required [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Moderate:** Dose reduction is required. If serum creatinine is >1.8 mg/dL, the maximum dose is 1.0 g/day, administered at 12-hour intervals [US].
    - **Severe:** If serum creatinine is >2.5 mg/dL, AHA is contraindicated due to the high risk of toxic accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dosage adjustments provided, but close monitoring of liver function is recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** AHA is a reversible inhibitor of the bacterial enzyme urease. By preventing the hydrolysis of urea, it reduces the production of ammonia and lowers urinary pH in the presence of urea-splitting organisms [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** AHA does not acidify urine directly or possess direct antibacterial activity. Its benefit is derived from enhancing the effectiveness of concurrent antibiotics by creating a less alkaline environment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and well absorbed from the GI tract; peak blood levels occur within 0.25 to 1 hour [US].
    - **Distribution:** Distributed throughout body water with no known tissue binding [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily excreted by the kidneys. In rodents, 25% is converted to acetamide or acetate [US].
    - **Elimination:** 36% to 65% is excreted unchanged in the urine. The plasma half-life is 5–10 hours in patients with normal renal function and is prolonged in renal impairment [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Phase II Trials:** Reported instances of superficial phlebitis and embolic phenomena, though these were often in patients with pre-existing vascular disease [US].
- **Phase III Trials:** In a 3-year controlled trial, no new instances of phlebitis were developed. AHA was shown to allow successful antibiotic treatment of *Proteus* infections post-surgery and reduced stone growth rates in non-surgical candidates [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Store in a dry place. Keep the container tightly closed [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
