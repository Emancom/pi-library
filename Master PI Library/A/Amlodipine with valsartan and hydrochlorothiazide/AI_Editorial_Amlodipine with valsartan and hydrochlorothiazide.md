# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) from the **United States (FDA)**. 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonized clinical overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory data into a single reference. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic), Carnitor (Reference Brand [US]).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for intravenous injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).

### Administration Instructions
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered in severe cases, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours (q3h/q4h) and never less frequent than every 6 hours (q6h).
- **Hemodialysis:** Administer as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Parenteral Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before administration.

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** Obtain a baseline plasma carnitine concentration before therapy. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine range: 35–60 µmol/L).
- **Dose Adjustment (ESRD):** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment if levels are adequate.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of IV administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [US].
- **Seizures:** Reports indicate seizures may occur in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients are often confounded by underlying pathology. 

| Frequency | System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>5%)** | General | Chest pain, fever, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| | Cardiovascular | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| | Digestive | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain |
| | Metabolic | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| | Nervous | Dizziness, paresthesia |
| | Respiratory | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea |
| **Uncommon/Rare** | Neurologic | Seizures |
| | General | Body odor, gastritis |

*Note: Transient nausea and vomiting are frequently observed. Body odor has been reported with carnitine therapy.*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation of treatment or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labeling, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), which is applicable to pediatric patients. 
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects.
- **Renal Impairment:** IV levocarnitine is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. However, the oral formulation should be avoided in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (excluding endogenous carnitine). The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetics:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed that trough levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients demonstrated dose-proportional increases in plasma carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion.
