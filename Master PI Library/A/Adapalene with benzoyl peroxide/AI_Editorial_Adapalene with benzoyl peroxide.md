# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)** via Baxter Healthcare Corporation.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational use by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h); frequency should never be less than every 6 hours (q6h).
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Timing:** Administer as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with renal insufficiency has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Events occurring at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), chest pain (12–15%), headache (3–37%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%) [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), rhinitis (6–11%) [US].

### Other Adverse Effects
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
- **Uncommon/Rare:** Body odor, gastritis.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [Post-marketing].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound, suggesting a low potential for protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned, but follows standard "clearly needed" criteria]. Reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate/controlled human studies exist [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Animal data (dairy cows) shows increased carnitine in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is indicated for inborn errors of metabolism; dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 (ESRD dosing) and Section 5 (Precautions regarding oral metabolites) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).
- **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine. Trough levels increased from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
