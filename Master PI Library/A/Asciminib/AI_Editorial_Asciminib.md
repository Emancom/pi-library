# Editorial Product Information: Asciminib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of asciminib for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and dosing strategies into a single, coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive prescribing instructions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Asciminib hydrochloride (a BCR::ABL1 tyrosine kinase inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **Scemblix** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EA06.
- **Dosage Form and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 20 mg, 40 mg, and 100 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Scemblix** is indicated for the treatment of adult patients (≥18 years) with:
- Newly diagnosed Philadelphia chromosome-positive chronic myeloid leukaemia (Ph+ CML) in chronic phase (CP) [Australian].
- Ph+ CML in CP previously treated with two or more tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) [Australian].
- Ph+ CML in CP harbouring the T315I mutation [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Ph+ CML in CP:** The recommended total daily dose is 80 mg. This may be administered as 80 mg once daily at the same time each day, or 40 mg twice daily at approximately 12-hour intervals [Australian].
- **Ph+ CML in CP with T315I Mutation:** The recommended dose is 200 mg taken orally twice daily at approximately 12-hour intervals [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Restriction:** Asciminib must be taken on an empty stomach. Food consumption should be avoided for at least 2 hours before and 1 hour after the dose.
- **Tablets:** Must be swallowed whole; do not break, crush, or chew.

### Missed Dose
- **Once-daily regimen:** If a dose is missed by >12 hours, skip the dose and take the next scheduled dose.
- **Twice-daily regimen:** If a dose is missed by >6 hours, skip the dose and take the next scheduled dose.

### Dose Modifications for Toxicity
- **Myelosuppression:** If ANC <1 x 10⁹/L and/or PLT <50 x 10⁹/L, withhold until recovery (ANC ≥1 and PLT ≥50). Resume at the starting dose if resolved within 2 weeks; resume at a reduced dose if resolution takes >2 weeks.
- **Pancreatic Toxicity:** For asymptomatic amylase/lipase elevation >2 x ULN, withhold until resolved to <1.5 x ULN, then resume at a reduced dose. Discontinue if events recur at the lower dose.
- **Non-haematologic Toxicity:** For Grade 3 or higher reactions, withhold until Grade 1 or lower, then resume at a reduced dose.
- **Permanent Discontinuation:** Discontinue if the patient cannot tolerate 40 mg total daily (for standard CP) or 160 mg twice daily (for T315I mutation).

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to asciminib or any excipients (e.g., lactose, soybean products) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and anaemia occur frequently. Severe (Grade 3/4) reactions are reported. Monitor complete blood counts (CBC) every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then monthly or as clinically indicated.
- **Pancreatic Toxicity:** Pancreatitis occurred in 2% of patients. Asymptomatic elevation of serum lipase and amylase is common (approx. 20%). Monitor lipase and amylase monthly or if abdominal symptoms occur.
- **QT Prolongation:** Asciminib is associated with exposure-related QT prolongation. Correct hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia prior to administration. Monitor ECG as clinically indicated. Avoid use with other QT-prolonging agents, particularly at the 200 mg BID dose.
- **Hypertension:** Reported in approx. 17% of patients. Monitor and manage with standard antihypertensive therapy.
- **Hepatitis B Reactivation:** BCR::ABL1 TKIs have been associated with HBV reactivation. Test for HBV before treatment and monitor carriers closely.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Animal studies indicate potential fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before initiation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Based on a pooled safety dataset (N=556).*

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Musculoskeletal pain (34%), thrombocytopenia (28%), fatigue (25%), upper respiratory tract infection (25%), headache (23%), neutropenia (22%), arthralgia (21%), diarrhoea (21%), increased pancreatic enzymes (20%), abdominal pain (20%), nausea (17%), hypertension (17%), anaemia (13%), dyslipidaemia (14%), cough (12%), dizziness (11%), constipation (11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Pyrexia, vomiting, pruritus, dry eye, palpitations, dyspnoea, pleural effusion, non-cardiac chest pain, hypothyroidism, decreased appetite, blood CPK increased |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Febrile neutropenia, hypersensitivity, pancreatitis, QT prolongation |

- **Serious ADRs:** Pleural effusion, lower respiratory tract infection, thrombocytopenia, and pancreatitis (all >1.1%) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Increase asciminib exposure. Caution is required, especially at the 200 mg BID dose. No dose adjustment of asciminib is currently required [Australian].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** May decrease asciminib exposure. Caution is advised.
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Asciminib is a CYP3A4 inhibitor. Exercise caution with narrow therapeutic index (NTI) substrates (e.g., fentanyl, alfentanil, ergotamine).
- **CYP2C9 Substrates:** Asciminib inhibits CYP2C9. Avoid or reduce the dose of NTI substrates (e.g., warfarin, phenytoin) when using asciminib 200 mg BID. If using with warfarin, increase INR monitoring frequency.
- **Transporter Substrates:** Asciminib inhibits BCRP, P-gp, and OATP1B. Avoid co-administration with rosuvastatin (or reduce rosuvastatin dose). Exercise caution with NTI P-gp substrates (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D** [Australian]: Based on animal data, asciminib may cause fetal malformations (aortic arch stenosis, cardiac defects, cleft palate) at exposures similar to or higher than human therapeutic doses. 
    - **Contraception:** Sexually active females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for at least **3 days** after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended during treatment and for at least 3 days after the last dose due to potential serious ADRs in the infant.
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed slight effects on sperm motility/count at high exposures, but no effect on overall reproductive function.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years not established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients ≥65 years. Data are limited for patients ≥75 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe impairment. Caution is advised in severe impairment at the 200 mg BID dose.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for any degree of hepatic impairment. Caution is advised in severe impairment at the 200 mg BID dose.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Asciminib is a STAMP (Specifically Targeting the ABL Myristoyl Pocket) inhibitor. Unlike other TKIs that bind the ATP-binding site, asciminib binds the myristoyl pocket of the BCR::ABL1 protein, locking it into an inactive conformation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (Tmax 2-3 hours). Bioavailability approx. 73%.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent Vd of 111 L; 97.3% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4-mediated oxidation (36%), UGT2B7, and UGT2B17.
    - **Elimination:** Fecal (80%); renal (11%). Accumulation half-life is approx. 5.2 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ASC4FIRST Study:** A Phase III trial in newly diagnosed Ph+ CML-CP. Scemblix 80 mg QD demonstrated superior Major Molecular Response (MMR) at 48 weeks (67.7%) compared to investigator-selected TKIs (49.0%).
- **ASCEMBL Study:** A Phase III trial in patients previously treated with ≥2 TKIs. Scemblix 40 mg BID showed a significantly higher MMR at 24 weeks (25.5%) versus bosutinib (13.2%).
- **CABL001X2101 Study:** Demonstrated an MMR of 42% by 24 weeks in patients with the T315I mutation treated with 200 mg BID.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Blister packs:** Store below 25°C.
- **Bottles:** Store below 30°C.
- **General:** Store in original package to protect from moisture.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
