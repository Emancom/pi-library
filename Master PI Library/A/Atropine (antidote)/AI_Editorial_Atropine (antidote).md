# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided for the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the current official PI approved in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/generic name). 
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative. Chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Severe Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered first, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though up to 300 mg/kg has been administered [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Adjustments:** Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels is recommended.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **General Precautions:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
Based on double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in ESRD patients, the following events occurred regardless of causality [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Transient Effects:** Nausea and vomiting are common; less frequent reports include body odor and gastritis.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dosage adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified]. Animal reproductive studies (rats and rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows suggest carnitine concentrations in milk increase with supplementation. The benefits of treatment to the mother should be weighed against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as per the metabolic disorder guidelines [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used to treat carnitine deficiency in ESRD. Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if transitioning to oral formulations in patients with severe renal impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
- **Elimination:** Following intravenous bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In ESRD patients, levocarnitine clears acylCoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted. Normal plasma free carnitine concentrations are 40 to 50 µmol/L; deficiency is defined as <20 µmol/L [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Trials:** Bioavailability of oral forms (tablets and solution) is approximately 15–16% compared to intravenous administration [US].
- **ESRD Trials:** Clinical trials demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L to trough levels of ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Waste:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
