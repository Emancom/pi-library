# Editorial Product Information: Amikacin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document has been synthesized based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of amikacin for use by healthcare professionals. It aims to consolidate regulatory information into a structured, clinically relevant format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for information purposes only. It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information approved in a clinician's specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should always consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Amikacin (as sulfate), a semisynthetic aminoglycoside antibiotic derived from kanamycin.
- **Brand Names:** Amikacin Kabi [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01GB06 (Amikacin).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Solution for injection: 5 mg/mL available in 50 mL (250 mg), 100 mL (500 mg), and 200 mL (1000 mg) bottles [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only [Australian - Amikacin Kabi formulation]. Note: Other generic formulations may be suitable for intramuscular (IM) use.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Fresenius Kabi Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Serious Gram-negative Infections:** Short-term treatment of infections caused by susceptible strains of *Pseudomonas* spp., *Escherichia coli*, *Proteus* spp., *Klebsiella* spp., *Enterobacter* spp., *Serratia* spp., *Acinetobacter* spp., and *Providencia* spp. [Australian].
- **Staphylococcal Infections:** Restricted to second-line therapy for severe infections caused by susceptible *Staphylococcus aureus* (including methicillin-resistant strains) when other antibiotics are ineffective or contraindicated [Australian].
- **Specific Clinical Syndromes:** 
    - Bacteraemia and septicaemia (including neonatal sepsis) [Australian].
    - Serious infections of the respiratory tract, bones/joints, central nervous system (CNS), skin and skin structures (including burns), and intra-abdominal organs [Australian].
    - Post-operative infections and complicated/recurrent urinary tract infections (UTIs) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children)
- The standard dose is **15 mg/kg/day** administered in two or three equally divided doses [Australian].

### Neonates and Premature Infants
- **Loading Dose:** 10 mg/kg [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** 7.5 mg/kg every 12 hours [Australian].
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** Should not exceed 15 mg/kg [Australian].

### Specific Indications
- **Uncomplicated UTIs (non-pseudomonal):** 500 mg/day in two equally divided doses [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Infusion:** Administer over 30 to 60 minutes. In infants and neonates, the infusion should be extended to 1 to 2 hours [Australian].
- **Dilution:** If required (particularly for paediatric use), dilute with a compatible solution (e.g., 0.9% NaCl, 5% Glucose) to a concentration of 2.5 mg/mL (1:1 dilution) [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Peak serum concentrations should ideally be **15–30 µg/mL**, and trough concentrations should **not exceed 5–10 µg/mL**. Prolonged peak levels >30–35 µg/mL increase toxicity risk [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to amikacin or any excipients (including sulfites) [Australian].
- History of hypersensitivity or serious toxic reactions to other aminoglycosides (due to potential cross-sensitivity) [Australian].
- Patients with pre-existing subclinical renal or eighth-nerve damage from prior aminoglycoside therapy [Australian].
- **Myasthenia gravis:** Aminoglycosides may impair neuromuscular transmission [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ototoxicity:** May cause irreversible vestibular and auditory damage. Risk increases with high doses, prolonged therapy (>7–14 days), dehydration, or renal impairment. Pre-treatment and periodic audiograms are recommended [Australian].
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Potentially nephrotoxic; renal function must be monitored (BUN, creatinine, urinalysis). Adequate hydration is essential [Australian].
- **Neurotoxicity:** Manifestations include numbness, skin tingling, muscle twitching, and convulsions [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blockage:** Risk of respiratory paralysis, especially when administered with anaesthetics or neuromuscular blockers [Australian].
- **Mitochondrial Mutations:** Increased risk of ototoxicity in patients with mitochondrial DNA mutations (e.g., nucleotide 1555 A to G), even within recommended serum ranges [Australian].
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** Contains sodium metabisulfite; may cause anaphylactic or asthmatic episodes in susceptible individuals [Australian].
- **Intravitreal Use Warning:** Not formulated for intravitreal injection; macular infarction and permanent vision loss have been reported following such use [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (≥1% to <10%)
- **Ear/Labyrinth:** Hearing loss (4%), typically starting with high-tone acuity diminution [Australian].
- **Renal:** Elevated serum creatinine, elevated BUN, albuminuria, oliguria [Australian].
- **Injection Site:** Pain (reported as 6% for IM use) [Australian].

### Uncommon (0.1% to 1%) and Rare (<0.1%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting [Australian].
- **Dermatological:** Rash, pruritus, urticaria [Australian].
- **Neurological:** Tremor, paraesthesia, headache, balance disorder [Australian].
- **Metabolic:** Hypomagnesaemia [Australian].
- **Vascular:** Hypotension [Australian].

### Serious/Life-threatening
- Irreversible deafness, acute renal failure, toxic nephropathy, and respiratory paralysis due to neuromuscular blockade [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Potent Diuretics:** (e.g., furosemide, etacrynic acid) increase the risk of ototoxicity and may alter antibiotic serum concentrations [Australian].
- **Nephrotoxic/Neurotoxic Agents:** Avoid concurrent/sequential use with bacitracin, cisplatin, amphotericin B, vancomycin, or other aminoglycosides [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blockers:** Enhanced blockade effect with suxamethonium, tubocurarine, or halogenated anaesthetics [Australian].
- **Beta-lactam Antibiotics:** In vitro mixing causes mutual inactivation. If used concomitantly, administer via separate routes. Significant in vivo inactivation occurs primarily in patients with severe renal impairment [Australian].
- **Bisphosphonates:** Increased risk of hypocalcaemia [Australian].
- **Indometacin:** May increase plasma amikacin concentrations in neonates [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Aminoglycosides cross the placenta and can cause fetal harm, including total, irreversible, bilateral congenital deafness. Amikacin should only be used in pregnancy if clearly needed and under strict medical supervision [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Amikacin is excreted in breast milk. A decision must be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effects on fertility or fetal toxicity [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Caution is required in neonates and premature infants due to renal immaturity, which prolongs the half-life [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Increased risk of age-related renal decline. Dosage should be based on creatinine clearance (CrCl) rather than serum creatinine alone. Monitor renal function closely [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Mandatory dose or interval adjustment is required.
    - **Interval Adjustment:** Dosage interval (hours) = Serum creatinine (mmol/L) × 101.8 [Australian].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Reduce maintenance dose based on the ratio of observed CrCl to normal CrCl [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits protein synthesis in susceptible bacteria.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels (approx. 38 µg/mL) occur immediately after a 1-hour IV infusion of 7.5 mg/kg [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Distributed into bone, heart, gallbladder, lung tissue, bile, sputum, and pleural/synovial fluids [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (94–98% excreted unchanged via glomerular filtration within 24 hours). 
    - **Half-life:** 2–3 hours in normal renal function; 30–86 hours in severe renal impairment [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging. Use as soon as practicable after dilution (if storage is necessary, hold at 2–8°C for no more than 12 hours) [Australian].
- **Handling:** Single-use only. Discard any residue [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
