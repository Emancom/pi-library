# Editorial Product Information: Alectinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Important:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Alectinib (as hydrochloride salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Alecensa** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01ED03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Hard capsules containing 150 mg of alectinib (equivalent to 161.3 mg alectinib hydrochloride) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Adjuvant Treatment of Resected NSCLC:** Indicated as adjuvant therapy for adult patients following complete tumour resection of anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK)-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) (specifically for tumours ≥4 cm or node-positive) [Australian].
- **Treatment of Advanced/Metastatic NSCLC:** Indicated for the treatment of patients with ALK-positive, locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 600 mg (four 150 mg capsules) taken orally twice daily. 
- **Total Daily Dose:** 1200 mg.
- **Food Requirement:** Must be taken with food.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Adjuvant Treatment:** Total duration of 2 years, or until disease recurrence or unmanageable toxicity [Australian].
- **Advanced/Metastatic Treatment:** Continue until disease progression or unmanageable toxicity [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules must be swallowed whole; they should not be opened or dissolved.
- **Missed Doses:** If a dose is missed, or if vomiting occurs after administration, the patient should not take an additional dose but should take the next dose at the scheduled time.

### Titration and Dose Modifications
Management of toxicity may require interruption, reduction, or discontinuation. Reductions should occur in steps of 150 mg twice daily:
1.  **First reduction:** 450 mg twice daily.
2.  **Second reduction:** 300 mg twice daily.
3.  **Discontinuation:** Permanently discontinue if the 300 mg twice daily dose is not tolerated.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **alectinib** or any of its excipients (including lactose monohydrate and sodium lauryl sulfate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** Occurred in approximately 1.3% of trial patients. Immediately withhold treatment for suspected ILD and permanently discontinue if no other cause is identified.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Significant elevations in ALT, AST, and bilirubin have occurred, primarily within the first 3 months.
    - **Monitoring:** Liver function tests (LFTs) should be performed at baseline, every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, and periodically thereafter.
- **Bradycardia:** Symptomatic bradycardia can occur. Heart rate and blood pressure should be monitored regularly. Adjustment or discontinuation is required for symptomatic or life-threatening cases.
- **Severe Myalgia and CPK Elevation:** Very common. 
    - **Monitoring:** Assess CPK levels every fortnight for the first month and in patients reporting symptoms (muscle pain, tenderness, or weakness).
- **Haemolytic Anaemia:** Reported in post-marketing settings and clinical trials. If haemoglobin is <100 g/L and anaemia is suspected, withhold treatment and initiate testing.
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Use with caution in patients at increased risk (e.g., history of diverticulitis, metastases to the GI tract, or concomitant use of drugs with perforation risk).
- **Photosensitivity:** Patients should avoid prolonged sun exposure and use broad-spectrum SPF ≥50 sunscreen and lip balm during treatment and for 7 days after discontinuation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Constipation (34–42%), Nausea (14–22%), Diarrhoea (12–18%), Vomiting (7–13%).
- **Musculoskeletal:** Myalgia/Musculoskeletal pain (23–34%).
- **General:** Fatigue/Asthenia (44%), Oedema (16–34%).
- **Skin:** Rash (15–20%).
- **Neurological:** Dysgeusia (3–13%), Headache (20%), Dizziness (12%).
- **Cardiac:** Bradycardia (11–12%).
- **Laboratory:** Increased AST (41–53%), Increased ALT (34–40%), Increased Bilirubin (39–53%), Increased CPK (37–77%), Increased Creatinine (31–41%), Anaemia (24–60%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Pneumonitis:** Most frequent serious reaction leading to discontinuation in adjuvant settings (2.3%).
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Grade ≥3 elevations in ALT/AST and bilirubin.
- **Pulmonary Embolism and Dyspnoea:** Observed in crizotinib-pretreated populations.
- **Renal Impairment/Acute Kidney Injury:** Reported in clinical trials (2.6%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Effect of Alectinib on Other Drugs:** 
    - **P-gp and BCRP Substrates:** Alectinib and its metabolite M4 inhibit P-gp and BCRP. Monitor drugs with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran, methotrexate).
    - **CYP Substrates:** No clinically meaningful effect on CYP3A (e.g., midazolam) or CYP2C8 (e.g., repaglinide) substrates.
- **Effect of Other Drugs on Alectinib:**
    - **CYP3A Inducers/Inhibitors:** Strong inducers (rifampicin) or inhibitors (posaconazole) have a minor effect on combined alectinib/M4 exposure; no dose adjustment is required.
    - **Gastric pH-Elevating Agents:** No clinically relevant effect from proton pump inhibitors (e.g., esomeprazole); no dose adjustment required.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. May cause foetal harm. Animal studies showed embryo-foetal loss and malformations.
- **Contraception:**
    - **Females:** Use highly effective contraception during treatment and for **5 weeks** after the final dose.
    - **Males:** Use highly effective contraception during treatment and for **3 months** after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established. Advise women not to breastfeed during treatment and for **1 week** after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** No specific animal fertility studies have been performed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years of age have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients ≥65 years. However, exploratory analysis suggests a higher incidence of serious adverse events (38% vs 25%) and discontinuations compared to younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required in mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment due to negligible renal clearance.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A) or Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** No starting dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Starting dose should be reduced to **450 mg twice daily** (900 mg daily).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Alectinib is a highly selective TKI that targets ALK and RET. It inhibits ALK phosphorylation and downstream signalling pathways (STAT3 and PI3K/AKT), inducing cell death in ALK-fusion-positive cells. Its major metabolite, M4, shows similar potency.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 36.9% (fed). Peak plasma concentration reached in 4–6 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (>99%). Vss is 475 L. Crosses the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4 to the active metabolite M4.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in faeces (98%), with 84% as unchanged parent drug. Mean half-life is 32.5 hours (alectinib) and 30.7 hours (M4).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ALINA Study (Adjuvant):** A Phase III trial in resected Stage IB–IIIA ALK+ NSCLC. Alectinib demonstrated a significant improvement in Disease-Free Survival (DFS) compared to platinum-based chemotherapy (HR 0.24).
- **ALEX Study (First-line Metastatic):** A Phase III trial comparing alectinib to crizotinib. Alectinib showed superior Progression-Free Survival (PFS) (HR 0.47) and superior CNS activity (Time to CNS progression HR 0.16).
- **NP28761 & NP28673 (Crizotinib-pretreated):** Phase I/II studies showed Objective Response Rates (ORR) between 42.5% and 52.9% in patients who had progressed on crizotinib.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- Keep in the original package to protect from light and moisture.
- Shelf life is specified on the regional packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
