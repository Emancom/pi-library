# Editorial Product Information: **Aminophylline**

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **aminophylline** clinical information for use by healthcare professionals.
*   **Prescribing Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** **Aminophylline** (a 2:1 complex of theophylline and ethylenediamine).
*   **Brand Name(s):** DBL Aminophylline Injection [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** R03DA05.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Injection: 250 mg aminophylline in 10 mL (25 mg/mL), equivalent to 20.63 mg/mL of anhydrous theophylline [Australian].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV). Intramuscular (IM) administration is not recommended due to intense local pain and tissue sloughing [Australian].
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   Treatment of reversible bronchospasm associated with:
    *   Bronchial asthma [Australian].
    *   Chronic bronchitis [Australian].
    *   Emphysema [Australian].
    *   Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) [Australian].
*   Treatment of paroxysmal dyspnoea associated with left heart failure [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
Dosage must be individualised based on clinical response, patient characteristics, and steady-state serum theophylline concentrations. Doses should be calculated using lean (ideal) body weight [Australian].

### Intravenous Dosing (Adults and Children ≥6 months)
*   **Loading Dose (for patients not currently receiving theophylline):** 6 mg/kg infused over 20 to 30 minutes. This aims for a peak serum concentration of approximately 10 microgram/mL.
*   **Loading Dose (for patients already receiving theophylline):** Ideally, a serum level should be obtained first. If unavailable, a cautious dose of 3 mg/kg may be administered.
*   **Maintenance Doses (mg/kg/hour of aminophylline):**
    *   Children 6 months to 9 years: 1.2 mg/kg/hr for the first 12 hours, then 1.0 mg/kg/hr.
    *   Children 9 to 16 years and young adult smokers: 1.0 mg/kg/hr for the first 12 hours, then 0.8 mg/kg/hr.
    *   Non-smoking adults: 0.7 mg/kg/hr for the first 12 hours, then 0.5 mg/kg/hr.
    *   Older patients or those with cor pulmonale: 0.6 mg/kg/hr for the first 12 hours, then 0.3 mg/kg/hr.
    *   Patients with congestive heart failure or hepatic failure: 0.5 mg/kg/hr for the first 12 hours, then 0.1–0.2 mg/kg/hr.

### Administration Instructions
**Aminophylline** must be administered slowly. Intravenous injection should not exceed a rate of 20 to 25 mg/min [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to xanthines (e.g., theophylline) or ethylenediamine [Australian].
*   Coronary artery disease in cases where myocardial stimulation is considered harmful [Australian].
*   Active bronchiolitis (bronchopneumonia) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Theophylline Toxicity:** Infusion must be stopped immediately if signs of toxicity (e.g., repetitive vomiting, nausea) occur, even if other causes are suspected [Australian].
*   **Monitoring:** Serum theophylline concentrations must be monitored to ensure they remain within the therapeutic range (5–20 microgram/mL). Monitoring is critical when adding/removing interacting drugs or during new acute illnesses [Australian].
*   **Seizure Threshold:** **Aminophylline** may lower the seizure threshold; use with caution in patients with seizure disorders unless they are adequately controlled with anticonvulsants [Australian].
*   **Cardiovascular Risks:** Use with caution in patients with cardiac arrhythmias (excluding bradyarrhythmias), hypertension, angina pectoris, or acute myocardial injury [Australian].
*   **Other Clinical Cautions:** Use with caution in patients with active peptic ulcer, glaucoma, diabetes mellitus, or gastroesophageal reflux [Australian].
*   **Rapid Administration:** Excessive speed of IV administration can cause severe CNS and cardiovascular toxicity, including cardiac arrest [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally mild when serum levels are <20 microgram/mL.
*   **Common/Transient (<20 microgram/mL):** Nausea, vomiting, headache, insomnia, irritability, restlessness, fine skeletal muscle tremors, and transient diuresis [Australian].
*   **Serious/High Concentration (>20 microgram/mL):** Persistent vomiting, cardiac arrhythmias, and intractable seizures (which may be lethal) [Australian].
*   **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, palpitations, extrasystoles, hypotension, and circulatory failure [Australian].
*   **Dermatological:** Rare but severe allergic reactions including exfoliative dermatitis (related to ethylenediamine sensitivity) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
*   **Inhibitors of Metabolism (Increase Serum Levels):** Alcohol, allopurinol (>600 mg/day), beta-blockers (propranolol), cimetidine, fluvoxamine, oral contraceptives, diltiazem, disulfiram, methotrexate, mexiletine, verapamil, and certain antibiotics (ciprofloxacin, erythromycin, clarithromycin) [Australian].
*   **Inducers of Metabolism (Decrease Serum Levels):** Barbiturates, carbamazepine, phenytoin, rifampicin, St John's Wort, and smoking (tobacco or marijuana) [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
*   **Adenosine:** **Aminophylline** antagonises the cardiovascular effects of adenosine.
*   **Beta-agonists:** Concurrent use may increase cardiotoxicity and potentiate hypokalaemia.
*   **Lithium:** May increase lithium excretion, reducing its therapeutic effect.
*   **Benzodiazepines:** May reverse the sedative effects of benzodiazepines.
*   **Ketamine:** May lower the seizure threshold [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category A]. Pharmacokinetics may change during pregnancy; serum levels should be monitored frequently [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Theophylline is excreted into breast milk (approx. 70% of serum concentration). It may cause irritability or toxicity signs in the infant [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Clearance is highly variable. Use with extreme caution in neonates and infants <1 year due to immature metabolic pathways. Children are particularly sensitive to CNS stimulant effects [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** Patients >60 years have reduced clearance and increased sensitivity to toxicity. The maximum infusion rate should generally not exceed 17 mg/hr of theophylline (21 mg/hr **aminophylline**) [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Neonates with renal impairment require significant dose reductions as they excrete about 50% of the dose unchanged in urine (compared to 10% in adults) [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Theophylline clearance is decreased in cirrhosis, hepatitis, and cholestasis; frequent serum monitoring and dose reductions are mandatory [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** A xanthine derivative that directly relaxes bronchial smooth muscle. It is thought to act via phosphodiesterase inhibition, increasing intracellular cyclic AMP. It also stimulates the medullary respiratory centre and increases gastric secretion and cardiac contractility [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:** **Aminophylline** dissociates rapidly to theophylline in the body. Theophylline is 60% protein-bound in adults (35% in neonates) and is metabolised by the hepatic CYP450 system. Metabolism can be non-linear (capacity-limited) in some individuals [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   Not specified in available sources (Document notes "No data available") [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 25°C. 
*   Protect from light.
*   **Stability:** Do not use if crystals have separated from the solution. **Aminophylline** is incompatible with acidic media and many other drugs (e.g., adrenaline, ciprofloxacin, morphine) [Australian].
