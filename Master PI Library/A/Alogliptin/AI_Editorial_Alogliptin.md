# Editorial Product Information: Alogliptin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial product information has been synthesised from the following official source: Australian (TGA) Product Information for **Nesina**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current official PI in their respective region before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Alogliptin (as benzoate).
- **Chemical Class:** Dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Nesina** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A10BH04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 6.25 mg (light pink), 12.5 mg (yellow), and 25 mg (light red) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Takeda Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM):** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in adults (≥18 years) [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** 
    - Dual therapy as add-on to metformin, a sulphonylurea, a thiazolidinedione (TZD), or insulin (with or without metformin) [Australian].
    - Triple therapy in combination with metformin and a TZD [Australian].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not indicated for initial combination therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 25 mg taken orally once daily [Australian].
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food. Tablets must be swallowed whole with water [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy Dosing:**
    - When used with metformin or TZDs, the existing dose of those agents should be maintained [Australian].
    - When used with insulin or insulin secretagogues (e.g., sulphonylureas), a lower dose of the secretagogue or insulin may be required to mitigate the risk of hypoglycaemia [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Take as soon as remembered on the same day. A double dose must not be taken [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to alogliptin or any excipients in the formulation, including history of serious hypersensitivity reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema, Stevens-Johnson syndrome) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General:** Not for use in patients with Type 1 diabetes or for the treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis [Australian].
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** Post-marketing reports exist. Patients should be monitored for persistent severe abdominal pain. Discontinue promptly if pancreatitis is suspected [Australian].
- **Heart Failure:** Caution is advised in patients with NYHA functional class IV congestive heart failure due to limited clinical trial experience [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including angioedema and severe cutaneous adverse reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome), have been reported. Discontinue immediately if suspected [Australian].
- **Hepatic Effects:** Post-marketing reports include hepatic failure. Promptly obtain liver function tests in patients reporting symptoms of liver injury. Discontinue if an alternative etiology for liver abnormality is not found [Australian].
- **Arthralgia:** Severe and disabling joint pain has been reported with DPP-4 inhibitors. Onset varies from one day to years after initiation [Australian].
- **Bullous Pemphigoid:** Cases requiring hospitalisation have occurred. Advise patients to report blisters or erosions; discontinue treatment if suspected [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is based on clinical trials involving over 9,000 patients. The most frequent adverse reaction reported with the 25 mg dose was headache [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infections** | Nasopharyngitis, Upper Respiratory Tract Infection | Common |
| **Nervous System** | Headache | Common |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Abdominal pain, GERD | Common |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | Pruritus, Rash | Common |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Acute pancreatitis, hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis, angioedema), Stevens-Johnson syndrome, and tubulointerstitial nephritis (TIN) [Australian].
- **Hypersemia:** Risk is neutral when used as monotherapy or with metformin/TZDs, but increased when combined with insulin or sulphonylureas [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Alogliptin is primarily excreted renally. Metabolism via Cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes is negligible [Australian].
- **In Vitro Data:** Alogliptin is not an inducer or inhibitor of major CYP enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C9, 2C19, 3A4, 2D6) or key renal transporters (OAT1, OAT3, OCT2) [Australian].
- **Clinically Studied Interactions:** No dose adjustments are required when co-administered with:
    - Metformin, Glibenclamide (Sulphonylurea), Pioglitazone (TZD).
    - Digoxin, Warfarin, Atorvastatin.
    - Cimetidine, Ciclosporine, Voglibose.
    - Oral contraceptives [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** Animal studies have not shown evidence of increased fetal damage. Human data is absent; as a precaution, use should be avoided during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Alogliptin is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk. A risk to the infant cannot be excluded; a decision must be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility or early embryonic development were observed in animal models (rats) at doses up to 170 times the human clinical exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** No dose adjustment is required based on age alone, though caution is advised due to the higher prevalence of decreased renal function in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose adjustments are mandatory based on renal function:
    - **Mild (CrCl >50 to ≤80 mL/min):** 25 mg once daily (no adjustment) [Australian].
    - **Moderate (CrCl ≥30 to ≤50 mL/min):** 12.5 mg once daily [Australian].
    - **Severe/ESRD (CrCl <30 mL/min or requiring dialysis):** 6.25 mg once daily. May be administered without regard to dialysis timing [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** No dose adjustment required [Australian].
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended due to lack of clinical data [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective and potent inhibitor of DPP-4. It slows the degradation of incretin hormones (GLP-1 and GIP), thereby increasing their postprandial concentrations. This stimulates glucose-dependent insulin secretion and suppresses glucagon secretion [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~100%. Peak plasma concentrations (Tmax) occur 1–2 hours post-dose [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 20% plasma protein bound; volume of distribution is 417 L [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Limited; two minor metabolites (M-I active; M-II inactive) account for <1% and <6% of the parent compound [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (76% in urine; 13% in faeces). Terminal half-life is ~21 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Glycaemic Efficacy:** In phase 3 trials, alogliptin 25 mg significantly reduced HbA1c compared to placebo as monotherapy and in combination with metformin, sulphonylureas, TZDs, and insulin. Reductions were generally consistent across age, gender, and BMI subgroups [Australian].
- **EXAMINE Study (Cardiovascular Outcomes):** A trial of 5,380 patients with T2DM and a recent acute coronary syndrome (ACS) event. Alogliptin did not increase the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) compared to placebo (Hazard Ratio 0.96) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
