# Editorial Product Information: Amitriptyline

## Editorial Note
- The following information is derived from the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- This document serves as a harmonised editorial synthesis of the available product information intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial overview and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Amitriptyline hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Tricyclic antidepressant (TCA); dibenzocycloheptadiene derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Amitriptyline Viatris [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06AA09.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 10 mg film-coated tablets (blue, convex) [Australian].
    - 25 mg film-coated tablets (yellow, convex) [Australian].
    - 50 mg film-coated tablets (tan, hexagonal) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd trading as Viatris [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depression:** For the treatment of major depression. Note: The 50 mg tablet is indicated solely for maintenance treatment [Australian].
- **Nocturnal Enuresis:** In cases where organic pathology has been excluded [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Depression
Dosage should be individualised, starting at a low level and increasing gradually based on clinical response and tolerability.
- **Outpatient Adults:** Initially 75 mg daily in divided doses. May be increased to 150 mg daily if required. Increments should preferably be added to the late afternoon or bedtime dose. 
- **Alternative Initiation:** 50 mg to 100 mg at bedtime, increasing by 25 mg to 50 mg as needed to a total of 150 mg daily.
- **Hospitalised Patients:** Initially 100 mg daily, increasing gradually to 200 mg daily if necessary. A maximum of 300 mg daily may be required for a small number of patients.
- **Maintenance:** Usually 50 mg to 100 mg daily, preferably as a single dose at bedtime. Therapy should continue for 3 months or longer to prevent relapse.

### Nocturnal Enuresis (Paediatric)
The recommended dose for enuresis is significantly lower than for depression and must not be exceeded.
- **Under 6 years:** 10 mg at bedtime.
- **6 to 10 years:** 10 mg to 20 mg daily.
- **11 to 16 years:** 25 mg to 50 mg at bedtime.

### Administration Instructions
- Dose adjustments should be based on clinical response rather than plasma levels [Australian].
- Abrupt cessation should be avoided; gradual reduction is recommended to prevent withdrawal symptoms (e.g., nausea, headache, malaise).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known prior hypersensitivity to amitriptyline.
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use or use within 14 days of discontinuing an MAOI (including selegiline). Risk of severe convulsions, hyperpyretic crises, and death.
- **Cisapride:** Risk of adverse cardiac interactions, including QT interval prolongation and arrhythmias.
- **Myocardial Infarction:** Use during the acute recovery phase following a myocardial infarction.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Clinical Worsening and Suicidality
- **Suicide Risk:** Depression carries an inherent risk of suicide which may persist until significant remission occurs.
- **Age-Specific Risks:** Pooled analyses show an increased risk of suicidal thinking and behaviour in children, adolescents, and young adults (ages 18–24) taking antidepressants for psychiatric disorders. 
- **Monitoring:** Patients of all ages must be monitored closely for clinical worsening, emergent suicidality, agitation, or unusual behavioural changes, especially during the first few months of therapy or following dose changes.

### Cardiovascular Effects
- Patients with cardiovascular disorders must be monitored closely. High doses have been associated with arrhythmias, sinus tachycardia, and prolonged conduction time. Myocardial infarction and stroke have been reported in this class.

### Bipolar Disorder
- Antidepressants may precipitate a mixed/manic episode in patients with bipolar disorder. Patients should be screened for bipolar risk factors prior to initiation.

### Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs)
- Life-threatening reactions, including Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS), have been reported, typically appearing within 2 to 6 weeks of initiation. Discontinue immediately if suspected [Australian].

### General Precautions
- **Seizures:** Use with caution in patients with a history of seizures.
- **Glaucoma:** Due to anticholinergic (atropine-like) activity, use with caution in narrow-angle glaucoma or increased intraocular pressure.
- **Hepatic/Renal:** Use cautiously in patients with hepatic disease or a history of urinary retention.
- **Elective Surgery:** Discontinue several days before surgery if possible.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Pharmacological similarities among TCAs mean the following effects should be considered even if not specifically reported for amitriptyline.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Tachycardia, palpitations, orthostatic hypotension, somnolence, tremors, dizziness, headache, dysarthria, hyperhidrosis, dry mouth, constipation, nausea, disturbance of accommodation, weight gain, aggression. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | AV block, bundle branch block, disturbance in attention, dysgeusia, paraesthesia, ataxia, confusional states, agitation, libido decrease, congested nose, micturition disorders, erectile dysfunction, fatigue, thirst, oedema, hyponatremia, ECG changes (QT/QRS prolongation). |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypertension, convulsions, hypomania, mania, anxiety, insomnia, nightmares, rash, urticaria, pruritus, facial/tongue oedema, diarrhoea, vomiting, hepatic impairment, tinnitus, urinary retention, galactorrhoea, increased intraocular pressure. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Arrhythmias, decreased appetite, delirium (elderly), hallucinations, suicidal thoughts/behaviour, alopecia, photosensitisation, bone marrow depression (agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia), parotid swelling, paralytic ileus, jaundice, hepatitis, gynaecomastia, pyrexia. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Cardiomyopathy, torsades de pointes, akathisia, polyneuropathy, alveolitis, acute glaucoma. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Potential for lethal interaction; 14-day washout period required.
- **CNS Depressants:** Enhances response to alcohol, barbiturates, and other CNS depressants.
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Risk of **Serotonin Syndrome** when used with SSRIs or other serotonin-enhancing medicines.
- **Anticholinergics:** Risk of paralytic ileus and hyperpyrexia when combined with other anticholinergic or neuroleptic medicines.
- **Adrenaline (Epinephrine):** Close supervision required when used with local anaesthetics as TCAs may potentiate the pressor response.
- **Tramadol:** May increase seizure risk.

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors/Substrates:** Medicines that inhibit CYP2D6 (e.g., quinidine, cimetidine, SSRIs like fluoxetine/sertraline) may significantly increase amitriptyline plasma levels. Fluoxetine may necessitate a 50% dose reduction of amitriptyline [Australian].
- **Guanethidine:** Amitriptyline may block the antihypertensive action of guanethidine.
- **Sodium Valproate:** May increase amitriptyline plasma concentrations.
- **Cimetidine:** Delays elimination and increases steady-state concentrations of amitriptyline.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Not associated with an increased incidence of birth defects, but withdrawal symptoms (irritability, sleep disturbance) have been reported in newborns after prolonged maternal use. Use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Amitriptyline is excreted in breast milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse effects in infants, a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** No data available in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy for depression in children under 18 have not been established. Amitriptyline should **not** be used for paediatric depression [Australian]. See Section 3 for enuresis dosing.
- **Elderly Use:** Generally require lower dosages; 50 mg daily is often sufficient for those who do not tolerate higher doses. Increased risk of delirium and cardiac side effects.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to extensive hepatic metabolism.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution, particularly in patients with a history of urinary retention.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Amitriptyline inhibits the membrane pump mechanism responsible for the reuptake of noradrenaline and serotonin in adrenergic and serotonergic neurons. This potentiation of monoamine activity is believed to underlie its antidepressant effect. It also possesses significant sedative and anticholinergic properties.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Readily absorbed from the GI tract; peak plasma concentrations occur within approximately 6 hours.
- **Distribution:** Approximately 96% bound to plasma proteins.
- **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism via N-demethylation to its active metabolite, **nortriptyline**. Paths also include hydroxylation and N-oxidation.
- **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in the urine as metabolites (free or conjugated). 
- **Half-life:** Mean apparent elimination half-life is approximately 22.4 hours for amitriptyline and 26 hours for nortriptyline.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children, as TCA overdose is particularly toxic in the paediatric population.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (S4 - Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
