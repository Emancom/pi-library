# Editorial Product Information: **Azilsartan Medoxomil**

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidance before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Azilsartan medoxomil** (administered as **azilsartan kamedoxomil**, the potassium salt) [US].
- **Chemical Class:** Angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB); benzimidazole derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Edarbi (Reference product); generic versions available (e.g., Lupin) [US].
- **ATC Code:** C09CA09.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 40 mg and 80 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lupin Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (and others by jurisdiction) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Indicated for the treatment of hypertension in adults to lower blood pressure. Lowering blood pressure reduces the risk of fatal and nonfatal cardiovascular events, including strokes and myocardial infarctions [US].
- **Note:** May be used as monotherapy or in combination with other antihypertensive agents [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended dose is 80 mg taken once daily [US].
- **Initial Therapy in Specific Patients:** Consider a starting dose of 40 mg for patients treated with high doses of diuretics [US].
- **Administration:** May be administered with or without food [US].
- **Handling Instructions:** Do not repackage; dispense and store in the original container to protect from light and moisture [US].
- **Titration:** Most of the antihypertensive effect is observed within the first two weeks of dosing [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Dual RAS Blockade:** Do not co-administer with aliskiren-containing products in patients with diabetes [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Fetal Toxicity [US]**
    - Drugs acting on the renin-angiotensin system (RAS) can cause injury and death to the developing fetus.
    - Discontinue **azilsartan medoxomil** immediately if pregnancy is detected.
- **Hypotension in Volume- or Salt-Depleted Patients:** Symptomatic hypotension may occur, particularly in patients treated with high-dose diuretics. Volume/salt depletion should be corrected prior to administration, or treatment should start at 40 mg [US].
- **Impaired Renal Function:** 
    - Monitor for worsening renal function in susceptible patients (e.g., those with severe congestive heart failure, renal artery stenosis, or volume depletion) [US].
    - In patients with renal artery stenosis, ARBs may increase serum creatinine or blood urea nitrogen (BUN) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of renal function and electrolytes is recommended, especially when used in dual blockade of the RAS [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Treatment is generally well-tolerated with an adverse reaction incidence similar to placebo. Reactions are typically mild and not dose-related [US].

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhea (2%) [US] |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Nausea, asthenia, fatigue, muscle spasm, dizziness, postural dizziness, cough [US] |
| **Serious / Postmarketing** | Angioedema, rash, pruritus [US] |

- **Discontinuation:** The most common adverse event leading to discontinuation is hypotension/orthostatic hypotension (0.4%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Dual Blockade of the RAS:** Combining ARBs with ACE inhibitors or aliskiren increases the risk of hypotension, hyperkalemia, and renal impairment (including acute renal failure). Avoid combined use; if necessary, closely monitor blood pressure and renal function [US].
- **NSAIDs (including selective COX-2 inhibitors):** In elderly, volume-depleted, or renally impaired patients, co-administration may result in further deterioration of renal function and attenuation of the antihypertensive effect [US].
- **Lithium:** Increased serum lithium concentrations and toxicity have been reported. Monitor serum lithium levels during concomitant use [US].
- **Non-significant Interactions:** No clinically significant interactions observed with amlodipine, antacids, chlorthalidone, digoxin, fluconazole, glyburide, ketoconazole, metformin, pioglitazone, or warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified by letter, but carries a Boxed Warning for fetal toxicity. 
    - Exposure during the second and third trimesters reduces fetal renal function and increases neonatal morbidity and death (e.g., lung hypoplasia, skeletal deformations, skull hypoplasia, anuria, and renal failure) [US].
    - Perform serial ultrasound examinations if exposure occurs; observe infants for hypotension, oliguria, and hyperkalemia [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Azilsartan is excreted in the milk of lactating rats; it is unknown if it is excreted in human milk [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on male or female rat fertility at doses up to 122 times the Maximum Recommended Human Dose (MRHD) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in patients <18 years. Use is not recommended in children <2 years due to potential deleterious effects on immature kidneys [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No general dose adjustment required. Patients ≥75 years are more likely to report abnormally high serum creatinine values [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild-to-severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease. Monitor for increased serum creatinine in moderate-to-severe impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild-to-moderate impairment. Not studied in severe hepatic impairment [US].
- **Race:** The antihypertensive effect is smaller (approximately half) in Black patients when used as monotherapy [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A selective AT₁ subtype angiotensin II receptor blocker. It inhibits the pressor effects of angiotensin II by blocking its binding in tissues such as vascular smooth muscle and the adrenal gland [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Inhibits the pressor effect of angiotensin II infusion in a dose-related manner. Does not affect bradykinin levels (unlike ACE inhibitors) [US]. No evidence of QT interval prolongation at doses up to 320 mg [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly converted from the medoxomil prodrug to active azilsartan by esterases in the GI tract. Bioavailability is ~60%. Peak plasma levels ($C_{max}$) reached in 1.5 to 3 hours. Food does not affect bioavailability [US].
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution ~16 L; highly protein bound (>99%), primarily to albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised by CYP2C9 to two primary inactive metabolites: M-II (O-dealkylation) and M-I (decarboxylation) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life ~11 hours. Approximately 55% recovered in feces and 42% in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hypertension Efficacy:** In seven double-blind, randomised studies (n=5941), **azilsartan medoxomil** 80 mg was statistically superior to placebo and active comparators (olmesartan 40 mg and valsartan 320 mg) in reducing both clinic and 24-hour mean ambulatory blood pressure [US].
- **Duration of Effect:** Sustained antihypertensive effect during long-term treatment; no rebound effect observed upon abrupt cessation [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep in original container; protect from light and moisture. Do not repackage [US].
