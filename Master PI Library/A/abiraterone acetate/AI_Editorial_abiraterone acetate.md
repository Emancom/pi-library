# Editorial Product Information: abiraterone acetate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **abiraterone acetate**
- Chemical class: CYP17 (17α-hydroxylase/C17,20-lyase) inhibitor (inhibits androgen biosynthesis)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment, in combination with a corticosteroid (prednisone or prednisolone), of:
  - Newly diagnosed high‑risk metastatic hormone‑sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) in combination with androgen‑deprivation therapy (ADT).
  - Metastatic castration‑resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) in patients who are asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic after failure of ADT.
  - Metastatic castration‑resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) in patients who have received prior chemotherapy containing a taxane.
- Use with corticosteroid dose differs by indication (see Section 4).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended total daily dose: 1000 mg once daily (1 g) administered orally.
  - Administration may be as two 500 mg tablets or four 250 mg tablets as clinically available.
- Must be given in combination with low‑dose prednisone or prednisolone:
  - For hormone sensitive metastatic disease (mHSPC): prednisone or prednisolone 5 mg once daily.
  - For metastatic castration‑resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC): prednisone or prednisolone 10 mg once daily.

### Administration instructions
- **Do not take with food.** Take as a single daily dose on an empty stomach:
  - Take at least 2 hours after eating.
  - Do not eat for at least 1 hour after administration.
- Swallow tablets whole with water.
- Reduced doses (see hepatic toxicity re‑treatment below) must also not be taken with food.

### Dose modifications and re‑treatment after hepatotoxicity
- If ALT or AST > 5× upper limit of normal (ULN) or bilirubin > 3× ULN: interrupt treatment immediately and monitor LFTs.
- Re‑treatment may be considered after liver function returns to baseline, at a reduced dose of 500 mg once daily (one 500 mg tablet or two 250 mg tablets).
  - For re‑treatment: monitor serum transaminases and bilirubin at least every 2 weeks for 3 months, then monthly.
- If hepatotoxicity recurs at 500 mg daily: discontinue permanently.
- If severe hepatotoxicity occurs (ALT or AST ≥ 20× ULN anytime while on therapy): discontinue and do not re‑treat.

### Other dose adjustments
- Renal impairment: no dosage adjustment necessary.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - No dose adjustment required for pre‑existing mild hepatic impairment.
  - Use with caution in moderate hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh B); benefit should clearly outweigh risk.
  - Not recommended / contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C) — see Section 5.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known pregnancy or potential to be pregnant (women who are or may be pregnant) — **contraindicated**.
- Severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh Class C) — **contraindicated**.
- Concomitant use of abiraterone acetate plus prednisone/prednisolone with radium‑223 dichloride (radium‑223) — **contraindicated**.
- Other contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- **Women who are or may be pregnant must not be exposed to abiraterone acetate**. Exposure may affect fetal development due to changes in maternal steroid hormones.
- **Severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C) is a contraindication.**

### General warnings and precautions
- Mineralocorticoid excess (hypertension, hypokalaemia, fluid retention) due to CYP17 inhibition:
  - Correct hypokalaemia and control hypertension before initiating therapy.
  - Use with caution in patients with a history of cardiovascular disease (including congestive heart failure, recent myocardial infarction, ventricular arrhythmia, existing uncontrolled hypertension).
  - Safety not established in patients with LVEF < 50% or NYHA Class III/IV heart failure in some pivotal studies.
  - Monitor blood pressure, serum potassium and signs of fluid retention at least monthly.
  - QT prolongation and Torsades de Pointes have been observed post‑marketing in patients who develop hypokalaemia or have underlying cardiovascular disease.
- Hepatotoxicity:
  - Marked increases in ALT/AST and bilirubin have occurred; very rarely fulminant hepatitis and hepatic failure reported.
  - Measure liver transaminases and bilirubin prior to initiation, every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then monthly thereafter; measure immediately if symptoms/signs of hepatotoxicity arise.
  - Interrupt, reduce dose, or permanently discontinue per criteria in Section 4.
  - No data to support use in patients with active or symptomatic viral hepatitis.
- Corticosteroid withdrawal and adrenal insufficiency:
  - Withdrawal of prednisone/prednisolone may lead to adrenocortical insufficiency; monitor if corticosteroids are reduced or stopped.
  - During unusual stress, increase corticosteroid dose as clinically indicated to cover stress.
  - Abiraterone decreases glucocorticoid production via 17α‑hydroxylase inhibition.
- Metabolic effects:
  - Glucocorticoid co‑therapy may increase hyperglycaemia; monitor blood glucose frequently in patients with diabetes.
  - Isolated reports of hypoglycaemia have occurred when co‑administered with pioglitazone or repaglinide in diabetic patients; monitor blood glucose.
- Use with chemotherapy:
  - Safety and efficacy of concomitant cytotoxic chemotherapy have not been established.
- Use with radium‑223:
  - Combination of abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone with radium‑223 showed increased mortality and fracture rates in a randomized trial; radium‑223 is not recommended in combination with abiraterone plus corticosteroid.
  - If radium‑223 is to be started after abiraterone plus corticosteroid, wait at least 5 days after the last abiraterone dose.
- Use in paediatrics: not for use in children.
- Handling precautions:
  - Women who are pregnant or may be pregnant should not handle abiraterone acetate without protection (e.g., gloves).

### Monitoring requirements
- Liver function tests (ALT, AST, bilirubin): baseline, every 2 weeks for first 3 months, then monthly.
- Blood pressure, serum potassium and fluid status: at least monthly.
- Blood glucose monitoring in patients with diabetes.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of adrenal insufficiency if corticosteroid dose is reduced or stopped.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Abiraterone is a substrate of CYP3A4.
  - Strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifabutin, rifapentine, phenobarbital) reduced abiraterone exposure (rifampicin decreased Cmax and AUC by ~55% in a single‑dose interaction study). Avoid concomitant strong CYP3A4 inducers or use with careful evaluation of efficacy if no alternative.
  - Co‑administration with ketoconazole (strong CYP3A4 inhibitor) had no clinically meaningful effect on abiraterone pharmacokinetics in a small study.
- In vitro, abiraterone is a strong inhibitor of CYP1A2, CYP2D6 and CYP2C8, and a moderate inhibitor of CYP2C9, CYP2C19 and CYP3A4/5; major metabolites (abiraterone sulphate and N‑oxide abiraterone sulphate) are also strong CYP2C8 inhibitors. Clinical relevance varies by substrate.
- Clinical interaction data:
  - CYP2D6: Co‑administration increased dextromethorphan AUC ≈ 200% (single‑dose interaction), indicating significant inhibition of CYP2D6. Exercise caution with drugs activated by or metabolised by CYP2D6, particularly narrow therapeutic index (NTI) drugs; consider dose reduction of the concomitant drug.
  - CYP2C8: Co‑administration increased pioglitazone AUC by 46% (single‑dose interaction) and decreased some pioglitazone metabolites; monitor patients for toxicity of CYP2C8 substrates. Examples: pioglitazone, repaglinide.
  - CYP3A4: Abiraterone is affected by CYP3A4 inducers (see above).
- Transporters:
  - In vitro inhibition of OATP1B1 by abiraterone and metabolites observed; clinical relevance unclear.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Co‑administration with spironolactone not recommended: spironolactone binds androgen receptor and may increase PSA levels; avoid combined use.
- Glucocorticoids (prednisone/prednisolone) required to suppress ACTH and reduce mineralocorticoid excess caused by abiraterone.
- Increased hyperglycaemic effect with concomitant glucocorticoids—monitor diabetic patients.
- Hypoglycaemia has been reported when given with pioglitazone or repaglinide in diabetic patients.

### Management recommendations
- Avoid strong CYP3A4 inducers during therapy.
- Monitor and adjust doses of concomitant narrow therapeutic index drugs metabolised by CYP2D6 or CYP2C8 as clinically indicated.
- Monitor blood glucose when corticosteroids are used and when interacting antidiabetic therapies are co‑administered.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - **Contraindicated in women who are or may be pregnant.**
  - Pregnancy category (Australian): Category D.
  - No human data on use in pregnancy. Animal studies (rats) showed embryofetal adverse effects (reduced fetal weight and survival, delayed ossification, increased late resorptions and post‑implantation loss, decreased ano‑genital distance) at doses related to pharmacologic activity.
  - Semen: it is not known whether abiraterone or metabolites are present in semen. Sexual activity with a pregnant woman: condom required. Sexual activity with a woman of child‑bearing potential: condom required plus an additional effective contraceptive method.
  - Women who are pregnant or may be pregnant should not handle the product without protection (e.g., gloves).
- Breastfeeding:
  - Abiraterone acetate is not indicated for use in women. It is unknown whether abiraterone or metabolites are excreted in human milk.
- Fertility:
  - In animal fertility studies, abiraterone acetate reduced fertility in both sexes; effects were reversible in 4–16 weeks after cessation in rats. Reproductive system changes consistent with antiandrogenic pharmacology observed in mice, rats and monkeys at exposures similar to or below human exposure.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Not for use in children. Clinical trials excluded paediatric patients; no dosing information available.
- Elderly:
  - In phase 3 studies, 70% of patients were ≥ 65 years and 27% were ≥ 75 years. Adverse effects were observed more commonly in patients ≥ 75 years in both treatment and placebo groups. No specific dose adjustment provided for elderly.
- Renal impairment:
  - No dosage adjustment necessary for patients with renal impairment. Specific eGFR/stage‑based dosing recommendations: Not specified in available sources.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Mild impairment (Child‑Pugh A): no dose adjustment necessary.
  - Moderate impairment (Child‑Pugh B): no clinical data on multiple doses; use with caution only if benefit clearly outweighs risk.
  - Severe impairment (Child‑Pugh C): contraindicated / not to be used.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Abiraterone acetate is converted to abiraterone, which inhibits CYP17 (17α‑hydroxylase/C17,20‑lyase)**, key enzymes in androgen biosynthesis. This reduces androgen production from the adrenals, testes and tumor, resulting in decreased androgen receptor activation. CYP17 inhibition also leads to increased mineralocorticoid precursors and can cause mineralocorticoid excess (hypertension, hypokalaemia, fluid retention).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - **Must not be taken with food**; dosing on an empty stomach is required due to food‑related increases in exposure (see Section 4). Detailed absorption parameters (Cmax, Tmax) not specified in available sources.
- Distribution:
  - Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Metabolised to active abiraterone; major metabolites include abiraterone sulphate and N‑oxide abiraterone sulphate. Abiraterone is a substrate of CYP3A4.
- Elimination:
  - Not specified in available sources.
- Interaction studies:
  - Rifampicin (strong CYP3A4 inducer) reduced abiraterone systemic exposure by ~55% (single‑dose interaction study).
  - Ketoconazole (strong CYP3A4 inhibitor) had no clinically meaningful effect on abiraterone exposure in a small study.
  - Abiraterone and metabolites inhibit multiple CYP enzymes in vitro (see Section 8).

### Pharmacodynamics
- Suppression of androgen synthesis with resultant clinical benefits in prostate cancer; reduction of glucocorticoid production via 17α‑hydroxylase inhibition necessitates glucocorticoid co‑therapy to suppress ACTH and mitigate mineralocorticoid excess.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal phase 3 trials described in available sources include:
  - Study 301 (phase 3): patients with mCRPC who had received prior chemotherapy (taxane‑containing regimen). Abiraterone 1 g daily plus low‑dose prednisone/prednisolone (10 mg daily) vs placebo plus prednisone/prednisolone. Average treatment duration with abiraterone was ~8 months. Safety data reported (see Sections 6–7).
  - Study 302 (phase 3): asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic, chemotherapy‑naïve patients with metastatic prostate cancer. Abiraterone 1 g daily plus low‑dose prednisone/prednisolone (10 mg daily) vs placebo plus prednisone/prednisolone. Average treatment duration with abiraterone was ~13.8 months. Safety data reported.
  - Study 3011 (phase 3): newly diagnosed high‑risk metastatic hormone‑sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) receiving ADT (LHRH agonist or orchiectomy). Abiraterone 1 g daily plus prednisone 5 mg daily and ADT vs ADT plus placebo. Median duration of treatment with abiraterone was 24 months. Safety data reported.
- Trial designs: randomized, placebo‑controlled phase 3 studies. Specific primary efficacy endpoints, statistical outcomes, and numeric efficacy results are Not specified in available sources.
