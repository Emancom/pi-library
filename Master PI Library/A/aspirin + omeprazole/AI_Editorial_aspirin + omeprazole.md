# Editorial Product Information: aspirin + omeprazole

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **aspirin + omeprazole**
- Chemical class:
  - Aspirin: salicylate (nonsteroidal anti‑inflammatory/antiplatelet)
  - Omeprazole: proton pump inhibitor (substituted benzimidazole)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2016 (as stated in the available sources)
- Document revision / recent major changes reported in available sources:
  - Recent major change noted: 06/2018
  - Revision date noted: 01/2020
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Registration/approval dates for jurisdictions other than the U.S.: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indication (combined product): For patients who require aspirin for secondary prevention of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events and who are at risk of developing aspirin‑associated gastric ulcers.
- Aspirin component indications (as stated for the combination product):
  - Reduction of the combined risk of death and nonfatal stroke in patients with ischemic stroke or transient ischemia of the brain due to fibrin platelet emboli.
  - Reduction of the combined risk of death and nonfatal myocardial infarction (MI) in patients with previous MI or unstable angina pectoris.
  - Reduction of the combined risk of MI and sudden death in patients with chronic stable angina pectoris.
  - Use in patients who have undergone revascularization procedures (CABG or PTCA) when there is a pre‑existing condition for which aspirin is already indicated.
- Omeprazole component indication (as stated for the combination product):
  - Decreasing the risk of developing aspirin‑associated gastric ulcers in patients at risk for developing aspirin‑associated gastric ulcers due to age (≥ 55) or documented history of gastric ulcers.
- Limitations of use:
  - Not for use as the initial dose of aspirin therapy during onset of acute coronary syndrome, acute MI, or before percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) — immediate‑release aspirin is appropriate in those settings.
  - Has not been shown to reduce the risk of gastrointestinal bleeding due to aspirin.
  - Do not substitute the combination product with separate single‑ingredient products of aspirin and omeprazole.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dose: One tablet once daily.
- Available strengths (as described in the sources): aspirin 81 mg + omeprazole 40 mg; aspirin 325 mg + omeprazole 40 mg.
- Clinical note: 81 mg aspirin is generally accepted as effective for secondary cardiovascular prevention; consider need for 325 mg based on individual patient circumstances and current clinical practice guidelines.

### Dosing by indication
- Secondary prevention of cardiovascular/cerebrovascular events: Use dose consistent with clinical judgment and guideline recommendations (commonly 81 mg daily; 325 mg may be used when indicated). Use the lowest effective dose to achieve therapeutic goals and to minimize dose‑dependent adverse reactions (including bleeding).
- Not indicated as initial aspirin therapy in acute coronary syndrome/acute MI/ prior to PCI.

### Administration instructions
- Take one tablet once daily at least 60 minutes before a meal.
- Swallow whole with liquid. Do not split, chew, crush, or dissolve the tablet.
- Use the lowest effective dose and shortest duration necessary for the clinical condition being treated.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose
- If a dose is missed, take it as soon as remembered. If it is nearly time for the next dose, skip the missed dose. Do not take two doses at the same time unless instructed by a prescriber.
- Do not stop therapy abruptly without medical advice, as sudden discontinuation of antiplatelet therapy could increase risk of thrombotic events.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known allergy to aspirin or other nonsteroidal anti‑inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), including history of the syndrome of asthma, rhinitis, and nasal polyps (aspirin‑exacerbated respiratory disease).
- History of asthma, urticaria, or other allergic‑type reactions after taking aspirin or other NSAIDs.
- Pediatric patients with suspected viral infections (with or without fever) because of the risk of Reye's syndrome with aspirin use in certain viral illnesses.
- Known hypersensitivity to omeprazole, substituted benzimidazoles, or to any excipient in the formulation.
- Concomitant use with rilpivirine‑containing products (contraindicated).
- Any other contraindications: Not specified beyond the items above in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None explicitly labeled as boxed warnings in the available sources. If jurisdictional boxed warnings exist, they are not specified in the available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Coagulation abnormalities: Even low doses of aspirin inhibit platelet function and increase bleeding time; monitor patients with inherited or acquired bleeding disorders (e.g., hemophilia, liver disease, vitamin K deficiency) for bleeding.
- Gastrointestinal adverse reactions: Aspirin is associated with serious GI events including inflammation, bleeding, ulceration, and perforation of the upper and lower GI tract. Monitor for signs/symptoms of ulceration and bleeding; discontinue therapy if clinically significant bleeding occurs.
- Bleeding risk with alcohol: Counsel patients who consume three or more alcoholic drinks daily about increased bleeding risk.
- Interaction with clopidogrel: Omeprazole can impair CYP2C19‑mediated bioactivation of clopidogrel; concomitant use should be avoided or alternative antiplatelet therapy considered.
- Interaction with ticagrelor: Maintenance doses of aspirin above 100 mg reduce ticagrelor efficacy; avoid use of the aspirin 325 mg strength of the combination product with ticagrelor.
- Renal failure: Regular aspirin use is associated with dose‑dependent increased risk of chronic renal impairment. Avoid the combination product in severe renal failure (GFR < 10 mL/min).
- Presence of gastric malignancy: Symptomatic response to acid suppression does not exclude gastric malignancy; consider additional diagnostic evaluation in adults with gastric symptoms or relapse.
- Acute interstitial nephritis: Reported with proton pump inhibitors, including omeprazole; can occur at any time during therapy; discontinue if suspected.
- Clostridium difficile‑associated diarrhea (CDAD): PPI therapy has been associated with increased risk of CDAD, particularly in hospitalized patients—consider diagnosis for persistent diarrhea.
- Bone fracture: Observational data suggest long‑term, high‑dose, or multiple daily dose PPI therapy may increase risk of osteoporosis‑related fractures (hip, wrist, spine); use lowest dose and shortest duration appropriate.
- Cutaneous and systemic lupus erythematosus: PPI therapy has been associated with new onset or exacerbation of cutaneous lupus erythematosus (CLE) and, less commonly, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE); discontinue if consistent signs/symptoms develop and refer for specialist evaluation.
- Hepatic impairment: Systemic exposure to omeprazole is increased in hepatic impairment. Avoid the combination product in patients with any degree of hepatic impairment.
- Cyanocobalamin (vitamin B‑12) deficiency: Long‑term daily acid suppression (e.g., >3 years) may lead to malabsorption and vitamin B‑12 deficiency; consider diagnosis if clinical signs emerge.
- Hypomagnesemia: Reported rarely with prolonged PPI treatment (usually after ≥3 months, often >1 year). Serious events (tetany, arrhythmias, seizures) have occurred; consider monitoring magnesium in patients expected to be on prolonged therapy or taking medications that predispose to hypomagnesemia (e.g., diuretics, digoxin).
- Reduced effect of omeprazole with inducers: Drugs inducing CYP2C19 or CYP3A4 (e.g., St. John's Wort, rifampin) can decrease omeprazole concentrations—avoid concomitant use.
- Interference with diagnostic investigations: Omeprazole increases serum chromogranin A levels via reduced gastric acidity; temporarily stop therapy at least 14 days before testing CgA to avoid false positives.
- Interaction with methotrexate: Concomitant PPI use may elevate and prolong methotrexate concentrations (primarily with high‑dose methotrexate); consider temporary withdrawal of the combination product with high‑dose methotrexate.
- Premature closure of fetal ductus arteriosus: NSAIDs (including aspirin) may cause fetal ductus arteriosus closure; avoid use in pregnancy starting at 30 weeks gestation.
- Abnormal laboratory tests: Aspirin has been associated with elevated hepatic enzymes, elevated blood urea nitrogen and serum creatinine, hyperkalemia, proteinuria, and prolonged bleeding time.
- Fundic gland polyps: PPI use associated with increased risk of fundic gland polyps, particularly with long‑term (>1 year) therapy; use shortest duration appropriate.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of bleeding (GI and other sources).
- Monitor renal function in patients with pre‑existing renal disease or on chronic aspirin therapy.
- Monitor hepatic function where clinically indicated (avoid product in hepatic impairment).
- Monitor magnesium levels prior to initiation and periodically in patients expected to be on prolonged therapy or on concomitant drugs that may cause hypomagnesemia.
- Monitor INR and prothrombin time when initiating or changing concomitant warfarin therapy (increased INR and bleeding reported with concomitant PPI and warfarin).
- Consider testing for vitamin B‑12 deficiency in long‑term PPI users with compatible clinical signs.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Do not substitute the combined product with separate aspirin and omeprazole products without clinical rationale.
- Counsel patients to avoid heavy alcohol use while on therapy.
- Use lowest effective dose of aspirin to minimize bleeding risk.
- Temporarily discontinue omeprazole before diagnostic testing of chromogranin A.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Clopidogrel: Omeprazole inhibits CYP2C19 and can reduce formation of clopidogrel's active metabolite, decreasing clopidogrel’s antiplatelet effect. Co‑administration with omeprazole at therapeutic doses reduces clopidogrel activity; avoid concomitant use or consider alternative antiplatelet therapy.
- Rifampin and St. John's Wort: Inducers of CYP2C19/CYP3A4 reduce omeprazole concentrations and can reduce efficacy of the PPI component; avoid concomitant use.
- Methotrexate: Concomitant PPI use may elevate and prolong methotrexate and/or metabolite concentrations (primarily at high methotrexate doses); consider temporary withdrawal of the combination product in patients receiving high‑dose methotrexate.
- Antiretrovirals:
  - Rilpivirine‑containing products: Concomitant use is contraindicated.
  - Atazanavir and nelfinavir: Avoid concomitant use.
  - Saquinavir: Omeprazole can increase saquinavir exposure; monitor for toxicity per saquinavir labeling.
  - Other antiretrovirals: The effect of PPIs is variable; consult specific product information for each agent.
- Warfarin and heparin: Omeprazole and aspirin may increase anticoagulant effect (increased INR and prothrombin time reported); aspirin increases anticoagulant activity of heparin and warfarin. Monitor INR/prothrombin time and adjust doses as appropriate.
- Drugs affected by gastric pH: Omeprazole‑mediated reduction in gastric acidity can alter absorption of drugs requiring an acidic environment.
- Drugs metabolized by CYP2C19/CYP3A4: Omeprazole may affect plasma levels of co‑administered drugs metabolized by these enzymes; consult product information for the co‑administered drug.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Antiplatelet agents: Aspirin’s antiplatelet effect increases bleeding risk when combined with other antiplatelet or anticoagulant medications.
- Ticagrelor: Aspirin doses >100 mg reduce ticagrelor effectiveness; avoid coadministration with the aspirin 325 mg strength of the combination product.
- Alcohol: Heavy alcohol use (≥3 drinks/day) increases bleeding risk when combined with aspirin.

### Clinically significant interactions — management notes
- Avoid coadministration with rilpivirine‑containing antiretroviral products (contraindicated).
- Avoid concomitant clopidogrel when possible; if needed, consider alternative antiplatelet therapy or clinical monitoring.
- Avoid use of the 325 mg aspirin strength with ticagrelor.
- Monitor INR and prothrombin time with warfarin; adjust dosing as needed.
- Consider temporary discontinuation of omeprazole at least 14 days before chromogranin A testing for neuroendocrine tumor evaluation.
- With high‑dose methotrexate administration, consider temporary withdrawal of the combination product.

### Jurisdictional interaction notes

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Avoid use of NSAIDs, including aspirin‑containing products, starting at 30 weeks gestation because of the risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus.
- Pregnancy category/classifications by jurisdiction (e.g., Australian category letters or other systems): Not specified in available sources.
- Clinical counsel: Use in pregnancy should weigh maternal benefit against fetal risk; avoid in late pregnancy as above.

### Breastfeeding
- Breastfeeding: Breastfeeding is not recommended while using the combination product (as stated in the available sources).

### Fertility
- NSAIDs, including aspirin, are associated with reversible infertility in females; consider withdrawal if difficulty conceiving.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Aspirin: Antiplatelet agent; irreversibly inhibits platelet cyclooxygenase (COX), reducing thromboxane A2‑mediated platelet aggregation (antiplatelet effect described in available sources).
- Omeprazole: Proton pump inhibitor that reduces gastric acid secretion by inhibiting the H+/K+ ATPase in gastric parietal cells (PPI activity described in available sources).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination details: Not specified in available sources in full detail.
- Noted pharmacokinetic points from the available sources:
  - Systemic exposure to omeprazole is increased in patients with hepatic impairment.
  - Omeprazole decreases gastric acidity and can increase serum chromogranin A levels.
  - Omeprazole interacts with CYP2C19 (and CYP3A4 to some degree), affecting metabolism of other drugs (e.g., clopidogrel).

### Pharmacodynamics
- Aspirin exerts antiplatelet effects at low doses used for secondary prevention of cardiovascular events.
- Omeprazole reduces gastric acid, decreasing aspirin‑associated gastric ulcer risk in at‑risk patients (age ≥ 55 or documented history of gastric ulcers) as intended by the combination product.
- Interaction effect: Omeprazole can reduce clopidogrel activation via CYP2C19 inhibition, reducing antiplatelet efficacy of clopidogrel.

- Overall efficacy summary: The combination product is indicated to provide aspirin’s antiplatelet benefits for secondary cardiovascular/cerebrovascular prevention while providing omeprazole’s acid suppression to decrease risk of aspirin‑associated gastric ulcers in selected at‑risk patients (as stated in the indications).
