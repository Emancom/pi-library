# Editorial Product Information: Agalsidase Alfa

**Editorial Note:** 
This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled to provide a comprehensive overview of agalsidase alfa for healthcare professionals. Please note that while the request specified documents from both the TGA (Australia) and the FDA (United States), agalsidase alfa (marketed as Replagal) is currently approved by the TGA but is not an FDA-approved therapy. Furthermore, the second document provided for review pertained to Levocarnitine; as this is a different pharmacological agent, it has been excluded from this unified PI to ensure medical accuracy and patient safety. Consequently, the clinical and regulatory data below are primarily derived from the TGA Product Information.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Agalsidase alfa  
**Active Ingredient:** Agalsidase alfa ghu

Agalsidase alfa is a human α-galactosidase A produced by genetic engineering technology using a human cell line. It is a homodimer comprised of two subunits of approximately 50,000 daltons each. The amino acid sequence is identical to the naturally occurring human enzyme.

## 2. Approved Indications
Agalsidase alfa is indicated for the long-term management of patients with a confirmed diagnosis of Fabry Disease (α-galactosidase A deficiency) [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
Agalsidase alfa is contraindicated in patients who have experienced life-threatening hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reactions to the active substance or any of the excipients contained within the formulation.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### General Supervision
Treatment should be initiated and maintained under the supervision of a physician experienced in the management of Fabry Disease or other inherited metabolic disorders.

### Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs)
Idiosyncratic infusion-related reactions occur in approximately 13.7% of adult patients [TGA]. 
*   **Symptoms:** Most commonly rigors, headache, nausea, pyrexia, flushing, and fatigue. 
*   **Serious Reactions:** Uncommon but may include tachycardia, urticaria, angioneurotic edema (with throat tightness and swollen tongue), and stridor.
*   **Cardiac Considerations:** In patients with pre-existing cardiac involvement, IRRs may induce hemodynamic stress, potentially triggering cardiac events such as arrhythmias, heart failure, or myocardial ischemia.
*   **Management:** If a mild-to-moderate reaction occurs, the infusion may be temporarily interrupted (5–10 minutes) or the rate slowed. Pre-treatment with antihistamines and/or corticosteroids (1 to 24 hours prior) may prevent recurrence. In the event of severe anaphylaxis, administration must be discontinued immediately and emergency medical standards observed.

### Immunogenicity
Patients may develop IgG antibodies to the protein. Approximately 24% of male patients have shown a low-titre IgG response, typically developing 3–12 months into therapy [TGA]. A small percentage of patients (approx. 7%) may develop immunologic tolerance over time.

### Renal and Hepatic Impairment
*   **Renal:** Extensive, irreversible renal damage may limit the clinical response to enzyme replacement therapy.
*   **Hepatic:** As the drug is metabolized via peptide hydrolysis, hepatic impairment is not expected to significantly alter pharmacokinetics.

### Paediatric Use
Clinical experience in children is limited. In children aged 6.5 to 18 years, the incidence of infusion-related reactions is significantly higher (57.1%) compared to adults [TGA]. Safety and efficacy for children under 6.5 years of age have not yet been established.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Recommended Dose
The standard dose for adults and children (6.5 years and older) is **0.2 mg/kg body weight** administered every two weeks via intravenous infusion.

### Administration Instructions
1.  **Preparation:** Calculate the required dose and dilute into 100 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride.
2.  **Handling:** Mix by gentle rocking; **do not shake**.
3.  **Infusion:** Administer over a period of 40 minutes using an IV line equipped with an **in-line 0.2 micron filter**.
4.  **Home Infusion:** May be considered only for patients who have been stabilized in a hospital setting and are tolerating infusions well.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequent adverse events are infusion-related reactions. Other common adverse drug reactions (ADRs) reported in ≥10% of patients include:
*   **Nervous System:** Headache, dizziness, paraesthesia, neuropathic pain, tremor.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain.
*   **General:** Pyrexia, fatigue, rigors, chest pain, peripheral edema.
*   **Respiratory:** Dyspnoea, cough, nasopharyngitis, pharyngitis.
*   **Musculoskeletal:** Arthralgia, back pain, myalgia, pain in limbs.

Post-marketing reports have also identified cardiac arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation, ventricular extrasystoles) specifically during or following infusion in patients with cardiac Fabry manifestations.

## 7. Drug Interactions
Agalsidase alfa should **not** be administered concurrently with the following medications as they may inhibit intracellular α-galactosidase activity:
*   Chloroquine
*   Amiodarone
*   Benoquin
*   Gentamicin

No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been performed; however, as a protein enzyme, it is not a candidate for cytochrome P450-mediated interactions.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy (Category B2)
Animal studies have shown no evidence of fetal harm. However, there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Agalsidase alfa should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [TGA].

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown whether agalsidase alfa is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised, and the benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the potential for adverse effects in the infant.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Biphasic distribution and elimination. The volume of distribution is approximately 17% of body weight.
*   **Half-life:** The elimination half-life is approximately 89 to 108 minutes in adults.
*   **Clearance:** Clearance is faster in children than in adults (4.2 mL/min/kg in children aged 7-11 vs 2.3 mL/min/kg in adults).
*   **Metabolism:** Expected to occur via peptide hydrolysis into small peptides and amino acids.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Fabry Disease is characterized by a deficiency in α-galactosidase A, leading to the systemic accumulation of globotriaosylceramide (Gb3). Agalsidase alfa catalyzes the hydrolysis of Gb3 by cleaving the terminal galactose residue. Regular administration reduces Gb3 storage in the vascular endothelium, renal epithelial cells, and myocardial cells, resulting in stabilized renal function, reduced pain, and improved cardiac mass.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store at **2°C to 8°C** (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
*   Protect from light.
*   **Do not shake** the vials.
*   Once diluted, the solution is chemically stable for 24 hours at 25°C, though it should be used as soon as possible to reduce microbiological risk.
