# Editorial Product Information: Allopurinol

## Editorial Note

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Allopurinol (a xanthine oxidase inhibitor).
- **Brand Names:** ALLOPURINOL-WGR [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M04AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 100 mg tablets: White to off-white, biconvex, round, uncoated tablets (marked ‘AL’ & ‘100’) [Australian].
    - 300 mg tablets: Peach-coloured, biconvex, round, uncoated tablets (marked ‘AL’ & ‘300’) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GM Pharma International Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Allopurinol is indicated for the management of conditions associated with urate/uric acid deposition, including:
- **Gout:** Idiopathic gout, gouty arthritis, and skin tophi.
- **Renal Conditions:** Uric acid lithiasis, acute uric acid nephropathy, and recurrent mixed calcium oxalate renal stones (when hyperuricosuria is present and lifestyle measures have failed).
- **Secondary Hyperuricaemia:** Neoplastic and myeloproliferative diseases with high cell turnover (occurring spontaneously or after cytotoxic therapy).
- **Enzyme Disorders:** Overproduction of urate related to deficiencies or disorders in hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase (including Lesch-Nyhan syndrome), glucose 6-phosphatase (glycogen storage disease), phosphoribosylpyrophosphate synthetase, or phosphoribosylpyrophosphate amidotransferase.
- **Specific Stones:** Management of 2,8-dihydroxyadenine (2,8-DHA) renal stones related to adenine phosphoribosyl transferase deficiency.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Dosage must be tailored to the individual based on serum urate levels and urinary urate/uric acid concentrations.
- **Dose Frequency:** Usually taken once daily after a meal to enhance tolerability. If the daily dose exceeds 300 mg and gastrointestinal intolerance occurs, divided doses are recommended.
- **Mild conditions:** 100 to 200 mg daily.
- **Moderately severe conditions:** 300 to 600 mg daily.
- **Severe conditions:** 700 to 900 mg daily.
- **Weight-based dosing:** 2 to 10 mg/kg bodyweight/day.

### Children (Under 15 years)
- **Indications:** Use is restricted to malignant conditions (e.g., leukaemia) and specific enzyme disorders (e.g., Lesch-Nyhan syndrome).
- **Dose:** 10 to 20 mg/kg bodyweight/day or 100 to 400 mg daily.

### Administration Instructions
- **Hydration:** Patients should maintain high fluid intake to ensure a daily urinary output of at least 2 litres.
- **Prophylaxis:** During the initial stages of treatment, allopurinol may precipitate an acute gout attack. Prophylaxis with colchicine (0.5 mg three times daily) or a suitable anti-inflammatory agent is recommended for at least one month.
- **Titration:** In patients at higher risk of adverse reactions (e.g., renal impairment), start at a low dose (50–100 mg/day) and titrate based on response.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to allopurinol or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- **Iron Salts:** Concomitant administration with iron salts in patients with idiopathic haemochromatosis or their immediate relatives [Australian].
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children except for hyperuricaemia secondary to malignancy or Lesch-Nyhan syndrome.
- **Rechallenge:** Absolute contraindication to re-initiating therapy in patients who previously experienced hypersensitivity syndrome or SJS/TEN.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Critical Alerts
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Allopurinol must be **DISCONTINUED IMMEDIATELY** at the first sign of a skin rash or other hypersensitivity symptoms. Severe reactions include Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN/Lyell's disease), and drug rash with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS).
- **HLA-B*5801 Allele:** This allele is strongly associated with a higher risk of allopurinol-induced SJS/TEN. Prevalence is highest in Han Chinese (up to 20%), Thai (8-15%), and Korean (12%) populations. Genotyping should be considered before starting therapy in these groups.

### General Precautions
- **Bone Marrow Depression:** Rare cases affecting one or more cell lines; may occur as early as six weeks or as late as six years into therapy.
- **Thyroid Disorders:** Increases in TSH have been observed; monitoring is recommended in patients with pre-existing thyroid dysfunction.
- **Hepatic Effects:** Reversible clinical hepatotoxicity and asymptomatic rises in LFTs have occurred. Discontinue if hepatic function abnormalities increase.
- **Renal Impairment:** Increased risk of toxicity; requires strict dose adjustment (see Section 9).
- **Asymptomatic Hyperuricaemia:** Generally not an indication for allopurinol; lifestyle modifications should be the first line of management.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are most common in patients with renal or hepatic impairment.

- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Dermatological: Pruritic, maculopapular, or scaly rashes.
- **Rare (<0.1%):**
    - **Generalised Hypersensitivity:** Fever, chills, arthralgia, lymphadenopathy, and vasculitis (which may lead to hepatitis or interstitial nephritis).
    - **SCARs:** SJS, TEN, DRESS.
    - **Haematological:** Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, aplastic anaemia, agranulocytosis.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, stomatitis; very rarely steatorrhoea or haematemesis.
    - **Hepatic:** Hepatitis, hepatic necrosis, granulomatous hepatitis.
- **Frequency Not Known:**
    - Lichenoid drug reactions, angioedema, somnolence, vertigo, ataxia, taste perversion, erectile dysfunction, and gynaecomastia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **6-Mercaptopurine (6-MP) and Azathioprine:** Allopurinol inhibits the oxidative metabolism of these drugs. The dose of 6-MP or azathioprine **must be reduced to 25%** of the usual dose to prevent life-threatening pancytopenia.
- **Diuretics:** Thiazides may increase the risk of allopurinol toxicity, especially in renal impairment. Furosemide may increase plasma oxipurinol levels.
- **ACE Inhibitors:** Increased risk of hypersensitivity, particularly in patients with renal dysfunction.
- **Amoxicillin/Ampicillin:** Increased frequency of skin rashes.
- **Anticoagulants (Coumarins):** Potential for increased anticoagulant effect; monitor PT/INR closely.
- **Cytostatics:** Increased risk of blood dyscrasias when co-administered.
- **Vidarabine (Adenine Arabinoside):** Prolonged half-life and increased risk of toxicity.
- **Didanosine:** Increased plasma levels; co-administration is generally not recommended.
- **Antacids:** Aluminium hydroxide may attenuate the effect of allopurinol; separate doses by at least 3 hours.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B2 [Australian]:** There is inadequate evidence of safety in human pregnancy. Use only when no safe alternative exists and the disease itself carries significant risk to the mother or fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Both allopurinol and its active metabolite, oxipurinol, are excreted in human breast milk. Use during breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show no impairment at high doses; human reports of impotence are rare and a causal link is unestablished.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric Use
Rarely indicated except for leukaemia and Lesch-Nyhan syndrome. Safety and efficacy in other conditions are not established.

### Elderly Use
Dosage should be conservative, starting at the lower end of the range, due to the higher likelihood of decreased renal, hepatic, or cardiac function.

### Renal Impairment
Allopurinol and oxipurinol are renally excreted.
- **Initiation:** Start with a maximum of 100 mg/day (or 100 mg at intervals greater than one day).
- **Monitoring:** Monitor plasma oxipurinol levels where possible, keeping levels below 100 µmol/L (15.2 µg/mL).
- **Renal Dialysis:** Allopurinol is removed by dialysis. A dose of 300–400 mg immediately after each session (2–3 times a week) may be considered.

### Hepatic Impairment
Reduced dosages are required. Periodic LFTs are recommended, especially during the early stages of therapy.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Allopurinol and its metabolite oxipurinol inhibit xanthine oxidase (XO), the enzyme responsible for converting hypoxanthine to xanthine, and xanthine to uric acid. This reduces urate concentrations in both serum and urine and facilitates the mobilisation and dissolution of urate deposits.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Approximately 90% absorbed from the GIT.
- **Distribution:** Uniformly distributed in tissue water, excluding the brain (where levels are 50% of other tissues). Not bound to plasma proteins.
- **Metabolism:** Rapidly converted to the active metabolite oxipurinol. Peak plasma levels occur at 1.5 hours (allopurinol) and 4.5 hours (oxipurinol).
- **Elimination:** Approximately 20% excreted in faeces. Renal excretion is primary. Allopurinol half-life is 1–2 hours; oxipurinol half-life is approximately 15 hours, allowing for once-daily dosing.
