# Editorial Product Information: Atenolol

## Editorial Note
*   This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    *   **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a coherent reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Atenolol
*   **Chemical Class:** Beta-adrenoceptor blocking agent (Beta-selective)
*   **Brand Names:** APO-Atenolol [Australian]
*   **ATC Code:** C07AB03
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 50 mg film-coated, biconvex tablets [Australian]
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Hypertension:** Management of all grades of hypertension, including hypertension of renal origin [Australian].
*   **Angina Pectoris:** Treatment of frequent disabling angina in patients without evidence of cardiac failure [Australian].
*   **Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Maintenance treatment for supraventricular and ventricular arrhythmias previously controlled by intravenous agents, including those associated with acute myocardial infarction (MI) [Australian].
*   **Myocardial Infarction (Late Intervention):** Treatment initiated more than 12 hours after the onset of chest pain to reduce mortality and the risk of reinfarction [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Hypertension:** Initially 50 mg daily. May be increased weekly by 50 mg increments to a maximum of 200 mg daily. Doses up to 100 mg may be given once daily; doses exceeding 100 mg should be administered in divided doses [Australian].
*   **Angina Pectoris:** Initially 50 mg daily, increasing if necessary to 100 mg daily as a single or divided dose. Doses above 100 mg rarely provide additional benefit [Australian].
*   **Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Maintenance dose of 50 mg to 100 mg daily [Australian].
*   **Post-Myocardial Infarction:** For late intervention, maintenance oral therapy of 50 mg daily is recommended for one to three years [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
*   Patients transferring from other antihypertensives can generally be switched directly, except for clonidine (see Section 7, Drug Interactions).

### Renal Impairment Adjustments
Dosage adjustment is required for patients with severe renal impairment based on creatinine clearance (CrCl) [Australian]:
*   **CrCl 15–35 mL/min/1.73m²:** 50 mg daily or 100 mg on alternate days.
*   **CrCl <15 mL/min/1.73m²:** 50 mg on alternate days or 100 mg every fourth day.
*   **Haemodialysis:** 50 mg orally after each dialysis session, administered under hospital supervision due to the risk of significant blood pressure drops.

## 4. Contraindications
*   History of bronchospasm, asthma, or obstructive airway disease [Australian].
*   Congestive heart failure (uncontrolled) [Australian].
*   Right ventricular failure secondary to pulmonary hypertension or significant right ventricular hypertrophy [Australian].
*   Sinus bradycardia (heart rate <45–50 beats/minute) [Australian].
*   Sick sinus syndrome [Australian].
*   Second and third-degree atrioventricular (AV) block [Australian].
*   Shock (cardiogenic and hypovolaemic) [Australian].
*   Hypotension [Australian].
*   Metabolic acidosis [Australian].
*   Severe peripheral arterial circulatory disturbances [Australian].
*   Untreated phaeochromocytoma [Australian].
*   Concurrent use of anaesthetics that produce myocardial depression (e.g., ether, chloroform) [Australian].
*   Hypersensitivity to **atenolol** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Do not discontinue therapy abruptly in patients with coronary artery disease. Dosage should be tapered over 8–14 days to prevent severe exacerbation of angina, MI, or ventricular arrhythmias [Australian].
*   **Cardiac Failure:** Beta-blockade may precipitate heart failure in patients with impaired contractility. Use in heart failure should be managed only in specialist centres [Australian].
*   **First-Degree Heart Block:** Use with caution due to negative effects on conduction time [Australian].
*   **Diabetes:** **Atenolol** may mask tachycardia associated with acute hypoglycaemia and may delay recovery of blood glucose levels [Australian].
*   **Anaphylaxis:** Patients with a history of severe anaphylaxis may be more reactive to allergens and may be resistant to standard doses of adrenaline [Australian].
*   **Peripheral Circulation:** May exacerbate symptoms of peripheral vascular disease or Raynaud's phenomenon [Australian].
*   **Thyroid Disease:** May mask clinical signs of hyperthyroidism (e.g., tachycardia). Abrupt withdrawal may precipitate a thyroid storm [Australian].
*   **Phaeochromocytoma:** Must be treated with an alpha-blocker prior to the use of any beta-blocker to avoid hypertensive crisis [Australian].
*   **Oculomucocutaneous Syndrome:** While specifically associated with practolol, clinicians should be alert for skin rashes or dry eyes [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 10% of patients may experience some form of adverse reaction [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Common (>1–10%) | Less Common (<1%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Indigestion, constipation, dry mouth | Diarrhoea |
| **Nervous System** | Fatigue, dizziness | Vivid dreams, paraesthesia, insomnia, headache, confusion, nightmares |
| **Cardiovascular** | Bradycardia | Postural hypotension, cold extremities, heart block, Raynaud's phenomenon |
| **Respiratory** | Wheezing, bronchospasm | Dyspnoea, nasal congestion |
| **Psychiatric** | Not specified | Hallucinations, depression, psychoses |
| **Hepatic** | Not specified | Elevated transaminases, rare hepatotoxicity/cholestasis |
| **Dermatological** | Not specified | Rash, alopecia, psoriasiform reactions |
| **Other** | Not specified | Impotence, visual disturbances, dry eyes, thrombocytopenia |

**Biochemical Abnormalities:** Increases in AST, blood urea, and serum creatinine have been reported. An increase in Antinuclear Antibodies (ANA) has been noted, though clinical relevance is unclear [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Calcium Antagonists:** Combined use with verapamil or diltiazem may cause severe hypotension, bradycardia, and asystole. Use with nifedipine requires caution [Australian].
*   **Antiarrhythmic Drugs:** Potentiation of conduction time and negative inotropic effects may occur with Class IA (e.g., disopyramide), Class III (e.g., amiodarone), and Class IV agents [Australian].
*   **Clonidine:** Concurrent use should be avoided. If switching, **atenolol** must be withdrawn several days before clonidine is gradually discontinued to avoid rebound hypertension [Australian].
*   **Catecholamine-depleting agents:** Reserpine or guanethidine may have additive effects, leading to excessive bradycardia or hypotension [Australian].
*   **Digitalis Glycosides:** May increase the risk of bradycardia [Australian].
*   **NSAIDs:** Prostaglandin synthetase inhibitors (e.g., ibuprofen, indomethacin) may reduce the hypotensive efficacy of beta-blockers [Australian].
*   **Sympathomimetics:** Adrenaline and related agents may counteract the effects of beta-blockers [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. **Atenolol** crosses the placental barrier. Use in the first and second trimesters has been associated with intrauterine growth retardation, embryo/foetal resorptions (in animal studies), and potential for early labour. Use in the third trimester requires strict risk-benefit assessment; neonates may exhibit bradycardia and hypoglycaemia [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** **Atenolol** accumulates significantly in breast milk. Infants should be monitored for signs of beta-blockade (bradycardia, hypoglycaemia) [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** Data not available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** No clinical experience or data available for children [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** Dosage requirements may be reduced, particularly in the presence of age-related renal impairment [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 for specific dose adjustments based on creatinine clearance.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though **atenolol** undergoes minimal hepatic metabolism [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** **Atenolol** is a beta-1 selective (cardioselective) adrenoceptor antagonist. It lacks intrinsic sympathomimetic activity (ISA) and membrane-stabilising activity (MSA). It reduces blood pressure, heart rate, and myocardial contractility [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Incomplete (40–60%) and variable [Australian].
    *   **Distribution:** Peak blood levels occur 2–4 hours post-dose [Australian].
    *   **Metabolism:** Minimal (<10%) hepatic metabolism [Australian].
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily renal excretion. Plasma half-life is 7–9 hours, but the therapeutic antihypertensive effect lasts at least 24 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Myocardial Infarction:** Data indicates that **atenolol** reduces infarct size, incidence of ventricular arrhythmias, and mortality in the first 7 days post-MI [Australian].
*   **Hypertension/Angina:** Effective across most ethnic populations, though response may be reduced in Afro-Caribbean patients [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 25°C.
*   Protect from light and moisture.
*   [Australian] shelf life is specified on the product packaging/ARTG summary.
