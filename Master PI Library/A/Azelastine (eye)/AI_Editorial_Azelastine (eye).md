# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was prepared using official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Healthcare providers must refer to the current approved PI in their specific region for prescribing and legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP)
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Titration:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are adequate [US].

### Monitoring and Administration Instructions
- **Plasma Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at 25°C in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Not specified in available sources as a formal contraindication, but hypersensitivity warnings apply.
- **General:** None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of intravenous administration, primarily in ESRD patients [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in both patients with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been noted [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety/efficacy of oral **levocarnitine** in renal impairment has not been established. Chronic high doses of oral formulations in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting. In dialysis patients: injection site reaction (33%), pharyngitis (23%), headache (22%), hypertension (20%), and diarrhea (16%) [US].
- **Uncommon to Common (1-10%):** Body odor, gastritis, abdominal pain, asthenia, fever, flu syndrome, and various cardiovascular disturbances (palpitations, tachycardia) [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity [US].
    - Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when **levocarnitine** is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of treatment or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but text provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA-based) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, no adequate, well-controlled human studies exist. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentration in milk following exogenous administration. Benefits to the mother should be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** **Levocarnitine** is indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD; however, caution is advised regarding oral administration due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Levocarnitine** is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation. It also clears accumulated acylCoA esters by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from ~20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients showed a dose-proportional increase in mean trough levels at 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing. 
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
