# Editorial Product Information: Adefovir Dipivoxil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial was developed using the following jurisdiction’s Product Information: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of adefovir dipivoxil for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the available regulatory data into a single, cohesive reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal and regional compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Adefovir dipivoxil (a diester prodrug of the acyclic nucleotide analog adefovir).
- **Chemical Class:** Nucleoside and nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitors.
- **Brand Name(s):** **APO-Adefovir** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AF08.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 10 mg oral tablets.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Hepatitis B (CHB):** Treatment of CHB in patients 12 years of age and older with evidence of active viral replication and either persistent elevations in serum aminotransferases (ALT/AST) or histologically active disease.
    - **Adults:** Indication based on responses in HBeAg+ and HBeAg-/HBVDNA+ patients with compensated liver function, and in those with lamivudine-resistant HBV with either compensated or decompensated liver function.
    - **Adolescents (12 to <18 years):** Indication based on responses in HBeAg+ patients with compensated liver disease.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose:** 10 mg once daily, taken orally without regard to food.
- **Adolescents (≥12 years):** 10 mg once daily (provided renal function is adequate).
- **Administration Instructions:** Doses higher than 10 mg daily are associated with increased nephrotoxicity and must not be administered.
- **Duration of Treatment:** The optimum duration of therapy is currently unknown.
- **Clinical Resistance Management:** If serum HBV DNA remains >1000 copies/mL at or beyond 1 year of monotherapy, a treatment modification should be considered. In lamivudine-resistant patients, adefovir should be used in combination with lamivudine rather than as monotherapy to reduce resistance risk.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **adefovir**, **adefovir dipivoxil**, or any excipients (e.g., lactose monohydrate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Post-treatment Exacerbations:** Severe acute exacerbations of hepatitis have occurred following discontinuation of anti-HBV therapy, including adefovir. Periodic monitoring of hepatic function is essential for several months post-discontinuation; resumption of therapy may be required.
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Chronic 10 mg daily dosing may result in renal impairment, characterised by gradual increases in serum creatinine and decreases in serum phosphorus. Risk is higher in patients with pre-existing renal dysfunction or those taking concomitant nephrotoxic agents.
- **Monitoring:** Calculate creatinine clearance (CrCl) for all patients prior to initiation and monitor renal function (creatinine and phosphate) regularly during treatment.
- **HIV Resistance:** Offer HIV antibody testing to all patients before starting adefovir. In patients with unrecognised or untreated HIV, adefovir 10 mg may induce the emergence of HIV resistance as it does not adequately suppress HIV RNA.
- **Lactic Acidosis and Steatosis:** Severe hepatomegaly with steatosis and lactic acidosis (sometimes fatal) have been reported with nucleoside analogs. Discontinue treatment if clinical or laboratory findings suggest these conditions.
- **HBV Transmission:** Patients must be advised that therapy has not been proven to reduce the risk of HBV transmission via sexual contact or blood contamination.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Category (compensated liver disease)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Asthenia (13%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Headache (9%), abdominal pain (9%), nausea (5%), flatulence (4%), diarrhoea (3%), dyspepsia (3%).
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** ALT elevations (>5x ULN: 20%), haematuria (11%), AST elevations (8%), CK elevations (7%).

### Special Populations (Pre- and Post-transplantation)
- **Very Common:** Increased creatinine.
- **Common:** Abnormal renal function, renal failure, hypophosphatemia, hyperkalemia, vomiting, rash, pruritus, myalgia, and jaundice.

### Post-Marketing/Serious Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Pancreatitis.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Osteomalacia and myopathy (associated with proximal renal tubulopathy).
- **Renal:** Fanconi syndrome, proximal renal tubulopathy.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Tenofovir Products:** Adefovir should **not** be administered concurrently with tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF) or tenofovir alafenamide (TAF) [Australian].
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Avoid or use with extreme caution alongside aminoglycosides, cyclosporine, tacrolimus, vancomycin, or NSAIDs.
- **Renal Competition:** Drugs that reduce renal function or compete for active tubular secretion (e.g., ibuprofen) may increase adefovir serum concentrations. Ibuprofen (800 mg tid) has been shown to increase adefovir AUC by 23%.
- **CYP450 System:** Adefovir does not inhibit common CYP450 enzymes; however, its potential to induce them is unknown.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B3 [Australian]:** Animal studies show no teratogenicity with oral dosing at 23-40 times human exposure. However, intravenous administration at maternally toxic doses (38 times human exposure) resulted in foetal malformations (e.g., anasarca, umbilical hernia). Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if adefovir is excreted in human milk. Mothers should be instructed **not to breastfeed** while taking adefovir.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models at doses 19 times the human therapeutic dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Recommended for patients 12 to <18 years. Not recommended for children under 12 years due to insufficient data on benefit/risk [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Caution is advised for patients >65 years due to the higher frequency of decreased renal, hepatic, or cardiac function and concomitant drug therapies.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing intervals must be adjusted based on creatinine clearance (CrCl):
    - **CrCl ≥50 mL/min:** 10 mg every 24 hours.
    - **CrCl 30–49 mL/min:** 10 mg every 48 hours.
    - **CrCl 10–29 mL/min:** 10 mg every 72 hours.
    - **Haemodialysis Patients:** 10 mg every 7 days (post-dialysis).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with moderate to severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Adefovir dipivoxil is a prodrug of adefovir. Intracellularly, it is phosphorylated to adefovir diphosphate, which competes with deoxyadenosine triphosphate for incorporation into viral DNA, causing DNA chain termination and inhibiting HBV DNA polymerase.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Approximately 59%.
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is 1.75 hours; unaffected by food.
    - **Distribution:** Minimal protein binding (≤4%).
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal (45% recovered in urine as adefovir within 24 hours).
    - **Half-life:** Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 7.48 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies (437 & 438):** Demonstrated significant histological improvement (≥2 point decrease in Knodell necro-inflammatory score) compared to placebo at 48 weeks.
    - **HBeAg+ adults:** 53% improvement vs 25% (placebo).
    - **HBeAg- adults:** 64% improvement vs 35% (placebo).
- **Long-term Efficacy:** Extended treatment up to 240 weeks showed a 29% cumulative probability of developing resistance mutations (rtN236T or rtA181V).
- **Lamivudine Resistance:** Study 461 showed significant reductions in HBV DNA when adefovir was added to or replaced lamivudine in resistant patients.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C. Keep in the original container to protect from heat and moisture. 
- **Handling:** Bottles contain a desiccant and may feature child-resistant closures [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
