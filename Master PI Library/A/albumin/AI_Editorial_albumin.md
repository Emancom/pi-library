# Editorial Product Information: Human Albumin (5% and 20% Solutions)

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized by combining official regulatory guidance from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It provides a comprehensive overview of human albumin in both iso-oncotic (5%) and hyper-oncotic (20%) concentrations. Clinicians should note that specific brand recommendations or local availability may vary by region. Sources are indicated in brackets where specific differences in regulatory emphasis occur.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Human Albumin (Generic)
**Active Ingredient:** Human Albumin (Fractionated from pooled human plasma)

This product is available as a sterile, preservative-free aqueous solution for intravenous administration in two primary concentrations:
*   **5% (50 g/L):** Approximately isotonic and iso-oncotic with normal human plasma [FDA].
*   **20% (200 g/L):** Hyper-oncotic, providing an oncotic effect roughly four times that of normal plasma [TGA].

## 2. Approved Indications
Albumin is indicated for the restoration and maintenance of circulating blood volume where a volume deficiency has been demonstrated.

*   **Shock:** Emergency treatment of shock due to major hemorrhage, trauma, or surgery. While 5% albumin is used for volume expansion, 20% albumin may be used but should ideally be accompanied by crystalloids to avoid tissue dehydration [TGA, FDA].
*   **Burns:** Maintenance of plasma volume and colloid osmotic pressure after the first 24 hours of fluid resuscitation.
*   **Hypoproteinaemia:** Management of acute protein loss in critically ill patients where oncotic pressure is significantly reduced, or as an adjunct to diuretic therapy [TGA].
*   **Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS):** Used in conjunction with diuretics to correct interstitial pulmonary oedema and maintain vital signs [TGA].
*   **Plasma Exchange/Haemodialysis:** Used as a replacement fluid in therapeutic plasma exchange or to assist with the removal of excess extravascular fluid during dialysis [TGA].
*   **Acute Pancreatitis and Peritonitis:** Management of shock associated with significant fluid loss into the peritoneal cavity [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity or "incompatibility reactions" to human albumin preparations.
*   Severe anaemia or cardiac failure where the sudden increase in intravascular volume could be life-threatening [TGA, FDA].
*   Chronic conditions where protein deficiency can be managed by dietary means or treating the underlying cause (e.g., chronic nephrosis, malabsorption, or stable cirrhosis) [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Circulatory Overload
Due to its high oncotic pressure (particularly the 20% solution), albumin carries a significant risk of hypervolaemia. Patients must be monitored for signs of cardiac failure or pulmonary oedema (e.g., dyspnoea, jugular vein congestion, or increased blood pressure). Infusions should be stopped immediately if these symptoms appear [TGA].

### Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)
The use of albumin for fluid resuscitation in patients with traumatic brain injury is specifically not recommended. Post-hoc analysis of clinical trial data (SAFE study) indicated higher mortality rates in TBI patients treated with albumin compared to those receiving saline [TGA].

### Pathogen Safety
As a plasma-derived product, the risk of transmitting infectious agents (including viruses and theoretically Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease) cannot be totally eliminated. However, manufacturing involves stringent donor screening and inactivation steps such as pasteurization at 60°C for 10 hours [FDA, TGA].

### Specific Populations and Constraints
*   **Sodium Restriction:** Both preparations contain significant sodium levels (e.g., 48–100 mmol/L in some 20% solutions or up to 0.14 M in 5% solutions) and should be used with caution in patients requiring sodium restriction [TGA, FDA].
*   **Aluminum Toxicity:** Trace amounts of aluminum (≤ 200 µg/L) are present. Long-term use in patients with renal failure may lead to aluminum accumulation and associated toxicities like osteodystrophy or encephalopathy [TGA].
*   **Administration Window:** Infusions should begin within 4 hours of piercing the container stopper to prevent bacterial growth [FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Administration Principles
*   **Route:** Intravenous infusion only.
*   **Monitoring:** Continuous monitoring of blood pressure, pulse, and central venous pressure is essential.
*   **Incompatibility:** Do not mix with protein hydrolysates, amino acid solutions, or alcohol-containing fluids. 

### Recommended Dosing
*   **Shock (5% Solution):** Initial adult dose of 250–500 mL. In children, a dose of 22–33 mL/kg is typically used [FDA].
*   **Hypoproteinaemia (20% Solution):** Usually 50–75 g (250–375 mL) per day. The rate should not exceed 2 mL/minute to avoid rapid volume expansion [TGA].
*   **Burns:** Dosage is adjusted to maintain a plasma albumin level of approximately 25 g/L or a colloid osmotic pressure of 20 mmHg [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally rare, mild, and transient.
*   **Common/Mild:** Flushing, urticaria (hives), fever, chills, nausea, and increased salivation. These usually resolve when the infusion rate is slowed or stopped.
*   **Severe:** Anaphylactic shock or severe hypotension may occur very rarely.
*   **Systemic (SAFE Study Data):** Clinical trials have noted occurrences of hypoxia and pulmonary oedema, particularly when compared to saline resuscitation [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **ACE Inhibitors:** Patients taking Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) inhibitors may be at an increased risk of hypotension when receiving albumin infusions [TGA].
*   **Binding Interactions:** Albumin may bind to and affect the activity of other medications, such as calcium channel blockers or benzodiazepines; these should not be mixed in the same infusion line [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Category B. No animal reproduction studies have been conducted. Use in pregnant women only if clearly necessary, though no specific evidence suggests harm to the fetus [FDA, TGA].
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if albumin is excreted in human milk in quantities that affect the infant. Caution is advised, although albumin is a normal constituent of human milk [TGA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Under normal health, 55–60% of the body's albumin is in the extravascular space. In states of shock or severe burns, capillary permeability increases, leading to abnormal distribution [TGA].
*   **Elimination:** The metabolic half-life is approximately 19 days. Breakdown occurs primarily intracellularly via lysosomal proteases [TGA].
*   **Duration of Effect:** In healthy individuals, less than 10% of infused albumin leaves the intravascular space within the first two hours. However, in the critically ill, the rate of "leakage" is unpredictable [TGA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Albumin is the most abundant plasma protein and provides roughly 75–80% of the total colloid osmotic (oncotic) pressure of the blood. It functions according to the **Starling Principle**, maintaining the balance of fluid between the intravascular and interstitial compartments [FDA]. Additionally, albumin serves as a transport protein for various hormones, enzymes, and medicinal products.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **General:** Store at temperatures not exceeding 30°C (86°F). 
*   **Refrigeration:** Certain smaller presentations (e.g., 10 mL vials) may require refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C [TGA].
*   **Protection:** Protect from light and do not freeze, as freezing may damage the container and lead to contamination [TGA, FDA].
*   **Inspection:** Do not use if the solution is turbid or contains particulate matter.
