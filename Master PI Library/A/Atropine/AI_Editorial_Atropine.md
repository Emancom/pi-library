# Editorial Product Information: Atropine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the local approved PI and relevant local guidelines before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Atropine sulfate monohydrate (an antimuscarinic alkaloid derived from *Atropa belladonna*).
- **Brand Name(s):** MINIJET Atropine Sulfate [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in provided source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile injection, 0.1 mg/mL in a 10 mL prefilled syringe (1.0 mg total) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV), Intramuscular (IM), or Subcutaneous (SC).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** UCB Pharma / UCB Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Atropine is indicated for the following clinical uses [Australian]:
- **Cardiac Care:** Management of severe bradycardia and bradyarrhythmias; treatment of Type I atrioventricular (AV) conduction deficits to mitigate heart block.
- **Anaesthesia:** Used as an antisialogogue (to reduce/prevent respiratory tract secretions) in premedication. 
- **Reflex Management during Anaesthesia:** Prevention of reflex bradycardia and restoration of cardiac rate/arterial pressure caused by vagal activity (e.g., laryngoscopy, tracheal intubation, intra-abdominal manipulation, or oculo-cardiac reflex in ophthalmic surgery).
- **Neuromuscular Blockade Reversal:** To antagonise the muscarinic effects of neostigmine when used to reverse muscle relaxants.
- **Anticholinesterase Poisoning:** Treatment of cardiovascular collapse following exposure to organophosphorus insecticides (e.g., parathion, malathion), nerve gases, or mushroom poisoning.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **Standard Dose:** 0.4 to 0.6 mg via IV, IM, or SC routes, up to a cumulative total of 2 mg [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication (Adults)
- **Anaesthetic Premedication:** 0.2 to 0.6 mg IM or SC (administered 30–60 minutes before surgery) or the same dose IV immediately prior to surgery [Australian].
- **Reversal of Muscarinic Effects (Neostigmine):** 0.6 to 1.2 mg IV for every 0.5 to 2.5 mg of neostigmine administered [Australian].
- **Anticholinesterase Poisoning:** 1 to 2 mg IM or IV, repeated every 5 to 60 minutes until symptoms resolve. In severe cases, up to 50 mg may be required within the first 24 hours [Australian].

### General Paediatric Dosing
- **Standard Dose:** 0.01 mg/kg or 0.3 mg/m² (IV, IM, or SC). The maximum single dose should not exceed 0.4 mg. May be repeated every 4 to 6 hours if necessary [Australian].
- **Cardiac (Advanced Life Support):** 0.02 mg/kg. Minimum dose: 0.1 mg. Maximum dose: 1 mg (children) or 2 mg (adolescents) [Australian].
- **Anaesthetic Premedication (Subcutaneous):**
    - Up to 3 kg: 0.1 mg
    - 7 to 9 kg: 0.2 mg
    - 12 to 16 kg: 0.3 mg
    - Over 20 kg: Adult dose [Australian].
- **Anticholinesterase Poisoning:** 0.05 mg/kg IM or IV every 10 to 30 minutes until symptoms disappear [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- The MINIJET is a single-use prefilled syringe. Once assembled and used, any remaining solution must be discarded [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to atropine or any of its excipients.
- Prostatic hypertrophy or bladder neck obstruction.
- Obstructive gastrointestinal disease (e.g., pyloric obstruction, pyloroduodenal stenosis, achalasia, paralytic ileus, or intestinal atony, especially in the elderly).
- Severe ulcerative colitis or toxic megacolon.
- Reflux oesophagitis.
- Unstable cardiac rhythm.
- Narrow-angle glaucoma.
- Myasthenia gravis (unless utilised specifically to treat the adverse effects of an anticholinesterase agent) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Precautions
- **Vulnerable Populations:** Use with caution in children and the elderly due to increased susceptibility to adverse events [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular:** Caution is required in patients with hyperthyroidism, hypertension, tachyarrhythmias, congestive heart failure, or coronary heart disease (tachycardia may induce angina pectoris) [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Use with extreme caution in mild-to-moderate ulcerative colitis. Antimuscarinics delay gastric emptying and relax the lower oesophageal sphincter; use cautiously in gastric ulcer, diarrhoea, or GI infection [Australian].
- **Respiratory:** Parenteral atropine may reduce bronchial secretions, potentially leading to the formation of bronchial plugs in patients with chronic pulmonary disease [Australian].
- **Neurological:** Use with extreme caution in patients with autonomic neuropathy. Individuals with Down’s syndrome, spastic paralysis, or brain damage may be hypersensitive to atropine's effects [Australian].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Thermoregulation:** Use with caution in febrile patients or in high ambient temperatures, as atropine can inhibit sweating and increase body temperature [Australian].
- **Driving/Machinery:** Atropine may impair vision (mydriasis/cycloplegia) and cause drowsiness; patients should not drive or operate machinery following administration [Australian].
- **Glaucoma Risk:** Conventional doses may precipitate acute glaucoma in susceptible individuals [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are typically dose-related and reversible upon discontinuation.

- **Gastrointestinal/Exocrine:** Dry mouth (xerostomia), loss of taste, nausea, vomiting, bloated feeling, and constipation. Reduced salivary, bronchial, and sweat secretions (anhidrosis) [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, palpitations, atrial arrhythmias, atrioventricular dissociation, ventricular ectopics, and flushing [Australian].
- **Ocular:** Dilated pupils (mydriasis), blurred vision (cycloplegia), and increased ocular tension [Australian].
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Headache, nervousness, dizziness, drowsiness, insomnia, and weakness. Mental confusion or excitement may occur, particularly in elderly patients [Australian].
- **Genitourinary:** Urinary retention [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rash, urticaria, and anaphylaxis; occasionally progressing to exfoliation [Australian].
- **Serious Effects:** Severe dehydration of bronchial secretions leading to thick bronchial plugs [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Potentiation:** The effects of atropine are enhanced by concurrent use of other drugs with anticholinergic activity, including:
    - Tricyclic antidepressants
    - Antispasmodics
    - Antiparkinsonian agents
    - Certain antihistamines
    - Phenothiazines
    - Disopyramide and quinidine [Australian].
- **Absorption Alteration:** By delaying gastric emptying, atropine may modify the absorption of other orally administered medications [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category A]. Atropine has been used extensively in pregnant women without a proven increase in malformations or harmful fetal effects. It crosses the placenta and may cause fetal tachycardia [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Trace amounts are excreted in breast milk, which may cause antimuscarinic effects in the infant. Atropine may also inhibit lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in provided source.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Highly susceptible to adverse effects; specific weight-based dosing and maximum limits apply (see Section 3) [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Increased risk of mental confusion, excitement, and complications from intestinal atony. Caution is advised in those with pre-existing cardiac or obstructive conditions [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; specific dosage adjustment tables were not provided in the source [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Atropine is a competitive antagonist of acetylcholine at postganglionic muscarinic nerve endings. It affects exocrine glands, smooth muscle, cardiac muscle, and the central nervous system [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases sinus rate and sinoatrial/AV conduction by blocking vagal tone. It inhibits secretions (respiratory, salivary, gastric, sweat) and relaxes bronchial smooth muscle [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations (IM) reached within 30 minutes. IV and IM levels are comparable after one hour [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed; crosses the blood-brain barrier and placenta. Approximately 50% is protein-bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised in the liver via oxidation and conjugation to inactive metabolites [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is 2 to 5 hours. Approximately 90% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (30–50% as unchanged drug) [Australian].
