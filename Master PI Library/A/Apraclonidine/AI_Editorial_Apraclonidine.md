# Editorial Product Information: Apraclonidine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a coherent reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Apraclonidine hydrochloride (an alpha-2 adrenergic agonist).
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Iopidine** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Eye drops, solution: 0.5% (contains 5.75 mg/mL apraclonidine hydrochloride, equivalent to 5.0 mg/mL apraclonidine base) [Australian].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Ophthalmic (topical).
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Clinect Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Glaucoma Management:** Short-term control of intraocular pressure (IOP) in patients with glaucoma who are receiving maximally tolerated medical therapy. The recommended duration of use is 3 months [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*   **General Adult Dosing:** Instil one drop into the affected eye(s) three times per day [Australian].
*   **Administration Instructions:**
    *   To reduce systemic absorption, patients should apply pressure to the tear duct (punctal occlusion) for two minutes immediately following administration.
    *   If using multiple topical ophthalmic medications, a minimum five-minute interval should be observed between instillations to prevent "washout" of the previous dose.
*   **Monitoring:** Given that the IOP-lowering effect may diminish over time (tachyphylaxis, often observed after Day 60), IOP must be monitored closely. Treatment should be discontinued if IOP rises significantly.
*   **Contact Lenses:** Patients should remove soft contact lenses prior to instillation and wait at least 15 minutes before reinsertion due to the presence of benzalkonium chloride [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   Hypersensitivity to apraclonidine, systemic clonidine, or any excipients in the formulation.
*   Concurrent use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs).
*   Concurrent use of systemic sympathomimetics.
*   Concurrent use of tricyclic antidepressants [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Cardiovascular Disease:** Use with caution in patients with coronary insufficiency, recent myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular disease, severe uncontrolled hypertension, Raynaud's disease, or thromboangiitis obliterans.
*   **Allergic-like Reactions:** Topical use may trigger an allergic-like reaction (characterised by hyperaemia, pruritus, and oedema). In clinical trials, the discontinuation rate due to these reactions was approximately 15% [Australian].
*   **Tachyphylaxis:** Effectiveness may decrease over time. Patients using the drug to delay surgery require frequent follow-up.
*   **Central Nervous System (CNS):** May cause dizziness or somnolence; patients should be advised against driving or operating machinery if affected.
*   **Vasovagal Attack:** Caution is advised in patients with a history of vasovagal episodes.
*   **Corneal Changes:** While primarily seen in animal studies (rats/mice), patients using the drug beyond 3 months should be monitored for corneal changes [Australian].
*   **Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Apraclonidine is not effective in treating angle-closure glaucoma where the primary goal is re-opening the angle via miotics.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are primarily ocular and generally mild to moderate.

| Frequency | Ocular Effects | Systemic Effects |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Ocular hyperaemia (13%), conjunctivitis, eye pruritus | Dry mouth (10.3%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Discomfort (6%), tearing (4%), lid oedema, dry eye, conjunctival follicles, foreign body sensation, lid margin crusting | Rhinitis, headache, dysgeusia, nasal dryness, asthenia, taste perversion (3%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Mydriasis, keratitis, blurred vision, photophobia, corneal infiltrates, blepharitis, eyelid ptosis, eye pain | Depression, nervousness, dizziness, somnolence, dyspnoea, nausea, constipation, dermatitis, malaise, fatigue |

**Serious/Post-Marketing Effects:** Syncope, bradycardia, hypotension, and hypertension have been reported; frequencies cannot be estimated from available data [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **MAOIs/Sympathomimetics/TCAs:** Contraindicated due to the risk of blunting hypotensive effects or affecting the metabolism of circulating amines.
*   **Cardiovascular Agents:** Caution and frequent monitoring of pulse/blood pressure are required when co-administered with beta-blockers (ophthalmic or systemic), anti-hypertensives, or cardiac glycosides.
*   **CNS Depressants:** Potential for additive or potentiating effects with alcohol, barbiturates, opiates, and sedatives.
*   **Topical Glaucoma Products:** No specific interactions identified with betaxolol, timolol, pilocarpine, or acetazolamide in clinical studies [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [Australian: Category not specified by letter, but not recommended]. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus. Animal studies showed embryotoxicity at high oral doses (3.0 mg/kg/day in rabbits) [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if apraclonidine is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment to avoid risks to the suckling child [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** No human data available. Rat studies showed no effect on male or female fertility at oral doses up to 0.5 mg/kg/day.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for use in children. Apraclonidine is specifically contraindicated/not recommended in infants under 1 year of age due to the risk of serious systemic reactions including lethargy, bradycardia, and decreased oxygen saturation [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** No special precautions required beyond standard monitoring [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied; however, as structurally related clonidine has an increased half-life in severe renal impairment, close cardiovascular monitoring is advised [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Close monitoring of cardiovascular parameters is advised as systemic clonidine is partly metabolised in the liver [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Apraclonidine is a potent alpha-2 adrenergic agonist. Its primary action is the reduction of aqueous humour formation via stimulation of the alpha-adrenergic system. Unlike beta-blockers, it reduces aqueous flow both during the day and at night.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Onset:** Within one hour of instillation.
    *   **Peak Effect:** Three to five hours post-dose.
    *   **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is approximately 8 hours. The aqueous humour half-life is approximately 2 hours. Excretion occurs primarily via the urine (65–75%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Clinical studies involving 12 normal volunteers using the 0.5% solution TID for 10 days showed peak plasma concentrations <1.0 ng/mL. Studies confirmed that 0.5% is the optimal concentration at the top of the dose-response curve for IOP reduction [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
*   **Conditions:** Protect from light [Australian].
*   **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be disposed of in accordance with local requirements.
