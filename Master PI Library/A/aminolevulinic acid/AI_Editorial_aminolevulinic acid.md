# Product Information: Aminolevulinic Acid (ALA)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Active Ingredient:** Aminolevulinic acid hydrochloride (ALA HCl).  
**Chemical Name:** 5-Amino-4-oxo-pentanoic acid hydrochloride.  
**Molecular Formula:** $C_5H_9NO_3 \cdot HCl$.

Aminolevulinic acid is a white to off-white crystalline powder, freely soluble in water. It is available as:
*   **Topical Dermal Patch:** 8 mg of ALA HCl per $4\text{ cm}^2$ patch (2 mg per $\text{cm}^2$).
*   **Oral Solution:** 1,500 mg ALA HCl lyophilized powder (equivalent to 1,170 mg ALA) for reconstitution in drinking water.

## 2. Approved Indications
Aminolevulinic acid is indicated for two primary clinical applications based on its formulation:

*   **Actinic Keratosis (Topical):** Treatment of mild to moderate actinic keratosis (AK) lesions located on the face and hairless areas of the scalp [TGA].
*   **Malignant Glioma Surgery (Oral):** An optical imaging adjunct used preoperatively for the intraoperative visualization of malignant tissue in patients with suspected WHO Grade III or IV gliomas [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to aminolevulinic acid or porphyrins. For the topical patch, this includes hypersensitivity to any components of the patch material [TGA, FDA].
*   **Porphyria:** History of acute or chronic porphyria [TGA, FDA].
*   **Photodermatoses:** Known photodermatoses such as xeroderma pigmentosum, lupus erythematosus, or polymorphic light reaction [TGA].
*   **Non-responsiveness:** Previous history of failing to respond to photodynamic therapy (PDT) with ALA-containing preparations [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Phototoxic Reactions
Patients are at risk of severe phototoxic skin reactions (similar to severe sunburn) following administration. 
*   **Concurrent Medications:** Do not administer known phototoxic drugs (e.g., St. John’s Wort, griseofulvin, thiazide diuretics, sulfonylureas, phenothiazines, sulphonamides, quinolones, or tetracyclines) for 24 hours before and after ALA administration [FDA].
*   **Light Exposure:** Patients must avoid exposure to direct sunlight or intense indoor light (e.g., operating room lights, tanning beds) for 48 hours following treatment [TGA, FDA].

### Risk of Misinterpretation (Glioma Surgery)
Fluorescence does not guarantee the presence of a tumor, nor does its absence rule out malignancy. Errors may include false negatives (especially in low-grade gliomas) and false positives (due to inflammation or other tumor types) [FDA].

### Specific Populations and Lesions
*   **Skin Type:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients with Fitzpatrick skin types IV to VI (moderate brown to black skin), where AK is rare [TGA].
*   **Lesion Characteristics:** Very thick, hyperkeratotic, or pigmented AK lesions should not be treated with the topical patch [TGA].
*   **Co-existing Conditions:** Assessment may be compromised by existing skin diseases (psoriasis, eczema, or malignancies) or tattoos in the treatment area [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Actinic Keratosis (Topical Patch)
1.  **Application:** Apply up to eight patches on individual lesions during a single session.
2.  **Duration:** Patches should remain in place for 4 hours.
3.  **Illumination:** Following patch removal, lesions are exposed to a narrow-band red light (LED source, $630 \pm 3\text{ nm}$) with a total dose of $37\text{ J/cm}^2$.
4.  **Protection:** Both patient and operator must wear protective goggles suited to the lamp spectrum [TGA].

### Malignant Glioma (Oral Solution)
1.  **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute 1,500 mg of powder with 50 mL of drinking water to achieve a concentration of 30 mg ALA HCl per mL.
2.  **Dose:** The recommended dose is 20 mg/kg body weight.
3.  **Timing:** Administered orally 3 hours (range 2 to 4 hours) prior to the induction of anesthesia [FDA].
4.  **Visualization:** Surgery is performed using a surgical microscope equipped with a blue light source (375 to 440 nm) to visualize red fluorescence [FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Topical Route
Nearly all patients (99%) experience local phototoxicity at the treatment site.
*   **During Application/Illumination:** Pruritus, burning, pain, and erythema. In 1% of cases, pain may require early termination of light therapy.
*   **Post-therapy:** Erythema, scabbing, exfoliation, and hyper/hypopigmentation, generally resolving within 1–2 weeks.
*   **Systemic:** Headache is common ($<10\%$) [TGA].

### Oral Route
*   **Systemic ($>1\%$):** Pyrexia, hypotension, nausea, and vomiting.
*   **Neurological:** Related to the surgical procedure, events include aphasia, hemiparesis, seizure, and brain edema in approximately 29% of patients.
*   **Hepatic:** Asymptomatic elevation of liver enzymes (ALT, GGT) within the first week, typically resolving within six weeks without liver failure [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Photosensitizing Agents:** Interaction with other topical or systemic phototoxic agents may enhance the phototoxic reaction.
*   **Supplements/Enzymes:** In vitro data suggest efficacy may be reduced by antioxidants/scavengers like glutathione, melatonin, or N-acetylcysteine [TGA].
*   **Anti-convulsants:** Phenytoin and other anti-convulsants may decrease the intracellular accumulation of the active metabolite [FDA].
*   **Metabolic:** ALA does not inhibit major CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, or 3A) [FDA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**Category B2 [TGA].** There are no adequate or well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies in rabbits showed no embryo-fetal developmental toxicity at doses up to 3 times the human oral dose. Use is not recommended unless the clinical benefit clearly outweighs the potential risk to the fetus [TGA, FDA].

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if ALA is excreted in human milk. 
*   **Conservative Recommendation:** Breastfeeding should be discontinued for **48 hours** following the application of ALA to ensure safety [TGA]. 
*   *Note: The FDA suggests a 24-hour pumping and discarding window.*

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Bioavailability is 100% after oral administration, with peak plasma concentrations (Tmax) reached in 0.8 hours [FDA]. For topical application, Tmax is reached at 4 hours [TGA].
*   **Metabolism:** ALA is the precursor to Protoporphyrin IX (PpIX). Exogenous administration leads to significant accumulation of photoactive PpIX in tumor cells or AK lesions.
*   **Elimination:** The mean half-life of ALA is approximately 0.9 hours. About one-third of an oral dose is excreted unchanged in the urine [FDA]. Excretion is significantly lower ($<2\%$) following topical application [TGA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Aminolevulinic acid is a metabolic precursor of Protoporphyrin IX (PpIX) in the biosynthetic pathway of heme. When administered exogenously, PpIX accumulates intracellularly in rapidly dividing or neoplastic cells (such as glioma tissue or AK lesions). 
*   **Fluorescence:** Under blue light (375-440 nm), PpIX fluoresces red, allowing for surgical visualization [FDA].
*   **Phototoxicity:** Under red light (630 nm) and in the presence of oxygen, PpIX produces singlet oxygen, causing localized oxidative damage and cell death [TGA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at or below 25°C [TGA, FDA].
*   **Stability:** Following reconstitution of the oral solution, it is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature. Following the opening of the dermal patch sachet, patches must be used within 3 months [TGA, FDA].
