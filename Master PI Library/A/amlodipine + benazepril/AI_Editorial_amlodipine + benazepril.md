# Editorial Product Information: amlodipine + benazepril

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **amlodipine + benazepril**
- Chemical class:
  - Amlodipine: dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker (DHP CCB)
  - Benazepril: angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Last revision noted as 6/2026 in available source
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of hypertension in patients not adequately controlled on monotherapy with either amlodipine or benazepril.
- May be used as add‑on therapy for patients uncontrolled on a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker or an ACE inhibitor.
- May be substituted for individually titrated components (replacement therapy).
- If any jurisdictional differences in approved indications exist in other sources, these are not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended initial dose: amlodipine 2.5 mg / benazepril 10 mg orally once daily.
- Titration: dose may be increased according to clinical response up to amlodipine 10 mg / benazepril 40 mg once daily.
- Antihypertensive effect largely attained within 2 weeks of therapy.

### Dosing by indication
- Hypertension: initiate as above only after a patient has:
  - Failed to achieve adequate blood‑pressure control with either amlodipine or benazepril monotherapy, or
  - Required add‑on therapy because amlodipine monotherapy produced edema that limited up‑titration.
- In clinical trials, amlodipine dose response was observed across 2.5–10 mg; benazepril dose response seen in non‑black patient groups at 10–40 mg.

### Administration instructions
- Give orally once daily.
- Individualize dose and adjust according to clinical response.

### Titration schedules
- Increase dose stepwise based on blood pressure response and tolerability up to maximum stated dose (amlodipine 10 mg / benazepril 40 mg once daily).
- Titrate cautiously in patients with hepatic impairment or severe renal impairment.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
- History of angioedema related to previous ACE inhibitor therapy or history of angioedema with or without prior ACE inhibitor exposure.
- Known hypersensitivity to benazepril, amlodipine, any other ACE inhibitor, or to any excipient in the product.
- Concomitant use with neprilysin inhibitors (e.g., sacubitril). Do not administer within 36 hours of switching to or from a neprilysin inhibitor (e.g., sacubitril/valsartan).
- Do not coadminister aliskiren with ACE inhibitors (including amlodipine + benazepril) in patients with diabetes.
- Additional contraindications or regional restrictions are not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / highlighted warning
- **Fetal toxicity:** Drugs that act directly on the renin‑angiotensin system (RAS), including benazepril component, can cause fetal injury and death when administered during pregnancy. When pregnancy is detected, discontinue amlodipine + benazepril as soon as possible.

### General warnings and precautions
- Angioedema and anaphylactoid reactions: angioedema (face, extremities, lips, tongue, glottis, larynx) has been reported and can be life‑threatening. Intestinal angioedema has also been reported. If angioedema occurs, discontinue immediately and treat appropriately (airway management, epinephrine if indicated). Patients with prior angioedema may be at increased risk.
- Anaphylactoid reactions reported during specific procedures: severe reactions reported during hymenoptera venom desensitization, high‑flux dialysis membrane exposure, and LDL apheresis with dextran sulfate absorption in patients on ACE inhibitors.
- Myocardial ischemia/angina: initiation or dose increase of amlodipine may worsen angina or precipitate myocardial infarction in patients with severe obstructive coronary artery disease.
- Hypotension: symptomatic hypotension may occur, especially in volume‑ or salt‑depleted patients. Correct volume depletion prior to initiation of therapy. If hypotension occurs, place patient supine and administer IV fluids as needed.
- Renal function: changes in renal function, including acute renal failure, may occur in patients on agents that affect the RAS. Monitor renal function periodically. Patients with renal artery stenosis, severe heart failure, post‑myocardial infarction, volume depletion, or concomitant NSAID/ARB therapy are at increased risk. Consider withholding or discontinuing therapy if clinically significant deterioration of renal function occurs.
- Hyperkalemia: ACE inhibitor therapy can cause hyperkalemia. Monitor serum potassium periodically, especially in patients with renal insufficiency, diabetes mellitus, or those taking potassium supplements or potassium‑sparing diuretics.
- Hepatic injury: rare reports of cholestatic hepatitis and isolated cases of acute liver failure, some fatal, have been reported with ACE inhibitors. Discontinue if jaundice or marked transaminase elevations develop.
- Drug interaction‑related increased angioedema risk: coadministration of ACE inhibitors with mTOR inhibitors or neprilysin inhibitors may increase angioedema risk.
- Edema: peripheral edema associated with amlodipine is dose‑dependent; addition of benazepril reduces incidence of amlodipine‑associated edema.
- Titrate slowly in patients with impaired hepatic function and in patients with severely impaired renal function.

### Monitoring requirements
- Blood pressure and heart rate (monitor during initiation and dose changes).
- Renal function (serum creatinine, BUN) periodically and after dose changes or when combined with drugs affecting renal function.
- Serum potassium periodically, particularly when used with potassium supplements, potassium‑sparing diuretics, or in patients with renal impairment or diabetes.
- Signs/symptoms of angioedema (including intestinal angioedema); hepatic function tests if clinically indicated; for unexplained jaundice or abnormal liver tests, discontinue ACE inhibitor.

### Specific clinical precautions
- In patients with congestive heart failure, start under close medical supervision and monitor closely for the first 2 weeks and with dose changes of benazepril or diuretic therapy.
- In surgical/anesthetic settings with agents that produce hypotension, ACE inhibitor therapy may blunt angiotensin II formation secondary to compensatory renin release; hypotension can usually be corrected with volume expansion.
- Black patients may have a higher incidence of angioedema with ACE inhibitors.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Simvastatin: coadministration with amlodipine increases systemic exposure of simvastatin. Limit simvastatin dose to no greater than 20 mg daily when coadministered with amlodipine.
- CYP3A4 inhibitors: moderate-to-strong CYP3A4 inhibitors increase amlodipine exposure and may require amlodipine dose reduction; monitor for hypotension and edema.
- CYP3A4 inducers: quantitative effects on amlodipine are not provided; monitor blood pressure when coadministered with CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampicin, St. John’s Wort).

### Pharmacodynamic and other clinically significant interactions
- Potassium supplements and potassium‑sparing diuretics (e.g., spironolactone, amiloride, triamterene): concomitant use with benazepril increases risk of hyperkalemia. Monitor serum potassium frequently if coadministered.
- Lithium: ACE inhibitors have been reported to increase serum lithium levels and produce lithium toxicity. If coadministration is required, monitor lithium levels frequently.
- Injectable gold (sodium aurothiomalate): rare nitritoid reactions (facial flushing, nausea, vomiting, hypotension) have been reported with concomitant ACE inhibitor therapy.
- Nonsteroidal anti‑inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), including selective COX‑2 inhibitors: coadministration may lead to deterioration of renal function (including acute renal failure), particularly in elderly or volume‑depleted patients; may attenuate antihypertensive effect of ACE inhibitors. Monitor renal function; use caution in elderly or volume‑depleted patients.
- Antidiabetic agents (insulin, oral hypoglycemics): rare cases of hypoglycemia reported when combined with ACE inhibitors; advise patients and monitor glucose as appropriate.
- Mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitors (e.g., temsirolimus, sirolimus, everolimus): concomitant ACE inhibitor therapy may increase risk of angioedema.
- Dual blockade of the renin‑angiotensin system (RAS): combined use of ACE inhibitors with ARBs or aliskiren increases risks of hypotension, hyperkalemia, and changes in renal function (including acute renal failure). Avoid combined use of RAS inhibitors in general. Specifically:
  - Do not coadminister aliskiren with ACE inhibitors (amlodipine + benazepril) in patients with diabetes.
  - Avoid aliskiren with amlodipine + benazepril in patients with renal impairment (GFR < 60 mL/min) — monitor and avoid per available guidance.
- Neprilysin inhibitors (e.g., sacubitril): coadministration increases risk of angioedema; combination is contraindicated. Do not administer within 36 hours of switching to or from a neprilysin inhibitor.

### Management notes
- Monitor blood pressure, renal function, and serum potassium when combining with agents that affect renal function or potassium balance.
- Adjust doses or discontinue interacting agents as clinically indicated.
- Use caution and clinical monitoring when combining with CYP3A4 inhibitors or substrates with narrow therapeutic indices (e.g., simvastatin).

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Risk summary:** Use of drugs that act on the renin‑angiotensin system during the second and third trimesters can reduce fetal renal function and increase fetal and neonatal morbidity and death (oligohydramnios, pulmonary hypoplasia, skeletal deformations, neonatal renal failure, hypotension, anuria, death). When pregnancy is detected, discontinue amlodipine + benazepril as soon as possible.
- Estimated background risk of major birth defects/miscarriage in general population: Not relevant to product-specific risk and general background estimates provided in available sources (general U.S. population estimates were cited in source).

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Amlodipine:** a long‑acting dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker that inhibits calcium influx into vascular smooth muscle and cardiac muscle, producing vasodilation and lowering blood pressure.
- **Benazepril:** an ACE inhibitor that blocks conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II, resulting in vasodilation, decreased aldosterone secretion, and reduction in blood pressure.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Antihypertensive effect of the combination is achieved largely within 2 weeks.
- Antihypertensive effects increase with increasing doses of amlodipine across 2.5–10 mg; benazepril dose‑response observed in non‑black patient groups at 10–40 mg.
- Addition of benazepril to amlodipine reduces incidence of amlodipine‑induced peripheral edema.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Specific pharmacokinetic parameters (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination) for the combination are not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Safety and efficacy data derive from pooled analyses of randomized, placebo‑controlled trials and other clinical studies:
  - Amlodipine + benazepril evaluated for safety in over 2,991 hypertensive patients; over 500 treated for at least 6 months and over 400 treated for more than 1 year.
  - Pooled analysis of five placebo‑controlled trials (doses up to amlodipine 5 mg / benazepril 20 mg) showed adverse events generally mild and transient; discontinuation due to adverse reactions occurred in approximately 4% of patients on combination therapy vs 3% on placebo. Most common reasons for discontinuation were cough and edema.
  - Antihypertensive effects increased with higher amlodipine dose across patient groups; increased benazepril dose produced greater effect in non‑black patients.
- Detailed trial designs, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and primary endpoint data for individual pivotal trials are not specified in available sources.
