# Editorial Product Information: Apremilast

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration). 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of apremilast clinical data for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises regulatory information to provide a comprehensive reference for clinical practice.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Apremilast, a selective small-molecule inhibitor of phosphodiesterase 4 (PDE4).
*   **Brand Name:** **Otezla** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** L04AA32.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   10 mg film-coated tablets (pink, diamond-shaped, engraved with "APR" and "10") [Australian].
    *   20 mg film-coated tablets (brown, diamond-shaped, engraved with "APR" and "20") [Australian].
    *   30 mg film-coated tablets (beige, diamond-shaped, engraved with "APR" and "30") [Australian].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amgen Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Psoriatic Arthritis:** Treatment of signs and symptoms of active psoriatic arthritis in adult patients [Australian].
*   **Plaque Psoriasis:** Treatment of adult patients with plaque psoriasis who are candidates for phototherapy or systemic therapy [Australian].
*   **Behçet’s Disease:** Treatment of adult patients with oral ulcers associated with Behçet’s Disease who are candidates for systemic therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The standard maintenance dose is 30 mg taken orally twice daily, approximately 12 hours apart.

### Administration Instructions
Tablets must be swallowed whole and should not be crushed, split, or chewed. They may be taken with or without food.

### Titration Schedule
To reduce gastrointestinal symptoms, an initial titration is required. 
*   **Day 1:** 10 mg (AM)
*   **Day 2:** 10 mg (AM), 10 mg (PM)
*   **Day 3:** 10 mg (AM), 20 mg (PM)
*   **Day 4:** 20 mg (AM), 20 mg (PM)
*   **Day 5:** 20 mg (AM), 30 mg (PM)
*   **Day 6 and thereafter:** 30 mg (AM), 30 mg (PM)
No re-titration is necessary following the initial period.

### Missed Dose
The missed dose should be taken as soon as possible. If it is nearly time for the next scheduled dose, the missed dose should be skipped, and the regular schedule resumed. Doses should not be doubled.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Hypersensitivity to apremilast or any of the inactive tablet excipients.
*   **Pregnancy** [Australian].
*   **Nursing women** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Weight Decrease:** Treatment is associated with weight loss. Weight should be monitored regularly. If significant or unexplained weight loss occurs, the patient should be evaluated and discontinuation considered.
*   **Depression:** An increase in depression, suicidal ideation, and mood changes has been observed. Risks and benefits must be weighed carefully in patients with a history of depression or suicidal behaviour. Caregivers and patients should be advised to monitor for emerging psychiatric symptoms.
*   **Severe Gastrointestinal Events:** Post-marketing reports include severe diarrhoea, nausea, and vomiting, often occurring within the first few weeks of therapy. Patients ≥65 years and those taking medications that cause volume depletion or hypotension are at higher risk of complications. Dose reduction or suspension may be required.
*   **Lactose Intolerance:** Tablets contain lactose. Patients with rare hereditary conditions such as galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption should not use this product.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions typically occur within the first two weeks of treatment and often resolve within four weeks.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea, Nausea |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vomiting, Upper respiratory tract infection, Tension headache, Headache, Nasopharyngitis, Bronchitis, Insomnia, Fatigue, Dyspepsia, Decreased appetite, Abdominal pain upper, GERD, Back pain, Cough |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypersensitivity, Rash, Weight decrease |

*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** In Behçet’s Disease trials, the incidence of serious adverse reactions was low (2.9%) [Australian]. Severe diarrhoea and nausea were reported in approximately 0.3% of patients in psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis trials.
*   **Long-term Safety:** Safety profiles remained consistent for treatment durations up to 5 years.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
*   **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Co-administration with strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, phenobarbitone, carbamazepine, St. John’s Wort) significantly decreases apremilast exposure (e.g., rifampicin reduces AUC by 72% and Cmax by 43%). This may result in a loss of clinical efficacy.
*   **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Ketoconazole increases apremilast AUC by 36%, which is not considered clinically meaningful; no dose adjustment is required.
*   **Methotrexate and Oral Contraceptives:** No clinically relevant interactions were observed; apremilast may be co-administered with these agents.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
*   Apremilast has not been studied in combination with cyclosporin or biologic therapies (e.g., TNF-blockers).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Contraindicated** [Australian]. Classified as **Category B3** [Australian]. Animal studies (mice and monkeys) showed increased prenatal loss and reduced fetal weight at exposures >1.3-fold the clinical dose. Available human data from registries are insufficient to exclude a risk of fetal loss.
*   **Breastfeeding:** **Contraindicated** [Australian]. Apremilast was detected in the milk of lactating mice. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk.
*   **Fertility:** No effects on male fertility were observed in animal models (up to 3-fold clinical exposure). In female mice, prolonged oestrous cycles were noted at exposures 1.0-fold clinical levels, though pregnancy rates remained unaffected.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years of age.
*   **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required. Exposure (AUC) is approximately 13% higher in subjects aged 65–85, but no significant safety or efficacy differences were observed compared to younger adults.
*   **Renal Impairment:**
    *   **Mild to Moderate:** No dose adjustment required.
    *   **Severe (CrCl < 30 mL/min):** Reduce dose to 30 mg once daily. For titration, use only the AM doses and skip PM doses.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Apremilast inhibits PDE4, the dominant cAMP-specific phosphodiesterase in inflammatory cells. Inhibition increases intracellular cAMP, which down-regulates pro-inflammatory mediators (TNF-α, IL-17, IL-23) and up-regulates anti-inflammatory mediators (IL-10).
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Bioavailability is ~73%; Tmax is ~2.5 hours. Absorption is not affected by food.
    *   **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is ~68%. Mean Vd is 87 L.
    *   **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via CYP (primarily CYP3A4, with minor CYP1A2 and CYP2A6) and non-CYP pathways (oxidation, hydrolysis, conjugation).
    *   **Elimination:** Plasma clearance is ~10 L/hr; terminal half-life is ~9 hours. Approximately 58% is recovered in urine and 39% in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Psoriatic Arthritis (PALACE 1, 2, 3, & 4):** Trials demonstrated significant ACR 20 responses at Week 16 compared to placebo, regardless of prior DMARD or biologic use. Improvements were also seen in physical function (HAQ-DI) and skin manifestations.
*   **Plaque Psoriasis (ESTEEM 1 & 2; ADVANCE; STYLE):** Otezla showed significant PASI-75 responses at Week 16 in moderate-to-severe disease (ESTEEM) and significant sPGA responses in mild-to-moderate disease (ADVANCE). STYLE trial showed efficacy in scalp-specific psoriasis.
*   **Behçet’s Disease (RELIEF):** Significant reduction in the number of oral ulcers (AUC) and ulcer pain (VAS) compared to placebo through Week 12.
