# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized, clinically focused editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulations and full prescribing information before administration.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients diagnosed with an inborn error of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often utilized, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered based on clinical requirement [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for discoloration or particulate matter prior to use [US].
- The formulation contains no preservatives; any unused portion must be discarded [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical care [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

### Serious and Other Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (both new-onset and exacerbated) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and transient nausea/vomiting [US].
- **Other:** Body odor has been reported with levocarnitine use [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Classified as use during pregnancy only if clearly needed. Animal reproductive studies (rats and rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal constituent of human milk. While specific studies on supplementation in nursing mothers are unavailable, studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have been established; dosing is weight-based (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, dose selection should generally be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients, intravenous levocarnitine is indicated to treat deficiency. However, chronic high doses of **oral** levocarnitine should be avoided in patients with severely compromised renal function due to the potential accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities resulting from the accumulation of toxic organic acids and restores plasma carnitine levels [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg thrice weekly post-dialysis) demonstrated an increase in mean trough plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While the ability to increase carnitine concentrations is proven, the definitive effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
