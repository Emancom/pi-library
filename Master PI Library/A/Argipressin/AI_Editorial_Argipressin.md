# Editorial Product Information: Argipressin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Synthetic argipressin (8-arginine argipressin).
- **Chemical Class:** Polypeptide hormone (antidiuretic hormone).
- **Brand Names:** Argipressin Medsurge [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H01BA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 20 pressor units per 1 mL vial [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intramuscular (i.m.) or Subcutaneous (s.c.). **Intravenous (i.v.) administration is not recommended.**
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Medsurge Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Postoperative Abdominal Distention:** Used for both the prevention and treatment of distention. This indication also extends to distention complicating acute toxaemias or pneumonia [Australian].
- **Abdominal Radiography:** To dispel gas shadows that may interfere with imaging results [Australian].
- **Diabetes Insipidus:** For the management of polyuria and polydipsia associated with the deficiency of antidiuretic hormone [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- Individual doses administered via i.m. or s.c. routes should **not exceed 0.75 mL** (15 units) [Australian].
- For paediatric use, dosages should be appropriately reduced based on age and clinical status [Australian].

### Postoperative Abdominal Distention
- **Adult Dosing:** Initially 0.25 mL (5 units) via i.m. or s.c. injection.
- **Titration:** Dose may be increased to 0.5 mL (10 units) for subsequent injections if required.
- **Frequency:** Injections may be repeated at 3 to 4-hour intervals as clinically indicated [Australian].

### Abdominal Radiography
- **Standard Regimen:** Two doses of 0.5 mL (10 units) each, administered via i.m. or s.c. injection.
- **Timing:** The first dose is typically given 2.5 hours before imaging, and the second dose 30 minutes before imaging.
- **Radiological Advice:** An enema may be recommended prior to the first dose [Australian].

### Diabetes Insipidus
- **Dosing:** 0.25 mL to 0.5 mL (5 to 10 units) administered i.m. or s.c.
- **Frequency:** Repeated 2 to 3 times daily as needed for symptom control [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to argipressin or any excipients [Australian].
- **Renal Disease:** Chronic nephritis accompanied by nitrogen retention is a contraindication until blood nitrogen levels have returned to a reasonable range [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Vascular Disease:** Use with extreme caution in patients with vascular disease, particularly coronary artery disease. Small doses may trigger anginal pain; higher doses may precipitate myocardial infarction [Australian].
- **Water Intoxication:** Argipressin may lead to water intoxication. Clinicians should monitor for early signs such as listlessness, drowsiness, and headache to prevent progression to convulsions and coma [Australian].
- **Administration Route:** Avoid i.v. administration. Maintain subcutaneous or intramuscular doses below 0.75 mL [Australian].
- **Specific Clinical Conditions:** Use with caution in patients with:
    - Epilepsy, migraine, or asthma.
    - Hypertension associated with nephritis.
    - Goitre with cardiac complications.
    - Coronary thrombosis, angina pectoris, or arteriosclerosis.
    - Any condition where a rapid increase in extracellular water could overburden the cardiovascular or renal system (e.g., toxaemia of pregnancy) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following adverse effects have been reported; frequencies are not specified in the available source [Australian]:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General/Allergic** | Local or systemic allergic reactions, sweating, urticaria, anaphylaxis (including cardiac arrest/shock) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Arrhythmias, decreased cardiac output, angina, myocardial ischaemia, peripheral vasoconstriction, gangrene |
| **Neurological** | Tremor, vertigo, "pounding" in the head, circumoral pallor |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, passage of gas |
| **Respiratory** | Bronchial constriction |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Rhabdomyolysis |
| **Dermatological** | Cutaneous gangrene |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Potentiating Agents:** The antidiuretic effect of argipressin may be increased by concurrent use of carbamazepine, fludrocortisone, tricyclic antidepressants, or urea [Australian].
- **Inhibiting Agents:** The antidiuretic effect may be reduced by concurrent use of alcohol, demeclocycline, heparin, lithium, or noradrenaline [Australian].
- **Ganglionic Blocking Agents:** May cause a significant increase in sensitivity to the pressor (vasoconstrictive) effects of argipressin [Australian].
- **H2 Antagonists:** Isolated cases of heart block and severe bradycardia have been reported when used alongside argipressin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Classified as **Category B2** [Australian]. (Medicines taken by a limited number of pregnant women without observed increases in malformations; animal studies are inadequate but available data show no evidence of increased foetal damage). Use with caution in toxaemia of pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety data and recommendations regarding use during lactation are not specified in available sources [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data regarding the effects of argipressin on fertility are not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosage should be reduced; however, specific pharmacokinetic or safety data are not specified in available sources [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data or dose adjustment requirements for the elderly are not specified in available sources [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in chronic nephritis with nitrogen retention until nitrogen levels improve. Use with caution in other forms of nephritis with hypertension [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Data regarding use in hepatic impairment are not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Antidiuretic:** Increases the resorption of water by the renal tubules.
    - **Vasopressor:** Induces contraction of smooth muscle throughout the vascular bed, specifically in capillaries, arterioles, and venules. This effect is direct and not prevented by vascular denervation or adrenergic blockers.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Stimulates contraction of smooth muscle in the gastrointestinal tract [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The hormone acts directly on contractile elements of the smooth musculature. It has a less pronounced effect on the smooth muscle of large veins [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate) [Australian].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Protect the solution from light [Australian].
