# Editorial Product Information: alanine + arginine + glycine + histidine + isoleucine + leucine + lysine + methionine + phenylalanine + proline + serine + taurine + threonine + tryptophan + tyrosine + valine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **alanine + arginine + glycine + histidine + isoleucine + leucine + lysine + methionine + phenylalanine + proline + serine + taurine + threonine + tryptophan + tyrosine + valine**
- Chemical class: Parenteral amino acid solution for nutritional support (mixture of essential and non-essential amino acids)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous infusion (central venous administration is the recommended route for continuous infusion; peripheral infusion also reported in clinical studies).

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: 
  - Registration of a 15% amino acid solution containing these amino acids was reported in January 1999. [Australian]
  - Registration of a three‑chamber product containing a 10% amino acid solution was reported in January 2003. [Australian]
  - Registration of a three‑chamber peripheral 10% product was reported in October 2003. [Australian]
- Date of last revision/update of source PI where stated: Periodic Safety Update Report (PSUR) referenced dated July 2006 in the available sources.
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- To supply amino acids as part of a composite admixture for Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN).
- Use is described as part of parenteral nutrition regimens for patients requiring intravenous amino acid supplementation.  
- Jurisdictional differences in marketing authorisations for specific concentrations and multi‑component (three‑chamber) products are reported in the source documents. If a specific concentration or multi‑component formulation is considered, consult the appropriate regional labelling. [Australian]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Typical daily amino acid provision commonly used: 1.0–1.5 g amino acids per kg body weight per day.
- Clinical trial dosing (reported): 1.0–2.0 g amino acids per kg body weight per day (equivalent to 10–20 mL/kg/day of a 10% solution where applicable).
- Example: For a 70 kg adult, 700–1400 mL/day of a 10% solution provides approximately 70–140 g amino acids/day.

### Maximum rates and doses
- Maximum infusion rate (reported): 1.0 mL/kg/h of a 10% solution (equivalent to 0.1 g amino acids/kg/h).
- Maximum daily dose (reported): 20 mL/kg/day of a 10% solution (equivalent to 2.0 g amino acids/kg/day). Example: 140 g amino acids/day for a 70 kg adult.

### Paediatric dosing
- Children and adolescents (2–18 years): Dosing should be individualized according to hydration, developmental status and body weight; commonly 10–20 mL/kg/day (equivalent to 1.0–2.0 g amino acids/kg/day) is used.
- Children < 2 years: **Contraindicated** (see Section 5). Use age‑appropriate paediatric amino acid preparations formulated for infants when indicated.

### Administration instructions
- Intended for intravenous infusion; continuous infusion via a central vein is recommended for typical TPN use.
- Use aseptic technique for any additions (trace elements, electrolytes, vitamins); perform admixture immediately prior to infusion.
- Contains no antimicrobial preservative; solutions are for single patient, single use only. Discard unused contents.
- When admixtures are prepared, use under strictly aseptic conditions (for example, laminar air flow) and follow local hospital compounding procedures.

### Admixture compatibility and sequence
- Documented compatibility (for 1000 mL of a 10% amino acid solution) with:
  - Intravenous fat emulsions (10%, 20%, 30% preparations reported)
  - Fat‑soluble and water‑soluble vitamin preparations (examples reported)
  - Glucose solutions (20%–50% reported)
  - Electrolyte additions up to specified amounts:
    - Sodium up to 150 mmol
    - Potassium up to 150 mmol
    - Calcium up to 5 mmol
    - Magnesium up to 5 mmol
    - Chloride 286 mmol
    - Organic phosphate 15 mmol
  - Trace elements in reported amounts (examples given for Cr, Cu, Fe, Mn, I, F, Mo, Se, Zn)
- Recommended admixing sequence (paraphrased):
  1. Add trace elements and phosphate‑free electrolytes to the amino acid solution.
  2. Add phosphate‑containing electrolytes to the glucose solution (do not add phosphate directly to the amino acid bag if the sequence would risk precipitation).
  3. Combine the amino acid/trace element solution and the glucose/electrolyte solution into the final bag.
  4. Reconstitute multicomponent vitamin preparations as required and mix with the lipid emulsion.
  5. Add the lipid/vitamin mixture to the final bag.
  6. Gently mix the combined admixture.
- Practical cautions: Do not mix glucose directly into lipid emulsion; dilute electrolytes in amino acid and glucose solutions prior to mixing with the emulsion. If creaming occurs when electrolytes are added to emulsions, invert or gently shake; if homogeneity is not restored, do not use. Precipitation of calcium phosphate is possible—follow established compatibility checks.

### Storage after preparation
- Use immediately after opening with sterile transfer equipment.
- If storage is necessary, prepared admixtures may be held at 2–8°C for up to 24 hours; vitamins and trace elements should be added just prior to administration if refrigerated storage is used.

### Titration and missed doses
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Disturbances of amino acid metabolism (any inborn errors of metabolism affecting amino acid handling).
- Metabolic acidosis.
- Renal insufficiency unless patient is receiving haemodialysis or haemofiltration.
- Advanced liver insufficiency.
- Fluid overload.
- Shock.
- Hypoxia.
- Decompensated heart failure.
- Children less than 2 years of age: **administration of a 10% adult amino acid solution is contraindicated**; paediatric‑specific amino acid preparations should be used.
- Any other contraindications specific to particular formulations or co‑components (e.g., electrolytes, lipids) should be checked in the regional product labelling. [Australian]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Highlighted warnings
- Not specified in available sources as a separate boxed warning; the following critical cautions are emphasized in the product information and should be regarded as high‑priority clinical warnings:
  - **Do not administer to children < 2 years** (contraindication).
  - **Administer as part of a comprehensive TPN regimen only with appropriate monitoring** (see monitoring requirements below).

### General warnings and precautions
- Use only as part of a comprehensive TPN regimen that accounts for individual patient needs and organ function.
- Assess tolerance and need for TPN prior to initiation; dosing must consider metabolic state and organ function.
- Monitor vital signs and clinical status during infusion.
- Monitor serum electrolytes, fluid balance and renal function regularly during administration.
- Frequent laboratory monitoring should include blood and urine glucose, serum proteins, kidney and liver function tests, serum electrolytes, full blood count, serum and urine osmolarities, and blood urea.
- Check triglycerides when lipids are administered concurrently; the amino acid solution itself does not alter triglyceride levels, but concurrent lipid administration does.
- Exercise caution when administering large infusion volumes to patients with cardiac insufficiency to avoid pulmonary oedema.
- In hypokalaemia and/or hyponatraemia, provide adequate potassium and/or sodium supplementation concurrently.
- Amino acid infusion may precipitate or worsen folate deficiency; daily folic acid supplementation is recommended in at‑risk patients.
- TPN without any oral/enteral intake may lead to jaundice due to cholestasis; small enteral feeding when tolerated may help prevent or reverse this.
- Excessive glucose and/or lipid administration may lead to hepatic steatosis.
- Hypertonic TPN regimens increase the risk of thrombophlebitis with peripheral infusions; central venous access reduces this risk but carries other risks (see below).
- Central venous catheterisation carries risks of central thrombosis and embolism.
- Extravasation of hyperosmolar TPN admixtures can cause tissue damage; severity depends on osmolarity, volume extravasated and anatomic location. Consider dilution measures in large extravasations if clinically appropriate.
- TPN should not be administered in patients with septic shock; withhold TPN in early sepsis and investigate cause. If TPN is indicated during stable chronic sepsis, monitor vital signs and laboratories more frequently.
- Hypersensitivity to components of the full TPN admixture (not specifically to the amino acid solution) may occur; no labelled allergic reactions to the amino acid solution itself were reported in the available data.
- Rapid infusion of amino acid solution given separately from other nutrients may cause nausea; slow or stop the infusion if nausea occurs.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic monitoring as above (electrolytes, renal and hepatic function, glucose, triglycerides, haematology).
- Monitor for signs of fluid overload, catheter‑related complications, sepsis, and infusion site reactions.
- Monitor for signs of folate deficiency during prolonged use.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use in hepatic impairment: see contraindications for advanced liver insufficiency; otherwise exercise clinical judgement. [Australian]
- Use in renal impairment: contraindicated unless patient is receiving dialysis/haemofiltration (see contraindications). [Australian]
- Use in the elderly: no specific studies reported; monitor clinically and adjust dosing based on organ function.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: No interactions are shown to date in the available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Incompatibilities/intramixing interactions:
  - Compatibility with a range of lipid emulsions, vitamins, glucose concentrations, electrolytes and trace elements is reported (see Section 4 Dosage and Administration for specific compatibility lists and recommended admixing sequence).
  - Precipitation of calcium phosphate is possible when calcium and phosphate ions are both added—follow compatibility checks and recommended sequence to minimise risk.
- Management notes: Additives and admixtures must be performed aseptically immediately before infusion; any residual contents/discarded admixtures should be discarded to minimise microbiological hazard.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - No specific reproductive or pregnancy studies with this amino acid solution are reported in the available sources.
  - Clinical experience with similar parenteral amino acid solutions reported in the sources indicates no evidence of risk during pregnancy, but formal data are lacking. The risk–benefit relationship should be considered before administering the amino acid solution during pregnancy.
- Breastfeeding:
  - No specific lactation studies with this product are reported. Clinical experience with similar parenteral amino acid solutions indicates no evidence of risk during breastfeeding. Consider the risk–benefit relationship before use during lactation.
- Fertility:
  - No specific studies on fertility are reported in the available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Children aged 2–18 years: Dosing guidance reported as 10–20 mL/kg/day (equivalent to 1.0–2.0 g amino acids/kg/day) for most paediatric patients, with dose adjustment according to hydration status, biological development and body weight.
- Children < 2 years: **Contraindicated** — paediatric formulations specifically designed for infants should be used instead. [Australian]

### Elderly
- No specific studies have been reported in the available sources. Exercise clinical caution, monitor organ function and adjust dosing as clinically indicated.

### Renal impairment
- Contraindicated in renal insufficiency unless the patient is receiving haemodialysis or haemofiltration. If dialysis/haemofiltration is in place, use may be considered with appropriate monitoring. [Australian]

### Hepatic impairment
- Contraindicated in advanced liver insufficiency. Use caution in hepatic dysfunction and consider monitoring liver function tests. [Australian]

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Duration of clinical trial data in post‑operative patients is limited to up to 7 days of administration as described in the available sources.
- Key clinical trial datasets and design features reported in the source material (paraphrased):
  - Several comparative and non‑comparative clinical studies reported involving central and peripheral venous infusion of amino acid solutions (including 10% and 15% concentrations) and multi‑chamber admixture products combining amino acids, glucose and lipid emulsions.
  - Reported trial codes and designs included central vein infusion and peripheral vein infusion studies with small sample sizes (example sample sizes included n=16, n=19, n=20, n=26, n=27 in various arms).
  - Trials compared the amino acid solution (10% or 15% concentration) in composite admixtures with comparator amino acid products and multi‑component bags; adverse events, laboratory abnormalities and surgical/procedural outcomes were reported.
  - Several laboratory and clinical adverse events were captured across studies (see Section 7 Adverse Effects). Many reported events appeared related to the underlying surgical population and perioperative course; causal assessment in the original reports frequently judged events as unrelated or unlikely related to amino acid administration.
  - Post‑marketing safety reporting included a small number of serious adverse reactions in specific patient cohorts (for example, patients receiving intradialytic parenteral nutrition) with most serious events assessed as not or unlikely related; one serious metabolic/nutrition disorder was possibly related where underlying diabetes was a major contributing factor.

- Primary outcomes and detailed efficacy endpoints: Not specified in available sources beyond general tolerability and safety observations in the trial reports.
