**Editorial Note:** This document represents a unified Product Information (PI) summary for multivitamin preparations containing ascorbic acid. It integrates data from the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA, Australia) regarding parenteral formulations (Cernevit) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA, United States) regarding oral prescription supplements (Altriva). Information has been synthesized to provide healthcare professionals with a comprehensive overview of safety, administration, and pharmacological profiles across delivery routes.

# Unified Product Information: Ascorbic Acid and Multivitamin Complexes

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
This PI covers multivitamin formulations available as both lyophilised powder for intravenous (IV) use and oral chewable tablets. 

**Active Ingredients (representative):**
*   **Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C):** 120 mg to 125 mg.
*   **Fat-Soluble Vitamins:** Retinol (A), Colecalciferol (D3), and dl-alpha-tocopherol (E).
*   **Water-Soluble Vitamins:** Thiamine (B1), Riboflavin (B2), Nicotinamide/Niacinamide (B3), Dexpanthenol (B5), Pyridoxine (B6), Biotin (B7), Folic Acid/Folate (B9), and Cyanocobalamin (B12).
*   **Minerals:** Certain oral formulations may also include Ferrous Fumarate (Iron) [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Parenteral Administration:** Indicated for adults and children over 11 years of age to prevent or correct vitamin deficiencies when oral nutrition is contraindicated, insufficient, or impossible [TGA].
*   **Oral Administration:** Indicated as a prescription dietary supplement to ensure adequate nutritional intake and prevent deficiencies of key vitamins and minerals [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to any active ingredient or excipient.
*   **Specific Allergies:** Patients with a known hypersensitivity to thiamine or soy/peanut protein products (due to lecithin components in some IV formulations) [TGA].
*   **Pre-existing Conditions:** Existing hypervitaminosis, impaired hepatic function, or hyperparathyroidism (due to potential hypercalcemic complications from Vitamin D) [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Allergic Reactions:** Severe systemic hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported with B-complex vitamins. Mild symptoms like sneezing or respiratory distress may precede anaphylactic shock. If a reaction occurs, the infusion or oral intake must be ceased immediately [TGA, FDA].
*   **Pediatric Safety (Oral):** Accidental overdose of iron-containing products is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under six. Products containing iron must be kept out of reach of children [FDA].
*   **Vitamin Toxicity:** Chronic or high-dose intake can lead to toxicity, particularly with fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, and E). Monitoring is essential for patients on long-term supplementation or those receiving vitamins from multiple sources [TGA].
*   **B12 Deficiency Masking:** Folic acid dosages above 0.1 mg may improve hematological parameters in patients with Vitamin B12 deficiency (pernicious anemia) while allowing neurological damage to progress. B12 status should be evaluated before or during long-term therapy [TGA, FDA].
*   **Refeeding Syndrome:** In severely malnourished patients, rapid reintroduction of nutrients can trigger shifts in electrolytes. Patients should be monitored closely while intake is gradually increased [TGA].
*   **Parenteral Precipitates:** Pulmonary vascular precipitates may occur in IV lines, especially when calcium and phosphates are added to the solution. Infusion sets should be inspected regularly [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
*   **Oral (Adults):** Typically one chewable tablet daily or twice daily, or as directed by a licensed practitioner [FDA].
*   **Intravenous (Adults and Children >11 years):** One vial daily, reconstituted with 5 mL of compatible diluent (e.g., Water for Injection, 0.9% Saline). 
*   **Administration Rate:** IV injections must be administered slowly over at least 10 minutes. If used as an infusion, the solution must be thoroughly mixed [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Immune System:** Urticaria, rash, respiratory distress, and rare cases of cardiac arrest [TGA, FDA].
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal discomfort.
*   **Hepatobiliary:** Transient increases in liver enzymes (ALT, SGPT) and bile acids have been observed, particularly following bolus IV injection in patients with inflammatory bowel disease [TGA].
*   **General:** Injection site pain, metallic taste (dysgeusia), tachycardia, and flushing [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Folate Antagonists/Antiepileptics:** Folic acid may decrease serum concentrations of phenytoin, phenobarbital, and primidone, potentially increasing seizure risk [TGA, FDA].
*   **Anticoagulants:** Vitamin E can potentiate the effects of Vitamin K antagonists (e.g., warfarin), increasing the risk of bleeding [TGA].
*   **Antibiotics:** Certain vitamins may reduce the efficacy of tetracyclines or bleomycin [TGA].
*   **Vitamin C/Iron:** High-dose Vitamin C supplementation can increase iron mobilization, which may be hazardous in patients using deferoxamine [TGA].
*   **Laboratory Interference:** Ascorbic acid may interfere with blood and urine glucose testing, leading to false readings [TGA]. Biotin may also interfere with streptavidin-based immunoassays (e.g., thyroid or troponin tests) [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Classified as Category D [TGA]. While vitamins are essential, high doses of Vitamin A (exceeding 10,000 IU/day) have been associated with teratogenic effects. Use should only occur under strict medical supervision where the benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is not confirmed if all components are excreted in human milk. Due to the high vitamin concentrations, use in lactating women is generally not recommended unless deemed necessary by a clinician [TGA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Bioavailability is 100% for the IV route [TGA]. Oral absorption occurs via active and passive transport mechanisms in the gastrointestinal tract.
*   **Distribution/Metabolism:** Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E) are primarily stored in the liver. Water-soluble vitamins (B-complex, C) act as co-enzymes in various metabolic pathways.
*   **Excretion:** Excess water-soluble vitamins are readily excreted in the urine. Fat-soluble vitamins are metabolized and excreted more slowly, increasing the potential for accumulation [TGA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
This multivitamin complex provides a physiological balance of essential nutrients. 
*   **Ascorbic Acid:** Essential for collagen formation and tissue repair.
*   **Vitamin A:** Critical for retinal function and epithelial integrity.
*   **Vitamin D:** Regulates bone formation and mineral homeostasis.
*   **B-Vitamins:** Serve as essential co-factors in energy metabolism and the synthesis of nucleic acids and neurotransmitters [TGA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Parenteral:** Store below 25°C and protect from light. Once reconstituted, the solution should be used immediately or stored at 2°C to 8°C for no more than 24 hours [TGA].
*   **Oral:** Store at controlled room temperature (20°C to 25°C). Protect from excessive heat and moisture to prevent surface discoloration of the tablets [FDA].
