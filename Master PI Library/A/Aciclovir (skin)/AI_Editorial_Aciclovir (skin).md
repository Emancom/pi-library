# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine (Intravenous) for use by healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data, dosing protocols, and safety information into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the current PI approved in their specific region before administration.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer exclusively via the **intravenous** route [US].
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h) during acute treatment [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Timing:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on levels [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before starting therapy.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately and do not re-administer if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Oral Formulation Restriction:** The safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Frequent Reactions
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Gastritis has also been reported [US].
- **Body Odor:** A "fishy" body odor may occur during therapy [US].

### ESRD Clinical Trial Data (≥5% Incidence)
The following events were reported in controlled trials of dialysis patients, regardless of causality [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (30–49%), headache (22%), fever (7–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (14–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (9–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress (bronchospasm) [US].
- New-onset or worsening seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when co-administered with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned in source text, but described as follows]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal constituent of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, milk carnitine levels increase with exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (see Section 3). Specific age-range limitations are not provided in the source [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine different responses compared to younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing is specifically adjusted for ESRD patients post-dialysis to account for dialytic losses (see Section 3) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urinary) and γ-butyrobetaine (fecal) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis showed that trough carnitine levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C).
- **Preservative Status:** Preservative-free; discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial [US].
