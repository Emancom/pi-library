# Editorial Product Information: Anifrolumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a coherent reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any specific region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Anifrolumab (human immunoglobulin G1 kappa [IgG1κ] monoclonal antibody).
- **Brand Name:** **SAPHNELO** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Concentrated solution for infusion (Vial):** 300 mg of anifrolumab in 2.0 mL (150 mg/mL) [Australian].
    - **Solution for injection (Pre-filled pen):** 120 mg of anifrolumab in 0.8 mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion or Subcutaneous (SC) injection [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AstraZeneca Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):** Indicated as an add-on therapy for adult patients with moderate to severe, active SLE despite receiving standard therapy [Australian].
- **Limitations of Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients with severe active lupus nephritis or severe active central nervous system lupus [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Intravenous:** 300 mg administered via IV infusion over 30 minutes every 4 weeks [Australian].
- **Subcutaneous:** 120 mg administered once weekly [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Intravenous:** Must be diluted in 50 mL or 100 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride. Administer via an IV line containing a sterile, low-protein binding 0.2 to 15 micron filter. Flush the line with 25 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride post-infusion. Do not administer as a bolus [Australian].
- **Subcutaneous:** Injected into the thigh or abdomen (avoiding 5 cm around the navel). If administered by a professional, the upper arm may be used. Rotate sites and ensure the site is at least 3 cm from the previous injection area [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- **Intravenous:** Administer as soon as possible. Maintain a minimum of 14 days between IV doses [Australian].
- **Subcutaneous:** Administer as soon as remembered. The patient may then begin a new weekly schedule from that day or resume their usual day, provided there is a minimum of 3 days between injections [Australian].

### Transitioning Between Routes
- **IV to SC:** Administer the first SC dose approximately 2 weeks after the final IV dose [Australian].
- **SC to IV:** Administer the first IV infusion approximately 3 to 4 weeks after the final SC dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to anifrolumab or any of the excipients (histidine, L-histidine hydrochloride monohydrate, L-lysine hydrochloride, trehalose dihydrate, polysorbate 80) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis and angioedema, have been reported. If a serious reaction occurs, discontinue administration immediately and initiate treatment [Australian].
- **Infections:** Anifrolumab increases the risk of respiratory infections and herpes zoster. Treatment should not be initiated during a clinically significant active infection. Caution is advised in patients with chronic or recurrent infections [Australian].
- **Tuberculosis (TB):** Patients should be evaluated for TB. Anti-TB therapy should be considered for untreated latent TB prior to starting anifrolumab. Do not administer to patients with active TB [Australian].
- **Immunisations:** Avoid concurrent use of live or attenuated vaccines. Update all necessary immunisations according to guidelines before starting therapy [Australian].
- **Malignancy:** Immunomodulators may increase malignancy risk. The impact of anifrolumab on malignancy development is unknown; caution is required in patients with a history of malignancy [Australian].
- **Concomitant Biologics:** Use in combination with other biologic therapies (e.g., B-cell targeted therapies) is not recommended as it has not been studied [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily based on 52-week clinical trials.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Upper respiratory tract infection, Bronchitis |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Herpes zoster, Respiratory tract infection (viral/bacterial), Hypersensitivity, Infusion-related reactions (IV only) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anaphylactic reaction |
| **Not Known** | Arthralgia (post-marketing) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** The most common serious adverse reaction was herpes zoster (0.4%). Disseminated and multidermatomal herpes zoster have been reported [Australian].
- **Infusion Reactions:** Generally mild to moderate; symptoms include headache, nausea, vomiting, fatigue, and dizziness [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug interaction studies have been performed. Anifrolumab is not expected to be metabolised by hepatic enzymes [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Population PK analysis suggests that oral corticosteroids, antimalarials, and immunosuppressants (e.g., azathioprine, methotrexate, mycophenolate) do not significantly influence anifrolumab pharmacokinetics [Australian].
- **Management:** Avoid combination with other biologics due to lack of safety data [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Data in pregnant women are limited. Anifrolumab is known to cross the placenta in non-human primates. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if anifrolumab is excreted in human milk (detected in monkey milk). A risk to the breastfed child cannot be excluded. The decision to breastfeed should consider the clinical need of the mother and potential risks to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show no adverse effects on indirect measures of fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients ≥65 years, though data are limited for this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment required for mild (eGFR 60–89 mL/min/1.73 m²) or moderate (eGFR 30–59 mL/min/1.73 m²) impairment. No data for severe renal impairment or ESRD [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment required for mild to moderate hepatic impairment. Specific studies in hepatic impairment have not been conducted, but as a monoclonal antibody, it is cleared via catabolism rather than hepatic metabolism [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A human IgG1κ monoclonal antibody that binds to subunit 1 of the type I interferon receptor (IFNAR1). This blocks type I IFN signalling (IFN-α, IFN-β, IFN-κ), which is often elevated in SLE patients and associated with disease activity [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability after SC administration is ~73%. Steady-state is reached at ~16 weeks [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Estimated central volume is 3.46 L and peripheral volume is 1.70 L for a 68 kg patient [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Exhibits non-linear pharmacokinetics (IFNAR1-mediated clearance). Typical systemic clearance is 0.146 L/day. Serum concentrations are typically undetectable 16 weeks after the last dose [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Intravenous (MUSE, TULIP 1, TULIP 2):** Three 52-week trials in moderate to severe SLE patients. The 300 mg IV dose demonstrated significant improvements in BICLA (British Isles Lupus Assessment Group-based Composite Lupus Assessment) response rates and SRI-4 (SLE Responder Index) compared to placebo. It also showed a reduction in oral corticosteroid use and annualised flare rates [Australian].
- **Subcutaneous (TULIP SC):** A 52-week study (120 mg weekly) demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in disease activity (BICLA response) compared to placebo at week 52 (59.4% vs 43.9%) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Protect from light by keeping in the original carton [Australian].
- **Stability:**
    - **Pen (Room Temp):** If needed, the pre-filled pen can be kept at 20°C to 25°C for up to 7 days; if not used within this window, it must be discarded [Australian].
    - **Diluted Infusion:** Use immediately. If necessary, store at 2°C to 8°C for up to 24 hours or at 25°C for up to 4 hours [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
