# Editorial Product Information: Amiodarone

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their specific region before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Amiodarone hydrochloride (a Class III antiarrhythmic).
- **Brand Names:** **Amdarone** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Tablets: 100 mg (scored) and 200 mg (biconvex, embossed '200' and 'action potential') [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi-aventis Australia Pty Ltd; Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Tachyarrhythmias:** Approved for cases not responding to other therapy, including:
    - Wolff-Parkinson-White Syndrome.
    - Supraventricular, nodal, and ventricular tachycardias.
    - Atrial flutter and fibrillation.
    - Ventricular fibrillation.
- **Requirement for Hospitalisation:** Initiation of treatment must occur in a hospital setting [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Due to erratic absorption and significant inter-patient variability, dosage must be individually titrated to the minimum effective dose.

- **Initial Stabilisation:** 200 mg three times daily for one week, followed by 200 mg twice daily for a second week [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** Generally 200 mg daily or less. While some patients may require higher doses, maintenance levels exceeding 200 mg daily require regular review. Use of the 100 mg scored tablet is recommended for fine titration [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Patients require regular monitoring for toxicity (thyroid, hepatic, pulmonary, and ophthalmic) throughout therapy and for several months after discontinuation [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to iodine, amiodarone, or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- **Thyroid Dysfunction:** Evidence or history of thyroid dysfunction [Australian].
- **Cardiac Conditions:**
    - Sinus bradycardia and sino-atrial heart block [Australian].
    - Severe atrioventricular (AV) conduction disorders or sick sinus syndrome (unless a pacemaker is in situ) [Australian].
    - Combined therapy with drugs that may induce *torsades de pointes* [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Contraindicated for use in pregnant or breastfeeding women [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- Not specified in available sources for the Australian jurisdiction.

### General Warnings
- **Thyroid Abnormalities:** Amiodarone contains iodine (~75 mg per 200 mg tablet) and may induce hyper- or hypothyroidism. Monitoring of TSH (usTSH) is required before, during, and after treatment [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** May cause interstitial pneumonitis or fibrosis. Onset of dyspnoea or non-productive cough requires urgent investigation via chest X-ray [Australian].
- **Hepatic Dysfunction:** Dose-dependent elevation of transaminases is common. Therapy should be discontinued if transaminases exceed three times the normal range [Australian].
- **Ocular Changes:** Corneal microdeposits develop in nearly all patients (>90%). Appearance of optic neuropathy or neuritis requires immediate withdrawal to prevent potential blindness [Australian].
- **Dermatological Reactions:** Photosensitivity is common. Severe skin reactions, including SJS and TEN, require immediate discontinuation [Australian].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **ECG Monitoring:** QT prolongation (up to 0.60 seconds) is expected; however, wider intervals or the appearance of U-waves may necessitate therapy modification [Australian].
- **HCV Co-infection:** Severe, life-threatening bradycardia has been reported when used with sofosbuvir-containing regimens. Co-administration is not recommended [Australian].
- **Post-Cardiac Transplant:** Associated with increased risk of primary graft dysfunction (PGD) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect incidence and severity are often related to dosage and treatment duration.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Corneal microdeposits (>90%), Photosensitivity, Elevated transaminases (up to 5x normal), ECG changes (QT prolongation). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Moderate bradycardia, Nausea, Vomiting, Metallic taste (dysgeusia), Constipation, Tremor, Insomnia, Vivid dreams, Sleep disorders, Extrapyramidal symptoms (4% at high doses). |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Peripheral sensorimotor neuropathy, Myopathy (reversible upon withdrawal). |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Optic neuropathy/neuritis, Atypical ventricular tachycardia (*torsades de pointes*), Hypersensitivity vasculitis, Thrombocytopenia, Aplastic anaemia, SIADH, Cerebellar ataxia, Benign intracranial hypertension. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Pulmonary fibrosis, fatal thyrotoxicosis, acute hepatic failure, and SJS/TEN [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Torsadogenic Drugs:** Contraindicated with Class IA antiarrhythmics (quinidine, procainamide, disopyramide), sotalol, bepridil, and non-antiarrhythmics like cisapride, IV erythromycin, and certain neuroleptics [Australian].
- **Heart Rate/Conduction Modifiers:** Not recommended with beta-blockers or calcium channel blockers (verapamil, diltiazem) due to risk of bradycardia and AV block [Australian].
- **Hypokalaemic Agents:** Stimulant laxatives and diuretics that induce hypokalaemia increase the risk of *torsades de pointes* [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
Amiodarone and its metabolite (desethylamiodarone) inhibit CYP1A1, 1A2, 3A4, 2C9, 2D6, and P-glycoprotein.
- **Digoxin:** Plasma levels increase by ~70%; decrease digoxin dose by half [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Potentiates anticoagulant effect; requires frequent INR monitoring and dose adjustment [Australian].
- **Phenytoin:** Increases plasma levels; monitor for neurological toxicity [Australian].
- **Statins:** Increased risk of myotoxicity with CYP3A4-metabolised statins (simvastatin, atorvastatin) [Australian].
- **Dabigatran:** Increased bleeding risk [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. The drug crosses the placenta and has a long half-life. Potential for foetal goitre, hypothyroidism, and bradycardia. Avoid for several months before conception [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Amiodarone and metabolites are secreted in breast milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established; use not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased susceptibility to bradycardia and conduction defects; monitor thyroid function closely [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Minimal renal excretion; no dosage adjustment necessary [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with extreme caution due to risk of hepatotoxicity and drug accumulation [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Class III antiarrhythmic. Prolongs the action potential duration and refractory period in atrial, nodal, and ventricular tissues. Decreases sinus automaticity and slows AV conduction [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Incomplete and erratic (bioavailability 22–86%) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound; large volume of distribution (~6.3 L/kg). Accumulates in adipose tissue and highly perfused organs (liver, lung, heart) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via the liver to the active metabolite desethylamiodarone [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Biliary excretion; negligible renal clearance. Terminal half-life is exceptionally long (14 to 110 days) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Container:** Blister packed in strips of 10 tablets [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) or packaging [Australian].
