## 3. Approved Indications
- Adjuvant (early) breast cancer:
  - Adjuvant treatment in combination with endocrine therapy for patients with hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, node‑positive early breast cancer at high risk of recurrence. In pre- or peri‑menopausal women, endocrine therapy should be combined with a luteinising hormone‑releasing hormone (LHRH) agonist. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.1] [Australian]
- Advanced / metastatic breast cancer:
  - Treatment of HR-positive, HER2-negative, locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer in combination with an aromatase inhibitor or fulvestrant as initial endocrine‑based therapy, or following prior endocrine therapy. In pre- or peri‑menopausal women, the endocrine therapy should be combined with an LHRH agonist. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.1] [Australian]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- Recommended starting dose: **150 mg orally twice daily**, administered in combination with endocrine therapy at its recommended dose. May be taken with or without food. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2] [Australian]

### Dosing by indication
- Early breast cancer (adjuvant): Administer **150 mg twice daily** in combination with endocrine therapy (plus LHRH agonist in pre-/peri‑menopausal women) and continue for **two years**, or until disease recurrence or unacceptable toxicity, whichever occurs first. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2] [Australian]
- Advanced / metastatic breast cancer: Administer **150 mg twice daily** in combination with appropriate endocrine therapy. Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2] [Australian]

### Dose modification for adverse effects
- Dose reductions: decrease dose in 50 mg twice‑daily decrements (150 mg → 100 mg → 50 mg twice daily). Discontinue if patient cannot tolerate 50 mg twice daily. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2] [Australian]
- Haematological toxicity (neutropenia): Monitor full blood counts prior to start, every 2 weeks for first 2 months, monthly for next 2 months, then as clinically indicated. For CTCAE Grade 3 suspend until ≤ Grade 2 (no dose reduction required for first occurrence). For recurrent Grade 3 or Grade 4 suspend until ≤ Grade 2 and resume at next lower dose. If blood cell growth factors are used, suspend abemaciclib for at least 48 hours after last dose of growth factor and until toxicity resolves to ≤ Grade 2; resume at next lower dose unless already reduced for that toxicity. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Table 2] [Australian]
- Diarrhoea: At first sign of loose stools, initiate antidiarrhoeal therapy (e.g., loperamide), fluids and notify clinician. For CTCAE Grade 1 no dose adjustment; Grade 2 — if does not resolve within 24 hours to Grade 1, suspend until resolution; persistent/recurrent Grade 2 despite supportive measures, or Grade 3–4, suspend until Grade 1 or less and resume at next lower dose. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Table 3] [Australian]
- Hepatotoxicity (ALT/AST elevations): Monitor ALT/AST prior to start, every 2 weeks for first 2 months, monthly for next 2 months, then as clinically indicated. For persistent/recurrent Grade 2 or Grade 3 suspend until baseline or Grade 1 and resume at next lower dose. Discontinue if AST/ALT >3×ULN with total bilirubin >2×ULN (absence of cholestasis) or Grade 4 (>20×ULN). [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Table 4] [Australian]
- Interstitial lung disease (ILD)/pneumonitis: For Grade 1–2 no modification required initially; suspend for Grade 2 that persists/recur despite measures and does not return to baseline/Grade 1 within 7 days — resume at next lower dose when resolved. Permanently discontinue for Grade 3–4. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Table 5] [Australian]
- Venous thromboembolism (VTE): In early breast cancer setting any-grade VTE — suspend and treat as clinically indicated; abemaciclib may be resumed when clinically stable. In metastatic setting follow CTCAE‑based management per PI (see Table 6). [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Table 6] [Australian]
- Dose adjustments for interactions:
  - Avoid concomitant use of strong CYP3A inhibitors; if unavoidable, follow specific reduced-dose recommendations (examples and recommended adjusted doses provided in Table 8). If a strong inhibitor is discontinued, increase abemaciclib dose back to prior dose after 3–5 half‑lives of inhibitor. Avoid concomitant use of CYP3A inducers; consider alternatives. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Table 8; Section 4.5] [Australian]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed (highlighted) warnings are specified in the provided source. [Source: TGA PI, entire document] [Australian]

### General warnings and precautions (selected)
- Neutropenia: Grade ≥3 neutropenia reported; monitor blood counts as recommended; fatal neutropenic sepsis occurred in <1% of metastatic patients. Patients should report fever promptly. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Infections: Higher rate of infections with abemaciclib plus endocrine therapy versus endocrine therapy alone; lung infections reported even without neutropenia; fatal events <1% in metastatic patients. Monitor and treat infections appropriately. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Venous thromboembolism (VTE): VTEs were observed in adjuvant and metastatic settings (adjuvant: 2.5% with abemaciclib vs 0.6% control; metastatic: 5.3% with abemaciclib vs 0.8% control). Consider VTE risk when selecting endocrine therapy and monitor for DVT/PE signs. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Arterial thromboembolic events (ATE): ATEs (including ischaemic stroke and myocardial infarction) reported more frequently with abemaciclib plus endocrine therapy in metastatic studies (4.4% vs 2.4% control); causal relationship not established; evaluate benefits/risks if ATE occurs. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Hepatotoxicity: ALT/AST elevations including Grade ≥3 have been reported; monitor liver enzymes as specified and modify dosing per severity. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Interstitial lung disease (ILD) / pneumonitis: Severe, life‑threatening and fatal ILD/pneumonitis can occur; in clinical studies ILD/pneumonitis any grade ≈3.2–3.3% with small percentages Grade 3–4 and rare fatalities; monitor for respiratory symptoms; exclude infectious, neoplastic and other causes. Suspend/reduce/stop per severity. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Diarrhoea: Most common adverse reaction; median time to first event 6–8 days; median durations for Grade 2 = 7–12 days and Grade 3 = 5–8 days. Diarrhoea can lead to dehydration; initiate antidiarrhoeal therapy at first loose stools. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Use in hepatic impairment: Abemaciclib is metabolised hepatically; severe hepatic impairment increases unbound exposure ~2.69‑fold and prolongs half‑life from 24 to 55 hours — reduce dosing frequency to once daily in severe hepatic impairment. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.2] [Australian]
- Use in renal impairment: Abemaciclib and metabolites are not significantly renally cleared; dose adjustment not necessary for mild or moderate renal impairment; no data for severe renal impairment, end‑stage renal disease or dialysis. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Fertility and reproductive risks: Animal data indicate potential male reproductive organ effects and fetal harm; advise use of highly effective contraception during treatment and for 3 weeks after last dose. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.6] [Australian]
- Paediatric use: Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years not established. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.6] [Australian]

### Monitoring requirements
- Full blood counts: prior to start, every 2 weeks for first 2 months, monthly for next 2 months, then as clinically indicated. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Liver transaminases (ALT/AST): prior to start, every 2 weeks for first 2 months, monthly for next 2 months, then as clinically indicated. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Monitor for diarrhoea, respiratory symptoms suggestive of ILD, signs of thromboembolism, and infection. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]

Adverse effects summary (most commonly occurring)
- Very common (>10%): diarrhoea; infections; neutropenia; leukopenia; anaemia; fatigue; nausea; vomiting; decreased appetite; alopecia. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8] [Australian]

Serious / selected adverse events reported
- Neutropenia: Grade ≥3 reported; fatal neutropenic sepsis occurred in <1% (metastatic setting). [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.8] [Australian]
- Infections (including lung infection): higher rates when combined with endocrine therapy; fatal events <1% in metastatic patients. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.8] [Australian]
- Venous thromboembolic events: early setting 2.5% (abemaciclib + endocrine) vs 0.6% comparator; metastatic setting 5.3% vs 0.8% comparator. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Arterial thromboembolic events: metastatic studies any‑grade 4.4% with abemaciclib vs 2.4% control. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Hepatotoxicity (ALT/AST elevations): Grade ≥3 observed; monitoring and dose modifications required per severity. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4; Table 4] [Australian]
- Interstitial lung disease / pneumonitis: early breast cancer (monarchE) ILD/pneumonitis any grade 3.2% (0.4% Grade 3–4; one fatality <0.1%); metastatic studies combined ILD/pneumonitis any grade 3.3%, Grade 3–4 0.6%, fatal 0.4%; postmarketing fatalities reported. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.8] [Australian]
- Diarrhoea: very common; median onset 6–8 days; Grade 2 median duration 7–12 days; Grade 3 median duration 5–8 days. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]

Selected trial-specific safety data (monarchE; safety in early breast cancer)
- In monarchE, 2791 patients received abemaciclib + endocrine therapy; 2800 received endocrine therapy alone. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8] [Australian]
- Most frequent Grade ≥3 adverse reactions (≥5%): neutropenia, leukopenia, diarrhoea and lymphopenia. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8] [Australian]
- Fatal adverse reactions in the abemaciclib arm: 0.8% overall, including cardiac failure (0.1%) and single occurrences (0.03% each) of cardiac arrest, myocardial infarction, ventricular fibrillation, cerebral haemorrhage, cerebrovascular accident, pneumonitis, hypoxia, diarrhoea and mesenteric artery thrombosis. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8] [Australian]
- Treatment discontinuation due to adverse reactions: 19% in abemaciclib + endocrine therapy arm (most common causes: diarrhoea 5%, fatigue 2%, neutropenia 0.9%); comparator discontinuation due to adverse reaction 1%. Dose interruptions and reductions were frequent (interruptions 62%, reductions 44%) with diarrhea and neutropenia among leading causes. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8] [Australian]

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Abemaciclib is primarily metabolised by CYP3A4. Co‑administration with CYP3A4 inhibitors increases abemaciclib plasma concentrations; coadministration with CYP3A4 inducers decreases exposure. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5] [Australian]
- Examples / quantitative interaction data:
  - Clarithromycin (CYP3A4 inhibitor) co‑administration increased abemaciclib plasma exposure ≈3.4‑fold and increased combined unbound potency‑adjusted exposure ≈2.5‑fold (in patients). [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5] [Australian]
  - Ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin, diltiazem and verapamil and other inhibitors listed with modelled or clinical expected exposure changes and recommended reduced abemaciclib doses in Table 8 of the PI. Specific reduced-dose recommendations for some inhibitors are provided in the PI (see Table 8). [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Table 8; Section 4.5] [Australian]
  - Rifampicin (strong CYP3A4 inducer) decreased abemaciclib plasma concentration by ~95% and reduced potency‑adjusted exposure by ~77% (AUC0–∞). Strong CYP3A4 inducers should be avoided due to risk of reduced efficacy. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5] [Australian]

### Transporter interactions
- Abemaciclib and its major active metabolites inhibit renal transporters OCT2, MATE1 and MATE2‑K in vitro; clinical interactions with substrates of these transporters may occur. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5] [Australian]
  - Metformin (OCT2/MATE substrate): co‑administration with 400 mg abemaciclib produced a ≈37% increase in metformin plasma exposure attributable to reduced renal secretion; judged not clinically relevant in that study. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5] [Australian]
- P‑glycoprotein (P‑gp) substrate interactions:
  - Loperamide (P‑gp substrate): co‑administration in healthy subjects increased loperamide AUC by 9% and Cmax by 35% (not considered clinically relevant in that study). However, in vitro inhibition of P‑gp and BCRP suggests potential interactions with narrow therapeutic index substrates (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran etexilate). [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5] [Australian]

### Interactions with endocrine agents and contraceptives
- No clinically relevant pharmacokinetic interactions were observed between abemaciclib and anastrozole, fulvestrant, exemestane, letrozole or tamoxifen in a clinical study. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5] [Australian]
- It is unknown whether abemaciclib may reduce the effectiveness of systemically acting hormonal contraceptives; patients using systemic hormonal contraception are advised to add a barrier method. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5] [Australian]

### Management recommendations
- Avoid concomitant strong CYP3A inhibitors and inducers where possible; if unavoidable, follow the PI’s dose adjustment recommendations and monitor for toxicity or reduced efficacy. Avoid grapefruit or grapefruit juice. Monitor for interactions with transporter substrates, and consider monitoring or alternative therapy for narrow therapeutic index substrates. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.5] [Australian]

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Abemaciclib can cause fetal harm based on animal studies (cardiovascular and skeletal malformations in rats at exposures similar to human AUC). Use in pregnancy is not recommended. Patients of childbearing potential should use highly effective contraception during treatment and for 3 weeks after the last dose. The Australian pregnancy classification listed in the source is Category D. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.6] [Australian]

### Breastfeeding
- No data on presence in human milk; due to potential for serious adverse reactions in breastfed child, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 3 weeks after last dose. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.6] [Australian]

### Fertility
- Animal studies indicate potential impairment of male fertility (testis, epididymis, prostate and seminal vesicle findings) at exposures in some species; no effects on female fertility observed in rats at specified doses. Patients who could become pregnant should use effective contraception; male fertility impairment was observed in animal toxicology. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.6] [Australian]

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use: Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years not established; no data available. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.6] [Australian]
- Elderly: Population pharmacokinetic analysis found no effect of age on abemaciclib exposure (age range 24–91 years in pooled analysis); no dose adjustment specified based on age alone. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Renal impairment:
  - Mild or moderate renal impairment: no dose adjustment necessary. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]
  - Severe renal impairment / dialysis: no data available; dosing guidance not specified. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Severe hepatic impairment: increased unbound exposure (≈2.69‑fold) and prolonged half‑life; reduce dosing frequency to once daily (i.e., dosing frequency change recommended). [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.2] [Australian]
  - Mild/moderate hepatic impairment: specific dose adjustments not provided in supplied extract. Not specified in available sources beyond severe impairment recommendations. [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4] [Australian]

### Pharmacokinetics (selected data reported in supplied PI)

## 12. Clinical Trials
