# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals. It integrates clinical data, regulatory warnings, and administration guidelines from the provided official product information.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Initial Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours).
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dosing:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate.

### Administration Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before parenteral therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of plasma levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs is recommended.
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No specific contraindications are listed in the available US source; however, see Section 5 regarding hypersensitivity reactions.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. Treatment should be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Post-marketing reports indicate seizures may occur in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In ESRD patients, the effects of supplemental carnitine on clinical outcomes (beyond increasing plasma concentrations) have not been fully determined.
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but noted as use only if clearly needed]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks to the infant; discontinuation of nursing or treatment may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment observed in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight; see Section 3 for specific instructions.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; general clinical caution is advised.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, **oral** formulations are cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. 
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic acids or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in patients on chronic haemodialysis demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to steady-state troughs of 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations (tablets/solution) is approximately 15–16% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C.
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentrations) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.
