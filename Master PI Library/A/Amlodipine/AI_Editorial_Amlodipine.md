## 1. Product Identification

- Chemical class: Dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker (class attribution indicated by hypersensitivity contraindication to dihydropyridines) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3].

## 3. Approved Indications
- Hypertension: the fixed‑dose product containing olmesartan medoxomil and amlodipine (i.e. containing amlodipine 5 mg or 10 mg) is indicated for the treatment of hypertension. Treatment should not be initiated with the fixed‑dose combination product (see dosing section) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.1; Section 4.2].
- Use of amlodipine as monotherapy or other indications: Not specified in available sources for single‑agent amlodipine; where clinical trial data in the source refer to amlodipine (e.g. heart failure studies) these are described in the Clinical Trials section below [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].

## 4. Dosage and Administration
- General dosing (adult):
  - For the fixed‑dose tablets combining olmesartan medoxomil and amlodipine, the recommended regimen is one tablet once daily, with or without food; the available combination strengths contain amlodipine 5 mg or 10 mg per tablet [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 2].
  - The source explicitly states that treatment should not be initiated with this fixed‑dose combination (i.e. initial therapy should be started with appropriate monotherapy prior to switching to the combination) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.1; Section 4.2].
- Dosing by indication: Not specified in available sources for single‑agent amlodipine. Combination titration recommendations for hypertension:
  - Patients uncontrolled on monotherapy may be switched to the combination; titration steps recommended for the combination: 20/5 → 40/5 → 40/10 (olmesartan/amlodipine mg) as needed based on blood pressure control [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2].
- Administration instructions:
  - Take one tablet daily, with or without food (applies to the fixed‑dose combination product described) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2].
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.
- Special dosing notes:
  - No general dose adjustment required for elderly patients (statement refers to the combination product) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2].
  - No adjustment required for mild‑to‑moderate renal impairment; severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance <20 mL/min) is a contraindication for the combination product (see Section 5) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.3].
  - Use with caution in mild‑to‑moderate hepatic impairment; not recommended in severe hepatic impairment and biliary obstruction [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.3; Section 4.4].

## 5. Contraindications
List of contraindications (as stated in the source). Jurisdiction: [Australian].
- Hypersensitivity to any component of the tablet or to dihydropyridines (amlodipine class) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3].
- Pregnancy (use of the combination is contraindicated in pregnancy) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3].
- Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 20 mL/min) — use of the combination is contraindicated [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3].
- Severe hepatic impairment or biliary obstruction — combination is contraindicated [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3].
- Patients with diabetes who are taking aliskiren — contraindicated for the combination product [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3].
- Additional contraindications attributable to the amlodipine component (dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker class):
  - Cardiogenic shock [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3].
  - Acute myocardial infarction (within the first 4 weeks) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3].
  - Unstable angina pectoris [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3].

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed warnings: Not specified in available sources.
- General warnings and precautions (all jurisdictional statements from the source are attributed to [Australian]):
  - Intravascular volume depletion: Symptomatic hypotension may occur in volume‑ or sodium‑depleted patients (e.g. on vigorous diuretic therapy, dietary salt restriction, diarrhoea or vomiting), particularly after the first dose; correct the condition before starting or use close monitoring [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Patients dependent on renin‑angiotensin‑aldosterone system (RAS) activity (e.g. severe congestive heart failure, underlying renal disease including renal artery stenosis) may experience acute hypotension, azotaemia, oliguria or rarely acute renal failure and/or death when treated with RAS‑affecting drugs (applies to the olmesartan component but relevant when considering the combination) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Renovascular hypertension: increased risk of severe hypotension and renal insufficiency in bilateral renal artery stenosis or stenosis of a single functioning kidney when treated with RAS‑acting drugs [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Sprue‑like enteropathy: severe, chronic diarrhoea with substantial weight loss and villous atrophy has been reported with olmesartan medoxomil; consider discontinuation if other causes excluded [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Hyperkalaemia: may occur with RAS inhibitors (olmesartan component), especially with renal impairment and/or heart failure; monitor serum electrolytes periodically and exercise caution with concomitant potassium‑sparing diuretics, potassium supplements or potassium‑containing salt substitutes [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Aortic or mitral valve stenosis and obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy: use vasodilators with special caution in these patients [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Primary aldosteronism: patients typically will not respond to RAS‑acting antihypertensives; use not recommended [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Angioedema: reported with olmesartan medoxomil; discontinue drug immediately if angioedema develops and do not re‑administer [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Increased angina and/or myocardial infarction: rare reports of increased frequency, duration and/or severity of angina or acute myocardial infarction on starting calcium channel blocker therapy or at dose increase, particularly in patients with severe obstructive coronary artery disease [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Congestive heart failure: RAS inhibition may change renal function in susceptible patients; olmesartan/amlodipine combination has not been studied in heart failure—exercise caution. Amlodipine has been studied in patients with NYHA class III/IV heart failure on background therapy without overall adverse effect on survival/morbidity in the cited trial(s) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Beta‑blocker withdrawal: amlodipine is not a beta‑blocker and does not protect against the risks of abrupt beta‑blocker withdrawal [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Peripheral oedema: mild to moderate peripheral oedema is the most common adverse event with amlodipine and is dose dependent (incidence 3.0–10.8% in the 5–10 mg dose range in clinical trials cited) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Ethnic differences: blood pressure response to angiotensin receptor antagonists (olmesartan component) may be less in black patients than in non‑black patients; stated as a class observation [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Concomitant use of ACE inhibitors or angiotensin receptor antagonists and NSAIDs and thiazide diuretics: combined use increases risk of renal impairment; monitor serum creatinine, especially on treatment initiation and in elderly or those with pre‑existing renal impairment [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Lithium: combination with olmesartan not recommended; if necessary, monitor lithium levels closely [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.5].
  - Use in hepatic impairment: amlodipine is extensively metabolised by the liver and exposure is increased in hepatic impairment; use with caution in mild–moderate hepatic impairment, and avoid in severe hepatic impairment and biliary obstruction [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.2].
  - Use in renal impairment: periodic monitoring of serum potassium and creatinine is recommended in renal impairment; the combination is not recommended in severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance <20 mL/min) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.2].
  - Use in the elderly: no routine dose adjustment generally required for elderly patients, but exercise caution and monitor as appropriate [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4].
  - Paediatric use: the combination product is not recommended for use in children and adolescents <18 years due to lack of data [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4].
- Monitoring requirements:
  - Periodic monitoring of serum electrolytes (including potassium) and creatinine in patients with renal impairment or heart failure, and when used concomitantly with agents that affect RAS or potassium levels [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.5].
  - Monitor for signs of angioedema and severe diarrhoeal illness (sprue‑like enteropathy) attributable to olmesartan if relevant [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
- Effects on laboratory tests:
  - Olmesartan medoxomil: increased blood creatinine and hyperkalaemia reported in post‑marketing experience [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4: "Effects on Laboratory Tests"].
  - Amlodipine: no clinically significant changes in routine laboratory tests reported in clinical trials (no clinically relevant changes noted in serum potassium, glucose, triglycerides, cholesterol, HDL, uric acid, BUN, creatinine or liver function tests in the data summarised in source) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4: "Effects on Laboratory Tests"].

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacodynamic and pharmacokinetic interactions (all items cited to the source; jurisdiction: [Australian]):
  - Potassium supplements / potassium‑sparing diuretics / potassium‑containing salt substitutes: concomitant use with olmesartan may increase serum potassium; such use is not recommended and requires close monitoring if unavoidable [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
  - Other antihypertensive medications: additive blood pressure‑lowering effects when combined with olmesartan or amlodipine [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
  - Non‑steroidal anti‑inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs, including aspirin >3 g/day and COX‑2 inhibitors): concomitant use with angiotensin receptor antagonists may reduce glomerular filtration and increase risk of acute renal failure; it may also reduce antihypertensive efficacy — recommend monitoring renal function and ensuring adequate hydration [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
  - Dual blockade of the renin‑angiotensin system (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, aliskiren): associated with increased risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia and renal dysfunction versus monotherapy; monitor blood pressure, renal function and electrolytes; do not co‑administer aliskiren with olmesartan in patients with diabetes; avoid aliskiren with olmesartan if GFR <60 mL/min [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
  - Colesevelam hydrochloride: coadministration reduced olmesartan Cmax and AUC (28% and 39% respectively) when given concomitantly; if colesevelam is required, consider administering olmesartan medoxomil 4 hours before colesevelam to reduce interaction [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
  - Lithium: reversible increases in serum lithium and lithium toxicity reported with RAS inhibitors; concomitant use not recommended; if necessary, monitor lithium levels closely [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
  - Warfarin, digoxin, antacid (aluminium magnesium hydroxide), hydrochlorothiazide and pravastatin: specific studies with olmesartan showed no clinically relevant interactions (no effect on warfarin or digoxin pharmacokinetics) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
  - CYP450 interactions — olmesartan: in vitro data showed no clinically relevant inhibition of multiple CYP isoenzymes and minimal induction in animal data; clinically relevant CYP‑mediated interactions not expected for olmesartan [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
  - Amlodipine interactions (selected clinically relevant items):
    - Grapefruit juice: may increase bioavailability of calcium channel blockers including amlodipine — co‑administration is not recommended [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
    - CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g. erythromycin, diltiazem; strong inhibitors such as ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir): may increase plasma concentrations of amlodipine; use with caution and monitor for hypotension or adverse effects [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
    - CYP3A4 inducers (e.g. rifampicin, St John’s Wort): may reduce amlodipine plasma concentrations; use with caution [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
    - Antacid (aluminium/magnesium): no significant effect on amlodipine pharmacokinetics [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
    - Sildenafil: no effect on amlodipine pharmacokinetics; combined use produces independent blood pressure lowering effects of each agent [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
    - Atorvastatin: coadministration of multiple 10 mg amlodipine with 80 mg atorvastatin produced no significant change in atorvastatin PK [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
    - Simvastatin: coadministration with multiple 10 mg amlodipine and 80 mg simvastatin resulted in a 77% increase in simvastatin exposure; limit simvastatin dose to 20 mg daily when combined with amlodipine [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
    - Ethanol (alcohol): no significant effect on ethanol pharmacokinetics with single or multiple 10 mg amlodipine doses [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
    - Ciclosporin: prospective study in renal transplant patients showed an average 40% increase in trough ciclosporin levels with amlodipine; monitor trough ciclosporin levels and adjust ciclosporin dose as necessary [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
    - Tacrolimus: coadministration may increase tacrolimus exposure; monitor tacrolimus blood levels and adjust dose as necessary [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
- Management notes:
  - Monitor electrolytes and renal function when combining with agents affecting RAS, NSAIDs, or diuretics [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
  - Avoid combinations explicitly contraindicated (e.g. aliskiren in diabetics on olmesartan) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3; Section 4.5].
  - Consider timing adjustments when coadministering olmesartan with colesevelam (olmesartan 4 hours before colesevelam) [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
  - Reduce simvastatin dose to ≤20 mg daily if used with amlodipine [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - The olmesartan/amlodipine combination is not recommended for use in children and adolescents <18 years due to lack of safety and efficacy data [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4].
  - Specific paediatric dosing for single‑agent amlodipine: Not specified in available sources.
- Elderly:
  - No routine dose adjustment generally required for elderly patients (statement in the source refers to the combination product), but monitor for expected pharmacodynamic effects and comorbidities [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.4].
- Renal impairment:
  - Mild to moderate renal impairment: no dose adjustment required for the combination product [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2].
  - Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance <20 mL/min): use of the combination product is not recommended / contraindicated [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2; Section 4.3].
  - End‑stage renal disease / recent kidney transplant: no experience reported in the source for the combination product; Not specified for single‑agent amlodipine [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Amlodipine is extensively metabolised in the liver; exposure to amlodipine and olmesartan increases in hepatic impairment. Use combination product with caution in mild to moderate hepatic impairment; not recommended in severe hepatic impairment and biliary obstruction [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4; Section 4.2].
  - Amlodipine half‑life has been prolonged in a small number of patients with mild to moderate hepatic impairment after single 5 mg dosing in the source; careful monitoring and consideration of a lower starting dose may be required [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].

## 11. Pharmacology
- Mechanism of action:
  - Specific, detailed mechanism statements for amlodipine monotherapy are Not specified in available sources. The source classifies amlodipine within dihydropyridines (calcium channel blockers) by implication via hypersensitivity references [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3].
- Pharmacokinetics (as reported in the source):
  - Amlodipine is extensively metabolised by the liver; exposure is increased in patients with hepatic impairment and amlodipine half‑life may be prolonged in mild–moderate hepatic impairment [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - The source reports specific interaction‑related pharmacokinetic findings (e.g. reduction of olmesartan exposure with colesevelam; increases in amlodipine plasma concentrations with certain CYP3A4 inhibitors; increases in simvastatin exposure when coadministered with amlodipine) which are described in the Interactions section [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5].
- Pharmacodynamics:
  - Detailed pharmacodynamic parameters for amlodipine monotherapy (e.g. onset, duration) are Not specified in available sources beyond clinical trial outcome summaries referenced in Section 12 [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Heart failure trials involving amlodipine (as described in the source):
  - A placebo‑controlled trial of amlodipine (5–10 mg/day) in 1,153 patients with NYHA Class III or IV heart failure on stable ACE inhibitor, digoxin and diuretics: follow‑up at least 6 months (mean ≈14 months); no overall adverse effect on survival or cardiac morbidity reported in that trial [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
  - Four short (8–12 week) studies in patients with NYHA class II/III heart failure (total N=697): no evidence of worsened heart failure based on exercise tolerance, NYHA class, symptoms, or LVEF in these studies [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
- Other pivotal trials: Not specified in available sources for single‑agent amlodipine beyond the heart failure trials noted above and the peripheral oedema incidence figures taken from clinical trial data summarised in the source [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4].
