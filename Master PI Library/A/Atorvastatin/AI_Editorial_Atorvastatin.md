## 3. Approved Indications
- As an adjunct to diet for the treatment of patients with hypercholesterolaemia (primary hypercholesterolaemia and mixed dyslipidaemia). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.1]
- To reduce the risk of non-fatal myocardial infarction and non-fatal stroke in hypertensive patients with multiple risk factors for coronary heart disease (CHD) including diabetes, prior cerebrovascular disease, peripheral vascular disease or existing asymptomatic CHD (Australian PI context). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.1]
- Indication in homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia (compassionate-use experience): treated patients may respond to high-dose therapy (see Section 4.2). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Sections 4.2 and 4.2, “Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolaemia”]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Usual single daily dose range: 10 mg to 80 mg once daily; therapy should be individualised to lipid targets and patient response. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]
- Most patients are controlled with 10 mg once daily; therapeutic response apparent within 2 weeks and maximal by ~4 weeks. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]
- After initiation or dose titration, re-assess lipid levels within about 4 weeks and adjust dose according to response. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]

### Dosing by indication / special situations
- Homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia (adults): many patients in a compassionate-use study responded to 80 mg with LDL-C reductions >15% (range reported 18–42%). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]
- When co-administered with ciclosporin, telaprevir, or the combination tipranavir/ritonavir, do not exceed 10 mg atorvastatin daily. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]
- When co-administered with letermovir (without ciclosporin), do not exceed 20 mg atorvastatin daily. Use of atorvastatin with letermovir co-administered with ciclosporin is not recommended. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Sections 4.2; 4.5]
- Caution and consideration of the lowest efficacious dose are recommended when co-prescribing with transporter inhibitors or CYP3A4 inhibitors such as elbasvir/grazoprevir or simeprevir; specific dose limits not defined beyond “use lowest dose necessary.” [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Sections 4.2; 4.5]

### Method of administration
- Oral administration; may be taken at any time of day, with or without food. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]

### Dose adjustment
- Renal impairment: no dose adjustment required because renal disease does not influence atorvastatin plasma concentrations or LDL-C reduction. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]
- Hepatic impairment: plasma concentrations are markedly increased in patients with chronic alcoholic liver disease (Child–Pugh B); weigh benefits vs risks and use with caution; active liver disease is a contraindication (see Section 5 and Section 4.3). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Sections 4.2; 4.4; 4.3]

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI — missed dose guidance not included in supplied excerpts]

## 5. Contraindications
List compiled from the supplied PI (Australian). All items below are from the provided source; jurisdiction tag [Australian] applies:
- Hypersensitivity to atorvastatin or any excipient. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3]
- Active liver disease or unexplained persistent elevations of serum transaminases. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3]
- Pregnancy and lactation (women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception and be highly unlikely to conceive). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3]
- Concomitant systemic fusidic acid hemihydrate (co-administration contraindicated). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3]
- Concomitant treatment with glecaprevir/pibrentasvir (co-administration contraindicated). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3]
- Use of atorvastatin is not recommended in patients taking letermovir co-administered with ciclosporin (i.e., this combination should be avoided). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Sections 4.2; 4.5]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed/black-box warning text is provided in the supplied Australian PI excerpts. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI — boxed warnings not present in provided sections]

### General warnings and precautions
- Liver dysfunction: moderate elevations of serum transaminases (>3 × ULN) have been reported; persistent elevations >3 × ULN occurred in 0.7% of atorvastatin-treated patients overall, with incidence by dose: 10 mg 0.2%; 20 mg 0.2%; 40 mg 0.6%; 80 mg 2.3%. Monitor liver function prior to initiation and periodically thereafter; if ALT or AST elevation >3 × ULN persists, reduce dose or stop therapy. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Skeletal muscle: myalgia and myopathy (including CK elevations >10 × ULN) have been reported; discontinue atorvastatin if myopathy is diagnosed or suspected, or if CK markedly elevated. Inform patients to report unexplained muscle pain, tenderness or weakness, particularly with malaise or fever. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Rhabdomyolysis (including fatalities) has been reported; risk is increased with concomitant drugs that increase systemic atorvastatin concentrations (see Section 8 Drug Interactions). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy (IMNM): rare reports of persistent proximal muscle weakness with elevated CK that do not resolve after statin discontinuation; consider IMNM if symptoms persist. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Myasthenia gravis / ocular myasthenia: rare reports of new-onset or exacerbation of myasthenia gravis; discontinue atorvastatin if these occur. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Haemorrhagic stroke: SPARCL post-hoc analysis showed increased haemorrhagic stroke incidence with atorvastatin 80 mg vs placebo (2.3% vs 1.4%); consider risk in patients with recent (1–6 months) stroke or TIA and in those with prior haemorrhagic stroke or lacunar infarct where subgroup risk appeared greater. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Endocrine/metabolic: small increases in HbA1c and fasting serum glucose have been reported with statins, including atorvastatin; clinical adrenal/gonadal steroid production not impaired in studies referenced. Exercise caution if co-administered with drugs that decrease endogenous steroid hormones. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Ubiquinone (CoQ10): statins including atorvastatin can reduce circulating ubiquinone levels; clinical significance is not established. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Interstitial lung disease: exceptional (rare) reports with some statins, particularly after long-term therapy; discontinue if suspected. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Paediatric and elderly use: paediatric experience is limited (see Section 10); elderly patients (≥70 years) evaluated up to 80 mg/day showed similar safety and efficacy to younger adults. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]

### Monitoring requirements
- Liver function tests: perform baseline tests prior to initiation and periodically thereafter; monitor until resolution if transaminase elevations occur. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Creatine kinase (CK): consider CK measurement in patients with symptoms of muscle pain/tenderness/weakness; periodic CK monitoring may be considered in high-risk co-administration scenarios (but does not guarantee prevention). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- If co-administering with daptomycin and concomitant atorvastatin cannot be avoided, consider measuring CK 2–3 times weekly and closely monitor for myopathy signs. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]

### Specific clinical precautions
- Temporarily withhold or discontinue atorvastatin in patients with acute serious conditions suggestive of myopathy or conditions that predispose to renal failure secondary to rhabdomyolysis (e.g., severe acute infection, major surgery, trauma, severe metabolic disorders, uncontrolled seizures). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- If systemic fusidic acid is essential, discontinue statin therapy for the duration of fusidic acid treatment and reintroduce statin at least seven days after the last fusidic acid dose. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Sections 4.3; 4.4; 4.5]

## 8. Drug Interactions
(Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions stated in the supplied PI)

- Atorvastatin is metabolised primarily by CYP3A4 and is a substrate for hepatic transporters (OATP1B1/1B3, P‑gp, BCRP, etc.); concomitant drugs that inhibit CYP3A4 or relevant transporters can increase atorvastatin exposure and risk of myopathy. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
- CYP3A4 inhibitors associated with increased atorvastatin concentrations: macrolide antibiotics (erythromycin, clarithromycin), azole antifungals (itraconazole), HIV/HCV protease inhibitors (e.g., lopinavir/ritonavir combinations, darunavir/ritonavir, fosamprenavir/ritonavir; specific agents and combinations cited), ciclosporin. Clinicians should exercise caution when co-prescribing and consider dose limits (see Section 4.2). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
- Transporter inhibitors:
  - Ciclosporin increases atorvastatin exposure (inhibits OATP1B1/1B3, BCRP, MDR1 and CYP3A4); do not exceed 10 mg atorvastatin daily when co-administered. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
  - Glecaprevir/pibrentasvir (OATP1B1/1B3, MDR1, BCRP inhibitors) — co-administration contraindicated. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
  - Letermovir increases atorvastatin exposure (reported atorvastatin AUC ratio 3.29 and Cmax ratio 2.17 when co-administered 480 mg once daily with atorvastatin 20 mg); do not exceed 20 mg atorvastatin daily when co-administered without ciclosporin; atorvastatin is not recommended if letermovir is co-administered with ciclosporin. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
  - Elbasvir/grazoprevir and simeprevir increase atorvastatin exposure (OATP1B1/1B3 and efflux transporter inhibitors); use lowest necessary atorvastatin dose and clinical assessment. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Sections 4.2; 4.5]
- Fusidic acid: systemic fusidic acid co-administration with statins has been associated with reports of rhabdomyolysis (including fatalities); co-administration is contraindicated — discontinue statin during fusidic acid therapy and reintroduce 7 days after the last fusidic acid dose if therapy required. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Sections 4.3; 4.4; 4.5]
- Colchicine: cases of myopathy reported with co-administration; exercise caution. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
- Daptomycin: co-administration with HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (including atorvastatin) has been associated with myopathy/rhabdomyolysis; consider temporarily suspending atorvastatin while on daptomycin unless benefits outweigh risks; if co-administered, monitor CK closely (see Section 4.4). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4; 4.5]
- Antacid (magnesium/aluminium hydroxide suspension): reduces atorvastatin plasma concentrations by ~35%, but LDL-C reduction was not altered in the study cited. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
- Colestipol: lowers plasma atorvastatin concentrations (~25%) but combined therapy produced greater LDL-C reductions than either alone. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
- Grapefruit juice: contains components that inhibit CYP3A4 and can increase atorvastatin concentrations, especially with excessive intake (>1.2 L/day). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
- Ticagrelor: co-administration increased atorvastatin acid Cmax by 23% and AUC by 36%; these increases were not considered clinically significant in the cited study. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
- Effects of atorvastatin on other medicines: digoxin pharmacokinetics were noted to be altered in co-administration studies (full details truncated in provided excerpt); consult full PI for details. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]

Clinically significant interaction management notes:
- Avoid contraindicated combinations (e.g., glecaprevir/pibrentasvir; systemic fusidic acid). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Sections 4.3; 4.5]
- Use lower starting/maintenance atorvastatin doses or avoid co-administration with strong CYP3A4/transport inhibitors; monitor for muscle symptoms and CK. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Sections 4.2; 4.4; 4.5]
- When combining with daptomycin, either suspend atorvastatin or perform frequent CK monitoring (2–3 times weekly) if co-administration cannot be avoided. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Sections 4.4; 4.5]

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy: Atorvastatin is contraindicated in pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception and be unlikely to conceive while taking atorvastatin. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3]
- Breastfeeding: Atorvastatin is contraindicated during lactation. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.3]
- Jurisdictional pregnancy category systems (e.g., US letter categories, Australian category letters) and any category assignments are not specified in the supplied excerpts. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI — pregnancy category not stated in supplied text]
- Fertility: Effects on male fertility have not been studied in adequate numbers; effects on the pituitary–gonadal axis in pre-menopausal women unknown; clinical adrenal reserve not impaired in studies cited. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use: Treatment experience in paediatric patients is limited; supplied data describe up to 80 mg/day for 1 year in 8 patients with homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia with no reported clinical/biochemical abnormalities; general paediatric dosing/approvals not specified. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Elderly use: In adults ≥70 years, dosing up to 80 mg/day was evaluated in 221 patients and safety/efficacy were similar to younger adults. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Renal impairment: Renal disease does not affect atorvastatin plasma concentrations or LDL-C reduction; no dose adjustment recommended. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]
- Hepatic impairment: Plasma concentrations markedly increased in patients with chronic alcoholic liver disease (Child–Pugh B); use with caution and weigh benefits versus risks; active liver disease is a contraindication. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Sections 4.2; 4.3; 4.4]

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Atorvastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol biosynthesis, thereby lowering LDL-C and total cholesterol. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 5.1 referenced]

### Pharmacokinetics
- Metabolism: primarily metabolised by cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.5]
- Transporters: atorvastatin is a substrate of hepatic uptake transporters (e.g., OATP1B1/1B3) and efflux transporters (e.g., P-gp, BCRP); inhibitors of these transporters increase atorvastatin exposure. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Sections 4.5; 5.2 referenced]
- Absorption/other PK parameters: specific numeric PK parameters (bioavailability, Tmax, half-life, clearance) are not provided in the supplied excerpts. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI — detailed PK parameters not included in provided text]

### Pharmacodynamics
- LDL-C reduction: dose-dependent LDL-C lowering is described with therapeutic response within 2 weeks and maximal response by about 4 weeks; exact percent reductions by dose are not included in supplied excerpts. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]

## 12. Clinical Trials
- SPARCL (secondary analysis reference): a post-hoc analysis in patients without known coronary heart disease but with recent stroke/TIA reported higher haemorrhagic stroke incidence with atorvastatin 80 mg vs placebo (2.3% vs 1.4), with subgroup signals in those with prior haemorrhagic stroke or lacunar infarct. All-cause mortality differences were small/numerical in the study report. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.4]
- Compassionate-use study in homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia: most patients responded to 80 mg with LDL-C reductions >15% (18–42% range reported). [Source: Australian (TGA) PI, Section 4.2]
- Additional pivotal trial summaries and primary outcome data commonly present in full PIs are not included in the provided excerpts; consult the full jurisdictional PI for comprehensive trial results. [Source: Australian (TGA) PI — Clinical trials referenced in Section 5.1 and Section 4.1 but full details not present in supplied excerpts]
