# Editorial Product Information: Azacitidine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinically relevant reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Azacitidine (a pyrimidine nucleoside analogue).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Azacitidine-Teva** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01BC07.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Lyophilised powder for injection; 100 mg per single-use vial.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection or Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Teva Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Azacitidine** is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with:
- Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS): Specifically Intermediate-2 and High-risk cases according to the International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS) [Australian].
- Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia (CMML): Patients with 10%–29% marrow blasts without myeloproliferative disorder [Australian].
- Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML): Patients with 20%–30% blasts and multi-lineage dysplasia (per WHO classification) for whom allogeneic stem cell transplantation is not indicated [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **First Cycle:** The recommended starting dose for all patients is 75 mg/m² of body surface area. This is administered daily for 7 days, followed by a 21-day rest period (28-day cycle) [Australian].
- **Subsequent Cycles:** Cycles should be repeated every 28 days. A minimum of 6 cycles is recommended. Treatment should continue as long as the patient derives clinical benefit or until disease progression [Australian].

### Dose Modification or Interruption
- **Hematological Toxicity:** Defined as Platelets < 50.0 x 10⁹/L and/or ANC < 1 x 10⁹/L. 
    - If recovery occurs within 14 days, no dose adjustment is needed. 
    - If recovery takes > 14 days, the dose must be reduced by 50% or based on bone marrow cellularity (if ≤ 50%, specific reductions to 50% or 33% of the dose apply) [Australian].
- **Renal/Electrolyte Toxicity:** If unexplained reductions in serum bicarbonate to < 20 mmol/L occur, reduce the next dose by 50%. For unexplained elevations in BUN or serum creatinine, delay the next cycle until values return to baseline and reduce the subsequent dose by 50% [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Premedication:** Patients should receive anti-emetics prior to treatment [Australian].
- **Subcutaneous:** Reconstitute with 4 mL Water for Injections (25 mg/mL suspension). Rotate injection sites (thigh, abdomen, or upper arm). Doses > 4 mL (100 mg) should be split into two separate injection sites [Australian].
- **Intravenous:** Reconstitute with 10 mL Water for Injections (10 mg/mL solution). Further dilute into 50–100 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s. Infuse over 10–40 minutes. Administration must be completed within 45 minutes of reconstitution [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to azacitidine or any of the excipients (e.g., sucrose) [Australian].
- Advanced malignant hepatic tumours [Australian].
- Pregnancy [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment (Creatinine Clearance < 30 mL/min) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hematological Toxicity:** Treatment frequently causes anaemia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia, particularly in the first two cycles. Complete blood counts (CBC) must be monitored prior to each cycle [Australian].
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Patients with a high tumour burden are at risk and should be monitored closely [Australian].
- **Differentiation Syndrome:** Cases (potentially fatal) have been reported. Symptoms include respiratory distress, pulmonary infiltrates, fever, and rapid weight gain. Treatment with high-dose IV corticosteroids is required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Patients with extensive metastatic disease have rarely experienced progressive hepatic coma and death. Safety is not established in patients with significant liver dysfunction (Bilirubin > 1.5x ULN) [Australian].
- **Cardiac and Pulmonary Disease:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients with a history of severe congestive heart failure or unstable cardiac/pulmonary disease [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary
- **Very Common (>10%):** Thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, anaemia, febrile neutropenia, nausea, vomiting, constipation, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, pyrexia, fatigue, injection site erythema/reaction, petechiae, pruritus, arthralgia, and nasopharyngitis [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Neutropenic sepsis, pneumonia, urinary tract infection, hypokalaemia, insomnia, dizziness, headache, hypertension/hypotension, dyspnoea, alopecia, and haematuria [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Hypersensitivity reactions (0.25%) [Australian].

### Serious and Post-Marketing Effects
- **Post-marketing:** Interstitial lung disease, injection site necrosis, necrotising fasciitis, Sweet’s syndrome (acute febrile neutrophilic dermatosis), and pyoderma gangrenosum [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- No formal clinical drug interaction studies have been conducted.
- **Microsomal Enzymes:** Based on *in vitro* data, azacitidine metabolism is not mediated by CYP450 isoenzymes. It does not induce or inhibit major CYP enzymes (1A2, 2C19, 3A4) at clinical concentrations [Australian].
- **Recommendations:** Concomitant use of strong microsomal enzyme inducers or inhibitors is not recommended [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** IV azacitidine is incompatible with 5% Dextrose, Volulyte, or solutions containing bicarbonate [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X**. Azacitidine is teratogenic and embryotoxic in animal studies. It is contraindicated during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during and for at least 6 months after the last dose. Males are advised not to father a child during treatment and for at least 3 months after the last dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if azacitidine is excreted in human milk. Due to potential serious toxicity to the infant, breastfeeding must be discontinued during therapy [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show adverse effects on male fertility (decreased sperm count and pregnancy rates) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents under 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustments are required, but since renal function decline is more likely in the elderly, renal monitoring is recommended [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min). Patients with mild-to-moderate impairment should be monitored closely for toxicity [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not formally studied; safety is not established in those with significant baseline hepatic dysfunction [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Believed to exert antineoplastic effects via two mechanisms:
    1. DNA Hypomethylation: Inhibits DNA methyltransferase, allowing re-expression of genes involved in cell cycle regulation and differentiation.
    2. Direct Cytotoxicity: Incorporation into RNA inhibits protein synthesis and induces apoptosis in abnormal haematopoietic cells in the bone marrow [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed after SC administration (bioavailability ~89%); peak plasma concentration at 0.5 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Mean volume of distribution is 76 ± 26 L [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via spontaneous hydrolysis and deamination by cytidine deaminase (not CYP-mediated) [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal; 85% of IV dose and 50% of SC dose recovered in urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study (AZA PH GL 2003 CL 001):** An international, Phase 3 trial comparing azacitidine plus best supportive care (BSC) against conventional care regimens (CCR) in MDS and AML (20–30% blasts) patients.
- **Outcome:** Azacitidine demonstrated a significant improvement in median overall survival: **24.5 months** versus **15 months** for the CCR group (Hazard Ratio 0.58). The 2-year survival rate was 50.8% for azacitidine versus 26.2% for CCR [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Powder:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted SC Suspension:** 
    - Use as soon as possible.
    - If using non-refrigerated water: Stable for 1 hour at 25°C or 8 hours at 2°C–8°C.
    - If using refrigerated water (2°C–8°C): Stable for 22 hours at 2°C–8°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted IV Solution:** Must be administered within 45 minutes of reconstitution [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Injectable azacitidine must not be used interchangeably with oral azacitidine due to differences in exposure and dosing [Australian].
