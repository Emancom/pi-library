# Editorial Product Information: Antithrombin III (Human)

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by integrating clinical and regulatory data from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). While both agencies provide guidance on the use of human-derived antithrombin III, clinicians should note specific regional differences in dosing calculations and storage requirements as indicated in brackets (e.g., [TGA] or [FDA]).

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
*   **Generic Name:** Antithrombin III (Human)
*   **Active Ingredient:** Purified human antithrombin III (ATIII), an alpha2-glycoprotein derived from pooled human plasma.

## 2. Approved Indications
Antithrombin III is indicated for use in both adult and pediatric patients [FDA] with hereditary antithrombin III deficiency in the following clinical settings:
*   **Prophylaxis:** Prevention of thromboembolism and pulmonary embolism during high-risk periods, such as surgical procedures or during pregnancy and childbirth (peri-partum).
*   **Treatment:** Management of acute thrombosis or pulmonary embolism.

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** This product must not be used in patients with a known history of hypersensitivity to human antithrombin III or any of its excipients [TGA].
*   **General:** The FDA identifies no specific contraindications; however, clinical caution regarding hypersensitivity is universally advised.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Pathogen Safety
As a plasma-derived medicinal product, the risk of transmitting infectious agents (e.g., viruses like HIV, HBV, HCV, HAV, and parvovirus B19) cannot be totally excluded. This also applies to the theoretical risk of Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease (CJD) and its variant (vCJD). Safety is maintained through:
*   Rigorous screening of plasma donors and testing of plasma pools.
*   Manufacturing steps dedicated to viral inactivation/removal, such as heat treatment (pasteurization) and nanofiltration.

### Hypersensitivity Reactions
Patients should be monitored for early signs of hypersensitivity, including hives, chest tightness, wheezing, hypotension, or anaphylaxis. If these symptoms occur, the infusion should be discontinued immediately.

### Laboratory Monitoring
To prevent excessive or insufficient anticoagulation, functional plasma ATIII levels must be monitored regularly using amidolytic (chromogenic) or clotting assays. **Note:** Immunoassays are not recommended as they may not accurately detect all hereditary deficiencies [FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Dosing Principles
Dosing must be individualized based on the patient’s baseline ATIII level, body weight, and the desired target level (generally 80% to 120% of normal).

**Loading Dose Formulas:**
The calculated dose differs by region based on expected in vivo recovery:
*   **[TGA Guidance]:** `Dose (IU) = [Desired % - Baseline %] x weight (kg) / 2.2`
*   **[FDA Guidance]:** `Dose (Units) = [Desired % - Baseline %] x weight (kg) / 1.4`

### Maintenance and Monitoring
*   **Target Levels:** Maintain plasma ATIII levels >80% [TGA] or between 80%–120% [FDA].
*   **Frequency:** Levels should be checked every 12 hours initially and before each subsequent dose.
*   **Shortened Half-life:** In settings of acute thrombosis, surgery, or concomitant heparin use, the half-life of ATIII may be significantly reduced, requiring more frequent monitoring and dosing.
*   **Maintenance Dose [FDA]:** A suggested maintenance regimen is 60% of the loading dose administered every 24 hours, adjusted based on trough levels.

### Administration Technique
*   **Reconstitution:** Use the provided sterile water for injection and transfer device. Swirl gently to dissolve; do not shake.
*   **Rate:** Administer slowly via the intravenous route. 
    *   [TGA]: Approximately 3 mL per minute.
    *   [FDA]: Typically the full dose over 10 to 20 minutes.
*   **Compatibility:** Do not mix with other medicinal products or intravenous fluids. Use within 3 hours of reconstitution.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Clinical trials have reported the following adverse reactions:
*   **Common (≥5%):** Dizziness, chest discomfort, nausea, dysgeusia (foul taste), and abdominal cramps/pain [FDA].
*   **Infrequent:** Transient rash, chills, pyrexia, blurred vision, urticaria, and hematoma at the injection site.
*   **Management:** If adverse reactions occur, the infusion rate should be reduced or interrupted until symptoms resolve.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Heparin Synergy:** Antithrombin III significantly enhances the anticoagulant effect of heparin. When administered concurrently, the dosage of heparin (unfractionated or low molecular weight) may need to be reduced to avoid bleeding complications.
*   **Coagulation Tests:** Coagulation parameters (e.g., aPTT) should be monitored closely when ATIII is added to or withdrawn from a heparin regimen.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **Risk Summary:** There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. It should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
*   **Clinical Practice:** Literature supports the safety and efficacy of ATIII in pregnant women with hereditary deficiency for the prevention of peri-partum thromboembolism. 
*   **Labor/Delivery:** It is recommended to suspend heparin administration and continue with ATIII alone during labor and delivery [FDA].

### Breastfeeding
It is not known whether ATIII is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised, and the developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need for the drug.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **In Vivo Recovery:** Incremental recovery ranges from 1.4% to 2.2% per IU/kg administered, depending on the specific product and assay used.
*   **Half-life:** The biological half-life is approximately 2.5 to 3.8 days [FDA], though this can drop to hours in patients with active thrombosis or those undergoing major surgery.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Antithrombin III is the primary physiological inhibitor of thrombin and other serine proteases in the coagulation cascade, including Factors IXa, Xa, XIa, and XIIa. It functions by forming a stable, inactive 1:1 covalent complex with these factors. The rate of these inhibitory reactions is accelerated several thousand-fold in the presence of heparin.

## 11. Storage Conditions
Storage requirements vary by specific product brand and region:
*   **Refrigerated [TGA]:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Protect from light.
*   **Room Temperature [FDA]:** Store at temperatures not exceeding 25°C. Avoid freezing to prevent breakage of the diluent vial.
*   **Post-Reconstitution:** Do not refrigerate after the product has been reconstituted. Administer within 3 hours.
