# Editorial Product Information: Acetazolamide

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized by combining official regulatory guidance from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It serves as a comprehensive reference for healthcare professionals, incorporating the most conservative safety warnings and the broadest range of indications and dosing strategies provided by both agencies. Source-specific variations are noted in brackets (e.g., [TGA] or [FDA]).

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Acetazolamide (Example Brands: Diamox)  
**Active Ingredient:** Acetazolamide  
**Chemical Class:** Non-bacteriostatic sulfonamide; Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitor.

## 2. Approved Indications
Acetazolamide is indicated for the adjunctive treatment of the following conditions:
*   **Glaucoma:** Chronic simple (open-angle) glaucoma, secondary glaucoma, and preoperative management of acute angle-closure glaucoma when a reduction in intraocular pressure is required.
*   **Epilepsy:** Centrencephalic epilepsies, including petit mal and unlocalized seizures.
*   **Edema (Oedema):** Fluid retention associated with congestive heart failure or induced by medication.
*   **Acute Mountain Sickness:** For the prevention or reduction of symptoms associated with rapid ascent in climbers [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
Acetazolamide must not be used in patients with:
*   Known hypersensitivity to acetazolamide, sulfonamides, or any excipients in the formulation.
*   Depleted serum levels of sodium or potassium (hyponatremia or hypokalemia).
*   Marked kidney or liver disease/dysfunction, including cirrhosis (due to the risk of hepatic encephalopathy).
*   Adrenal gland failure (suprarenal gland failure).
*   Hyperchloremic acidosis.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Specifically contraindicated when the glomerular filtration rate (GFR) is less than 10 mL/min [TGA].
*   Chronic non-congestive angle-closure glaucoma (long-term use) as it may mask worsening glaucoma by lowering intraocular pressure while permitting organic closure of the angle.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### General Hypersensitivity
Fatalities have occurred rarely due to severe reactions to sulfonamide derivatives. Acetazolamide should be discontinued immediately if the patient develops a skin rash or a significant drop in formed blood cell elements. Severe reactions may include Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, or fulminant hepatic necrosis.

### Hematological Effects
Periodic monitoring of blood counts and electrolytes is recommended, especially during long-term therapy. Significant blood dyscrasias, such as aplastic anemia or agranulocytosis, have been reported.

### Respiratory and Ocular Risks
*   **Pulmonary Issues:** Caution is advised in patients with pulmonary obstruction or emphysema, as acetazolamide may aggravate acidosis or precipitate respiratory distress.
*   **Ocular Effects:** Cases of choroidal effusion or detachment have been reported, manifesting as acute vision loss or ocular pain. Treatment involves rapid discontinuation of the drug.

### Mental Health and Nervous System
*   **Suicidal Ideation:** Patients treated with anti-epileptic drugs, including acetazolamide for certain indications, should be monitored for changes in mood or suicidal behavior [TGA].
*   **Neurological Impairment:** Drowsiness, paresthesia (tingling in extremities), and ataxia may occur, potentially impairing the ability to drive or operate machinery.

### Metabolic Balance
Acetazolamide may cause electrolyte imbalances (hypokalemia, hyponatremia) and metabolic acidosis. Caution is required in patients with diabetes, as the drug can cause both increases and decreases in blood glucose levels.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
*Note: Dosages should be titrated to individual patient response and ocular tension.*

### Glaucoma
*   **Chronic Open-Angle:** 250 mg to 1 g per 24 hours, usually administered in divided doses for amounts exceeding 250 mg.
*   **Secondary/Acute Closure (Pre-op):** 250 mg every 4 hours, or an initial 500 mg dose followed by 125–250 mg every 4 hours.

### Epilepsy
*   **Adults:** 250 mg to 1,000 mg daily in divided doses. 
*   **Pediatrics:** 8 to 30 mg/kg daily, not to exceed 750 mg/day [TGA].
*   When replacing other anticonvulsants, the transition should be gradual.

### Congestive Heart Failure/Medication-Induced Edema
*   **Initial Dose:** 250 to 375 mg (approx. 5 mg/kg) once daily in the morning.
*   **Maintenance:** For optimal diuresis, acetazolamide should be administered on alternate days or for two days followed by a day of rest to allow for renal recovery.

### Acute Mountain Sickness [FDA]
*   **Dosage:** 500 mg to 1,000 mg daily in divided doses.
*   **Timing:** Dosing should ideally begin 24 to 48 hours before ascent and continue for at least 48 hours while at high altitude.

### Renal Adjustment [TGA]
In patients with a GFR > 10 mL/min, the dose should be reduced by half or the dosing interval increased to every 12 hours.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and melena.
*   **Nervous System:** Paresthesia, drowsiness, confusion, depression, and convulsions.
*   **Dermatological:** Urticaria, photosensitivity, and severe rashes (SJS/TEN).
*   **Metabolic:** Metabolic acidosis, hypokalemia, and taste alterations.
*   **Urogenital:** Crystalluria, increased risk of renal calculi (kidney stones), and glycosuria.
*   **Pediatric Specific:** Growth retardation has been reported in children during long-term therapy, likely due to chronic acidosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Aspirin (High Dose):** Concurrent use may result in severe toxicity, leading to anorexia, tachypnea, lethargy, coma, and even death.
*   **Anticonvulsants:** May increase serum levels of phenytoin (potentially leading to osteomalacia) and primidone.
*   **Lithium:** Increases lithium excretion, potentially reducing its therapeutic effect.
*   **Amphetamines and Quinidine:** Acetazolamide increases urinary pH, which may enhance the effects of these drugs by reducing their excretion.
*   **Sodium Bicarbonate:** Concurrent use increases the risk of renal stone formation.
*   **Cyclosporine:** May elevate cyclosporine blood levels.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**Category B3 [TGA]:** Animal studies have shown teratogenic effects (limb defects) at doses ten times the human recommendation. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Acetazolamide should only be used if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus, and it is generally avoided in the first trimester.

### Breastfeeding
Acetazolamide is excreted in low levels in breast milk. A decision must be made between discontinuing nursing or the drug, considering the treatment's importance to the mother and the potential for adverse reactions in the infant.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
Acetazolamide is rapidly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract. It is not metabolized and is excreted unchanged by the kidneys via both glomerular filtration and tubular secretion. The drug has a high affinity for carbonic anhydrase and is highly concentrated in tissues containing this enzyme, such as red blood cells and the renal cortex.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Acetazolamide is a potent inhibitor of carbonic anhydrase, the enzyme responsible for the reversible hydration of carbon dioxide and dehydration of carbonic acid. 
*   **Ocular:** Reduces the rate of aqueous humor secretion, thereby lowering intraocular pressure.
*   **Renal:** Results in the renal loss of bicarbonate, sodium, and potassium ions, leading to alkaline urine and diuresis.
*   **CNS:** Inhibition of carbonic anhydrase in the central nervous system appears to retard abnormal, excessive neuronal discharge.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Standard Storage:** Store below 30°C [TGA].
*   **Controlled Environment:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [FDA]. 
*   Keep the container tightly closed and out of reach of children.
