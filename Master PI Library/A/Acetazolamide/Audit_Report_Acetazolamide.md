## Close Paraphrases

*   **Drug Interactions:** The list of interactions (Lithium, Amphetamines, Quinidine, Sodium Bicarbonate, Cyclosporine) follows the exact sequence and phrasing found in Doc 2 (p. 4).

## Originality Strengths
*   **Editorial Note:** The introductory paragraph is an original creation that sets the context for the unified document.
*   **Section 11 (Storage):** Successfully merges two different regulatory standards (Celsius vs. Fahrenheit/USP) into a concise, comparative table/list.
*   **Adverse Effects:** This section successfully aggregates the various systems (Gastrointestinal, Nervous, Urogenital) into a bulleted list that, while containing the same facts, uses more concise formatting than the source tables.

## Differences from Source PIs
*   **Harmonisation:** The editorial specifically calls out source-specific requirements, such as identifying that the GFR < 10 mL/min contraindication is a TGA-specific instruction.
*   **Structure:** The editorial imposes a more modern, logical numbering system (1-11) compared to the source documents which vary in their sectioning (e.g., TGA uses a 4.1/4.2/4.3 structure, while FDA uses a standard header format).
*   **Formatting:** Merges the "Acute Mountain Sickness" indication from the FDA source into the primary indications list, noting its absence from the TGA primary indications list.

## Recommendation
**REVISION REQUIRED.**

The editorial fails the originality audit because it relies on verbatim or near-verbatim strings for almost every critical clinical instruction (dosing, contraindications, and warnings). While the synthesis of the two sources is helpful, the writing style too closely mirrors the technical phrasing of the TGA and FDA documents. 

**Required Changes:**
1.  **Rewrite Indications and Dosing:** Use original phrasing to describe the conditions and dosage ranges (e.g., instead of "usually administered in divided doses for amounts exceeding 250 mg," use "doses higher than 250 mg should be split throughout the day").
2.  **Restructure Warnings:** The "Aspirin Interaction" and "Hematological Effects" sections need a complete stylistic overhaul to avoid the exact list of symptoms provided in the sources.
3.  **Reformulate MOA:** The mechanism of action section is currently a collection of sentence fragments lifted directly from the clinical pharmacology sections of the sources. These must be synthesized into a cohesive, original explanation.
