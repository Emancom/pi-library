# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine, USP [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport; naturally occurring quaternary ammonium compound.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic, Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only [US].
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    *   **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    *   **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours.
    *   **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    *   **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations and may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring and Administration Instructions
*   **Pre-treatment Monitoring:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before starting parenteral therapy.
*   **Routine Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended.
*   **Target Concentration:** Plasma free carnitine should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
*   **Visual Inspection:** Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, often within minutes of IV administration. Most reported cases involved patients with ESRD on dialysis. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported [US].
*   **ESRD Patients:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; chronic high doses of oral formulations in ESRD patients may result in the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Common (>5% in ESRD clinical trials, regardless of causality):**
    *   **General:** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain.
    *   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia.
    *   **Digestive:** Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia.
    *   **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia.
    *   **Nervous:** Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia.
    *   **Respiratory:** Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis.
*   **Other Reported Effects:**
    *   **Body Odor:** Noted with levocarnitine therapy.
    *   **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Warnings and Precautions).
    *   **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a specific letter category in provided text]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine after administration, the risks to the human infant of excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug should be considered [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
*   **Geriatric Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety of oral formulations is not established in renal failure (see Warnings and Precautions) [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces).
    *   **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean trough plasma concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
*   **Clinical Outcomes:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific signs, symptoms, or clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
*   **Stability:** When diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
