# Editorial Product Information: amiloride + hydrochlorothiazide

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **amiloride + hydrochlorothiazide**
- Chemical class:
  - Amiloride: potassium‑sparing diuretic (antikaliuretic)
  - Hydrochlorothiazide: thiazide diuretic (sulfonamide derivative)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Oedema of cardiac origin
- Hepatic cirrhosis with ascites
- Hypertension in patients in whom potassium depletion might be anticipated

Notes:
- The combination is described as minimizing the likelihood of excessive potassium loss during vigorous or prolonged diuresis and is particularly indicated where maintaining potassium balance is important.
- May be used alone or as adjunctive therapy with other antihypertensive agents; concomitant antihypertensive dosing may need reduction to avoid excessive blood pressure lowering.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
- Drug strength mentioned in source: combination products described containing amiloride HCl 5 mg plus hydrochlorothiazide 50 mg per oral dose unit.
- General adult dosing:
  - Initial and usual dosing: 1 tablet once daily or 1–2 tablets once daily (or divided doses), depending on indication and clinical response.
  - Maximum: Do not exceed 4 tablets per day.
- Dosing by indication:
  - Oedema of cardiac origin: Start at 1 or 2 tablets daily; increase if necessary but do not exceed 4 tablets/day. Optimal dose determined by diuretic response and serum potassium. After initial diuresis, attempt dose reduction for maintenance; maintenance may be intermittent.
  - Hypertension: Usual dose 1 or 2 tablets once daily or in divided doses; may increase if necessary but not beyond 4 tablets/day.
  - Hepatic cirrhosis with ascites: Initiate with a small dose (1 tablet once daily). If required, increase gradually to achieve effective diuresis. Do not exceed 4 tablets/day. Maintenance doses are often lower; attempt dose reduction once weight stabilises. Gradual weight reduction is desirable to reduce diuretic-associated untoward reactions.
- Administration instructions:
  - Oral administration.
  - Monitor serum electrolytes and renal function at appropriate intervals, particularly during initiation, dose titration, or in patients at higher risk of electrolyte disturbances.
- Titration schedules:
  - Gradual dose escalation recommended when initiating in cirrhosis and when increasing for inadequate response; reduce dose when maintenance achieved.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List of contraindications provided in source material:
- Hyperkalaemia (interpreted in source as serum potassium > 5.5 mmol/L).
- Concomitant antikaliuretic therapy, potassium supplementation or potassium‑rich diet (combination therapy with other antikaliuretic agents or potassium supplements is contraindicated).
- Impaired renal function:
  - Anuria.
  - Acute renal failure.
  - Severe progressive renal disease.
  - Diabetic nephropathy.
  - Patients with BUN > 10.7 mmol/L, serum creatinine > 0.13 mmol/L, or whole blood urea > 10.0 mmol/L should not receive the drug without careful, frequent monitoring of serum electrolytes and BUN levels (as per source).
- Known hypersensitivity to either component or to other sulfonamide‑derived drugs.
- Paediatric use: Safety for use of amiloride in children has not been established; therefore the product is not recommended in the paediatric age group (listed under contraindications/contra‑recommendation in source).
- Note: The source also warns that hydrochlorothiazide should not be administered to patients who previously experienced acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) following hydrochlorothiazide intake (see Warnings and Precautions). This is presented as a safety restriction in the source.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Highlighted warnings
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Risk of clinically significant hyperkalaemia (serum K+ > 5.5 mmol/L) has been observed with amiloride-containing therapy, particularly in elderly, diabetic, hospitalized patients, those with hepatic cirrhosis or cardiac oedema with renal involvement, seriously ill patients, and during vigorous diuresis. Hyperkalaemia can be fatal. Monitor serum potassium and ECG as clinically indicated.
- **Non‑melanoma skin cancer (NMSC):** An increased risk of basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma with increasing cumulative exposure to hydrochlorothiazide has been observed in epidemiological studies. Patients should be counseled to monitor skin for suspicious lesions and to minimize UV exposure and use protective measures.
- **Acute respiratory toxicity/ARDS:** Severe acute respiratory toxicity including ARDS and pulmonary oedema have been reported after hydrochlorothiazide intake; typically occurs within minutes to hours. If ARDS suspected, discontinue therapy immediately.

### General warnings and precautions
- Monitor for electrolyte disturbances: hyponatraemia, hypochloraemic alkalosis, hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, and possible reversible increases in blood urea nitrogen (BUN) during vigorous diuresis.
- Signs/symptoms of fluid or electrolyte imbalance: dryness of mouth, thirst, weakness, lethargy, drowsiness, restlessness, seizures, confusion, muscle pains/cramps, muscular fatigue, hypotension, oliguria, tachycardia, nausea/vomiting.
- Hyponatraemia may be dilutional in oedematous patients during hot weather — treat by water restriction rather than salt administration unless salt depletion is present.
- Hypokalaemia may still occur in certain circumstances (e.g., brisk diuresis, severe cirrhosis, concomitant corticosteroid/ACTH use, prolonged therapy) despite the presence of amiloride.
- Metabolic/respiratory acidosis: Use antikaliuretic therapy with caution in patients prone to acidosis (e.g., cardiopulmonary disease, decompensated diabetes) since acidosis can increase serum potassium.
- Diabetes: In diabetic or suspected diabetic patients, renal function should be known prior to initiating therapy. Amiloride may precipitate hyperkalaemia in diabetics, especially with renal impairment. Hydrochlorothiazide may alter glucose tolerance tests and may unmask latent diabetes. Discontinue at least 3 days before conducting a glucose tolerance test. Insulin or oral antidiabetic requirements may increase, decrease, or remain unchanged during thiazide therapy.
- Azotaemia/renal impairment: Hydrochlorothiazide may precipitate or worsen azotaemia; discontinue if increasing azotaemia and oliguria occur.
- Hepatic disease: In severe pre‑existing liver disease, diuretics including this combination have been associated with hepatic encephalopathy, increased jaundice and worsening clinical status. Use with caution; in cirrhosis, start low and titrate carefully.
- Choroidal effusion, acute myopia and secondary angle‑closure glaucoma: Sulfonamide diuretics (including hydrochlorothiazide) can cause idiosyncratic reactions leading to choroidal effusion and angle‑closure glaucoma. Symptoms (acute decreased visual acuity or ocular pain) typically occur within hours to weeks of initiation. Discontinue drug promptly if suspected and manage appropriately.
- Sensitivity reactions: Thiazide‑associated hypersensitivity reactions can occur in patients with or without prior allergy or bronchial asthma. Possible exacerbation or activation of systemic lupus erythematosus has been reported with thiazides.
- Metabolic effects: Hyperuricaemia and precipitation of gout; thiazides decrease urinary calcium excretion and may interfere with parathyroid function tests; prolonged use has been associated with hypercalcaemia and hypophosphataemia in some patients. Thiazides may increase serum cholesterol and triglyceride levels.
- Use in elderly: No data available in source; exercise caution given potential for electrolyte and renal function abnormalities.
- Paediatric use: Safety not established; not recommended.
- Effects on laboratory tests: Thiazides may interfere with tests for parathyroid function; other effects on laboratory tests not specified.

### Monitoring requirements
- Frequent monitoring of serum electrolytes (including potassium, sodium, chloride, bicarbonate), renal function (BUN/creatinine), and clinical status during initiation, titration, or in patients at risk of renal impairment, fluid/electrolyte disturbances, or hyperkalaemia.
- ECG monitoring when hyperkalaemia suspected or in patients at risk of significant electrolyte‑related cardiac effects.
- Monitor skin for suspicious lesions with long‑term hydrochlorothiazide exposure.
- In diabetic patients, monitor blood glucose and adjust antidiabetic therapy as needed.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions and clinically significant interaction management
- Agents that increase risk of hyperkalaemia:
  - Angiotensin‑converting enzyme inhibitors (ACE inhibitors), angiotensin II receptor antagonists, cyclosporine, tacrolimus: concomitant use with amiloride may increase hyperkalaemia risk. If used, do so with caution and with frequent monitoring of serum potassium.
- Combination therapy increasing risk of renal impairment:
  - Combined use of an ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor antagonist, an anti‑inflammatory drug (NSAID or COX‑2 inhibitor), and a thiazide diuretic increases risk of renal impairment; use with caution and increase monitoring of serum creatinine, particularly at initiation. This combination is of special concern in elderly and those with pre‑existing renal impairment.
- NSAIDs (including COX‑2 inhibitors):
  - May reduce diuretic and antihypertensive effects and in some patients reduce the natriuretic/diuretic effect; coadministration with potassium‑sparing agents (amiloride) may cause hyperkalaemia and renal failure, particularly in elderly or volume‑depleted patients. Monitor renal function and serum potassium.
- Lithium:
  - Diuretics reduce lithium renal clearance and can lead to lithium toxicity. Lithium generally should not be given with diuretics.
- Other antihypertensive drugs:
  - Additive blood pressure lowering effects; dose reduction of concomitant agents may be required.
- Corticosteroids, ACTH:
  - Intensified electrolyte depletion, particularly hypokalaemia (relevant to the hydrochlorothiazide component).
- Antidiabetic agents (oral hypoglycaemics, insulin):
  - Thiazides may alter glucose tolerance; dosage adjustment of antidiabetic therapy may be required.
- Alcohol, barbiturates, narcotics:
  - Potentiation of orthostatic hypotension may occur.
- Pressor amines (e.g., noradrenaline):
  - Possible decreased response.
- Skeletal muscle relaxants, non‑depolarising (e.g., tubocurarine):
  - Possible increased responsiveness to the muscle relaxant.
- Cholestyramine and colestipol:
  - Absorption of hydrochlorothiazide is impaired by anionic exchange resins; single doses of cholestyramine or colestipol may reduce hydrochlorothiazide absorption by up to 85% and 43%, respectively.
- Other interactions:
  - Thiazide diuretics may interfere with tests for parathyroid function due to effects on calcium metabolism.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Specific metabolic or distribution interactions not detailed in available sources.

### Management recommendations
- Monitor serum electrolytes and renal function when combining with agents that affect renal function or potassium balance.
- Avoid combining potassium supplements or other potassium‑sparing agents unless under careful monitoring.
- Consider temporary discontinuation of diuretics 2–3 days prior to initiation of ACE inhibitors to reduce risk of first‑dose hypotension (as per source).
- When initiating or adjusting combinations that increase risk of renal impairment (ACE inhibitor/ARB + NSAID + thiazide), monitor serum creatinine and electrolytes closely.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy classification (as provided in source): **Category C [Australian]**.
- Key pregnancy information from source:
  - Thiazides and related diuretics cross the placenta and may cause fetal electrolyte disturbances. Neonatal thrombocytopenia has been reported with thiazides.
  - During the latter part of pregnancy products of this type should only be given for clear indications and then at the lowest effective dose.
  - Use in pregnancy: Not recommended unless potential benefit justifies potential risk to fetus; if pregnancy is present or suspected, weigh potential benefits against possible hazards (including fetal/neonatal jaundice, thrombocytopenia, and other possible side effects).
  - Routine use of diuretics in otherwise healthy pregnant women with or without mild oedema is not indicated.
- If information from other regions differs: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Thiazides appear in breast milk. If treatment is essential, breastfeeding should be discontinued (as per source).

### Fertility
- Effects on fertility: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety in children: Not established. The product is not recommended in the paediatric age group (source).

### Elderly
- Specific dose adjustments: No data available in source. Exercise caution due to potential for renal impairment, electrolyte disturbances, and interactions.

### Renal impairment
- Contraindicated in anuria, acute renal failure, severe progressive renal disease and diabetic nephropathy.
- Source provides laboratory thresholds at which use should be avoided or require frequent monitoring: BUN > 10.7 mmol/L, serum creatinine > 0.13 mmol/L, whole blood urea > 10.0 mmol/L (use only with careful, frequent monitoring of serum electrolytes and BUN).
- Hyperkalaemia risk is accentuated in renal impairment; monitor frequently and avoid concomitant potassium‑raising therapies.

### Hepatic impairment
- In severe pre‑existing liver disease, diuretic therapy (including this combination) has been associated with hepatic encephalopathy and increased jaundice; use with caution. In cirrhotic patients, initiate with small doses and titrate gradually; maintenance doses may be lower.
