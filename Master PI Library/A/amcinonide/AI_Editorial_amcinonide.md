# Editorial Product Information: Amcinonide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local PI for definitive regulatory guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Amcinonide (a synthetic fluorinated corticosteroid).
- **Chemical Class:** Topical Corticosteroid (Group II/III - High/Medium potency, depending on vehicle and concentration).
- **Brand Name(s):** Amcinonide [US].
- **ATC Code:** D07AC11.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Ointment: 0.1% (1 mg/g) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical (Dermatologic use only).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ayurax, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Corticosteroid-Responsive Dermatoses:** Indicated for the relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of dermatoses that respond to topical corticosteroids [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Apply a thin film of the 0.1% ointment to the affected area two to three times daily, depending on the severity of the condition [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Not for ophthalmic use.
    - Avoid contact with the eyes.
    - Occlusive dressings may be used as a therapeutic adjunct for the management of psoriasis or other recalcitrant conditions [US].
- **Management of Infection:** If a dermatological infection develops, the use of occlusive dressings must be discontinued and appropriate antimicrobial therapy initiated [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to amcinonide or any of the inactive components of the preparation (e.g., benzyl alcohol, white petrolatum, propylene glycol) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **HPA Axis Suppression:** Systemic absorption of topical corticosteroids can produce reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression. This may lead to manifestations of Cushing's syndrome, hyperglycemia, and glucosuria in some patients [US].
- **Risk Factors for Absorption:** Risk is increased by the use of high-potency steroids, application over large surface areas, prolonged use, and the addition of occlusive dressings [US].
- **Monitoring:** Patients receiving high doses over large areas or under occlusion should be periodically evaluated for HPA axis suppression using the urinary free-cortisol test or ACTH stimulation test [US].
- **Withdrawal:** If HPA axis suppression is detected, clinicians should attempt to withdraw the drug, reduce the frequency of application, or substitute with a less potent corticosteroid. Recovery is generally prompt upon discontinuation, though steroid withdrawal symptoms may infrequently require supplemental systemic corticosteroids [US].
- **Local Irritation:** If irritation occurs, discontinue use and institute appropriate therapy [US].
- **Concomitant Infections:** If a skin infection is present, appropriate antifungal or antibacterial agents should be used. If the infection does not respond promptly, discontinue the corticosteroid until the infection is controlled [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are reported infrequently but may occur more often with the use of occlusive dressings. They are listed below in approximate descending order of occurrence [US]:

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Infrequent/Unknown** | Burning, itching, irritation, dryness, folliculitis, hypertrichosis, acneiform eruptions, hypopigmentation, perioral dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis, maceration of the skin, secondary infection, skin atrophy, striae, and miliaria. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Dynamic Interactions:** Specific drug interaction studies were not detailed in the available source [US]. 
- **Clinical Management:** As with all topical corticosteroids, the cumulative effect of using multiple corticosteroid-containing products (topical, oral, or inhaled) should be considered regarding systemic toxicity.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C [US]:** Corticosteroids are generally teratogenic in laboratory animals at low dosages systemically, and potent corticosteroids have shown teratogenicity after dermal application in animals. 
    - **Recommendation:** There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Amcinonide should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. It should not be used extensively, in large amounts, or for prolonged periods in pregnant patients [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown whether topical administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable quantities in breast milk. Systemically administered corticosteroids are secreted into breast milk but are unlikely to have a deleterious effect on the infant. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the clinical importance of the medication to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies have not been performed to evaluate the effect of topical amcinonide on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Pediatric patients are more susceptible to HPA axis suppression and Cushing’s syndrome than adults due to a higher skin surface area-to-body weight ratio [US].
    - Reported effects in children include linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, and low plasma cortisol levels. 
    - Manifestations of intracranial hypertension (e.g., bulging fontanelles, headaches, bilateral papilledema) have also been reported [US].
    - **Recommendation:** Administration should be limited to the least amount compatible with an effective therapeutic regimen. Parents should be advised not to use tight-fitting diapers or plastic pants on a child treated in the diaper area, as these act as occlusive dressings [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Like other topical corticosteroids, amcinonide has anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. The exact mechanism of anti-inflammatory activity remains unclear [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Vasoconstrictor assays are used to compare potencies; evidence suggests a correlation exists between vasoconstrictor potency and therapeutic efficacy in humans [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Determined by the vehicle, integrity of the epidermal barrier, and use of occlusive dressings. Inflammation or disease processes in the skin increase percutaneous absorption [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Once absorbed systemically, corticosteroids are primarily metabolized in the liver [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted by the kidneys; some corticosteroids and their metabolites are also excreted into the bile [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trial Data:** Detailed clinical trial results were not provided in the source PI. Efficacy is established based on the class properties of topical corticosteroids and vasoconstrictor potency assays [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children. Do not freeze. Ensure the seal is intact before first use [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine) [US].
- **NDC Code:** 73308-407-60 (60 gram tube) [US].
