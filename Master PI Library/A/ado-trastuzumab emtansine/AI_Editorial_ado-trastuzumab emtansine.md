# Editorial Product Information: Ado-trastuzumab Emtansine (Kadcyla)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory guidance into a clinically actionable format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the local approved labelling before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ado-trastuzumab emtansine (a HER2-targeted antibody and microtubule inhibitor conjugate).
- **Chemical Class:** Antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) consisting of the humanised IgG1 trastuzumab covalently linked to the cytotoxin DM1 (a maytansine derivative) via the stable thioether linker MCC.
- **Brand Name:** **Kadcyla** [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01FX05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Lyophilized powder for injection in single-dose vials: 100 mg and 160 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Genentech, Inc. (A member of the Roche Group) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Breast Cancer (MBC):** As a single agent for the treatment of HER2-positive, metastatic breast cancer in patients who previously received trastuzumab and a taxane (separately or in combination). Eligible patients must have received prior therapy for metastatic disease or developed recurrence within six months of completing adjuvant therapy [US].
- **Early Breast Cancer (EBC):** As a single agent for the adjuvant treatment of HER2-positive early breast cancer in patients who have residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant taxane and trastuzumab-based treatment [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 
    - The recommended dose is 3.6 mg/kg administered intravenously every 3 weeks (21-day cycle).
    - **Maximum Dose:** Do not exceed 3.6 mg/kg.
    - **Interchangeability:** **DO NOT substitute Kadcyla for or with trastuzumab.**
- **Duration of Treatment:**
    - **MBC:** Until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
    - **EBC:** For a total of 14 cycles, unless disease recurs or toxicity becomes unmanageable.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer via IV infusion with a 0.2 or 0.22-micron in-line polyethersulfone (PES) filter.
    - **First infusion:** Administer over 90 minutes. Observe for at least 90 minutes post-dose for infusion-related reactions (IRRs).
    - **Subsequent infusions:** Administer over 30 minutes if previous infusions were well tolerated. Observe for at least 30 minutes post-dose.
    - **Dilution:** Use 0.9% Sodium Chloride; **do not use Dextrose (5%) solution.**
- **Dose Modifications:**
    - **Dose Reduction Schedule:** First reduction to 3 mg/kg; second reduction to 2.4 mg/kg. If further reduction is required, discontinue treatment.
    - **Re-escalation:** Once a dose is reduced, do not re-escalate.
- **Missed Dose:** Administer as soon as possible; do not wait until the next planned cycle. Adjust the schedule to maintain a 3-week interval.

## 4. Contraindications
- None listed in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [US]:**
    - **Hepatotoxicity:** Serious hepatotoxicity, including liver failure and death, has occurred. Monitor transaminases and bilirubin prior to each dose.
    - **Cardiac Toxicity:** Risk of reductions in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF). Assess LVEF prior to and during treatment.
    - **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Exposure during pregnancy can result in fetal harm.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** 
    - Monitor serum transaminases and bilirubin before each dose. 
    - Permanently discontinue if transaminases > 3 × ULN and total bilirubin > 2 × ULN.
    - Cases of **Nodular Regenerative Hyperplasia (NRH)** of the liver have been identified; NRH requires permanent discontinuation.
- **Left Ventricular Dysfunction:** 
    - Monitor LVEF every 3 months. 
    - Withhold if LVEF is < 40% (MBC) or < 45% (EBC), or if there is a significant absolute decrease from baseline.
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** Permanently discontinue for interstitial lung disease (ILD) or pneumonitis.
- **Hemorrhage:** Fatal cases have occurred. Use caution with anticoagulants/antiplatelets and in patients with thrombocytopenia.
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Monitor platelet counts prior to each dose. Incidence is higher in Asian patients [US].
- **Neurotoxicity:** Monitor for peripheral neuropathy; withhold dosing for Grade 3 or 4 symptoms.
- **Extravasation:** Monitor the infusion site for infiltration; specific treatment is unknown.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (≥ 25%):**
    - **MBC:** Fatigue, nausea, musculoskeletal pain, hemorrhage, thrombocytopenia, headache, increased transaminases, constipation, and epistaxis.
    - **EBC:** Fatigue, nausea, increased transaminases, musculoskeletal pain, hemorrhage, thrombocytopenia, headache, peripheral neuropathy, and arthralgia.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Hepatotoxicity, LVEF reduction, ILD/pneumonitis, severe hemorrhage, Grade 3-4 thrombocytopenia, and Grade 3-4 peripheral neuropathy.
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs):** Characterized by flushing, chills, pyrexia, and dyspnea. Overall incidence ~1.4–1.6%.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** DM1 (the cytotoxic component) is metabolized by CYP3A4/5. Concomitant use of strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir) should be avoided due to the risk of increased DM1 exposure and toxicity.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - Use caution with **anticoagulants and antiplatelet agents** due to the increased risk of hemorrhage.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified as a letter grade under current US labeling, but carries a Boxed Warning for embryo-fetal toxicity. 
    - Exposure to trastuzumab (the antibody component) is associated with oligohydramnios, pulmonary hypoplasia, and neonatal death.
    - **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for **7 months** after the final dose. 
    - **Male Patients:** Advise use of effective contraception for **4 months** after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Advise women not to breastfeed during treatment and for 7 months after the last dose due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants.
- **Fertility:** Based on animal data, may impair female and male fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety/efficacy observed in patients ≥ 65 years. Insufficient data for patients ≥ 75 years to draw conclusions.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment needed for mild (CLcr 60–89 mL/min) or moderate (CLcr 30–59 mL/min) impairment. Data are limited for severe impairment (CLcr < 30 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment to starting dose for mild or moderate impairment. Not studied in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C). Closely monitor all hepatic-impaired patients.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Binds to sub-domain IV of the HER2 receptor. Upon internalization, the conjugate undergoes lysosomal degradation, releasing DM1. DM1 binds to tubulin, disrupting microtubule networks, causing cell cycle arrest and apoptosis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Central volume of distribution of ADC is 3.13 L.
    - **Metabolism:** DM1 metabolized by CYP3A4/5. 
    - **Elimination:** Clearance is 0.68 L/day; terminal half-life is approximately 4 days. No accumulation observed after repeated 3-week dosing.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **EMILIA (MBC):** A randomized, open-label trial (n=991). Kadcyla demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in progression-free survival (9.6 vs 6.4 months; HR 0.65) and overall survival (30.9 vs 25.1 months; HR 0.68) compared to lapatinib plus capecitabine.
- **KATHERINE (EBC):** A randomized, open-label trial (n=1486). Kadcyla showed significant improvement in invasive disease-free survival (IDFS) compared to trastuzumab (HR 0.50) in patients with residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant therapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Do not freeze or shake.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Can be stored up to 120 hours (5 days) at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Diluted Infusion Bag:** Use immediately; if necessary, store for up to 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C. This 24-hour period is in addition to the 120 hours allowed for reconstituted vials.
