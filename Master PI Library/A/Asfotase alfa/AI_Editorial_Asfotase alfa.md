# Editorial Product Information: Asfotase alfa (Strensiq)

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- Information from other major regulatory authorities (e.g., US FDA, European EMA) was not provided in the source material for this specific draft; therefore, this document primarily reflects Australian regulatory stances and clinical data.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Asfotase alfa rch.
- **Chemical Class:** Human recombinant tissue-nonspecific alkaline phosphatase (TNSALP) – Fc-deca-aspartate fusion protein produced by recombinant DNA technology using mammalian Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cell culture.
- **Brand Name:** **Strensiq** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile, preservative-free solution for subcutaneous injection.
    - Available in single-use vials of 40 mg/mL (strengths: 12 mg, 18 mg, 28 mg, 40 mg) or 100 mg/mL (strength: 80 mg) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alexion Pharmaceuticals [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Paediatric-Onset Hypophosphatasia (HPP):** Indicated as enzyme replacement therapy for patients with a diagnosis of paediatric-onset HPP [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended total weekly dose is 6 mg/kg of body weight.
- **Dosing Regimens:**
    - 2 mg/kg administered subcutaneously three times per week, OR
    - 1 mg/kg administered subcutaneously six times per week [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Maximum Volume:** The maximum volume per injection site is 1 mL. If the required dose exceeds 1 mL, the volume must be split and administered at separate sites using separate syringes.
    - **Preparation:** Vials should be removed from refrigeration 15–30 minutes prior to injection to reach room temperature. Do not heat via other methods (e.g., microwave).
    - **Technique:** Use aseptic technique. A larger bore needle (e.g., 25 gauge) is recommended for withdrawal, and a smaller bore (e.g., 29 gauge) for injection. 
    - **Site Rotation:** Rotate sites among the abdomen, thigh, deltoid, or buttocks. Do not inject into areas that are reddened, inflamed, or swollen.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to asfotase alfa, Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell proteins, or any excipients.
- **Severity:** Severe or life-threatening hypersensitivity to the active substance or excipients if the reaction is not controllable [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis:** Reactions consistent with anaphylaxis (e.g., respiratory distress, periorbital oedema, dizziness) have occurred within minutes of administration. These may occur even after one year of treatment. If a severe reaction occurs, immediate discontinuation is recommended.
- **Injection Site Reactions (ISRs):** Very common (e.g., erythema, rash, pain, atrophy). Most are mild-to-moderate, but severe cases have led to discontinuation.
- **Lipodystrophy:** Localised lipoatrophy or lipohypertrophy may occur after several months of therapy. Strict adherence to site rotation is required.
- **Craniosynostosis:** Reported in HPP patients < 5 years of age. While HPP itself causes craniosynostosis, periodic monitoring (including fundoscopy for papilloedema) is recommended in this age group.
- **Ectopic Calcification:** Ophthalmic (corneal/conjunctival) calcification and nephrocalcinosis have been reported. Baseline and periodic ophthalmology exams and renal ultrasounds are recommended.
- **Lab Interference:** 
    - **ALP-conjugated assays:** Asfotase alfa can interfere with clinical laboratory assays that use alkaline phosphatase as a detection reagent, leading to erroneous results. Clinicians must inform laboratory personnel if a patient is receiving **Strensiq** and request non-ALP-conjugated platforms.
    - **Serum ALP levels:** Treated patients will show high serum ALP measurements reflecting the circulating drug; these should not be used for clinical decision-making.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Monitor serum parathyroid hormone (PTH) and calcium, as PTH may increase during the first 12 weeks of therapy.

### Very Common (>10%)
| Adverse Event | Frequency (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Injection site reactions (ISRs) | 74% |
| Tooth loss | 43.8% |
| Ectopic calcifications | 39% |
| Upper respiratory tract infection | 36.6% |
| Pain in extremity | 29.5% |
| Nasopharyngitis | 26.8% |

| Diarrhoea / Vomiting | 25–40% |
| Craniosynostosis | 23.2% |
| Hypersensitivity reactions | 20% |

### Common (1–10%) or Less Frequent
- Hypocalcaemia, renal stones, chronic hepatitis, decreased vitamin B6, skin discolouration, and anaemia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal studies performed. Asfotase alfa is a large fusion protein and is unlikely to be a candidate for Cytochrome P450-mediated interactions.
- **Management:** No specific drug-drug interactions identified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C.** 
    - No data available in pregnant women. Animal studies are insufficient regarding skeletal effects. Use is not recommended during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using contraception [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown. A risk to newborns cannot be excluded; breastfeeding should not be commenced while on treatment.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at doses up to 19 times the human clinical exposure.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Studied in patients aged 0–18 years. Dosing is based strictly on body weight.
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients >65 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and efficacy not studied; no specific dose regimens can be recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** HPP is caused by a deficiency in TNSALP activity, leading to elevated inorganic pyrophosphate (PPi). PPi blocks hydroxyapatite crystal growth, causing rickets and osteomalacia. Asfotase alfa replaces the missing enzyme, reduces PPi levels, and promotes bone mineralisation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 45.8–98.4%. Median $T_{max}$ is 1–2 days.
    - **Distribution:** Primarily distributes to intra-vascular space before partitioning into tissues, including bone.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is approximately 5 days.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reductions in plasma PPi and PLP (pyridoxal 5'-phosphate) typically occur within 6–12 weeks of treatment.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Data:** Evidence is based on four prospective studies (including ENB-002-08, ENB-010-10, and ENB-006-09) involving 112 patients (perinatal to adult-onset).
- **Key Outcomes:**
    - **Survival:** In perinatal/infantile-onset HPP, 1-year survival was 94% in treated patients vs. 42% in historical controls.
    - **Skeletal Manifestations:** Significant radiologic improvements were noted on the RGI-C (Radiographic Global Impression of Change) scale, with most patients classified as "responders."
    - **Physical Function:** Improvements in gait and 6-minute walk test (6MWT) were sustained over 6 years in juvenile-onset patients.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. **Do not freeze.**
- **Light:** Store in the original packaging to protect from light.
- **Post-Refrigeration:** Once removed from the refrigerator, the product should be administered within 3 hours.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date (EXP) on the packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
