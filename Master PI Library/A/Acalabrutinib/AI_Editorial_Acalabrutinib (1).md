## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dose for CLL (adults ≥18 years): 100 mg orally twice daily (one 100 mg capsule twice daily), either as monotherapy or in combination with obinutuzumab; doses should be separated by approximately 12 hours [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2 "Recommended dosage (18 years and above)"].  
- When given with obinutuzumab on the same day, administer acalabrutinib prior to obinutuzumab [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2 "Recommended dosage (18 years and above)"].

### Administration instructions
- Swallow the capsule whole with water at approximately the same time each day; acalabrutinib can be taken with or without food; do not chew, dissolve, or open the capsule [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2 "Method of administration"].  
- If a dose is missed by more than 3 hours, take the next dose at the regularly scheduled time; do not take extra capsules to make up a missed dose [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2 "Missed dose"].

### Dose modifications and interruptions
- For Grade ≥3 adverse reactions: temporarily interrupt acalabrutinib; on recovery to Grade 1 or baseline, resume at 100 mg twice daily for first/second Grade ≥3 non‑haematologic toxicities or Grade 3 thrombocytopenia with significant bleeding. For recurrent severe events, resume at 100 mg once daily after recovery, and discontinue for defined fourth-level toxicity per the PI dosing table. See full dose modification table in the TGA PI [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2 "Dose adjustments" and Table 1].  
- Dose adjustments when co‑administered with CYP3A inhibitors/inducers or gastric acid reducing agents are described (see Section 8 in this document) and in TGA PI Table 2 [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2 "Dose adjustments" and Table 2].

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources (no boxed warnings included in the provided sections). [Source: TGA PI, provided sections]

### General warnings and precautions
- Haemorrhage: Serious and fatal haemorrhagic events have occurred with acalabrutinib; major haemorrhage (Grade ≥3, serious, or any CNS events) occurred in 3.6% of patients in the combined safety database; fatalities in 0.1%; overall bleeding events (including bruising and petechiae) occurred in 46% of patients with haematologic malignancies in the combined database. Concomitant antithrombotic agents may increase bleeding risk; assess risks and benefits of antithrombotic therapy when co‑administered. Consider withholding acalabrutinib 3–7 days pre‑ and post‑surgery depending on bleeding risk [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Haemorrhage"].

- Infection: Serious infections (bacterial, viral, fungal), including fatal events, have occurred. Grade ≥3 infections occurred in 18% of patients in the combined safety database; pneumonia was the most frequent Grade ≥3 infection. Cases of hepatitis B reactivation, aspergillosis, and progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) have occurred. Consider prophylaxis for patients at increased risk for opportunistic infections. Monitor and treat infections promptly [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Infection"].

- Cytopenias: Grade 3–4 cytopenias observed in clinical trials (neutropenia 21%, anaemia 10%, thrombocytopenia 7% in the combined safety database). Monitor complete blood counts as clinically appropriate during treatment [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Cytopenias"].

- Second primary malignancies: Second primary malignancies (including non-skin cancers) occurred in 12% of patients in the combined safety database; skin cancers were the most frequent (7%); advise sun protection and monitor for skin cancers [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Second primary malignancies"].

- Cardiac arrhythmias: Atrial fibrillation and atrial flutter have been reported; in the combined safety database Grade 3 atrial fibrillation/flutter occurred in 1% and Grade 1–2 in 3% of patients. Monitor for arrhythmia symptoms and obtain ECGs as appropriate [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Cardiac arrhythmias including atrial fibrillation and flutter"].

- Hepatotoxicity / drug‑induced liver injury (DILI): Cases of severe, life‑threatening, and fatal liver injury have occurred with BTK inhibitors including acalabrutinib. Evaluate bilirubin and transaminases at baseline and regularly during treatment; withhold acalabrutinib if DILI is suspected and discontinue if DILI is confirmed [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Hepatotoxicity"].

- Tumour lysis syndrome (TLS): TLS was reported in patients treated with acalabrutinib plus obinutuzumab (2% in ELEVATE‑TN) and with acalabrutinib monotherapy or comparator arms in other trials at low frequency; monitor and manage TLS risk clinically [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 "Tumour Lysis Syndrome"; trial tables].

- Infusion-related reactions: Infusion reactions reported when acalabrutinib was used in combination with intravenous anti‑CD20 antibody (e.g., obinutuzumab) — reported incidences vary by regimen (e.g., 14% with acalabrutinib + obinutuzumab vs 40% in obinutuzumab + chlorambucil arm in one trial) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 "Infusion related reaction" and Table 5].

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic monitoring as clinically appropriate:
  - Complete blood counts to monitor for cytopenias [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Cytopenias"].  
  - Liver function tests (bilirubin and transaminases) at baseline and during treatment [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Hepatotoxicity"].  
  - Monitor for signs of infection and bleeding; assess cardiac symptoms and ECG as indicated [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Infection"; "Haemorrhage"; "Cardiac arrhythmias including atrial fibrillation and flutter"].

### Special clinical precautions
- Withhold around surgical procedures depending on bleeding risk (consider 3–7 days pre‑ and post‑surgery) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Haemorrhage"].  
- Consider prophylaxis in patients at increased risk for opportunistic infections [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Infection"].  
- Use caution and monitor in elderly patients; no dose modification solely for age was required in trials, but monitor as clinically appropriate [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Use in the elderly"; Section 4.2 "Use in the elderly"].

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The following adverse reactions are reported in the TGA capsule PI clinical trial experience and combined safety database. Frequencies are presented where available from the TGA PI tables (ELEVATE‑TN and ASCEND) and combined safety data. Each entry is cited to the PI section/tables as indicated.

Note: Frequency categories used here follow the requested convention (very common >10%; common 1–10%; uncommon 0.1–1%; rare <0.1%; very rare <0.01%). Frequency estimates are taken from the TGA PI tables and descriptions; where multiple trial arms provide different percentages, the values are listed with trial context and source.

### Summary (most common events)
- Very common (≥10%):
  - Infection (all grades): e.g., 69% in acalabrutinib + obinutuzumab arm in ELEVATE‑TN; grade ≥3 infection 21% in that arm [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 Table 5].  
  - Neutropenia / decreased absolute neutrophil count: e.g., decreased ANC all grades 53% (acalabrutinib + obinutuzumab, ELEVATE‑TN) and 47% (acalabrutinib, ASCEND); grade ≥3 neutropenia 35% (ELEVATE‑TN arm) and 22% (ASCEND) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 Table 6; Table 8].  
  - Anaemia / haemoglobin decreased: e.g., 51% all grades (acalabrutinib + obinutuzumab, ELEVATE‑TN) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 Table 6].  
  - Thrombocytopenia / platelets decreased: e.g., 51% all grades (acalabrutinib + obinutuzumab, ELEVATE‑TN) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 Table 6].  
  - Headache: 40% (acalabrutinib + obinutuzumab, ELEVATE‑TN); 22% (acalabrutinib, ASCEND) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 Tables 5 and 7].  
  - Diarrhoea: 39% (acalabrutinib + obinutuzumab, ELEVATE‑TN); 35% (acalabrutinib monotherapy, ELEVATE‑TN); 18% (acalabrutinib, ASCEND) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 Tables 5 and 7].  
  - Musculoskeletal pain (includes musculoskeletal pain terms): 37% (acalabrutinib + obinutuzumab, ELEVATE‑TN); 15% (acalabrutinib, ASCEND) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 Tables 5 and 7].  
  - Bruising: 34% (acalabrutinib + obinutuzumab, ELEVATE‑TN); 12% (acalabrutinib, ASCEND) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 Tables 5 and 7].  
  - Nausea: 20% (acalabrutinib + obinutuzumab, ELEVATE‑TN); 7% (acalabrutinib, ASCEND) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 Tables 5 and 7].  

- Common (1–10%):
  - Vomiting, constipation, abdominal pain, fatigue, asthenia, arthralgia, rash, haemorrhage/haematoma — see trial-specific frequencies in tables below [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 Tables 5 and 7].  
  - Atrial fibrillation/atrial flutter: reported 3–5% across trial arms; grade ≥3 events ~1% in some arms [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Cardiac arrhythmias" and Section 4.8 Tables 5 and 7].  
  - Infusion-related reaction when combined with intravenous anti‑CD20 antibody: 14% in the acalabrutinib + obinutuzumab arm (ELEVATE‑TN) vs 40% in obinutuzumab + chlorambucil comparator arm [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 "Infusion related reaction" and Table 5].  

- Serious / notable adverse reactions (trial or combined safety database data):
  - Major haemorrhage (Grade ≥3 or serious): 3.6% (combined safety database); fatal haemorrhage 0.1% [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Haemorrhage"].  
  - Grade 3–4 cytopenias (neutropenia 21%, anaemia 10%, thrombocytopenia 7% in the combined database) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Cytopenias"].  
  - Second primary malignancies (including skin cancers): occurred in 12% of patients in combined safety database; skin cancer in 7% [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Second primary malignancies"].  
  - Serious infections including pneumonia; HBV reactivation, aspergillosis, and PML have been reported (see Warnings) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Infection"].  
  - Tumour lysis syndrome: reported in 2% in CAL + obinutuzumab arm (ELEVATE‑TN) and 1% in ASCEND in both acalabrutinib and idelalisib + rituximab arms [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 "Tumour Lysis Syndrome" and ASCEND trial data].

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions and clinical management
- CYP3A inhibitors:
  - Strong CYP3A inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole): co‑administration increases acalabrutinib plasma concentrations and may increase toxicity; avoid concomitant use. If a strong inhibitor will be used short‑term (e.g., anti‑infectives up to 7 days), interrupt acalabrutinib during inhibitor use [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5 "Interactions with CYP3A inhibitors and inducers" and Table 3].  
  - Moderate CYP3A inhibitors (e.g., diltiazem, erythromycin, fluconazole): may increase acalabrutinib concentrations; reduce acalabrutinib dose to 100 mg once daily when co‑administered [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5 Table 3].

- CYP3A inducers:
  - Strong CYP3A inducers (e.g., rifampin): co‑administration decreases acalabrutinib plasma concentrations and may reduce activity; avoid concomitant use [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5 Table 3].

### Gastric acid reducing agents
- Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs): co‑administration may decrease acalabrutinib plasma concentrations; avoid concomitant use because PPIs have long-lasting effects that separation of doses may not overcome [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5 Table 4].  
- H2‑receptor antagonists: take acalabrutinib 2 hours before an H2‑receptor antagonist [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5 Table 4].  
- Antacids: separate dosing by at least 2 hours [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5 Table 4].

### Effects of acalabrutinib and metabolite on drug-metabolising enzymes and transporters
- In vitro, acalabrutinib and its active metabolite ACP‑5862 do not inhibit major CYPs (CYP1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4/5) or UGT1A2/2B7 at therapeutic concentrations; acalabrutinib is a weak inducer of CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP3A4 and ACP‑5862 weakly induces CYP3A4 [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5 "Effects of acalabrutinib and its active metabolite, ACP-5862, on CYP450 and UGT enzymes"].  
- Acalabrutinib may increase exposure to co‑administered BCRP substrates (e.g., methotrexate) via intestinal BCRP inhibition; ACP‑5862 may increase exposure to MATE1 substrates (e.g., metformin) via MATE1 inhibition [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5 "Effects of acalabrutinib and its active metabolite, ACP-5862, on drug transport systems"].  
- Acalabrutinib and ACP‑5862 are substrates of P‑glycoprotein (P‑gp) and BCRP in vitro; they are not substrates of OAT1, OAT3, OCT2, OATP1B1 or OATP1B3 (acalabrutinib) and ACP‑5862 is not a substrate of OATP1B1 or OATP1B3. Acalabrutinib/ACP‑5862 do not inhibit P‑gp, OAT1/3, OCT2, OATP1B1/B3, or MATE2‑K at clinically relevant concentrations [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5 "Effects of acalabrutinib and its active metabolite, ACP-5862, on drug transport systems"].

### Food effect
- In healthy subjects, a single 75 mg dose taken with a high‑fat, high‑calorie meal did not change mean AUC compared with fasting dosing, but Cmax decreased by 73% and Tmax was delayed by 1–2 hours [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5 "Effect of food on acalabrutinib"].

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant use with antithrombotic agents may increase risk of bleeding; balance risks versus benefits and monitor for bleeding [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4 "Haemorrhage"].

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - Australian pregnancy categorisation for acalabrutinib in the provided PI: Category C. Advise pregnant women of potential fetal risk based on animal findings [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.6 "Use in pregnancy – Category C"].  
  - Animal data: In rabbits during organogenesis, decreased fetal body weights and delayed skeletal ossification occurred at maternal exposures (AUC) approximately 3.6‑fold clinical exposure; in rats, no embryofetal developmental effects at exposures ≈16‑fold, but dystocia observed in a perinatal/lactation rat study at ≥100 mg/kg/day (exposures >3.5‑fold clinical). Presence of acalabrutinib and ACP‑5862 was confirmed in fetal rat plasma in rat studies [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.6 "Use in pregnancy – Category C"].

- Breastfeeding:
  - No human data on presence in human milk, effects on breastfed infant, or on milk production. Acalabrutinib and its active metabolite were present in rat milk. Advise lactating women not to breastfeed while taking acalabrutinib and for at least 2 weeks after the final dose due to potential for adverse reactions in the breastfed child [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.6 "Use in lactation"].

- Fertility:
  - In a rat fertility study, no effects on fertility were observed in male rats at exposures 16‑times and in female rats at exposures 14‑times the human AUC at the recommended dose [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.6 "Effects on fertility"].

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use: Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents (<18 years) have not been established [Source: TGA PI, Sections 4.2 "Paediatric use" and 4.4 "Paediatric use"].  
- Elderly: No dose adjustment required based on age; among clinical trial patients, no clinically relevant differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger [Source: TGA PI, Sections 4.2 "Use in the elderly" and 4.4 "Use in the elderly"].  
- Renal impairment:
  - No dose adjustment recommended for mild to moderate renal impairment (eGFR ≥30 mL/min/1.73 m² estimated by MDRD). Pharmacokinetics and safety in severe renal impairment (eGFR <29 mL/min/1.73 m²) or end‑stage renal disease have not been studied [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2 "Special patient populations - Renal impairment"].  
- Hepatic impairment:
  - No dose adjustment recommended for mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh A or B, or total bilirubin 1.5–3×ULN and any AST). Not recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C or total bilirubin >3×ULN and any AST) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2 "Special patient populations - Hepatic impairment"].

## 12. Clinical Trials
- ELEVATE‑TN (previously untreated CLL): Randomised, multicentre, open‑label, phase 3 study in 526 patients with previously untreated CLL comparing acalabrutinib + obinutuzumab, acalabrutinib monotherapy, and obinutuzumab + chlorambucil. Median duration of exposure in the acalabrutinib arms was ~27.7 months; adverse event-related discontinuation rates and dose reductions are reported in the PI (e.g., discontinuation due to adverse events: 11% acalabrutinib + obinutuzumab; 9% acalabrutinib monotherapy) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 "ELEVATE‑TN" and Tables 5–6; Section 5.1 referenced for trial details].

- ASCEND (relapsed/refractory CLL): Randomised, multicentre, open‑label, phase 3 study in 307 patients comparing acalabrutinib to investigator’s choice (idelalisib + rituximab or bendamustine + rituximab). Median exposure: acalabrutinib 15.7 months; safety and discontinuation statistics reported in the PI (e.g., discontinuation due to adverse events in acalabrutinib arm 10%) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 "ASCEND" and Tables 7–8; Section 5.1 referenced for trial details].

- The PI provides safety tables summarising non‑haematologic and haematologic adverse reactions observed in these pivotal trials with arm‑specific percentages (see Section 4.8 Tables 5–8) [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.8 Tables 5–8].
