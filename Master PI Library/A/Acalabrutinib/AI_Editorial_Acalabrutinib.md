# Product Information: Calquence (Acalabrutinib)

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Calquence
**Active Ingredient:** Acalabrutinib

Calquence is available as 100 mg hard capsules and 100 mg film-coated tablets. Each tablet contains acalabrutinib maleate, which provides higher solubility at increased gastric pH compared to the acalabrutinib base found in the capsules [EMA].

## 2. Approved Indications
Calquence is a Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor indicated for the following:

*   **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL) / Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma (SLL):**
    *   As monotherapy or in combination with obinutuzumab for previously untreated adult patients.
    *   In combination with venetoclax, with or without obinutuzumab, for previously untreated adult patients [EMA].
    *   As monotherapy for adult patients who have received at least one prior therapy.
*   **Mantle Cell Lymphoma (MCL):**
    *   In combination with bendamustine and rituximab (BR) for adult patients with previously untreated MCL who are not eligible for autologous stem cell transplant (ASCT).
    *   As monotherapy for adult patients with relapsed or refractory MCL.

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of the product excipients [EMA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hemorrhage:** Serious and sometimes fatal hemorrhagic events, including central nervous system and gastrointestinal bleeding, have occurred. These events can occur in patients with or without cytopenias. Use caution in patients requiring antithrombotic agents. Warfarin or other vitamin K antagonists should be avoided. **Surgical Consideration:** Withhold Calquence for 3 to 7 days pre- and post-surgery based on the type of procedure and bleeding risk [FDA].
*   **Infections:** Fatal and serious infections (bacterial, viral, or fungal) have been reported. This includes HBV reactivation and Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML). Monitor patients for fever and symptoms of infection and treat promptly. Consider prophylaxis for patients at increased risk for opportunistic infections.
*   **Cytopenias:** Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia, anemia, and thrombocytopenia are common. Monitor complete blood counts (CBC) regularly during treatment.
*   **Second Primary Malignancies:** Other malignancies, particularly skin cancers, have occurred. Advise patients to use sun protection and monitor for new skin lesions.
*   **Atrial Fibrillation and Flutter:** Monitor for symptoms of cardiac arrhythmia (e.g., palpitations, dizziness, syncope). Manage appropriately and evaluate the risk-benefit of continuing therapy if arrhythmia develops.
*   **Hepatotoxicity:** Drug-induced liver injury (DILI) has been reported. Evaluate bilirubin and transaminases at baseline and periodically during treatment [FDA].
*   **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Reported especially in patients with a high tumor burden. Assess risk at baseline and monitor closely.
*   **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Specifically reported when used in combination with BR in MCL patients. Monitor for pulmonary symptoms such as cough or dyspnea [EMA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
**Recommended Dose:** 100 mg taken orally approximately every 12 hours.

*   **Administration:** Swallow whole with water; do not open, break, or chew. Calquence may be taken with or without food.
*   **Duration of Therapy:** Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. In specific fixed-duration CLL regimens (e.g., in combination with venetoclax), treatment may be limited to 14 cycles [EMA].

### Dose Modifications for Adverse Reactions
For Grade 3 or greater toxicities (non-hematological, Grade 3 thrombocytopenia with bleeding, Grade 4 thrombocytopenia, or Grade 4 neutropenia lasting > 7 days):
*   **First through Second Occurrence:** Interrupt therapy. Once resolved to Grade 1 or baseline, resume at 100 mg BID.
*   **Third Occurrence:** Interrupt therapy. Once resolved, resume at a reduced dose of 100 mg once daily.
*   **Fourth Occurrence:** Discontinue Calquence.

### Missed Dose
If a dose is missed by more than 3 hours, it should be skipped, and the next dose should be taken at the regularly scheduled time. Do not double the dose.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is based on pooled data from over 2,000 patients.
*   **Very Common (≥ 20%):** Infection, headache, diarrhea, musculoskeletal pain, bruising, fatigue, nausea, rash, and arthralgia.
*   **Common Laboratory Abnormalities:** Decreased absolute neutrophil count, decreased hemoglobin, and decreased platelet count.
*   **Serious Adverse Reactions:** Pneumonia, COVID-19, and second primary malignancies. 

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors:** Avoid concomitant use. If short-term use is required (e.g., anti-infectives for 7 days), interrupt Calquence. 
*   **Moderate CYP3A Inhibitors:** Reduce Calquence dose to 100 mg once daily [FDA].
*   **Strong CYP3A Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use. If unavoidable, increase the Calquence dose to 200 mg every 12 hours [FDA].
*   **Gastric Acid Reducing Agents:**
    *   *Capsules:* Avoid Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs). Take Calquence 2 hours before H2-receptor antagonists or 2 hours before/after antacids.
    *   *Tablets:* No clinically significant interaction with PPIs, H2-receptor antagonists, or antacids; no dose staggering is required [EMA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Acalabrutinib may cause fetal harm. It is not recommended during pregnancy unless the clinical condition requires it. Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 1 week after the final dose [FDA].
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if acalabrutinib is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for at least 2 weeks after the last dose [FDA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Bioavailability is approximately 25%. Peak plasma concentrations (Tmax) are reached in 0.5–1.5 hours for capsules and 0.2–3.0 hours for tablets. 
*   **Distribution:** Highly plasma protein bound (97.5%–99.4%).
*   **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP3A enzymes. The active metabolite, ACP-5862, is approximately 50% less potent than the parent drug.
*   **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 1 hour for acalabrutinib and 7 hours for ACP-5862. Excretion is primarily via feces (84%).

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Acalabrutinib is a potent, selective inhibitor of Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK). It forms a covalent bond with a cysteine residue (Cys481) in the BTK active site, leading to irreversible inactivation. BTK is a critical signaling molecule in the B-cell antigen receptor (BCR) pathway; its inhibition prevents B-cell proliferation, survival, and chemotaxis.

## 11. Storage Conditions
Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F), with excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [FDA]. No special storage conditions are required according to European regulatory standards [EMA]. Keep out of the reach of children.
