## 3. Approved Indications

  - Jurisdictional limitations or differences: None specified in available sources; indication described as an antiretroviral agent to be used in combination.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General principles
- Screen for the HLA‑B*5701 allele prior to initiating abacavir therapy or prior to reinitiation, unless a prior documented HLA‑B*5701 result is available.
- Review prior medication history for exposure to any abacavir‑containing product; do not reintroduce abacavir in patients with prior abacavir hypersensitivity.

### Adult dosing (recommended)
- Total daily dose: 600 mg orally once daily (single 600 mg dose) OR 300 mg orally twice daily (300 mg every 12 hours), administered in combination with other antiretroviral agents.

### Pediatric dosing (recommended)
- Oral solution (for infants and children):
  - 3 months of age and older: 8 mg/kg orally twice daily OR 16 mg/kg orally once daily, not to exceed 600 mg total daily dose.
- Scored 300 mg tablet (for children ≥14 kg who can reliably swallow tablets):
  - Dosing by weight categories (recommended tablet regimens described for patients ≥14 kg):
    - 14 to <20 kg: Once‑daily — 1 tablet (300 mg) once daily; Twice‑daily — ½ tablet (150 mg) twice daily (total 300 mg/day)
    - ≥20 to <25 kg: Once‑daily — 1½ tablets (450 mg) once daily; Twice‑daily — ½ tablet (150 mg) in AM and 1 tablet (300 mg) in PM (total 450 mg/day)
    - ≥25 kg: Once‑daily — 2 tablets (600 mg) once daily; Twice‑daily — 1 tablet (300 mg) twice daily (total 600 mg/day)
  - Note: Data supporting once‑daily dosing are limited in some pediatric populations and, in at least one trial, were derived from subjects who switched from twice‑daily dosing after prolonged therapy.

### Hepatic impairment
- Mild hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh Class A): Recommended dose is 200 mg twice daily; to achieve this reduced dose, use the oral solution (10 mL twice daily referenced as an example for dose reduction).
- Moderate to severe hepatic impairment: Abacavir is contraindicated.

### Renal impairment
- Specific dosing recommendations by level of renal impairment (eGFR thresholds) are not specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions and precautions
- Prior to prescribing tablets for children, assess the child’s ability to swallow tablets; if the child cannot reliably swallow tablets, prescribe the oral solution.
- Dispense Medication Guide and Warning Card to patients with each new prescription and refill that describe hypersensitivity reaction recognition and instructions.
- If hypersensitivity reaction is suspected, discontinue abacavir immediately and permanently unless hypersensitivity has been definitively ruled out by a clinician; never restart after a confirmed hypersensitivity reaction.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
- Presence of the HLA‑B*5701 allele.
- Prior hypersensitivity reaction to abacavir (any previous abacavir‑containing product).
- Moderate or severe hepatic impairment (abacavir is contraindicated in these patients).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / prominent warnings
- **Hypersensitivity reactions:** Serious and sometimes fatal multi‑organ hypersensitivity reactions have occurred with abacavir. Patients who carry HLA‑B*5701 are at higher risk. Abacavir is contraindicated in HLA‑B*5701‑positive patients and in patients with prior abacavir hypersensitivity. Screen all patients for HLA‑B*5701 prior to initiation. If hypersensitivity is suspected, discontinue immediately and do not reintroduce abacavir or any abacavir‑containing product after a hypersensitivity reaction.

### General warnings and precautions
- Lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis, including fatal cases, have been reported with nucleoside analogue antiretroviral agents including abacavir. Suspend therapy if clinical or laboratory findings suggest lactic acidosis or pronounced hepatotoxicity (which may include hepatomegaly and steatosis even without marked transaminase elevations).
- Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS) has been reported after initiation of combination antiretroviral therapy, including abacavir; monitor for inflammatory responses to opportunistic infections or emergence of autoimmune conditions.
- Myocardial infarction: Some observational studies have reported an association between abacavir use and increased risk of myocardial infarction, while randomized controlled trials and meta‑analyses have not shown consistent excess risk. The causal relationship is inconclusive. Consider underlying coronary heart disease risk when prescribing and address modifiable risk factors (e.g., hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes, smoking).
- Reintroduction risk: Reintroduction of abacavir after even an apparent non‑hypersensitivity interruption can result in life‑threatening reactions; reintroduction should be attempted only where immediate medical care is accessible.
- Dispense patient education materials (Medication Guide and Warning Card) that explain hypersensitivity signs and the need for immediate cessation if suspected.

### Monitoring requirements
- HLA‑B*5701 testing prior to starting therapy (or documented prior result).
- Clinical monitoring for signs of hypersensitivity (fever, rash, gastrointestinal symptoms, constitutional symptoms, respiratory symptoms).
- Monitor liver function tests and clinical signs of hepatic dysfunction and lactic acidosis.
- Monitor for signs of IRIS in patients with advanced immunosuppression starting therapy.
- Consider assessment and monitoring of cardiovascular risk factors given uncertain association with myocardial infarction.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Do not restart abacavir after confirmed hypersensitivity reaction. If hypersensitivity is suspected but ruled out, patients may restart under clinical supervision.
- Use oral solution for dose reduction in mild hepatic impairment; do not use in moderate to severe hepatic impairment.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The following adverse events are reported in the available sources. Frequency categorization follows the provided frequency information when available. Where an exact frequency is not provided in the available sources, frequency is stated as "Not specified in available sources."

- Summary of most commonly reported adverse reactions (adults): nausea, headache, malaise and fatigue, nausea and vomiting, dreams/sleep disorders — these were among the most frequently observed events in adult clinical trials.

- Hypersensitivity: Serious, sometimes fatal hypersensitivity reactions characterized by ≥2 of: fever; rash; gastrointestinal symptoms (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain); constitutional symptoms (malaise, fatigue, achiness); respiratory symptoms (dyspnea, cough, pharyngitis). Almost all include fever and/or rash. Severe outcomes reported with hypersensitivity include anaphylaxis, multi‑organ failure, hypotension, adult respiratory distress syndrome, hepatic failure, renal failure, myolysis, and death.
  - Frequency: Reported ~8% in clinical trials where HLA‑B*5701 screening was not performed; incidence approximately 1% when HLA‑B*5701‑positive subjects were excluded.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Clinically significant interactions described in available sources:
  - Methadone: Concurrent use may require an increased methadone dose in a small number of patients. Monitor methadone effect and adjust dose as clinically indicated.
  - Riociguat: Concomitant use may require reduction of riociguat dose. Monitor and adjust riociguat dosing per clinical judgment.
- Other pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic interactions with antiretroviral agents or other drug classes are not detailed in the available sources beyond these items.
- Management notes:
  - Review concomitant medications for potential interactions and monitor for clinical or laboratory signs of altered therapeutic effect or toxicity when abacavir is co‑administered with other agents.
- Jurisdictional interaction differences: Not specified in available sources

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key trials described in the available sources (safety and comparative adverse event data):
  - CNA30024: Trial of abacavir + lamivudine + efavirenz versus zidovudine + lamivudine + efavirenz in therapy‑naive adults; reported rates of treatment‑emergent adverse reactions and laboratory abnormalities through 48 weeks (examples: dreams/sleep disorders ~10%; hypersensitivity events 9% in blinded phase reported as suspected).
  - CNA3005: Trial comparing abacavir + lamivudine/zidovudine versus indinavir + lamivudine/zidovudine; reported adverse event frequencies (nausea 19%, headache 13%, malaise/fatigue 12%, hypersensitivity 8%).
  - CNA30021: Once‑daily 600 mg vs twice‑daily 300 mg abacavir (both with lamivudine and efavirenz) — similar overall adverse event profiles but higher incidence of severe hypersensitivity and severe diarrhea reported with once‑daily 600 mg in one trial (severe hypersensitivity 5% once daily vs 2% twice daily; severe diarrhea 2% once daily vs 0% twice daily).
  - CNA3006: Therapy‑experienced pediatric subjects (abacavir + lamivudine + zidovudine vs lamivudine + zidovudine) through 16 weeks — adverse events included fever/chills (9%), nausea and vomiting (9%), skin rashes (7%), ENT infections (5%).
  - ARROW trial (pediatric): Compared once‑daily vs twice‑daily dosing; primary safety assessment based on Grade 3/4 adverse events; overall similar rates of Grade 3/4 events between dosing strategies, with one Grade 4 hepatitis in once‑daily group considered uncertain causality.
- Trial efficacy endpoints, virologic outcomes, and long‑term efficacy results: Not specified in available sources beyond safety/adverse event data summarized above.
