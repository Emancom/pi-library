# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory information.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes for healthcare professionals and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for this synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP). It is a carrier molecule responsible for transporting long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Subsequent daily maintenance doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration should occur as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels below 40–50 µmol/L.
    - Dose adjustments should be guided by trough concentrations, with potential downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) possible as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These often occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Treatment should be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients receiving levocarnitine, regardless of pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Oral Formulation in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may result in the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Plasma Concentrations:** For metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy and monitor weekly/monthly. The target plasma free carnitine concentration should generally be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Warfarin Therapy:** Monitor International Normalized Ratio (INR) levels in patients receiving concurrent warfarin, both at the initiation of levocarnitine and following dose adjustments [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology of the patient population (ESRD/metabolic crisis).

### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD trials)
| Body System | Adverse Event (Regardless of Causality) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, abdominal pain, asthenia |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |

### Other Reported Effects
- **Body Odor:** Transient occurrences have been noted.
- **Gastritis:** Reported less frequently.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (postmarketing) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in INR when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Close monitoring of INR is required [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins, reducing the likelihood of protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/unspecified]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation. Consideration should be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg and is applicable to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose adjustments for intravenous use in ESRD are provided (see Section 3). Caution is advised regarding the use of *oral* formulations in severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are then cleared from the body [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The apparent terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis, 20 mg/kg IV three times per week significantly increased plasma levocarnitine levels from a pre-dialysis baseline of ~20 µmol/L to trough levels of ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations, clinical outcomes on specific symptoms of deficiency in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
