# Editorial Product Information: Atovaquone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of atovaquone oral suspension for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing strategies, and safety profiles into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. It does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidelines before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Atovaquone (a hydroxy-1,4-naphthoquinone and analogue of ubiquinone).
- **Brand Names:** ATOVACUE [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** P05BD01 (Antiprotozoals).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral liquid suspension containing 750 mg of atovaquone per 5 mL. 
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Glenmark Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Atovaquone oral suspension is indicated for the acute treatment of mild to moderate *Pneumocystis carinii* pneumonia (PCP) (also known as *Pneumocystis jirovecii*) in adult patients with AIDS who are intolerant to trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) therapy [Australian].

**Clinical Criteria for Use:**
- Alveolar-arterial oxygen tension difference [(A-a) DO₂] ≤ 45 mmHg (6 kPa).
- Oxygen tension in arterial blood (PaO₂) ≥ 60 mmHg (8 kPa) while breathing room air.

*Note: Atovaquone has not been systematically evaluated for the treatment of severe PCP, as a prophylactic agent, or in patients failing other PCP therapies.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Standard Regimen:** 750 mg (5 mL) administered twice daily for 21 days.
- **Alternative Regimen:** For patients with difficulty swallowing or those unable to consume two meals a day, 1,500 mg (10 mL) once daily with food for 21 days may be considered.

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Requirement:** It is critical to administer atovaquone **with food**, preferably high-fat meals. Food increases the bioavailability of the drug by two to three-fold.
- **Preparation:** Shake the suspension well before use. Do not dilute.
- **Missed Doses:** Not specifically addressed in the source document; standard clinical practice suggests taking the missed dose as soon as remembered with food, unless it is nearly time for the next dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to atovaquone or any of the excipients (e.g., benzyl alcohol, saccharin sodium) in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Absorption and Efficacy:** Absorption is limited. Low plasma concentrations correlate with higher failure and mortality rates. Patients with gastrointestinal disorders that limit oral absorption or those unable to take the drug with food should consider alternative therapies.
- **Diarrhoea:** Presence of diarrhoea at the start of treatment is associated with significantly lower plasma levels and may lead to therapy failure.
- **Concurrent Infections:** Atovaquone is specific to PCP and is not effective against concurrent bacterial, viral, fungal, or mycobacterial pathogens.
- **Excipient Warning:** This product contains 50 mg of benzyl alcohol per 5 mL, which may cause allergic reactions in sensitive individuals [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Close monitoring of plasma concentrations is recommended in patients with conditions precluding optimal absorption.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event assessment in this population is confounded by the underlying complications of advanced HIV disease.

### Common Adverse Effects (≥10%)
| Adverse Effect | Frequency (Approximate) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Rash (including maculopapular)** | 23% – 28% |
| **Nausea** | 21% – 38% |
| **Diarrhoea** | 19% – 42% |
| **Fever** | 14% – 46% |
| **Vomiting** | 14% – 23% |
| **Headache** | 15% – 17% |
| **Insomnia** | 8% – 20% |

### Other Notable Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** Asthenia, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, pruritus, oral monilia (candidiasis), sinusitis, and sweating.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities (≥5%):** Anaemia (Hb < 8.0 g/dL), neutropenia (ANC < 750 cells/mm³), elevated ALT/AST (>5x ULN), elevated alkaline phosphatase, elevated amylase, and hyponatraemia.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rare reports of angioedema, bronchospasm, and throat tightness.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Rifampicin:** Concomitant use is **not recommended** as it significantly reduces atovaquone plasma concentrations (mean decrease of 7.6 µg/mL).
- **Rifabutin:** Associated with a 34% reduction in atovaquone levels and a 19% reduction in rifabutin levels.
- **Metoclopramide:** May significantly decrease atovaquone plasma concentrations.
- **Tetracycline:** Associated with an approximate 40% reduction in atovaquone levels.
- **Indinavir:** Concomitant use results in a 23% decrease in indinavir trough levels (Cmin).
- **Zidovudine:** Atovaquone may decrease the metabolism of zidovudine, increasing its AUC by 33%. Monitor for zidovudine-related adverse effects during prolonged therapy.

### Pharmacodynamic/Protein Binding Interactions
- **Highly Protein-Bound Drugs:** Atovaquone is >99% protein-bound. Exercise caution when co-administering with drugs with narrow therapeutic indices that are also highly protein-bound (e.g., **warfarin**).
- **Non-Interacting Drugs:** No significant interactions observed with fluconazole, clotrimazole, ketoconazole, antacids, or systemic corticosteroids.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category B2 [Australian]**. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal studies showed maternal and embryotoxic effects in rabbits at high doses (600–1200 mg/kg), but no teratogenicity. Use only if the maternal benefit outweighs the fetal risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if atovaquone is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggest no effect on reproductive potential at doses up to 1000 mg/kg.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available for atovaquone suspension in children. While tablet studies showed similar pharmacokinetics to adults, current data are insufficient to recommend use in paediatric populations [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No clinical experience in patients over 65 years. Use with caution and close monitoring due to the higher prevalence of hepatic, renal, and cardiac dysfunction in this group.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied; however, as renal excretion is negligible, no dose adjustment is theoretically required.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied. Since elimination is primarily liver-dependent, caution is advised.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Atovaquone selectively and potently inhibits the mitochondrial electron transport chain (specifically the cytochrome bc1 complex/Complex III) in parasitic protozoa. This leads to the inhibition of nucleic acid and ATP synthesis.
- **Microbiology:** Active against *Pneumocystis carinii*, *Toxoplasma gondii*, and *Plasmodium* species.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Highly lipophilic with low aqueous solubility. Bioavailability is ~47% when taken with food.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>99%).
    - **Metabolism:** Negligible metabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Predominantly excreted unchanged in faeces (>90%). Half-life is 2 to 3 days in adults.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PCP Treatment:** In comparative trials against TMP-SMX, atovaquone showed a 62% success rate versus 64% for TMP-SMX. However, atovaquone had significantly fewer treatment-limiting adverse events (7% vs 20%).
- **Comparison with Pentamidine:** Atovaquone demonstrated a 57% success rate compared to 40% for IV pentamidine in primary treatment cohorts.
- **Concentration Correlation:** Clinical success is highly correlated with steady-state plasma concentrations. Concentrations >15 µg/mL are associated with success rates >90%, while concentrations <5 µg/mL are associated with high mortality.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Stability:** Do not refrigerate or freeze.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused product 21 days after opening the bottle [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
