# Editorial Product Information: Azithromycin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of azithromycin for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing within any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Azithromycin (as dihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Azalide (a subclass of macrolide antibiotics).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Azithromycin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01FA10.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 500 mg film-coated tablets (white, oval, biconvex) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Azithromycin is indicated for the treatment of mild to moderate infections caused by susceptible organisms in adults:

- **Lower Respiratory Tract Infections (LRTI):** 
    - Acute bacterial bronchitis (due to *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*, or *M. catarrhalis*).
    - Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) suitable for outpatient oral treatment.
    - CAP requiring initial intravenous therapy caused by susceptible organisms (including *Chlamydia pneumoniae*, *Legionella pneumophila*, and *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*).
- **Upper Respiratory Tract Infections (URTI):** 
    - Acute sinusitis (due to *S. pneumoniae* or *H. influenzae*).
    - Acute streptococcal pharyngitis. (Note: Penicillin remains the first-line choice for *S. pyogenes* and rheumatic fever prophylaxis; azithromycin efficacy in preventing rheumatic fever is not yet established).
- **Uncomplicated Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** Due to *S. aureus*, *S. pyogenes*, or *S. agalactiae*.
- **Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STD):** 
    - Uncomplicated urethritis and cervicitis (due to *Chlamydia trachomatis*).
- **Ocular Infections:** 
    - *Chlamydia trachomatis* conjunctivitis and trachoma.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
Azithromycin should be administered as a single daily dose. Tablets may be taken with or without food [Australian].

- **Sexually Transmitted Chlamydial Infections:** 1 g as a single oral dose.
- **Trachoma and Conjunctivitis (Chlamydia):** 1 g as a single dose, or once weekly for up to three weeks.
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia (following IV therapy):** 500 mg daily to complete a 7 to 10-day total course.
- **All other indications (including oral treatment of CAP):** 1.5 g total dose, administered as 500 mg daily for 3 days.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to azithromycin, erythromycin, any other macrolide or ketolide antibiotic, or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rare, serious allergic reactions including angioedema, anaphylaxis, and severe dermatologic reactions (AGEP, SJS, TEN, and DRESS) have been reported. Allergic symptoms may recur after discontinuation of symptomatic treatment due to the long tissue half-life of azithromycin.
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** Risk of ventricular arrhythmias, including *torsades de pointes*. Use with caution in patients with proarrhythmic conditions, electrolyte imbalances (hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia), or those taking Class IA/III antiarrhythmics, antipsychotics, or fluoroquinolones.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Abnormal liver function, hepatitis, and hepatic failure (including fatal cases) have occurred. Discontinue immediately if signs of hepatitis appear.
- **Clostridioides difficile-associated diarrhoea (CDAD):** May range from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis. Avoid drugs that delay peristalsis (e.g., opiates).
- **Cardiovascular Death:** Some observational studies suggest a rare, short-term increased risk of cardiovascular death, particularly during the first five days of treatment [Australian].
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Exacerbations of symptoms have been reported.
- **Ergotism:** Theoretical risk of ergotism; do not co-administer with ergot derivatives.
- **Outpatient CAP Limitations:** Not recommended for patients inappropriate for oral therapy (e.g., cystic fibrosis, nosocomial infections, bacteraemia, or immunodeficiency).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse effects are mild to moderate and gastrointestinal in nature.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>1%)** | Diarrhoea/loose stools (5–7%), Nausea (3–5%), Abdominal pain (3–5%), Vomiting (2%), Vaginitis (2%), Dyspepsia (1%). |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | Dizziness, headache, vertigo, somnolence, palpitations, chest pain, dyspepsia, flatulence, melaena, rash, photosensitivity, angioedema, fatigue. |
| **Post-Marketing/Rare** | Thrombocytopenia, hypotension, pancreatitis, acute renal failure, interstitial nephritis, pseudomembranous colitis, hearing loss/tinnitus, SJS/TEN/DRESS. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN, QT prolongation, hepatic failure, and infantile hypertrophic pyloric stenosis (IHPS) in neonates.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antacids:** Reduced peak serum concentrations (Cmax) by up to 30%. Do not administer simultaneously.
- **Ciclosporin:** Significantly elevated Cmax and AUC of ciclosporin. Monitor levels and adjust dose if co-administration is necessary.
- **Digoxin and Colchicine:** Possible increased levels of P-glycoprotein substrates. Clinical monitoring is required.
- **Coumarin-type Anticoagulants (Warfarin):** Potentiated anticoagulation reported post-marketing. Monitor prothrombin time/INR.
- **Zidovudine:** Increased concentrations of phosphorylated zidovudine (active metabolite).
- **Ergot Derivatives:** Theoretical risk of ergot toxicity; avoid co-administration.
- **Cytochrome P450:** No significant interaction with the hepatic CYP450 system is expected.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** No evidence of increased foetal damage in animal studies. Limited evidence in humans; use only if the benefit outweighs the risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Azithromycin is present in human milk. A clinical decision must be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, considering the benefit to both mother and child.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed decreased fertility in rats at doses of 20–30 mg/kg/day; human significance is unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Neonates:** Risk of Infantile Hypertrophic Pyloric Stenosis (IHPS) following use in the first 42 days of life.
    - **Pharyngitis:** A 20 mg/kg/day dose in children is associated with higher rates of GI adverse events compared to lower doses.
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics are similar to younger adults; no dose adjustment is generally required. However, elderly patients may be more susceptible to QT interval effects.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for GFR 10–80 mL/min. Use with caution in patients with GFR <10 mL/min (Cmax and AUC significantly increased).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. Use with caution in severe hepatic disease as the liver is the primary route of elimination.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Binds to the 50S ribosomal subunit of susceptible microorganisms, inhibiting protein synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 37% absolute bioavailability; Cmax achieved in 2–3 hours. Food does not significantly affect tablet bioavailability.
    - **Distribution:** Extensive tissue distribution; concentrations in tissues (lung, tonsil, prostate) are significantly higher than in plasma. Very low penetration into cerebrospinal fluid.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily excreted unchanged; metabolites do not contribute to antimicrobial activity.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 68 hours. Primarily eliminated via biliary excretion; 12% excreted in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharyngitis/Tonsillitis (Study 96-001):** In children (2–12 years), azithromycin 20 mg/kg/day for 3 days demonstrated superior bacteriological eradication of GABHS (94.2%) compared to 10 mg/kg/day (57.8%) and comparable efficacy to Penicillin V (84.2%).
- **Trachoma:** Data supports the efficacy of a 20 mg/kg (up to 1 g) single dose. Repeat dosing at 6 months may extend the trachoma-free period.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Unused medicine should be disposed of via local pharmacy programs [Australian].
