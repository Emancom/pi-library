# Editorial Product Information: Rivastigmine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of rivastigmine for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of publication, only the Australian (TGA) source was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rivastigmine (present as rivastigmine hydrogen tartrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Carbamate derivative; brain-selective, pseudo-irreversible acetyl- and butyrylcholinesterase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Exelon** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Hard capsules: 1.5 mg, 3.0 mg, 4.5 mg, and 6.0 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Alzheimer's Disease:** Treatment of patients with mild to moderately severe dementia of the Alzheimer’s type [Australian].
- **Parkinson's Disease Dementia:** While clinical trial data for Parkinson’s disease dementia is discussed within the provided source, the formal therapeutic indication section in the available source explicitly lists only Alzheimer's type dementia [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The effective therapeutic range is 6–12 mg/day, administered in two divided doses.
- **Starting Dose:** 1.5 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Titration Schedule:** 
    - If well tolerated after a minimum of two weeks, the dose may be increased to 3 mg twice daily.
    - Subsequent increases to 4.5 mg and 6 mg twice daily may be considered after a minimum of two weeks at each dose level, based on individual tolerability [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole.
    - Doses should be taken twice daily with morning and evening meals [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** 1.5 mg to 6 mg twice daily. Patients should be maintained on their highest well-tolerated dose to achieve maximum benefit [Australian].
- **Missed Dose / Interruption of Therapy:**
    - If treatment is interrupted for more than three days, it must be re-initiated at the lowest daily dose (1.5 mg twice daily) and re-titrated to reduce the risk of severe adverse reactions (e.g., severe vomiting) [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustment for Adverse Effects:** If GI upset (nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain) or weight loss occurs, the clinician may omit one or more doses. If effects persist, the daily dose should be reduced to the previous well-tolerated level [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **rivastigmine**, other carbamate derivatives, or any formulation excipients [Australian].
- Previous history of application site reactions suggestive of allergic contact dermatitis with rivastigmine patches [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment (not studied in this population) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal Effects:** High incidence of nausea (47%), vomiting (31%), and anorexia (17%). Effects are most frequent during titration. Dehydration may occur and can lead to serious outcomes [Australian].
- **Weight Monitoring:** Patients may lose significant weight. Monitoring is required, particularly for patients <50 kg, who are more prone to adverse events [Australian].
- **Peptic Ulcers/GI Bleeding:** Cholinesterase inhibitors may increase gastric acid secretion. Use with caution in patients with a history of ulcers or those using NSAIDs [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular (Bradycardia/Syncopal Episodes):** Increased cholinergic activity may have vagotonic effects on heart rate. Caution in patients with "sick sinus syndrome" or conduction abnormalities. Syncopal episodes occurred in 3% of patients on 6–12 mg/day [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation and Torsade de Pointes:** Rivastigmine may cause QT prolongation and bradycardia. Caution is advised in patients with existing risk factors (e.g., uncompensated heart failure, recent MI, hypokalaemia, or concomitant use of QT-prolonging drugs) [Australian].
- **Neurological Conditions:** 
    - **Seizures:** May exacerbate seizure activity, though Alzheimer's itself is associated with seizures.
    - **Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS):** May induce or exacerbate tremor and parkinsonian symptoms, particularly in patients with Parkinson’s disease dementia [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Conditions:** Caution in patients with a history of asthma or obstructive pulmonary disease due to potential bronchoconstriction [Australian].
- **Dermatological Reactions:** Discontinue if application site reactions spread beyond patch size or if disseminated allergic dermatitis occurs [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are dose-dependent and typically more frequent during the titration phase.

| Frequency | Adverse Event [Australian] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, dizziness, loss of appetite. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, dyspepsia, headache, somnolence, tremor, agitation, confusion, anxiety, hyperhydrosis, fatigue, asthenia, malaise, weight loss. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Syncope, insomnia, depression, abnormal liver function tests, accidental falls. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Seizures, angina pectoris, myocardial infarction, gastric/duodenal ulcers, rash, pruritus. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | GI haemorrhage, pancreatitis, oesophageal rupture (associated with severe vomiting), cardiac arrhythmia (bradycardia, AV block, AF), hypertension, hallucinations, urinary tract infection. |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Hepatitis, dehydration, aggression, restlessness, sick sinus syndrome, disseminated allergic dermatitis. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Nicotine:** Increases oral clearance of rivastigmine by 23% [Australian].
    - **Cytochrome P450:** Rivastigmine is primarily metabolised via hydrolysis by esterases. Minimal metabolism occurs via CYP450; no PK interactions are expected with drugs metabolised by CYP1A2, 2D6, 3A4/5, 2E1, 2C9, 2C8, or 2C19 [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Metoclopramide:** Potential for additive extrapyramidal effects; concomitant use is not recommended [Australian].
    - **Cholinomimetics/Anticholinergics:** Avoid use with other cholinomimetic drugs (additive effect). Rivastigmine may interfere with the activity of anticholinergics (e.g., oxybutynin) [Australian].
    - **Succinylcholine-type Relaxants:** Rivastigmine may exaggerate muscle relaxation during anaesthesia [Australian].
    - **Beta-blockers:** Additive bradycardic effects may lead to syncope. Cardioselective beta-blockers pose the greatest risk [Australian].
    - **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Caution and clinical monitoring required when used with quinidine, amiodarone, haloperidol, sotalol, etc. [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. Not teratogenic in animal studies, but safety in human pregnancy is not established. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in the milk of lactating rabbits. It is unknown if it enters human milk; breastfeeding is not recommended for patients taking rivastigmine [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; no experience in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance is 30% lower in elderly volunteers (>60 years) compared to younger subjects. However, in patients with Alzheimer's (aged 50–92), bioavailability did not change with age [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance is significantly lower in moderate impairment (GFR 10–50 mL/min). No specific dose adjustment is required, but titration must be closely followed according to individual tolerability [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is 60–65% lower in mild-to-moderate hepatic impairment. No specific dose adjustment is required, but clinicians must follow titration recommendations closely. Contraindicated in severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Rivastigmine is a carbamate-type, brain-selective acetylcholinesterase (AChE) and butyrylcholinesterase (BuChE) inhibitor. It facilitates cholinergic neurotransmission by slowing the degradation of acetylcholine in the synaptic cleft.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Binds covalently to target enzymes to temporarily inactivate them. AChE activity in CSF is reduced by ~40% within 1.5 hours of a 3 mg oral dose [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and complete. Tmax ~1 hour. Bioavailability ~36–40%. Food delays Tmax by 90 minutes and increases AUC by ~30% [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Weak protein binding (~40%). Readily crosses the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly and extensively metabolised via cholinesterase-mediated hydrolysis to a decarbamylated metabolite. Half-life ~1 hour [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal (>90% as metabolites). Less than 1% excreted in faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Effectiveness was demonstrated in two 26-week, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in patients with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's (MMSE 10–26).
- **Outcomes:** 
    - **ADAS-cog:** Patients on 6–12 mg/day showed a significant improvement (4.94 units difference vs. placebo at 26 weeks) [Australian].
    - **CIBIC-Plus:** Significant global clinical improvement observed compared to placebo.
    - **Activities of Daily Living (PDS):** Placebo patients showed significant decline (4.9 units) compared to minimal decline (1 unit) in the high-dose rivastigmine group [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children.
- **Shelf Life:** Consult the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Not specified for other jurisdictions in available sources.
