# Editorial Product Information: Risperidone

## Editorial Note
- This document is an editorial synthesis based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview of **Risperidone** for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information approved by the relevant regulatory authority in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local official documentation for prescribing purposes.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Risperidone, a benzisoxazole-derivative atypical antipsychotic.
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Risperidone [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05AX08 (Not specified in available sources, but standard classification).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 0.5 mg, 1 mg, 2 mg, 3 mg, and 4 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia:** Treatment of schizophrenia and related psychoses [Australian].
- **Bipolar Disorder:** Short-term treatment of acute manic episodes associated with Bipolar 1 disorder [Australian].
- **Dementia-related Behavioural Disturbances:** Management (up to 12 weeks) of psychotic symptoms, persistent agitation, or aggression in patients with moderate to severe Alzheimer-type dementia who are unresponsive to non-pharmacological interventions [Australian].
- **Conduct and Disruptive Behaviour Disorders:** Treatment of prominent destructive behaviours (e.g., aggression, impulsivity, self-injury) in children over 5 years, adolescents, and adults with sub-average intellectual functioning or mental retardation [Australian].
- **Autism:** Management of behavioural disorders associated with autism in children and adolescents [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Risperidone can be administered with or without food.
- When switching from other antipsychotics, gradual discontinuation of the previous agent is recommended [Australian].

### Adult Dosing
- **Schizophrenia:** 
    - Initial: 1 mg twice daily (BID). 
    - Titration: Increase to 2 mg BID on the second day. Subsequent adjustments may be made as needed.
    - Maintenance: Most patients require 4–6 mg daily (given once daily or BID).
    - Note: Doses exceeding 10 mg/day have not shown superior efficacy and may increase extrapyramidal symptoms [Australian].
- **Bipolar Mania:** 
    - Initial: 2 mg once daily.
    - Titration: Adjustments in 1 mg/day increments should occur at intervals of at least 24 hours.
    - Dosing Range: 2–6 mg/day [Australian].

### Specialist Dosing
- **Behavioural Disturbances in Dementia:**
    - Initial: 0.25 mg BID.
    - Titration: Increments of 0.25 mg BID, not more frequently than every other day.
    - Target: 0.5 mg BID for most patients; max 1 mg BID. Treatment should not exceed 12 weeks [Australian].
- **Conduct/Disruptive Behaviour Disorders:**
    - Weight ≥ 50 kg: Start 0.5 mg once daily. Target 1 mg once daily; max 1.5 mg once daily.
    - Weight < 50 kg: Start 0.25 mg once daily. Target 0.5 mg once daily; max 0.75 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Autism (Paediatric):**
    - Weight < 20 kg: Start 0.25 mg/day. Increase to 0.5 mg/day on Day 4.
    - Weight ≥ 20 kg: Start 0.5 mg/day. Increase to 1.0 mg/day on Day 4.
    - Further adjustments should occur at intervals of ≥ 2 weeks [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **Risperidone** or any of the inactive excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Mortality in Elderly Patients with Dementia:** Atypical antipsychotics are associated with increased mortality in this population. Concomitant use with **furosemide** in elderly dementia patients has been associated with even higher mortality rates (7.3% vs 3.1% for risperidone alone) [Australian].
- **Cerebrovascular Adverse Events (CVAE):** Increased risk of stroke and transient ischaemic attacks (TIA) has been observed in elderly patients with dementia [Australian].
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal syndrome characterised by hyperthermia, muscle rigidity, and autonomic instability. Immediate discontinuation is required [Australian].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia (TD):** Risk of irreversible, involuntary dyskinetic movements increases with treatment duration and cumulative dose [Australian].
- **Orthostatic Hypotension:** Due to alpha-blocking activity, especially during initial titration. Use caution in patients with known cardiovascular disease [Australian].
- **Haematological Effects:** Reports of leukopenia, neutropenia, and agranulocytosis. Monitor WBC in patients with a history of low counts [Australian].
- **Metabolic Changes:** Risk of hyperglycaemia, exacerbation of diabetes (including ketoacidosis/coma), and significant weight gain [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** Caution in patients with congenital long QT syndrome or a history of cardiac arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Intraoperative Floppy Iris Syndrome (IFIS):** Observed during cataract surgery in patients treated with alpha-1a-adrenergic antagonists like risperidone [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Insomnia, Parkinsonism (including rigidity, bradykinesia), somnolence, headache [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Anxiety, dizziness, akathisia, dystonia, tremor, blurred vision, tachycardia, orthostatic hypotension, nasopharyngitis, cough, dyspepsia, nausea, vomiting, constipation, rash, arthralgia, fatigue, pyrexia, weight gain [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Anaemia, hypersensitivity, syncope, dyspnoea, epistaxis, dysphagia, urinary incontinence, erectile dysfunction, galactorrhoea, oedema [Australian].
- **Rare/Very Rare (<0.1%):** Agranulocytosis, anaphylactic reaction, inappropriate ADH secretion, diabetic ketoacidosis, water intoxication, priapism, jaundice, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, rhabdomyolysis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Centrally Acting Drugs/Alcohol:** Use caution due to additive CNS effects.
- **Levodopa/Dopamine Agonists:** Risperidone may antagonise their effects.
- **Psychostimulants (e.g., methylphenidate):** Combined use may lead to EPS upon dose adjustments of either agent [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Risperidone may potentiate hypotensive effects.

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors (e.g., fluoxetine, paroxetine):** May significantly increase risperidone plasma levels (up to 10-fold increase with paroxetine 40mg), though the active antipsychotic fraction increases less substantially. Re-evaluation of dosing is necessary [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., carbamazepine, rifampicin):** May decrease the plasma levels of the active antipsychotic fraction.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole, verapamil):** May increase plasma concentrations of the active fraction [Australian].
- **Furosemide:** Associated with increased mortality when used concomitantly in elderly patients with dementia [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Neonates exposed to antipsychotics during the third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms post-delivery. Use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Risperidone and its metabolite are excreted in breast milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show prolactin-mediated effects on female mating behaviour. Male reproductive toxicity (reduced sperm motility/concentration) was noted in dogs at high doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Experience is lacking in children with schizophrenia < 15 years and in those with conduct disorder < 5 years [Australian].
- **Elderly:** A starting dose of 0.5 mg BID is recommended. Titration should be gradual in 0.5 mg BID increments [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance of the active fraction is reduced by 60% in renal impairment. Starting and maintenance doses should be halved (e.g., 0.5 mg BID initial), with slower titration [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective monoaminergic antagonist with high affinity for serotonergic 5-HT₂ and dopaminergic D₂ receptors. It also binds to alpha-1, alpha-2, and H₁ receptors, but has no affinity for cholinergic receptors [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Antagonism of D₂ improves positive symptoms of schizophrenia but induces EPS and prolactin release. Balanced 5-HT₂ and D₂ antagonism may reduce EPS liability and improve negative/affective symptoms [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and well absorbed (peak 1–2 hours); unaffected by food.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised by CYP2D6 to the active metabolite, 9-hydroxy-risperidone. Both form the "active antipsychotic fraction."
    - **Elimination:** Half-life of risperidone is ~3 hours in extensive metabolisers; the active fraction half-life is ~24 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Schizophrenia:** Pivotal studies (up to 8 weeks) established efficacy in control of both positive and negative symptoms. Long-term studies (1 year) showed superiority/comparability to haloperidol in relapse prevention [Australian].
- **Bipolar Mania:** Efficacy established in three 3-week monotherapy studies and two 3-week adjunctive therapy studies (with lithium or valproate) [Australian].
- **Autism:** Two 8-week trials in children/adolescents (5–17 years) showed significant improvement in irritability and other behavioural scores (ABC scale) compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
