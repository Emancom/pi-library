# Editorial Product Information: Regadenoson

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of regadenoson for healthcare professionals, incorporating regulatory requirements and clinical guidance.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. It does not replace the official PI required for prescribing and dispensing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Regadenoson (anhydrous).
- **Chemical Class:** $A_{2A}$ adenosine receptor agonist; coronary vasodilator.
- **Brand Names:** Regadenoson Injection (various manufacturers, e.g., Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** C01EB21.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Single-dose pre-filled syringe: 0.4 mg/5 mL (0.08 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]; Gland Pharma Limited [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Radionuclide Myocardial Perfusion Imaging (MPI):** Indicated as a pharmacologic stress agent for MPI in adult patients who are unable to undergo adequate exercise stress [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 0.4 mg regadenoson (5 mL) administered as a rapid intravenous injection [US].
- **Administration Procedure:**
    - Administer the 5 mL dose within approximately 10 seconds into a peripheral vein (22 gauge or larger catheter/needle).
    - Immediately follow with a 5 mL saline flush.
    - Administer the radionuclide MPI agent 10–20 seconds after the saline flush. The radionuclide may be injected into the same catheter as the regadenoson [US].
- **Pre-procedural Instructions:**
    - Patients must avoid methylxanthines (e.g., caffeine, theophylline, aminophylline) for at least 12 hours prior to the procedure [US].
- **Instruction for Use:** Inspect the solution for particulate matter or discoloration before administration. The product is a clear, colorless solution [US].

## 4. Contraindications
Do not administer regadenoson to patients with [US]:
- **Second- or third-degree AV block**, unless the patient has a functioning artificial pacemaker.
- **Sinus node dysfunction**, unless the patient has a functioning artificial pacemaker.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myocardial Ischemia:** Fatal and nonfatal myocardial infarctions, ventricular arrhythmias, and cardiac arrest have occurred. Avoid use in patients with unstable angina or cardiovascular instability. Resuscitation equipment and trained staff must be available [US].
- **Sinoatrial (SA) and Atrioventricular (AV) Nodal Block:** Can depress nodal function, potentially leading to first-, second-, or third-degree AV block or sinus bradycardia [US].
- **Atrial Fibrillation/Flutter:** New-onset or recurrent atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response and atrial flutter have been reported [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, and respiratory arrest have occurred. Personnel and resuscitative equipment should be immediately available [US].
- **Hypotension:** Regadenoson induces vasodilation. Increased risk of serious hypotension exists in patients with autonomic dysfunction, hypovolemia, stenotic valvular disease, or carotid artery disease [US].
- **Hypertension:** Clinically significant blood pressure increases may occur, particularly in patients with pre-existing hypertension or when combined with low-level exercise [US].
- **Bronchoconstriction:** May induce dyspnea and respiratory compromise in patients with COPD or asthma. Appropriate bronchodilator therapy should be available [US].
- **Seizure:** Regadenoson may lower the seizure threshold. Methylxanthines are NOT recommended for managing seizures associated with regadenoson [US].
- **Cerebrovascular Accident (Stroke):** Hemorrhagic and ischemic strokes have been reported [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions typically begin soon after dosing and resolve within 15 minutes, except for headache (resolves within 30 minutes) [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Myocardial infarction, cardiac arrest, ventricular arrhythmias, third-degree heart block, asystole, syncope, and transient ischemic attacks [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Methylxanthines (Caffeine, Theophylline, Aminophylline):** Competitive adenosine receptor antagonists. They interfere with regadenoson's vasodilatory activity. Avoid for 12 hours before MPI [US].
- **Aminophylline:** Used specifically to attenuate or terminate severe or persistent adverse reactions to regadenoson [US].
- **Dipyridamole:** May increase regadenoson activity. Withhold dipyridamole for at least two days prior to administration [US].
- **Cardioactive Drugs:** In clinical trials, no reported issues occurred with concomitant use of $\beta$-blockers, calcium channel blockers, ACE inhibitors, or nitrates [US].
- **Cytochrome P450:** Regadenoson does not inhibit CYP1A2, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, or 3A4 in human liver microsomes [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not Categorized]. There are no available data in pregnant women. Animal studies showed reduced fetal weights and ossification delays at 10–20 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD), but only at doses causing maternal toxicity [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There is no information on presence in human milk. Due to potential serious cardiac reactions in infants, mothers should **pump and discard breast milk for 10 hours** after administration [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term fertility studies in animals have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed, though patients $\ge$ 75 years of age had a higher incidence of hypotension (2% vs $\le$ 1%) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required, including in patients with end-stage renal disease or those on dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Low-affinity agonist for the $A_{2A}$ adenosine receptor. Activation produces coronary vasodilation and increases coronary blood flow (CBF) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - Causes a rapid increase in CBF (doubling within 30 seconds) which returns to near-baseline within 10 minutes.
    - Myocardial uptake of the radiopharmaceutical is proportional to CBF, allowing for the detection of stenotic vs. normal arteries [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption/Distribution:** Achieves maximal plasma concentration within 1–4 minutes. Follows a 3-compartment model.
    - **Metabolism:** Unknown in humans; no detectable metabolites in animal or human liver microsomes.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 2 hours. Approximately 57% is excreted unchanged in the urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Efficacy (Studies 1 and 2):** Randomized, double-blind trials compared regadenoson to adenosine. Regadenoson demonstrated a high agreement rate (62–63%) with adenosine in identifying the extent of reversible perfusion abnormalities, establishing its efficacy as a stress agent [US].
- **Inadequate Exercise Study:** An open-label study showed that regadenoson could be safely administered 1 hour after inadequate exercise. Administration 3 minutes after exercise was associated with a higher rate of cardiac events (3% vs 0.5%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F). Excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F).
- **Handling:** Single-dose container; discard any unused portion [US].
