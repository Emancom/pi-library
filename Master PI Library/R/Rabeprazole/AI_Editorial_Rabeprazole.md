# Editorial Product Information: Rabeprazole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rabeprazole sodium (a substituted benzimidazole).
- **Chemical Class:** Proton Pump Inhibitor (PPI).
- **Brand Name(s):** **APO-Rabeprazole** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Enteric-coated (EC) tablets: 10 mg (pink, biconvex) and 20 mg (yellow, biconvex) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Rabeprazole is indicated for the following conditions [Australian]:
- **Gastro-oesophageal Reflux Disease (GORD):** Treatment and prevention of relapse.
- **Symptomatic GORD:** Management of symptoms in patients without oesophagitis.
- **Peptic Ulcer Disease:** Treatment of active duodenal ulcers and active gastric ulcers.
- **Helicobacter pylori Eradication:** In combination with amoxicillin and clarithromycin for the eradication of *H. pylori* and healing of associated peptic ulcers in patients with peptic ulcer disease or chronic gastritis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
Tablets must be swallowed whole and should not be crushed or chewed. They should be taken at the same time each day. Rabeprazole may be taken with or without food [Australian].

### Adult Dosing [Australian]
- **Active GORD:** 20 mg once daily for 4 to 8 weeks.
- **GORD Relapse Prevention:** 10 mg once daily; may be increased to 20 mg once daily if required.
- **Symptomatic GORD (without oesophagitis):** Initially 10 mg once daily. If no response after 4 weeks, increase to 20 mg once daily. For resolved symptoms, an on-demand regimen of 10 mg once daily as needed may be used.
- **Duodenal Ulcer:** 20 mg once daily for 4 to 8 weeks. Some patients may respond to 10 mg once daily.
- **Gastric Ulcer:** 20 mg once daily for 6 to 12 weeks.
- **H. pylori Eradication:** 20 mg twice daily plus clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily and amoxicillin 1 g twice daily for 7 days.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to rabeprazole sodium, other substituted benzimidazoles (PPIs), or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastric Malignancy:** Symptomatic response does not exclude the presence of gastric malignancy. Exclude malignancy prior to treatment [Australian].
- **Acute Tubulointerstitial Nephritis (TIN):** May occur at any point during therapy and can progress to renal failure. Discontinue immediately if TIN is suspected [Australian].
- **Cyanocobalamin (Vitamin B-12) Deficiency:** Long-term therapy (typically >3 years) may lead to malabsorption due to hypo- or achlorhydria [Australian].
- **Hypomagnesaemia:** Reported rarely, typically with prolonged use. Serious events include tetany, arrhythmias, and seizures. Monitor magnesium levels in patients on long-term therapy or concomitant digoxin/diuretics [Australian].
- **Fractures:** Increased risk of osteoporosis-related fractures (hip, wrist, spine) with high-dose and long-term (≥1 year) PPI therapy [Australian].
- **Clostridium difficile:** PPI therapy may increase the risk of gastrointestinal infections such as *C. difficile* [Australian].
- **Subacute Cutaneous Lupus Erythematosus (SCLE):** Reported rarely. If lesions occur (especially in sun-exposed areas) with arthralgia, discontinue the drug [Australian].
- **Fundic Gland Polyps:** Long-term use is associated with an increased risk; use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interferences:** PPIs increase Chromogranin A (CgA) levels, which may interfere with investigations for neuroendocrine tumours. Stop rabeprazole at least 14 days before CgA testing [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Rabeprazole is generally well-tolerated; most adverse events are mild to moderate and transient [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (≥1% to <10%)** | Headache, dizziness, diarrhoea, nausea, abdominal pain, flatulence, vomiting, constipation, rhinitis, pharyngitis, cough, back pain, myalgia, rash, asthenia, insomnia, chest pain. |
| **Uncommon (0.1% to <1%)** | Dyspepsia, eructation, dry mouth, sinusitis, bronchitis, arthralgia, leg cramps, urinary tract infection, fever, somnolence, peripheral oedema. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anorexia, gastritis, stomatitis, vision/taste disturbances, depression, leucocytosis, pruritus, sweating, weight gain. |

**Post-Marketing Experience:** 
Serious but rare reports include erythema multiforme, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, hepatitis, jaundice, and hepatic encephalopathy (in patients with cirrhosis) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **pH-Dependent Absorption:** Rabeprazole produces sustained inhibition of gastric acid. Co-administration with **atazanavir** is not recommended. Use with caution/monitoring for **ketoconazole** (33% decrease in levels) and **digoxin** (22% increase in trough levels) [Australian].
- **Methotrexate:** Concomitant use may elevate and prolong serum levels of methotrexate/hydroxymethotrexate, potentially leading to toxicity [Australian].
- **Clopidogrel:** Inhibition of CYP2C19 by rabeprazole may reduce clopidogrel’s antiplatelet activity; concomitant use is discouraged [Australian].
- **Mycophenolate Mofetil:** May reduce exposure to the active metabolite (mycophenolic acid). Use with caution in transplant patients [Australian].
- **Interactions Excluded:** No clinically significant interactions found with warfarin, theophylline, diazepam, phenytoin, or antacids [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Clinical data in pregnant women are limited; use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if rabeprazole is excreted in human milk. Radioactivity was detected in the milk of lactating rats. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in rat studies at doses up to 10 times the human clinical exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended due to a lack of clinical experience [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required. Elimination is slightly decreased, but no accumulation occurs [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is necessary for patients with renal failure, including those on haemodialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Exposure (AUC) is doubled and half-life is increased in mild to moderate cirrhosis. Exercise caution when initiating treatment in patients with severe hepatic dysfunction [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Rabeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor that suppresses gastric acid secretion by inhibiting the $H^+/K^+$-ATPase enzyme at the secretory surface of gastric parietal cells.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Anti-secretory effect begins within 1 hour; maximum effect occurs within 2 to 4 hours. Steady-state inhibition is reached after 3 days of once-daily dosing. Gastric acid secretion normalises within 2 to 3 days after discontinuation [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak plasma levels ($C_{max}$) at ~3.5 hours. Absolute bioavailability is ~52%.
    - **Distribution:** ~97% bound to human plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP2C19 and CYP3A4 to thioether and carboxylic acid metabolites.
    - **Excretion:** ~90% eliminated in urine (as metabolites); 10% in faeces. Plasma half-life is ~1 hour [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **GORD:** In placebo-controlled trials, rabeprazole 10 mg and 20 mg were significantly superior to placebo and as effective as omeprazole 20 mg in endoscopic healing and symptom relief [Australian].
- **Duodenal/Gastric Ulcers:** Studies demonstrated healing rates (endoscopically confirmed) equivalent or superior to ranitidine and equivalent to omeprazole [Australian].
- **H. pylori Eradication:** Triple therapy (rabeprazole, amoxicillin, clarithromycin) for 7 days achieved an eradication rate of 94% and a duodenal ulcer healing rate of 91% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Expiry date is located on the packaging [Australian].
