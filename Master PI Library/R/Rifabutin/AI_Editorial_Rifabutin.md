# Editorial Product Information: Rifabutin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of rifabutin for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing recommendations, and safety profiles.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labelling for definitive legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rifabutin (a semi-synthetic ansamycin antibiotic).
- **Brand Name:** Mycobutin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J04AB04.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Hard gelatin capsules containing 150 mg of rifabutin.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Rifabutin is indicated for the following:
- **Prophylaxis of *M. avium-intracellulare* complex (MAC) infections:** In patients with advanced HIV infection (CD4+ counts < 200/μL).
- **Treatment of MAC infections and other atypical mycobacteria:** Including in immunocompromised patients.
- **Treatment of chronic multidrug-resistant pulmonary tuberculosis (MDR-TB):** In the presence of rifampicin-resistant but rifabutin-sensitive *M. tuberculosis* strains.
- **Treatment of newly diagnosed pulmonary tuberculosis:** In the presence of rifampicin-resistant but rifabutin-sensitive *M. tuberculosis* strains.

*Note: In accordance with standard mycobacterial therapy, rifabutin must always be used in combination with other anti-mycobacterial agents not belonging to the rifamycin family.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Rifabutin may be administered as a single daily dose, independently of meals.

- **MAC Prophylaxis (Monotherapy):** 300 mg (2 capsules) once daily.
- **MAC Treatment (Combination Regimens):** 300 mg to 600 mg daily for up to 6 months after negative cultures are achieved.
- **Chronic MDR-TB:** 300 mg to 450 mg daily for up to 6 months after sputum culture conversion.
- **Newly Diagnosed TB:** 150 mg to 300 mg daily for 6 months.

### Dose Adjustments for Co-administration
- **With Clarithromycin:** Reduce rifabutin dose to 300 mg daily.
- **With Indinavir:** Reduce rifabutin dose by 50% (and increase indinavir to 1,000 mg four times daily).
- **With Protease Inhibitors (PIs):** Significant adjustments are often required (see Section 7). For example, with Atazanavir/Ritonavir or Darunavir/Ritonavir, a 75% reduction in rifabutin dose (to 150 mg daily or every other day) may be necessary [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to rifabutin or other rifamycins (e.g., rifampicin).
- **Paediatric use:** Contraindicated due to insufficient clinical experience [Australian].
- **Concomitant Medications:** 
    - Ritonavir [Australian].
    - Rilpivirine prolonged-release suspension for injection [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Secretions:** May impart a red-orange colour to urine, skin, and body secretions. Soft contact lenses may be permanently stained.
- **Haematological Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of white blood cell (WBC) counts, platelet counts, and liver enzymes is recommended. Rifabutin is associated with neutropenia and, more rarely, thrombocytopenia.
- **Clostridium difficile-associated diarrhoea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterials; severity can range from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS). Discontinue if lesions progress.
- **Uveitis:** Risk increases with higher doses or when co-administered with clarithromycin or fluconazole. Patients should be referred to an ophthalmologist if ocular symptoms occur.
- **Vitamin K:** Rifamycins may induce hepatic breakdown of vitamin K, potentially leading to haemorrhagic disturbances in pregnant women and neonates.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often difficult to distinguish from the underlying disease or concurrent multi-drug therapy.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect(s) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Blood and Lymphatic** | Anaemia (4–9%), leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, pancytopenia. |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Jaundice (8–12%), increased liver enzymes. |
| **General/Skin** | Pyrexia (2–4%), rash (3–4%), skin discolouration. |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Arthralgia, myalgia (~3%). |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, *C. difficile* colitis. |
| **Ocular** | Uveitis, corneal deposits (noted particularly in paediatric surveillance). |
| **Immune System** | Hypersensitivity, bronchospasm, anaphylactic shock (rare). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
Rifabutin is an inducer of the **CYP450 3A** subfamily, though to a lesser extent than rifampicin.

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Drugs with Decreased Concentration/Efficacy:**
    - Antiretrovirals (e.g., Bictegravir, Elvitegravir, Doravirine, Rilpivirine).
    - Anti-HCV agents (e.g., Sofosbuvir).
    - Oral contraceptives (alternative contraception methods are required).
    - Antifungals (e.g., Itraconazole, Voriconazole, Posaconazole).
    - Others: Methadone, Warfarin, Phenytoin, Theophylline, Cyclosporin, Tacrolimus, Corticosteroids.
- **Drugs that Increase Rifabutin Levels:**
    - Clarithromycin (increases AUC by ~77%).
    - Fluconazole (increases AUC by ~82%).
    - Protease Inhibitors (e.g., Ritonavir, Indinavir, Lopinavir) can dramatically increase rifabutin and its metabolite levels, significantly increasing the risk of uveitis.

### Interaction Management
- Concomitant use with many antiretrovirals is not recommended or requires significant dose modification of either drug [Australian].
- Prophylactic monitoring for neutropenia and uveitis is essential when using rifabutin with CYP3A4 inhibitors.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Animal studies show skeletal variants (rats) and maternotoxicity with fetal skeletal anomalies (rabbits). There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefit outweighs potential risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if rifabutin is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the importance of the medication to the mother.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats (160 mg/kg/day) showed impaired spermatogenesis and gonadal effects.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended due to lack of clinical experience [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No specific dose alterations suggested, though data are limited.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild to moderate: No adjustment required.
    - **Severe (CrCl < 30 mL/min):** Reduce dosage by 50%.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in liver insufficiency. Consider dose reduction in severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits DNA-dependent RNA polymerase in susceptible strains of *M. tuberculosis* and *M. avium* complex. It shows incomplete cross-resistance with rifampicin.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; Tmax 2–4 hours. Bioavailability is ~85%. High-fat meals slow the rate of absorption.
    - **Distribution:** Highly lipophilic with extensive tissue distribution (especially in the lungs), except for the brain. 85% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Undergoes hepatic metabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 35–40 hours. Excreted mainly via urine (metabolites) and faeces (~30%).
