# Editorial Product Information: Risdiplam

## Editorial Note
- The information contained in this editorial was synthesized from the following official product information source: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of risdiplam for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult the local PI for prescribing and legal requirements within their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Risdiplam (a survival of motor neuron 2 [SMN2] pre-mRNA splicing modifier).
- **Brand Name**: **Evrysdi** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - **Powder for oral solution**: 60 mg risdiplam per bottle; following reconstitution, the solution contains 0.75 mg/mL [Australian].
    - **Film-coated tablet**: 5 mg risdiplam [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration**: Oral or enteral (via nasogastric or gastrostomy tube for oral solution only).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **5q Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA)**: Indicated for the treatment of patients with 5q SMA [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
Risdiplam is administered once daily at approximately the same time each day, with or without food. Treatment should be initiated by a specialist experienced in SMA management.

### Dosing Regimen by Age and Body Weight
| Age and Body Weight | Recommended Daily Dose | Recommended Form |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| < 2 months of age | 0.15 mg/kg | Oral solution |
| 2 months to < 2 years | 0.20 mg/kg | Oral solution |
| ≥ 2 years weighing < 20 kg | 0.25 mg/kg | Oral solution |
| ≥ 2 years weighing ≥ 20 kg | 5 mg | Oral solution or tablet |

*Note: For preterm infants, age is based on corrected age.*

### Administration Instructions
- **Oral Solution**: Must be reconstituted by a healthcare professional prior to dispensing. Patients should swallow water after administration. If not taken within 5 minutes of being drawn into the syringe, the dose must be discarded.
- **Film-Coated Tablets**: Should be swallowed whole with water. For patients with swallowing difficulties, the tablet may be dispersed in approximately 15 mL of non-chlorinated drinking water (bottled water). The dispersion must be taken within 10 minutes of preparation and protected from sunlight. Tablets must not be crushed, chewed, or cut.
- **Enteral Administration**: Only the oral solution should be used for nasogastric or gastrostomy tube administration. The tube should be flushed with water after the dose.

### Missed Dose
- If within 6 hours of the scheduled time: Administer as soon as possible.
- If > 6 hours after the scheduled time: Skip the dose and resume the next dose at the usual time the following day.
- If vomiting occurs or the dose is not fully swallowed: Do not administer a replacement dose; wait until the next scheduled dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to risdiplam or any excipients (e.g., benzoate, sucralose, or sugar alcohols depending on the formulation) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity**: Animal studies demonstrate teratogenic and embryo-fetotoxic effects. Pregnancy status must be verified in females of reproductive potential before initiation.
- **Contraception**: 
    - Females: Highly effective contraception is required during treatment and for at least 1 month after the final dose.
    - Males: Highly effective contraception is required for the patient and their female partners during treatment and for at least 4 months after the final dose.
- **Male Fertility**: Potential for compromised fertility (reversible upon discontinuation). Male patients should not donate sperm during treatment and for 4 months following the last dose. Fertility preservation should be discussed prior to treatment.
- **Retinal Toxicity**: Structural and functional abnormalities were observed in animal studies. While not yet observed in clinical trials with SMA patients, long-term human data are limited. Caution is advised when co-administering with retinotoxic drugs.
- **Type 0 or IV SMA**: Limited clinical data are available; treatment decisions should be based on individualized risk-benefit expert evaluation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Infantile-Onset SMA (FIREFISH Trial)
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Upper respiratory tract infection (90.3%), Pyrexia (64.5%), Lower respiratory tract infection (67.7%), Rash (32.3%), Constipation (25.8%), Diarrhoea (25.8%), Vomiting (21%), Teething (12.9%), Cough (12.9%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Urinary tract infection, Rhinorrhoea, Respiratory failure, Ear infection, Gastroesophageal reflux disease, Eczema, Dehydration |

### Later-Onset SMA (SUNFISH Trial)
- **Very Common (>10%)**: Headache (26.3%), Pyrexia/Fever (22%–35.2%), Upper respiratory tract infection (84.4%), Diarrhoea (17%–25.1%), Vomiting (25.7%), Constipation (11.2%), Abdominal pain (11.2%).
- **Common (1–10%)**: Nausea, Arthralgia, Urinary tract infection, Pneumonia, Back pain, Anxiety, Ear pain.

### Post-Marketing Experience
- **Uncommon**: Tachycardia.
- **Unknown frequency**: Cutaneous vasculitis (symptoms recovered after permanent discontinuation).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Metabolism**: Risdiplam is primarily metabolized by FMO1 and FMO3, and partially by CYPs 1A1, 2J2, 3A4, and 3A7. 
- **Effects of other drugs on Risdiplam**: Strong CYP3A inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole) do not significantly alter risdiplam exposure. No dose adjustment is required with CYP3A inhibitors.
- **Effects of Risdiplam on other drugs**: Risdiplam is a weak inhibitor of CYP3A. It is an in vitro inhibitor of **MATE1** and **MATE2-K** transporters. Co-administration may increase plasma levels of MATE substrates (e.g., **metformin**, **fexofenadine**). Monitor for toxicity; dose reduction of the substrate may be necessary.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Other SMA Therapies**: No safety or efficacy data support the concomitant use of risdiplam with nusinersen. There is no data regarding use in patients previously treated with SMN1 gene therapy.
- **Retinotoxic Drugs**: Potential for synergistic retinal toxicity is unknown; use caution.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category D** [Australian]. Risdiplam should not be used during pregnancy unless the potential benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the fetus. It is known to cross the placental barrier in rats.
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if risdiplam is excreted in human milk (excretion is confirmed in rat milk). Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment due to the potential for harm to the infant.
- **Fertility**: Non-clinical data suggest impacts on male reproductive organs and sperm parameters (reversible), and decreased female reproductive performance in rats at high doses.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy are supported in patients 16 days of age and older. No data are available for infants < 16 days old or preterm infants before reaching a corrected age of 16 days.
- **Elderly Use**: Clinical studies did not include patients > 60 years of age. Based on PK data in subjects up to 69 years old, no dose adjustment is required.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dedicated studies have been performed. Renal excretion of unchanged risdiplam is minor (8%); no dose adjustment is expected to be required.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No dose adjustment is required for patients with **mild or moderate** hepatic impairment. Risdiplam has not been studied in patients with **severe** hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Risdiplam modifies the splicing of survival motor neuron 2 (SMN2) pre-mRNA. It promotes the inclusion of exon 7 in the mRNA transcript, increasing the production of functional and stable SMN protein throughout the body, including the central nervous system.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Rapidly absorbed; Tmax 1–5 hours (oral solution) or 2–4.5 hours (tablets). Food delays Tmax by approximately 1 hour but does not significantly affect total exposure.
    - **Distribution**: Distributes evenly to all parts of the body; crosses the blood-brain barrier. 89% protein bound (primarily to albumin).
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily via FMO1 and FMO3.
    - **Elimination**: Terminal half-life is approximately 50 hours in patients with SMA. Approximately 53% is excreted in feces and 28% in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **FIREFISH**: Open-label study in infantile-onset SMA (Type 1). Showed significant improvement in motor milestones (e.g., sitting without support) and event-free survival compared to natural history.
- **SUNFISH**: Double-blind, placebo-controlled study in later-onset SMA (Type 2 and 3; ages 2–25). Showed statistically significant improvement in motor function (MFM32 score) at 12 months compared to placebo.
- **RAINBOWFISH**: Open-label study in pre-symptomatic infants. Demonstrated the ability of most infants to achieve motor milestones such as sitting, standing, and walking independently.
- **JEWELFISH**: Open-label study in patients previously treated with other SMA-modifying therapies (e.g., nusinersen, onasemnogene abeparvovec), showing a consistent safety profile and sustained SMN protein increase.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Powder for Oral Solution**: Store below 25°C in the original amber bottle [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution**: Store in a refrigerator (2°C–8°C). Discard after 64 days. If necessary, it may be kept at room temperature (below 40°C) for a combined total of no more than 5 days [Australian].
- **Film-Coated Tablets**: Store below 30°C in the original package to protect from moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
