# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The jurisdictions contributing to this editorial include: **US (FDA)**. Information from other major regulatory authorities (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) was not provided for this synthesis.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals based on available regulatory documentation.
- Note: This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discolouration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions manifest within minutes of intravenous administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor the patient if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in the frequency or severity of events has been reported [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Precaution):** While this editorial concerns the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Very Common Effects
Adverse events reported in ESRD clinical trials with a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), flu syndrome (15–29%), headache (3–37%), chest pain (6–15%), abdominal pain (5–21%), fever (5–12%), infection (10–24%), accidental injury (8–12%), asthenia (8–12%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), palpitations (3–8%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), dyspepsia (5–9%), anorexia (3–6%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), rhinitis (6–11%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%), insomnia (3–6%) [US].
- **Other:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), anemia (3–12%), pruritus (3–8%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and respiratory distress** (bronchospasm, laryngeal edema).
- **Seizures.**
- **Body Odor:** Transient nausea and vomiting, and less frequent body odor, have been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned by letter, but label states "should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for inborn errors of metabolism is weight-based (50 mg/kg) and applicable to paediatric populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of the *oral* formulation have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; however, the *intravenous* formulation is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. The drug is easily removed by dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine levels from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the effect on specific clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
