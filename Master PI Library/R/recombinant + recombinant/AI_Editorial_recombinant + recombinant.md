# Editorial Product Information: recombinant + recombinant

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **recombinant + recombinant**
- Chemical class: Recombinant viral protein vaccine (stabilised prefusion F glycoprotein antigens of respiratory syncytial virus subgroups A and B; produced by recombinant DNA technology in Chinese Hamster Ovary [CHO] cells)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular injection only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Additional regulatory note: This vaccine is subject to additional monitoring in Australia to support rapid identification of new safety information. [Australian]

## 3. Approved Indications
- Active immunisation of pregnant individuals between 24 and 36 weeks' gestation for the prevention of lower respiratory tract disease caused by respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) in infants from birth through 6 months of age.  
- Active immunisation of individuals 60 years of age and older for prevention of lower respiratory tract disease caused by RSV.  
- Use should follow official national recommendations.  
- Paediatric active immunisation (from birth to <18 years): Not approved / safety and efficacy have not been established.

(If an indication is limited to a specific jurisdiction it is noted in the text above. No other jurisdiction-specific indications were stated in the available sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Standard dose: 0.5 mL as a single intramuscular dose for indicated populations.

### Dosing by indication
- Pregnant individuals: Single 0.5 mL dose administered in late second or third trimester (recommended window 24–36 weeks of gestation). Revaccination in subsequent pregnancies has not been studied.
- Individuals ≥60 years: Single 0.5 mL dose. The need for revaccination has not been established.

### Administration instructions
- Route: Intramuscular injection only, preferably into the deltoid muscle of the upper arm.
- Do not administer intravascularly, intradermally, or subcutaneously.
- Do not mix with other vaccines or medicinal products in the same syringe.
- Administer different injectable vaccines at different injection sites.
- The vaccine is presented as a lyophilised component that must be reconstituted with the diluent provided; follow the manufacturer-supplied reconstitution procedure (reconstitute using the provided diluent only; visually inspect reconstituted vaccine and do not use if particulate matter or discolouration is present).
- Single-use: discard any residue after administration.

### Titration schedules
- Not applicable / not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to the active substances (stabilised prefusion F proteins of RSV A and RSV B) or to any excipients listed for the product.  
- Any other contraindications specific to particular jurisdictions were not specified in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- Not specified as a boxed warning in the available sources. (Where jurisdictionally present, specific boxed warnings were not included in the supplied material.)

### General warnings and precautions
- Guillain–Barré syndrome (GBS) has been reported rarely after vaccination in older adults (≥60 years). Healthcare professionals should be alert to signs and symptoms of GBS to allow prompt diagnosis and management.
- Hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis: Appropriate medical treatment and supervision should be immediately available in case of a rare anaphylactic reaction following vaccination.
- Thrombocytopenia / coagulation disorders: Use with caution in individuals with thrombocytopenia or bleeding disorders due to risk of bleeding following intramuscular injection.
- Concurrent illness: Vaccination should be postponed in individuals with an acute febrile illness. Minor infections without fever (e.g., common cold) are not a reason to defer vaccination.
- Anxiety-related reactions: Vasovagal syncope, hyperventilation, or other stress-related reactions can occur; procedures should be in place to avoid injury from fainting.
- Immunocompromised individuals (including those on immunosuppressive therapy) may have a diminished immune response; data on use in immunocompromised persons are limited.
- Use in pregnant individuals <24 weeks' gestation: Not studied.
- As with any vaccine, immunisation may not protect all recipients.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for immediate adverse events post-vaccination (anaphylaxis and syncope risk); observe per local guidance for post-vaccination observation period.
- For individuals with a history suggestive of GBS, monitor for new neurological symptoms after vaccination.
- No specific laboratory monitoring parameters were specified in the available sources.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Do not mix with other vaccines/medicines in the same syringe.
- When coadministered with other vaccines (see Section 8), consider potential modest effects on immune responses to some antigens (see interaction data); the clinical relevance of modest reductions in immune responses (e.g., to pertussis antigens when coadministered with dTpa) is unknown.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### General principles
- Do not mix vaccines/medicinal products in the same syringe.
- Different injectable vaccines should be administered at different injection sites.

### Clinically studied coadministration (summary)
- Concomitant administration with tetanus-diphtheria-acellular pertussis (dTpa):
  - In healthy non-pregnant women, concomitant administration of **recombinant + recombinant** with dTpa demonstrated non-inferior immune response to RSV antigens compared with **recombinant + recombinant** alone; diphtheria and tetanus components met non-inferiority when coadministered.
  - Immune responses to pertussis antigens (pertussis toxin, FHA, pertactin) were lower when dTpa was given concomitantly with **recombinant + recombinant** compared with dTpa alone. The clinical relevance of the observed reduction in pertussis antibody responses is unknown.
  - Example immunogenicity metrics (one study): anti-PT geometric mean concentration ratio ~0.80 (0.64, 1.00); anti-FHA GMR ~0.59 (0.50, 0.70). [No jurisdiction-specific restriction noted]

- Concomitant administration with seasonal inactivated influenza vaccine (SIIV):
  - In adults ≥65 years, coadministration met predefined non-inferiority criteria for immune responses to both vaccines versus separate administration.
  - Example findings (one study): SIIV HAI titres in coadministration vs sequential groups had GMRs ranging approximately 0.77–0.90 depending on strain; RSV neutralising titres had GMRs ~0.85–0.86 (coadministration vs sequential). These differences met the non-inferiority criteria used in the studies.

- Concomitant administration with COVID-19 mRNA vaccine (BNT162b2):
  - In adults ≥65 years, coadministration met predefined non-inferiority criteria for immune responses to RSV and to the COVID-19 mRNA vaccine compared with separate administration.
  - Example findings (one study): SARS-CoV-2 neutralising GMTs (Omicron BA.4/BA.5 and reference strain) had GMRs near unity (e.g., ~0.94–0.97). RSV A and RSV B neutralising titres were non-inferior and in some comparisons higher when coadministered (GMRs >1.3 for RSV A/B in certain groups).

- Concomitant administration with high-dose influenza vaccine and COVID-19 mRNA vaccine:
  - Coadministration of **recombinant + recombinant** with both a COVID-19 mRNA vaccine and high-dose influenza vaccine demonstrated immunologic non-inferiority across antigens (RSV A/B, SARS-CoV-2 strains, and influenza HAI titres) in the evaluated study population (adults ≥65 years).

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Not applicable / not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Coadministration may modestly affect immune responses to certain antigens (e.g., pertussis components when coadministered with dTpa). Clinical significance of those observations is unknown; avoid mixing in same syringe and follow local immunisation guidance on coadministration.

### Management notes
- When coadministration is planned, inform patients of potential for altered antibody responses to some antigens (pertussis) though clinical impact is uncertain.
- Observe standard precautions for coadministration of multiple vaccines (different injection sites, monitor for adverse events).

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category/system:
  - [Australian: Pregnancy Category A]
  - Other jurisdictions: pregnancy categorisation not specified in available sources.
- Use in pregnancy:
  - Indicated for active immunisation of pregnant individuals between 24 and 36 weeks' gestation to prevent RSV lower respiratory tract disease in infants up to 6 months of age.
  - A large amount of data in pregnant women (more than 4,000 exposed outcomes) were referenced in the source materials; the detailed outcomes and safety tables were not fully included in the available excerpts.
  - There are no data on use prior to 24 weeks' gestation.
- Fertility and developmental toxicity:
  - In animal studies (female New Zealand White rabbits) receiving multiple IM doses of a bivalent formulation at higher exposures, there were no effects on female fertility or on embryofetal or postnatal development. Effects on male fertility have not been evaluated.

### Breastfeeding

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **recombinant + recombinant** contains stabilised prefusion F glycoprotein antigens from RSV subgroups A and B intended to elicit neutralising antibody responses against the RSV prefusion F protein, thereby reducing risk of lower respiratory tract disease caused by RSV in vaccinated persons or, when given to pregnant individuals, in their infants via transplacental antibody transfer.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination following intramuscular injection: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Vaccine-induced immune responses were measured by RSV neutralising antibody titres (NT or NT50) to RSV A and RSV B and by antigen-specific antibody assays; coadministration studies evaluated effects on immune responses to concomitant vaccines (influenza, dTpa, COVID-19 mRNA vaccine).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Several clinical studies described immunogenicity and coadministration outcomes in adult populations. Key trial summaries provided in source material are summarised below.

### Study C3671004 (dTpa coadministration study)
- Population: Healthy non-pregnant women.
- Design: Randomised evaluation of concomitant administration of **recombinant + recombinant** with dTpa versus controls.
- Key outcomes:
  - Immune response to RSV antigens with concomitant administration was non-inferior to RSV response when **recombinant + recombinant** was administered alone.
  - Diphtheria and tetanus antibody responses met non-inferiority criteria when coadministered.
  - Immune responses to pertussis antigens (pertussis toxin, FHA, pertactin) were lower with concomitant administration compared to dTpa alone; clinical relevance unknown.
  - Selected immunogenicity metrics (e.g., RSV A and B GMT ratios ~0.96–0.97 comparing ABRYSVO/dTpa to placebo/dTpa) indicated non-inferiority for RSV antigens in this study population.

### Study C3671006 (seasonal influenza coadministration)
- Population: Adults ≥65 years.
- Design: Randomised comparison of coadministration vs sequential administration of **recombinant + recombinant** and seasonal inactivated influenza vaccine (SIIV).
- Key outcomes:
  - Predefined non-inferiority criteria for immune responses to both vaccines (RSV neutralising titres and SIIV HAI titres) were met in coadministration versus sequential administration.
  - Example results: RSV NT GMRs of ~0.85–0.86 (coadministration/sequential); influenza HAI GMRs ranged ~0.77–0.90 by strain; non-inferiority was achieved per study criteria.

### Study C5481001 (COVID-19 mRNA vaccine and multi-vaccine coadministration)
- Population: Adults ≥65 years.
- Design: Randomised immunogenicity study evaluating coadministration of **recombinant + recombinant** with a COVID-19 mRNA vaccine (BNT162b2) and with combinations including high-dose influenza vaccine.
- Key outcomes:
  - Concomitant administration met predefined immunologic non-inferiority criteria for RSV, SARS-CoV-2, and influenza antigens compared to separate administration.
  - Example results: SARS-CoV-2 neutralising GMTs were similar with GMRs near unity (e.g., 0.94–0.97); RSV A/B NT50 GMRs in some comparisons >1.3 when coadministered with BNT162b2; coadministration with BNT162b2 and QIV also demonstrated non-inferiority across measured antigens.
