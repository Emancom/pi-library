# Editorial Product Information: radium-223 dichloride

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **radium-223 dichloride**
- Chemical class: Radionuclide alpha-emitting therapeutic (isotope of radium)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous administration (slow intravenous injection)

## 2. Regulatory History

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of castration‑resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) patients with symptomatic bone metastases and no known visceral metastatic disease.
- Use in other indications, populations or combinations is not established unless explicitly stated in warnings/contraindications below.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Activity: 55 kBq per kg body weight administered intravenously.
- Schedule: Given at 4‑week intervals for a total of 6 injections.
- Safety and efficacy beyond 6 injections have not been studied.

### Administration instructions
- Administer by slow intravenous injection (generally up to 1 minute).
- Flush intravenous access line or cannula with isotonic saline before and after injection.
- Inspect product visually prior to use. Do not use if discoloured, particulate matter present, or container defective.
- The product is ready-to-use: do not dilute or mix with other medicinal products (no compatibility data).
- Each vial is for single patient use only. Any residue should be treated and discarded as radioactive waste in accordance with local radiation safety regulations.
- Handling and administration must be performed by personnel authorised to handle radiopharmaceuticals in designated clinical settings and in accordance with national/regional radiation protection regulations.

## 5. Contraindications
- Concurrent use with abiraterone acetate in combination with prednisone or prednisolone (combination contraindicated because of increased fractures and deaths observed in a clinical trial).
- Known hypersensitivity to radium‑223 or to any component of the product.
- Pregnancy (see Section 9) — product is not to be used in women who are or may be pregnant.
- Breastfeeding (see Section 9) — product is not to be used in women who are breastfeeding.
- Any other contraindication explicitly stated in a regional label: Not specified beyond the above in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- **Contraindicated in combination with abiraterone acetate plus prednisone/prednisolone** due to an observed increase in fractures and mortality in a randomized trial where the combination was used in patients with asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic CRPC and bone metastases. See Section 8 (Interactions) and Section 12 (Clinical trials) for details.

### General warnings and precautions
- Bone marrow suppression: Radium‑223 can cause thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leucopenia and pancytopenia. Baseline haematological evaluation and monitoring prior to each dose is required (see Monitoring requirements).
  - Before first dose: ANC ≥ 1.5 × 10^9/L, platelets ≥ 100 × 10^9/L, haemoglobin ≥ 100 g/L.
  - Before subsequent doses: ANC ≥ 1.0 × 10^9/L, platelets ≥ 50 × 10^9/L.
  - If these thresholds are not met, treatment should be delayed; if recovery does not occur within 6 weeks after the last administration despite receiving standard of care, further radium‑223 should only be continued after careful benefit‑risk assessment.
- Bone fractures and osteoporosis:
  - Radium‑223 increases the risk of bone fractures. This risk was markedly increased when combined with abiraterone acetate plus prednisone/prednisolone.
  - Radium‑223 may accumulate at sites of high bone turnover (e.g. osteoporosis, recent or micro‑fractures) and increase fracture risk.
  - Assess bone status (e.g. imaging, bone mineral density) and baseline fracture risk prior to initiating therapy and monitor patients for at least 24 months.
  - Consider preventive measures such as bisphosphonates or denosumab before starting or resuming radium‑223, especially in patients at high baseline risk of fractures.
  - Orthopaedic stabilisation should be performed for established bone fractures before initiating or resuming treatment.
- Combination with other systemic cancer therapies:
  - Concomitant initiation of radium‑223 with abiraterone acetate plus prednisone/prednisolone is contraindicated (see above).
  - Safety and efficacy of combining radium‑223 with systemic cancer therapies other than luteinising hormone‑releasing hormone (LHRH) analogues have not been established; an increased risk of mortality and fractures is possible. Such combinations are therefore not recommended.
  - Concomitant chemotherapy may have additive effects on bone marrow suppression.
- Spinal cord compression: Treat established or imminent spinal cord compression per standard of care before starting or resuming radium‑223.
- Inflammatory bowel disease: Safety and efficacy in Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis have not been studied; exercise caution.
- Radiation safety:
  - Potential radiation exposure to healthcare workers, caregivers and household members from decaying radionuclide and contaminated body fluids (urine, faeces, vomit). Implement radiation protection measures in accordance with national/local regulations.
  - Adhere to ALARA principles: minimise time in radiation areas, maximise distance, and apply shielding where appropriate.
  - Any unused product or materials used in preparation/administration must be treated as radioactive waste and disposed of per local regulations.
- Secondary malignancy: Radium‑223 contributes to cumulative radiation exposure which may increase long‑term risk of cancer and hereditary defects; no radium‑223–related cancers were reported in clinical trials with follow‑up up to three years.
- Fertility: Potential adverse effects on spermatogenesis; advise contraception (see Section 9).
- Paediatric use: Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents (< 18 years) not studied; not recommended.

### Monitoring requirements
- Haematology: full blood count (including ANC, platelets, haemoglobin) at baseline and prior to each dose.
- Bone health: baseline assessment of bone status (imaging, bone mineral density) and fracture risk; ongoing monitoring for at least 24 months.
- Monitor for gastrointestinal adverse effects and signs of marrow suppression during treatment and post‑treatment follow‑up.
- Radiation contamination monitoring per institutional protocols.

## 7. Adverse Effects

- The safety profile is primarily derived from a phase III trial of approximately 600 patients with castration‑resistant prostate cancer with symptomatic bone metastases.
- Serious adverse reactions: thrombocytopenia and neutropenia (may be severe or life‑threatening).
- Long‑term radiation‑related malignancies are a theoretical risk; none reported in clinical trials with follow‑up to three years.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- No formal clinical interaction studies have been performed. Specific pharmacokinetic interaction data are not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions / clinically significant interactions
- **Contraindicated combination:** radium‑223 with abiraterone acetate plus prednisone/prednisolone — a randomized trial (ERA‑223) showed increased incidence of fractures and increased all‑cause mortality in the radium‑223 arm versus placebo when combined with abiraterone plus prednisone/prednisolone (see Section 12).
  - In that study: fractures 28.6% (radium‑223 + abiraterone/pred) vs 11.4% (placebo + abiraterone/pred); deaths 38.5% vs 35.5%.
  - Concurrent use of bone‑protecting agents (bisphosphonates or denosumab) reduced fracture incidence in both arms.
  - Recommendation: do not combine radium‑223 with abiraterone acetate plus prednisone/prednisolone.
  - Timing: limited data on safe intervals; recommended that radium‑223 not be initiated for at least 5 days after last abiraterone + prednisone/prednisolone dose, and subsequent systemic cancer therapy not be initiated for at least 30 days after last radium‑223 administration (data are limited).
- Concomitant chemotherapy: may increase bone marrow suppression (additive effect). Safety of concomitant chemotherapy with radium‑223 has not been established.
- Concomitant systemic cancer therapies other than LHRH analogues: safety/efficacy not established; increased risk of mortality and fractures possible; combination not recommended.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - **Not to be used in women who are or may be pregnant.**
  - Australian pregnancy category: **Category X** (radionuclide therapeutic agent; not for use in women who are or may be pregnant).

- Breastfeeding:
  - **Not to be used in women who are breastfeeding.**
- Fertility:
  - No human fertility studies available. Radiation exposure may affect spermatogenesis and fertility. Men who are sexually active should use condoms, and female partners of reproductive potential should use highly effective contraception during and for up to 6 months after completing treatment.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- **radium‑223 dichloride** is an alpha particle‑emitting radiopharmaceutical. The Ra‑223 ion mimics calcium and selectively localises to bone, particularly areas of increased bone turnover associated with bone metastases, by forming complexes with hydroxyapatite.
- The emitted high linear energy transfer (LET) alpha particles induce dense ionisation tracks and high rates of double‑strand DNA breaks in adjacent tumour cells and elements of the bone microenvironment (osteoblasts/osteoclasts), producing a potent, localized antitumour effect.
- Alpha particle range from Ra‑223 is less than 100 micrometres (approximately less than 10 cell diameters), limiting damage to adjacent normal tissues.

### Pharmacokinetics / Dosimetry
- Radium‑223 physical half‑life: 11.4 days.
- Specific activity: ~1.9 MBq/ng Ra‑223 (as reported).
- Biodistribution: preferential uptake in bone and bone metastases; little uptake in most soft tissues. Calculated absorbed doses (mGy/MBq) from clinical biodistribution data are provided below.

- Effects on biochemical markers: Compared with placebo, treatment decreased biomarkers of bone turnover (bone formation markers: bone alkaline phosphatase, total ALP, PINP; bone resorption markers: S‑CTX‑I and ICTP) in a phase II study.

## 12. Clinical Trials

### ALSYMPCA (pivotal trial)
- Design: Double‑blind, randomized, multiple‑dose, phase III, multicentre trial in patients with castration‑resistant prostate cancer and symptomatic bone metastases. Patients with visceral metastases or lymphadenopathy > 3 cm were excluded.
- Primary endpoint: Overall survival (OS).
- Safety data: Approximately 600 patients received radium‑223 in the phase III programme. Most common adverse reactions included nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting and thrombocytopenia. Major haematological toxicities observed included thrombocytopenia and neutropenia.

### ERA‑223 (combination trial)
- Design: Randomised, placebo‑controlled, multicentre phase III trial assessing concurrent initiation of radium‑223 and abiraterone acetate plus prednisone/prednisolone in chemotherapy‑naïve patients with asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic CRPC with bone metastases.
- Outcome: The study was unblinded early on recommendation of an Independent Data Monitoring Committee due to safety concerns.
  - Findings at primary analysis: Increased incidence of fractures (28.6% vs 11.4%) and increased deaths (38.5% vs 35.5%) among patients receiving radium‑223 in combination with abiraterone acetate plus prednisone/prednisolone compared with placebo plus abiraterone/prednisone.
  - Concomitant use of bone‑protecting agents (bisphosphonates or denosumab) reduced fracture incidence in both arms.
- Clinical implications: Concurrent use of radium‑223 with abiraterone acetate plus prednisone/prednisolone is contraindicated. The safety of combining radium‑223 with other systemic cancer therapies (other than LHRH analogues) has not been established and may increase risk.

## 13. Overdose
- No specific antidote for radium‑223.
- Management: general supportive measures and close monitoring for hematologic and gastrointestinal toxicity; treat as radioactive exposure per institutional and national radiation safety procedures.
- Single doses up to 276 kBq/kg were evaluated in a phase I study without dose‑limiting toxicities.

## 14. Handling, Use and Radiation Protection
- Administration and handling must be performed by authorised personnel experienced in radiopharmaceuticals and radiation safety.
- Use appropriate aseptic technique and radiation protection measures (ALARA: minimise time, maximise distance, use shielding where appropriate).
- Monitor for contamination and measure radioactivity using instruments capable of detecting gamma emissions from Ra‑223 and daughters.
- Dispose of unused product and contaminated materials as radioactive waste according to local regulations and codes of practice.
