# Editorial Product Information: Riluzole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **riluzole** for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, regulatory requirements, and pharmacological profiles.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Riluzole (a benzothiazole derivative).
- **Chemical Name:** 2-amino-6-trifluoromethoxybenzothiazole.
- **Brand Names:** Pharmacor Riluzole [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N07XX02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 50 mg film-coated tablets.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS):** Indicated for the treatment of patients with ALS to extend survival or the time to mechanical ventilation [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 100 mg daily, administered as 50 mg every 12 hours.
- **Dose Escalation:** No significant increase in clinical benefit has been demonstrated with doses higher than 100 mg/day [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets should be taken at approximately the same time each day.
    - **Food Effect:** Riluzole should not be taken with a high-fat meal, as the rate and extent of absorption are reduced (Cmax decreases by 44%, AUC by 17%) [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources; however, standard practice suggests skipping the missed dose and returning to the regular schedule.
- **Titration:** No specific titration schedule is required.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of severe hypersensitivity to riluzole or any of the tablet excipients [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Baseline hepatic disease or impairment where transaminases are greater than 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Use is contraindicated in women who are pregnant or breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Riluzole is associated with a high incidence of serum transaminase elevations. 
    - **Monitoring:** ALT levels should be measured monthly for the first 3 months of treatment, every 3 months for the remainder of the first year, and periodically thereafter.
    - **Discontinuation:** Treatment must be discontinued if ALT levels increase to 5 times the ULN. Rechallenge is not recommended [Australian].
- **Neutropenia:** Cases of marked neutropenia (ANC <500/mm³) have been reported, typically within the first 2 months of treatment. Patients must be advised to report any febrile illness immediately [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** ILD has been reported, sometimes severely. If respiratory symptoms (dry cough, dyspnoea) develop, treatment should be suspended pending radiological investigation (e.g., chest X-ray for bilateral diffuse lung opacities) [Australian].
- **Driving/Machinery:** Patients should be cautioned regarding the potential for dizziness, vertigo, or somnolence [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequent side effects reported in clinical trials were asthenia, nausea, and elevations in liver function tests.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Asthenia, nausea, increased ALT, dysphagia, bronchitis, respiratory disorder [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, vomiting, tachycardia, dizziness, somnolence, circumoral paraesthesia, hypertension, pruritus [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pancreatitis, anaemia, anaphylactoid reaction, angioedema, interstitial lung disease [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Neutropenia, hepatitis, jaundice, exfoliative dermatitis, arrhythmia [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** Agents that inhibit CYP1A2 (e.g., caffeine, diclofenac, diazepam, fluvoxamine, quinolones, theophylline) may decrease riluzole clearance, increasing the risk of toxicity [Australian].
    - **CYP1A2 Inducers:** Agents that induce CYP1A2 (e.g., cigarette smoke, rifampicin, omeprazole, charcoal-broiled food) may increase the rate of riluzole elimination [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** 
    - Animal models suggest riluzole may potentiate the hypnotic effects of chlorpromazine and hexobarbitone [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Transfer of riluzole across the placenta has been detected in animal models. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in humans. Riluzole must not be used in pregnant women [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Riluzole and its metabolites are excreted in the milk of lactating rats. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed impaired fertility at doses approximately 13 times the human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children or adolescents have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No clinically significant differences in pharmacokinetics have been observed between elderly and young subjects; no dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Riluzole should be used with caution. While single-dose studies showed no significant PK changes in moderate/severe renal insufficiency, multiple-dose studies have not been performed [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with baseline transaminases >3x ULN. In mild chronic liver insufficiency, AUC increases by 1.7-fold; in moderate insufficiency, AUC increases by 3-fold [Australian].
- **Race:** Clearance in native Japanese subjects may be 50% lower compared to Caucasians, potentially due to lower metabolic capacity (oxidative or conjugative), though no specific dose reduction is currently recommended in the Australian PI [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The exact mechanism in ALS is not fully elucidated. It is proposed to involve the inactivation of voltage-dependent sodium channels and the inhibition of glutamatergic neurotransmission, protecting motor neurons from excitotoxicity [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; absolute bioavailability is approximately 60%.
    - **Distribution:** Extensively distributed (Vd ≈ 3.4 L/kg); 97% protein bound (mainly to albumin and lipoproteins); crosses the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP1A2-mediated hydroxylation and glucuronidation.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is 9–15 hours; 90% excreted in urine (mostly as metabolites, only 2% as unchanged drug) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trial 1 (n=155):** Riluzole 100 mg/day significantly prolonged survival compared to placebo. Median survival was 17.7 months vs 14.9 months.
- **Pivotal Trial 2 (Dose-ranging, n=959):** Compared 50, 100, and 200 mg/day vs placebo. Riluzole 100 mg/day showed significantly longer survival (median 16.5 months vs 13.5 months for placebo). The 50 mg dose was not statistically significant, and the 200 mg dose offered no additional benefit over 100 mg [Australian].
- **Japanese Study:** A smaller study (n=204) did not show a significant difference in tracheostomy-free survival, though this may have been due to low statistical power [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from light.
- **Safety:** Keep out of reach of children [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Prescription Only Medicine; Poison Schedule 4 (S4).
