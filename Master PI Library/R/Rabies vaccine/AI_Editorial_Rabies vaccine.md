# Editorial Product Information: Rabies Vaccine (Purified Chick Embryo Cell)

## Editorial Note
- This harmonised editorial document was prepared using Product Information (PI) sources from the following jurisdictions: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a single, coherent, and clinically accurate overview for healthcare professionals by synthesising regulatory data.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference. It does not replace the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the local PI for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rabies virus antigen (Strain Flury Low Egg Passage [LEP] inactivated).
- **Brand Name:** **RabAvert** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** J07BB02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile, freeze-dried vaccine for reconstitution. After reconstitution with 1 mL of Sterile Diluent (Water for Injection), each dose contains a potency of at least 2.5 IU of rabies antigen.
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bavarian Nordic A/S [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Preexposure Vaccination:** Indicated for both primary series and booster doses in persons at high risk of exposure (e.g., veterinarians, animal handlers, certain laboratory workers, and international travelers to endemic areas) [United States].
- **Postexposure Prophylaxis (PEP):** Indicated for the prevention of rabies after a recognized or suspected exposure to a rabid animal in all age groups [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing
The individual dose for adults, children, and infants is **1.0 mL**.

### 3.2 Preexposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)
- **Primary Series:** 3 injections of 1.0 mL each administered on Days 0, 7, and either 21 or 28 [United States].
- **Booster Doses:** A single 1.0 mL dose. Frequency depends on risk category:
    - **Continuous Risk (e.g., lab workers):** Serologic testing every 6 months; boost if antibody titer falls below acceptable levels [United States].
    - **Frequent Risk (e.g., veterinarians in enzootic areas):** Serologic testing every 2 years; boost if antibody titer is low [United States].

### 3.3 Postexposure Prophylaxis (PEP)
- **Unvaccinated Individuals:** A series of 5 doses (1.0 mL each) on Days 0, 3, 7, 14, and 28. This must be used in conjunction with **Rabies Immune Globulin (RIG)** administered on Day 0 [United States].
- **Previously Vaccinated Individuals:** 2 doses (1.0 mL each) on Days 0 and 3. **RIG should not be administered** to this group as it may interfere with the anamnestic response [United States].

### 3.4 Administration Instructions
- **Site:** Administer IM into the deltoid muscle in adults and older children. In infants and small children, the anterolateral zone of the thigh is preferred [United States].
- **Avoidance:** **Do not inject into the gluteal region**, as this may result in lower neutralizing antibody titers [United States].
- **Aspiration:** Perform aspiration before injection. If blood appears, discard the dose and repeat the procedure at a different site.
- **Reconstitution:** Inject the entire volume of supplied diluent into the vial at a 45-degree angle. Mix gently to avoid foaming. The resulting suspension should be clear to slightly opalescent and colorless to slightly pink [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Preexposure Prophylaxis:** Contraindicated in persons with a history of anaphylaxis to the vaccine or any of its components [United States].
- **Postexposure Prophylaxis:** In view of the almost invariably fatal outcome of rabies, there are **no contraindications** for postexposure prophylaxis, including pregnancy [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Severe Hypersensitivity:** Patients at risk of severe hypersensitivity to vaccine components (e.g., egg or chicken protein, neomycin, chlortetracycline, amphotericin B) should receive an alternative vaccine if available. If **RabAvert** must be used, manage in a facility capable of treating anaphylaxis [United States].
- **Neuroparalytic Events:** Rare reports of encephalitis, Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS), meningitis, and transient paralysis have been temporally associated with the vaccine [United States].
- **Syncope:** Fainting can occur; procedures should be in place to prevent falling injuries and manage cerebral perfusion.
- **Immunocompromised Patients:** The immune response may be impaired. For PEP in these patients, a serum sample should be tested for rabies antibody on Day 14 to confirm an adequate response [United States].
- **Blood Products:** Contains human albumin; carries an extremely remote risk of viral transmission or Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typically mild and transient. Local reactions occur more frequently than systemic ones.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site pain (34-84%), induration, erythema; Headache (10-52%), Myalgia (38-53%), Malaise (15-20%), Dizziness (10-15%) [United States]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Fever, fatigue, asthenia, lymphadenopathy, arthralgia, nausea, rash [United States]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Temperatures >38°C, swollen lymph nodes, pain in limbs, gastrointestinal complaints [United States]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Severe headache, circulatory reactions, sweating, chills, monoarthritis, paresthesia [United States]. |

- **Post-Marketing Experience:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, edema, neuroparalysis, encephalitis, vertigo, palpitations, and extensive limb swelling have been reported [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Immunosuppressive Agents:** Corticosteroids, radiation therapy, and antimalarials can interfere with the development of active immunity. Preexposure vaccination should be avoided in these patients if possible; if necessary for PEP, antibody titers must be monitored [United States].
- **Rabies Immune Globulin (RIG):** Must not exceed the recommended dose (20 IU/kg) and must never be administered in the same syringe or at the same anatomical site as the vaccine [United States].
- **Other Vaccines:** No data are available regarding concurrent administration with other vaccines [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not specified in available sources (No formal FDA letter category assigned in provided text). Animal reproductive studies have not been conducted. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed. For PEP, the risk of rabies outweighs any potential fetal risk [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if the vaccine is excreted in human milk. Nursing is not a contraindication for PEP [United States].
- **Fertility:** Long-term studies on the impairment of fertility have not been conducted [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are similar to adults. Children and infants receive the same **1.0 mL** dose [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and older to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [United States].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Induces the production of neutralizing antibodies against the rabies virus. Protection is achieved once a titer of ≥0.5 IU/mL (WHO) or a 1:5 dilution (CDC) is reached [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Seroconversion generally occurs within 14 days of initiating the PEP series.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not applicable to vaccines.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Immunogenicity:** In trials across the US, UK, Croatia, and Thailand, 100% of subjects attained protective titers (≥0.5 IU/mL) following the recommended 3-dose preexposure schedule by Day 28.
- **Booster Response:** Significant increases in antibody titers were observed 14 days after a booster dose, even in individuals who previously received a different rabies vaccine (Human Diploid Cell Vaccine) [United States].
- **PEP Efficacy:** In a study of 216 patients, 215 had protective titers by Days 28 to 38 following a 5-dose regimen. No cases of rabies were observed in a follow-up of 203 patients for 10 months [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at **2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F)** [United States].
- **Protection:** Protect from light [United States].
- **Shelf Life:** Use immediately after reconstitution [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [United States].
- **Product Components:** Contains residues of egg protein, bovine gelatin (polygeline), and trace antibiotics (neomycin ≤ 10 mcg; chlortetracycline ≤ 200 ng; amphotericin B ≤ 20 ng) [United States].
