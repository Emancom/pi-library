# Editorial Product Information: Rifapentine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of rifapentine for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the current approved PI in their specific region for prescribing and legal purposes.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rifapentine, a cyclopentyl rifamycin derivative.
- **Chemical Class:** Rifamycin antimycobacterial.
- **ATC Code:** J04AB05.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated tablets, 150 mg [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Macleods Pharmaceuticals Limited [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Active Pulmonary Tuberculosis (TB):** Indicated for the treatment of active pulmonary TB caused by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* in adults and pediatric patients aged 12 years and older. It must be used in combination with other susceptible antituberculosis agents [United States].
- **Latent Tuberculosis Infection (LTBI):** Indicated for the treatment of LTBI in adults and pediatric patients aged 2 years and older who are at high risk of progression to active disease. Must be used in combination with isoniazid [United States].
- **Limitations of Use:** 
    - Should not be used as monotherapy.
    - Not recommended for patients with TB caused by rifamycin-resistant organisms [United States].
    - In HIV-infected patients with active pulmonary TB, rifapentine should not be used in a once-weekly continuation phase due to high failure/relapse rates with rifampin-resistant organisms [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Active Pulmonary Tuberculosis
Treatment consists of a 2-month initial phase followed by a 4-month continuation phase.
- **Initial Phase (2 Months):** 600 mg administered twice weekly as Directly Observed Therapy (DOT). There must be an interval of at least 72 hours between doses. Use in combination with other anti-TB drugs (e.g., isoniazid, ethambutol, pyrazinamide) [United States].
- **Continuation Phase (4 Months):** 600 mg once weekly as DOT in combination with isoniazid or another susceptible anti-TB agent [United States].

### 3.2 Latent Tuberculosis Infection
Administered once weekly for 12 weeks as DOT in combination with isoniazid.
- **Adults and Pediatrics (≥12 years):** Rifapentine dose is weight-based (up to 900 mg maximum) plus isoniazid (15 mg/kg; 900 mg maximum) [United States].
- **Pediatric Patients (2 to 11 years):** Rifapentine dose is weight-based (up to 900 mg maximum) plus isoniazid (25 mg/kg; 900 mg maximum) [United States].

**Weight-Based Weekly Dosing (LTBI):**
| Weight Range | Rifapentine Dose | Number of 150 mg Tablets |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 10–14 kg | 300 mg | 2 |
| 14.1–25 kg | 450 mg | 3 |
| 25.1–32 kg | 600 mg | 4 |
| 32.1–50 kg | 750 mg | 5 |
| >50 kg | 900 mg (max) | 6 |

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
- **Food:** Tablets must be taken with meals. Food increases bioavailability and may reduce gastrointestinal side effects [United States].
- **Difficulty Swallowing:** Tablets may be crushed and mixed with a small amount of semi-solid food for immediate consumption [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to rifapentine or any other rifamycin derivative (e.g., rifampin, rifabutin) [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevations in liver transaminases and liver injury have been reported. Monitor for symptoms (nausea, vomiting, jaundice). For patients with existing liver disease, monitor transaminases every 2 to 4 weeks during therapy [United States].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Discontinue immediately if signs of hypersensitivity (e.g., hypotension, angioedema, bronchospasm, or flu-like symptoms) occur [United States].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** SJS and DRESS have been reported. Discontinue rifapentine at the first appearance of skin rash or mucosal lesions [United States].
- **Paradoxical Drug Reactions:** New or worsening symptoms/radiological signs may occur during treatment despite initial improvement. These are often transient and should not necessarily be interpreted as treatment failure [United States].
- **Drug Interactions:** Rifapentine is a potent inducer of CYP450 enzymes (specifically 3A4 and 2C8/9). This may significantly reduce the efficacy of concomitantly administered drugs [United States].
- **Discoloration of Body Fluids:** May turn urine, sweat, sputum, and tears a red-orange color. Contact lenses or dentures may be permanently stained [United States].
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all systemic antibacterials. Evaluate if diarrhea occurs [United States].
- **Porphyria:** Avoid use in patients with porphyria due to enzyme induction properties similar to rifampin [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Frequency Summary
- **Common (>3%) in Active TB:** Anemia, lymphopenia, neutropenia, leukocytosis, thrombocytosis, hemoptysis, cough, increased sweating, increased transaminases (ALT/AST), back pain, rash, anorexia, arthralgia, headache, and increased blood urea [United States].
- **Common (>3%) in LTBI:** Hypersensitivity reactions [United States].

### 6.2 Serious Adverse Effects
- Hepatotoxicity and liver failure.
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and DRESS.
- CDAD/Pseudomembranous colitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
Rifapentine is a strong inducer of **CYP3A4** and **CYP2C8/9**.
- **Antiretrovirals:** May significantly decrease plasma concentrations of protease inhibitors and certain reverse transcriptase inhibitors. However, once-weekly 900 mg rifapentine does not appear to clinically affect the combination of efavirenz, emtricitabine, and tenofovir [United States].
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** May reduce contraceptive efficacy. Advise patients to use non-hormonal or barrier methods during treatment [United States].
- **Other CYP Substrates:** Dosage adjustments may be required for warfarin, phenytoin, sildenafil, diazepam, digoxin, and various azole antifungals, among others [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: No specific letter category provided in recent labeling; however, animal data suggests potential for fetal harm (cleft palate, aortic arch malformations)]. Use during the final weeks of pregnancy may increase the risk of maternal postpartum hemorrhage and neonatal bleeding; monitoring of prothrombin time and Vitamin K administration may be indicated [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if rifapentine is excreted in human milk. Monitor infants for signs of hepatotoxicity (irritability, jaundice, dark urine) and red-orange discoloration of body fluids [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Active TB: Safety and efficacy not established for patients <12 years [United States].
    - LTBI: Approved for ages 2 years and older [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics in patients ≥65 years are similar to younger adults, but clinical data are limited [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Only 17% is excreted renally. The clinical impact of renal impairment on rifapentine disposition is unknown [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics were similar across various degrees of hepatic impairment in clinical studies [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits DNA-dependent RNA polymerase in susceptible *M. tuberculosis* strains, preventing RNA transcription and protein synthesis [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is not determined; 70% relative to oral solution. Tmax is 5–6 hours. Food (specifically high-fat) increases AUC by up to 50% [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Hydrolyzed by esterases to the active metabolite, 25-desacetyl rifapentine [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (70%); 17% via urine. Half-life is approximately 13 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Active TB:** Trials showed that rifapentine-based regimens (twice weekly in initial phase, once weekly in continuation) had higher relapse rates (up to 12%) compared to daily rifampin-based regimens (7%) in certain populations, particularly those with cavitary lesions or positive sputum at 2 months [United States].
- **LTBI:** A large trial (3RPT/INH) demonstrated that 12 weekly doses of rifapentine + isoniazid was non-inferior to 9 months of daily isoniazid, with higher treatment completion rates [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- Protect from excessive heat and humidity [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** [United States: Prescription Only (Rx-only)].
