# Editorial Product Information: Rucaparib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s current approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rucaparib (as rucaparib camsylate).
- **Chemical Class:** Poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **RUBRACA**.
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 200 mg (blue, round, "C2"), 250 mg (white, diamond, "C25"), and 300 mg (yellow, oval, "C3") [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** pharmaand GmbH (distributed by Summit SD, LLC in the United States).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Ovarian Cancer (Maintenance):** For the maintenance treatment of adult patients with deleterious **BRCA** mutation (germline and/or somatic)-associated recurrent epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer who are in complete or partial response to platinum-based chemotherapy [US].
- **Prostate Cancer:** For the treatment of adult patients with a deleterious **BRCA** mutation (germline and/or somatic)-associated metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) who have previously been treated with androgen receptor-directed therapy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 600 mg (two 300 mg tablets) taken orally twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** May be taken with or without food. Tablets should be swallowed whole. Doses should be spaced approximately 12 hours apart [US].
- **Treatment Duration:** Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
- **Special Requirements for mCRPC:** Patients must concurrently receive a gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) analog or have undergone bilateral orchiectomy [US].
- **Dose Modifications for Adverse Reactions:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 600 mg twice daily.
    - **First reduction:** 500 mg twice daily.
    - **Second reduction:** 400 mg twice daily.
    - **Third reduction:** 300 mg twice daily [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed or if the patient vomits, do not take an additional dose; the next dose should be taken at the scheduled time [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS) / Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML):** MDS and AML, including fatal cases, have been reported. Most cases occurred in patients previously treated with platinum-based chemotherapy or other DNA-damaging agents.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Obtain a baseline complete blood count (CBC) and monitor monthly thereafter. Do not initiate rucaparib until the patient has recovered from prior chemotherapy-induced haematological toxicity (≤ Grade 1) [US]. 
- **Haematological Toxicity:** For prolonged cytopenias (>4 weeks), interrupt treatment and monitor CBC weekly. If levels do not recover to ≤ Grade 1 within 4 weeks, refer to a haematologist for bone marrow analysis and cytogenetics. If MDS/AML is confirmed, discontinue rucaparib [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Based on its mechanism of action, rucaparib can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential prior to initiation [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Analysis
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, constipation, dyspepsia, stomatitis.
    - **General:** Fatigue/asthenia, decreased appetite, dizziness, dysgeusia, peripheral oedema, pyrexia.
    - **Haematological:** Anemia/decreased haemoglobin, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, lymphopenia.
    - **Dermatological:** Rash, photosensitivity reaction, pruritus.
    - **Respiratory:** Dyspnoea, cough, nasopharyngitis.
    - **Investigations:** Increased ALT/AST, increased blood creatinine, increased cholesterol, increased alkaline phosphatase, weight loss.
- **Common (1–10%):** Insomnia, depression, headache, urinary tract infection, peripheral neuropathy, musculoskeletal pain, hypertension, hemorrhage, venous thromboembolism.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- MDS/AML (1.6% in pooled safety populations) [US].
- Serious reactions in mCRPC (TRITON3) included pneumonia (4%), anemia (4%), acute kidney injury (3%), and urinary tract infection (3%). 
- Fatalities (1.5% in TRITON3) were attributed to cardiac failure, myocardial ischemia, and sepsis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Effect of Rucaparib on Other Drugs:**
    - **CYP1A2, CYP3A, CYP2C9, and CYP2C19 Substrates:** Concomitant use increases the systemic exposure of these substrates, potentially increasing toxicity. If use is unavoidable, consider dose reductions for the substrate drug [US].
    - **Warfarin:** Concomitant use may increase INR; monitor frequently [US].
    - **P-gp Substrates:** May increase exposure (e.g., digoxin increased by 1.2-fold) [US].
- **Effect of Other Drugs on Rucaparib:**
    - **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):** No clinically meaningful effect on rucaparib steady-state concentrations [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not lettered per current PLLR]. Rucaparib can cause fetal harm. Animal data showed embryo-fetal death at 0.04 times the human exposure at the 600 mg BID dose [US].
- **Contraception:**
    - **Females:** Use effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after the final dose [US].
    - **Males:** Use effective contraception and do not donate sperm during treatment and for 3 months after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of rucaparib in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in the child, breastfeeding is advised against during treatment and for 2 weeks after the last dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Based on animal studies, rucaparib is not expected to affect reproductive organ morphology, though specific fertility studies have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness observed between patients ≥65 years and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (CLcr 30–89 mL/min):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe/ESRD (CLcr <30 mL/min):** Not studied; no recommendation possible [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Bilirubin ≤ULN and AST >ULN, or Bilirubin 1.0–1.5x ULN):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate (Bilirubin >1.5–3.0x ULN):** Increased AUC by 45%; no dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Bilirubin >3x ULN):** Not studied; no recommendation possible [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibitor of PARP-1, PARP-2, and PARP-3. By inhibiting PARP enzymatic activity and increasing PARP-DNA complex formation (PARP trapping), rucaparib induces DNA damage and cell death, particularly in tumor cells with deficiencies in **BRCA1/2** or other DNA repair genes [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 36%. Median Tmax is 1.9 hours. High-fat meals increase AUC by 38% and delay Tmax by 2.5 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is 2300 L; 70% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP2D6, and to a lesser extent by CYP1A2 and CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life is 26 hours. Total clearance is 44.2 L/h. Excretion is primarily faecal (95%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ARIEL3 (Ovarian Cancer Maintenance):** A randomised, double-blind trial in patients with recurrent ovarian cancer in response to platinum-based chemotherapy. In the **BRCA**-mutated subgroup, rucaparib significantly improved median progression-free survival (PFS) (16.6 months) compared to placebo (5.4 months) (HR 0.23; p < 0.0001) [US].
- **TRITON3 (mCRPC):** A randomised, open-label trial in patients with **BRCA** or **ATM** mutations. Rucaparib significantly improved radiographic PFS in the **BRCA**-mutated subgroup (11.2 months) vs. physician's choice of ARPI or docetaxel (6.4 months) (HR 0.50; p < 0.0001). No benefit was observed in the **ATM**-mutated subgroup [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription only (Rx-only) [US].
