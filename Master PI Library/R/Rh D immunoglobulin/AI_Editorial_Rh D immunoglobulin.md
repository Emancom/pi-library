# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis of the provided Product Information (PI) and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the United States PI was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (A16AA01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism leading to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
  - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
  - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. 
  - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours. 
  - Maximum reported dose: 300 mg/kg.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
  - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
  - Administer as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
  - Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL.

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended.
- **ESRD:** Therapy is often prompted by trough levels below 40–50 µmol/L.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Reactions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks/benefits before re-administration.
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.

### General Precautions
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral formulations in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in clinical trials (incidence ≥5%) in ESRD patients include:

| Body System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, allergic reaction, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression, anxiety |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |

- **Serious/Notable Effects:** Seizures, anaphylaxis, and transient nausea/vomiting.
- **Specific Effects:** Body odor and gastritis have been reported less frequently.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concurrently with levocarnitine. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation. 
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See dosage recommendations for metabolic disorders; specific age-related data beyond general dosing was not provided in the source.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Heavily addressed for ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). Levocarnitine is easily removed by dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating fatty acid oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
  - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Normal plasma free carnitine levels are 40–50 µmol/L.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Bioavailability Study:** In 15 healthy volunteers, absolute bioavailability of oral formulations (tablets/solution) was approximately 15–16%.
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg after dialysis showed trough concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Evaluated 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients over 12 and 24 weeks, demonstrating a dose-proportional increase in trough carnitine levels.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.
