# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not specified in available sources; sourced from standard pharmacy nomenclature).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients presenting with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
  - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
  - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours; doses should never be spaced less than every 6 hours [US].
  - Subsequent daily maintenance is typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
  - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
  - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before administration [US].
- For ESRD patients, the injection is delivered into the venous return line after the dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating therapy [US].
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Monitor pre-dialysis trough levels (normal range 40 to 50 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications for **levocarnitine** injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred—primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase seizure frequency or severity in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation):** The safety of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function has not been established, as metabolites (trimethylamine and TMAO) may accumulate [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), flu syndrome (25–29%), headache (22%), fever (7–12%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%) [US].
- **Digestive/Metabolic:** Diarrhea (10–35%), vomiting (9–21%), hypercalcemia (6–15%) [US].
- **Respiratory/Nervous:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), dizziness (10–18%) [US].

### Common (1%–10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, nausea, gastritis, body odor (transient) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, palpitations, arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation [US].
- **Nervous:** Insomnia, paresthesia, anxiety, depression [US].
- **Other:** Anemia, hyperkalemia, peripheral edema, pruritus [US].

### Postmarketing Experience
- Neurologic: Seizures [US].
- Hypersensitivity: Anaphylaxis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when **levocarnitine** is co-administered with warfarin.
  - **Management:** Monitor INR levels closely after initiating **levocarnitine** or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **US Category:** Not assigned a letter grade in this source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA-based) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US].
  - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
  - Data in humans are limited; however, studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine levels in milk following exogenous administration [US].
  - **Recommendation:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefit of supplementation for the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral therapy should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Levocarnitine** is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria for beta-oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are then cleared from the body [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In 9-week and 24-week trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, IV **levocarnitine** (20 mg/kg) significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Pre-dialysis (trough) concentrations rose from baseline levels of ~20 µmol/L to nearly 200 µmol/L after 8 to 12 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the direct effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in ESRD patients has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion as the formulation lacks preservatives [US].

- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
