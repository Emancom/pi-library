# Editorial Product Information: Radium Ra 223 dichloride

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Radium Ra 223 dichloride (an alpha particle-emitting radioactive therapeutic agent).
- **Brand Name:** Xofigo [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Clear, colorless, isotonic, sterile solution for injection. Supplied in single-dose vials containing 6 mL at a concentration of 1,100 kBq/mL (30 microcurie/mL) at the reference date, with a total radioactivity of 6,600 kBq per vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bayer HealthCare Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Castration-Resistant Prostate Cancer (CRPC):** Indicated for the treatment of patients with CRPC, symptomatic bone metastases, and no known visceral metastatic disease [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 55 kBq (1.49 microcurie) per kg body weight [US].
- **Dosing Schedule:** Administered at 4-week intervals for a total of 6 injections [US]. 
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Administer via slow intravenous injection over 1 minute [US].
    - Flush the intravenous access line or cannula with isotonic saline before and after the injection [US].
- **Volume Calculation:** The volume to be administered must be calculated based on the patient's weight, the radioactivity concentration at the reference date, and a decay correction factor specific to the time of administration [US].
- **Handling Requirements:** 
    - Verify the net patient dose by measurement in a calibrated radioisotope dose calibrator prior to administration [US].
    - Follow universal precautions for radiopharmaceuticals, including the use of gloves and barrier gowns [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None:** No contraindications are listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:**
    - May cause pancytopenia, leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia. 
    - Deaths related to vascular hemorrhage in association with myelosuppression have occurred [US].
    - **Monitoring:** Perform hematologic evaluation at baseline and prior to every dose. 
    - **Thresholds:** Before the first dose, Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) must be ≥1.5 x 10⁹/L, platelets ≥100 x 10⁹/L, and hemoglobin ≥10 g/dL. Before subsequent doses, ANC must be ≥1 x 10⁹/L and platelets ≥50 x 10⁹/L [US].
    - **Management:** Discontinue treatment if hematologic values do not recover within 6 to 8 weeks after the last dose despite supportive care [US].
- **Combination with Abiraterone:** Use in combination with abiraterone acetate plus prednisone/prednisolone is not recommended outside of clinical trials due to observed increases in fractures and mortality [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Radium Ra 223 can cause fetal harm. Males with female partners of reproductive potential must use effective contraception [US].
- **Secondary Malignant Neoplasms:** Due to cumulative radiation exposure and mechanism of action, there is a theoretical risk of osteosarcoma or other secondary malignancies [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - Gastrointestinal: Nausea (36%), Diarrhea (25%), Vomiting (19%) [US].
    - General: Peripheral edema (13%) [US].
    - Hematologic (Laboratory abnormalities): Anemia (93%), Lymphocytopenia (72%), Leukopenia (35%), Thrombocytopenia (31%), Neutropenia (18%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Renal/Urinary: Renal failure and impairment (3%) [US].
    - General: Dehydration (3%), Injection site reactions (1%), Pancytopenia (2%) [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Bone marrow failure, pancytopenia, and severe myelosuppression leading to infection or hemorrhage [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Formal Studies:** No formal clinical drug interaction studies have been conducted [US].
- **Subgroup Analyses:** The concurrent use of bisphosphonates or calcium channel blockers did not appear to affect the safety or efficacy of the drug in randomized trials [US].
- **Concomitant Chemotherapy:** Safety and efficacy with concomitant chemotherapy are not established. Use is not recommended due to the potential for additive myelosuppression [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not indicated for use in females. Based on its mechanism of action as a radioactive therapeutic agent, it can cause fetal harm. No human or animal data are available regarding use in pregnancy [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of the drug in human milk, its effects on the breastfed child, or milk production [US].
- **Fertility:** Infertility in males may occur due to radiation effects on spermatogenesis.
- **Contraception:** Male patients must use condoms, and their female partners of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after the final dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients below 18 years of age. Animal studies (rats) showed significant bone and tooth toxicity, including depletion of osteocytes and disruption of growth plates [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients ≥65 years. No overall differences in efficacy or safety were observed compared to younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is needed for mild (CrCl 60-89 mL/min) or moderate (CrCl 30-59 mL/min) renal impairment. No recommendation can be made for severe impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min) due to limited data [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is needed for mild hepatic impairment. No recommendation is available for moderate or severe hepatic impairment due to lack of clinical data [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Radium-223 is a calcium-mimetic that targets bone mineral (hydroxyapatite) at areas of high bone turnover (metastases). It emits high-energy alpha particles with a short range (<100 micrometers), inducing double-strand DNA breaks in adjacent cells (tumor, osteoblasts, osteoclasts), leading to an anti-tumor effect while limiting damage to surrounding healthy tissue [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly cleared from blood; 15 minutes post-injection, 20% remains in blood, decreasing to <1% by 24 hours. Primary distribution is to bone and intestine [US].
    - **Metabolism:** It is a radioactive isotope that decays; it is not metabolically transformed [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily via feces (cumulative ~63% at 7 days). Urinary excretion is minimal (~2% at 48 hours) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Favors markers of bone turnover (e.g., bone alkaline phosphatase, PINP) compared to placebo [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ALSYMPCA Trial:** A double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled Phase 3 study in 809 patients with CRPC and bone metastases.
    - **Primary Outcome:** Overall Survival (OS).
    - **Results:** Median OS was 14.9 months for the Radium-223 group vs. 11.3 months for placebo (HR 0.695; 95% CI: 0.581, 0.832) [US].
    - **Secondary Outcome:** Delay in time to first symptomatic skeletal event (SSE), favoring the treatment arm [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at room temperature, below 40°C (104°F) [US].
- **Shielding:** Store in the original container or with equivalent radiation shielding [US].
- **Disposal:** Any unused product or radioactive waste must be disposed of in accordance with local regulations [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Licensing:** Approved for use by persons under license by the Nuclear Regulatory Commission or relevant regulatory authorities for radioactive materials [US].
