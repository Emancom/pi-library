# Editorial Product Information: Rasagiline

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of rasagiline for use by healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, safety warnings, and pharmacological profiles.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rasagiline (as mesilate).
- **Chemical Class:** Propargylamine derivative; potent, irreversible, selective monoamine oxidase type B (MAO-B) inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** Akm Rasagiline [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N04BD02 [Not explicitly stated in source; confirmed via standard pharmacology references].
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 1 mg tablets (white to off-white, round, flat bevelled, debossed with "RAS" and "1").
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Idiopathic Parkinson’s Disease (PD):** Indicated for symptomatic treatment as monotherapy or as adjunct therapy to dopamine agonists or levodopa [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** 1 mg once daily.
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food.
- **Adjunct Therapy with Levodopa:** A reduction in levodopa dosage may be considered based on individual patient response when initiating rasagiline.
- **Titration:** Not required; clinical trials demonstrated no additional efficacy at doses higher than 1 mg daily.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to rasagiline or any tablet excipients.
- **Concomitant Medications:**
    - **Other MAO Inhibitors:** Risk of non-selective MAO inhibition leading to hypertensive crisis. A 14-day washout is required between discontinuing rasagiline and starting another MAOI.
    - **Analgesics:** Pethidine (14-day washout required), tramadol, tapentadol, methadone, and dextropropoxyphene.
    - **Antitussives:** Dextromethorphan.
    - **Herbal Products:** St John's wort.
    - **CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors such as ciprofloxacin [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with any degree of hepatic insufficiency [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Reported with concomitant use of antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, tricyclics, tetracyclics) or other MAOIs. Symptoms include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular abnormalities.
- **Hypertensive Crisis:** While rasagiline is MAO-B selective at 1 mg, selectivity decreases at higher doses. Very rare cases of "cheese reaction" (hypertensive crisis after tyramine ingestion) have occurred post-marketing. Patients should avoid foods very high in tyramine (e.g., aged cheeses).
- **Dopaminergic Effects:**
    - **Dyskinesia:** When used as an adjunct to levodopa, rasagiline may exacerbate pre-existing dyskinesia.
    - **Hallucinations and Psychotic Behaviour:** Patients should be monitored for hallucinations; risk is higher when used as adjunct therapy.
- **Impulse Control Disorders:** Possible development of pathological gambling, increased libido, hypersexuality, or compulsive spending/eating.
- **Sleep Disorders:** Excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) and sudden sleep onset (SOS) episodes may occur, particularly with other dopaminergic medications. 
- **Postural Hypotension:** Most frequent in the first two months of treatment; patients with PD are particularly vulnerable to falls.
- **Melanoma:** Epidemiological data suggest PD patients have a higher risk of melanoma. Periodic skin examinations are recommended for all patients treated with rasagiline.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect profiles vary by treatment regimen (monotherapy vs. adjunct).

### Monotherapy (Incidence ≥ 1% and > Placebo)
| System Organ Class | Adverse Event | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, Depression | Common (1-10%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Dyspepsia | Common (1-10%) |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Arthralgia, Arthritis | Common (1-10%) |
| **General** | Headache (14.1%), Flu syndrome, Fever | Very Common/Common |

### Adjunct Therapy
- **To Dopamine Agonists:** Peripheral oedema (7%), fall (6%), nausea (6%), dizziness (7%), insomnia (4%), and cough (4%).
- **To Levodopa:** Dyskinesia (10.3%), accidental injury/falls (8.2%), nausea (6.8%), and postural hypotension (4.7%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Hypertensive crisis, serotonin syndrome, melanoma, and rhabdomyolysis (rarely reported, pathogenesis unclear).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (CYP1A2):** Rasagiline is primarily metabolised by CYP1A2. Ciprofloxacin increases rasagiline AUC by 83%. Potent CYP1A2 inhibitors are contraindicated.
- **Serotonergic Agents:** Avoid concomitant use with fluoxetine or fluvoxamine. Wait 5 weeks after stopping fluoxetine before starting rasagiline; wait 14 days after stopping rasagiline before starting fluoxetine/fluvoxamine.
- **Sympathomimetics:** Use caution with decongestants and cold remedies containing sympathomimetics (e.g., ephedrine, pseudoephedrine). One case of hypertension reported with ophthalmic tetrahydrozoline.
- **Entacapone:** Increases rasagiline oral clearance by 28%.
- **Levodopa:** No significant effect on rasagiline clearance.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B3 [Australian]:** Animal studies showed reduced offspring survival and body weights at doses 10x the human exposure. Use in pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Rasagiline inhibits prolactin secretion and may inhibit lactation. It is unknown if rasagiline is excreted in human milk; exercise caution.
- **Fertility:** No impairment of mating or fertility observed in animal models at 30x the human dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and efficacy not established in patients <18 years.
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required. Generally well-tolerated in patients >65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required for moderate renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** **Contraindicated.** Plasma concentrations increase up to 2-fold in mild and 7-fold in moderate hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent, irreversible, selective inhibitor of MAO-B. Prevents the breakdown of striatal dopamine, increasing extracellular dopamine levels.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Selectivity for MAO-B is maintained at the 1 mg dose.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; $T_{max}$ ~0.5 hours. Bioavailability ~36%. High-fat meals decrease $C_{max}$ by 60% but do not significantly affect AUC.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 243 L; protein binding is 88–94%.
    - **Metabolism:** Almost complete hepatic biotransformation via CYP1A2 (N-dealkylation and hydroxylation). Major metabolite is 1-aminoindan (not an MAO-B inhibitor).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via urine (62.6%) and faeces (21.8%). Terminal half-life is 0.6–2 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy (TEMPO):** 26-week trial (n=404). Rasagiline 1 mg showed a significant reduction in total UPDRS score (-4.2 vs. placebo; p<0.0001).
- **Adjunct to Dopamine Agonists (ANDANTE):** 18-week trial (n=321). Significant improvement in total UPDRS score compared to placebo (p=0.012).
- **Adjunct to Levodopa (LARGO and PRESTO):** 18 and 26-week trials. Rasagiline 1 mg significantly reduced "OFF" time (0.78 to 0.94 hours/day) and increased "ON" time without troublesome dyskinesia.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 3 years [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Alu-Alu blisters or HDPE bottles with child-resistant closures.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (S4) [Australian].
