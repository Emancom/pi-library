# Editorial Product Information: Rotigotine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of rotigotine transdermal patches for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian (TGA) source document was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rotigotine (non-ergolinic dopamine agonist).
- **Chemical Class:** Aminotetralin derivative.
- **Brand Name:** **Neupro** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N04BC09.
- **Dosage Form and Strengths:** 
    - Matrix-type transdermal patch in six strengths [Australian]:
        - 1 mg/24 h (5 cm² patch containing 2.25 mg)
        - 2 mg/24 h (10 cm² patch containing 4.5 mg)
        - 3 mg/24 h (15 cm² patch containing 6.75 mg)
        - 4 mg/24 h (20 cm² patch containing 9.0 mg)
        - 6 mg/24 h (30 cm² patch containing 13.5 mg)
        - 8 mg/24 h (40 cm² patch containing 18.0 mg)
- **Route of Administration:** Transdermal.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** UCB Pharma [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Parkinson's Disease:** Indicated for the treatment of idiopathic Parkinson’s disease, either as monotherapy or in combination with levodopa, from early stage through to advanced disease [Australian].
- **Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS):** Symptomatic treatment of moderate to severe idiopathic restless legs syndrome in adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Apply once daily at approximately the same time each day. 
- The patch should remain on the skin for 24 hours, then be replaced with a new patch at a different application site.
- **Application Sites:** Clean, dry, intact skin on the abdomen, thigh, hip, flank, shoulder, or upper arm. 
- **Rotation:** Avoid the same application site for 14 days [Australian].
- **Handling:** Press firmly with the palm for 20–30 seconds. Do not cut patches.

### Parkinson's Disease Dosing
- **Early Stage:** Start at 2 mg/24 h. Increase in weekly increments of 2 mg/24 h to an effective dose. Maximum dose: 8 mg/24 h [Australian].
- **Advanced Stage:** Start at 4 mg/24 h. Increase in weekly increments of 2 mg/24 h. Maximum dose: 16 mg/24 h [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Taper gradually in steps of 2 mg/24 h every other day.

### Restless Legs Syndrome Dosing
- **Dosing:** Start at 1 mg/24 h. Increase in weekly increments of 1 mg/24 h based on response. Maximum dose: 3 mg/24 h [Australian].
- **Review:** Re-evaluate the need for continued treatment every 6 months.
- **Discontinuation:** Taper gradually in steps of 1 mg/24 h every other day.

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is forgotten or the patch detaches, apply a new patch for the remainder of the day [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to rotigotine or any excipients (including sodium metabisulfite).
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) or Cardioversion:** The patch backing contains aluminium; removal is mandatory prior to these procedures to avoid skin burns [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Somnolence and Sudden Sleep Onset:** Patients may fall asleep without warning during activities of daily living. Continuous reassessment for drowsiness is required.
- **Impulse Control Disorders:** Patients may experience intense urges (pathological gambling, hypersexuality, binge eating, compulsive spending). Dose reduction or tapering should be considered.
- **Orthostatic Hypotension:** Monitor blood pressure, particularly during dose titration.
- **Hallucinations and Psychosis:** Rotigotine can cause abnormal thinking and behaviour; use with caution in patients with major psychotic disorders.
- **Augmentation (RLS):** Dopaminergic treatment for RLS may lead to earlier symptom onset in the evening, increased severity, or spread to other limbs [Australian].
- **Application Site Reactions:** Usually mild to moderate. Discontinue if a generalised skin reaction occurs. Avoid direct sunlight on the application site until the skin heals.
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** Contains sodium metabisulfite, which may cause anaphylaxis or asthma in susceptible individuals.
- **Fibrotic Complications:** Cases of retroperitoneal fibrosis, pleural effusion, and valvulopathy have been reported with ergot-derived agonists; while rotigotine is non-ergot, clinicians should monitor for these symptoms [Australian].
- **Dystonic Reactions:** Occasionally reported following initiation or dose increase.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data [Australian]
| Frequency | Adverse Effects (Parkinson's) | Adverse Effects (RLS) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea, vomiting, application site reactions, somnolence, dizziness, headache. | Nausea, application site reactions, asthenic conditions, headache. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hallucinations, insomnia, ICD, dyskinesia, palpitations, orthostatic hypotension, constipation, pruritus, peripheral oedema, falls. | Somnolence, sleep attacks, ICD, insomnia, vomiting, dyspepsia, pruritus, hypertension, increased CPK. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypersensitivity, paranoia, confusional state, blurred vision, atrial fibrillation, hypotension. | Obsessive-compulsive disorder, disorientation, orthostatic hypotension. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Sudden sleep onset, neuroleptic malignant syndrome (associated with abrupt withdrawal), and severe impulse control disorders.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Dopamine Antagonists:** Neuroleptics (e.g., phenothiazines) and metoclopramide may reduce rotigotine efficacy; co-administration should be avoided [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with sedatives, benzodiazepines, antipsychotics, or alcohol.
- **Levodopa:** Rotigotine may potentiate dopaminergic side effects of levodopa (e.g., dyskinesia).
- **CYP Isoforms:** Rotigotine is metabolised by multiple CYP isoforms (including CYP2C19); however, omeprazole (CYP2C19 inhibitor) did not significantly alter rotigotine pharmacokinetics in healthy volunteers [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category: B3]. There are no adequate data in pregnant women. Animal studies show embryofetal toxicity at maternotoxic doses. Rotigotine is not recommended for use during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Rotigotine is expected to inhibit lactation as it decreases prolactin. Animal data indicate excretion in breast milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data show disrupted implantation in females due to hypoprolactinaemia (clinical relevance in humans is low) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended due to lack of safety and efficacy data.
- **Elderly:** No dose adjustment required; titration is based on clinical tolerability [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required in mild to severe impairment, including dialysis. Unexpected accumulation may occur if renal function acutely worsens.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. Not investigated in severe hepatic impairment; caution is advised [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Non-ergolinic dopamine agonist with high affinity for D1, D2, D3, D4, and D5 receptors, with highest affinity for D3. It also acts as an alpha-2B antagonist and 5HT1A agonist [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Elicits beneficial effects via activation of D3, D2, and D1 receptors in the caudate-putamen.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~37%. Steady state reached in 1–2 days.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is ~92%.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via N-dealkylation and conjugation (sulfate and glucuronide).
    - **Elimination:** ~71% excreted in urine, ~23% in faeces. Initial half-life is 2.4–3 hours; overall elimination half-life is 5–7 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Parkinson’s Disease:** Pivotal Phase III trials demonstrated significant improvements in UPDRS scores (Parts II + III) for early-stage patients and significant reduction in "off" time for advanced-stage patients compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Restless Legs Syndrome:** Pivotal 6-month trials showed clinically relevant improvements in International RLS Study Group Rating Scale (IRLS) scores and Clinical Global Impression (CGI) scores for doses up to 3 mg/24 h. Sleep lab trials demonstrated a significant reduction in the Periodic Limb Movement Index (PLMI) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Shelf Life:** 30 months [Australian].
- **Disposal:** Used patches still contain active substance. Fold the patch in half (adhesive side inwards) and discard safely out of reach of children [Australian].
