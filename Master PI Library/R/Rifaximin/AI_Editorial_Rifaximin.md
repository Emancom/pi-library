# Editorial Product Information: Rifaximin

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of rifaximin, synthesised from official product information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference purposes. It does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rifaximin (a non-aminoglycoside semi-synthetic, non-systemic antibiotic derived from rifamycin SV).
- **Brand Name(s):** Xifaxan [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 550 mg film-coated tablets (pink, oval, biconvex, marked "RX" on one side) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Norgine Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hepatic Encephalopathy (HE):** Indicated for the prevention of the recurrence of hepatic encephalopathy in cases where other treatments have either failed or are contraindicated [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** One 550 mg tablet taken orally twice daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** May be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Concomitant Therapy:** In clinical trials, the majority of patients (91%) were using lactulose concomitantly with rifaximin [Australian].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to rifaximin, other rifamycin antimicrobial agents, or any excipients in the formulation [Australian]. 
    - *Note:* Reported hypersensitivity reactions include anaphylaxis, angioneurotic oedema, and exfoliative dermatitis.
- Cases of intestinal obstruction [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Cases of Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) have been reported, particularly in patients with cirrhosis. Treatment should be discontinued immediately at the first sign of a severe cutaneous reaction or other hypersensitivity symptoms [Australian].
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhoea (CDAD):** Like most antibacterial agents, rifaximin use may result in CDAD, ranging from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis. If CDAD is suspected or confirmed, rifaximin or other antibiotics not directed against *C. difficile* may need to be discontinued [Australian].
- **Development of Resistant Bacteria:** Long-term exposure may increase the risk of developing resistant strains, including *Staphylococcus aureus*. Strains resistant to rifaximin are likely to show cross-resistance to rifampicin. Use is not recommended in patients at low risk of HE recurrence [Australian].
- **Co-administration with Warfarin:** Both increases and decreases in International Normalised Ratio (INR), occasionally leading to bleeding, have been reported. Monitor INR closely when starting or stopping rifaximin in patients on oral anticoagulants [Australian].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Inhibitors:** Caution is advised when co-administering with potent P-gp inhibitors such as ciclosporin [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450 Enzymes:** Rifaximin does not significantly inhibit CYP1A2, 2A6, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, or 3A4 *in vitro*. However, in patients with hepatic impairment, increased systemic exposure might lead to a reduction in the exposure of concomitant CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., warfarin, oral contraceptives, antiarrhythmics) [Australian].
- **Transporter Systems:** Rifaximin is a substrate of P-gp and CYP3A4. Co-administration with a single 600 mg dose of ciclosporin (a potent P-gp inhibitor) resulted in an 83-fold increase in Cmax and a 124-fold increase in AUC of rifaximin [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Significant fluctuations in INR have been observed. Dose adjustments for oral anticoagulants may be required [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B1 [Australian]:** Oral doses in rats and rabbits showed no evidence of teratogenicity. Systemic exposure in animal studies was slightly higher than healthy humans but lower than in patients with hepatic impairment. Use should be avoided during pregnancy as a precautionary measure.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if rifaximin or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. A decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the benefit of breastfeeding for the infant against the benefit of therapy for the mother [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at doses up to 2.5 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials included approximately 20% of patients aged ≥65 years. No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed compared to younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinical data are available; however, systemic absorption is naturally low [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Rifaximin systemic exposure is significantly increased in patients with hepatic impairment (10- to 13-fold higher AUC in Child-Pugh A and B). Use with caution in patients with severe (Child-Pugh C) hepatic impairment (MELD scores >25) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Rifaximin binds to the beta-subunit of bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, inhibiting bacterial RNA synthesis. It has a broad spectrum of activity against Gram-positive, Gram-negative, aerobic, and anaerobic bacteria [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In HE, rifaximin is thought to reduce the ammonia-producing enteric flora [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed (<1%). Peak plasma concentrations (Cmax) are reached in approximately 1 hour. A high-fat breakfast slightly increases absorption, but this is not clinically relevant [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Moderately bound to plasma proteins (approx. 62–67%) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by unknown enzymes; minimal renal excretion [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Almost exclusively excreted in faeces (96.6% as unchanged drug) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study:** A 6-month, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial evaluated rifaximin 550 mg BID in 299 adults in remission from HE (Conn score 0 or 1).
- **Outcomes:** Rifaximin significantly reduced the risk of HE breakthrough by 58% (p < 0.0001) compared to placebo. Breakthrough episodes occurred in 22% of the rifaximin group vs. 46% of the placebo group.
- **Hospitalisation:** HE-related hospitalisations were reduced by 50% in the rifaximin-treated group (14% vs. 23% in placebo) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Packaged in PVC/PE/PVDC/Aluminium blisters.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
