# Editorial Product Information: Rimegepant

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised, clinically focused overview of rimegepant for healthcare professionals, synthesising data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rimegepant (as sulfate), a calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **NURTEC ODT** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N02CD06.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Orally disintegrating tablet (ODT) containing 75 mg of rimegepant.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral (sublingual or supralingual).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Treatment:** Acute treatment of migraine (with or without aura) in adults [Australian].
- **Prophylactic Treatment:** Prophylaxis of episodic migraine in adults who experience at least four migraine attacks per month [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Acute Treatment:** 75 mg as needed, limited to once per 24-hour period.
- **Prophylaxis:** 75 mg administered every other day.
- **Maximum Dose:** The maximum dose in any 24-hour period is 75 mg, regardless of whether the drug is used for acute treatment, prophylaxis, or both [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- The ODT should be placed on or under the tongue. It will disintegrate without the need for additional liquid.
- Patients should be advised to use dry hands when handling the blister.
- The medication may be taken with or without food, though high-fat meals may delay absorption (Tmax) and reduce overall exposure (AUC) by approximately 32% to 38% [Australian].

### Concomitant Dosing Restrictions
- **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Avoid a second dose of rimegepant within 48 hours.
- **Strong P-gp Inhibitors:** Avoid a second dose of rimegepant within 48 hours.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to rimegepant or any excipients (including gelatin, mannitol, and sucralose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis and dyspnoea, have occurred in <1% of patients. Clinicians should note that these reactions can be delayed, occurring several days after administration. If a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately.
- **Medication Overuse Headache (MOH):** While daily use up to once per day has not been shown to cause MOH in trials, overuse of any acute migraine treatment can exacerbate headache frequency. Monitor patients for suspected MOH if headaches become daily or more frequent.
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Trials generally excluded patients with unstable cardiovascular disease or uncontrolled hypertension/diabetes. Use with caution in these populations [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Rimegepant is subject to additional monitoring in certain regions to identify new safety information quickly [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate in severity.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea (1.2% in acute; 1.4–2.7% in prophylaxis) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypersensitivity (including rash and dyspnoea), Anaphylaxis [Acute treatment] |
| **Not Known** | Anaphylaxis, Hypersensitivity [Prophylaxis treatment] |

*Note: In prophylaxis trials, other reported effects included nasopharyngitis (3.5%) and urinary tract infection (2.4%), which occurred at rates similar to placebo.*

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole, clarithromycin) significantly increase rimegepant exposure (AUC increased 4-fold); concomitant use is **not recommended**. Moderate inhibitors (e.g., fluconazole) increase AUC by 1.8-fold; avoid another rimegepant dose within 48 hours.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong (e.g., rifampicin, St John’s wort) or moderate (e.g., modafinil) inducers significantly reduce rimegepant exposure (rifampicin reduces AUC by 80%); concomitant use is **not recommended**.
- **P-gp Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ciclosporin, verapamil) increase exposure; avoid another rimegepant dose within 48 hours.
- **Transporters:** Rimegepant is an inhibitor of OATP1B3, OCT2, and MATE1 *in vitro*. However, clinical studies with metformin (a MATE1 substrate) showed no significant interaction.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- No clinically significant differences in blood pressure were observed when rimegepant was co-administered with subcutaneous sumatriptan [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1.** 
    - Data in pregnant women are limited. Animal studies at high exposures (>40x human AUC) did not show malformations or embryofetal death. As a precaution, avoid use during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** A lactation study (n=12) showed a low milk-to-plasma ratio (0.20) and a relative infant dose (RID) of <1%. Breastfeeding may be considered based on the mother’s clinical need and potential infant risks.
- **Fertility:** No impact on human fertility is known. Animal studies showed decreased fertility only at maternally toxic doses (>80x human exposure).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years of age have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data in patients ≥65 years. No dose adjustment is required as pharmacokinetics are not significantly altered by age [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild, moderate, or severe impairment. Use in **end-stage renal disease (CLcr <15 mL/min)** should be **avoided** [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B). Use in **severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C)** should be **avoided** due to significantly higher plasma concentrations [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Rimegepant is a high-affinity antagonist at the human CGRP receptor, blocking the functional activity of the CGRP neuropeptide, which is elevated during migraine attacks and linked to pain transmission.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is 1.5 hours. Absolute bioavailability is approximately 64%.
    - **Distribution:** 96% protein bound; Vd is 120 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4 and, to a lesser extent, CYP2C9. No major metabolites (>10%) are detected.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is approximately 11 hours. Excretion is primarily via faeces (78%) and urine (24%), with unchanged drug being the major component.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Acute Treatment (Studies 1-3):** Three randomised, placebo-controlled trials demonstrated that a single 75 mg dose was superior to placebo for pain freedom and freedom from the most bothersome symptom (MBS) at 2 hours post-dose. Photophobia and phonophobia incidence were significantly reduced.
- **Prophylaxis (Study 4):** A double-blind trial of patients with 4–18 migraine attacks per month showed that rimegepant 75 mg every other day significantly reduced Mean Monthly Migraine Days (MMDs) by 4.3 days compared to 3.5 days for placebo. 49.1% of patients achieved a ≥50% reduction in moderate-to-severe MMDs.
- **Long-term Safety:** Open-label extensions up to one year showed sustained efficacy and a safety profile consistent with short-term trials [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Keep in the original blister packaging until use to protect the ODT from moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
