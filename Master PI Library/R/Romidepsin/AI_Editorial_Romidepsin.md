# Editorial Product Information: Romidepsin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **romidepsin** for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory guidance into a single clinically relevant reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical practice in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Romidepsin (a histone deacetylase [HDAC] inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** Istodax [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XX39.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Powder for injection; 10 mg lyophilised powder in a single-use vial, supplied with a 2.2 mL solvent vial (80% propylene glycol and 20% anhydrous ethanol).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bristol-Myers Squibb Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Peripheral T-cell Lymphoma (PTCL):** Indicated for the treatment of patients with PTCL who have received at least one prior systemic therapy [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** 14 mg/m² administered intravenously over a 4-hour period on Days 1, 8, and 15 of a 28-day cycle.
- **Cycle Duration:** Cycles should be repeated every 28 days as long as the patient continues to benefit and tolerates therapy.
- **Pre-administration Requirements:** Serum potassium and magnesium levels must be within the normal range prior to every administration.
- **Dose Adjustments for Toxicity:**
    - **Haematological:** Delay dose if Grade 3/4 neutropenia or thrombocytopenia occurs. Resume at 14 mg/m² once ANC ≥ 1.5 x 10⁹/L and platelets ≥ 75 x 10⁹/L. If Grade 4 febrile neutropenia or thrombocytopenia requiring transfusion occurs, delay until recovery to Grade 1 or baseline, then permanently reduce dose to 10 mg/m².
    - **Non-haematological (except Alopecia):** Delay dose for Grade 2/3 toxicity until recovery to Grade 1/baseline. Resume at 14 mg/m². If Grade 3 recurs or Grade 4 occurs, delay until recovery, then permanently reduce dose to 10 mg/m². Discontinue if Grade 3/4 toxicity recurs despite dose reduction.
- **Administration Instructions:** Must be reconstituted to 5 mg/mL using the supplied diluent and then further diluted in 500 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection.
- **Missed Doses:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to romidepsin or any of the excipients (povidone, propylene glycol, anhydrous ethanol).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haematological Toxicity:** Treatment frequently causes thrombocytopenia, leukopenia (neutropenia/lymphopenia), and anaemia. Monitor blood counts regularly.
- **Infection:** Serious and fatal infections (e.g., pneumonia, sepsis) have occurred. 
    - **Viral Reactivation:** Reactivation of Hepatitis B (HBV) and Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) has been reported. EBV reactivation has led to liver failure. Consider prophylaxis in patients with a history of HBV.
- **Electrocardiographic (ECG) Changes:** QTc prolongation and T-wave/ST-segment changes have been observed. Perform a baseline ECG. Use with caution in patients with congenital long QT syndrome, significant cardiovascular disease, or those taking QT-prolonging medications.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Reported in 2% of patients with Stage III/IV PTCL. Monitor patients with high tumour burden closely.
- **Gastrointestinal Disturbances:** Nausea and vomiting are very common; prophylactic anti-emetic use is recommended.
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of electrolytes (Potassium, Magnesium) and ECGs is required.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following data is based on clinical trials in PTCL (N=131) [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Events (All Grades) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (59%), Asthenia/Fatigue (55%), Thrombocytopenia (41%), Vomiting (39%), Diarrhoea (36%), Neutropenia (30%), Constipation (30%), Anorexia (28%), Anaemia (25%), Dysgeusia (21%), Pyrexia (35%), Cough (18%), Headache (15%), Abdominal pain (14%), Dyspnoea (13%), Leukopenia (12%), Weight decreased (11%), Stomatitis (11%), Chills (11%), Hypokalaemia (11%), Tachycardia (10%), Oedema peripheral (10%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Decreased appetite (9%), Muscle spasms (7%), Dyspepsia (5%), Hypomagnesaemia (5%), Rash (5%), Hypotension (5%), Upper respiratory tract infection (5%). |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Infections (20% of patients), pyrexia (8%), pneumonia, sepsis, deep vein thrombosis, and pulmonary embolism.
- **Discontinuation:** Approximately 19% of patients discontinued due to adverse events, most commonly thrombocytopenia and pneumonia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Potential for increased Prothrombin Time (PT) and INR. Monitor closely when co-administered.
- **QT-Prolonging Medicines:** Increased risk of cardiac arrhythmias. Clinical monitoring and periodic ECGs are recommended.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) increase romidepsin exposure by ~25%. Monitor for increased toxicity.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Paradoxically, co-administration with rifampin (a strong inducer) increased romidepsin exposure (AUC) by ~80% and Cmax by ~60%. Avoid use with potent CYP3A4 inducers if possible.
- **Oestrogen-Containing Contraceptives:** Romidepsin may reduce the efficacy of oestrogen-based contraceptives. Use non-hormonal methods (e.g., condoms, IUD).
- **Transporters:** Romidepsin is a P-gp substrate; however, P-gp inhibitors are unlikely to significantly alter its PK. It may inhibit OATP1B1.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Based on its mechanism and animal data, romidepsin can cause foetal harm. 
    - **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for at least 1 month after the final dose. Male patients should use effective contraception and avoid fathering a child for 1 month post-treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** No data is available regarding presence in human milk. Due to potential risks to the infant, a decision must be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or the drug.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies suggest potential for testicular degeneration and ovarian atrophy. Human fertility may be compromised.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustments are required. Age does not significantly influence pharmacokinetics.
- **Renal Impairment:** No formal studies in renal impairment. Population PK suggests no impact of mild-to-moderate impairment. Use with caution in end-stage renal disease (ESRD).
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Bilirubin ≤ULN and AST >ULN; or Bilirubin 1.0–1.5x ULN):** No adjustment (14 mg/m²).
    - **Moderate (Bilirubin >1.5–3x ULN):** Reduce dose to 7 mg/m².
    - **Severe (Bilirubin >3x ULN):** Reduce dose to 5 mg/m².

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor. It causes the accumulation of acetylated histones, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis in cancer cells.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Associated with a delayed, concentration-dependent increase in heart rate (mean max increase of 20 bpm at 6 hours post-infusion).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** 92%–94% protein bound (primarily to alpha-1-acid-glycoprotein).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised primarily by CYP3A4.
    - **Excretion:** Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 3.7 hours. Clearance is roughly 8.4 L/hr.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study GPI-06-0002:** A multicenter, single-arm trial of 131 patients with PTCL who failed at least one prior therapy.
    - **Outcomes:** Objective Response Rate (ORR) was 26% (IRC-assessed), with a Complete Response (CR/CRu) rate of 15%.
    - **Response Duration:** Median time to response was 1.8 months. Median duration of response was not reached for CR in some assessments but was at least 12 months for several responders.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store below 25°C. Keep vials in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Use as soon as possible. If storage is necessary, the diluted solution in saline can be held at 2–8°C for up to 24 hours.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
