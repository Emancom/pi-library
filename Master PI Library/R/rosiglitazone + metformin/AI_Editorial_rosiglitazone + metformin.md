# Editorial Product Information: rosiglitazone + metformin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **rosiglitazone + metformin**
- Chemical class:
  - Rosiglitazone: thiazolidinedione (PPAR‑gamma agonist; improves insulin sensitivity) — described as a thiazolidinedione in source materials.
  - Metformin: biguanide (associated with risk of lactic acidosis and gastrointestinal adverse effects) — described as the metformin component in source materials.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- As an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus.
- Indicated for use as dual‑combination therapy in patients already treated with rosiglitazone and metformin given together, or in patients inadequately controlled on either metformin or rosiglitazone monotherapy.
- Use in type 1 diabetes mellitus: **Not indicated** (product is effective only in presence of endogenous insulin).
- Use in combination with insulin: **Not indicated** (initiation of rosiglitazone combinations with insulin is contraindicated; combinations are not approved).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Usual starting total daily dose: 4 mg rosiglitazone / 1000 mg metformin, given as divided doses (twice daily).
- Maximum recommended total daily dose: 8 mg rosiglitazone / 2000 mg metformin.
- Only one rosiglitazone + metformin strength should be prescribed at a time; concurrent use of other metformin‑containing products should be discontinued to avoid accidental overdosing.

### Dosing by prior therapy (examples)
- Patients inadequately controlled on metformin monotherapy:
  - Start rosiglitazone component (total daily rosiglitazone 4 mg) plus the pre‑existing metformin dose.
- Patients inadequately controlled on rosiglitazone monotherapy:
  - Start metformin component (total daily metformin 1000 mg) plus the pre‑existing rosiglitazone dose.
- When switching from separate rosiglitazone and metformin tablets:
  - Start at the same total daily doses already being taken.

(Refer to product-specific conversion tables in local prescribing information for tablet strengths and numbers.)

### Administration instructions
- Give twice daily (due to the metformin component).
- Taking with or immediately after food may reduce gastrointestinal adverse effects associated with metformin.

### Titration schedules
- Dose escalation should be gradual to reduce GI intolerance (largely due to metformin). Typical increments:
  - Increase rosiglitazone by up to 4 mg/day increments.
  - Increase metformin by up to 1000 mg/day increments.
- After increasing metformin: reassess glycaemic control after 1–2 weeks and titrate as needed.
- After increasing rosiglitazone: reassess glycaemic control after 6–8 weeks and titrate as needed.
- Full glycaemic effect for rosiglitazone may not be evident for 6–8 weeks; for metformin 1–2 weeks.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications stated in available sources:
- Hypersensitivity to rosiglitazone, metformin, or any excipient.
- Diabetic ketoacidosis, diabetic pre‑coma.
- Renal failure: creatinine clearance < 60 mL/min (AVANDAMET contraindicated) — creatinine clearance threshold specified.
- Juvenile (type 1) diabetes mellitus that is uncomplicated and well regulated on insulin.
- Diabetes mellitus controlled by diet alone.
- Acute complications of diabetes (metabolic acidosis, coma, infection, gangrene) or during/immediately following surgery where insulin is essential.
- Use of iodinated contrast media — product should be temporarily withheld for radiologic studies with intravascular iodinated contrast and not restarted until renal function is normal.
- Presence of certain conditions associated with increased risk of lactic acidosis (see Warnings): cardiovascular disease including coronary insufficiency, myocardial infarction and hypertension; severe hepatic dysfunction; conditions associated with tissue hypoxia (e.g. gangrene, circulatory shock, acute significant blood loss); pulmonary embolism; pancreatitis; excessive alcohol intake; concomitant use of diuretics.
- Initiation of rosiglitazone combination regimens is contraindicated in patients with NYHA Class III and IV heart failure.
- Use of rosiglitazone combination regimens is contraindicated in patients with NYHA Class I to IV heart failure or a history of cardiac failure.
- Use is contraindicated in patients experiencing an acute coronary syndrome (unstable angina, NSTEMI, STEMI) — initiation contraindicated and discontinuation recommended during the acute phase.
- Concomitant use with insulin: initiation of AVANDAMET in patients on insulin is contraindicated, and insulin initiation in patients on AVANDAMET is not approved.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/high-impact warnings
- **Life‑threatening lactic acidosis can occur due to metformin accumulation; risk increases with renal impairment, advanced age, and high metformin doses (>2000 mg/day).** Lactic acidosis is a medical emergency with high mortality; immediate hospital treatment required.
- **Rosiglitazone has been associated with an increased risk of myocardial ischaemia in pooled short‑term trials.** The product is not recommended for patients with known ischaemic heart disease, particularly those treated with nitrates.
- **Rosiglitazone (as a thiazolidinedione) can cause or exacerbate congestive heart failure (CHF).** Contraindicated in patients with current or prior heart failure; monitor for signs/symptoms of heart failure and discontinue if CHF develops.

### General warnings and precautions
- Product is effective only in presence of insulin; not for type 1 diabetes.
- Premenopausal anovulatory women with insulin resistance (e.g. PCOS) may resume ovulation when insulin sensitivity improves — counsel on pregnancy risk.
- Combination use with insulin increases risk of fluid retention and CHF; concomitant use with insulin is not approved.
- Triple oral therapy (thiazolidinedione + metformin + sulfonylurea) may increase risks of fluid retention, heart failure and hypoglycaemia — triple oral therapy is not recommended.
- Fluid retention and weight gain may occur; monitor for rapid weight gain, dyspnoea, oedema.
- Monitor liver function prior to initiation and periodically thereafter. Do not initiate therapy if baseline ALT > 2.5 × ULN. If ALT increases to > 3 × ULN during therapy, recheck and discontinue if persistent > 3 × ULN.
- Lactic acidosis risk factors include renal impairment, age, dehydration, sepsis, hypoxaemia, excessive alcohol intake, and use of certain concomitant medications (e.g. diuretics). Temporarily stop metformin component in circumstances predisposing to lactic acidosis (e.g. acute illness, dehydration, hypoxaemia, major surgery).
- Eye disorders: very rare post‑marketing reports of new or worsening diabetic macular oedema with decreased visual acuity; some cases improved after drug discontinuation. Be alert for changes in visual acuity.
- Bone effects: increased incidence of fractures reported in female patients in a long‑term monotherapy study (see Adverse Effects). Small decreases in spinal and hip bone mineral density reported.
- Special caution in situations likely to impair renal function (elderly, dehydration, severe infection, shock); determine creatinine clearance before initiating and regularly thereafter.
- Alcohol potentiates metformin effect on lactic metabolism; advise patients against excessive alcohol intake.
- Periodic assessment of renal, hepatic and cardiovascular function recommended during prolonged therapy.
- Patients on continuous metformin therapy should have annual assessment of vitamin B12 because decreased absorption has been reported.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic liver enzymes (ALT).
- Baseline and periodic renal function (creatinine clearance); do not use if CrCl < 60 mL/min.
- Monitor weight and signs/symptoms of heart failure (dyspnoea, oedema).
- Monitor visual symptoms suggestive of macular oedema.
- Annual vitamin B12 measurement for patients on long‑term metformin therapy.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Discontinue metformin component prior to or at time of intravascular iodinated contrast administration; only resume following confirmation of normal renal function.
- Avoid initiation during or close to an acute coronary syndrome; discontinue during acute phase if occurring.
- Avoid co‑administration with insulin (not indicated/approved).

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions (from rosiglitazone component / PK data)
- Rosiglitazone is predominantly metabolised by CYP2C8, with CYP2C9 as a minor pathway.
- Co‑administration with gemfibrozil (CYP2C8 inhibitor) increases rosiglitazone plasma concentrations; dose reduction of rosiglitazone may be necessary.
- Co‑administration with rifampicin (CYP2C8 inducer) decreases rosiglitazone plasma concentrations; monitor glycaemic control and consider diabetes therapy adjustment.
- Rosiglitazone caused moderate inhibition of CYP2C8 and minor inhibition of CYP2C9 in vitro; significant enzyme inhibition at therapeutic doses is considered unlikely.
- Rosiglitazone had no clinically relevant effects on steady‑state pharmacokinetics of digoxin or warfarin and did not affect warfarin anticoagulant activity.
- Ranitidine pre‑treatment did not alter rosiglitazone pharmacokinetics.

### Pharmacodynamic / clinically significant interactions (from metformin component and combined considerations)
- Concomitant administration with other oral antidiabetic agents (metformin, glibenclamide, acarbose) did not show clinically significant PK/PD interactions with rosiglitazone in the data cited.
- Concomitant use of rosiglitazone with insulin increases the risk of oedema and heart failure — combinations with insulin are not approved/contraindicated.
- Triple oral therapy (thiazolidinedione + metformin + sulfonylurea) may increase risk of fluid retention, heart failure and hypoglycaemia — not recommended.
- Iodinated contrast agents may precipitate renal failure; stop metformin component prior to intravascular iodinated contrast administration and only restart when renal function confirmed normal.
- Alcohol potentiates metformin‑associated impairment of lactic metabolism — advise avoidance of excessive alcohol.
- Concomitant use of diuretics may increase risk of lactic acidosis (listed among risk conditions).
- Management notes: monitor glycaemic control and signs/symptoms of fluid overload/heart failure when combining with agents that affect fluid balance or cardiac function; adjust rosiglitazone dose when combined with strong CYP2C8 inhibitors or inducers.

### Regional considerations
- Not specified in available sources for differing regional interaction guidance.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy category/classification: Not specified in available sources.
- Pregnancy: Premenopausal anovulatory women may resume ovulation during rosiglitazone treatment and therefore be at increased risk of pregnancy; counsel accordingly. Formal safety data in pregnancy not provided in available sources.
- Breastfeeding: Not specified in available sources.
- Fertility: Preclinical hormonal imbalance noted; clinical significance in humans not demonstrated — no significant menstrual disorders observed in clinical studies. Detailed fertility data not provided.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- There are no data available on use in children; not recommended.

### Elderly use
- Metformin is renally excreted; use conservative initial and maintenance dosing in elderly due to potential reduced renal function. Monitor renal function and adjust dosing accordingly.

### Renal impairment
- **Contraindicated if creatinine clearance < 60 mL/min** (metformin component). Measure creatinine clearance before initiation and periodically thereafter.
- Special caution in patients likely to develop renal impairment (elderly, dehydration, severe infection, shock).

### Hepatic impairment
- In mild hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh A) no rosiglitazone dose adjustment required per available text, but due to limited experience with both components, use of the combined product in hepatic impairment is **not recommended**. Lactic acidosis risk is a concern in severe hepatic dysfunction.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Rosiglitazone: described in sources as a thiazolidinedione that improves insulin sensitivity (effective only in presence of insulin).
- Metformin: specific mechanism details are not specified in available sources; the source emphasizes metformin's association with lactic acidosis and gastrointestinal effects.

### Pharmacokinetics (selected information from available sources)
- Rosiglitazone:
  - Predominantly metabolised by CYP2C8; CYP2C9 is a minor pathway.
  - Plasma concentrations are increased by CYP2C8 inhibitors (e.g. gemfibrozil) and decreased by CYP2C8 inducers (e.g. rifampicin).
  - No clinically relevant pharmacokinetic effect on digoxin or warfarin reported.
  - In vitro, rosiglitazone moderately inhibits CYP2C8 and minimally inhibits CYP2C9.
- Metformin:
  - Excreted unchanged by the kidney (renal clearance); accumulation can cause lactic acidosis.
  - Metformin plasma levels > 5 µg/mL are commonly found when implicated in lactic acidosis.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Rosiglitazone improves peripheral insulin sensitivity; the full antihyperglycaemic effect on glycaemic control may take several weeks (rosiglitazone: 6–8 weeks; metformin: 1–2 weeks as per dosing guidance).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Retrospective pooled analysis of 42 clinical trials (mean duration 6 months): rosiglitazone was associated with an increased incidence of myocardial ischaemia (2.00% vs 1.53% combined active/placebo control). Higher relative risk observed when rosiglitazone was added to insulin therapy and in patients receiving nitrates.
- ADOPT (A Diabetes Outcome Progression Trial): a 4–6 year study in recently diagnosed type 2 diabetes patients comparing rosiglitazone, glibenclamide and metformin as monotherapies. Patients with heart failure, unstable/severe angina or uncontrolled hypertension were excluded. Incidence rates for myocardial ischaemia were comparable among monotherapy groups.
- Two long‑term studies (mean duration 3–4 years) of rosiglitazone monotherapy or as add‑on second drug: no statistically significant difference in myocardial ischaemic risk compared with active control in the analyses cited; however, pooled short‑term data suggested increased risk in certain subgroups.
- Fracture data: In a 4–6 year monotherapy study, female patients on rosiglitazone had higher fracture incidence (9.3%) compared with metformin (5.1%) and glibenclamide (3.5%). Fractures were predominantly in upper arm, hand and foot.
