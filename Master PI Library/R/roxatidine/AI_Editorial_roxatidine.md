# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesised from the following jurisdiction-specific Product Information (PI) source:
    - **US (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current, locally approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class**: A naturally occurring carrier molecule; the (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium inner salt.
- **Brand Names**: Distributed as a generic injection by various sponsors [US: Baxter Healthcare Corporation].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - **Intravenous Injection**: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- **Route**: Intravenous use only.
- **Method**: Administer as a slow bolus injection over 2 to 3 minutes or by infusion [US].
- **Monitoring**: It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations prior to starting therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is advised [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Metabolic Disorders**:
    - **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg as a slow bolus or infusion.
    - **Loading Dose**: In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours (administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours).
    - **Maintenance Dose**: Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required.
    - **Maximum Dose**: The highest recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
    - **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Timing**: Administer after each dialysis session into the venous return line.
    - **Dose Adjustment**: Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels (target >40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known**: There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the provided source material [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of IV administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate appropriate medical treatment [US].
- **Seizures**: Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].

### General Precautions
- **Oral Formulation in Renal Impairment**: The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of the oral formulation in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

### Other Reported Effects
- **General**: Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Postmarketing**: Reports of seizures and serious hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management**: Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine treatment or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (on a surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Refer to the "Dosage and Administration" section; weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) is utilised for metabolic disorders [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specifically detailed in the provided source.
- **Renal Impairment**: See "Dosage and Administration" for ESRD IV dosing. Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if using the **oral** formulation in renal failure [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Excretion**: Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trials**: In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in dialysis patients, its effects on clinical outcomes in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection**: Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability**: Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
- **Handling**: Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; contains no preservative [US].
