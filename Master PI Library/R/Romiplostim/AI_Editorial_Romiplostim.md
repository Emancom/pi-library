# Editorial Product Information: Romiplostim

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **romiplostim** clinical data and regulatory requirements for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the provided official Product Information to ensure clinical clarity across regions.
- **Prescribing Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved documentation for definitive legal and prescribing guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Romiplostim (an Fc-peptide fusion protein/peptibody).
- **Chemical Class:** Thrombopoietin (TPO) receptor mimetic.
- **Brand Name:** **Nplate** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B02BX04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Lyophilised powder for injection (subcutaneous) in single-dose vials: 230 µg, 375 µg, and 625 µg [Australian].
    - *Note: After reconstitution, the final concentration is 500 µg/mL.*
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amgen Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Adult Primary Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP):** Indicated for the treatment of thrombocytopenia in patients with primary ITP who are:
    - Non-splenectomised and have had an inadequate response or intolerance to corticosteroids and immunoglobulins [Australian].
    - Splenectomised and have had an inadequate response to splenectomy [Australian].
- **Paediatric Primary ITP:** Indicated for the treatment of thrombocytopenia in patients aged 1 year and older with primary ITP for at least 6 months who have had an insufficient response or intolerance to standard therapies (as listed for adults) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- **Goal:** Use the lowest dose necessary to achieve and maintain a platelet count ≥ 50 x 10⁹/L to reduce bleeding risk. 
- **Monitoring:** Obtain weekly platelet counts until a stable count (≥ 50 x 10⁹/L for ≥ 4 weeks without dose adjustment) is achieved. Obtain counts monthly thereafter.

### Recommended Dosing Regimen
- **Initial Dose:** 1 µg/kg once weekly (based on actual body weight at initiation).
- **Titration:** Increase the weekly dose by 1 µg/kg increments until the target (50–200 x 10⁹/L) is reached.
- **Maximum Dose:** 10 µg/kg per week.
- **Dose Adjustments (Adults):**
    - Platelets < 50 x 10⁹/L: Increase dose by 1 µg/kg.
    - Platelets > 200 x 10⁹/L for 2 consecutive weeks: Reduce dose by 1 µg/kg.
    - Platelets > 400 x 10⁹/L: Do not dose. Reinitiate at a dose reduced by 1 µg/kg once platelets fall below 200 x 10⁹/L.
- **Dose Adjustments (Paediatrics):** Similar to adults; however, future adjustments should account for both platelet response and changes in body weight (reassessment recommended every 12 weeks).

### Administration and Reconstitution
- **Reconstitution:** Use ONLY Sterile Water for Injections. Gently swirl; do not shake.
- **Dilution:** If the calculated individual dose is < 23 µg, a secondary dilution step with preservative-free 0.9% sodium chloride is required to ensure dosing accuracy (to a final concentration of 125 µg/mL).
- **Administration:** Administer via SC injection. Only adults who have demonstrated stable counts and proper technique may self-administer; self-administration is not recommended for children [Australian].

### Discontinuation
- **Lack of Response:** Discontinue if the platelet count does not reach a level sufficient to avoid clinically important bleeding after 4 weeks at the maximum weekly dose of 10 µg/kg.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **romiplostim**, *E. coli*-derived products, or any excipients (mannitol, sucrose, histidine, polysorbate 20, and hydrochloric acid) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **MDS and AML Risk:** TPO receptor stimulators may stimulate the progression of existing myeloid malignancies or Myelodysplastic Syndromes (MDS). In clinical trials for MDS, romiplostim was associated with an increased risk of progression to Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML) [Australian].
- **Thrombotic/Thromboembolic Complications:** Events (e.g., DVT, PE, MI) have been observed regardless of the platelet count. Portal vein thrombosis has been reported in patients with chronic liver disease. Use with caution in patients with known risk factors (e.g., Factor V Leiden, advanced age, smoking, or prolonged immobilisation).
- **Bone Marrow Reticulin:** Increased reticulin fibre deposition has been observed; this may be suggested by peripheral blood smear abnormalities (teardrop cells, immature WBCs). Discontinue if new morphological abnormalities or cytopenias develop.
- **Loss of Response:** If a patient loses response, evaluate for causative factors, including the formation of neutralising antibodies or increased bone marrow reticulin.
- **Cessation Risk:** Discontinuation is likely to result in a recurrence of thrombocytopenia, occasionally more severe than baseline. Monitor weekly CBCs for at least 2 weeks post-cessation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Adult Frequency Data
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (most common), arthralgia, dizziness, insomnia, myalgia, pain in extremity, nausea, and diarrhoea.
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, dyspepsia, paraesthesia, hypersensitivity, and haemorrhage.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Bone marrow reticulin fibrosis, angioedema, and erythromelalgia.

### Paediatric Frequency Data
- **Very Common (>10%):** Upper respiratory tract infection, nasopharyngitis, rhinitis, cough, oropharyngeal pain, pyrexia, contusion, abdominal pain upper, and diarrhoea.
- **Common (1–10%):** Pharyngitis, sinusitis, conjunctivitis, gastroenteritis, rash, purpura, and urticaria.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Serious events reported include bone marrow disorders (increased reticulin), peripheral embolism, peripheral ischaemia, and myocardial infarction. Serious bleeding events (including intracranial haemorrhage) have occurred, particularly after treatment discontinuation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **ITP Medical Therapies:** Romiplostim has been used in combination with corticosteroids, danazol, azathioprine, IVIG, and anti-D immunoglobulin. Reduction of these concurrent therapies may be possible as platelet counts increase.
- **Laboratory Tests:** No identified interactions with laboratory or diagnostic tests.
- **Formal Studies:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been performed.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B3 [Australian]:** Safety and efficacy in pregnant women have not been established. Animal studies showed increased stillbirths and decreased perinatal survival at high doses. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk is unknown. A decision should be made to discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, considering the benefit to the mother and the potential risk to the infant.
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility were observed in rats at doses up to 100 µg/kg 3 times weekly.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients ≥ 1 year with primary ITP duration ≥ 6 months. Dosing must be carefully calculated and frequently adjusted for body weight changes.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy observed in patients ≥ 65 years, though greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically assessed in these populations. Use with caution; portal vein thrombosis risk is notably increased in chronic liver disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Romiplostim binds to and activates the TPO receptor, stimulating the proliferation and differentiation of megakaryocyte progenitor cells, thereby increasing platelet production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Peak platelet counts occur approximately 2 to 3 weeks post-dose. The response is dose-dependent but shows high inter-patient variability.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution/Elimination:** Target-mediated disposition. Romiplostim binds to TPO receptors on platelets and megakaryocytes; thus, higher platelet counts result in lower serum concentrations (and vice versa).
    - **Half-life:** Ranges from 1 to 34 days (median 3.5 days).
    - **Metabolism:** Likely via the reticuloendothelial system.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Studies (Study 1 & 2):** In placebo-controlled trials, **durable platelet response** (≥ 50 x 10⁹/L for ≥ 6 weeks) was achieved in 61% of non-splenectomised patients and 38% of splenectomised patients, compared to 5% and 0% in placebo groups, respectively.
- **Paediatric Studies (Study 5 & 6):** In Phase III trials, 52% of paediatric patients achieved a durable response compared to 10% in the placebo arm.
- **Long-term Extension:** Integrated analysis supports the long-term use of romiplostim (median duration 78 weeks), with many patients maintaining doses ≤ 2 µg/kg.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze). Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Stable for up to 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C when protected from light. Use as soon as practicable.
- **Diluted Solution:** To reduce microbiological hazards, use immediately. If necessary, it may be stored for up to 4 hours at 25°C in syringes or 4 hours at 2°C to 8°C in original vials.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
