# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic/Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Standard Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration:** Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Visual Inspection:** Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry and vital signs. The target plasma free carnitine concentration should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** While this document covers the injection, it is noted that oral levocarnitine safety has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency, and chronic high doses in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, the following adverse events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), Pain (30–35%), Headache (2–37%), Chest pain (6–15%), Fever (5–12%), Flu syndrome (15–29%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%), Tachycardia (5–9%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%), Abdominal pain (5–21%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (6–15%), Hypervolemia (3–12%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (10–18%), Paresthesia (3–12%) |

### Post-Marketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Warnings and Precautions).
- **Other:** Body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not specified in available sources; traditionally assessed as Category B]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, etc.) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Dosage and Administration; safety and efficacy are based on metabolic and ESRD clinical requirements [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD on dialysis. Oral formulations should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters which are rapidly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Clinical data in ESRD patients show that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increase plasma carnitine concentrations. In a 9-week study, trough concentrations rose from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8 [US].
- While the drug effectively increases carnitine levels, the specific effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; contains no preservative [US].
