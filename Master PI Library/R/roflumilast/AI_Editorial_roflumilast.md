# Editorial Product Information: **Roflumilast**

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of roflumilast, synthesized from official Product Information for use by healthcare professionals.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Roflumilast
*   **Chemical Class:** Selective phosphodiesterase 4 (PDE4) inhibitor.
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Daliresp** (reference brand); generic versions available [US].
*   **ATC Code:** R03DX07
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Oral tablets: 250 micrograms (mcg) and 500 mcg [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Jubilant Cadista Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD):** Indicated as a treatment to reduce the risk of COPD exacerbations in adult patients with severe COPD associated with chronic bronchitis and a history of exacerbations [US].
*   **Limitations of Use:**
    *   **Roflumilast** is not a bronchodilator and is not indicated for the relief of acute bronchospasm.
    *   The 250 mcg strength is a starting dose for the initial four weeks only and is not considered an effective therapeutic dose.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Maintenance Dose:** 500 mcg once daily [US].
*   **Administration:** May be taken with or without food.

### Titration Schedule
*   **Initial Phase:** 250 mcg once daily for the first 4 weeks. This initiation schedule is intended to reduce the rate of treatment discontinuation due to adverse effects [US].
*   **Maintenance Phase:** Increase to 500 mcg once daily after the initial 4-week period.

### Missed Dose
*   Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Moderate to severe liver impairment, classified as Child-Pugh B or C [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None identified in available sources.
*   **Acute Bronchospasm:** Should not be used for emergency relief of acute respiratory symptoms.
*   **Psychiatric Events including Suicidality:**
    *   Treatment is associated with increased psychiatric adverse reactions, including insomnia, anxiety, and depression.
    *   Instances of suicidal ideation and behavior (including completed suicide) have been reported in clinical trials and post-marketing settings.
    *   **Management:** Carefully weigh risks/benefits in patients with a history of depression. Patients and caregivers should be advised to monitor for mood changes and contact providers immediately if they occur.
*   **Weight Decrease:**
    *   Significant weight loss (5–10% of body weight) is common. 
    *   **Monitoring:** Monitor patient weight regularly. If unexplained or clinically significant weight loss occurs, evaluate the cause and consider discontinuation.
*   **Drug Interactions:** Concurrent use with strong cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzyme inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenobarbital) is not recommended due to reduced efficacy.

*   **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, dyspepsia, gastritis, vomiting.
*   **Infections:** Rhinitis, sinusitis, urinary tract infection.
*   **Musculoskeletal:** Muscle spasms.
*   **Nervous System:** Tremor.
*   **Psychiatric:** Anxiety, depression.

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Atrial fibrillation, lung cancer, prostate cancer, acute pancreatitis, acute renal failure, and hypersensitivity reactions (including angioedema and urticaria).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
*   **CYP3A4 and CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** Co-administration with strong inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, ketoconazole, fluvoxamine, enoxacin, cimetidine) increases systemic exposure to **roflumilast** and may increase the risk of adverse reactions. Weigh risks against benefits [US].
*   **CYP Enzyme Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, phenytoin) significantly reduce **roflumilast** exposure and therapeutic efficacy; use is not recommended [US].
*   **Oral Contraceptives:** Co-administration with gestodene/ethinyl estradiol may increase **roflumilast** systemic exposure by approximately 51% [US].

### Management Notes
*   No significant interactions were observed with inhaled salbutamol, formoterol, budesonide, or oral montelukast, digoxin, theophylline, warfarin, sildenafil, or antacids [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   [US]: There are no randomized clinical studies in pregnant women. Animal data indicate post-implantation loss and decreased pup viability at doses ≥ 10–16 times the Maximum Recommended Human Dose (MRHD). Should not be used during labor and delivery.
*   **Breastfeeding:** 
    *   [US]: Excretion into human milk is probable. Because of the potential for adverse effects on the infant, nursing mothers should not use **roflumilast**.
*   **Fertility:** In animal studies, male fertility was decreased at high doses (29x MRHD), but no effects were observed at 10x MRHD. No effects on human semen parameters were observed in a 3-month study.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required for geriatric patients, though increased sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is necessary for patients with renal impairment [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:**
    *   **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** Use with caution; weigh risk-benefit [US].
    *   **Moderate/Severe (Child-Pugh B or C):** Contraindicated [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** A selective inhibitor of phosphodiesterase 4 (PDE4). Inhibition leads to the accumulation of intracellular cyclic AMP (cAMP) in lung tissue, which is thought to mediate its therapeutic effects in COPD [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~80%. Food delays $T_{max}$ but does not affect total absorption.
    *   **Distribution:** 99% plasma protein bound.
    *   **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized via CYP3A4 and CYP1A2 to **roflumilast N-oxide**, the major active metabolite. Roflumilast N-oxide is 10 times more prevalent in plasma than the parent drug.
    *   **Elimination:** Effective half-life is ~17 hours for roflumilast and ~30 hours for the N-oxide metabolite. Primarily excreted in the urine (~70%) as metabolites.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Efficacy:** Evaluated in 8 randomized, double-blind trials involving 9394 patients. Key trials (Trials 5 and 6) in patients with severe COPD and chronic bronchitis showed significant reductions in moderate-to-severe exacerbations (15% to 18% reduction compared to placebo).
*   **Lung Function:** While not a bronchodilator, **roflumilast** demonstrated a mean improvement in $FEV_1$ of approximately 50 mL across pivotal 1-year trials [US].
*   **Add-on Therapy:** Trial 9 showed that adding **roflumilast** to ICS/LABA combination therapy further reduced the rate of exacerbations (Rate Ratio 0.92) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children.
