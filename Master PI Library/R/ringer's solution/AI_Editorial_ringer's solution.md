# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States PI was available for review; clinical practices in other regions should be verified against local guidelines.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corp) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, provided for clinical reference).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Administered intravenously as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Compatibility:** Stable and compatible when diluted in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL. Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [United States].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg given as a slow bolus or infusion [United States].
- **Acute Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours).
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though therapy may require up to 300 mg/kg in some cases.
- **Monitoring:** Pre-therapy plasma carnitine levels are recommended. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [United States].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [United States].
- **Method:** Administer as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line immediately following each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if trough levels exceed normal ranges (40 to 50 µmol/L).

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no known contraindications listed in the available source [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, primarily in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [United States].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulations):** While this document covers the injection, it is noted that chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients are often confounded by the underlying pathology of the disease.

### Frequency >5% (Regardless of Causality) [United States]
| System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia. |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting. |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia. |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia. |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis. |

### Other Notable Reactions
- **Less Frequent:** Body odor, gastritis.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (postmarketing reports) [United States].
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not specified by letter, but the narrative is provided]. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of carnitine following exogenous administration. Benefits to the mother must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg). Specific age-range safety profiles beyond metabolic/ESRD indications are not detailed in the source [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing in ESRD is specifically defined (see Section 3). The injection is preferred over oral forms in severe renal impairment to avoid metabolite accumulation [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. 
    - **Elimination:** Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. About 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [United States].
    - **Dialysis:** Levocarnitine is easily removed from plasma via hemodialysis [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine effectively increases plasma carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) has not been definitively determined in this population [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [United States].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [United States].
