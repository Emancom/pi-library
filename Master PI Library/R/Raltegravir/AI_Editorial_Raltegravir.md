# Editorial Product Information: Raltegravir

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data from the provided regulatory source.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Raltegravir (as potassium salt).
- **Chemical Class:** HIV integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI).
- **Brand Names:** ISENTRESS, ISENTRESS HD [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AJ01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablet: 400 mg (ISENTRESS).
    - Film-coated tablet: 600 mg (ISENTRESS HD).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme (Australia) Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated in combination with other antiretroviral agents for the treatment of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV-1) infection in adults and paediatric patients aged 2 years and older weighing at least 25 kg [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Treatment-naïve or Virologically Suppressed (on 400 mg BID):** 1200 mg (two 600 mg tablets) once daily OR 400 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Treatment-experienced:** 400 mg twice daily [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (Aged ≥ 2 years)
- **Weight ≥ 40 kg (Naive or Virologically Suppressed):** 1200 mg (two 600 mg tablets) once daily OR 400 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Weight ≥ 25 kg:** 400 mg twice daily [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- May be administered with or without food.
- Tablets should be swallowed whole; it is not recommended to chew, crush, or split the 400 mg or 600 mg tablets [Australian].
- **Non-Interchangeability:** The 400 mg and 600 mg formulations have different pharmacokinetic profiles. Do not substitute three 400 mg tablets to achieve a 1200 mg once-daily dose [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Severe Skin and Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Severe, potentially life-threatening, and fatal skin reactions (including Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, and DRESS) have been reported. Discontinue immediately if signs of severe rash or hypersensitivity (e.g., fever, general malaise, organ dysfunction, eosinophilia) occur [Australian].
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome:** Inflammatory responses to indolent or residual opportunistic infections (e.g., MAC, CMV, TB) may occur during the initial treatment phase. Autoimmune disorders (e.g., Graves’ disease) have also been reported [Australian].
- **Myopathy and Rhabdomyolysis:** Reported in patients treated with raltegravir. Use with caution in patients with a history of these conditions or those taking concomitant medications associated with these risks [Australian].
- **Cancers:** The risk of developing cancer appears similar to that of other antiretroviral agents in highly immunodeficient populations [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (1–10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, flatulence.
- **Nervous System:** Headache, dizziness.
- **Psychiatric:** Insomnia, abnormal dreams, depression, anxiety.
- **General:** Fatigue, pyrexia, asthenia.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity:** SJS, TEN, and DRESS [Australian].
- **Hepatobiliary:** Hepatitis, hepatic failure (particularly in patients with underlying liver disease).
- **Musculoskeletal:** Rhabdomyolysis.
- **Psychiatric:** Suicidal ideation and behaviours (particularly in those with pre-existing psychiatric illness).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Profile
- **Metabolism:** Primarily mediated by UGT1A1-mediated glucuronidation. Raltegravir is not a substrate, inhibitor, or inducer of Cytochrome P450 enzymes [Australian].

### Clinically Significant Interactions
- **UGT1A1 Inducers (e.g., Rifampin):** Decreases raltegravir plasma levels. Caution is advised with the 400 mg BID dose; a dose increase to 800 mg BID may be considered. Co-administration with the 1200 mg once-daily dose is not recommended [Australian].
- **UGT1A1 Inhibitors (e.g., Atazanavir):** Increases raltegravir levels. While the 400 mg BID dose is well-tolerated with atazanavir, co-administration with the 1200 mg once-daily dose is not recommended [Australian].
- **Antacids:** 
    - **Aluminium/Magnesium:** Reduces absorption via chelation. Co-administration is not recommended with any raltegravir dose [Australian].
    - **Calcium Carbonate:** Reduces raltegravir levels. While tolerated with 400 mg BID, co-administration with the 1200 mg once-daily dose is not recommended [Australian].
- **Iron Salts:** May reduce raltegravir levels; administration should be separated by at least two hours [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category A:** 400 mg twice daily regimen only.
    - **Australian Category B3:** All other strengths/regimens [Australian].
    - Data from the Antiretroviral Pregnancy Registry (APR) show no increased risk of major birth defects compared to background rates. Raltegravir 400 mg BID may be used during pregnancy if clinically needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding is not recommended for HIV-infected mothers to avoid postnatal transmission of HIV. It is unknown if raltegravir is excreted in human milk, though it is present in rat milk [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in children under 2 years of age or weighing less than 25 kg. Use of the 1200 mg once-daily dose is supported for children weighing ≥ 40 kg [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently. Caution is advised due to higher frequencies of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment necessary. Avoid dosing immediately before a dialysis session [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. Not studied in patients with severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Raltegravir inhibits the catalytic activity of HIV integrase, preventing the covalent insertion of the HIV genome into the host cell genome, thereby blocking viral replication [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed with a Tmax of approx. 3 hours (400 mg) or 1.5–2 hours (1200 mg).
    - **Distribution:** Approx. 83% protein bound. Readily crosses the placenta; detected in CSF (3–6% of plasma concentration).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via UGT1A1 glucuronidation. Terminal half-life is approximately 9 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BENCHMRK 1 & 2:** Demonstrated efficacy of 400 mg BID in treatment-experienced adults with resistance to multiple antiretroviral classes.
- **STARTMRK:** Demonstrated non-inferiority and superior long-term (240-week) efficacy of raltegravir 400 mg BID compared to efavirenz in treatment-naïve adults.
- **ONCEMRK:** Established non-inferiority of the 1200 mg once-daily dose compared to the 400 mg twice-daily dose in treatment-naïve adults over 96 weeks [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Available in HDPE bottles of 60 tablets [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
