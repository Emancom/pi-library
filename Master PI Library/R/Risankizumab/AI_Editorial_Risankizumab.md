# Editorial Product Information: Risankizumab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the product information for risankizumab. It is designed as a high-level reference for healthcare professionals to support clinical decision-making.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) mandated for use in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult the current approved local PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Risankizumab (a humanised IgG1 monoclonal antibody; interleukin-23 blocker).
- **Brand Name:** SKYRIZI [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AC18.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Subcutaneous Injection:** 150 mg/mL solution in a pre-filled pen or pre-filled syringe [Australian].
    - **Subcutaneous Injection (On-body Injector):** 180 mg/1.2 mL or 360 mg/2.4 mL solution in a pre-filled cartridge [Australian].
    - **Intravenous Infusion (Concentrate):** 600 mg/10 mL solution in a single-dose vial [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (S.C.) or Intravenous (I.V.) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AbbVie Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Plaque Psoriasis:** Treatment of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis in adults (≥18 years) who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy [Australian].
- **Psoriatic Arthritis:** Treatment of active psoriatic arthritis in adults with an inadequate response or intolerance to one or more disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (DMARDs). May be used as monotherapy or in combination with conventional synthetic DMARDs (csDMARDs) [Australian].
- **Crohn’s Disease:** Treatment of moderate to severe Crohn’s disease in adults who have had an inadequate response, lost response, or were intolerant to conventional or biologic therapy [Australian].
- **Ulcerative Colitis:** Treatment of adults with moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis who have had an inadequate response, lost response, or were intolerant to conventional or biologic therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Plaque Psoriasis and Psoriatic Arthritis
- **Standard Dose:** 150 mg administered subcutaneously at Week 0, Week 4, and every 12 weeks thereafter [Australian].

### Crohn’s Disease
- **Induction Dose:** 600 mg administered by I.V. infusion at Week 0, Week 4, and Week 8 [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** 360 mg administered by S.C. injection (via on-body injector) at Week 12 and every 8 weeks thereafter [Australian].

### Ulcerative Colitis
- **Induction Dose:** 1200 mg administered by I.V. infusion at Week 0, Week 4, and Week 8 [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** 180 mg or 360 mg administered by S.C. injection (via on-body injector) at Week 12 and every 8 weeks thereafter. Dosing is based on clinical presentation: 180 mg for patients with adequate improvement after induction; 360 mg for those with inadequate improvement [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **S.C. Injection:** Administer in the thigh or abdomen. Injection into the upper arm should only be performed by a healthcare professional or caregiver. Rotate injection sites. Do not inject into tender, bruised, or psoriatic skin [Australian].
- **I.V. Infusion:** Must be diluted (in 5% dextrose or 0.9% saline) and administered by a healthcare professional over at least one hour (600 mg dose) or two hours (1200 mg dose) [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Administer as soon as possible, then resume the regular scheduled dosing [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to risankizumab or any of its excipients.
- Clinically important active infections (e.g., active tuberculosis) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Infections:** Risankizumab may increase infection risk. Evaluate for chronic/recurrent infections before prescribing. If a serious infection occurs, monitor closely and withhold treatment until the infection resolves [Australian].
- **Tuberculosis (TB):** Screen patients for TB prior to initiation. Do not administer to patients with active TB. Consider anti-TB therapy for patients with latent TB or a history of inadequate treatment [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity (IBD):** Drug-induced liver injury has been reported in patients treated for Crohn’s disease. Evaluate liver enzymes and bilirubin at baseline, during induction (up to 12 weeks), and periodically thereafter [Australian].
- **Immunisations:** Ensure all vaccinations are current before starting therapy. Avoid **live vaccines** during treatment and for at least 21 weeks after the final dose [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally similar across all indications. Frequencies are based on 16-week pooled data for psoriasis [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Upper respiratory tract infections |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache, Fatigue, Injection site reactions, Tinea infections |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Folliculitis |

- **IBD-Specific Reactions:** Arthralgia, abdominal pain, anaemia, pyrexia, and urinary tract infections were reported in >3% of patients during Crohn’s and Ulcerative Colitis trials [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Serious hypersensitivity and serious infections (though the rate of serious infection was lower than placebo in some trials) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450:** Risankizumab does not significantly impact the pharmacokinetics of CYP450 substrates (e.g., caffeine, warfarin, omeprazole). No dose adjustments are required when co-administering [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** The safety/efficacy of risankizumab in combination with other biologics or potent immunosuppressants has not been evaluated [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1.** 
    - Human IgG is known to cross the placental barrier. Data in pregnant women are insufficient to inform drug-associated risks. Use only if benefits outweigh potential risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if risankizumab is excreted in human milk. Endogenous IgG is excreted in milk in small amounts; a risk to the newborn cannot be excluded in the first few days after birth. A decision should consider the benefit of breastfeeding to the infant and the benefit of therapy to the mother [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no direct or indirect harmful effects on male or female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** No dose adjustment is required. Limited data are available for patients ≥75 years [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific studies were conducted. Pharmacokinetic analyses indicate no meaningful impact on clearance; therefore, no dose adjustments are necessary [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A humanised IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity to the p19 subunit of the IL-23 cytokine. By blocking IL-23, it inhibits the development and activation of Th17 cells and the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-17A, IL-17F, IL-22) [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment leads to reduced epidermal thickness and decreased infiltration of inflammatory cells in psoriatic lesions. In IBD, it reduces inflammatory biomarkers like C-reactive protein (CRP) and faecal calprotectin [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 74–89%. Peak plasma concentrations are reached 3–14 days post-S.C. dose.
    - **Distribution:** Confined primarily to vascular and interstitial spaces.
    - **Metabolism:** Degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 21 days (Crohn's) to 28 days (Psoriasis) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Psoriasis:** Significant efficacy (sPGA 0/1 and PASI 90) was demonstrated in pivotal trials (ULTIMMA-1, ULTIMMA-2, IMMHANCE, IMMVENT) compared to placebo, ustekinumab, and adalimumab [Australian].
- **Psoriatic Arthritis:** Improved ACR20/50/70 responses and skin clearance were noted in KEEPsAKE-1 and KEEPsAKE-2 trials [Australian].
- **IBD:** Induction (ADVANCE, MOTIVATE, INSPIRE) and maintenance (FORTIFY, COMMAND) studies demonstrated significant rates of clinical remission and endoscopic response compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Refrigerate; do not freeze [Australian].
- **Light Protection:** Keep in the original carton until use [Australian].
- **Temperature Excursion:** Pre-filled pens/syringes and cartridges can be kept at room temperature (up to 25°C) for up to 24 hours once, protected from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
