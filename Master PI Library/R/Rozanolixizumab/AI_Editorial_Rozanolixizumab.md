# Editorial Product Information: Rozanolixizumab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) provided by the Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **rozanolixizumab** for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current documentation before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rozanolixizumab; a recombinant, humanised anti-neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) IgG4P monoclonal antibody.
- **Brand Name:** **Rystiggo** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection (subcutaneous infusion) available as 140 mg/mL in a 2 mL single-use vial (280 mg per vial). [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** UCB Pharma [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Generalised Myasthenia Gravis (gMG):** Indicated as add-on therapy for the treatment of gMG in adult patients who are positive for anti-acetylcholine receptor (AChR) or anti-muscle-specific tyrosine kinase (MuSK) antibodies. [Australian]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Treatment should be initiated and supervised by specialists experienced in neuromuscular or neuro-inflammatory disorders. **Rozanolixizumab** is administered as a weekly subcutaneous infusion for a treatment cycle of 6 weeks. 

The recommended weekly dose is weight-based:
- **≥ 35 kg to < 50 kg:** 280 mg (2 mL / 1 vial)
- **≥ 50 kg to < 70 kg:** 420 mg (3 mL / 2 vials*)
- **≥ 70 kg to < 100 kg:** 560 mg (4 mL / 2 vials)
- **≥ 100 kg:** 840 mg (6 mL / 3 vials)
*\*Note: Vials contain excess volume for priming; doses requiring multiple vials must be calculated precisely.*

### Cycle Frequency and Discontinuation
- **Subsequent Cycles:** Administered based on clinical evaluation of symptom recurrence.
- **Intervals:** Clinical trials showed treatment-free intervals of 4–13 weeks. Safety of initiating a new cycle sooner than 4 weeks from the last infusion has not been established.
- **Discontinuation:** Therapy must be discontinued if a patient fails to demonstrate a response (decrease of ≥2 points on the MG-ADL scale) within three 6-week treatment cycles.

### Administration Instructions
- **Method:** Subcutaneous infusion using an appropriate pump at a constant flow rate up to 20 mL/hr. 
- **Site:** Lower right or left abdomen (below the belly button). Avoid tender, erythematous, or indurated skin.
- **Equipment:** Use tubing ≤ 61 cm and needles ≥ 26 gauge. Do not use devices containing DEHP.
- **Home Administration:** May be considered if tolerated in-clinic, provided it is performed by a qualified healthcare professional.

### Missed Doses
If an infusion is missed, it may be administered up to 4 days after the scheduled time. If later than 4 days, the missed dose is skipped, and the original schedule resumes until the cycle is complete.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **rozanolixizumab** or any of the excipients (histidine, histidine hydrochloride monohydrate, proline, polysorbate 80). [Australian]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Aseptic Meningitis:** Drug-induced aseptic meningitis has been reported. If symptoms occur (e.g., severe headache, neck stiffness, fever), initiate a diagnostic workup and treat according to standard care.
- **Infections:** Due to its mechanism (IgG reduction), the risk of infection may increase. Do not initiate treatment in patients with clinically important active infections. Monitor for signs of infection during treatment and consider withholding the drug if a serious infection develops.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Discontinue infusion immediately and initiate supportive care if systemic hypersensitivity occurs.
- **Hyperprolinaemia:** This product contains 29 mg/mL of proline. Use should be restricted in patients with hyperprolinaemia where no alternative treatment is available.
- **Body Weight < 50 kg:** Limited data exist for this group; efficacy for repeated cycles has not been reliably demonstrated.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse drug reactions (ADRs) reported are headache, diarrhoea, and pyrexia.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Headache (including migraine), Diarrhoea, Pyrexia |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Upper respiratory tract infections, Herpes viral infections, Rash (various types), Arthralgia, Injection site reactions, Angioedema (including swollen tongue) |
| **Not Known** | Aseptic meningitis |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Immunogenicity:** Approximately 26.9% of patients developed anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) after one cycle, increasing to 61.4% after five cycles. While no impact on overall safety/efficacy was noted, patients with ADAs had higher rates of dyslipidaemia and dyspnoea.
- **Infection Risk:** Potential for serious infections due to reduced serum IgG levels.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **IgG-based Therapies:** **Rozanolixizumab** reduces serum IgG concentrations. Concomitant use with monoclonal antibodies or IVIg may decrease the efficacy of these treatments. It is recommended to wait at least 2 weeks after a **rozanolixizumab** infusion before initiating such drugs.
- **Human Immunoglobulin:** May decrease serum concentrations of **rozanolixizumab**.
- **Vaccinations:** The response to vaccines is unknown. Live-attenuated or live vaccines are not recommended during treatment. 
- **CYP450 Interactions:** Interactions with medications that are substrates, inducers, or inhibitors of cytochrome P450 enzymes or transporters are unlikely.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **[Australian Category D]**. There are limited data in humans. Animal studies showed an increased risk of early pregnancy loss. Because it inhibits FcRn, it may interfere with the placental transfer of maternal IgGs, potentially affecting neonatal immunocompetence. Avoid use unless the benefit to the mother outweighs the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made based on the maternal clinical need and potential infant risks.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in monkeys showed no adverse effects on male or female reproductive tissues or cycles.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children < 18 years have not been established. [Australian]
- **Elderly Use (≥ 65 years):** Limited data (n=17 in pivotal trials). No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed compared to younger adults, but the sample size is insufficient for a definitive conclusion.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is considered necessary. Based on PK analysis, eGFR between 38–161 mL/min/1.73m² did not affect clearance. No data exist for severe impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No data available. However, since the drug is degraded into peptides/amino acids, hepatic impairment is unlikely to affect pharmacokinetics.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A humanised IgG4 monoclonal antibody that binds to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn). This blocks the recycling of IgG, leading to increased intracellular degradation and a rapid reduction in total serum IgG and pathogenic IgG autoantibodies.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Weekly administration leads to a maximum IgG reduction of approximately 73% at 3 weeks. Levels typically recover within 8 weeks of treatment cessation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is approximately 2 days; bioavailability is ~70%.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is ~7 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways.
    - **Elimination:** Clearance is ~0.9 L/day. Half-life is concentration-dependent. Plasma concentrations become undetectable within one week of dosing.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study (MG0003):** A Phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 200 adult patients with gMG.
- **Primary Outcome:** Patients treated with ~7 mg/kg demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in the MG-Activities of Daily Living (MG-ADL) score at Day 43 (mean decrease of 3.37 vs 0.78 for placebo).
- **Secondary Outcomes:** Significant improvements were also noted in the Quantitative Myasthenia Gravis (QMG) and Myasthenia Gravis-Composite (MG-C) scores.
- **Onset:** Clinical response was observed as early as Day 8 (one week after the first dose).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Excursion:** Vials may be stored at room temperature (up to 25°C) for a single period of up to 20 days. If not used within 20 days of removal from refrigeration, the product must be discarded.
- **Protection:** Keep the vial in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** 36 months. [Australian]

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **[Australian Schedule 4]** (Prescription Only Medicine).
