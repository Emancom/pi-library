# Editorial Product Information: Rufinamide

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (TGA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of rufinamide, synthesized from official Product Information for use by healthcare professionals.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should refer to their local regulatory authority's current approved PI for complete prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rufinamide (a triazole derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Carboxamide derivative; Antiepileptic.
- **Brand Names:** Inovelon [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N03AF03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 100 mg, 200 mg, and 400 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Eisai Australia Pty. Ltd. [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Lennox-Gastaut Syndrome (LGS):** Indicated as adjunctive treatment for seizures associated with LGS in patients aged 4 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- Administration should be overseen by a specialist (neurologist or paediatrician) with experience in treating epilepsy.
- **Frequency:** Total daily doses must be divided into two equal doses, taken morning and evening.
- **Food Effect:** Administer with food to increase bioavailability.
- **Administration Note:** Tablets are scored and can be divided equally. For patients with swallowing difficulties, tablets may be crushed and administered in half a glass of water [Australian].

### Dosing in Patients < 30 kg
- **Without Valproate:** Start at 200 mg/day. Titrate by 200 mg/day increments every third day to a maximum of 1000 mg/day.
- **With Valproate:** Valproate significantly reduces rufinamide clearance. Start at 200 mg/day. After at least two days, increase by 200 mg/day to a maximum of 600 mg/day [Australian].

### Dosing in Patients ≥ 30 kg (Children, Adolescents, and Adults)
- **Without Valproate:** Start at 400 mg/day. Titrate by 400 mg/day increments every other day. Maximum daily doses are weight-dependent:
    - 30.0 – 50.0 kg: 1,800 mg/day.
    - 50.1 – 70.0 kg: 2,400 mg/day.
    - ≥ 70.1 kg: 3,200 mg/day.
- **With Valproate:** Maximum daily doses are reduced:
    - 30.0 – 50.0 kg: 1,200 mg/day.
    - 50.1 – 70.0 kg: 1,600 mg/day.
    - ≥ 70.1 kg: 2,200 mg/day [Australian].

### Discontinuation
- Withdraw treatment gradually to minimise the risk of rebound seizures. In clinical trials, the dose was reduced by approximately 25% every two days [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **rufinamide**, triazole derivatives, or any tablet excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Status Epilepticus:** Observed in clinical trials. Reassess the benefit-risk ratio if new seizure types or increased frequency of status epilepticus occur.
- **Central Nervous System Reactions:** High incidence of dizziness, somnolence, ataxia, and gait disturbances. Caution is required due to the risk of accidental falls.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including DRESS (Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms) and Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, have occurred, particularly in paediatric populations and shortly after treatment initiation. Discontinue immediately if suspected.
- **QT Shortening:** Rufinamide causes a concentration-dependent decrease in the QTc interval. Exercise clinical judgment in patients with Congenital Short QT Syndrome or a relevant family history.
- **Suicidal Ideation and Behaviour:** As with other antiepileptics, patients should be monitored for changes in mood or suicidal tendencies.
- **Excipients:** Contains lactose; avoid in patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Somnolence, headache, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, fatigue.
- **Common (1–10%):** Pneumonia, influenza, nasopharyngitis, anorexia, anxiety, insomnia, status epilepticus, tremor, nystagmus, diplopia, blurred vision, abdominal pain, constipation, diarrhoea, rash, acne, back pain, and weight decrease.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Hypersensitivity, hepatic enzyme increase.
- **Serious Effects:** Status epilepticus, DRESS/SJS, and gait disturbances leading to injury [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Effects of Other Drugs on Rufinamide
- **Valproate:** Significantly increases rufinamide concentrations (up to 70% in children). Rufinamide dose reduction is required.
- **Enzyme Inducers (Phenytoin, Phenobarbital, Primidone):** May decrease rufinamide concentrations by 25–46% depending on age.
- **Carbamazepine/Vigabatrin:** May cause a modest decrease (<30%) in rufinamide levels [Australian].

### Effects of Rufinamide on Other Drugs
- **CYP3A4 Induction:** Rufinamide is a modest inducer. It may reduce exposure to triazolam and **oral contraceptives** (ethinylestradiol and norethindrone). Women of childbearing potential should use an additional non-hormonal contraceptive method.
- **Narrow Therapeutic Window Drugs:** Monitor patients closely when starting or stopping rufinamide if they are taking CYP3A4 substrates like warfarin or digoxin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Animal studies show no teratogenicity but indicate foetotoxicity (reduced growth/survival and stillbirths) at maternally toxic doses. 
    - Clinical data in humans are unavailable. Use only if clearly necessary. Registration in the Australian Pregnancy Register for Women on Antiepileptic Medication is encouraged.
- **Breastfeeding:** Likely excreted in human milk. A decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the importance of the medication to the mother against the potential risk to the infant [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for LGS in patients $\ge$ 4 years. Data for children 1 to < 4 years are limited; while safety profiles appear similar, no posology recommendations are established for this age group [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Limited data available. Pharmacokinetics appear similar to younger adults; no routine dose adjustment is required for those over 65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment required for severe renal impairment. Haemodialysis reduces plasma levels by ~30%.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in mild-to-moderate impairment. **Contraindicated** in severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Modulates sodium channel activity by prolonging the inactive state, thereby limiting high-frequency repetitive firing of neurons.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is ~6 hours. Bioavailability decreases as dose increases (non-linear). Food increases AUC by ~34%.
    - **Distribution:** 34% protein bound (primarily to albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Almost exclusively via hydrolysis (non-CYP450) to the inactive metabolite CGP 47292.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (85%). Elimination half-life is 6–10 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Lennox-Gastaut Syndrome Study:** A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=139, ages 4–30 years) using doses up to 45 mg/kg/day. Rufinamide demonstrated significant superiority over placebo in reducing total seizure frequency (-35.8% vs -1.6%), reducing tonic-atonic ("drop") attacks (-42.9% vs 2.2%), and improving seizure severity ratings [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- Shelf life is specified on the secondary packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
