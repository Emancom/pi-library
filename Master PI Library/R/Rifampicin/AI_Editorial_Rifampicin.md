# Editorial Product Information: Rifampicin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a structured format for quick reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of publication, only the Australian source was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rifampicin (a semi-synthetic derivative of rifamycin B).
- **Chemical Class:** Rifamycins (Antimycobacterial antibiotic).
- **Brand Names:** **Rifadin** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J04AB02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** [Australian]
    - Oral Capsules: 150 mg and 300 mg.
    - Oral Syrup: 100 mg/5 mL.
    - Intravenous (IV) Infusion: 600 mg powder for reconstitution (with 10 mL sterile water for injection solvent).
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral; Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Tuberculosis (TB):** Indicated for both initial treatment and re-treatment. Rifampicin must always be used in combination with at least one other active antituberculosis medication [Australian].
- **Leprosy:** Used in the management of lepromatous and dimorphous leprosy to achieve rapid non-infectious conversion (typically within 3–4 months). It is an alternative for patients resistant to sulfones or those with allergies to primary antileprosy agents. It must be used in conjunction with at least one other antileprosy drug [Australian].
- **Meningococcal Disease Prophylaxis:** Indicated for the elimination of *Neisseria meningitidis* in close contacts of known cases and carriers. It is **not** indicated for the treatment of active meningococcal infections [Australian].
- **Haemophilus influenzae Prophylaxis:** Indicated for household contacts of patients with *H. influenzae* type B [Australian].
- **Note on Syrup Formulation:** In Australia, the syrup is restricted to exceptional cases where all other alternatives for meningococcal or *H. influenzae* prophylaxis are exhausted due to potential carcinogenic risks related to excipients [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- **Oral:** Administer once daily on an empty stomach, either 30 minutes before or 2 hours after a meal to ensure optimal absorption [Australian].
- **Intravenous:** Reserved for patients unable to tolerate oral therapy. Administered via infusion over 1 to 3 hours. Must not be given via IM or SC routes [Australian].

### Pulmonary Tuberculosis
- **Adults:** 600 mg daily in a single dose.
- **Children:** 10 to 20 mg/kg daily (maximum 600 mg/day).

### Leprosy
- **Adults:** 450 to 600 mg daily in a single dose [Australian].

### Prophylaxis
- **Meningococcal Disease:**
    - Adults: 600 mg daily for 4 days.
    - Children (>5 years): 10 mg/kg daily for 4 days (maximum 600 mg/day).
    - Children (<5 years): Dosage data not established in source [Australian].
- **Haemophilus influenzae (Type B):**
    - Adults/Children: 20 mg/kg once daily for 4 days (maximum 600 mg/day).
    - Neonates (<1 month): 10 mg/kg once daily for 4 days [Australian].

### Missed Dose and Interruption
- Continuous daily treatment is preferred over intermittent therapy to prevent immunopathological reactions. If therapy is resumed after interruption, initiate with small incremental doses (e.g., 150 mg/day) and monitor renal function [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to rifampicin or any other rifamycins [Australian].
- Presence of jaundice [Australian].
- **Concomitant Medication Contraindications:** [Australian]
    - Saquinavir/ritonavir combination (increased hepatotoxicity risk).
    - Cabotegravir, fostemsavir, and lenacapavir (significant reduction in antiviral exposure).
    - Lurasidone (marked decrease in lurasidone exposure).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Rifampicin can cause liver dysfunction and fatalities. Baseline and periodic (every 2–4 weeks) liver function tests (LFTs) are mandatory for adults, especially those with pre-existing liver disease or those taking other hepatotoxic drugs. Therapy should be discontinued if signs of hepatocellular damage or cholestasis occur [Australian].
- **Body Fluid Discolouration:** May cause red-orange, yellow, or brown discolouration of urine, faeces, saliva, sputum, sweat, tears, and teeth. Soft contact lenses may be permanently stained [Australian].
- **Antibiotic-Associated Colitis:** *Clostridium difficile*-associated diarrhoea has been reported, ranging from mild to life-threatening [Australian].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Immunological Reactions:** Intermittent therapy (less than 2–3 times/week) is discouraged due to risks of anaphylaxis, "flu-like syndrome," and renal failure. If thrombocytopenia, purpura, or haemolytic anaemia occurs, rifampicin must be stopped permanently [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Cases of SJS, TEN, AGEP, and DRESS have been reported. Discontinue immediately at the first sign of a systemic skin reaction [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Reports of potentially fatal ILD or pneumonitis; monitor for new or worsening pulmonary symptoms [Australian].
- **Vitamin K Coagulopathy:** Rifampicin may cause Vitamin K dependent coagulopathy and severe bleeding. Monitor patients at risk and consider supplemental Vitamin K [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** (Common) Heartburn, epigastric distress, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, cramps, and diarrhoea [Australian].
- **Hepatobiliary:** Elevations in serum bilirubin and liver enzymes; hepatitis and cholestasis (occasional to rare) [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Flushing, pruritus, urticaria, and rare cases of SJS/TEN or DRESS [Australian].
- **Haematological:** Thrombocytopenia (associated with intermittent therapy), leucopenia, eosinophilia, and rare agranulocytosis [Australian].
- **Neurological:** Headache, drowsiness, fatigue, ataxia, and mental confusion [Australian].
- **Other:** "Flu-like syndrome" (episodes of fever, chills, bone pain) occurs in up to 50% of patients on high-dose weekly intermittent regimens [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic (Enzyme Induction):** Rifampicin is a potent inducer of **CYP1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, and 3A4**, as well as **P-glycoprotein (P-gp)**. It significantly reduces the plasma concentration of many drugs [Australian].
- **Clinically Significant Reductions:**
    - **Contraceptives:** Reduces efficacy of oral and systemic hormonal contraceptives. Advise non-hormonal methods [Australian].
    - **Anticoagulants:** Increases the requirement for coumarin-type drugs (e.g., warfarin); monitor prothrombin time daily [Australian].
    - **Antivirals:** Contraindicated or requires avoidance with various HIV and Hepatitis C antivirals [Australian].
    - **Others:** Reduces levels of anticonvulsants, antiarrhythmics, antipsychotics, antifungals, benzodiazepines, beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, corticosteroids, and oral hypoglycaemics [Australian].
- **Other Interactions:** 
    - **Antacids:** May reduce rifampicin absorption; take rifampicin at least 1 hour before antacids [Australian].
    - **Isoniazid/Halothane:** Increased risk of hepatotoxicity when used concurrently [Australian].
    - **Atovaquone:** Decreased atovaquone levels and increased rifampicin levels [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. There are no well-controlled human studies. Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus. Rifampicin crosses the placenta. Use in late pregnancy may lead to postnatal haemorrhage in mother or infant; Vitamin K administration is recommended [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Due to genotoxic potential, males should use effective contraception during and for 3 months post-treatment. Females should use effective contraception (non-hormonal) during and for 6 months post-treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Rifampicin is excreted in breast milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended for patients receiving this drug [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in premature and newborn infants only in "most grave emergencies" due to underdeveloped liver enzymes [Australian]. 
- **Elderly Use:** Information not specified in available source [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Monitor closely during intermittent therapy or if treatment is resumed after interruption due to risk of renal hypersensitivity [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use only if strictly necessary and under close medical supervision with frequent LFT monitoring [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits DNA-dependent RNA polymerase in susceptible bacterial cells by interacting with the bacterial enzyme, but does not inhibit the mammalian enzyme. It is bactericidal against both extracellular and intracellular organisms [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed from the GI tract; peak serum levels (approx. 7 microgram/mL) occur 2–4 hours post-dose (600 mg). Food reduces absorption [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed in body fluids and organs, including CSF. Protein binding is ~80% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Undergoes progressive deacetylation in the liver; the metabolite remains antibacterially active [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily eliminated via bile (enterohepatic circulation) and urine (~30% of dose) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Data regarding specific pivotal trials was not provided in the primary source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted IV Solution:** Stable for 30 hours. However, dilutions in 5% glucose are stable for only 8 hours; dilutions in normal saline are compatible for up to 6 hours [Australian].
- **Handling:** Unstable to light and moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
