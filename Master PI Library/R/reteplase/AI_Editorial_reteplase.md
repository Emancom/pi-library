# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial has been prepared based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg given as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours by infusion or intravenous injection.
- **Daily Dose Range:** Usually 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L).

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range 40 to 50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of an opened vial as the formulation is preservative-free.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no known contraindications for levocarnitine injection [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical management.
- **Risk of Metabolite Accumulation (Oral Formulation):** While not directly applicable to the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In ESRD patients, trough carnitine levels should be monitored. In patients on warfarin, INR should be monitored closely.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients (occurring at ≥5% regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia [US: uncommon]. |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia. |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight increase/decrease. |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression. |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis. |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash. |

- **Neurologic Effects:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Body Odor:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and body odor have been observed with carnitine supplementation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specifically lettered in provided text, but states "use only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have been established for the treatment of inborn errors of metabolism and carnitine deficiency in ESRD. Specific age-related normal concentrations define deficiency in premature infants and newborns [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety of high-dose *oral* carnitine in renal impairment is not established due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities resulting from the accumulation of toxic organic acids (e.g., glutaric aciduria II, methyl malonic aciduria).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical trials (9-week study and dose-ranging studies) demonstrated that 20 mg/kg levocarnitine injection three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy.
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the effects on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined in these trials.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL.
