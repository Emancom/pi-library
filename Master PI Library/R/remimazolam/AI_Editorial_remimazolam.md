# Editorial Product Information: Remimazolam

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial consolidation for informational purposes. It does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) documentation was provided for analysis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Remimazolam (as remimazolam besylate).
- **Chemical Class:** Benzodiazepine.
- **Brand Names:** **BYFAVO** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** N05CD14 (Noted by convention; not explicitly in source).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilized powder for injection. Each single-use glass vial contains 20 mg remimazolam (equivalent to 27.2 mg remimazolam besylate) [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Acacia Pharma, Ltd. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Procedural Sedation:** Indicated for the induction and maintenance of procedural sedation in adults for procedures with a duration of 30 minutes or less [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Dosing must be individualised and titrated to the desired clinical sedative effect. 
- **Analgesic Pre-treatment:** In clinical trials, patients received fentanyl (25 mcg to 75 mcg) for analgesia prior to the first dose of remimazolam [United States].

### Specific Dosing Regimens
- **Standard Adult Patients:** 
    - **Induction:** 5 mg administered as an intravenous push over 1 minute.
    - **Maintenance (as needed):** 2.5 mg administered intravenously over 15 seconds. At least 2 minutes must elapse between any supplemental doses [United States].
- **ASA III-IV Patients (Physician Discretion):**
    - **Induction:** 2.5 mg to 5 mg administered over 1 minute, depending on the patient's general condition.
    - **Maintenance (as needed):** 1.25 mg to 2.5 mg administered over 15 seconds. At least 2 minutes must elapse between supplemental doses [United States].

### Administration and Preparation
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with 8.2 mL of sterile 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, USP. Direct the stream toward the vial wall and swirl gently (do not shake). The final concentration is 2.5 mg/mL [United States].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible with 0.9% NaCl, 5% Dextrose, 20% Dextrose, 5% Dextrose/0.45% NaCl, and Ringer’s Solution [United States].
- **Incompatibility:** Incompatible with Lactated Ringer’s Solution (also known as Hartmann's Solution) and Acetated Ringer’s Solution [United States].
- **Timing:** Reconstituted product should be used immediately. It may be stored in the vial for up to 8 hours at room temperature; discard any unused portion thereafter [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Dextran Hypersensitivity:** Known history of severe hypersensitivity reactions to **dextran 40** or products containing dextran 40 [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [United States]
- **Personnel and Monitoring:** Should only be administered by personnel trained in procedural sedation who are not involved in the conduct of the diagnostic/therapeutic procedure. 
- **Resuscitation:** Personnel must be proficient in airway management, circulatory resuscitation, and the management of hypoventilation/apnea. Resuscitative drugs and age/size-appropriate equipment must be immediately available.
- **Concomitant Opioid Use:** Concurrent use with opioid analgesics or other CNS depressants may cause profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

### General Precautions
- **Cardiorespiratory Effects:** Associated with hypoxia, bradycardia, and hypotension. Continuous vital sign monitoring is required during sedation and the recovery period [United States].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contains dextran 40; may cause rash, urticaria, pruritus, or anaphylaxis [United States].
- **Neonatal Risk:** Benzodiazepine use late in pregnancy can cause neonatal sedation and withdrawal syndrome [United States].
- **Pediatric Neurotoxicity:** Animal data suggests that exposure to sedative/anaesthetic drugs for >3 hours in early life (third trimester to age 3) can lead to neuronal apoptosis and cognitive deficits. The clinical significance in humans is unclear, but caution is advised for elective procedures in young children [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions (>10%) include blood pressure fluctuations and respiratory changes [United States].

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency [United States] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hypotension** | Very Common (>10%) |
| **Hypertension** | Very Common (>10%) |
| **Hypoxia** | Very Common (>10%) |
| **Bradycardia** | Common (1–10%) |
| **Tachycardia** | Common (1–10%) |
| **Nausea** | Common (1–10%) |
| **Headache** | Common (1–10%) |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory acidosis (observed in rescue midazolam arm), profound hypoxia, and bradycardia leading to treatment discontinuation [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Opioids and CNS Depressants:** The sedative effect is significantly accentuated by concomitant opioids, other benzodiazepines, and propofol. Titrate remimazolam doses downward when used with these agents [United States].
- **Alcohol:** May increase sedative effects; patients should be advised to avoid alcohol post-procedure [United States].

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP450 System:** Remimazolam and its metabolite (CNS7054) do not inhibit major CYP enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, or 3A4) and are not relevant substrates for major drug transporters (OATP, BCRP).
- **Carboxylesterases:** Metabolism is mediated by tissue carboxylesterases (primarily type 1A). There is low potential for PK-based drug interactions [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: No assigned letter category]. Available human data from observational studies have not identified a clear drug-associated risk of major birth defects. However, benzodiazepines cross the placenta. Use late in pregnancy may result in neonatal sedation (hypotonia, respiratory depression) and withdrawal symptoms.
- **Breastfeeding:** Remimazolam is present in animal milk; human presence is unknown. To minimise exposure, a lactating woman may consider pumping and discarding breast milk for **5 hours** (approximately 5 elimination half-lives) after administration [United States].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats and rabbits showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility at doses significantly higher than the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [United States].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety/efficacy compared to younger adults, but elderly patients may exhibit increased sensitivity (faster onset, longer duration). Administer supplemental doses slowly [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with mild to end-stage renal disease (not requiring dialysis). Exposure to the inactive metabolite CNS7054 increases with impairment severity [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild/Moderate:** No dose adjustment needed.
    - **Severe:** In patients with severe hepatic impairment, the half-life is prolonged. Carefully titrate the dose to effect and use lower frequency supplemental doses if required [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A benzodiazepine that binds to brain GABA-A receptors. It increases the frequency of chloride channel opening, resulting in hyperpolarisation and inhibition of neuronal firing.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Onset:** Peak sedation occurs approximately 3 to 3.5 minutes after initial 5 mg dose [United States].
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 0.76 to 0.98 L/kg. Highly protein-bound (>91%), mainly to albumin [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly metabolised by tissue carboxylesterases to an inactive carboxylic acid metabolite (CNS7054), which has 300-fold lower affinity for GABA-A receptors [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 37 to 53 minutes. Mean clearance is 54 to 75 L/h (not related to body weight) [United States].
    - **Excretion:** Primarily excreted in urine as the inactive metabolite CNS7054 [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Colonoscopy Studies (Study 1 & 2):** Success rates (composite of procedure completion, no rescue sedation, and $\le$ 5 doses in 15 mins) were 91.3% for remimazolam vs. 1.7% for placebo/rescue midazolam in Study 1. In ASA III/IV patients (Study 2), success was 84.4% [United States].
- **Bronchoscopy Study:** Success rate was 80.6% for remimazolam vs. 4.8% for placebo/rescue midazolam. Median time to fully alert after the procedure was 6 minutes [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at controlled room temperature between 20°C and 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C.
- **Light Protection:** Protect vials from light once removed from original packaging [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [United States].
- **Controlled Substance Schedule:** Schedule IV (CIV) [United States].
