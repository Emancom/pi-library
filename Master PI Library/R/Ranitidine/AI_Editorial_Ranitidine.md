# Editorial Product Information: Ranitidine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Not all strengths, dosage forms, or indications may be available in all regions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ranitidine hydrochloride (a selective, competitive histamine H₂-receptor antagonist).
- **Chemical Class:** Aminoalkyl substituted furan.
- **Brand Names:** **APO-Ranitidine** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A02BA02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Oral Tablets:** 150 mg and 300 mg [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral (Information regarding injection and syrup forms is referenced in precautions but specific dosing for those forms was not detailed in the provided tablet source).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Duodenal Ulcer:** Short-term treatment of proven duodenal ulcer and maintenance therapy at reduced dosage after healing.
- **Gastric Ulcer:** Short-term treatment of proven benign gastric ulcer and maintenance therapy for up to one year.
- **Reflux Oesophagitis:** Short-term symptomatic treatment (specifically for cases unresponsive to conservative measures/antacids) and maintenance therapy.
- **Scleroderma Oesophagitis:** Treatment of oesophagitis associated with scleroderma.
- **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome:** Management of gastrinoma.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- Administration is not required to be timed with meals.
- **Acute Duodenal and Gastric Ulceration:** 150 mg twice daily (morning and bedtime) OR 300 mg once daily at bedtime. Healing typically occurs within 4 weeks, though some patients may require 8 weeks.
- **Maintenance (Ulcer):** 150 mg at bedtime.
    - *Note:* For duodenal ulcer maintenance in patients who continue to smoke, a higher dose of 300 mg at bedtime may provide additional benefit [Australian].
- **Reflux Oesophagitis:** 150 mg twice daily OR 300 mg at bedtime. In severe cases, 300 mg at bedtime may be used for up to 3 months.
- **Reflux Oesophagitis Maintenance:** 150 mg twice daily.
- **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome:** Initial dose of 150 mg three times daily. This may be titrated up to 600–900 mg/day based on clinical response.

### Administration Notes
- If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered, unless it is nearly time for the next dose.
- **Haemodialysis:** Clearance of ranitidine is increased during haemodialysis; dosing should be scheduled accordingly.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ranitidine hydrochloride or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Exclusion of Malignancy:** Treatment may mask symptoms of gastric carcinoma. Malignancy should be excluded before initiating therapy for gastric ulcers, particularly in middle-aged or elderly patients with new or recently changed dyspeptic symptoms.
- **Renal Impairment:** Ranitidine is primarily excreted renally. In patients with significant impairment, plasma levels are prolonged; dosage reduction is required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution as ranitidine is metabolised in the liver. While cirrhosis studies show minor changes in half-life, monitoring of ALT/AST is prudent during prolonged or high-dose therapy.
- **Pneumonia Risk:** Elevation of gastric pH may increase the risk of community-acquired pneumonia. This risk is notably higher in elderly patients, those with chronic lung disease, diabetes, or the immunocompromised.
- **Porphyria:** Avoid use in patients with a history of acute porphyria, as ranitidine may precipitate acute attacks.
- **Bradycardia:** Rarely reported with rapid intravenous administration (not typically applicable to tablets, but relevant to the active ingredient profile).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally rare, and a causal relationship is not always established.

| System Organ Class | Frequency/Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | **Rare:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, fever, bronchospasm, hypotension, chest pain. |
| **Central Nervous System** | **Very small proportion:** Severe headache. **Rare:** Malaise, dizziness, somnolence, insomnia, vertigo. **Predominantly in elderly/severely ill:** Reversible mental confusion, agitation, depression, and hallucinations. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Abdominal discomfort, nausea, constipation, diarrhoea. **Rare:** Acute pancreatitis. |
| **Haematological** | **Rare:** Leukopaenia, thrombocytopaenia (usually reversible). **Very Rare:** Agranulocytosis, pancytopaenia, marrow hypoplasia. |
| **Cardiovascular** | **Rare:** Bradycardia, AV block, asystole, tachycardia, premature ventricular beats. |
| **Hepatic** | Transient changes in LFTs. **Rare:** Hepatitis (hepatocellular or hepatocanalicular), with or without jaundice. |
| **Dermatological** | **Rare:** Rash, erythema multiforme, alopecia, vasculitis. |
| **Musculoskeletal** | **Rare:** Arthralgia, myalgia. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 System:** At therapeutic doses, ranitidine does not significantly inhibit the cytochrome P450 system and does not typically potentiate drugs like phenytoin, theophylline, or diazepam.
- **Warfarin:** Reports of altered prothrombin time exist; close monitoring is recommended.
- **pH-Dependent Absorption:** Increased gastric pH may increase the absorption of triazolam, midazolam, and glipizide, while decreasing the absorption of ketoconazole, atazanavir, delavirdine, and gefitinib.
- **Sucralfate:** High doses (2 g) of sucralfate may reduce ranitidine absorption. Administer ranitidine at least 2 hours prior to sucralfate.
- **Renal Tubular Competition:** High doses of ranitidine may reduce the excretion of procainamide and N-acetylprocainamide.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Ranitidine crosses the placenta. Safety has not been established; use only if considered essential by the clinician.
- **Breastfeeding:** Ranitidine is secreted in breast milk. The clinical significance is unevaluated; use only if essential [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats/rabbits) show no evidence of impaired fertility. Human data are not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited data available. Has been used in children aged 8 to 18 years at doses up to 150 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of mental confusion and depression. Malignancy must be excluded. Dose selection should be cautious due to the higher likelihood of decreased renal function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Accumulation occurs in renal failure. Dosage reduction is necessary based on creatinine clearance or serum level monitoring [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific oral dose reduction is required if renal function is normal, but caution is advised.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive antagonist of histamine at H₂-receptors. It inhibits basal and stimulated gastric acid secretion (induced by histamine, pentagastrin, and food).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Approximately 4 to 9 times more potent than cimetidine on a weight basis. A plasma concentration of 50–100 ng/mL effectively inhibits stimulated acid secretion by 50%.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 50% bioavailability. Peak plasma levels occur 2–3 hours post-oral dose. Absorption is not affected by food.
    - **Distribution:** 15% protein bound; large volume of distribution (96–142 L).
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised to N-oxide, S-oxide, and desmethylranitidine.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (tubular secretion). Terminal half-life is 2–3 hours. Half-life is prolonged (3–4 hours) in patients over 50 years of age due to declining renal function.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal clinical trial data summaries were not provided in the source documents. The source notes that controlled trials for up to 18 months did not reveal undue untoward effects.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging. In Australia, shelf life information is maintained on the ARTG public summary.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
