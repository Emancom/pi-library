# Editorial Product Information: Rosiglitazone and Metformin

## Editorial Note
- Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and US (Food and Drug Administration).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the clinical and regulatory profiles of the rosiglitazone/metformin combination for use by healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information or Prescribing Information required for clinical practice in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Rosiglitazone (as maleate) and Metformin hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Rosiglitazone is a thiazolidinedione (insulin sensitizer); Metformin is a biguanide.
- **Brand Names:** **AVANDAMET** [Australian], [US].
- **ATC Code:** A10BD03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Film-coated tablets in the following rosiglitazone/metformin HCl strengths:
    - 2 mg / 500 mg
    - 4 mg / 500 mg
    - 2 mg / 1000 mg
    - 4 mg / 1000 mg
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline [Australian]; Physicians Total Care, Inc. (Repackager) / GlaxoSmithKline [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM):** As an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycaemic control.
- **Specific Jurisdiction Criteria:**
    - [Australian]: Indicated as dual combination therapy for patients already treated with the combination, or those inadequately controlled on metformin or rosiglitazone monotherapy.
    - [US]: Indicated for adults who are already taking rosiglitazone, OR for those not already taking rosiglitazone who are unable to achieve control on other medications and, in consultation with a provider, have decided not to take pioglitazone or pioglitazone-containing products for medical reasons.
- **Limitations of Use:** Not indicated for Type 1 diabetes or diabetic ketoacidosis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Must be individualised based on the patient's current regimen. Administered in divided doses with meals to reduce gastrointestinal (GI) side effects associated with metformin.
- **Starting Dose:** Usually 4 mg/1000 mg daily (total daily dose). 
    - For patients on metformin monotherapy: Start with 4 mg rosiglitazone plus the existing metformin dose.
    - For patients on rosiglitazone monotherapy: Start with 1000 mg metformin plus the existing rosiglitazone dose.
- **Titration:**
    - Metformin component: Increase in increments of 500 mg/day every 1–2 weeks based on glycaemic response.
    - Rosiglitazone component: Increase in increments of 4 mg/day after 6–8 weeks (Australian) or 8–12 weeks (US) if glycaemic control is inadequate.
- **Maximum Dose:** 8 mg rosiglitazone / 2000 mg metformin daily.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered unless it is nearly time for the next dose. Do not double doses.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to rosiglitazone, metformin, or any excipients.
- **Cardiac Status:** Established NYHA Class III or IV heart failure.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - [Australian]: Renal failure or dysfunction (CrCl < 60 mL/min).
    - [US]: Renal disease or dysfunction suggested by serum creatinine ≥1.5 mg/dL (males) or ≥1.4 mg/dL (females), or abnormal CrCl.
- **Metabolic Acidosis:** Acute or chronic metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis, with or without coma.
- **Acute Conditions:** Conditions with the potential to alter renal function, such as dehydration, severe infection, or shock.
- **Diagnostic Procedures:** Temporary withholding is required for radiological studies involving intravascular administration of iodinated contrast materials.
- **Other:** Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:**
    - **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** Thiazolidinediones can cause or exacerbate CHF. Monitor for rapid weight gain, dyspnoea, or oedema. Discontinue if CHF is diagnosed.
    - **Myocardial Infarction (MI):** Meta-analysis suggests rosiglitazone may be associated with an increased risk of MI. Use is not recommended in patients with known ischaemic heart disease, particularly those taking nitrates [Australian], [US].
    - **Lactic Acidosis:** A rare but serious (often fatal) metabolic complication due to metformin accumulation. Risk factors include renal impairment, sepsis, and excessive alcohol intake.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Rosiglitazone has been rarely associated with hepatocellular dysfunction. Liver enzymes (ALT) should be checked prior to initiation and periodically thereafter. Do not initiate if ALT >2.5x ULN.
- **Oedema and Fluid Retention:** Use with caution in patients at risk of heart failure.
- **Bone Fractures:** Increased incidence of bone fracture has been observed, particularly in females (upper arm, hand, foot).
- **Macular Oedema:** Rare reports of new-onset or worsening diabetic macular oedema with decreased visual acuity.
- **Vitamin B12:** Long-term metformin use may decrease B12 absorption; annual monitoring is recommended.
- **Ovulation:** May result in resumption of ovulation in premenopausal anovulatory women with insulin resistance.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, loss of appetite (primarily related to metformin), upper respiratory tract infection.
- **Common (1–10%):** Anaemia, hypoglycaemia, headache, dizziness, fatigue, oedema, back pain, sinusitis, arthralgia.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Weight increase, hyperlipidaemia, constipation, anorexia.
- **Rare/Very Rare (<0.1%):** Lactic acidosis, macular oedema, hepatic dysfunction, skin reactions (erythema, pruritus, urticaria, angioedema), Stevens-Johnson syndrome [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP2C8 Inhibitors:** Gemfibrozil may significantly increase rosiglitazone plasma concentrations; a dose reduction of rosiglitazone may be necessary.
- **CYP2C8 Inducers:** Rifampin may decrease rosiglitazone concentrations; monitor glycaemic control closely.
- **Cationic Drugs:** Drugs eliminated by renal tubular secretion (e.g., digoxin, morphine, ranitidine, trimethoprim) may increase metformin levels.
- **OCT1/OCT2 Interactions [Australian]:** 
    - Inhibitors of OCT1 (e.g., verapamil) may reduce metformin efficacy.
    - Inducers of OCT1 (e.g., rifampicin) may increase metformin absorption.
    - Inhibitors of OCT2 (e.g., cimetidine, dolutegravir) may increase metformin concentrations.
- **Alcohol:** Potentiates the effect of metformin on lactate metabolism; avoid excessive intake.
- **Hyperglycaemic Agents:** Thiazides, corticosteroids, and phenothiazines may lead to loss of glycaemic control.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C** [Australian], [US]. 
    - Rosiglitazone crosses the placenta. Metformin also crosses the placenta.
    - Not recommended during pregnancy; insulin is preferred for maintaining blood glucose levels.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Rosiglitazone and metformin are excreted in the milk of lactating rats. It is unknown if they are excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** Rosiglitazone may cause hormonal imbalances in women [Australian] and reduced fertility in animal studies. Metformin showed no effect on fertility in animals.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years; use is not recommended.
- **Elderly Use:** Dosing should be conservative due to potential for decreased renal function. Metformin is contraindicated in those ≥80 years unless CrCl confirms normal renal function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in significant renal dysfunction (CrCl < 60 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended due to metformin's link to lactic acidosis in hepatic disease and rosiglitazone’s potential for hepatic effects.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Rosiglitazone:** A PPAR-gamma agonist that increases insulin sensitivity in adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, and the liver.
    - **Metformin:** A biguanide that decreases hepatic glucose production, decreases intestinal absorption of glucose, and improves insulin sensitivity by increasing peripheral glucose uptake.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rosiglitazone bioavailability ~99%; Metformin bioavailability 50–60%. Food delays Tmax but does not affect AUC.
    - **Distribution:** Rosiglitazone is 99.8% protein bound; Metformin is negligibly protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Rosiglitazone is extensively metabolised by CYP2C8 and CYP2C9; Metformin is excreted unchanged in the urine.
    - **Elimination:** Rosiglitazone half-life is 3–4 hours; Metformin plasma half-life is ~6.2 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ADOPT Trial:** A 4–6 year study comparing monotherapy (rosiglitazone vs. metformin vs. glibenclamide). Rosiglitazone showed a significantly longer time to monotherapy failure but was associated with weight gain, oedema, and increased fractures in women.
- **Combination Studies:** Trials (Studies 093, 094, 044) demonstrated that adding rosiglitazone to metformin significantly reduced HbA1c (by 0.8% to 1.2%) compared to metformin alone.
- **RECORD Trial [US-Reference]:** Evaluated cardiovascular outcomes, noting no significant difference in overall mortality but confirming risks of CHF and fractures.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Store below 30°C** [Australian].
- **Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C** [US].
- **Packaging:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **US Classification:** Rx-only.
- **Monitoring Status:** [Australian]: Subject to additional monitoring (Black Triangle Scheme).
