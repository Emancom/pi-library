# Editorial Product Information: Raloxifene

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinical overview for healthcare professionals by integrating regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Raloxifene hydrochloride (a selective oestrogen receptor modulator [SERM] of the benzothiophene class).
- **Brand Name(s):** Evista [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G03XC01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Film-coated tablets containing 60 mg raloxifene hydrochloride (equivalent to 56 mg free base) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Eli Lilly Australia Pty. Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Osteoporosis Management:** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of osteoporosis in postmenopausal women [Australian].
- **Invasive Breast Cancer Risk Reduction:**
    - Indicated to reduce the risk of invasive breast cancer in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis [Australian].
    - Indicated to reduce the risk of invasive breast cancer in postmenopausal women identified as being at high risk [Australian]. 
    - *Note:* High risk is defined by clinical factors such as prior biopsy showing LCIS or atypical hyperplasia, first-degree relatives with breast cancer, or a 5-year predicted risk >1.66% based on the modified Gail model [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard dose is one 60 mg tablet taken once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** May be administered at any time of day without regard to food [Australian].
- **Supplementation:** If dietary calcium or Vitamin D intake is inadequate, supplemental intake is recommended [Australian].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Continuous daily treatment is required to maintain clinical effect, as the drug does not accumulate in target tissues [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Thromboembolic Disorders:** Active or past history of venous thromboembolic events (VTE), including deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), and retinal vein thrombosis [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Childbearing Potential:** Contraindicated in women who are or may become pregnant [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to raloxifene or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- **Gender/Life Stage:** Contraindicated in males and premenopausal women [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Stroke Mortality:** In postmenopausal women with documented coronary heart disease or at high risk for coronary events, an increased risk of death due to stroke has been observed (2.2 vs 1.5 per 1000 women per year). Use caution when prescribing to patients with a history of stroke or significant stroke risk factors (e.g., TIA or atrial fibrillation) [Australian].
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Risk is increased, appearing similar to that associated with hormone replacement therapy. The risk is highest during the first four months of treatment. Discontinue therapy at least 72 hours prior to and during prolonged immobilisation (e.g., post-surgery, bed rest). Patients should be advised to move periodically during long travel [Australian].
- **Uterine Bleeding:** Raloxifene does not cause endometrial proliferation. Any unexplained uterine bleeding during therapy is considered abnormal and must be investigated [Australian].
- **Lipid Metabolism:** Use with caution in patients with a history of oral oestrogen-induced hypertriglyceridaemia (>5.6 mmol/L); serum triglycerides should be monitored in this population [Australian].
- **Breast Cancer:** Raloxifene is not effective in treating invasive breast cancer and has not been adequately studied in women with a prior history of breast cancer [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trial data:

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Hot flushes (most common in first 6 months), flu syndrome, infection, arthralgia, rhinitis [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Leg cramps, muscle spasms, headache, nausea, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, vomiting, peripheral oedema, weight gain, sinusitis, cough, rash, sweating [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Venous thromboembolic events (DVT, PE, retinal vein thrombosis), superficial vein thrombophlebitis [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Increased risk of fatal stroke in high-risk populations and increased risk of VTE [Australian].
- **Breast/Uterus:** Compared to HRT, raloxifene is associated with significantly lower rates of breast pain, tenderness, and vaginal bleeding [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cholestyramine:** Significantly reduces the absorption and enterohepatic cycling of raloxifene; co-administration is not recommended [Australian].
- **Warfarin/Coumarins:** Small decreases in prothrombin time have been observed. Monitor prothrombin time closely if these agents are used concurrently [Australian].
- **Ampicillin:** Reduces peak concentrations of raloxifene, but the overall extent of absorption is unaffected; they may be used together [Australian].
- **Highly Protein-Bound Drugs:** While raloxifene is highly protein-bound (98-99%), it did not affect the binding of phenytoin, tamoxifen, or warfarin in vitro [Australian].
- **Systemic Oestrogens:** Concurrent use has not been studied and is not recommended [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X [Australian]**. Raloxifene is strictly for postmenopausal use. Animal studies indicate foetal malformations and abnormalities in the reproductive system.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. In animal studies, raloxifene suppressed pup growth and impaired the fertility of female offspring [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for use in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustments are necessary for elderly patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution. While blood levels are not significantly affected in women with creatinine clearance as low as 21 mL/min, safety in severe renal impairment is limited [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended. Raloxifene is primarily metabolised in the liver; patients with cirrhosis have shown plasma concentrations approximately 2.5 times higher than controls [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A Selective Oestrogen Receptor Modulator (SERM). It acts as an oestrogen agonist in bone (reducing resorption) and on lipid metabolism (decreasing total and LDL cholesterol). It acts as an oestrogen antagonist in uterine and breast tissues [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (60%); however, absolute bioavailability is only 2.0% due to extensive presystemic glucuronidation [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Extensively distributed; 98-99% bound to plasma proteins [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via glucuronide conjugation in the liver; does not undergo cytochrome P450 metabolism [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted in faeces; less than 6% is excreted in urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Osteoporosis (MORE Trial):** A study of 7,705 postmenopausal women showed that 60 mg daily increased bone mineral density (BMD) by 2-3% and reduced the risk of vertebral fractures by up to 55% over three years [Australian].
- **Breast Cancer Risk (RUTH & STAR Trials):**
    - **RUTH Trial:** In 10,101 women, raloxifene reduced the incidence of invasive breast cancer by 44% (primarily ER-positive) but showed no reduction in ER-negative cancer or non-invasive cancer [Australian].
    - **STAR Trial:** In 19,747 women, raloxifene 60 mg/day was found to be comparable to tamoxifen 20 mg/day in reducing the risk of invasive breast cancer, with a similar safety profile [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C in a dry place [Australian].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze; protect from excessive heat and sunlight [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian]**.
