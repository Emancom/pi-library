# Editorial Product Information: Rocuronium Bromide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of rocuronium bromide for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was available for review; therefore, all information presented reflects the current Australian regulatory position.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rocuronium bromide (a quaternary aminosteroid).
- **Chemical Class:** Non-depolarising neuromuscular blocking agent.
- **Brand Names:** Rocuronium-hameln [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection: 50 mg/5 mL (10 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (bolus or continuous infusion).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** hameln pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Endotracheal Intubation:** Adjunct to general anaesthesia to facilitate intubation during routine induction (Adults and Paediatrics: term newborn to adolescent).
- **Muscle Relaxation:** To provide skeletal muscle relaxation and facilitate mechanical ventilation (Adults and Paediatrics: term newborn to adolescent).
- **Rapid Sequence Induction (RSI):** Facilitation of intubation when suxamethonium is contraindicated [Australian-approved for Adults only].
- **Intensive Care Unit (ICU):** Adjunct to facilitate mechanical ventilation [Australian-approved for Adults only].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Rocuronium must only be administered by clinicians experienced in the use of neuromuscular blocking agents. Dosage must be individualised based on the anaesthetic technique, surgical duration, and patient-specific factors (e.g., age, comorbidities). Neuromuscular monitoring is strongly recommended.

### Surgical Procedures (Adults)
- **Tracheal Intubation (Routine):** 0.6 mg/kg.
- **Rapid Sequence Induction (RSI):** 1.0 mg/kg [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dosing:** 0.15 mg/kg. This should be reduced to 0.075–0.1 mg/kg during long-term inhalational anaesthesia. Maintenance should ideally be administered when twitch height recovers to 25%.
- **Continuous Infusion:** 
    - Loading dose: 0.6 mg/kg.
    - Rate: 0.3–0.6 mg/kg/h under intravenous anaesthesia; 0.3–0.4 mg/kg/h under inhalational anaesthesia.

### ICU Use (Adults)
- **Loading Dose:** 0.6 mg/kg.
- **Initial Infusion Rate:** 0.3–0.6 mg/kg/h during the first hour. Rates must be titrated to effect (maintaining 80-90% blockade) and typically decrease over the following 6–12 hours.

### Administration Instructions
- **Physical Compatibility:** Compatible with 0.9% NaCl, 5% glucose (with or without saline), sterile water for injections, Compound Sodium Lactate, and Haemaccel.
- **Incompatibilities:** Documented incompatibility with amoxicillin, amphotericin B, azathioprine, cefazolin, cloxacillin, dexamethasone, diazepam, erythromycin, enoximone, famotidine, furosemide, hydrocortisone sodium succinate, insulin, Intralipid, methohexital, methylprednisolone, prednisolone sodium succinate, thiopentone sodium, trimethoprim, and vancomycin.
- **Medication Safety:** To prevent accidental administration (which can be fatal), store the vial with the cap and ferrule intact [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to rocuronium bromide, the bromide ion, or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Respiratory Paralysis:** Rocuronium causes paralysis of respiratory muscles; mandatory ventilatory support is required until spontaneous respiration is restored.
- **Medication Errors:** Accidental administration can lead to fatal outcomes. Ensure proper labeling and selection in critical care environments.
- **Anaphylaxis:** Severe, sometimes fatal, anaphylactic reactions can occur. Cross-reactivity between different neuromuscular blockers has been reported.
- **Residual Curarisation:** Clinicians must monitor for residual block, especially in elderly patients or those with complicating clinical factors. Reversal with sugammadex or acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (e.g., neostigmine) should be considered.
- **Long-term ICU Use:** Use for more than 48 hours should be avoided to prevent prolonged paralysis or skeletal muscle weakness.
- **Myopathy:** Reported in ICU patients receiving both neuromuscular blockers and corticosteroids.
- **Neuromuscular Disease:** Use with extreme caution in patients with myasthenia gravis or Eaton-Lambert syndrome due to profound sensitivity.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Injection site pain/reaction (up to 16% in RSI with propofol), tachycardia (paediatrics: 1.4%). |
| **Uncommon/Rare (<1%)** | Hypotension, rash, urticaria, prolonged neuromuscular block. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, bronchospasm, flaccid paralysis, circulatory collapse/shock, malignant hyperthermia, angioedema. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, fatal respiratory arrest (if not ventilated), and Kounis syndrome [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Increased Effect (Potentiation)
- **Anaesthetics:** Halogenated volatile anaesthetics (potentiate maintenance dosing and inhibit reversal).
- **Antibiotics:** Aminoglycosides, lincosamides, polypeptides, and acylamino-penicillin antibiotics.
- **Other Medications:** Diuretics, quinidine/quinine, magnesium salts, calcium channel blockers, lithium, local anaesthetics (e.g., IV lidocaine), and acute phenytoin or β-blockers.
- **Prior Use:** Suxamethonium used for intubation prior to rocuronium administration.

### Decreased Effect (Antagonism)
- Chronic administration of corticosteroids, phenytoin, or carbamazepine.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B2]. Animal studies (rats) showed no embryotoxicity at doses of 0.3 mg/kg. Human data are lacking; use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Detected in the milk of lactating rats, but human data are unavailable. Use only when the clinician determines the benefits justify the risks.
- **Fertility:** Studies have not been conducted.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved from term newborn infants to adolescents. RSI is **not recommended** in paediatrics due to limited experience [Australian]. Children may require higher infusion rates than adults.
- **Elderly Use:** Patients ≥65 years may have a prolonged duration of action and an increased risk of residual curarisation. Standard intubation dose (0.6 mg/kg) applies, but maintenance doses should be reduced (0.075–0.1 mg/kg).
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Prolonged duration of action is observed due to reduced clearance. Titration to effect is essential.
- **Obesity:** Dosing should be based on **lean body mass** if the patient's weight is >30% above ideal body weight.
- **Burns:** Resistance to non-depolarising agents can develop; doses should be titrated to response.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive antagonist at nicotinic acetylcholine receptors at the motor end-plate of striated muscle.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Intermediate-acting; fast onset. ED90 is approximately 0.3 mg/kg [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is ~203 mL/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily recovered as the parent compound (50%).
    - **Elimination:** Three-phase elimination. Mean half-life is ~73 minutes. Approximately 40% is excreted in urine within 24 hours, and 43% in faeces over 9 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RSI Studies:** Pivotal studies (n=681) compared 0.6 mg/kg and 1.0 mg/kg rocuronium with 1.0 mg/kg suxamethonium. While 1.0 mg/kg rocuronium provided acceptable intubating conditions in ~93–96% of patients within 60 seconds, "excellent" conditions were more frequent with suxamethonium.
- **ICU Studies:** Studies (n=95) evaluated rocuronium for up to 7 days to facilitate mechanical ventilation. Variability in infusion requirements was high, particularly in patients with organ failure.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2–8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Ex-refrigerator Storage:** May be stored at room temperature (up to 30°C) for a maximum of 12 weeks. Once removed from the refrigerator, the product must be used or discarded within 12 weeks.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry on packaging. Diluted infusions are stable for 24 hours.
