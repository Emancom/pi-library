# Editorial Product Information: Rivaroxaban

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of rivaroxaban for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rivaroxaban
- **Chemical Class:** Antithrombotic agent; highly selective direct Factor Xa inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** **APO-RIVAROXABAN** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B01AF01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Film-coated (immediate-release) tablets in 2.5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg, and 20 mg strengths [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Rivaroxaban** is indicated for the following:
- Prevention of venous thromboembolism (VTE) in adult patients undergoing major orthopaedic surgery of the lower limbs (elective total hip or knee replacement) [Australian-approved].
- Prevention of stroke and systemic embolism in patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF) who possess at least one additional stroke risk factor [Australian-approved].
- Treatment of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE), and the prevention of recurrent DVT and PE [Australian-approved].
- Prevention of major cardiovascular events (composite of stroke, myocardial infarction, and cardiovascular death) in patients with coronary artery disease (CAD) and/or peripheral artery disease (PAD), used in combination with aspirin [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **VTE Prevention (Surgery):** 10 mg once daily. Initial dose taken 6–10 hours post-surgery once haemostasis is established. Duration: 5 weeks (hip replacement) or 2 weeks (knee replacement) [Australian].
- **Stroke Prevention (NVAF):** 20 mg once daily. For moderate/severe renal impairment (CrCl 15–49 mL/min), reduce dose to 15 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Treatment of DVT/PE:** Initially 15 mg twice daily for the first 3 weeks, followed by 20 mg once daily for continued treatment and prevention of recurrence [Australian].
- **CAD/PAD Prevention:** 2.5 mg twice daily in combination with 100 mg aspirin once daily [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **2.5 mg and 10 mg tablets:** May be taken with or without food.
- **15 mg and 20 mg tablets:** Must be taken **with food**.
- **Crushed Tablets:** Tablets may be crushed and mixed with water or apple sauce for oral use. If the 15 mg or 20 mg tablets are crushed, the dose must be followed immediately by food. Crushed tablets are stable for administration via gastric tubes (PVC or silicone) [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- **2.5 mg BID:** Continue with the next regular dose; do not double the dose.
- **10, 15, or 20 mg QD:** Take the dose immediately on the same day and return to the once-daily schedule the following day.
- **15 mg BID (Initial DVT/PE treatment):** Take the missed dose immediately to ensure a total intake of 30 mg per day; two 15 mg tablets may be taken at once [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to rivaroxaban or any excipients (e.g., lactose).
- Clinically significant active bleeding (e.g., intracranial, gastrointestinal).
- Lesions or conditions at high risk of major bleeding (e.g., spontaneous impairment of haemostasis).
- Significant hepatic disease associated with coagulopathy and clinically relevant bleeding risk, including Child-Pugh B and C [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment (CrCl < 15 mL/min) or patients undergoing dialysis [Australian].
- Concomitant systemic treatment with strong inhibitors of both CYP3A4 and P-gp (e.g., HIV protease inhibitors like ritonavir, or azole antimycotics like ketoconazole).
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haemorrhagic Risk:** Like all anticoagulants, rivaroxaban increases the risk of serious or fatal bleeding. Use with caution in patients with an increased risk of bleeding (e.g., uncontrolled hypertension, recent GI ulceration, vascular retinopathy).
- **Spinal/Epidural Haematoma:** Patients undergoing neuraxial anaesthesia or spinal puncture are at risk of developing an epidural or spinal haematoma, which can result in long-term paralysis. Indwelling catheters should not be removed until at least 2 half-lives (18–26 hours) after the last dose [Australian].
- **Surgery and Interventions:** Stop 10, 15, or 20 mg doses at least 24 hours before a procedure. Stop 2.5 mg doses at least 12 hours before. Restart as soon as haemostasis is established [Australian].
- **Prosthetic Heart Valves:** Use is not recommended in patients who have recently undergone transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) or those with other prosthetic valves due to lack of safety/efficacy data [Australian].
- **Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS):** Not recommended for patients with a history of thrombosis diagnosed with APS, particularly triple-positive patients, due to increased rates of recurrent thrombotic events compared to Vitamin K antagonists (VKAs) [Australian].
- **Dermatological Reactions:** Discontinue at the first appearance of severe skin rash (e.g., Stevens-Johnson syndrome) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trial data:
- **Common (1–10%):** Anaemia, dizziness, headache, epistaxis, GI haemorrhage, nausea, constipation, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, pruritus, rash, pain in extremity, and post-procedural haemorrhage [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Thrombocytosis, allergic reaction, cerebral/intracranial haemorrhage, syncope, tachycardia, hypotension, and liver function abnormalities [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Cholestasis, hepatitis, jaundice, and muscle haemorrhage [Australian].
- **Post-marketing:** Agranulocytosis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, and anticoagulant-related nephropathy [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Rivaroxaban is a substrate of CYP3A4, CYP2J2, and P-gp/Bcrp transporters.
    - **Strong Inhibitors (CYP3A4 and P-gp):** Concomitant use with ritonavir or ketoconazole is contraindicated as it significantly increases plasma concentrations. Fluconazole has a lesser effect and may be co-administered with caution [Australian].
    - **Strong Inducers (CYP3A4):** Rifampicin, phenytoin, and St. John’s Wort may reduce rivaroxaban plasma concentrations and should be used with caution [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Use with caution alongside NSAIDs, platelet aggregation inhibitors (e.g., clopidogrel), and SSRIs/SNRIs due to additive bleeding risks.
- **Conversion from/to VKAs:** Monitor INR closely during transitions. Rivaroxaban can independently elevate INR, so measurements are most reliable at least 24 hours after the last dose during transition to VKA [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Rivaroxaban is contraindicated in pregnancy due to the risk of placental haemorrhage and evidence that the drug crosses the placenta. Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Animal data (rats) indicate secretion into milk; safety in human infants has not been established [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on male or female fertility were observed in animal studies at doses up to 200 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for use in children under 18 years due to a lack of safety and efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required based on age alone; however, clinical caution is advised as renal function typically declines with age [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild (CrCl 50–80 mL/min):** No dose adjustment.
    - **Moderate (CrCl 30–49 mL/min):** Use with caution; adjust NVAF dose to 15 mg QD.
    - **Severe (CrCl 15–29 mL/min):** Use with caution; plasma levels may be increased 1.6-fold.
    - **CrCl < 15 mL/min:** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with Child-Pugh B or C hepatic disease associated with coagulopathy. No dose adjustment required for other hepatic diseases [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective, direct Factor Xa inhibitor. It interrupts the intrinsic and extrinsic pathways of the blood coagulation cascade by inhibiting free and clot-bound Factor Xa, thereby preventing thrombin generation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak concentrations (Cmax) in 2–4 hours. Absolute bioavailability is high (80–100%) for the 10 mg dose. Bioavailability for the 20 mg dose is 66% if fasting but increases to ~100% when taken with food.
    - **Distribution:** High plasma protein binding (92–95%), primarily to albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Approx. 2/3 of the dose undergoes metabolic degradation (CYP3A4/2J2); 1/3 is excreted unchanged renally.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 5–9 hours in young adults and 11–13 hours in the elderly [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RECORD 1-3:** Demonstrated superiority of 10 mg QD rivaroxaban over enoxaparin 40 mg QD in preventing VTE after hip and knee replacement.
- **ROCKET-AF:** A non-inferiority study showing 20 mg QD (15 mg in renal impairment) was as effective as warfarin in preventing stroke/systemic embolism in NVAF.
- **EINSTEIN DVT/PE/Extension:** Established efficacy and safety for treating acute DVT/PE and long-term prevention of recurrence compared to enoxaparin/VKA or placebo.
- **COMPASS:** Demonstrated that 2.5 mg BID plus aspirin 100 mg QD reduced major CV events in CAD/PAD more effectively than aspirin alone.
- **GALILEO:** Highlighted safety concerns (increased mortality/bleeding) in patients with prosthetic valves (TAVR), leading to recommendations against use in this population [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Handling:** Crushed tablets are stable in water or apple sauce for immediate use.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
