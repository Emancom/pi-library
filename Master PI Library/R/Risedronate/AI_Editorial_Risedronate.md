# Editorial Product Information: Risedronate Sodium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Risedronate sodium (a third-generation pyridinyl bisphosphonate).
- **Brand Names:** Actonel, Actonel Once-a-Month [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M05BA07.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 5 mg, 30 mg, and 150 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Theramex Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Treatment of Osteoporosis:** For postmenopausal women and men [Australian].
- **Treatment of Glucocorticoid-Induced Osteoporosis:** Specifically for patients initiating or continuing systemic corticosteroid therapy (prednisone equivalent >7.5 mg/day) [Australian].
- **Preservation of Bone Mineral Density (BMD):** In patients on long-term corticosteroid therapy [Australian].
- **Paget’s Disease of Bone:** Treatment to induce remission (normalisation of serum alkaline phosphatase) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration Instructions
To ensure adequate absorption and minimise oesophageal irritation:
- **Timing:** Take at least 30 minutes before the first food, beverage (other than plain water), or other medication of the day.
- **Fluid:** Take only with plain water. Note that mineral water or hard water with high calcium concentrations may reduce absorption.
- **Positioning:** Swallow tablets whole (do not chew or suck) while in an upright position (sitting or standing). Do not lie down for at least 30 minutes after dosing.

### Osteoporosis
- **Daily Regimen:** 5 mg orally once daily [Australian].
- **Monthly Regimen:** 150 mg orally once a month, taken on the same date each month [Australian].
- **Missed Dose (150 mg):** If a dose is missed, it should be taken the morning after it is remembered, unless the next scheduled dose is within 7 days. If within 7 days, skip the missed dose and resume the original schedule.

### Paget’s Disease
- **Standard Dose:** 30 mg orally once daily for 2 months [Australian].
- **Retreatment:** May be considered after a 2-month post-treatment observation period if relapse occurs or if alkaline phosphatase fails to normalise.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to risedronate sodium or any excipients.
- **Hypocalcaemia:** Must be corrected prior to therapy initiation.
- **Inability to stay upright:** Inability to stand or sit upright for at least 30 minutes [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Upper Gastrointestinal Irritation:** Bisphosphonates may cause oesophagitis, gastritis, and ulcerations. Use with caution in patients with active upper GI disorders or a history of strictures/achalasia.
- **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ):** Generally associated with tooth extraction or local infection. A dental examination with preventive dentistry is recommended prior to treatment in patients with risk factors (e.g., cancer, chemotherapy, corticosteroids, poor oral hygiene).
- **Atypical Femoral Fractures:** Long-term use (>3 years) has been associated with low-energy stress fractures of the proximal femoral shaft. Patients should report any new thigh, hip, or groin pain.
- **Ocular Inflammation:** Cases of iritis and uveitis have been reported. Orbital inflammation has also been noted in post-marketing data [Australian].
- **Osteonecrosis of the External Auditory Canal:** Reported with long-term bisphosphonate therapy; consider in patients with chronic ear infections or ear symptoms.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are generally mild to moderate and rarely require discontinuation.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, dyspepsia, nausea, diarrhoea, constipation, musculoskeletal pain, headache, dizziness, hypertension, flu-like symptoms. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Gastritis, duodenitis, glossitis, iritis. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Abnormal liver function tests, atypical femoral fractures. |
| **Very Rare (Post-marketing)** | Osteonecrosis of the jaw, uveitis, orbital inflammation, severe skin reactions (angioedema, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome). |

- **Laboratory Findings:** Small, asymptomatic decreases in serum calcium and phosphate levels may occur.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Polyvalent Cations:** Medications containing calcium, magnesium, iron, or aluminium (e.g., antacids, supplements) interfere with absorption. These must be taken at a different time of day.
- **NSAIDs and Aspirin:** While clinical trials showed no significant increase in upper GI adverse events when used with risedronate, caution is advised during concomitant use due to the shared risk of GI irritation.
- **H2 Blockers/PPIs:** No significant interactions noted in clinical trials.
- **Metabolism:** Risedronate is not systemically metabolised and does not induce or inhibit Cytochrome P450 enzymes.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3 [Australian]**. There are no adequate data in pregnant women. Risedronate should only be used if the potential benefit justifies the risk. Animal studies indicate maternal hypocalcaemia and foetal ossification delays at high doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if risedronate is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the importance of the medication to the mother.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients over 65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild to moderate (CrCl 30–60 mL/min): No adjustment required.
    - Severe (CrCl < 30 mL/min): **Not recommended** due to limited clinical data [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No studies performed; however, risedronate is not metabolised by the liver, so adjustments are unlikely to be needed.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Risedronate binds to bone hydroxyapatite and inhibits osteoclast-mediated bone resorption. This reduces bone turnover and increases bone mass and strength.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid (tmax ~1 hour). Oral bioavailability is very low (~0.63%).
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is 6.3 L/kg; protein binding is ~24%.
    - **Metabolism:** No evidence of systemic metabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately half of the absorbed dose is excreted in the urine. Unabsorbed drug is eliminated in the faeces. The terminal half-life is approximately 480 hours, reflecting dissociation from the bone surface.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Postmenopausal Osteoporosis (RVN and RVE Trials):** Pivotal three-year studies demonstrated that 5 mg daily risedronate reduced the risk of new vertebral fractures by 41–49% and non-vertebral fractures by 36% compared to placebo.
- **Therapeutic Equivalence:** The 150 mg monthly and 35 mg weekly doses (where available) have been shown to be therapeutically non-inferior to the 5 mg daily regimen based on BMD increases at the lumbar spine.
- **Paget’s Disease:** Risedronate 30 mg daily for 2 months was significantly more effective than etidronate 400 mg daily for 6 months in normalising serum alkaline phosphatase (77% vs 10.5%).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Typically supplied in PVC/Aluminium foil blister strips.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
