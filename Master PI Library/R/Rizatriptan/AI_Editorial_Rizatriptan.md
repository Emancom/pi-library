# Editorial Product Information: Rizatriptan

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing or dispensing in a particular region. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rizatriptan benzoate (a selective 5-hydroxytryptamine1B/1D [5-HT1B/1D] receptor agonist).
- **Chemical Class:** Triptan.
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Rizatriptan ODT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N02CC04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Orally disintegrating tablet (ODT) 10 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Treatment of Migraine:** Indicated for the acute treatment of migraine attacks with or without aura [Australian].
- **Important Limitation:** Not intended for the prophylactic therapy of migraine or for use in the management of hemiplegic or basilar migraine [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The recommended single dose is 10 mg.
- **Re-dosing:** 
    - If the migraine returns within 24 hours, a second dose may be taken, provided there is at least a 2-hour interval between doses.
    - If a patient does not respond to the first dose, the effectiveness of a second dose for the same attack has not been established in controlled trials.
- **Maximum Dose:** Not to exceed 30 mg in any 24-hour period [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - The ODT should be kept in the blister pack until immediately before use.
    - With dry hands, place the tablet on the tongue where it dissolves and is swallowed with saliva; administration with liquid is unnecessary.
- **Special Dosing (Propranolol):** For patients concurrently taking propranolol, the 10 mg dose of rizatriptan is inappropriate as plasma concentrations are increased; alternative therapies should be considered [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to rizatriptan or any excipients (including aspartame) [Australian].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use or use within 2 weeks of discontinuing MAO-A inhibitors or non-selective MAOIs [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** 
    - Established coronary artery disease (CAD), including ischemic heart disease (angina pectoris, history of myocardial infarction, or documented silent ischemia).
    - Prinzmetal's angina.
    - Uncontrolled hypertension.
- **Cerebrovascular/Peripheral Vascular Disease:**
    - History of stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA).
    - Peripheral vascular disease, including ischemic bowel disease [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Diagnosis Confirmation:** Only for use where a clear diagnosis of migraine has been established.
- **Cardiovascular Events:** Rare but serious coronary events (e.g., myocardial ischemia or infarction) may occur. Cardiovascular assessment is recommended for patients with multiple risk factors for CAD (e.g., hypertension, diabetes, smoking, strong family history) before initiating therapy [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening serotonin syndrome has been reported, particularly when used concomitantly with SSRIs or SNRIs. Symptoms include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular aberrations [Australian].
- **Medication Overuse Headache (MOH):** Overuse of acute migraine treatments (>10 days/month) may lead to exacerbation of headache. Detoxification may be required [Australian].
- **Atypical Headaches:** Not for use in "atypical" headaches associated with potentially serious conditions (e.g., stroke, ruptured aneurysm) where cerebrovascular vasoconstriction may be harmful [Australian].
- **Phenylketonurics:** The ODT formulation contains aspartame (8.8 mg per 10 mg tablet) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse experiences are typically mild and transient. The following frequencies are based on Phase III clinical trials [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, somnolence, asthenia/fatigue, nausea, dry mouth, headache, paraesthesia, pharyngeal discomfort, chest pain, flushing. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Abdominal pain, palpitation, tachycardia, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, thirst, neck pain, stiffness, muscle weakness, insomnia, tremor, vertigo, dyspnoea, pruritus, sweating, blurred vision, hot flashes. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Syncope, hypertension. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects (Post-marketing):** Myocardial ischemia/infarction, cerebrovascular accident, seizures, toxic epidermal necrolysis, anaphylaxis, angioedema, and ischemic colitis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAO-A Inhibitors:** Significant increase in rizatriptan plasma concentrations. Use is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Propranolol:** Increases rizatriptan AUC by approximately 70% and Cmax by 44%. Concomitant use with 10 mg rizatriptan is not recommended [Australian].
- **Ergotamine-type medications:** Potential for additive vasospastic effects; separate doses by at least 6 hours [Australian].
- **Other 5-HT1B/1D Agonists:** Do not use concomitantly with other triptans [Australian].
- **SSRIs/SNRIs:** Risk of serotonin syndrome [Australian].
- **St John’s Wort:** May result in pharmacodynamic interactions increasing CNS serotonin; use with caution [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1.** 
    - There are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies showed reduced pup birth weight at high doses. Use only if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Rizatriptan is excreted in the milk of lactating rats at levels 6 times higher than maternal plasma. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk; caution should be exercised [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed altered oestrus cyclicity and mating delays at very high exposures (215 times human MRDD) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Children (<12 years):** No data available; use not recommended [Australian].
    - **Adolescents (12–17 years):** Efficacy not established in controlled trials; use not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics are similar to younger adults, but clinical experience is limited in patients ≥65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** In haemodialysis patients, AUC is approximately 44% higher than in those with normal function. No significant difference for creatinine clearance 10–60 mL/min [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Plasma concentrations in patients with mild-to-moderate alcoholic cirrhosis were similar to healthy subjects [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective agonist at 5-HT1B and 5-HT1D receptors. It induces vasoconstriction of dilated intracranial extracerebral arteries and inhibits the release of vasoactive neuropeptides from trigeminal sensory nerve endings, reducing neurogenic inflammation [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and completely absorbed. Bioavailability: 40–45%. Tmax: 1–1.5 hours (tablet), 1.6–2.5 hours (ODT/wafer). Food delays absorption by ~1 hour [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Minimal plasma protein binding (14%).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via oxidative deamination by MAO-A to an inactive indole acetic acid metabolite.
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is 2–3 hours. Approximately 80% is excreted in urine (14% as unchanged drug) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Established in four multicenter, randomised, placebo-controlled trials. Rizatriptan 5 mg and 10 mg consistently provided significantly higher headache response rates at 2 hours compared to placebo. 
- **Symptoms:** Rizatriptan also reduced migraine-associated nausea, photophobia, and phonophobia [Australian].
- **ODT Formulation:** A specific trial for the ODT/wafer formulation showed a 2-hour response rate of approximately 66% compared to 47% for placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Do not remove ODT from the blister until ready for administration to prevent moisture degradation.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
