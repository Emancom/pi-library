# Editorial Product Information: Revefenacin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document was developed using Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States**: US Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. For full local requirements, consult the current approved PI in your region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Revefenacin.
- **Chemical Class**: Long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA); anticholinergic.
- **Brand Names**: 
    - **Yupelri** [US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources (Suggested: R03BB08).
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: 
    - **Inhalation Solution**: 175 mcg revefenacin per 3 mL sterile, clear, colourless, aqueous solution in unit-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral inhalation via nebulizer.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Mylan Specialty L.P. (a Viatris Company); licensed from Theravance Biopharma [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)**: Indicated for the maintenance treatment of patients with COPD [US-approved].
- **Note on Use**: Not indicated for the relief of acute bronchospasm or for use in children [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: 175 mcg (one unit-dose vial) administered once daily.
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - For oral inhalation use only. **Do NOT swallow or inject.**
    - Must be administered via a standard jet nebulizer connected to an air compressor using a mouthpiece.
    - Safety and efficacy using non-compressor-based nebulizer systems have not been established [US].
    - The vial should be removed from the foil pouch and opened **immediately before use**. 
    - Discard any vial or residual content after use.
- **Compatibility**: Physical and chemical compatibility, safety, and efficacy of mixing revefenacin with other drugs in a nebulizer have not been established [US].
- **Missed Dose**: Not specifically detailed in sources; however, the daily dose should not exceed one unit-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to revefenacin or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Deterioration of Disease/Acute Episodes**: Should not be initiated in patients during acutely deteriorating or life-threatening episodes of COPD. Do not use as rescue therapy for acute symptoms. Acute symptoms must be treated with an inhaled, short-acting beta$_2$-agonist (SABA).
- **Paradoxical Bronchospasm**: Like other inhaled medicines, revefenacin can produce life-threatening paradoxical bronchospasm. If this occurs, treat immediately with a SABA, discontinue revefenacin, and initiate alternative therapy.
- **Worsening of Narrow-Angle Glaucoma**: Use with caution in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma. Patients should be monitored for signs of acute glaucoma (e.g., eye pain, blurred vision, visual halos).
- **Worsening of Urinary Retention**: Use with caution in patients with prostatic hyperplasia or bladder-neck obstruction.
- **Immediate Hypersensitivity Reactions**: If signs of an allergic reaction occur (e.g., rash, swelling, breathing difficulties), therapy should be stopped immediately.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Adverse Effects ($\geq$2%)** [US]:
    - Cough
    - Nasopharyngitis
    - Upper respiratory tract infection
    - Headache
    - Back pain
- **Other Adverse Effects (1% to <2%)**: Hypertension, dizziness, oropharyngeal pain, and bronchitis.
- **Postmarketing Experience**: Dry mouth has been reported [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Paradoxical bronchospasm, worsening of narrow-angle glaucoma, worsening of urinary retention, and immediate hypersensitivity reactions.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticholinergics**: Potential for additive interaction. Avoid coadministration with other anticholinergic-containing medications (e.g., tiotropium, ipratropium) as this may increase the risk of antimuscarinic adverse effects.
- **Transporter-Related Interactions**: Revefenacin’s active metabolite is a substrate of OATP1B1 and OATP1B3.
    - **OATP1B1 and OATP1B3 Inhibitors**: (e.g., rifampicin, cyclosporine) May increase systemic exposure of the active metabolite. Coadministration is **not recommended** [US].
- **Cytochrome P450**: Neither revefenacin nor its metabolite inhibits or induces major CYP450 isoforms.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Category**: No specific letter category (PLLR compliance); however, there are no adequate or well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal data showed no evidence of fetal harm at exposures up to 209 times (rats) and 694 times (rabbits) the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: There is no information on the presence of revefenacin in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. In animal studies, revefenacin and its metabolite were present in the milk of lactating rats [US].
- **Fertility**: No effects on male or female fertility were observed in rat studies at doses up to 30 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness have not been established. Revefenacin is not indicated for use in children [US].
- **Elderly Use**: No dosage adjustment is required in patients aged 65 years and older. While clinical trials showed no overall differences in safety or efficacy, increased sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: No dosage adjustment is required. Patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min) should be monitored for systemic antimuscarinic side effects due to increased exposure (up to 2.5-fold increase in the active metabolite) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: **Avoid use** in patients with any degree of hepatic impairment. Systemic exposure to the active metabolite was significantly increased (up to 4.7-fold) in subjects with moderate hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: A long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA) with similar affinity for muscarinic receptor subtypes M$_1$ to M$_5$. Bronchodilation is achieved through the inhibition of M$_3$ receptors in the smooth muscle of the airways.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Rapidly absorbed following inhalation; C$_{max}$ reached within 14–41 minutes. Absolute oral bioavailability is low (<3%).
    - **Distribution**: Extensive distribution to tissues (steady-state V$_d$ of 218 L). Protein binding is approximately 71% for the parent drug and 42% for the metabolite.
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily metabolised via hydrolysis of the primary amide to a carboxylic acid, forming the major active metabolite. This metabolite has lower activity (1/3 to 1/10) than the parent drug but higher systemic exposure.
    - **Elimination**: Terminal half-life is approximately 22–70 hours. Excretion is primarily faecal (54% after IV; 88% after oral dose), with minimal renal excretion (<1%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies** [US]:
    - **Efficacy**: Two 12-week, replicate, Phase 3, placebo-controlled trials in 1,229 patients with moderate to very severe COPD.
    - **Primary Outcome**: Significant improvement in trough FEV$_1$ at Day 85. The LS mean difference from placebo was 146 mL and 147 mL in the respective trials.
    - **Safety**: A 52-week, open-label, active-controlled (tiotropium) trial in 1,055 patients demonstrated a safety profile consistent with the 12-week studies.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage**: Store at room temperature between 20°C and 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
- **Handling**: Protect from direct sunlight and excessive heat. Vials must be stored in the protective foil pouch and used immediately upon opening.
- **Shelf Life**: Not explicitly stated in years, but must not be used after the expiration date on the pouch/vial [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification**: 
    - **United States**: Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **Poison Schedule**: Not specified in available sources (Standard LAMA classification usually applies in other regions, e.g., Australian S4).
