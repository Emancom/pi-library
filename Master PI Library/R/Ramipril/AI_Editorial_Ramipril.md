# Editorial Product Information: Ramipril

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ramipril for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI approved for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to their local regulatory authority's current approved documentation for definitive prescribing guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ramipril
- **Chemical Class:** Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) inhibitor; 2-aza-bicyclo [3.3.0]-octane-3-carboxylic acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Ramipril [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C09AA05
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Capsules: 1.25 mg, 2.5 mg, 5 mg, and 10 mg [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
[All listed indications are Australian-approved]
- **Hypertension:** Treatment of essential hypertension. (Note: Data are currently insufficient to support use in renovascular hypertension).
- **Post-Myocardial Infarction (MI) Heart Failure:** Treatment of patients showing clinical signs of heart failure following the acute phase of an MI.
- **Nephropathy:** Prevention of progressive renal failure in patients with persistent proteinuria (> 1 g/day).
- **Cardiovascular Risk Reduction:** Reduction of the risk of MI, stroke, cardiovascular death, or the need for revascularisation in:
    - Patients $\ge$ 55 years with clinical evidence of coronary artery disease, stroke, or peripheral vascular disease.
    - Diabetic patients $\ge$ 55 years with at least one additional cardiovascular risk factor (hypertension, elevated total cholesterol, low HDL, smoking, or microalbuminuria).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Capsules should be swallowed whole with a generous amount of fluid. They may be taken before, during, or after meals [Australian].

### Hypertension
- **Initial Dose:** 2.5 mg once daily for patients not on diuretics. 
- **Titration:** Increase at 2–3 week intervals based on response.
- **Maintenance:** Usually 2.5 mg to 5 mg once daily; maximum dose is 10 mg once daily.
- **Diuretic-treated patients:** If possible, discontinue the diuretic 2–3 days before starting ramipril. If the diuretic cannot be stopped, initiate ramipril at 1.25 mg once daily.

### Post-MI Heart Failure
- **Initial Dose:** 5 mg daily, divided into two doses (2.5 mg twice daily). 
- **Poor Tolerance:** If 5 mg is not tolerated, start with 1.25 mg twice daily for two days.
- **Titration:** Dose may be doubled at 1–3 day intervals.
- **Maximum Dose:** 10 mg daily in divided doses.
- **Timing:** Initiate 2–10 days post-MI once the patient is haemodynamically stable.

### Progressive Renal Failure (Proteinuria > 1 g/day)
- **Initial Dose:** 1.25 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** Double the dose at 2–3 week intervals.
- **Maximum Dose:** No efficacy data available for doses above 5 mg/day in this population.

### Cardiovascular Risk Reduction
- **Initial Dose:** 2.5 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** Double the dose after one week, then increase to 10 mg after a further three weeks based on tolerability.
- **Maintenance:** 10 mg once daily.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to ramipril, other ACE inhibitors, or any excipients.
- **Angioedema:** History of hereditary, idiopathic, or previous ACE inhibitor-associated angioedema.
- **Renal Artery Stenosis:** Haemodynamically relevant bilateral renal artery stenosis or unilateral stenosis in a single kidney.
- **Haemodynamic Instability:** Use in hypotensive or haemodynamically unstable states.
- **Cardiovascular Impediment:** Significant left ventricular inflow or outflow obstruction (e.g., aortic or mitral stenosis).
- **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Contraindicated in pregnant or breastfeeding women.
- **Extracorporeal Treatments:** Treatments leading to contact between blood and negatively charged surfaces (e.g., high-flux dialysis membranes like AN69 or LDL apheresis with dextran sulfate) due to risk of anaphylactoid reactions.
- **Dual Blockade of RAAS:** 
    - Concomitant use with aliskiren in patients with diabetes or moderate-to-severe renal impairment (CrCl < 60 mL/min).
    - Concomitant use with Angiotensin II Receptor Antagonists (AIIRAs) in patients with diabetic nephropathy.
- **Sacubitril/Valsartan:** Concomitant use or use within 36 hours of the last dose of sacubitril/valsartan.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Angioedema:** May involve the face, extremities, or upper airway (laryngeal oedema can be fatal). Intestinal angioedema (presenting as abdominal pain) has also been reported. Discontinue therapy immediately if angioedema occurs.
- **Hypotension:** Risk is highest during treatment initiation, especially in salt/volume-depleted patients, those on diuretics, or those with heart failure.
- **Renal Effects:** Deterioration of renal function may occur; monitor renal function closely, particularly in the first few weeks of therapy.
- **Neutropenia/Agranulocytosis:** Bone marrow depression and reduced white cell counts have been reported. Monitor WBC in patients with collagen vascular disease or renal impairment.
- **Cough:** A persistent, non-productive dry cough is common (2–15%) and resolves upon discontinuation.
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Risk is increased in patients with renal impairment, diabetes, or those using potassium-sparing diuretics/supplements. Regular electrolyte monitoring is recommended.
- **Surgery/Anaesthesia:** ACE inhibitors may block secondary angiotensin II formation; hypotension occurring during surgery can be corrected by volume expansion.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild and transient.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (> 2%)** | Nausea, dizziness, headache, dry cough, symptomatic hypotension, fatigue, chest pain, muscle cramps, myalgia, and increased serum potassium. |
| **Uncommon ($\le$ 2%)** | Vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, rash, pruritus, dermatitis, syncope, palpitations, orthostatic hypotension, and peripheral oedema. |
| **Rare (< 0.1%)** | Angioedema (may be fatal), cholestatic jaundice, liver damage, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, haemolytic anaemia, and Stevens-Johnson syndrome. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylactic reactions, angioedema, acute renal failure, and agranulocytosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Sacubitril/Valsartan:** Contraindicated due to increased angioedema risk.
- **Dual RAAS Blockade (Aliskiren, AIIRAs):** Increased risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia, and renal impairment.
- **Potassium-Sparing Diuretics/Supplements/Heparin:** May lead to significant hyperkalaemia; monitor serum potassium.
- **Lithium:** ACE inhibitors reduce lithium excretion, increasing the risk of toxicity.
- **NSAIDs:** May reduce the antihypertensive effect of ramipril and increase the risk of renal deterioration and hyperkalaemia.
- **Antidiabetics (Insulin, Sulphonylureas):** Ramipril may increase insulin sensitivity, potentially leading to hypoglycaemia.
- **Vildagliptin/mTOR inhibitors (e.g., temsirolimus):** Increased risk of angioedema.
- **Alcohol:** Potentiation of vasodilation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Contraindicated. ACE inhibitors cause foetal and neonatal morbidity/mortality (hypotension, skull hypoplasia, renal failure) when used in the second and third trimesters. First-trimester exposure is associated with an increased risk of congenital malformations.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. While ramipril levels in breast milk are low after a 10 mg dose, the active metabolite (ramiprilat) is excreted in animal milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended if ramipril is necessary.
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Start at a lower dose (1.25 mg daily) as some patients may be more responsive to ACE inhibition.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - CrCl 20–50 mL/min: Initial dose 1.25 mg; maximum dose 5 mg daily.
    - CrCl < 20 mL/min: Insufficient experience; use with extreme caution.
    - Haemodialysis: Ramipril is not suitable for patients on haemodialysis as it is poorly dialysable.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Metabolism of ramipril to ramiprilat is delayed. Start under close supervision; dose must not exceed 2.5 mg daily.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ramipril is a pro-drug converted to the active metabolite, **ramiprilat**. It inhibits ACE (Kininase II), preventing the conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II (a potent vasoconstrictor) and reducing aldosterone secretion. It also inhibits the degradation of bradykinin (a vasodilator).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** At least 50–60% absorbed; peak plasma levels of ramipril reached within 1 hour. Food reduces the rate but not the extent of absorption.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily cleaved in the liver to ramiprilat (6x more potent than ramipril).
    - **Distribution:** Serum protein binding is ~73% (ramipril) and ~56% (ramiprilat).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 60% excreted in urine, 40% in faeces.
    - **Half-life:** Ramiprilat has a triphasic decline; the effective half-life for multiple dosing is 13–17 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **AIRE Study:** Evaluated 2000 patients with post-MI heart failure. Ramipril showed a 27% risk reduction in all-cause mortality over 15 months.
- **REIN Study:** Focused on non-diabetic nephropathy (proteinuria $\ge$ 1 g/day). Ramipril significantly reduced the rate of GFR decline and the risk of end-stage renal failure.
- **HOPE Study:** Included > 9,200 patients at high cardiovascular risk. Ramipril 10 mg once daily significantly reduced the composite endpoint of MI, stroke, and cardiovascular death (RR 0.78).
- **ACE II Study:** Demonstrated that ramipril maintains renal health and retards proteinuria in patients with overt diabetic nephropathy.
