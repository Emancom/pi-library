# Editorial Product Information: Rilonacept

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA). 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of rilonacept for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinical reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Rilonacept.
- **Chemical Class**: Interleukin-1 (IL-1) blocker; dimeric fusion protein (cytokine trap) consisting of the ligand-binding domains of the human IL-1 receptor component (IL-1R1) and IL-1 receptor accessory protein (IL-1RAcP) linked to the Fc portion of human IgG1.
- **Brand Name**: **Arcalyst** [US].
- **ATC Code**: L04AC04.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Lyophilized powder for injection (220 mg in a single-dose vial) [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Kiniksa Pharmaceuticals (UK), Ltd. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cryopyrin-Associated Periodic Syndromes (CAPS)**: Including Familial Cold Autoinflammatory Syndrome (FCAS) and Muckle-Wells Syndrome (MWS) in adults and paediatric patients 12 years and older [US-approved].
- **Deficiency of Interleukin-1 Receptor Antagonist (DIRA)**: Maintenance of remission in adults and paediatric patients weighing 10 kg or more [US-approved].
- **Recurrent Pericarditis (RP)**: Treatment of RP and reduction in risk of recurrence in adults and paediatric patients 12 years and older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **CAPS and RP**: 
    - **Loading Dose**: 320 mg (delivered as two separate 160 mg [2 mL] injections on the same day at different sites).
    - **Maintenance Dose**: 160 mg (one 2 mL injection) once weekly.
- **DIRA**: 
    - **Maintenance Dose**: 320 mg once weekly (delivered as two 160 mg injections at different sites).

### Paediatric Dosing
- **CAPS and RP (Ages 12 to 17 years)**:
    - **Loading Dose**: 4.4 mg/kg (maximum 320 mg), administered as one or two injections.
    - **Maintenance Dose**: 2.2 mg/kg (maximum 160 mg) once weekly.
- **DIRA (Weight $\ge$ 10 kg)**:
    - **Maintenance Dose**: 4.4 mg/kg (maximum 320 mg) once weekly.

### Administration Instructions
- For subcutaneous use only.
- Reconstitute with 2.3 mL of preservative-free Sterile Water for Injection to achieve a concentration of 80 mg/mL.
- Rotate injection sites (abdomen, thigh, or upper arm). Avoid areas that are bruised, red, or tender.
- Reconstituted solution is viscous, clear, and colorless to pale yellow.

### Missed Dose
- Administer within 7 days of the missed dose and resume the original schedule. 
- If more than 7 days have passed, administer the dose and start a new weekly schedule based on that date.

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serious Infections**: IL-1 blockade may increase the risk of serious, life-threatening infections. Treatment should not be initiated in patients with active or chronic infections. Discontinue if a serious infection develops.
- **Tuberculosis (TB)**: Risk of TB reactivation is possible. Evaluate patients for latent TB before initiation and follow current guidelines for treatment.
- **TNF Inhibitors**: Concomitant use with TNF-blocking agents is not recommended due to a potentially increased risk of serious infections and neutropenia.
- **Malignancy**: The impact on the development of malignancies is unknown; immunosuppressants may increase this risk.
- **Lipid Profile Changes**: Increases in total cholesterol, LDL, and triglycerides have been observed. Monitor lipid profiles and consider lipid-lowering therapy if indicated.
- **Hypersensitivity**: Discontinue treatment if a hypersensitivity reaction occurs.
- **Immunizations**: Avoid live vaccines during treatment. Complete all recommended vaccinations (e.g., pneumococcal, influenza) prior to starting therapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequently reported adverse reactions include:
- **Very Common (>10%)**: 
    - Injection-site reactions (e.g., erythema, swelling, pruritus, pain) [48% in CAPS; 60% in RP].
    - Upper respiratory tract infections (URTI) [26% in CAPS].
- **Common (1–10%)**: 
    - Sinusitis, cough, nausea, diarrhea, stomach discomfort, urinary tract infection, and hypoesthesia [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects**: 
    - Serious infections (bronchitis, bacterial meningitis, *Mycobacterium intracellulare* infection).
    - Transient neutropenia (observed with high intravenous doses in unapproved indications).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **TNF-Blocking Agents and other IL-1 Blockers**: Concomitant use with agents such as etanercept, infliximab, adalimumab, or anakinra is not recommended due to increased infection risk [US].
- **Cytochrome P450 Substrates**: Chronic inflammation suppresses CYP450 enzyme formation via cytokines like IL-1. Rilonacept may "normalize" CYP450 activity. Monitor patients on narrow therapeutic index drugs (e.g., warfarin, cyclosporine) as dose adjustments may be required upon starting rilonacept.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: [US: No category assigned under current labeling; however, animal data noted]. Animal studies in monkeys showed increased incidences of skeletal variations (lumbar ribs) at doses 2x the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD). Rilonacept should be used only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding**: Data on the presence of rilonacept in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant are unavailable. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother's clinical need.
- **Fertility**: No effects on fertility or reproductive performance were observed in animal studies using a murine analog.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**:
    - CAPS/RP: Approved for ages 12 and older. Safety/efficacy not established below age 12.
    - DIRA: Approved for weights $\ge$ 10 kg. Safety/efficacy not established for weights < 10 kg.
    - Growth and development should be monitored in paediatric patients.
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 65 years)**: Efficacy and safety appeared generally similar to younger adults, though clinical trial numbers were limited.
- **Renal Impairment**: No specific pharmacokinetic studies have been conducted [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No specific pharmacokinetic studies have been conducted [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Rilonacept acts as a soluble decoy receptor ("cytokine trap") that binds to IL-1$\alpha$ and IL-1$\beta$, preventing them from interacting with cell-surface receptors. It also binds the IL-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1ra).
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Reduces inflammatory markers, including C-Reactive Protein (CRP) and Serum Amyloid A (SAA). In RP, it is associated with a median time to CRP normalization of 7 days.
- **Pharmacokinetics**: 
    - **Steady State**: Reached by 6 weeks in CAPS and 2 weeks in RP.
    - **Trough Levels**: Approximately 23–24 mcg/mL in adults.
    - **Elimination**: Circulation half-life is approximately 7 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CAPS**: A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study showed significant reductions in symptom scores (joint pain, rash, fever, etc.) and inflammatory markers (CRP, SAA) compared to placebo.
- **DIRA**: A 2-year open-label study of 6 paediatric patients demonstrated maintenance of remission (no fever, rash, or bone pain) in all subjects who switched from anakinra.
- **Recurrent Pericarditis (RHAPSODY)**: A Phase 3 randomized withdrawal study demonstrated a 96% reduction in the risk of RP recurrence (HR 0.04) compared to placebo. Median time to treatment response was 5 days.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F).
- **Light Protection**: Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Stability after Reconstitution**: May be kept at room temperature but must be protected from light and used within 3 hours.
- **Shelf Life**: Do not use beyond the expiration date stamped on the label.
