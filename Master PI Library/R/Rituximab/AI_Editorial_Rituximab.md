# Editorial Product Information: Rituximab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for rituximab, specifically the biosimilar **RIXIMYO**. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rituximab (a genetically engineered chimeric murine/human monoclonal antibody)
- **Chemical Class:** Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody (IgG1 kappa immunoglobulin)
- **Brand Names:** **RIXIMYO** (Biosimilar to **MABTHERA**) [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01FA01 (derived from class)
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated solution for intravenous (IV) infusion; 100 mg/10 mL and 500 mg/50 mL single-use vials (10 mg/mL concentration).
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Rituximab is indicated for the treatment of:
- **Non-Hodgkin’s Lymphoma (NHL):** 
    - Previously untreated Stage III/IV follicular, B-cell NHL (CD20 positive).
    - Relapsed or refractory low grade or follicular, B-cell NHL (CD20 positive).
    - Diffuse large B-cell NHL (CD20 positive) in combination with chemotherapy.
- **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL):** CD20 positive CLL in combination with chemotherapy.
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):** Severe, active RA in adults with inadequate response or intolerance to at least one TNF inhibitor; used in combination with methotrexate.
- **Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis (GPA/Wegener’s) and Microscopic Polyangiitis (MPA):** Severely active GPA and MPA in combination with glucocorticoids for the induction of remission.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- **RIXIMYO** must be administered as an IV infusion in an environment with full resuscitation facilities under close medical supervision. 
- **Do not administer as an IV push or bolus.**
- Premedication with an analgesic/antipyretic (e.g., paracetamol) and an antihistamine should be given 30–60 minutes before every infusion. Glucocorticoids should be considered, and are mandatory in certain indications (e.g., RA).

### Specific Dosing by Indication [Australian]
- **Follicular NHL (Relapsed/Refractory):** 375 mg/m² weekly for 4 weeks (monotherapy) or Day 1 of each chemotherapy cycle (combination).
- **Follicular NHL (Maintenance):** 375 mg/m² every 3 months (relapsed/refractory) or every 2 months (previously untreated) for up to 2 years.
- **Diffuse Large B-cell NHL:** 375 mg/m² on Day 1 of each chemotherapy cycle for up to 8 cycles.
- **CLL:** 375 mg/m² in Cycle 1, followed by 500 mg/m² on Day 1 of Cycles 2–6.
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Two 1000 mg IV infusions separated by 2 weeks.
- **GPA/MPA:** 375 mg/m² once weekly for 4 weeks.

### Infusion Rates
- **First Infusion:** Start at 50 mg/h. If tolerated, escalate by 50 mg/h every 30 minutes to a maximum of 400 mg/h.
- **Subsequent Infusions:** Start at 100 mg/h. Increase by 100 mg/h every 30 minutes to a maximum of 400 mg/h.
- **Faster Schedule (NHL/CLL/RA):** A 90-minute (NHL/CLL) or 120-minute (RA) infusion may be considered for subsequent cycles if the first dose was well-tolerated.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to rituximab, murine proteins, or any excipients.
- Active, severe infections.
- Severely immunocompromised state.
- Severe heart failure (NYHA Class IV) or severe, uncontrolled cardiac disease (specifically for RA, GPA, and MPA indications) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML)
- Rituximab use is associated with an increased risk of fatal PML (an opportunistic viral brain infection).
- Monitor for new or worsening neurological symptoms. If PML is suspected, suspend treatment immediately. If confirmed, discontinue permanently.

### General Warnings
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs):** Severe, sometimes fatal, IRRs can occur, typically within 30–120 minutes of the first infusion. Symptoms include hypoxia, pulmonary infiltrates, and acute respiratory distress.
- **Hepatitis B Reactivation:** Fatalities have been reported. Screening for HBsAg and HBcAb is mandatory before treatment. Active disease is a contraindication.
- **Tumor Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Reported in patients with high tumor burden or high numbers of circulating malignant cells (>25 x 10⁹/L). Ensure adequate hydration and uricostatics.
- **Severe Mucocutaneous Reactions:** Rare cases of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), some fatal, have been reported. Discontinue if suspected.
- **Cardiovascular Events:** Hypotension may occur; consider withholding antihypertensives 12 hours prior to infusion. Monitor patients with a history of cardiac disease.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Bacterial/viral infections, neutropenia, leucopenia, IRRs (fever, chills), nausea, pruritus, rash, asthenia, headache, decreased IgG levels. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Sepsis, pneumonia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, hyperglycaemia, peripheral oedema, dizziness, paraesthesia, hypertension, bronchospasm, vomiting, alopecia. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML).
- Severe Infusion-Related Reactions.
- Hepatitis B Reactivation.
- Tumor Lysis Syndrome.
- Stevens-Johnson Syndrome/Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis.
- Bowel obstruction and perforation in NHL patients.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Data:** Currently limited. In CLL, co-administration with fludarabine or cyclophosphamide did not alter the PK of either drug. Co-administration with methotrexate did not affect rituximab PK in RA patients.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Use caution when combining with other DMARDs or biological agents in RA, as the safety of these combinations (other than methotrexate) is not well-defined.
- **Vaccination:** Safety of live viral vaccines has not been studied; vaccination with live virus vaccines is not recommended.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Rituximab causes B-cell depletion in the fetus. It should only be given to pregnant women if the benefit outweighs the potential risk. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for 12 months after treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Rituximab is excreted in breast milk. Due to the potential for B-cell depletion in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 6 months post-treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established. Hypogammaglobulinaemia has been observed in children treated with rituximab.
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** No dose adjustment is required. In GPA/MPA studies (RAVE), older patients showed an increased risk of serious adverse events.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established. In GPA/MPA, significant renal impairment was associated with an increased risk of adverse events.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Rituximab binds specifically to the CD20 antigen on pre-B and mature B lymphocytes. It mediates B-cell lysis through complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), and induction of apoptosis.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment results in a rapid and marked decline in peripheral B-cell counts. In NHL/CLL, recovery usually begins within 6 months, though it may take 12 months or longer.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** The terminal elimination half-life is approximately 22 days in NHL patients and 20.8 days in RA patients. Clearance is influenced by body surface area (BSA) and B-cell burden.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Biosimilarity:** **RIXIMYO** demonstrated clinical equivalence to the reference medicine (MabThera) in pivotal trials for follicular NHL (GP13-301) and RA (GP13-201), showing comparable efficacy, safety, and immunogenicity.
- **RAVE Study (GPA/MPA):** Demonstrated non-inferiority of rituximab to cyclophosphamide for the induction of complete remission at 6 months.
- **REFLEX Study (RA):** Showed significant improvement in ACR20 response rates and reduction in joint damage progression when added to methotrexate.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze). Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Unopened Vials:** May be stored at temperatures up to 30°C for a single period of up to 7 days [Australian].
- **Diluted Solution:** Prepared infusion solution should be used immediately. If necessary, it can be stored at 2°C to 8°C for up to 24 hours.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
