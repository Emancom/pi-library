# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the following jurisdiction-specific Product Information (PI): **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (also known as L-carnitine). It is a naturally occurring amino acid derivative and a carrier molecule involved in fatty acid metabolism.
- **Chemical Class:** Trimethylammonium-substituted butyrate.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 or 4 hours; should not be administered less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].

### Monitoring
- It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating therapy.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occurred within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic administration of high oral doses in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]), which are normally excreted in the urine [US]. *Note: The safety of the oral formulation has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency.*

## 6. Adverse Effects
### General
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
- **Less Frequent:** Body odor, gastritis.

### ESRD Clinical Trial Data (≥5% Frequency)
In placebo-controlled trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a rate of 5% or higher, regardless of causality [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), flu syndrome (25–29%), headache (22%), chest pain (12–15%), fever (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), anorexia (3–6%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).

### Postmarketing Experience
- Seizures and hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis) have been identified during post-approval use [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specifically assigned a letter category in the current PI, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Recommendation:** Should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after administration, the risk to a human infant must be weighed against the benefits of treatment for the mother. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See *Dosage and Administration* for weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources, though trials included ESRD patients which often encompass older populations.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of the **oral** formulation have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For **IV** use in ESRD, see specific dosing instructions in Section 3 [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L by week 8 [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed a dose-proportional increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. While concentrations increased, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation lacks preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Scheduled as a human prescription drug [US].
