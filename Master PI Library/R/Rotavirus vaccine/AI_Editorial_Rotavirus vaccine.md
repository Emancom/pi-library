# Editorial Product Information: Rotavirus Vaccine, Live, Oral, Pentavalent (RotaTeq)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource. It is not a substitute for the official PI and should not be used as the sole basis for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the local approved labelling for the most current information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rotavirus vaccine, live, oral, pentavalent. Each 2 mL dose contains five live human-bovine reassortant rotaviruses (G1, G2, G3, G4, and P1A[8]).
- **Brand Name:** **RotaTeq** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J07BH02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral solution. Each single dose is 2 mL.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral use only. Must NOT be injected.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prevention of Rotavirus Gastroenteritis:** Indicated for the prevention of rotavirus gastroenteritis caused by types G1, G2, G3, G4, and G9 [US].
- **Approved Age Range:** Approved for use in infants aged 6 weeks to 32 weeks [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Infants):** A 3-dose series of 2 mL each.
- **Schedule:** 
  - **First Dose:** Administer between 6 and 12 weeks of age.
  - **Subsequent Doses:** Administer at 4 to 10-week intervals.
  - **Maximum Age:** The third (final) dose should not be administered after 32 weeks of age.
- **Administration Instructions:**
  - For oral administration only.
  - **No restrictions** on the consumption of food or liquid (including breast milk) before or after vaccination.
  - **Incomplete Dose:** If an infant spits out or regurgitates the vaccine, a replacement dose is not recommended. The series should continue with the remaining scheduled doses.
- **Preparation:** Available in two presentations (Ampule and Tube). Both consist of a squeezable plastic container for direct oral delivery. The dispenser tip must be cleared of fluid before opening.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to the vaccine or any component. Infants exhibiting symptoms of hypersensitivity after a dose should not receive subsequent doses [US].
- **Severe Combined Immunodeficiency Disease (SCID):** Contraindicated in infants with SCID. Postmarketing reports indicate severe gastroenteritis and prolonged viral shedding in these patients [US].
- **History of Intussusception:** Contraindicated in infants with a history of intussusception [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Management of Allergic Reactions:** Appropriate medical treatment (e.g., epinephrine) must be available to manage potential anaphylaxis.
- **Immunocompromised Populations:** 
  - No clinical data exists for potentially immunocompromised infants (e.g., HIV/AIDS, blood dyscrasias, or those on high-dose systemic corticosteroids).
  - Immunosuppressive therapies (irradiation, antimetabolites, cytotoxic drugs) may reduce the immune response to the vaccine.
- **Intussusception:** Postmarketing observational studies in the US have shown an increased risk of intussusception, particularly within the first 7 days following the first dose, and up to 21 days [US]. 
- **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** Caution is advised for infants with a history of GI disorders (active illness, chronic diarrhea, failure to thrive, or abdominal surgery).
- **Shedding and Transmission:** Vaccine virus shedding in stools has been documented (highest after Dose 1: 8.9%). Transmission to non-vaccinated contacts has been reported postmarketing. Caution is advised near immunodeficient contacts.
- **Febrile Illness:** Vaccination may be delayed in the presence of serious febrile illness. Low-grade fever or mild upper respiratory infection is not a contraindication.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Frequency Data (from Clinical Trials):**
  - **Very Common (>10%):** Diarrhea (24.1%), Vomiting (15.2%), Otitis media (14.5%), Elevated temperature ≥100.5°F (17.1%–20.0% depending on dose).
  - **Common (1–10%):** Nasopharyngitis (6.9%), Irritability (4.3%–7.1%), Bronchospasm (1.1%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
  - **Intussusception:** Observed in temporal association post-approval.
  - **Hematochezia:** Reported in <0.1% as a serious experience.
  - **Seizures:** Reported in <0.1% as serious experiences; includes febrile seizures.
- **Postmarketing Reports:** Anaphylactic reaction, urticaria, angioedema, Kawasaki disease, and gastroenteritis associated with viral shedding in SCID patients.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Concomitant Vaccines:** Clinical trials support the simultaneous administration of **RotaTeq** with DTaP, IPV, Hib, Hepatitis B vaccine, and pneumococcal conjugate vaccine. No evidence of reduced antibody response to the concomitant vaccines was observed [US].
- **Immunosuppressants:** May reduce the vaccine’s immunogenic response.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not applicable. The vaccine is not approved for individuals 32 weeks of age or older. No human or animal data available [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding was permitted in clinical trials. There are no restrictions on breastfeeding before or after vaccination [US].
- **Fertility:** Not evaluated for potential to impair fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are not established for infants <6 weeks or >32 weeks of age.
- **Pre-term Infants:** Clinical data supports use in pre-term infants (25 to 36 weeks gestational age). Dosing should follow age in weeks since birth.
- **GERD:** Clinical data supports use in infants with controlled gastroesophageal reflux disease.
- **Elderly/Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not applicable to the indicated pediatric population.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **RotaTeq** is a live viral vaccine that replicates in the small intestine, inducing immunity. The exact immunologic mechanism of protection is unknown.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** After a 3-dose regimen, 92.9% to 100% of recipients achieved a 3-fold or more rise in serum anti-rotavirus IgA [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Efficacy (Study 006):** A large trial involving ~70,000 infants.
  - **Efficacy against any grade of G1-G4 rotavirus gastroenteritis:** 74.0% (95% CI: 66.8, 79.9).
  - **Efficacy against severe gastroenteritis:** 98.0% (95% CI: 88.3, 100.0).
  - **Reduction in hospitalisations:** 95.8% for G1-G4 types.
- **Durability:** Protection was shown to persist through a second rotavirus season.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store and transport refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from light.
- **Handling:** Administer as soon as possible after removal from refrigeration. Discard in approved biological waste containers.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
  - **United States:** Rx-only.
