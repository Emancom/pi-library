# Editorial Product Information: Rasburicase

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises complex clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rasburicase (recombinant urate-oxidase enzyme)
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant enzyme produced by a genetically modified *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* strain. It is a tetrameric protein with identical subunits (approx. 34 kDa).
- **Brand Name:** **Fasturtec** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** V03AF07
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Powder for injection. Each vial contains 1.5 mg of rasburicase (equivalent to 18.2 EAU per mg). [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi-aventis Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hyperuricaemia Management:** Indicated for the treatment and prophylaxis of acute hyperuricaemia in patients with haematological malignancies who are at risk of rapid tumour lysis. [Australian]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Children):** The recommended dose is 0.20 mg/kg/day. [Australian]
- **Duration of Treatment:** Typically varies between 5 and 7 days.
- **Initiation Timing:** 
    - In patients not yet hyperuricaemic: Start chemotherapy within 24 hours of the first rasburicase dose.
    - In hyperuricaemic patients: Start chemotherapy within 48 hours of the first rasburicase dose. [Australian]
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer via a 30-minute intravenous infusion in 50 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride solution.
    - **Do NOT use an in-line filter.**
    - Use a separate infusion line from chemotherapeutic agents. If a separate line is unavailable, flush the line thoroughly with saline between infusions.
    - No dose adjustment is required for patients with renal or hepatic impairment. [Australian]
- **Preparation:**
    - Reconstitute with the provided solvent (1 mL per 1.5 mg vial). Mix by swirling gently; do not shake.
    - Dilute further only in 0.9% sodium chloride. **Do NOT use glucose solutions** due to potential incompatibility. [Australian]

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to rasburicase, other uricases, or any excipients. [Australian]
- **Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency** and other cellular metabolic disorders known to cause haemolytic anaemia. Hydrogen peroxide is a byproduct of rasburicase activity, which may induce haemolytic anaemia in these patients. [Australian]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rasburicase may induce allergic responses, including anaphylaxis or anaphylactic shock (potentially fatal). Monitor closely for skin reactions or bronchospasm. Discontinue permanently if severe reactions occur. [Australian]
- **Methaemoglobinaemia:** Reported in patients receiving rasburicase. Discontinue treatment immediately and permanently if it develops. [Australian]
- **Haemolysis:** Reported in patients; treatment must be permanently discontinued if haemolysis occurs. [Australian]
- **Monitoring Requirements:** 
    - Closely monitor patients for uric acid levels, but also for hyperphosphataemia, hyperkalaemia, and hypocalcaemia, as rasburicase does not directly treat these other components of tumour lysis syndrome.
- **Uric Acid Sample Handling (CRITICAL):** To prevent *ex vivo* degradation of uric acid in the test tube, blood must be collected into pre-chilled heparinised tubes, immediately immersed in an ice/water bath, centrifuged at 4°C, and analysed within 4 hours. [Australian]

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event frequency is based on clinical trials and post-marketing data [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Fever |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vomiting, nausea, allergic reactions (mainly rash and urticaria) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Diarrhoea, headache, bronchospasm, convulsions, haemolysis |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Rhinitis, hypotension, anaphylaxis/anaphylactic shock |
| **Not Known** | Involuntary muscle contractions |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylactic shock, haemolysis (particularly in G6PD deficiency), and methaemoglobinaemia. [Australian]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No *in vivo* studies conducted. Rasburicase does not appear to inhibit or induce cytochrome P450 isoforms in animal models. [Australian]
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** In vitro, it does not metabolise 6-mercaptopurine, cytarabine, or methotrexate.
- **Specific Clinical Note:** There is no data to support the sequential use of rasburicase and allopurinol. [Australian]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. No clinical data on exposed pregnancies. Teratogenic effects observed in animal studies (rabbits and rats) at high doses. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety unknown. It is unknown if rasburicase is excreted in human milk; therefore, it should not be used in breastfeeding women. [Australian]
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility observed in rat models. [Australian]

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited pharmacokinetic data suggests clearance is approximately 35% higher in children/adolescents than in adults, resulting in lower systemic exposure. [Australian]
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in provided sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is necessary. [Australian]

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Rasburicase is a potent uricolytic agent that catalyses the enzymatic oxidation of uric acid into allantoin. Allantoin is water-soluble and easily excreted by the kidneys. This process generates hydrogen peroxide stoichiometrically. [Australian]
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces plasma uric acid levels rapidly (within 4 hours of the first dose). [Australian]
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Steady State:** Achieved by Day 2–3.
    - **Volume of Distribution:** 110–127 mL/kg (comparable to physiological vascular volume).
    - **Elimination Half-life:** Approximately 19 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to occur via peptide hydrolysis (protein degradation). Renal elimination is a minor pathway. [Australian]

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Phase II Studies (ACT2511, ACT2694):** Evaluated doses of 0.15 mg/kg and 0.20 mg/kg. Uric acid levels fell rapidly after the first dose, with mean percentage reductions of approximately 84–88% within 4 hours. [Australian]
- **Phase III Study (EFC2975):** Compared rasburicase (0.20 mg/kg) to allopurinol. Rasburicase achieved significantly faster control of uric acid (4 hours vs 24 hours for allopurinol) and prevented further deterioration of renal function from calcium/phosphorus precipitation. [Australian]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). **Do not freeze.** [Australian]
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Contains no preservative. Reconstitute immediately prior to dilution. The diluted solution should be infused immediately and must not be stored longer than 24 hours at 2-8°C. [Australian]

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
