# Editorial Product Information: Retapamulin

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of retapamulin for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Retapamulin (a semisynthetic pleuromutilin antibiotic).
- **Brand Names:** ALTABAX [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Ointment, 1% (10 mg retapamulin per 1 gram of ointment). Available in 5, 10, 15, and 30 gram tubes [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline; distributed by Physicians Total Care, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Impetigo:** Indicated for the topical treatment of impetigo caused by *Staphylococcus aureus* (methicillin-susceptible isolates only) or *Streptococcus pyogenes* [US].
- **Patient Population:** Approved for use in adults and paediatric patients aged 9 months and older [US].
- **Scope of Treatment:** Indicated for affected areas up to 100 cm² total area in adults or 2% total body surface area in paediatric patients [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Paediatrics ≥9 months):** Apply a thin layer of ointment to the affected area twice daily for 5 days [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - The treated area may be covered with a sterile bandage or gauze dressing if desired.
    - Hands should be washed after application unless the hands are the area being treated.
    - For external use only; avoid contact with eyes, mouth, nose, or female genital area.
- **Dosing Limits:** Treatment should not exceed 100 cm² in adults or 2% total body surface area in paediatric patients [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Monitoring:** If no clinical improvement is observed within 3 to 4 days, the patient should be re-evaluated [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **General:** None listed in provided US documentation. (Note: Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients is a standard clinical contraindication for topical agents).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Local Irritation:** In the event of sensitization or severe local irritation, treatment must be discontinued, the ointment wiped off, and alternative antibacterial therapy instituted [US].
- **Mucosal Use:** Retapamulin is not intended for oral, intranasal, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use. Safety on mucosal surfaces has not been evaluated [US].
- **Microbial Overgrowth:** Prolonged use may promote the selection of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi. If superinfection occurs, appropriate therapy should be initiated [US].
- **Drug Resistance:** To prevent the development of drug-resistant bacteria, retapamulin should only be used to treat infections proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible bacteria [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile was assessed in 2,115 patients (adult and paediatric) using a 5-day, twice-daily regimen. Drug-related adverse events occurred in 5.5% of patients [US].

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Application site irritation (1.4% in adults), Application site pruritus (1.9% in paediatrics), Headache (1.2–2.0%), Nasopharyngitis (1.2–1.5%), Diarrhea (1.4–1.7%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Application site pain, erythema, contact dermatitis, eczema, pyrexia, pruritus (general) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Not specifically highlighted as common occurrences in provided sources; however, severe local irritation and sensitization are noted as reasons for discontinuation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Co-administration of oral ketoconazole (200 mg BID) increased retapamulin systemic exposure (AUC and Cmax) by 81% in healthy adults with abraded skin. However, due to the consistently low systemic exposure following topical application, dosage adjustments are not required when used with CYP3A4 inhibitors [US].
- **Metabolism:** In vitro studies suggest retapamulin is unlikely to affect the metabolism of other P450 substrates [US].
- **Concurrent Topical Products:** The effect of applying retapamulin concurrently with other topical products to the same site has not been studied [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category B.**
    - Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed maternal toxicity and decreased fetal weight at high systemic doses (oral/IV), but no treatment-related malformations. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if retapamulin is excreted in human milk. Caution is advised, as many drugs are excreted in breast milk. Safety during lactation has not been established [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in male or female rats at oral doses up to 450 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients aged 9 months to 17 years. Safety and efficacy were found to be similar to adults. Use in infants younger than 9 months has not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** In clinical trials, 234 patients were ≥65 years. No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed compared to younger patients; dose adjustments are not required [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Retapamulin selectively inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by interacting with the 50S subunit of the bacterial ribosome. Specifically, it binds to ribosomal protein L3 in the P site and peptidyl transferase center. It is bacteriostatic against *S. aureus* and *S. pyogenes* at MIC levels, but may be bactericidal at 1,000x MIC [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Post-hoc analyses of 12-lead ECGs showed no significant effects on QT/QTc intervals. Systemic exposure is too low for significant QT prolongation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Minimal systemic absorption through intact skin. Higher absorption observed on abraded skin (median Cmax 11.7 ng/mL on Day 1).
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 94% bound to human plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4 via mono-oxygenation and N-demethylation in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Not investigated in humans due to low systemic exposure [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study:** A double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial compared retapamulin to placebo (2:1 ratio) for 5 days in patients with impetigo. 
- **Outcomes:** At the end of therapy (2 days post-treatment), the Clinical Per Protocol (PPC) success rate was 89.5% for retapamulin vs. 53.2% for placebo [US].
- **Efficacy by Pathogen:** Success rates were high for both *S. aureus* (89.8%) and *S. pyogenes* (90.6%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
