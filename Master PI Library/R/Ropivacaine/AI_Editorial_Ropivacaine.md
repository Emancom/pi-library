# Editorial Product Information: Ropivacaine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ropivacaine hydrochloride, synthesising clinical data for use by healthcare professionals. It aims to provide a clear, concise, and clinically accurate reference for the safe and effective use of the medicine.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ropivacaine hydrochloride (an amide-type local anaesthetic).
- **Brand Name(s):** Naropin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injection solution: 2 mg/mL (0.2%), 5 mg/mL (0.5%), 7.5 mg/mL (0.75%), and 10 mg/mL (1.0%) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Epidural (bolus and infusion), intrathecal, field block (infiltration), major nerve block, peripheral nerve block, and wound infusion. 
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
### Surgical Anaesthesia (Adults and Children >12 years)
- Epidural block for surgery, including Caesarean section.
- Intrathecal (spinal) anaesthesia.
- Field block (minor nerve block and infiltration).
- Major nerve block.

### Analgesia (Adults and Children >12 years)
- Continuous epidural infusion or intermittent bolus for postoperative or labour pain.
- Field block (minor nerve block and infiltration).
- Continuous peripheral nerve block or intermittent injections for postoperative pain.
- Continuous wound infusion for postoperative pain (Adults only).

### Analgesia (Children 0–12 years)
- Caudal epidural block (neonates >37 weeks gestation/over 2500g, infants, and children).
- Continuous epidural infusion (infants >30 days/over 2500g and children).
- Peripheral nerve block (infants/children aged 1–12 years).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- Use the lowest dose required to achieve the desired effect (surgical anaesthesia requires higher concentrations than analgesia).
- Clinicians must account for the patient's physical status, site of administration, and the use of concurrent anaesthetics.
- **Maximum Duration:** Data support use for up to 72 hours for epidural analgesia and up to 48 hours for peripheral nerve blocks or wound infusions [Australian].

### Adult Dosing Guide (Representative)
| Procedure | Concentration | Volume | Dose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Lumbar Epidural (Surgery) | 0.75–1.0% | 15–25 mL | 113–200 mg |
| Intrathecal (Surgery) | 0.5% | 3–4 mL | 15–20 mg |
| Labour Analgesia (Bolus) | 0.2% | 10–20 mL | 20–40 mg |
| Labour/Post-op (Infusion) | 0.2% | 6–14 mL/h | 12–28 mg/h |

### Administration Instructions
- **Aspiration:** Careful aspiration is mandatory before and during injection to avoid intravascular administration.
- **Test Dose:** For epidural blocks, a 3–5 mL test dose of a local anaesthetic with adrenaline (e.g., lidocaine 2.0% with adrenaline) is recommended to detect unintended intravascular or intrathecal injection.
- **Injection Speed:** Inject the main dose slowly at a rate of 25–50 mg/min while maintaining verbal contact and monitoring vitals.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to amide-type local anaesthetics.
- Intravenous administration (e.g., Bier’s block).
- Epidural or spinal anaesthesia in patients with uncorrected hypotension.
- Presence of inflammation, sepsis at the injection site, or septicaemia.
- Obstetric paracervical block [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Resuscitative Facilities:** Ropivacaine must only be administered where oxygen and resuscitative equipment/drugs are immediately available to manage cardiovascular or CNS toxicity.
- **Chondrolysis:** Post-marketing reports of chondrolysis (mostly shoulder joint) have occurred following unapproved continuous intra-articular infusion. Ropivacaine should **not** be used for postoperative intra-articular infusion [Australian].
- **Neurological Complications:** Use with extreme caution in patients with pre-existing CNS pathology (e.g., myasthenia gravis, meningitis, spinal cord lesions).
- **Spinal/Epidural Haematoma:** Increased risk in patients undergoing neuraxial blockade who are anticoagulated with low molecular weight heparins or heparinoids.
- **Cardiovascular Health:** Specific caution is required in patients with heart conduction blocks, hypovolaemia, or concomitant heart disease due to the risk of cardiac arrest.
- **Porphyria:** Ropivacaine is potentially porphyrinogenic; use only when no safer alternative is available [Australian].
- **Horner’s Syndrome:** High sympathetic blockade or cranial spread during epidural use (especially in pregnancy) may cause miosis, ptosis, and anhidrosis.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally related to high plasma levels (toxicity) or the physiological effects of the block itself (e.g., hypotension).

| Frequency | System | Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Cardiovascular | Hypotension |
| | Gastrointestinal | Nausea |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Cardiovascular | Bradycardia, hypertension, tachycardia |
| | Nervous System | Paraesthesia, headache, dizziness |
| | Gastrointestinal | Vomiting |
| | Other | Urinary retention, back pain, rigors, pyrexia |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Psychiatric | Anxiety |
| | Nervous System | Hypoaesthesia, syncope, convulsions |
| | Respiratory | Dyspnoea |
| | General | Hypothermia |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Cardiovascular | Cardiac arrest, arrhythmias |
| | Immune | Anaphylactic reactions/shock, angioedema |

**Paediatric Nuance:** In children, vomiting is very common (>10%) while hypotension is common (>1%) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP1A2 Inhibitors:** Fluvoxamine significantly reduces ropivacaine clearance (70%) and increases AUC (3-fold). Avoid repeated or long-term infusion in patients on potent CYP1A2 inhibitors.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Ketoconazole marginally reduces clearance (15%) and is unlikely to be clinically significant for short-term use.
- **Antiarrhythmics:** Use caution with Class III antiarrhythmics (e.g., amiodarone) as cardiac effects may be additive; ECG monitoring is advised.
- **Other Local Anaesthetics:** Toxic effects are additive; use total dose caution when multiple routes or agents are used simultaneously.
- **Alkaline Solutions:** Ropivacaine may precipitate at pH >6.0.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** Animal studies have not shown evidence of teratogenicity. 
    - Ropivacaine should only be used if the potential benefit outweighs the risk. 
    - **Note:** Intrathecal administration for Caesarean section is not documented in available sources [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Ropivacaine and/or its metabolites are excreted in rat milk; human excretion is not investigated. Due to the drug's profile, the risk to the infant is expected to be low at therapeutic doses.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** 
    - Doses must be weight-based. 
    - Maturation of clearance for ropivacaine is complete by age 3. 
    - Neonates require intensive monitoring for systemic toxicity due to immature organ function and slower elimination.
- **Elderly/Debilitated:** Dose reduction is necessary for patients with advanced age or poor physical condition.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Ropivacaine is primarily metabolised by the liver; use with caution in severe hepatic disease.
- **Renal Impairment:** Usually no dose modification is required for single doses. Acidosis and reduced protein concentration in chronic renal failure may increase toxicity risk.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reversible blockade of impulse propagation along nerve fibres by preventing sodium ion influx through the nerve cell membrane.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits both anaesthetic (higher doses) and analgesic (lower doses) effects. It is a pure S-(-) enantiomer. Unlike bupivacaine, it has a lower potential for CNS and cardiovascular toxicity.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Biphasic from the epidural space (half-lives ~14 min and 4 h).
    - **Distribution:** 94% protein-bound (primarily to alpha-1-acid glycoprotein).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively hepatic (aromatic hydroxylation via CYP1A2). Major metabolite is 3-hydroxy-ropivacaine.
    - **Excretion:** 86% via urine (mostly as metabolites; only 1% as unchanged drug). Terminal half-life is ~1.8 hours (IV).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adults:** Open-label randomized studies confirmed efficacy for post-op pain management (up to 48h) and intrathecal surgical anaesthesia (15–20 mg doses).
- **Paediatrics:** Studies across 246 patients (0–12 years) established that 2 mg/kg for caudal blocks provides adequate analgesia. Continuous infusions (0.2 mg/kg/h for <6 months; 0.4 mg/kg/h for >6 months) were well tolerated for up to 72 hours.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Do not re-autoclave Polyamps or Polybags.
- **Integrity:** Preservative-free; intended for single use only. Discard any remaining solution immediately after opening.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
