# Editorial Product Information: Rosuvastatin

## Editorial Note
- The information contained in this editorial synthesis is derived from official Product Information documents provided by the following regulatory authorities:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Product Information — APO-ROSUVASTATIN (Rosuvastatin calcium)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of rosuvastatin for use by healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, scannable format.
- **Note**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Rosuvastatin (as calcium).
- **Chemical Class**: HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin).
- **Brand Names**: APO-ROSUVASTATIN [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: C10AA07.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Film-coated tablets: 5 mg, 10 mg, 20 mg, and 40 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Rosuvastatin is indicated as an adjunct to diet when response to diet and exercise is inadequate for:

- **Hypercholesterolaemia**: Treatment of hypercholesterolaemia, including familial hypercholesterolaemia [Australian].
- **Prevention of Cardiovascular (CV) Events**: In men ≥50 years and women ≥60 years without clinically evident CV disease but with at least two conventional risk factors (hypertension, low HDL-C, smoking, or family history of premature CHD) to:
    - Reduce the risk of nonfatal myocardial infarction.
    - Reduce the risk of nonfatal stroke.
    - Reduce the risk of coronary artery revascularisation procedures [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Starting Dose**: 5 mg or 10 mg once daily for both statin-naïve patients and those switching from other HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors [Australian].
- **Maintenance**: Individualised based on therapeutic goals and patient response. Adjustments can be made after 4 weeks of therapy.
- **Maximum Dose**: 20 mg once daily is the usual maximum [Australian].

### Specific Dosing Guidance
- **40 mg Dose**: Reserved for patients at high cardiovascular risk (e.g., familial hypercholesterolaemia) who fail to reach goals at 20 mg. Requires specialist supervision and regular follow-up [Australian].
- **Asian Patients**: Initiation at 5 mg once daily should be considered due to increased systemic exposure [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken at any time of day, with or without food [Australian].
- If an antacid containing aluminium and magnesium hydroxide is required, it should be administered at least 2 hours after rosuvastatin to avoid a ~50% reduction in rosuvastatin plasma concentration [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to rosuvastatin or any excipients.
- **Active Liver Disease**: Including unexplained, persistent elevations of serum transaminases (exceeding 3 x ULN) [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Breastfeeding**: Contraindicated in pregnant women, nursing mothers, and women of childbearing potential not using adequate contraception [Australian].
- **Concomitant Fusidic Acid**: Systemic fusidic acid must not be co-administered with statins [Australian].
- **40 mg Dose Specific Contraindications**: 
    - Moderate renal impairment (CrCl < 60 mL/min) [Note: TGA specifically lists CrCl < 30 mL/min as a contraindication for 40mg in Section 4.3].
    - Hypothyroidism.
    - Personal or family history of hereditary muscular disorders.
    - Previous muscular toxicity with another HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor or fibrate.
    - Alcohol abuse.
    - Asian patients.
    - Concomitant use of fibrates [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Liver Effects**: Perform LFTs before initiation and periodically thereafter. If ALT or AST persists >3 x ULN, reduce dose or withdraw treatment [Australian].
- **Myopathy/Rhabdomyolysis**: Risk is dose-related. Rare cases of rhabdomyolysis with acute renal failure have occurred. 
    - **Monitoring**: Advise patients to report unexplained muscle pain/weakness immediately. Discontinue if CK levels are markedly elevated or myopathy is suspected [Australian].
    - **Temporary Withdrawal**: Withhold therapy in acute, serious conditions predisposing to renal failure (e.g., sepsis, major surgery, trauma) [Australian].
- **Endocrine Effects**: Increases in HbA1c and fasting serum glucose have been reported. Statins may increase the risk of developing diabetes mellitus [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)**: Exceptional cases reported with long-term therapy. Discontinue if ILD is suspected (symptoms include dyspnoea, cough, fatigue) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Rosuvastatin is generally well tolerated; adverse events are usually mild and transient.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache, dizziness, constipation, nausea, abdominal pain, myalgia, asthenia, diabetes mellitus [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pruritus, rash, urticaria [Australian]. |
| **Rare (0.01–0.1%)** | Pancreatitis, hypersensitivity (including angioedema), myopathy (including myositis), rhabdomyolysis [Australian]. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Jaundice, hepatitis, arthralgia, memory loss [Australian]. |
| **Frequency Unknown** | Ocular myasthenia, thrombocytopenia, hepatic failure, immune-mediated necrotising myopathy, peripheral neuropathy, depression, sleep disorders (insomnia/nightmares), gynaecomastia, DRESS, lichenoid drug eruption [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Transporter Proteins**: Rosuvastatin is a substrate for **OATP1B1** and **BCRP**. 
    - **Ciclosporin**: Increases rosuvastatin AUC 7-fold; limit rosuvastatin to 5 mg once daily [Australian].
    - **Protease Inhibitors**: Various combinations (e.g., ritonavir with atazanavir, lopinavir, or simeprevir) significantly increase exposure. Dose adjustments are required based on the specific agent (see PI Table 1) [Australian].
    - **Gemfibrozil**: Increases exposure 2-fold; limit rosuvastatin to 10 mg once daily. Avoid combination if possible [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Warfarin**: May cause clinically significant increases in INR. Monitor INR before starting and frequently during early therapy [Australian].
- **Fusidic Acid**: Increased risk of rhabdomyolysis; rosuvastatin must be suspended during fusidic acid treatment [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category D** [Australian]. Cholesterol is essential for foetal development; the risk of inhibition outweighs potential benefits. Discontinue as soon as pregnancy is recognised.
- **Breastfeeding**: Contraindicated. Rosuvastatin is excreted in rat milk; limited data suggests presence in human milk with a potential for serious adverse reactions in infants [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy not established. Not recommended for children with homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥65 years)**: Usual dose range applies; however, advanced age is a predisposing factor for myopathy [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**:
    - **Mild to Moderate**: Usual dose applies.
    - **Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min/1.73m²)**: Start at 5 mg; do not exceed 10 mg once daily. Contraindicated at 40 mg dose [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: 
    - **Mild to Moderate**: Usual dose applies.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh >9)**: Use with caution; start at 5 mg and limit doses above 10 mg [Australian].
- **Race**: Median exposure (AUC and Cmax) is approximately 2-fold higher in Asian subjects compared to Caucasians. This must be considered in dosing decisions [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: A fully synthetic, competitive inhibitor of HMG-CoA reductase. It increases the number of hepatic LDL receptors on the cell surface (enhancing uptake/catabolism) and inhibits hepatic synthesis of VLDL [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Peak plasma levels at 5 hours. Absolute bioavailability is ~20%.
    - **Distribution**: ~90% protein bound (mostly albumin).
    - **Metabolism**: Limited (~10%), mainly via CYP2C9. N-desmethyl rosuvastatin is the primary metabolite.
    - **Elimination**: ~90% excreted unchanged in faeces; the remainder in urine. Elimination half-life is ~19 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **JUPITER Study**: A trial in 17,802 subjects with low LDL-C (<3.3 mmol/L) but elevated hsCRP (≥2 mg/L). Rosuvastatin 20 mg significantly reduced the risk of major CV events (HR 0.56) compared to placebo [Australian].
- **METEOR Study**: Evaluated the effect of rosuvastatin 40 mg on Carotid Intima-Media Thickness (CIMT) in low-risk patients with subclinical atherosclerosis. Rosuvastatin significantly slowed the rate of progression of maximum CIMT compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Shelf life as per the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: **Schedule 4** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
