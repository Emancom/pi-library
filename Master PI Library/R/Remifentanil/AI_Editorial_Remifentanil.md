# Editorial Product Information: Remifentanil

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesised from the following official Product Information (PI) source: Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of remifentanil for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Practitioners must consult the local approved PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Remifentanil (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** A potent, selective, 4-anilidopiperidine $\mu$-opioid agonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Remifentanil-AFT** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N01AH06.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile, preservative-free, white to off-white lyophilised powder for injection in 1 mg, 2 mg, and 5 mg vials (expressed as remifentanil base) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Remifentanil is indicated for:
- **General Anaesthesia:** Use as an opioid adjunct for induction and/or maintenance of general anaesthesia during surgical procedures, including cardiac surgery in adults [Australian].
- **Paediatric Anaesthesia:** Use as an opioid adjunct for induction and/or maintenance of general anaesthesia during surgical procedures (excluding cardiac surgery) in children aged 1 to 12 years [Australian].
- **Post-operative Analgesia:** Transition to longer-acting analgesia following adult cardiac surgery while the patient is still under medical supervision with anticipated endotracheal intubation and controlled ventilation [Australian].
- **Intensive Care:** Provision of analgesia and sedation in mechanically ventilated patients [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Remifentanil must be individualised based on patient response and should never be used as the sole agent in general anaesthesia. It must be administered via a calibrated infusion device into a dedicated or fast-flowing IV line.

- **Induction of Anaesthesia:** 0.5 to 1 $\mu$g/kg/min with or without an initial slow bolus of 1 $\mu$g/kg (administered over at least 60 seconds) [Australian].
- **Maintenance (Ventilated):** 0.1 to 2 $\mu$g/kg/min depending on the concurrent hypnotic agent (e.g., nitrous oxide, isoflurane, or propofol) [Australian].
- **Intensive Care:** Starting rate of 0.1 to 0.15 $\mu$g/kg/min, titrated in 0.025 $\mu$g/kg/min increments every 5 minutes to achieve the desired level of sedation/analgesia. Typical maintenance range is 0.006 to 0.74 $\mu$g/kg/min [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Add 1 mL of diluent per mg of remifentanil to achieve a 1 mg/mL solution.
- **Dilution:** Must be further diluted to 20, 25, 50, or 250 $\mu$g/mL prior to use. 50 $\mu$g/mL is recommended for adults; 20–25 $\mu$g/mL is recommended for paediatric patients [Australian].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible with Sterile Water for Injection, 5% Glucose, 0.9% Sodium Chloride, and Lactated Ringer's solution [Australian].
- **Incompatibility:** Do **not** administer via the same IV line as blood/serum/plasma (non-specific esterases may hydrolyse the drug) or with propofol in the same admixture [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Sole Agent Use:** Not suitable as the sole agent for induction of general anaesthesia [Australian].
- **Administration Route:** Contraindicated for epidural or intrathecal use due to the presence of glycine in the formulation [Australian].
- **Respiratory Condition:** Contraindicated in patients with severe respiratory disease, acute respiratory depression, or during spontaneous ventilation anaesthesia (except in specific cardiac surgery protocols) [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to remifentanil, other fentanyl analogues, or any excipients [Australian].
- **Pain Management:** Not for use in chronic (long-term) non-cancer pain [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Hazardous and Harmful Use):** Remifentanil poses risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, which can lead to overdose and death. Monitor patients for signs of misuse [Australian].
- **Boxed Warning (Respiratory Depression):** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur. Monitoring of respiratory and cardiovascular function is mandatory in a setting equipped for resuscitation [Australian].
- **Boxed Warning (CNS Depressants):** Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or alcohol may result in profound sedation, coma, and death [Australian].
- **Muscle Rigidity:** May cause chest wall rigidity. Manage by reducing infusion rate, stopping administration, or using neuromuscular blocking agents [Australian].
- **Rapid Offset of Action:** Analgesic activity dissipates within 5 to 10 minutes of discontinuation. Alternative analgesia must be established prior to stopping the infusion [Australian].
- **IV Line Clearance:** Inadvertent administration of residual drug in the IV line (e.g., through flushing) can cause apnoea and rigidity [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally dose-dependent and dissipate quickly upon discontinuation.
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea (especially post-discontinuation), vomiting, hypotension, and muscle rigidity [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Bradycardia, hypertension (post-operative), shivering, dizziness, and pruritus [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Asystole/cardiac arrest (usually preceded by bradycardia), respiratory depression, and anaphylaxis [Australian].
- **Post-marketing/Rare:** Central sleep apnoea, pancreatitis, spasm of the sphincter of Oddi, and adrenal insufficiency [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Synergy with Anaesthetics:** Remifentanil decreases the required dose of inhaled anaesthetics, hypnotics, and benzodiazepines by up to 75% [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Agents:** Bradycardia and hypotension may be exacerbated by beta-blockers or calcium channel blockers [Australian].
- **Metabolism:** Not metabolised by plasma cholinesterase; therefore, no interaction with drugs metabolised by this enzyme is expected [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. Chronic maternal use may result in neonatal withdrawal syndrome. Remifentanil crosses the placenta and can cause respiratory depression in the newborn. Use in pregnant women or during labour/delivery is not recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if remifentanil is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised when administered to breastfeeding mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed reduced fertility and pathological changes in the testes/epididymides at high exposures [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children 1–12 years as an adjunct in general anaesthesia. Insufficient data for use in children under 1 year or in paediatric intensive care [Australian].
- **Elderly Use (>65 years):** Initial doses should be reduced by 50% for general anaesthesia due to increased sensitivity; however, no initial reduction is required for cardiac anaesthesia [Australian].
- **Obese Patients:** Dosing should be based on **ideal body weight** [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for the parent drug. However, the metabolite (GR90291) accumulates in renal failure; recovery from sedation may be slightly prolonged in moderate to severe impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No initial dose adjustment required, but patients may be more sensitive to respiratory depressant effects [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent, selective $\mu$-opioid agonist with rapid onset and very short duration of action.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Rapid blood-brain equilibration (half-time of $1 \pm 1$ minute). Effects follow blood concentrations closely. Does not cause histamine release [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly hydrolysed by non-specific blood and tissue esterases to an inactive carboxylic acid derivative (GR90291).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life of 10–20 minutes. Approximately 95% is recovered in urine as the metabolite [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **General Anaesthesia:** Evaluated in over 3,000 patients. Studies demonstrated that remifentanil allows for rapid titration and predictable recovery times (median extubation in ~5 minutes) regardless of infusion duration [Australian].
- **Intensive Care:** Studies up to 3 days showed remifentanil provided effective sedation and analgesia, with a rapid time to extubation (median $\le 1.3$ hours) compared to fentanyl or morphine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Chemically and physically stable for 24 hours at 2–8°C. To reduce microbiological hazard, it should be used as soon as possible after preparation [Australian].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 8 (Controlled Drug) [Australian].
