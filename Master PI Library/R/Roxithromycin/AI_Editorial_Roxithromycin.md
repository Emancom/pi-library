# Editorial Product Information: Roxithromycin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: APO-Roxithromycin (Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Roxithromycin (a semi-synthetic macrolide antibiotic).
- **Brand Names**: APO-Roxithromycin [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - 150 mg film-coated tablets (white to off-white, round, convex) [Australian].
    - 300 mg film-coated tablets (white to off-white, round, convex) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Roxithromycin is indicated for mild to moderately severe infections caused by susceptible organisms.

### Adult Indications
- **Upper Respiratory Tract Infections (URTI)**: Acute pharyngitis, tonsillitis, and sinusitis.
- **Lower Respiratory Tract Infections (LRTI)**: Acute bronchitis, acute exacerbations of chronic bronchitis, and community-acquired pneumonia.
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections**.
- **Non-gonococcal Urethritis**.

### Paediatric Indications
- **Acute pharyngitis**.
- **Acute tonsillitis**.
- **Impetigo**.
- *Note: 150 mg tablets are the indicated form for children [Australian].*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Tablets must be swallowed whole with liquid.
- **Timing**: Must be taken at least 15 minutes before food or on an empty stomach (at least 3 hours after a meal) as food reduces absorption.

### Adult Dosing
- **Usual Dosage**: 300 mg daily, administered as either:
    - 300 mg once daily, OR
    - 150 mg twice daily.
- **Atypical Pneumonia**: 150 mg twice daily.
- **Duration**: Usually 5 to 10 days. 
    - Streptococcal throat infections require at least 10 days.
    - Non-gonococcal genital infections may require up to 20 days.

### Paediatric Dosing (Children ≥40 kg)
- **Dosage**: 5 to 8 mg/kg/day, administered as one 150 mg tablet twice daily (morning and evening).
- **Duration**: 5 to 10 days (maximum 10 days).
- **Restriction**: Roxithromycin tablets are not recommended for children weighing less than 40 kg [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to macrolides, including erythromycin.
- Severely impaired hepatic function [Australian].
- Concomitant therapy with vasoconstrictive ergot alkaloids (e.g., ergotamine, dihydroergotamine).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Superinfection**: Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms.
- **Clostridium difficile-associated disease**: Ranging from mild diarrhoea to fatal pseudomembranous colitis. Discontinue treatment immediately if suspected.
- **QT Prolongation**: Caution in patients with congenital QT prolongation, proarrhythmic conditions (bradycardia, uncorrected hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia), or those taking Class IA and III antiarrhythmics.
- **Severe Bullous Reactions**: Rare cases of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrosis (TEN). Discontinue if progressive rash/blisters appear.
- **Myasthenia Gravis**: Potential for aggravation of symptoms.
- **Cardiovascular Risk**: Increased risk of cardiovascular events and mortality when used with hydroxychloroquine or chloroquine.
- **Ergotism**: Concomitant use with ergot alkaloids can cause severe vasoconstriction and limb necrosis.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Roxithromycin is generally well-tolerated. Discontinuation rates in trials were approximately 1.2% in adults and 1.0% in children.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, diarrhoea (sometimes bloody), anorexia, flatulence, pancreatitis. |
| **Hypersensitivity** | Rash, urticaria, pruritus, angioedema, asthma, bronchospasm, anaphylactic shock, SJS/TEN (rare). |
| **Hepatic** | Increased AST/ALT/Alkaline phosphatase; acute cholestatic hepatitis or hepatocellular injury (rare). |
| **Haematological** | Transient eosinophilia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia. |
| **Dermatological** | Mild itching (1-5%), nail discoloration. |
| **Neurological/Other** | Headache, dizziness, paraesthesia, hallucinations, confusion, taste/smell disorders, tinnitus, vertigo, hearing loss. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Ergot Alkaloids**: Contraindicated; risk of severe peripheral necrosis.
- **Terfenadine, Astemizole, Cisapride, Pimozide**: Concomitant use is not recommended due to potential for QT prolongation and Torsades de Pointes.
- **Theophylline**: May increase theophylline plasma levels; monitoring is advised for patients on high doses.
- **Disopyramide**: May increase serum levels of free disopyramide; monitor ECG and serum levels.
- **Vitamin K Antagonists (e.g., Warfarin)**: Increased INR and bleeding episodes reported; monitor INR.
- **Digoxin**: May increase absorption, potentially leading to cardiac glycoside toxicity; monitor ECG and serum levels.
- **Midazolam**: May enhance and prolong the effects of midazolam.
- **CYP3A Inhibitors**: Roxithromycin is a weak CYP3A inhibitor; use caution with drugs like rifabutin or bromocriptine.
- **Hydroxychloroquine/Chloroquine**: Increased risk of cardiovascular mortality.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category B1** [Australian]. Animal studies (rats, mice, rabbits) showed no developmental abnormalities. Embryotoxicity was observed in rats at doses causing maternal toxicity. Safety in human pregnancy is not established.
- **Breastfeeding**: Small amounts are excreted in breast milk. Breastfeeding or maternal treatment should be discontinued if necessary.
- **Fertility**: No effect observed in rats up to 180 mg/kg/day.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Strict adherence to the 5–8 mg/kg/day dose (max 10 days) is required due to bone growth plate abnormalities observed in young animals at very high doses. Neutropenia was observed in 31.6% of children in trials [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No dosage adjustment required. Hepatic enzyme increases may be more likely in patients >65 years.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dosage adjustment required; renal excretion is a minor pathway.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Caution is required. In severe impairment (e.g., cirrhosis with jaundice/ascites), the dose should be reduced to 150 mg daily (half the usual dose).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Bacteriostatic at low concentrations and bactericidal at high concentrations. It binds to the 50S subunit of the 70S ribosome, inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Exhibits a prolonged post-antibiotic effect. Active against *S. pneumoniae*, *S. pyogenes*, *M. pneumoniae*, *M. catarrhalis*, *U. urealyticum*, and *Chlamydia spp*. Partially sensitive organisms include *H. influenzae* and *S. aureus* (excluding MRSA).
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Absolute bioavailability ~50%. Peak plasma levels occur 1–2 hours post-dose. Absorption is non-linear above 150 mg.
    - **Distribution**: 92–96% protein bound (principally alpha-1-acid glycoprotein); binding is saturable. Highly concentrated in macrophages and polymorphonuclear leucocytes.
    - **Metabolism**: Limited hepatic metabolism; the main metabolite is descladinose roxithromycin.
    - **Elimination**: Half-life is ~12 hours in adults, ~20 hours in children, and ~27 hours in the elderly. Primary excretion is faecal (~53%).
