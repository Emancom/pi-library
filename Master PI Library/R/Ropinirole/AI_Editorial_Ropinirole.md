# Editorial Product Information: Ropinirole

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from the following regulatory authority: Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **ropinirole** intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing strategies, and safety profiles.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Ropinirole** (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Non-ergoline dopamine D2/D3 agonist.
- **Brand Names:** Appese [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Immediate-release film-coated tablets: 0.25 mg, 0.5 mg, 1 mg, and 2 mg [Australian]. 
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS):** Treatment of primary Restless Legs Syndrome, including the reduction of periodic limb movements and nocturnal arousal episodes [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Individual titration based on efficacy and tolerability is mandatory. **Ropinirole** should be administered once daily before bedtime; however, the dose may be taken up to three hours prior to retiring [Australian].

### Titration Schedule (RLS)
The following titration schedule is recommended to reach the optimal therapeutic response [Australian]:
- **Week 1:** 0.25 mg once daily for 2 days; increase to 0.5 mg once daily for the remainder of the week.
- **Week 2:** 1 mg once daily.
- **Week 3:** 1.5 mg once daily.
- **Week 4:** 2 mg once daily.
- **Week 5:** 2.5 mg once daily.
- **Week 6:** 3 mg once daily.
- **Week 7:** 4 mg once daily.

**Maximum Dose:** 4 mg/day [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- If treatment is interrupted for more than a few days, it must be re-initiated via the titration schedule described above [Australian].
- **Ropinirole** may be taken with or without food. Administration with food delays $T_{max}$ by 2.6 hours and decreases $C_{max}$ by 25%, though overall bioavailability remains unchanged [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **ropinirole** hydrochloride or any excipients (including lactose) [Australian].
- Pregnancy and lactation [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance <30 mL/min) in patients not receiving regular haemodialysis [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Augmentation and Rebound:** Paradoxical worsening of RLS symptoms (earlier onset, increased intensity, or spread to other limbs) or early morning rebound of symptoms may occur [Australian].
- **Sudden Sleep Onset and Somnolence:** Patients may fall asleep during activities of daily living without prior warning. Prescribers must screen for pre-existing sleep disorders and sedating co-medications. Dose reduction or discontinuation is usually required if significant daytime sleepiness occurs [Australian].
- **Impulse Control Disorders:** Patients should be monitored for pathological gambling, increased libido, hypersexuality, compulsive spending, and binge eating. These are generally reversible upon dose reduction [Australian].
- **Orthostatic Hypotension:** Dopamine agonists impair blood pressure regulation. Monitoring is recommended, especially during dose escalation and in patients with severe cardiovascular disease [Australian].
- **Dopamine Agonist Withdrawal Syndrome (DAWS):** Abrupt withdrawal or tapering may lead to apathy, anxiety, depression, fatigue, sweating, and pain. Tapered discontinuation is required [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Disorders:** Use with caution in patients with a history of major psychiatric or psychotic disorders. Hallucinations and mania have been reported [Australian].
- **Fibrotic Complications:** While associated primarily with ergot-derived agents, cases of retroperitoneal fibrosis, pleural effusion, and valvulopathy have been reported with dopamine agonists [Australian].
- **Ophthalmologic:** Based on animal data, human albinos might have increased susceptibility to retinal pathology and should be monitored via ophthalmological control [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are most frequent during treatment initiation or dose escalation.

| Frequency | Adverse Event [Australian] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (37.9%), Headache (22.3%), Vomiting (12.9%), Dizziness (12.3%), Somnolence (11.7%), Upper respiratory tract infection (10.7%), Fatigue (10.4%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, Arthralgia, Dry mouth, Diarrhoea, Vertigo, Nervousness, Syncope, Augmentation, Early morning rebound |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Psychotic reactions (delusion, paranoia, delirium), Impulse control symptoms |
| **Very Rare (<0.1%)** | Hepatic reactions (increased liver enzymes), Hypersensitivity (urticaria, angioedema, rash) |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Aggression, Mania, DAWS, Spontaneous penile erection, Hiccups |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP1A2 Interactions:** **Ropinirole** is primarily metabolised by CYP1A2. 
    - **Inhibitors:** Ciprofloxacin, enoxacin, and fluvoxamine may increase **ropinirole** plasma levels. Dose adjustment may be required [Australian].
    - **Inducers (Smoking):** Smoking induces CYP1A2 and increases **ropinirole** clearance. Dose adjustment may be necessary if a patient starts or stops smoking [Australian].
- **Dopamine Antagonists:** Neuroleptics (e.g., sulpiride, metoclopramide) may diminish efficacy; concomitant use should be avoided [Australian].
- **Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT):** High-dose oestrogens can reduce **ropinirole** clearance by approximately 33% [Australian].
- **Vitamin K Antagonists:** Reports of unbalanced INR have occurred; increased monitoring is warranted [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** Caution is advised when used with alcohol or other sedatives due to additive effects [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Contraindicated [Australian]. Animal studies show developmental toxicity (abortion, fetal death, and malformations) at maternotoxic doses. No human pregnancy data is available [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. **Ropinirole** may inhibit lactation and is excreted in the milk of lactating rats [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Reduced implantation was observed in rats (likely due to prolactin-lowering effects), but no effect on male fertility was noted [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is not recommended in patients under 18 years of age due to a lack of safety and efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Oral clearance is reduced by approximately 15% in patients $\ge$ 65 years. However, no specific dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (CrCl 30–50 mL/min):** No dosage adjustment required [Australian].
    - **End-Stage Renal Disease (Haemodialysis):** Initial dose 0.25 mg/day. Maximum dose limited to 3 mg/day. Supplemental doses after dialysis are not required [Australian].
    - **Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min) without Dialysis:** Not recommended [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Moderate:** Administer with caution and monitor closely [Australian].
    - **Severe:** Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Ropinirole** is a potent, non-ergoline D2/D3 dopamine agonist. It compensates for dopaminergic deficiency in the striatum, which is thought to underlie RLS pathophysiology. It also inhibits prolactin secretion [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; bioavailability ~46%. $T_{max}$ is approximately 1.5 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Highly lipophilic; Volume of distribution (Vd) ~7 L/kg. Plasma protein binding is low (10–40%) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Principally hepatic via CYP1A2. The major metabolite (N-despropyl) is $\ge$ 100 times less potent than the parent drug [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Clearance is primarily renal (metabolites) and hepatic. Elimination half-life is approximately 6 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy in RLS:** Two 12-week, flexible-dose, placebo-controlled trials demonstrated significant improvement in IRLS Scale scores and Clinical Global Impression (CGI) scores compared to placebo. The mean dose in these trials was approximately 2 mg/day [Australian].
- **Polysomnography (Study 191):** **Ropinirole** significantly reduced the number of periodic leg movements of sleep (PLMS) per hour compared to placebo (32.9 vs 5.7 reduction) [Australian].
- **Long-term Maintenance (Study 188):** Responders treated for 36 weeks demonstrated a significantly lower relapse rate when continuing **ropinirole** compared to those switched to placebo (32.6% vs 57.8%) [Australian].
