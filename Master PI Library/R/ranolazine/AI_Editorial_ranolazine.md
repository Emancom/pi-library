# Editorial Product Information: Ranolazine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of ranolazine for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesizes regulatory data into a clinically focused format.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing and dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ranolazine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Piperazine derivative; antianginal.
- **Brand Names:** Noted as **Ranolazine Extended-Release Tablets** (Distributed by Major Pharmaceuticals/Ascend Laboratories) [US].
- **ATC Code:** C01EB18.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Extended-release (ER) film-coated tablets: 500 mg and 1000 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Distributed by Major Pharmaceuticals/Ascend Laboratories; Manufactured by Alkem Laboratories Ltd. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Angina:** Indicated for the treatment of chronic angina pectoris. Ranolazine may be used in combination with beta-blockers, nitrates, calcium channel blockers, antiplatelet agents, lipid-lowering therapies, ACE inhibitors, and angiotensin receptor blockers [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Initial dose is 500 mg twice daily. Based on clinical symptoms, the dose may be increased to a maximum of 1000 mg twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole. Do not crush, break, or chew.
    - May be taken with or without meals [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, the patient should take the next prescribed dose at the regularly scheduled time. Doses should not be doubled [US].
- **Dose Modifications:**
    - **Moderate CYP3A Inhibitors:** Limit dose to 500 mg twice daily [US].
    - **P-gp Inhibitors:** Titrate ranolazine based on clinical response [US].

## 4. Contraindications
Ranolazine is contraindicated in patients:
- Receiving strong CYP3A inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin, nefazodone, nelfinavir, ritonavir, indinavir, saquinavir) [US].
- Receiving CYP3A inducers (e.g., rifampin, rifabutin, rifapentine, phenobarbital, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St. John’s wort) [US].
- With liver cirrhosis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** Ranolazine blocks $I_{Kr}$ and prolongs the QTc interval in a dose-related manner. Clinical experience is limited in patients with a family history of congenital long QT syndrome, known acquired QT prolongation, or those on other QT-prolonging drugs. Use caution when treating patients with these risks or when using high doses (>1000 mg twice daily) [US].
- **Renal Failure:** Acute renal failure has been observed in patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min). 
    - **Monitoring:** Monitor renal function at initiation and periodically in patients with moderate to severe renal impairment (CrCl <60 mL/min). 
    - **Discontinuation:** If acute renal failure develops, discontinue ranolazine immediately [US].
- **Neurologic Effects:** Postmarketing reports include abnormal coordination, myoclonus, tremor, and hallucinations, often associated with increased dose or exposure [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally dose-related. In controlled trials, approximately 6% of patients discontinued treatment due to adverse events (vs. 3% for placebo) [US].

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>4%)** | Dizziness (6.2%), headache (5.5%), constipation (4.5%), nausea (4.4%) |
| **Uncommon (0.5–4%)** | Bradycardia, palpitations, tinnitus, vertigo, blurred vision, abdominal pain, dry mouth, vomiting, dyspepsia, asthenia, peripheral edema, anorexia, syncope (vasovagal), confusional state, hematuria, dyspnea, hyperhidrosis, hypotension, orthostatic hypotension |
| **Rare (<0.5%)** | Angioedema, renal failure, eosinophilia, chromaturia, tremor, pulmonary fibrosis, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, pancytopenia |

- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Ranolazine causes a mean increase in serum creatinine of ~0.1 mg/dL. This is due to inhibition of tubular secretion and does not reflect a change in glomerular filtration rate (GFR) in patients with normal/moderate renal function [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors:** Contraindicated; significantly increase ranolazine exposure (~220%) [US].
- **Moderate CYP3A Inhibitors (e.g., diltiazem, verapamil, erythromycin):** Limit ranolazine to 500 mg twice daily [US].
- **CYP3A Inducers:** Contraindicated; decrease ranolazine levels by ~95% [US].
- **P-gp Inhibitors (e.g., cyclosporine):** May increase ranolazine levels; titrate based on clinical response [US].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Paroxetine increases ranolazine levels by 20%; no dose adjustment required [US].

### Effects of Ranolazine on Other Drugs
- **CYP3A Substrates:** Ranolazine increases levels of simvastatin (limit simvastatin dose to 20 mg daily) and lovastatin. Narrow therapeutic index substrates (cyclosporine, tacrolimus, sirolimus) may require dose reduction [US].
- **Digoxin:** Increases digoxin exposure by ~50%; monitor digoxin levels and adjust dose as necessary [US].
- **CYP2D6 Substrates:** May increase exposure to substrates like tricyclic antidepressants and antipsychotics; dose reductions may be required [US].
- **Metformin (OCT2 substrate):** 
    - At ranolazine 1000 mg twice daily, metformin exposure increases by 80%.
    - Limit metformin to 1700 mg daily when used with ranolazine 1000 mg twice daily.
    - No significant interaction at ranolazine 500 mg twice daily [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** There are no available data in pregnant women. Animal studies in rats and rabbits at 4 times the MRHD showed no evidence of fetal harm, although decreased fetal weight was seen at maternally toxic doses in rats [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of ranolazine in human milk. Ranolazine is present in rat milk. The benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the potential for adverse effects in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in male or female rats at exposures 3 to 5 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients <18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Patients ≥75 years of age have a higher incidence of adverse events and serious adverse events. Initial dose selection should be conservative [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Moderate/Severe (CrCl <60 mL/min):** Monitor renal function periodically.
    - **Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min):** Risk of acute renal failure; use with extreme caution.
    - **Dialysis:** Pharmacokinetics have not been assessed [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in liver cirrhosis. In Child-Pugh Class B, exposure increases by 80% [US].
- **Heart Failure:** No dose adjustment required for NYHA Class I–IV heart failure [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the late sodium current ($I_{Na}$) in myocardial cells. This reduces intracellular sodium accumulation and consequent calcium overload, improving myocardial relaxation and reducing ventricular diastolic wall tension. Ranolazine also inhibits the rapid delayed rectifier potassium current ($I_{Kr}$), which accounts for QTc prolongation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Highly variable; peak plasma concentrations reached in 2–5 hours.
    - **Effect of Food:** No significant effect on $C_{max}$ or AUC.
    - **Distribution:** ~62% bound to human plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized by CYP3A (major) and CYP2D6 (minor).
    - **Elimination:** Apparent terminal half-life is ~7 hours. Steady state achieved within 3 days. Approximately 75% excreted in urine, 25% in feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CARISA:** In 823 patients, ranolazine (750 mg or 1000 mg twice daily) significantly increased exercise duration and time to angina compared to placebo when added to other antianginals. No additional benefit was seen at 1000 mg vs 750 mg twice daily [US].
- **ERICA:** In patients symptomatic on max-dose amlodipine, ranolazine 1000 mg twice daily significantly reduced weekly angina frequency and nitroglycerin use [US].
- **MERLIN-TIMI 36:** In 6,560 patients with acute coronary syndrome, ranolazine did *not* show a benefit on outcome measures (death or MI) but showed no increased risk of proarrhythmia or sudden death [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at $25^{\circ}C$ ($77^{\circ}F$); excursions permitted to $15^{\circ}C$ to $30^{\circ}C$ ($59^{\circ}F$ to $86^{\circ}F$) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children. Unit dose packages are not child-resistant [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine (Rx-only) [US].
