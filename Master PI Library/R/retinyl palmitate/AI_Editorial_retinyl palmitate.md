## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypervitaminosis A.
- Hypersensitivity to retinyl palmitate or any component of the formulation.
- Pregnancy: Vitamin A capsules should not be administered to pregnant females [Australian]. (See Section 9 for full pregnancy guidance.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Teratogenicity**: High doses of vitamin A are associated with fetal malformations. Retinyl palmitate should not be administered to pregnant females [Australian]. Preparations with a daily dose greater than 1,500 microgram retinol equivalents (RE) should be avoided because of teratogenic effects [Australian]. The recommended adult daily requirement for vitamin A from all sources is 750 microgram RE; taking more than 750 microgram RE per day as a supplement during pregnancy may cause birth defects [Australian].
- Hepatic and pancreatic disease: Absorption of retinyl palmitate (the ester) requires intestinal hydrolysis and normal pancreatic/hepatic function; absorption may be reduced in patients with reduced hepatic or pancreatic function.
- Intracranial hypertension: Cases of increased intracranial pressure (with severe headache and papilloedema) and neurological symptoms mimicking intracranial mass effect have been reported with vitamin A intoxication; monitor for headache, vomiting, visual disturbance, papilloedema.
- Liver toxicity: Chronic excessive intake may lead to pathological hepatic changes including hepatomegaly, hypertrophy of fat-storing cells, fibrosis, sclerosis of central veins and cirrhosis; monitor liver function if high doses are used or if prolonged therapy is anticipated.
- Bone effects: Chronic excessive intake can cause bone pain, hyperostosis and premature closure of epiphyses in children.
- Lipid effects: Increases in plasma triglyceride and decreases in HDL cholesterol have been reported with chronic excess.
- Paediatric considerations: Infants may present with bulging fontanelle and vomiting as early signs of acute toxicity.
- Overdose management: Treatment is cessation of vitamin A administration and supportive therapy. For acute poisoning and management advice, contact local poisons information services (in Australia contact the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26) [Australian].

Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for clinical signs of vitamin A excess (see Section 7).
- Consider monitoring liver function tests, lipids, and calcium in patients receiving high doses or prolonged treatment.
- Monitor growth and epiphyseal maturation in children during prolonged use.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **retinyl palmitate** (vitamin A ester) provides retinol (vitamin A) after intestinal hydrolysis. Vitamin A is required for normal growth and differentiation of epithelial tissues, is essential for vision (particularly scotopic vision) via its role in the retina, and is involved in bone growth, embryonic development and reproduction. Different vitamin A forms have distinct tissue activities: retinol functions in the eye; retinoic acid is the active form for growth, differentiation and gene regulatory functions.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - The ester (retinyl palmitate) is readily absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract after hydrolysis by pancreatic enzymes. Uptake by intestinal cells occurs via a carrier‑mediated process. Absorption may be reduced with impaired hepatic or pancreatic function.
- Distribution and metabolism:
  - After absorption, retinol is re‑esterified and taken up by the liver until hepatic stores are saturated. Retinol is released from the liver largely bound (≈95%) to retinol‑binding protein (RBP). Retinol–RBP interacts with target cell surface sites, is transferred to membrane proteins and converted to retinol esters in cells. In the retina, retinol is converted to 11‑cis‑retinol and incorporated into rhodopsin. In other tissues, retinol is oxidised to retinoic acid and conveyed to the nucleus as a complex to exert gene regulatory effects.
- Elimination:
  - Retinol and metabolites are excreted after formation of glucuronide conjugates and subsequent oxidation to retinol and retinoic acid; metabolites are eliminated in urine and faeces.

### Pharmacodynamics
- As above under mechanism: supports epithelial differentiation, visual pigment cycle (retina), and regulation of growth and differentiation via retinoic acid–mediated nuclear receptor signalling.
