# Editorial Product Information: rh(d) immune globulin (human)

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **rh(d) immune globulin (human)**
- Chemical class: Human plasma‑derived anti‑D immunoglobulin (IgG)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous (IV) and intramuscular (IM) — route depends on indication (see Dosage and Administration)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Last revision stated as 5/2025 in one source document
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP) in non‑splenectomized, Rh(D)-positive patients:
  - Children with acute or chronic ITP
  - Adults with chronic ITP
  - Children and adults with ITP secondary to HIV infection
- Suppression of Rh isoimmunization in non‑sensitized, Rh(D)-negative individuals in Rh‑incompatible situations, including:
  - Routine antepartum prophylaxis (e.g., at ~28 weeks)
  - Postpartum prophylaxis after birth of an Rh(D)-positive infant
  - Prophylaxis after threatened abortion, invasive procedures (e.g., amniocentesis, chorionic villus sampling), obstetric complications, external trauma, or other procedures associated with fetomaternal hemorrhage
  - Repeated antepartum dosing if given early (e.g., repeated at ~12‑week intervals when indicated)
- Prevention of Rh isoimmunization after incompatible transfusion: suppression in Rh(D)-negative individuals transfused with Rh(D)-positive red blood cells or blood components containing Rh(D)-positive RBCs
- Limitations/notes:
  - Safety and efficacy have not been established for causes of thrombocytopenia other than ITP, for previously splenectomized ITP patients, or for Rh(D)-negative patients being treated for ITP.
  - Not indicated as immunoglobulin replacement therapy for immunoglobulin deficiency syndromes.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
General notes
- Dose may be calculated using International Units (IU) or micrograms (mcg). Conversion: 1 mcg = 5 IU. Take care to avoid confusion between IU and mcg.
- Patient weight must be measured in kilograms (kg). (Calculation example provided below.)
- Bring product to room temperature before use; inspect for particulate matter and discoloration. Do not use if cloudy or particulate matter present.
- Single‑use only. Discard any unused portion.
- If dilution is desired prior to IV administration, use only normal saline. Do not use dextrose 5% in water (D5W).
- Administration routes vary by indication (see below).

Dose calculation example (as provided in source material)
- weight in lbs ÷ 2.21 = weight in kg
- weight in kg × selected IU (or mcg) dosing level = total dose (IU or mcg)
- total dose ÷ vial size = number of vials required

Dosing by indication (adult and pediatric dosing provided where available)

A. Immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP) — IV route ONLY
- Initial and subsequent dosing guidance (either IU/kg or mcg/kg):
  - Hemoglobin ≥ 10 g/dL:
    - Initial: 250 IU/kg (50 mcg/kg) given as a single IV injection (single dose or divided over two days if clinically chosen)
    - Subsequent: 250–300 IU/kg (50–60 mcg/kg) determined by clinical response (platelet counts, RBC, Hgb, reticulocyte)
  - Hemoglobin 8 to <10 g/dL:
    - Initial: 125–200 IU/kg (25–40 mcg/kg)
    - Subsequent: 125–200 IU/kg (25–40 mcg/kg)
  - Hemoglobin < 8 g/dL:
    - Use alternative treatments; product is not recommended
- Maximum dose: Safety and efficacy for doses > 300 IU/kg (60 mcg/kg) have not been established.
- Administration technique:
  - Administer entire IV dose into a suitable vein over 3–5 minutes.
  - Administer separately from other drugs.
  - If diluted, use normal saline only.

B. Suppression of Rh isoimmunization — IV or IM
- Routine antepartum prophylaxis (at ~28 weeks): 1,500 IU (300 mcg) once
- Postpartum prophylaxis: 600 IU (120 mcg) within 72 hours of delivery of an Rh(D)-positive infant; if maternal administration delayed beyond 72 hours but within 28 days, administer as soon as possible
- Threatened abortion: 1,500 IU (300 mcg) immediately
- Amniocentesis or chorionic villus sampling before 34 weeks: 1,500 IU (300 mcg) immediately after procedure; if administered early in pregnancy, repeat dosing every ~12 weeks during pregnancy to maintain passive anti‑D levels
- Procedures, manipulations, or abortion after 34 weeks: 600 IU (120 mcg) within 72 hours
- Administration technique:
  - IV administration: give separately from other drugs; recommended IV infusion rate (for suppression dosing) approximately 2 mL per 5–15 seconds
  - IM administration: inject into deltoid or anterolateral thigh. Avoid gluteal region due to risk of sciatic nerve injury; if gluteal injection unavoidable, use upper outer quadrant.

C. Incompatible transfusion or massive fetal hemorrhage — IV or IM
- Begin treatment within 72 hours of exposure.
- IV dosing per volume exposed:
  - For exposure to Rh(D)-positive whole blood: 45 IU per mL blood (9 mcg/mL); dosing interval every 8 hours until total calculated dose administered (intravenous regimen guidance provided in source)
  - For exposure to Rh(D)-positive red blood cells: 90 IU per mL cells (18 mcg/mL); administer IV until total calculated dose administered
- IM dosing per volume exposed (alternative):
  - Whole blood exposure: 60 IU per mL blood (12 mcg/mL) every 12 hours until total dose administered
  - Red blood cell exposure: 120 IU per mL cells (24 mcg/mL) every 12 hours until total dose administered
- Example total dosing regimens (listed in source material as reference amounts for cumulative dosing):
  - IV example total dose: 3,000 IU (600 mcg) with dosing frequency guidance every 8 hours (examples provided in sources)
  - IM example total dose: 6,000 IU (1,200 mcg) with dosing frequency guidance every 12 hours (examples provided in sources)
- Administer the exposure‑based dose until the calculated total dose is achieved.

Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

Titration and re‑treatment
- For ITP, repeat dosing frequency is determined by clinical response (platelet counts, hemoglobin, reticulocyte count); clinical monitoring guides need for subsequent doses.
- For antepartum prophylaxis given early, repeat dosing at ~12‑week intervals recommended to maintain passive anti‑D levels.

Product strengths (for dose planning)
- Clinical dosing strengths described in source documents: 600 IU (120 mcg), 1,500 IU (300 mcg), 2,500 IU (500 mcg), 5,000 IU (1,000 mcg), and 15,000 IU (3,000 mcg). (These are cited as examples of available vial strengths for dose calculation; do not infer national availability.)

Preparation
- Bring to room temperature before use.
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration; do not use if cloudy or particulate matter present.
- The solution is ready to use; no reconstitution required.
- Withdraw entire vial contents when using a vial to ensure accurate dose calculations; single‑use vials only.

## 5. Contraindications
List of contraindications from available sources:
- Known anaphylactic or severe systemic reaction to any human immune globulin product (generalized severe hypersensitivity).
- IgA‑deficient patients who have anti‑IgA antibodies or a history of hypersensitivity to the product or any component.
- Patients with a history of autoimmune hemolytic anemia, pre‑existing hemolysis, or those at high risk for hemolysis.
- Infants for the suppression of Rh(D) isoimmunization (contraindicated).
- Note: the product contains maltose (a corn‑derived disaccharide); patients with corn allergy should avoid the product due to risk of hypersensitivity (advised in warnings/precautions).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / Major Warning
- **Intravascular hemolysis (IVH) leading to death has been reported in patients treated for ITP.** IVH can progress to clinically compromising anemia and multi‑system organ failure including acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS); cases of severe anemia, acute renal insufficiency/failure, and disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) have been reported. 
  - This boxed warning is explicitly noted to NOT apply to Rh(D)-negative patients receiving the product for suppression of Rh isoimmunization. [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis:
  - Severe hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis can occur. If allergic signs develop (urticaria, chest tightness, wheeze, hypotension, anaphylaxis), stop infusion and institute appropriate treatment.
  - The product contains up to ≤ 40 mcg/mL IgA. Patients with anti‑IgA antibodies are at increased risk of severe hypersensitivity reactions and the product is contraindicated in such patients.
  - Contains maltose (corn‑derived); patients with corn allergy should avoid use.
- Intravascular hemolysis (ITP treatment):
  - IVH has been observed after administration for ITP and can occur suddenly after any exposure, even if prior doses were tolerated.
  - Monitor patients treated for ITP in a healthcare setting for at least 8 hours after administration.
  - Perform dipstick urinalysis for hematuria/hemoglobinuria at baseline, ~2 hours, ~4 hours, and before the end of the monitoring period.
  - Alert patients to report back pain, shaking chills, fever, dark/discolored urine or hemoglobinuria. Absence of symptoms within 8 hours does not exclude later IVH.
  - If IVH is suspected, obtain post‑treatment laboratory tests (plasma hemoglobin, haptoglobin, lactate dehydrogenase [LDH], plasma bilirubin direct and indirect).
  - If transfusion after administration is necessary in ITP patients, use Rh(D)-negative packed RBCs to avoid exacerbating hemolysis.
- Hemolysis (extravascular) and hematologic effects:
  - Postulated mechanism in ITP: anti‑D coats Rh(D)-positive RBCs leading to preferential clearance of coated RBC complexes by reticuloendothelial macrophages, thereby sparing antibody‑coated platelets. A side effect is decreased hemoglobin from extravascular hemolysis.
  - Pooled ITP study data showed a mean hemoglobin decrease of approximately 1.2 g/dL within 7 days after administration.
  - If signs of hemolysis are present (e.g., reticulocyte count > 3%) or if the direct antiglobulin test (DAT) is positive (not attributed to prior immune globulin), use alternative therapies rather than this product.
  - For baseline hemoglobin < 10 g/dL, consider a reduced dose of 125–200 IU/kg (25–40 mcg/kg) to minimize worsening anemia. For hemoglobin < 8 g/dL, alternative therapies are recommended.
- Transmissible infectious agents:
  - Because product is plasma‑derived, there is a risk of transmission of infectious agents despite donor screening and viral reduction steps. Discuss risks and benefits with patients; report suspected transfusion‑transmitted infections.
- Acute renal dysfunction/failure and osmotic nephropathy:
  - Acute renal insufficiency/failure, osmotic nephropathy, acute tubular necrosis, and proximal tubular nephropathy (and death) have been reported with IV immunoglobulin products. Ensure patients are not volume‑depleted before administration and monitor renal function in patients at risk.
- Thromboembolic events:
  - Thrombotic complications have been associated with intravenous immunoglobulin products. Use caution in patients with risk factors for thrombosis; consider assessing blood viscosity or other risk mitigation in high‑risk patients.
- Transfusion‑related acute lung injury (TRALI):
  - Monitor for acute respiratory signs after administration; test for anti‑neutrophil antibodies if TRALI suspected.
- Interference with serologic testing and vaccinations:
  - Passive transfer of antibodies may confound serologic testing.
  - Administration of immune globulin may transiently interfere with response to live virus vaccines (e.g., measles, mumps, rubella); follow vaccine guidance regarding timing of vaccination relative to immunoglobulin.
- Interference with certain blood glucose assays:
  - Maltose in product can interfere with certain point‑of‑care blood glucose methods (resulting in falsely elevated glucose readings). Be aware and use appropriate glucose testing methods.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline laboratory tests before administration for ITP patients: hemoglobin, reticulocyte count, RBC indices, platelet count, and other tests as clinically indicated.
- For ITP patients receiving therapy:
  - Monitor in healthcare setting for at least 8 hours after dosing.
  - Perform dipstick urinalysis at baseline, ~2 hours, ~4 hours, and before end of monitoring to detect hematuria/hemoglobinuria.
  - If IVH suspected, obtain plasma hemoglobin, haptoglobin, LDH, direct and indirect bilirubin, coagulation testing (if DIC suspected), and renal function tests (creatinine, urine output).
- Monitor renal function in patients at risk for renal dysfunction and monitor for signs of thrombosis, pulmonary complications, and hypersensitivity.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Vaccination interactions:
  - Administration of immune globulin products may transiently interfere with the immune response to live viral vaccines (e.g., measles, mumps, rubella). Timing of vaccination relative to product administration should follow applicable immunization guidance.
- Serologic testing:
  - Passive transfer of anti‑D and other antibodies may confound serologic testing results (e.g., antibody screens).
- Blood glucose testing:
  - Maltose in the product can lead to falsely elevated blood glucose readings with certain point‑of‑care glucose monitoring systems. Use glucose assays not affected by maltose or laboratory glucose methods as appropriate.
- Transfusion management:
  - If a patient requires RBC transfusion after receiving this product for ITP, use Rh(D)-negative packed RBCs to avoid worsening hemolysis.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- The therapeutic effect in ITP is not fully elucidated but is postulated to involve anti‑D binding to Rh(D)-positive RBCs, leading to formation of antibody‑coated RBC complexes that are preferentially cleared by macrophages of the reticuloendothelial system; this preferential clearance is thought to reduce clearance of antibody‑coated platelets, thereby raising platelet counts.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination (ADME) parameters: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Clinical effect: increase in platelet counts in treated Rh(D)-positive, non‑splenectomized ITP patients; pooled clinical data in ITP showed mean hemoglobin decrease of ~1.2 g/dL within 7 days attributable to hemolytic effects.
- Onset/duration specifics: Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Summary of clinical evidence:
  - The product has been studied in ITP clinical trials with pooled data indicating a mean hemoglobin decrease of about 1.2 g/dL within 7 days after administration.
  - The safety and efficacy have not been evaluated in clinical trials for non‑ITP thrombocytopenia causes, previously splenectomized patients, or Rh(D)-negative patients treated for ITP.
  - Detailed pivotal trial designs, primary efficacy outcomes, and statistical results are not specified in the available source excerpts.
- Suppression of Rh isoimmunization:
  - Clinical practice guidance within the source supports antepartum and postpartum prophylaxis and indicated dosing regimens; detailed trial data are not presented in the available sources.
