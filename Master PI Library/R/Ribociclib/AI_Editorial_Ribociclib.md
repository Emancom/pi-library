# Editorial Product Information: Ribociclib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ribociclib product information for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing protocols, and safety considerations into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s current approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ribociclib (as ribociclib succinate).
- **Chemical Class:** Inhibitor of cyclin-dependent kinases 4 and 6 (CDK4/6).
- **Brand Name(s):** **KISQALI** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EF02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Film-coated tablets containing 200 mg ribociclib.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Early Breast Cancer:** In combination with an aromatase inhibitor (AI) for the adjuvant treatment of patients with hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative stage II and III early breast cancer at high risk of recurrence [Australian-approved].
- **Advanced or Metastatic Breast Cancer:** In combination with an AI or fulvestrant for the treatment of patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer, as initial endocrine-based therapy or following prior endocrine therapy [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment must be initiated by a physician experienced in anticancer therapies. Ribociclib is administered on a 28-day cycle: once daily for 21 consecutive days followed by 7 days off treatment.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Early Breast Cancer:** The recommended dose is 400 mg (two 200 mg tablets) once daily. Treatment should continue for 3 years or until disease recurrence/unacceptable toxicity [Australian].
- **Advanced or Metastatic Breast Cancer:** The recommended dose is 600 mg (three 200 mg tablets) once daily. Treatment continues as long as clinical benefit is derived or until unacceptable toxicity [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed whole (do not chew, crush, or split) at approximately the same time each day, preferably in the morning.
- May be taken with or without food. 
- **Missed Doses:** If the patient vomits or misses a dose, an additional dose should not be taken that day. The next dose should be taken at the usual time.
- **Co-administration:** In pre/perimenopausal women and in men, endocrine therapy must be combined with a luteinising hormone-releasing hormone (LHRH) agonist.

### Dose Modifications for Adverse Reactions
Management of severe or intolerable toxicities may require interruption, reduction, or permanent discontinuation.
- **Reductions for Early BC:** Decrease from 400 mg to 200 mg. Discontinue if further reduction is needed.
- **Reductions for Advanced/Metastatic BC:** 
  - First reduction: 400 mg. 
  - Second reduction: 200 mg. 
  - Discontinue if <200 mg is required.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Baseline QT Interval:** Corrected QT interval (QTcF) >450 ms prior to treatment.
- **Cardiac Conditions:** Congenital long QT syndrome or patients at significant risk of developing Torsades de Pointes (TdP).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ribociclib or any excipients, including soya lecithin [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** Ribociclib causes concentration-dependent QTc prolongation. Monitor ECG at baseline, Day 14 of Cycle 1, and as clinically indicated. Monitor electrolytes (K, Ca, P, Mg) before treatment and for the first 6 cycles. Avoid use with tamoxifen due to increased QT risk.
- **Hepatobiliary Toxicity:** Reversible transaminase elevations are common; life-threatening hepatotoxicity is uncommon. Perform Liver Function Tests (LFTs) before initiation, every 2 weeks for the first 2 cycles, and at the start of the next 4 cycles.
- **Neutropenia:** Concentration-dependent decrease in neutrophil count is the most common adverse reaction. Perform Full Blood Count (FBC) before initiation and monitor every 2 weeks for the first 2 cycles.
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** Can be severe or fatal. Monitor for hypoxia, cough, or dyspnoea. Permanently discontinue if ILD/pneumonitis is Grade 3/4 or recurrent Grade 2.
- **Severe Cutaneous Reactions:** Toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) has been reported. Discontinue permanently if TEN is suspected.
- **Thromboembolic Events:** Pulmonary embolism and other events have been reported. Monitor patients at high risk.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Neutropenia, infections, leukopenia, headache, nausea, diarrhoea, fatigue, alopecia, vomiting, constipation, rash, back pain, cough, anaemia, abnormal LFTs, lymphopenia. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypocalcaemia, hypomagnesaemia, dizziness, peripheral oedema, pruritus, dyspnoea, stomatitis, oropharyngeal pain, decreased appetite, QT prolongation, syncope. |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | Life-threatening hepatotoxicity, Grade 3/4 ILD/pneumonitis. |
| **Not Known** | Toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), erythema multiforme, thrombosis. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Neutropenia, hepatobiliary toxicity, QT prolongation, and ILD/pneumonitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir, clarithromycin, itraconazole) significantly increase ribociclib exposure. Avoid concomitant use; if unavoidable, reduce ribociclib dose by 200 mg.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St John's Wort) significantly decrease exposure. Avoid concomitant use.
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Ribociclib is a moderate-to-strong CYP3A4 inhibitor. Use caution and consider dose reduction for sensitive substrates with narrow therapeutic indices (e.g., alfentanil, cyclosporine, fentanyl, tacrolimus).
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Avoid co-administration with antiarrhythmics (e.g., amiodarone, sotalol) and other QT-prolonging agents (e.g., clarithromycin, haloperidol).
- **Food Interactions:** Avoid grapefruit, pomegranate, star fruit, and Seville oranges.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Ribociclib can cause foetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before initiation.
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 21 days after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is unknown. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for at least 21 days after the last dose.
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggest ribociclib may impair male fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required in patients ≥65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:**
  - Mild or Moderate: No adjustment required.
  - Severe: Recommended starting dose is 200 mg once daily.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
  - Early Breast Cancer: No adjustment required [Australian].
  - Advanced/Metastatic Breast Cancer: No adjustment for mild (Child-Pugh A); reduce starting dose to 400 mg for moderate (Child-Pugh B) or severe (Child-Pugh C).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ribociclib is a selective inhibitor of CDK4 and CDK6. These kinases, when bound to D-cyclins, phosphorylate the retinoblastoma protein (pRb), leading to cell cycle progression from G1 to S phase. Inhibition arrests the cell cycle in G1.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ 1–4 hours. Absolute bioavailability ~66%.
  - **Distribution:** ~70% plasma protein bound. $V_{ss}/F$ ~1090 L.
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised via CYP3A4.
  - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (69%); 23% renal. Effective half-life ~32 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **NATALEE (Early BC):** Demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in invasive disease-free survival (iDFS) when 400 mg ribociclib was added to an AI (HR: 0.749).
- **MONALEESA-2 (mBC):** Ribociclib 600 mg + letrozole showed superior progression-free survival (PFS) (median 25.3 vs 16.0 months) and overall survival (OS) improvement (median 63.9 vs 51.4 months) compared to letrozole alone.
- **MONALEESA-3 (mBC):** Ribociclib + fulvestrant demonstrated significant PFS and OS benefits in both first-line and second-line settings.
- **MONALEESA-7 (mBC):** Confirmed efficacy in pre/perimenopausal women using ribociclib + NSAI + goserelin.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
- **Handling:** Return any unused or broken tablets to a pharmacist for safe disposal.
