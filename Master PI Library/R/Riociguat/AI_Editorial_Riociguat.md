# Editorial Product Information: Riociguat

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized, clinically accurate editorial overview for healthcare professionals, incorporating regulatory nuances and therapeutic guidelines.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Riociguat (a soluble guanylate cyclase [sGC] stimulator).
- **Brand Name:** **Adempas** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C02KX05.
- **Dosage Form and Strengths:** Film-coated tablets in 0.5 mg (white), 1 mg (pale yellow), 1.5 mg (yellow-orange), 2 mg (pale orange), and 2.5 mg (red-orange) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bayer Australia Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH):**
Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with WHO Functional Class II, III, or IV symptoms. It may be used as monotherapy or in combination with endothelin receptor antagonists or prostanoids (inhaled or subcutaneous). Specific etiologies include:
- Idiopathic PAH.
- Heritable PAH.
- PAH associated with connective tissue diseases.
- PAH associated with congenital heart disease [Australian].

**Chronic Thromboembolic Pulmonary Hypertension (CTEPH):**
Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with WHO Functional Class II, III, or IV symptoms who have:
- Inoperable CTEPH.
- Persistent or recurrent CTEPH following surgical treatment [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Starting Dose:** 1.0 mg three times daily (TDS), approximately 6 to 8 hours apart.
- **Alternative Starting Dose:** Consider 0.5 mg TDS for patients at increased risk of hypotension.
- **Titration:** Increase dosage in 0.5 mg increments every 2 weeks, up to a maximum of 2.5 mg TDS. Dosage should only be increased if systolic blood pressure (SBP) is $\ge$ 95 mmHg and the patient is asymptomatic for hypotension.
- **Maintenance Dose:** The highest tolerated dose should be maintained unless signs of hypotension occur. Maximum daily dose: 7.5 mg.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets can be taken with or without food.
- **Antacids:** If required, antacids (e.g., magnesium/aluminium hydroxide) must be taken at least 1 hour after **Adempas** administration to avoid reduced absorption.

### Missed Dose and Interruption
- **Missed Dose:** Continue with the next scheduled dose; do not double the dose.
- **Treatment Interruption:** If treatment is paused for 3 days or more, restart therapy at 1.0 mg TDS for 2 weeks, then follow the standard titration schedule [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Absolutely contraindicated; may cause fetal harm [Australian].
- **Lactation:** Contraindicated during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Co-administration with PDE-5 Inhibitors:** Specific (e.g., sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil) or non-specific (e.g., dipyridamole, theophylline) phosphodiesterase inhibitors [Australian].
- **Nitrates/Nitric Oxide Donors:** Any form (e.g., amyl nitrite) is contraindicated due to severe hypotensive risk [Australian].
- **Other sGC Stimulators:** Co-administration with other soluble guanylate cyclase stimulators is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Hypertension associated with Idiopathic Interstitial Pneumonias (PH-IIP):** Contraindicated due to increased mortality and serious adverse events [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to riociguat or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning
**Teratogenicity:** **Adempas** is contraindicated in pregnancy as it may cause birth defects. Female patients of reproductive potential must have negative pregnancy tests before initiation, monthly during treatment, and one month after discontinuation. Two reliable forms of contraception are required [Australian].

### General Warnings
- **Hypotension:** Riociguat reduces blood pressure. Use caution in patients with hypovolemia, autonomic dysfunction, resting hypotension (SBP < 95 mmHg), or severe left ventricular outflow obstruction.
- **Bleeding:** Increased risk of respiratory tract bleeding (haemoptysis), especially in patients on anticoagulants. Serious and fatal cases have been reported. Avoid in patients with a history of serious haemoptysis or prior bronchial arterial embolization.
- **Pulmonary Veno-occlusive Disease (PVOD):** Pulmonary vasodilators may worsen cardiovascular status in PVOD. If pulmonary edema occurs, discontinue treatment and evaluate for PVOD.
- **Smoking:** Cigarette smoking reduces riociguat exposure by 50–60%. Patients should be advised to stop smoking; dose adjustments may be needed if smoking status changes.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse effects are secondary to the relaxant effect on smooth muscle in the vasculature or gastrointestinal tract.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache, dizziness, dyspepsia, peripheral edema, nausea, diarrhea, vomiting. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nasopharyngitis, hypotension, palpitations, anemia, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GORD), constipation, gastritis, epistaxis, nasal congestion, dysphagia, abdominal pain. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pulmonary hemorrhage (serious/fatal cases reported in extension studies). |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Serious haemoptysis and pulmonary hemorrhage are the most critical risks [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Strong CYP and P-gp/BCRP Inhibitors:** (e.g., azole antifungals, HIV protease inhibitors). These significantly increase riociguat exposure. Consider a starting dose of 0.5 mg TDS and monitor closely for hypotension.
- **Strong CYP1A1 Inhibitors:** (e.g., erlotinib). May increase exposure, particularly in smokers.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine, St. John’s Wort). May decrease riociguat plasma concentrations.
- **Antacids:** Reduce riociguat bioavailability; separate dosing by 1 hour.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Nitrates & PDE-5 Inhibitors:** Contraindicated due to additive/synergistic systemic blood pressure lowering effects.
- **Warfarin:** No effect on prothrombin time or INR [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category X]. Riociguat was teratogenic in rats (cardiac malformations). Not studied in human pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Animal data show riociguat is secreted into milk; risk to nursing infants cannot be excluded.
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility were observed in animal studies at 5 to 7-fold human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 18 years; safety and efficacy not established. Adolescent animal studies showed increased bone mass.
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 65 years):** Higher plasma concentrations observed. Exercise particular care during titration due to higher hypotension risk (16.4% in elderly vs 7.1% in < 65 years).
- **Renal Impairment:** Not recommended if CrCl < 15 mL/min or on dialysis. Use with caution if CrCl 15–80 mL/min due to increased exposure and hypotension risk.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in moderate impairment (Child-Pugh B). Not recommended in severe impairment (Child-Pugh C) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Dual action: 1) Sensitizes sGC to endogenous nitric oxide (NO) by stabilizing NO-sGC binding; 2) Directly stimulates sGC independently of NO. This increases cGMP production, leading to vasodilation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability 94%. $T_{max}$ 1–1.5 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 95% protein bound (albumin, $\alpha$1-acid glycoprotein).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP1A1, 3A4, 2C8, and 2J2 to an active metabolite (1/10 to 1/3 potency).
    - **Elimination:** Excreted via renal (33–45%) and biliary/fecal (48–59%) routes. Half-life ~7 hours in healthy adults; ~13 hours in patients.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CHEST-1 (CTEPH):** A 16-week study showing a mean increase of 46m in 6-minute walking distance (6MWD) vs placebo ($p<0.0001$).
- **PATENT-1 (PAH):** A 12-week study showing a mean increase of 36m in 6MWD vs placebo ($p<0.0001$).
- **Long-term Extensions (CHEST-2/PATENT-2):** Safety and efficacy profiles remained consistent over longer durations (up to 2 years) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Store in the original container to protect from moisture [Australian].
