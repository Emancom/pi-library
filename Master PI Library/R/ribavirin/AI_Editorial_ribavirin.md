# Editorial Product Information: Ribavirin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) documents from the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA, Australia) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA, United States).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **ribavirin** for use by healthcare professionals, integrating different indications and dosage forms approved across jurisdictions.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ribavirin (a synthetic nucleoside analogue).
- **Chemical Class:** Nucleoside antimetabolite / Antiviral.
- **Brand Names:** **IBAVYR** [TGA]; Ribavirin for Inhalation Solution, USP (Generic) [FDA].
- **ATC Code:** J05AP01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Oral:** 200 mg coated tablets [TGA].
    - **Inhalation:** 6 g sterile, lyophilized powder in a 100 mL glass vial for reconstitution (to 20 mg/mL) [FDA].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral; Inhalation (aerosolized via SPAG-2 generator).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Clinect Pty Ltd [TGA]; Bryant Ranch Prepack (Repackaged), Cameron Pharmaceuticals/Hospira [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Hepatitis C (CHC):** Indicated in combination with other oral agents (e.g., sofosbuvir) for the treatment of CHC in adults [TGA].
- **Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV):** Indicated for the treatment of hospitalized infants and young children with severe lower respiratory tract infections due to RSV [FDA].
    - *Note:* Ribavirin for inhalation is **not** indicated for use in adults [FDA]. Treatment should be reserved for severe cases, as most RSV infections are self-limiting [FDA].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Adult Dosing (Chronic Hepatitis C - Oral) [TGA]
Ribavirin must be used in combination with other oral agents; monotherapy is ineffective.
- **General Dosing:** Administered orally in two divided doses daily with food.
- **Weight-Based Dosing (with Sofosbuvir):**
    - **< 75 kg:** 1,000 mg daily.
    - **≥ 75 kg:** 1,200 mg daily.
- **Duration:** 12 weeks (Genotype 2) or 16–24 weeks (Genotype 3).

### 3.2 Pediatric Dosing (RSV - Inhalation) [FDA]
- **Concentration:** 20 mg/mL (reconstituted).
- **Regimen:** Continuous aerosol administration for 12–18 hours per day for 3 to 7 days.
- **Equipment:** Must only be administered using a Small Particle Aerosol Generator (SPAG-2).

### 3.3 Dose Modifications (Anaemia) [TGA]
For oral administration in CHC, dose reduction to 600 mg/day or discontinuation is required based on haemoglobin (Hgb) levels:
- **No Cardiac Disease:** Reduce if Hgb < 10 g/dL; Discontinue if Hgb < 8.5 g/dL.
- **Stable Cardiac Disease:** Reduce if > 2 g/dL Hgb decrease during any 4-week period; Discontinue if Hgb < 12 g/dL despite 4 weeks of reduced dosing.

### 3.4 Administration Instructions
- **Oral:** Tablets should be taken with food to increase bioavailability [TGA].
- **Inhalation:** Reconstitute with a minimum of 75 mL Sterile Water for Injection, then dilute to 300 mL (final concentration 20 mg/mL). Discard solutions in the SPAG-2 unit every 24 hours [FDA].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated in women who are pregnant or may become pregnant during treatment [TGA, FDA].
- **Men with Pregnant Partners:** Oral ribavirin is contraindicated in men whose female partners are pregnant [TGA].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ribavirin or any excipients.
- **Hemoglobinopathies:** Patients with thalassemia major or sickle-cell anaemia [TGA].
- **Drug Combinations:** Co-administration with **didanosine** due to risks of fatal hepatic failure and peripheral neuropathy [TGA].
- **Cardiac Disease:** History of severe pre-existing or unstable cardiac disease in the previous six months [TGA].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### 5.1 Boxed Warnings [FDA]
- **Mechanical Ventilator Use:** Use in patients requiring mechanical ventilation should only be performed by staff familiar with the specific ventilator. Precautions must be taken to prevent drug precipitate accumulation, which can cause ventilator dysfunction and increased pulmonary pressure.
- **Respiratory Deterioration:** Initiation of aerosolized treatment in infants can lead to sudden deterioration of respiratory function. Monitoring is mandatory.
- **Teratogenicity:** Ribavirin is teratogenic in all animal species tested.

### 5.2 General Warnings and Precautions
- **Haemolytic Anaemia:** The primary toxicity of oral ribavirin. Onset is usually within 1–2 weeks of initiation. May lead to fatal or nonfatal myocardial infarction [TGA].
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** Concomitant use with **azathioprine** may cause pancytopenia [TGA].
- **Occupational Exposure:** Health care workers (HCWs) may be exposed during aerosol administration. Measurable urine levels have been detected in HCWs. Pregnant HCWs should avoid direct care of patients receiving aerosolized ribavirin [FDA].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Discontinue immediately if acute reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema) occur [TGA].

### 5.3 Monitoring Requirements
- **Haematology:** CBC (including Hgb) at baseline, week 2, and week 4, then as clinically indicated [TGA].
- **Pregnancy Testing:** Monthly testing during therapy and for 6 months post-therapy for female patients and female partners of male patients [TGA].
- **Cardiac:** ECG monitoring for patients with pre-existing cardiac abnormalities [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Oral Combination Therapy (Adult CHC) [TGA]
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Fatigue, Headache, Nausea, Insomnia, Pruritus, Anaemia, Irritability, Diarrhoea |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Asthenia, Rash, Decreased appetite, Chills, Pyrexia, Myalgia, Neutropenia |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | Severe depression, Suicidal ideation |

### 6.2 Inhalation Therapy (Pediatric RSV) [FDA]
- **Pulmonary:** Worsening of respiratory status, bronchospasm, pulmonary oedema, pneumothorax, apnea, bacterial pneumonia.
- **Cardiovascular:** Cardiac arrest, hypotension, bradycardia (in patients with underlying congenital heart disease).
- **Other:** Rash, conjunctivitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Didanosine:** Co-administration is contraindicated; ribavirin increases levels of the active metabolite (dideoxyadenosine 5’-triphosphate), increasing toxicity risk [TGA].
- **Azathioprine:** Risk of severe pancytopenia and myelotoxicity due to inhibited metabolism [TGA].
- **NRTIs (Zidovudine, Stavudine, Lamivudine):** In vitro data suggest ribavirin may reduce phosphorylation and efficacy, though clinical significance in HIV/HCV co-infection is variable [TGA].
- **CYP450:** Ribavirin does not inhibit or induce CYP450 enzymes; minimal potential for these interactions [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **TGA Category X:** High risk of fetal malformations and survival reduction.
    - **FDA:** Contraindicated due to demonstrated teratogenicity in all animal species.
    - **Requirements:** Two reliable forms of contraception must be used during and for 6 months after treatment [TGA].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not known if ribavirin is excreted in human milk. Because of the potential for serious reactions in infants, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug [TGA, FDA].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, ribavirin caused testicular lesions and sperm abnormalities. Effects were generally reversible within 1–2 spermatogenic cycles post-cessation [TGA, FDA].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated for RSV in infants/young children [FDA]. Not recommended for CHC in patients under 18 years [TGA].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher risk of toxic reactions due to age-related renal decline [TGA].
- **Renal Impairment:** Oral ribavirin is contraindicated in patients with CrCl < 50 mL/min [TGA].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in decompensated cirrhosis. Safety/efficacy restricted to Child-Pugh Class A in CHC trials [TGA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Increases mutation frequency in viral RNA genomes and inhibits HCV polymerase via ribavirin triphosphate. In RSV, it may act as an analogue of cellular metabolites like guanosine.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability increases with high-fat meals (oral). Inhaled ribavirin is absorbed systemically, but plasma levels are 85-98% lower than those required to reduce RSV in vitro.
    - **Distribution:** Extensively sequestered in red blood cells (ratio 60:1).
    - **Metabolism:** Reversible phosphorylation and degradative metabolism (deribosylation/amide hydrolysis).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (5–15% as parent drug). Terminal half-life is extremely long: ~120–170 hours (single oral dose) and ~40 days in RBCs.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CHC (Oral):** Pivotal trials (FISSION, POSITRON, FUSION, VALENCE) demonstrated SVR12 rates ranging from 50% to 94% when used with sofosbuvir, depending on genotype and prior treatment history [TGA].
- **RSV (Inhalation):** Controlled trials showed reduction in disease severity by day 3 and significant reduction in viral titers. In mechanically ventilated infants, it decreased duration of ventilation and oxygen requirements [FDA].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Oral Tablets:** Store below 25°C. Keep bottle tightly closed and protect from light [TGA].
- **Inhalation Powder:** Store vials at 25°C (excursions to 15–30°C permitted). Reconstituted solution is stable at room temperature for 24 hours [FDA].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [TGA].
- **United States:** Rx-only [FDA].
