# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].

### Dosing Schedules and Titration
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered in severe cases, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours (and no less than every 6 hours) [US].
- **Maintenance (Metabolic):** Subsequent daily doses are generally 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **Maintenance (ESRD):** Administered as a slow bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session. Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough carnitine levels [US].

### Monitoring and Administration Instructions
- **Plasma Monitoring:** Trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels should be monitored. Normal levels are typically 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L. The therapeutic goal for plasma free carnitine is 35 µmol/L to 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Parenteral Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cuation:** The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal impairment. Chronic high doses of the oral form in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency in ESRD Patients (Trials)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials for ESRD patients (regardless of causality) include [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (21–49%), chest pain (6–15%), flu syndrome (15–29%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), abdominal pain (5–21%), asthenia (8–12%), fever (5–12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), palpitations (3–8%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), dyspepsia (5–9%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%), rhinitis (6–11%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).

### Serious and Other Notable Effects
- **Seizures:** Reported in both patients with and without pre-existing seizure disorders [US].
- **Body Odor:** Less frequent; associated with high carnitine levels [US].
- **Gastritis:** Reported as a less frequent adverse reaction [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not specified in available sources (US lettered category system not explicitly cited in the provided Baxter document, though it notes no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose). Human studies are inadequate; use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother; discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate no impairment of fertility at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Recommended dosing for metabolic disorders is 50 mg/kg [US]. Specific age-related secondary deficiency definitions are noted for premature infants and newborns (concentrations below age-related normal) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used for treatment of carnitine deficiency in ESRD (see Dosage and Administration). Caution is advised regarding the oral formulation in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities resulting from the accumulation of toxic organic acids (e.g., in glutaric aciduria II, methyl malonic aciduria) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV administration of 20 mg/kg after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** A study of ~90 patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels. At 24 weeks, the 40 mg/kg group reached mean trough concentrations of 456 ± 162 µmol/L [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
