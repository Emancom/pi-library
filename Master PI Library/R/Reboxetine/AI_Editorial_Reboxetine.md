# Editorial Product Information: Reboxetine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for reboxetine. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate, structured reference.
- **Note:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian (TGA) source was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Reboxetine (as mesilate).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor (SNRI).
- **Brand Names:** Edronax [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06AX18.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 4 mg tablets (containing 5.2 mg reboxetine mesilate) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depression:** Treatment of major depression and prevention of depressive symptom relapse [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial/Maintenance Dose:** 4 mg twice daily (8 mg/day).
- **Titration:** If the clinical response is insufficient after three weeks, the dose may be increased to 10 mg/day [Australian].

### Special Population Adjustments
- **Elderly (>65 years):** Initial dose of 2 mg twice daily (4 mg/day). May be increased to a maximum of 6 mg/day after three weeks if required [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** In patients with renal insufficiency or moderate-to-severe hepatic insufficiency, the starting dose should be 2 mg twice daily, with subsequent increases based on patient tolerance [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Onset of clinical effect typically occurs after 14 days of treatment.
- Absorption is not significantly affected by food intake [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to reboxetine or any tablet excipients.
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use with MAOIs is strictly contraindicated.
- **Narrow-Angle Glaucoma:** Not recommended due to reboxetine’s weak mydriatic effects [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicide Risk and Clinical Worsening:** Depression is associated with an inherent risk of suicide. Patients (particularly those aged 18–24) must be closely monitored for suicidal ideation, self-harm, or worsening of symptoms, especially during the first few months of therapy or following dose adjustments [Australian].
- **Seizures:** Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of convulsive disorders. Discontinue therapy immediately if seizures occur [Australian].
- **Bipolar Disorder:** Reboxetine may trigger manic or hypomanic episodes in patients with underlying bipolar disorder. Screening for bipolar risk is required prior to initiation.
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening serotonin syndrome may occur, especially when used with other serotonergic agents (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, lithium). Symptoms include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular abnormalities.
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use with caution in patients with cardiac disease, hypertension, or a history of myocardial infarction.
    - **Orthostatic Hypotension:** More frequent at doses exceeding recommendations.
    - **Tachycardia:** Reboxetine may increase heart rate; monitor patients with hyperthyroidism or heart failure.
- **Mydriasis:** Use with caution in patients with increased intraocular pressure.
- **Urinary Retention:** Close supervision is required for patients with prostatic hypertrophy or a history of urinary retention [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events occur in approximately 80% of patients, with discontinuation rates around 9% [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Insomnia, dry mouth, constipation, nausea, hyperhidrosis. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, tachycardia, palpitations, vasodilatation, decreased appetite, chills, erectile dysfunction, ejaculation disorder, urinary hesitation/retention. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Paraesthesia, blood pressure changes (hypertension/hypotension), dyspnoea, vertigo, pyrexia. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Mydriasis, glaucoma, hyponatraemia, seizures, hallucinations, Serotonin Syndrome. |

- **Gender Differences:** Urologic events (hesitancy, incomplete bladder emptying) are significantly more common in males (31.4%) than females (7.0%) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Metabolism:** Reboxetine is primarily metabolised by CYP3A4. 
    - **Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, erythromycin, fluvoxamine) increase reboxetine plasma concentrations by approximately 50% [Australian].
    - **Inducers:** Compounds like phenytoin, carbamazepine, or phenobarbital may lower reboxetine serum levels [Australian].
- **CYP2D6:** Reboxetine is not metabolised by CYP2D6; therefore, inhibitors like fluoxetine or paroxetine do not significantly affect its pharmacokinetics.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Risk of severe reactions; a washout period of at least 14 days is required after stopping an MAOI before starting reboxetine.
- **Serotonergic Agents:** Increased risk of Serotonin Syndrome.
- **Antihypertensives:** May exacerbate orthostatic hypotension.
- **Ergot Derivatives:** Concomitant use may lead to increased blood pressure [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Does not appear to potentiate cognitive impairment, though alcohol use is generally discouraged with antidepressants.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. Animal studies show no clear teratogenic effects but indicate increased post-implantation loss and delayed ossification. Use only if potential benefits clearly outweigh risks to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Reboxetine is excreted in animal milk. Data in humans is unavailable; therefore, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal studies at doses up to 90 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children or adolescents under 18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Increased risk of tachycardia and orthostatic hypotension. Dose reduction is mandatory (see Section 3).
- **Renal Impairment:** Systemic exposure (AUC) can increase up to threefold in severe renal insufficiency. Initial dose reduction is required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** AUC and half-life are approximately doubled in patients with moderate-to-severe hepatic failure. Initial dose reduction is required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective and potent inhibitor of noradrenaline reuptake. It possesses very weak effects on serotonin reuptake and does not affect dopamine uptake.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases the availability of noradrenaline in the synaptic cleft. It has negligible affinity for adrenergic, muscarinic, or histaminergic receptors.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Approximately 94%.
    - **Protein Binding:** 97% (primarily to $\alpha_1$-acid glycoprotein).
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 13 hours.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (78%), with only 10% excreted as unchanged drug [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** In five short-term (4–8 week) placebo-controlled studies, reboxetine demonstrated a significantly higher response rate (51.2%) compared to placebo (43.6%) based on HAMD total score reduction.
- **Active Comparators:** Studies against imipramine and fluoxetine showed comparable efficacy.
- **Maintenance:** A 1-year study confirmed that reboxetine is effective in preventing relapse; 71% of patients remained relapse-free compared to those on placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Protection:** Keep tablets in the original carton (blister packs) to protect from light. Bottles should be kept tightly closed to protect from light and moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** [Australian]: Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
