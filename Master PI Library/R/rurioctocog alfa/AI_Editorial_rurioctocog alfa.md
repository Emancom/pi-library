# Editorial Product Information: Rurioctocog alfa pegol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) documents: 
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Product Information for ADYNOVATE (rurioctocog alfa pegol).
- **Note on Discrepancy**: While the provided context labeled Document 2 as a US (FDA) PI for rurioctocog alfa, the actual content of the provided US document pertains to Levocarnitine. As these are different therapeutic entities, Document 2 has been excluded from this synthesis to ensure clinical accuracy for rurioctocog alfa pegol. Consequently, US-specific data for rurioctocog alfa pegol is marked as "Not specified in available sources" throughout this document.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Rurioctocog alfa pegol (Recombinant Coagulation Factor VIII, PEGylated).
- **Chemical Class**: GlycPEGylated full-length recombinant human coagulation factor VIII.
- **Brand Names**: 
    - **ADYNOVATE** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: B02BD02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**:
    - Lyophilised powder for injection supplied in single-use vials [Australian].
    - Nominal potencies: 250, 500, 750, 1000, 1500, 2000, and 3000 International Units (IU) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous (IV) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**:
    - **Takeda Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd** [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Haemophilia A (congenital factor VIII deficiency)**: 
    - Control and prevention of bleeding episodes [Australian].
    - Routine prophylaxis to prevent or reduce bleeding frequency [Australian].
    - Perioperative management/surgical prophylaxis [Australian].
- **Limitations of Use**: Not indicated for the treatment of von Willebrand disease [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- Dosing should be individualised based on the patient's weight, severity of factor VIII (FVIII) deficiency, and clinical response.
- One IU of FVIII activity per kg body weight is expected to raise plasma FVIII activity by 2 IU/dL [Australian].
- **Formula**: Required units (IU) = Body weight (kg) x Desired FVIII rise (%) x 0.5.

### On-Demand Treatment (Bleeding Episodes)
- **Early/Minor (e.g., oral bleeding, muscle):** FVIII level 20–40%; repeat every 12–24 hours for at least 1 day.
- **Extensive (e.g., haematoma):** FVIII level 30–60%; repeat every 12–24 hours for 3–4 days or until resolution.
- **Life-Threatening:** FVIII level 60–100%; repeat every 8–24 hours until threat resolved.

### Perioperative Management
- **Minor Surgery:** FVIII level 30–60% every 24 hours (12–24 hours for patients <6 years) for at least 1 day.
- **Major Surgery:** FVIII level 80–100% (pre- and post-op); repeat every 8–24 hours (6–24 hours for patients <6 years) until wound healing, then continue for 7+ days at 30–60% FVIII activity.

### Routine Prophylaxis
- **Adults and Adolescents (≥12 years):** 40–50 IU/kg twice weekly (3–4 day intervals). Doses may be adjusted up to 60 IU/kg [Australian].
- **Children (<12 years):** 40–60 IU/kg twice weekly. Doses may be adjusted up to 80 IU/kg [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer IV over 5 minutes or less (maximum rate 10 mL/min) [Australian].
- Use within 3 hours of reconstitution; do not refrigerate after reconstitution.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known life-threatening hypersensitivity (including anaphylaxis) to rurioctocog alfa pegol, the parent molecule (octocog alfa), mouse or hamster protein, or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity**: Allergic-type reactions and anaphylaxis are possible. Discontinue immediately if symptoms occur.
- **Neutralising Antibodies (Inhibitors)**: Formation of FVIII inhibitors is a known complication. Monitor all patients via clinical observation and laboratory tests (Bethesda assay). Risk is highest in the first 20 exposure days [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Risk**: Substitution therapy may increase risk in patients with existing cardiovascular risk factors.
- **Catheter-Related Complications**: Risk of infection, bacteraemia, and site thrombosis if a central venous access device (CVAD) is used.
- **Sodium Content**: Contains 0.45 mmol (10 mg) sodium per vial, which should be considered for patients on a controlled sodium diet [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%)**: 
    - Headache (11.2% by subject) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%)**: 
    - Diarrhoea, Nausea, Dizziness, Rash, Urticaria [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%)**: 
    - Factor VIII inhibition (previously treated patients), Ocular Hyperaemia, Hypersensitivity, Pruritic Rash, Flushing, Infusion-related reactions [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects**: 
    - **Anaphylaxis** (observed in post-marketing).
    - **FVIII Inhibition**: Insufficient clinical response may indicate inhibitor development.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- No interactions between rurioctocog alfa pegol and other medicinal products have been reported [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Australian Category B2**: Safety not established in humans. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. Use only if clearly needed.
    - **US**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Use only if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Fertility**: Effects on fertility have not been established [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**:
    - Approved for all age ranges. Children <12 years may require higher or more frequent dosing due to higher clearance and lower half-life compared to adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: 
    - Clinical studies did not include subjects aged 65 and over [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: 
    - Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: A full-length recombinant FVIII (derived from octocog alfa) covalently conjugated to a 20 kDa polyethylene glycol (PEG) reagent. PEGylation increases the half-life by reducing LRP-1 receptor-mediated clearance [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Replaces missing coagulation factor VIII, acting as a cofactor for activated factor IX to convert factor X to its activated form, ultimately leading to thrombin and fibrin clot formation.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Half-life**: 1.4 to 1.5-fold extension compared to the parent molecule (octocog alfa) in adults [Australian].
    - **Terminal Half-life (Adults)**: ~14.7 hours [Australian].
    - **Terminal Half-life (Children <6 years)**: ~11.8 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study (Adolescents/Adults)**: A multicentre, open-label trial (N=137) compared twice-weekly prophylaxis (40–50 IU/kg) to on-demand treatment.
    - **Results**: Median Annualised Bleed Rate (ABR) was 1.9 for the prophylaxis arm versus 41.5 for the on-demand arm [Australian].
- **Paediatric Study (<12 years)**: A separate study (N=66) used a 40–60 IU/kg twice-weekly regimen.
    - **Results**: Median overall ABR was 2.0. Approximately 38% of subjects experienced zero bleeding episodes [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration**: Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Room Temperature Option**: May be stored at room temperature (up to 30°C) for a single period of up to 3 months. Once removed from refrigeration, it must not be returned [Australian].
- **Light Protection**: Store vials in the original box.
- **Post-Reconstitution**: Use immediately or within 3 hours. Do not refrigerate after reconstitution.
