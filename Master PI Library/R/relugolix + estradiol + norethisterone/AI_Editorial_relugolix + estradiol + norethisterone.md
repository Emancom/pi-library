# Editorial Product Information: relugolix + estradiol + norethisterone

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **relugolix + estradiol + norethisterone**
- Chemical class:
  - Relugolix: gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) receptor antagonist
  - Estradiol: estrogen
  - Norethisterone (norethisterone acetate): progestogen
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of last revision/update of the provided product information: August 2025 [Australian].
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of moderate to severe symptoms of uterine fibroids in adult women of reproductive age.
- Symptomatic treatment of endometriosis in adult women with a history of previous medical or surgical treatment for their endometriosis.
- Where indications differ by region: Not specified in available sources (only Australian PI provided).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dose: one tablet once daily containing 40 mg relugolix, 1 mg estradiol (as hemihydrate) and 0.5 mg norethisterone acetate (strengths as provided in the available source).

### Dosing by indication
- Same daily dosing for uterine fibroids and endometriosis indications in adults as described above.

### Administration instructions
- Take once daily, at about the same time each day, with or without food.
- Take with some liquid as needed.
- When initiating treatment, the first tablet should be taken within 5 days of the onset of menstrual bleeding. If started on another day in the cycle, initial irregular and/or heavy bleeding may occur.
- Therapy can be continued without interruption; discontinuation should be considered on entry into menopause because symptoms commonly regress.

### Duration limits and monitoring-related guidance
- Use is recommended to be limited to 24 months. Extension beyond 24 months should be conditional on stability of bone mineral density (BMD) by DXA and reassessment of individual risk/benefit.
- DXA (dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry) assessment of BMD is recommended at baseline, after one year of treatment, and annually thereafter.

### Contraception and fertility guidance
- Discontinue any hormonal contraception prior to starting relugolix + estradiol + norethisterone (concomitant use of hormonal contraceptives is contraindicated).
- Use nonhormonal contraception for at least 1 month after initiation.
- After at least one month of therapy, the combination inhibits ovulation at the recommended dose and provides adequate contraception.
- Ovulation returns rapidly after discontinuation; alternative contraception must be started immediately when stopping therapy.
- If a dose is missed: take as soon as possible and then continue next day at usual time.
- If doses are missed for 2 or more consecutive days, use a nonhormonal method of contraception for the next 7 days of treatment.

### Missed dose instructions
- See contraceptive/missed-dose guidance above.

## 5. Contraindications
(Complete list as stated in the available source)
- Hypersensitivity to active substances or any excipients.
- Past or present venous thromboembolic disorder (e.g., deep venous thrombosis, pulmonary embolism).
- Past or present arterial thromboembolic cardiovascular disease (e.g., myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular accident, ischemic heart disease).
- Known thrombophilic disorders (e.g., protein C, protein S or antithrombin deficiency, activated protein C resistance, including Factor V Leiden).
- Known osteoporosis.
- Headaches with focal neurological symptoms or migraine with aura.
- Known or suspected sex steroid–influenced malignancies (e.g., genital organs or breasts).
- Presence or history of liver tumours (benign or malignant).
- Presence or history of severe hepatic disease as long as liver function values have not returned to normal.
- Pregnancy or suspected pregnancy.
- Breastfeeding.
- Genital bleeding of unknown aetiology.
- Concomitant use of hormonal contraceptives.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Highlighted warnings
- None specifically boxed in the provided source text. (If regional boxed warnings exist in other jurisdictions, they are not specified in the available sources.)

### General warnings and precautions
- Relugolix + estradiol + norethisterone must be prescribed only after careful diagnosis and appropriate medical assessment, including full medical and family history and blood pressure measurement.
- Pregnancy must be ruled out prior to initiation and prior to re-initiation.
- Discontinue and investigate any unexplained vaginal bleeding.

### Thromboembolic risk
- Products containing estrogen and progestogen increase risk of arterial and venous thromboembolism (ATE and VTE) compared with non-use. The specific risk with this combination has not been established.
- If ATE/VTE occurs, discontinue treatment immediately.
- Consider multiple additional risk factors that markedly increase VTE/ATE risk (examples listed below). Evaluate risk before prescribing.

  Risk factors for VTE include (non‑exhaustive):
  - Obesity (BMI > 30 kg/m2)
  - Prolonged immobilisation, major surgery, major trauma (temporary discontinuation advised in elective surgery at least 4 weeks before; resume two weeks after remobilisation)
  - Positive family history of VTE at a young age
  - Medical conditions associated with VTE (cancer, systemic lupus erythematosus, haemolytic uraemic syndrome, chronic inflammatory bowel disease, sickle cell disease)
  - Increasing age (particularly >35 years)

  Risk factors for ATE include (non‑exhaustive):
  - Increasing age (>35 years)
  - Smoking
  - Hypertension
  - Obesity (BMI >30 kg/m2)
  - Positive family history of ATE at a young age
  - Migraine (worsening or new focal neurologic features)
  - Diabetes mellitus, hyperhomocysteinaemia, valvular heart disease, atrial fibrillation, dyslipoproteinaemia, systemic lupus erythematosus

- Advise patients to seek urgent medical attention for symptoms suggesting DVT, PE, myocardial infarction, or cerebrovascular accident.

### Bone mineral density (BMD)
- Treatment is associated with reductions in BMD in some women. Mean BMD decrease during the first year was 0.69% (with declines >3% in 21% of patients in the studied population). An initial non‑clinically relevant decrease stabilised after 12–24 weeks and remained stable thereafter (observed up to 2 years in available data).
- Assess BMD by DXA at baseline, after 52 weeks, and annually while on therapy. Limit use to 24 months unless benefit outweighs risk and BMD is stable on reassessment.
- Evaluate history of low-trauma fractures and other osteoporosis risk factors before initiating; consider baseline DXA in such patients. Do not initiate if projected BMD loss risk exceeds expected treatment benefit.

### Hepatic effects and liver tumours
- Contraindicated in women with liver tumours (benign or malignant) or severe hepatic disease if liver function has not returned to normal.
- Treatment must be discontinued if jaundice develops.
- Asymptomatic transient ALT elevations ≥3× ULN occurred in <1% of participants; acute liver test abnormalities may necessitate treatment discontinuation until tests normalise.

### Changes in menstrual bleeding pattern
- Treatment commonly reduces menstrual blood loss and often leads to amenorrhoea within the first 2 months.
- Uterine fibroid outcomes: Week 24 — amenorrhoea ~51.6%, cyclic bleeding ~15.4%, irregular bleeding ~31.9%; Week 52 — amenorrhoea ~70.6%.
- Endometriosis outcomes: Week 24 — amenorrhoea ~65.2%.
- Inform patients of potential bleeding pattern changes. In persistent excessive bleeding, notify the physician.

### Reduced ability to recognise pregnancy
- Amenorrhoea or altered bleeding can obscure recognition of pregnancy. Perform pregnancy testing if pregnancy suspected and discontinue treatment if pregnancy is confirmed.

### Uterine fibroid prolapse/expulsion
- Submucosal fibroids may prolapse or be expelled during treatment, sometimes with transient worsening of bleeding. Advise known/suspected submucosal fibroid patients to contact their physician if severe bleeding reoccurs after initial improvement.

### Mood disorders and suicidal ideation
- GnRH receptor antagonists, including this combination, have been associated with mood disorders (including depression) and suicidal ideation. Evaluate patients with a history of mood disorders prior to initiation. Advise patients to report mood changes and to seek immediate help for suicidal ideation or behaviour. Discontinue if severe depression occurs.

### Hypertension
- Small increases in blood pressure have been reported; clinically relevant increases are rare. If sustained clinically significant hypertension develops, treat hypertension and reassess continued therapy. If discontinued for hypertension, therapy may be resumed if blood pressure is controlled.

### Gallbladder disease
- Gallbladder disease (cholelithiasis, cholecystitis) has been reported to occur or worsen with estrogen/progestogen use; evidence of association with this product is inconclusive.

### Lactose
- The product contains lactose; patients with rare hereditary problems of galactose intolerance, total lactase deficiency, or glucose‑galactose malabsorption should not take this product.

### Use in renal impairment
- Exposure to relugolix is increased in moderate or severe renal impairment; however, no dose adjustment is required according to the available source.
- The amount of relugolix removed by haemodialysis is unknown.

### Use in hepatic impairment
- No dose adjustment required for mild to moderate hepatic impairment per available data.
- Contraindicated in severe hepatic disease if liver function is abnormal.

### Paediatric and elderly use
- Paediatric (under 18 years): safety and efficacy not established; no data available.
- Elderly: no relevant use in the elderly population for the indicated conditions.

### Effects on laboratory tests
- Estrogens and progestogens may influence laboratory tests (liver, thyroid, adrenal, renal function; carrier proteins; lipids; carbohydrate metabolism; coagulation/fibrinolysis), generally within normal ranges.

## 8. Drug Interactions
(Interactions and clinical management described in the available source)

### Pharmacokinetic interactions — effects of other medicines on relugolix
- Relugolix is a P-glycoprotein (P-gp) substrate.
  - Concomitant oral P-gp inhibitors are **not recommended**. Example: erythromycin (P-gp and moderate CYP3A4 inhibitor) increased relugolix AUC by 4.1-fold and Cmax by 3.8-fold.
  - Classes/agents that may increase relugolix exposure: certain anti-infectives (erythromycin, clarithromycin, gentamicin, tetracycline), azole antifungals (ketoconazole, itraconazole), some antihypertensives (carvedilol, verapamil), antiarrhythmics (amiodarone, propafenone, quinidine), antianginal (ranolazine), cyclosporine, HIV/HCV protease inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir).
  - If co‑administration with once- or twice-daily oral P‑gp inhibitors is unavoidable (e.g., azithromycin), take relugolix first and separate dosing of the P‑gp inhibitor by at least 6 hours and monitor patients more frequently for adverse reactions.
- Strong CYP3A4 and/or P‑gp inducers are **not recommended**.
  - Example: rifampicin reduced relugolix Cmax by 23% and AUC by 55%.
  - Inducers include certain anticonvulsants (carbamazepine, topiramate, phenytoin, phenobarbital, primidone, oxcarbazepine), anti-infectives (rifampicin, rifabutin, griseofulvin), herbal St. John’s wort, bosentan, some HIV/HCV agents, efavirenz.
  - Reduced relugolix concentrations may decrease therapeutic effect.

- CYP3A4 inhibitors lacking P-gp inhibition (e.g., voriconazole as cited) did not increase relugolix exposure in a clinically meaningful way in available data. A weak CYP3A4 inhibitor (atorvastatin) did not change relugolix exposure meaningfully.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions — effects of relugolix on other medicines
- Relugolix is a weak inducer of CYP3A4.
  - In a study with midazolam (sensitive CYP3A4 substrate), relugolix 40 mg daily decreased midazolam AUC by 18% and Cmax by 26%; not expected to cause clinically meaningful effects on most CYP3A4 substrates based on this data.
- Relugolix is an inhibitor of breast cancer resistant protein (BCRP) in vitro.
  - In a study with rosuvastatin (BCRP and OATP1B1 substrate), coadministration with relugolix 40 mg daily decreased rosuvastatin AUC by 13% and Cmax by 23% — effects not considered clinically meaningful in the available data.
- Relugolix may cause saturation of intestinal P‑gp at the 40 mg dose (pharmacokinetic nonlinearity noted); clinical relevance for other P‑gp substrates is not fully established in the available source.

### Interactions with estradiol and norethisterone components
- CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) may increase circulating concentrations of estradiol and norethisterone.
- CYP enzyme inducers (e.g., anticonvulsants: phenobarbital, phenytoin, carbamazepine; anti-infectives: rifampicin, rifabutin, nevirapine, efavirenz) may increase metabolism of estrogens and progestogens and reduce their exposure; long‑term concomitant use of hepatic enzyme inducers with this product is not recommended.
- Ritonavir and nelfinavir have dual inhibitory and inductive properties and may decrease exposure of sex steroids.
- Herbal preparations containing St John’s wort may induce metabolism of estrogens and progestogens potentially reducing estrogen exposure and reducing bone-protective effects.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant hormonal contraceptives: contraindicated.
- Additive risk of thromboembolism with other estrogen/progestogen–containing products.

### Management notes
- Avoid concomitant oral P‑gp inhibitors and strong CYP3A4/P‑gp inducers where possible; if unavoidable, manage per the specific recommendations above (dosing separation, enhanced monitoring, avoid coadministration).
- Monitor for loss of efficacy when co‑administered with inducers and for increased toxicity when co‑administered with P‑gp inhibitors.
- Monitor blood pressure, mood, liver function tests, and BMD per recommendations.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy is contraindicated. Pregnancy must be ruled out before initiating or re‑initiating therapy.
- The product commonly causes amenorrhoea or changes in bleeding which may delay recognition of pregnancy; perform pregnancy testing if pregnancy suspected and discontinue treatment if pregnancy confirmed.
- Regional pregnancy category information: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Breastfeeding is contraindicated (product listed as contraindicated during breastfeeding in the available source).

### Fertility
- The product inhibits ovulation after at least one month of use and provides adequate contraception while used as recommended.
- Ovulation returns rapidly after discontinuation; patients should initiate alternative contraception immediately upon stopping therapy if pregnancy is to be avoided.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and efficacy have not been established in children and adolescents (<18 years); no data available.

### Elderly use
- No relevant use in the elderly population for the indicated conditions; no dosing recommendations provided.

### Renal impairment
- No dose adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment per available data.
- Exposure to relugolix is increased in moderate or severe renal impairment; clinical significance considered manageable without dose adjustment in the available source.
- Effect of haemodialysis on relugolix clearance is unknown.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dose adjustment required for mild or moderate hepatic impairment per available data.
- Contraindicated in severe hepatic disease if liver function tests have not returned to normal; refer to contraindications and warnings.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Relugolix: GnRH receptor antagonist — suppresses ovarian production of estrogen and progesterone.
- Estradiol: exogenous estrogen supplementation component.
- Norethisterone (norethisterone acetate): progestogen component to mitigate hypoestrogenic effects and provide endometrial protection and contraceptive effect.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Combined regimen maintains estradiol levels in the range observed in the early follicular phase of the menstrual cycle (per available data).
- Suppression of ovulation: observed after at least 1 month of treatment at the recommended dose.

### Pharmacokinetics (selected)
- Absorption/administration: product may be taken with or without food.
- Relugolix is a substrate of intestinal P‑gp; exhibits more-than-dose-proportional pharmacokinetics over 10–120 mg range (suggests potential P‑gp saturation at 40 mg).
- Relugolix interactions:
  - Erythromycin (P‑gp and moderate CYP3A4 inhibitor) increased relugolix AUC by 4.1-fold and Cmax by 3.8-fold.
  - Rifampicin (strong CYP3A4 and P‑gp inducer) reduced relugolix Cmax by 23% and AUC by 55%.
  - Relugolix decreased midazolam AUC by 18% and Cmax by 26% (weak CYP3A4 induction).
  - Relugolix decreased rosuvastatin AUC by 13% and Cmax by 23% in a drug–interaction study.
- Estradiol and norethisterone metabolism: substrates of hepatic drug‑metabolising enzymes (notably CYP3A4); levels may be increased by CYP inhibitors and decreased by inducers.
