# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information:
    - **United States (FDA)**: "Levocarnitine Injection" (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class**: Carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) involved in fatty acid metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s)**: Generic (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic)**: For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders**:
    - **Initial/Loading Dose**: 50 mg/kg may be given as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection or by infusion.
    - **Maintenance Dose**: Subsequent daily doses should generally be 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
    - **Frequency**: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; intervals must never be less than every 6 hours.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
    - **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration**: Administer as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Titration**: Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

### Monitoring
- Pre-therapy plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained.
- **Target Levels**: Plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations is recommended.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before re-administration [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Seizure Activity**: Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients both with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Accumulation of Metabolites**: In patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis, chronic administration of high doses of *oral* levocarnitine may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD Trials)
In clinical trials of dialysis patients, the following events were reported regardless of causality [US]:
- **General**: Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (30–49%), flu syndrome (25–40%), headache (22–37%), infection (10–24%), chest pain (6–15%), fever (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%).
- **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (14–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive**: Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–19%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional**: Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–17%).

### Rare and Serious Effects
- **Neurologic**: Seizures (frequency not specified) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity**: Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema [US].
- **Gastrointestinal**: Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis have been noted less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports indicate that levocarnitine may increase the International Normalized Ratio (INR) in patients receiving warfarin. 
- **Management**: Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Category**: Not specified by letter category in source. 
    - **Data**: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times human dose based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate studies in pregnant women exist. 
    - **Recommendation**: Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Benefits to the mother should be weighed against risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Refer to the "Metabolic Disorders" dosing section. Safety and efficacy follow the same weight-based guidelines as adults [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment**: Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For IV use in ESRD, refer to Section 3 [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. In patients with metabolic defects or ESRD, it aids in the excretion of excess organic acids or acylCoA esters by forming acylcarnitine [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism**: Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination**: 
    - Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. 
    - Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: Two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials were conducted in chronic hemodialysis patients. While IV levocarnitine significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations, the definitive effect on clinical outcomes (signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency) in the ESRD population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions**: Protect from freezing and avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability**: Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature when mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) in PVC bags [US].
