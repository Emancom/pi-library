# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesizes regulatory information to facilitate clinical decision-making.
*   **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) approved by regulatory authorities in a specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should consult the local PI for the most current and legally binding instructions.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule; amino acid derivative.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01 [Not specified in available sources; included for clinical context].
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Intravenous solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    *   **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    *   **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours.
    *   **Max Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg.
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   **Administration Timing:** Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   **Dose Adjustments:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels, typically as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Monitoring
*   **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** Prior to starting therapy, obtain a baseline concentration. 
*   **Target Range:** Free carnitine levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations is recommended.

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, discontinue therapy and initiate appropriate medical management.
*   **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, there may be an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures.
*   **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic administration of high doses of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). The relevance to intravenous administration is not explicitly stated but warrants caution in renal impairment [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **Category:** Not explicitly lettered, but animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
*   **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.

### Breastfeeding
*   **Safety:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied.
*   **Recommendation:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug.

### Fertility
*   No mutagenic effects were found in standard assays (Salmonella, Saccharomyces, Schizosaccharomyces) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders follows the mg/kg weight-based recommendations provided in Section 3.
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of high-dose oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. IV administration in ESRD is an approved indication (see Section 3 for dosing).
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
*   **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours.
*   **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging:** Studies across 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservative.
*   **Stability:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL).
