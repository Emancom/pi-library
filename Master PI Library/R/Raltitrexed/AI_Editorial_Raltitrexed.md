# Editorial Product Information: Raltitrexed

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of raltitrexed, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Raltitrexed (folate analogue, anti-metabolite).
- **Brand Name:** **TOMUDEX** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for injection containing 2 mg of raltitrexed per vial.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Advanced Colorectal Cancer:** Indicated as a single agent for the palliative treatment of advanced disease, including distant metastases or unresectable local disease [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Standard Dose:** 3 mg/m² administered as a single intravenous infusion.
- **Frequency:** Repeated every 3 weeks in the absence of toxicity. 
- **Dose Escalation:** Not recommended; doses above 3 mg/m² are associated with increased risks of life-threatening or fatal toxicity.

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute the 2 mg vial with 4 mL sterile Water for Injections (concentration 0.5 mg/mL).
- **Dilution:** Further dilute the dose in 50 to 250 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose.
- **Infusion Rate:** Administer over a period of 15 minutes.
- **Handling:** Raltitrexed is a cytotoxic agent. Reconstitution should be performed by trained personnel in a designated area. Handling by pregnant women is not recommended.

### Dose Adjustment Based on Toxicity
Doses must be modified or withheld based on the worst grade of gastrointestinal (diarrhoea/mucositis) or haematological (neutropenia/thrombocytopenia) toxicity observed in the previous cycle. Subsequent doses should be reduced (to 75% or 50% of the previous dose) as per the toxicity grade. Treatment must be discontinued for Grade 4 gastrointestinal toxicity or combined Grade 2/3 gastrointestinal and Grade 4 haematological toxicity.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to raltitrexed or any of its excipients.
- **Pregnancy and Breastfeeding:** Current pregnancy, women who may become pregnant during treatment, or breastfeeding women.
- **Renal Impairment:** Patients with severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance <25 mL/min).
- **Drug Interactions:** Concomitant administration of folinic acid (calcium folinate), folic acid, or vitamin preparations containing these agents [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Supervision:** Must only be administered under the supervision of a physician experienced in cancer chemotherapy and toxicity management.
- **Gastrointestinal Toxicity:** Extreme care is required for patients with signs of diarrhoea or mucositis. Severe diarrhoea (Grade 3/4) can be life-threatening and may be associated with concurrent myelosuppression.
- **Haematological Monitoring:** Full blood counts (including differential and platelets) must be performed prior to every treatment. Pre-treatment thresholds: WBC >4,000/mm³, neutrophils >2,000/mm³, and platelets >100,000/mm³.
- **Organ Function Monitoring:** Liver transaminases, bilirubin, and serum creatinine must be monitored before each dose.
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** Caution is required in patients with depressed bone marrow function, poor general condition, or prior radiotherapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions primarily affect the haemopoietic system, liver enzymes, and the gastrointestinal tract.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Asthenia (52.8%), Nausea (62.9%), Diarrhoea (41.0%), Vomiting (40.2%), Anorexia (30.1%), Fever (25.6%), Leukopenia (23.6%), Anaemia (19.2%), Abdominal pain (21.2%), Rash (15.7%), Stomatitis (10.4%), Increased ALT (17.7%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Thrombocytopenia (5.6%), Dyspepsia, Constipation, Weight loss, Dehydration, Peripheral oedema, Dizziness, Alopecia, Pruritus, Increased AST/Bilirubin |
| **Serious / Life-threatening** | Grade 4 neutropenia and thrombocytopenia (especially if concurrent with GI toxicity), sepsis, and severe gastrointestinal bleeding (very rare). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Folates:** Folinic acid, folic acid, and vitamins containing these agents interfere with the efficacy of raltitrexed and are contraindicated.
- **Nephrotoxic Drugs:** Concomitant use with drugs like cisplatin may result in delayed clearance of raltitrexed; monitor creatinine clearance closely.
- **Highly Protein-Bound Drugs:** While raltitrexed is 93% protein-bound, no displacement interactions with warfarin have been observed *in vitro*.
- **NSAIDs:** Potential interaction due to competition for active tubular secretion, though clinical trial data did not reveal significant interactions with NSAIDs or warfarin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Raltitrexed is contraindicated. It may cause foetal harm, including embryolethality and abnormalities. Pregnancy must be excluded before starting therapy.
- **Contraception:** Conception should be avoided during treatment and for at least 6 months after the last dose for either partner.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated; raltitrexed should not be administered to women who are breastfeeding.
- **Fertility:** Raltitrexed causes reversible impairment of male fertility in animal studies; fertility returned to normal 3 months after dosing ceased.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and efficacy have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** More vulnerable to toxicity. Caution is required as renal function declines with age, potentially leading to drug accumulation.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose adjustments are mandatory based on creatinine clearance (CrCl):
    - **CrCl >65 mL/min:** Full dose, 3-weekly.
    - **CrCl 55–65 mL/min:** 75% of dose, 4-weekly.
    - **CrCl 25–54 mL/min:** 50% of dose, 4-weekly.
    - **CrCl <25 mL/min:** Contraindicated.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment, though caution is advised. Use in severe hepatic impairment or jaundice is not recommended.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A direct and specific inhibitor of thymidylate synthase (TS). It is transported into cells via the reduced folate carrier (RFC) and extensively polyglutamated. Polyglutamation increases inhibitory potency and duration of action within cells, leading to DNA fragmentation and cell death.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Follows a triphasic decline after IV infusion with a terminal half-life of 198 hours. It is primarily excreted unchanged in the urine (40–50%) and faeces (15%). No clinically significant plasma accumulation occurs with 3-weekly dosing in patients with normal renal function.

## 11. Clinical Trials
Four pivotal trials in advanced colorectal cancer (Trial 002, 003, 010, 012) established efficacy. Raltitrexed 3 mg/m² showed comparable objective response rates to 5-fluorouracil plus calcium folinate. In Trial 0010, survival was significantly better in the 5-FU/folinate group (12.7 months vs 9.7 months for raltitrexed), though results may have been confounded by trial design and assessment frequencies.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 25°C and protect from light.
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** Contains no preservative. Use as soon as practicable. Can be held at 2°C to 8°C for up to 24 hours.
- **Light Sensitivity:** Reconstituted and diluted solutions do not require protection from light.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
