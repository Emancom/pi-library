# Editorial Product Information: Ranibizumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single, accessible reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes. It does not replace the official Product Information mandated by local regulatory authorities for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ranibizumab (rbe)
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant humanised monoclonal antibody fragment (Fab) targeted against vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Lucentis** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** S01LA04
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Solution for injection (Vial):** 2.3 mg of ranibizumab in 0.23 mL (concentration: 10 mg/mL).
    - **Solution for injection (Pre-filled syringe):** 1.65 mg of ranibizumab in 0.165 mL (concentration: 10 mg/mL).
- **Route of Administration:** Intravitreal injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Ranibizumab** is indicated for the following conditions in adults:
- Neovascular (wet) age-related macular degeneration (nAMD).
- Visual impairment caused by diabetic macular oedema (DME).
- Proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR).
- Visual impairment caused by choroidal neovascularisation (CNV).
- Visual impairment caused by CNV secondary to pathologic myopia (PM).
- Visual impairment caused by macular oedema secondary to retinal vein occlusion (RVO), including both branch (BRVO) and central (CRVO).

In paediatric populations, **Ranibizumab** is indicated for:
- Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) in preterm infants with zone I (stage 1+, 2+, 3 or 3+), zone II (stage 3+), or aggressive posterior ROP (AP-ROP) [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Standard Dose:** 0.5 mg (0.05 mL) administered as a single intravitreal injection.
- **Dosing Interval:** The minimum interval between two doses in the same eye is four weeks.
- **Regimen:** Treatment typically begins with one injection per month until maximum visual acuity is reached or disease activity ceases. Monthly injections (three or more) may be required initially. Maintenance may involve "pro re nata" (PRN) or "treat-and-extend" regimens based on anatomical and functional monitoring (e.g., OCT, visual acuity).

### Dosing (Preterm Infants)
- **Recommended Dose:** 0.2 mg (0.02 mL). Alternatively, a dose of 0.1 mg (0.01 mL) may be used [Australian].
- **Frequency:** Treatment starts with a single injection per eye (can be bilateral). Up to three injections per eye may be given within six months if disease remains active.

### Administration Instructions
- Must be administered by a qualified ophthalmologist experienced in intravitreal injections.
- Aseptic conditions are mandatory, including surgical hand disinfection, sterile gloves, drapes, and eyelid specula.
- **Adults:** The needle should be inserted 3.5 to 4.0 mm posterior to the limbus, aiming toward the centre of the globe.
- **Preterm Infants:** The needle should be inserted 1.0 to 2.0 mm posterior to the limbus, pointing toward the optic nerve.
- **Preparation:** Vials require a 5-micrometre filter needle (18G) for withdrawal, followed by a 30G x 1/2 inch needle for injection. Pre-filled syringes are for adult use only.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **ranibizumab** or any of the excipients (trehalose dihydrate, histidine, polysorbate 20).
- Active or suspected ocular or periocular infections.
- Active intraocular inflammation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Injection-Related Reactions:** Intravitreal injections are associated with risks of endophthalmitis, intraocular inflammation, rhegmatogenous retinal detachment, retinal tear, and iatrogenic traumatic cataract.
- **Intraocular Pressure (IOP):** Transient increases in IOP are common within 60 minutes of injection. Sustained increases have also been reported. Monitoring of both IOP and optic nerve head perfusion is required. Treatment should be withheld if IOP is ≥30 mmHg.
- **Arterial Thromboembolic Events (ATEs):** There is a theoretical risk of ATEs (nonfatal stroke, myocardial infarction, or vascular death) following VEGF inhibition. Caution is advised in patients with known risk factors for stroke.
- **Retinal Vasculitis:** Post-marketing reports include retinal vasculitis and/or occlusive vasculitis. Treatment must be discontinued if these events occur.
- **Retinal Pigment Epithelial (RPE) Tear:** Caution is required in patients with large/high RPE detachments, particularly in nAMD.
- **Bilateral Treatment:** Limited data suggest no increase in systemic adverse effects with same-day bilateral treatment compared to unilateral treatment.
- **Monitoring:** Patients should be reviewed within one week of injection to detect and treat potential infections early.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Table (Adult Population)
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nasopharyngitis, headache, intraocular inflammation, vitritis, vitreous detachment/floaters, retinal haemorrhage, visual disturbance, eye pain, conjunctival haemorrhage, lacrimation increased, blepharitis, dry eye, ocular hyperaemia, eye pruritis, arthralgia, increased IOP. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Influenza, urinary tract infection (DME only), anaemia, anxiety, stroke, retinal degeneration/detachment/tear, RPE tear, uveitis, iritis, cataract (including subcapsular), blurred vision, eyelid oedema, cough, nausea, allergic reactions (rash, urticaria). |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Blindness, endophthalmitis, hypopyon, hyphaema, keratopathy, iris adhesions, corneal oedema. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Endophthalmitis, retinal detachment, and iatrogenic cataract are the most significant procedure-related risks.
- Retinal vasculitis (with or without occlusion) has been identified in post-marketing surveillance.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- No formal interaction studies have been conducted.
- **Anti-VEGF Agents:** **Ranibizumab** should not be administered concurrently with other anti-VEGF medicinal products, whether systemic or ocular.
- **Adjunctive Therapies:** 
    - **Laser Photocoagulation:** In DME and BRVO trials, **ranibizumab** was successfully used with laser. If given on the same day, **ranibizumab** should be administered at least 30 minutes after laser.
    - **Visudyne (vPDT):** There is no clinical experience with the combination of **ranibizumab** and Visudyne in PM-related CNV.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Based on its mechanism (VEGF inhibition), **ranibizumab** must be regarded as potentially teratogenic and embryo-foetotoxic. Animal studies in monkeys showed no developmental toxicity at high doses, but the risk to humans remains unknown.
    - **Recommendation:** Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception. It is recommended to wait at least 3 months after the final dose before conceiving.
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts of **ranibizumab** may be present in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment as a precautionary measure.
- **Fertility:** No specific human data; animal studies with related antibodies (bevacizumab) showed reversible inhibition of ovarian function.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in preterm infants for ROP is approved. Use in children and adolescents (<18 years) for other indications is not recommended due to insufficient data, though limited data exists for CNV in adolescents (12–17 years).
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustments are required for patients aged 65 years and older.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is necessary. Systemic clearance is slightly lower in impaired patients but is not clinically significant.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied; however, as systemic exposure is negligible, no special measures are deemed necessary.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Ranibizumab** binds with high affinity to the VEGF-A isoforms (VEGF110, VEGF121, VEGF165). By blocking the binding of VEGF-A to its receptors (VEGFR-1 and VEGFR-2), it inhibits endothelial cell proliferation, neovascularisation, and vascular leakage.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Following intravitreal injection, serum concentrations are very low and dose-proportional.
    - **Distribution/Elimination:** In adults, the vitreous elimination half-life is approximately 9 days. Systemic exposure is roughly 90,000-fold lower than vitreal exposure.
    - **Infants:** Serum concentrations in preterm infants are significantly higher (up to 16-fold higher Cmax) than in adults, with an apparent systemic half-life of 6 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **nAMD:** Pivotal trials (MARINA, ANCHOR) demonstrated that monthly 0.5 mg injections prevented vision loss in ~95% of patients and improved vision (≥15 letters) in 34–40% at 12 months.
- **DME:** The RESTORE study showed significantly higher visual acuity gains with **ranibizumab** monotherapy or combined with laser (+6.1 and +5.9 letters) compared to laser monotherapy (+0.8 letters).
- **RVO:** BRAVO (BRVO) and CRUISE (CRVO) trials showed rapid visual improvement (+18.3 and +14.9 letters respectively) at 6 months compared to sham.
- **ROP:** The RAINBOW study compared 0.2 mg and 0.1 mg doses to laser. Success (absence of active ROP and unfavourable structural outcomes) was 80% with 0.2 mg vs. 66.2% with laser.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store between 2°C and 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- Protect from light by keeping the vial or syringe in the outer carton.
- Prior to use, the unopened tray may be kept at room temperature (25°C) for up to 24 hours.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only)** [Australian].
