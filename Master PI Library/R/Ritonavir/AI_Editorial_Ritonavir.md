# Editorial Product Information: Lopinavir / Ritonavir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local prescribing guidelines required for clinical practice in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Lopinavir and Ritonavir (co-formulated in a 4:1 ratio).
- **Chemical Class:** HIV Protease Inhibitors.
- **Brand Name:** **Kaletra** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AR10.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated Tablets:** 200 mg lopinavir / 50 mg ritonavir [Australian].
    - **Oral Solution:** 80 mg lopinavir / 20 mg ritonavir per mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AbbVie Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated for the treatment of HIV-1 infection in combination with other antiretroviral agents in adults and children aged 2 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Standard Dosage:** 400/100 mg (two 200/50 mg tablets) twice daily.
- **Once-Daily Option:** 800/200 mg (four 200/50 mg tablets) once daily may be used in patients with fewer than three lopinavir-associated mutations [Australian].
- **Oral Solution:** 5 mL (400/100 mg) twice daily or 10 mL once daily, taken with food [Australian].

### Dosing with Concomitant Therapy
- **Efavirenz, Nevirapine, or Nelfinavir:** In treatment-experienced patients where reduced susceptibility is suspected, a dose increase to 500/125 mg (tablets) twice daily or 6.25 mL (oral solution) twice daily should be considered [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Tablets:** Must be swallowed whole; do not chew, break, or crush. May be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Oral Solution:** Must be administered with food to enhance bioavailability [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specifically detailed in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to lopinavir, ritonavir, or any excipients [Australian].
- **CYP3A-Dependent Drugs:** Co-administration is contraindicated with drugs highly dependent on CYP3A for clearance and where elevated levels are associated with serious/life-threatening events:
    - **Alpha1-adrenoreceptor antagonist:** Alfuzosin.
    - **Analgesic/Sodium channel blocker:** Suzetrigine.
    - **Antianginal:** Ranolazine.
    - **Antiarrhythmic:** Dronedarone.
    - **Antibiotic:** Fusidic acid.
    - **Anticancer Agents:** Neratinib, Apalutamide.
    - **Antigout:** Colchicine (in patients with renal/hepatic impairment).
    - **Antipsychotics:** Lurasidone, Pimozide, Blonanserin.
    - **Ergot Derivatives:** Ergotamine, Dihydroergotamine, Ergometrine.
    - **GI Motility Agents:** Cisapride.
    - **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors:** Lovastatin, Simvastatin.
    - **PDE5 Inhibitor:** Sildenafil (when used for Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension only).
- **Herbal Products:** St John’s Wort (*Hypericum perforatum*).
- **Oral Solution Specific Contraindications:** Children < 2 years, pregnant women, hepatic or renal failure, and those treated with disulfiram or metronidazole (due to alcohol/propylene glycol content) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Pancreatitis:** Observed in patients receiving **Kaletra**, particularly those with marked triglyceride elevations. Fatalities have occurred [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Use with caution in patients with hepatic impairment. Patients with Hepatitis B or C are at increased risk for transaminase elevations. Avoid in severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Diabetes Mellitus/Hyperglycaemia:** New onset or exacerbation of pre-existing diabetes and hyperglycaemia have been reported with protease inhibitors [Australian].
- **Cardiac Effects:**
    - **PR Interval Prolongation:** Modest asymptomatic prolongation; caution in patients with structural heart disease or conduction abnormalities [Australian].
    - **QT Interval Prolongation:** Post-marketing cases of Torsade de Pointes reported; avoid in patients with congenital long QT or those taking other QT-prolonging drugs [Australian].
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome (IRS):** Inflammatory responses to residual opportunistic infections or autoimmune disorders (e.g., Graves’ disease) may occur [Australian].
- **Fat Redistribution:** Redistribution/accumulation of body fat may occur with antiretroviral therapy [Australian].
- **Lipid Elevations:** Triglyceride and cholesterol testing should be performed prior to and during therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Very Common Effects
- **Gastrointestinal (Very Common >10%):** Diarrhoea, nausea.
- **Other (Common 1–10%):** Vomiting, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, flatulence, headache, asthenia, rash, insomnia, and anorexia [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hepatobiliary:** Hepatitis, hepatic failure.
- **Dermatological:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis, erythema multiforme.
- **Metabolic:** Diabetic ketoacidosis, severe hypertriglyceridaemia.
- **Cardiac:** Bradyarrhythmia, second or third-degree atrioventricular block [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibition:** Lopinavir/ritonavir is a potent inhibitor of CYP3A. It may significantly increase plasma concentrations of drugs metabolised by this enzyme (e.g., midazolam, fentanyl, sildenafil, immunosuppressants) [Australian].
- **Induction Effects:** **Kaletra** induces glucuronidation and its own metabolism. It may decrease concentrations of drugs like methadone, zidovudine, abacavir, and ethinyl oestradiol [Australian].
- **Specific Interactions:**
    - **PDE5 Inhibitors:** Substantial increases in sildenafil, tadalafil, or vardenafil; use reduced doses with caution [Australian].
    - **Corticosteroids:** Concomitant use with fluticasone, budesonide, or triamcinolone is not recommended unless benefits outweigh the risk of Cushing’s syndrome [Australian].
    - **Antimycobacterials:** Rifampicin significantly reduces lopinavir levels; avoid co-administration with standard doses. Rifabutin doses must be reduced by at least 75% [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B3. Animal studies showed no malformations but noted foetal development toxicities at maternally toxic doses. Use only if potential benefits justify risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mothers should be instructed not to breastfeed due to the risk of HIV transmission and potential serious adverse reactions in infants [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at clinically relevant doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children $\ge$ 2 years. Safety and efficacy not established in infants < 6 months. Once-daily dosing is not recommended in paediatric patients [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient subjects aged 65+; caution is advised due to higher frequency of decreased organ function [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Lopinavir clearance is negligible; no dose adjustment expected for patients with renal insufficiency [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in mild to moderate impairment; not studied and not recommended in severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lopinavir prevents the cleavage of the HIV gag-pol polyprotein by inhibiting HIV-1 and HIV-2 proteases, resulting in non-infectious, immature virus particles. Ritonavir acts as a pharmacokinetic enhancer by inhibiting the CYP3A-mediated metabolism of lopinavir [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tablets may be taken without regard to food; solution must be taken with food.
    - **Distribution:** 98–99% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by hepatic CYP3A.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (82.6%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 863 (Antiretroviral-Naive):** 400/100 mg BD plus NRTIs showed 75% response (HIV RNA <400 copies/mL) at 48 weeks vs. 62% for nelfinavir-based therapy [Australian].
- **Study 888 (PI-Experienced):** In patients previously treated with a single PI, **Kaletra** plus nevirapine and NRTIs showed a 57% response rate at 48 weeks [Australian].
- **Study 802:** Comparison of once-daily vs. twice-daily dosing in experienced patients showed comparable efficacy (55% vs 52%) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Oral Solution:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (refrigerated) until dispensed. Once dispensed, can be stored below 25°C if used within 42 days; refrigeration by the patient is recommended where possible [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
