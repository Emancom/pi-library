# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial synthesis:
    - United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises complex regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, legally approved PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or via infusion. 
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). It should be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **ESRD Adjustment:** Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].
- **Monitoring:** Pre-therapy plasma carnitine levels are recommended. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While specific to chronic high-dose *oral* administration, clinicians should note that in patients with severely compromised renal function, metabolites such as trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) may accumulate [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Population)
Adverse events reported at a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials for ESRD patients (regardless of causality) include:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (approx. 18-21%), hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia, vascular disorder.
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (approx. 9-35%), nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, anorexia.
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes.
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression.
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis.

### Serious or Notable Reactions
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (Postmarketing experience).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequently, body odor and gastritis.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and bronchospasm.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of carnitine following exogenous administration. The risks to the infant of excess carnitine intake should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for paediatric patients with inborn errors of metabolism is the same as adults (50 mg/kg), with careful monitoring [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For IV use in ESRD, refer to Section 3 (Dosage and Administration) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific metabolic defects [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose, approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels typically stabilise or increase over 8 to 24 weeks of therapy [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean trough concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
