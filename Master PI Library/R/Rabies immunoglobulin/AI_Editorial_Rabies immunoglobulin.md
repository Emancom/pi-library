# Editorial Product Information: Rabies Immunoglobulin (Human)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Clinical trial data within the source material also references the brand name **KEDRAB**, which is the associated trade name in other international jurisdictions [e.g., US].
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines mandated within a specific jurisdiction for prescribing and administration.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rabies immunoglobulin (human).
- **Chemical Class:** Human immunoglobulin (passive immunising agent).
- **Brand Name(s):** **KamRAB** [Australian]; **KEDRAB** [US/International].
- **ATC Code:** J06BB05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection.
    - Concentration: 150 IU/mL.
    - Pack sizes: 2 mL (300 IU) and 10 mL (1,500 IU) single-use vials.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Infiltration (local wound) and Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products Pty Ltd [Australian]; manufactured by Kamada Ltd.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP):** Indicated for passive, transient prophylaxis against rabies infection following immediate contact with a rabid or suspected rabid animal. 
- **Requirement for Combination Therapy:** Must be administered concurrently with a complete course of rabies vaccine.
- **Exclusions:** 
    - Not indicated for repeat dosing once vaccine treatment has started (interference with active immunity). 
    - Not indicated for individuals with a documented history of complete pre-exposure or post-exposure vaccination and confirmed adequate rabies antibody titres.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult and Paediatric Dosing:** A single dose of **20 IU/kg** body weight.
- **Timing:** Administer as soon as possible after exposure, alongside the first dose of the rabies vaccine. If delayed, it may be given up to and including the seventh day after the first vaccine dose.
- **Local Wound Treatment:** Before administration, wounds must be thoroughly cleansed with soap and water. A viricidal antiseptic (e.g., povidone-iodine or alcohol) should be used for irrigation.
- **Administration Technique:**
    - **Infiltration:** Infiltrate the maximum possible volume of the dose into and around the bite wounds.
    - **IM Injection:** Any remaining volume should be injected intramuscularly at a site distant from the vaccine administration site (e.g., the deltoid if the vaccine was given in the opposite deltoid or the anterolateral thigh).
    - **Volume Constraints:** For large volumes, use divided doses at different anatomical sites (>2 mL for children; >5 mL for adults).
    - **Subcutaneous Route:** May be used only if IM is contraindicated (e.g., bleeding disorders), though clinical efficacy data for this route are lacking.
- **Critical Precautions:** 
    - Do NOT mix with rabies vaccine in the same syringe. 
    - Do NOT administer into the same anatomical site as the vaccine.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None:** Due to the near-certain fatality of rabies infection, there are no contraindications to the administration of rabies immunoglobulin when PEP is indicated.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Transmissible Infectious Agents:** As a product derived from human plasma, the risk of transmitting infective agents (e.g., HIV, HBV, HCV, HAV, Parvovirus B19) cannot be totally excluded despite rigorous donor screening and viral inactivation steps.
- **Traceability:** It is strongly recommended that the product name and batch number are recorded for every administration to maintain a link between the patient and the batch.
- **Interference with Active Immunity:** Exceeding the recommended 20 IU/kg dose or providing repeat doses may partially suppress the patient's own antibody production following vaccination.
- **Live Attenuated Vaccines:** Passive antibodies may interfere with the response to live vaccines. 
    - **Measles:** Avoid for 4 months post-administration.
    - **Other live vaccines (Mumps, Rubella, Polio):** Avoid for 3 months post-administration.
- **Anaphylaxis and Hypersensitivity:**
    - **Strictly contra-indicated for IV injection** due to the risk of anaphylactic shock.
    - Patients with IgA deficiency may develop IgA antibodies and experience anaphylactic reactions.
    - Epinephrine must be available for immediate use during administration.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate. Data from clinical trials (N=91 adults) and paediatric studies (N=30) indicate the following frequencies:

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site pain (33%), Headache (15%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Myalgia, Upper respiratory tract infection, Arthralgia, Dizziness, Fatigue, Abdominal pain, Nausea, Syncope/Presyncope, Ecchymosis, Diarrhoea, Vomiting, Malaise, Liver function test abnormalities. |
| **Paediatric Specific** | Pyrexia (13%), Oral pain, Injection site erythema/oedema, Insomnia (reported after 14 days). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Rabies Vaccine:** Pharmacodynamic interference occurs if administered in the same syringe or site, or if the immunoglobulin is repeated after vaccine initiation.
- **Live Virus Vaccines:** (See Section 5) Clinically significant interference with measles, mumps, and rubella vaccines.
- **Laboratory Tests:** Passive transfer of antibodies may cause transient false-positive results in serological tests (e.g., Coombs’ test/antiglobulin test).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B2** [Australian].
    - Not studied in pregnant women. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety information is unavailable. The clinical need for the mother and potential risk to the infant should be weighed against the benefits of breastfeeding.
- **Fertility:** No data available regarding effects on reproductive capacity.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness established in children (ages 0.5 to 14.9 years). Dosing is the same as adults (20 IU/kg).
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and effectiveness not specifically established in clinical trials; however, clinical experience has not identified significant differences compared to younger patients.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, as an immunoglobulin, dose adjustment is generally not required for hepatic/renal failure based on standard protein metabolism.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Provides immediate, passive, and transient virus-neutralising antibodies. This offers protection during the "window" before the patient's immune system produces its own antibodies in response to the rabies vaccine.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** A protective threshold of ≥0.5 IU/mL rabies virus neutralising activity (RVNA) is generally accepted as protective by the WHO.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Tmax (Peak levels):** Approximately 7 days (range 3–14 days) when administered alone.
    - **Elimination Half-life:** Approximately 17.9 days.
    - **Interaction with Vaccine:** When given as part of simulated PEP (with vaccine), RVNA concentrations decline in a biphasic manner once active immunity begins.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Study (Study 003):** A randomised, comparator-controlled study (N=118) showed that 98.2% of subjects receiving **KamRAB** achieved the WHO-recommended RVNA titre of ≥0.5 IU/mL by Day 14, establishing non-inferiority to existing rabies immunoglobulins.
- **Paediatric Study (Study 004):** An open-label trial (N=30) in children aged 0.5 to 14.9 years showed 93.3% achieved protective RVNA titres by Day 14. No cases of rabies were reported through Day 84.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigerated Storage:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **In-use Stability:** May be stored at room temperature (not exceeding 25°C) for up to one month. Once removed from refrigeration, it must not be returned to the refrigerator and should be discarded if not used within one month.
- **Handling:** Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use; the solution should be clear to slightly opalescent.
