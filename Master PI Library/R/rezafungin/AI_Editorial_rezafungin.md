# Editorial Product Information: Rezafungin

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) document contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of rezafungin for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines and official product labelling.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Rezafungin (as rezafungin acetate), a semi-synthetic echinocandin lipopeptide.
- **Brand Name(s):** **REZZAYO** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J02AX07.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** For injection: 200 mg of rezafungin (equivalent to 210 mg rezafungin acetate) as a sterile lyophilized solid (cake or powder) in a single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Melinta Therapeutics, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Candidemia and Invasive Candidiasis:** Indicated for patients 18 years of age or older who have limited or no alternative treatment options. This indication is approved under accelerated pathways based on limited clinical safety and efficacy data [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Rezafungin has not been adequately studied in patients with Candida-related endocarditis, osteomyelitis, or meningitis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Loading Dose:** 400 mg administered as a single IV infusion on Day 1 of Week 1.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 200 mg administered once weekly thereafter, beginning on Week 2.
- **Maximum Duration:** The safety of rezafungin has not been established beyond 4 weekly doses (including the loading dose) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Aseptically reconstitute each 200 mg vial with 9.5 mL Sterile Water for Injection to achieve a 20 mg/mL concentration. Swirl gently; do not shake to avoid foaming. 
- **Dilution:** Reconstituted solution must be further diluted in 250 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride, 0.45% Sodium Chloride, or 5% Dextrose Injection.
- **Infusion:** Administer via IV infusion over approximately one hour (rate of ~250 mL/h). If infusion-related reactions occur, the rate may be slowed or the infusion paused [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Administer the missed dose as soon as possible.
- If within 3 days of the scheduled day: Resume the next weekly dose on the original schedule.
- If more than 3 days after the scheduled day: Revise the schedule to ensure at least 4 days elapse before the next dose.
- If dosing is missed for $\ge$2 weeks: Restart therapy with the 400 mg loading dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to rezafungin or any other member of the echinocandin class [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported. Discontinue therapy immediately if such reactions occur [US].
- **Infusion-Related Reactions:** May cause flushing, sensation of warmth, urticaria, nausea, or chest tightness. Management involves slowing or pausing the infusion [US].
- **Photosensitivity:** Rezafungin may cause photosensitivity. Patients should be advised to use sun protection and avoid UV radiation during treatment [US].
- **Hepatic Adverse Reactions:** Abnormalities in liver function tests have been observed. Monitoring is required for patients developing abnormal liver tests; clinicians should evaluate the risk/benefit of continuing therapy in these cases [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are categorized by frequency based on clinical trial data (N=151).

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Hypokalemia (15%), Pyrexia (12%), Diarrhea (11%), Anemia (10%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vomiting (9%), Nausea (9%), Hypomagnesemia (8%), Abdominal pain (7%), Constipation (5%), Hypophosphatemia (5%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Tremors (2.6%), Infusion-related reactions, Disseminated intravascular coagulation, AKI, abnormal liver tests |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, significant hepatic abnormalities, and peripheral neuropathy (including peroneal nerve palsy) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Rezafungin is not a substrate, inhibitor, or inducer of common Cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes or major drug transporters (e.g., P-gp, OATP, BCRP, OCT, MATE).
- **Clinically Significant Interactions:** No dosage adjustments are expected when co-administered with CYP substrates (e.g., cyclosporine, tacrolimus, midazolam) or other common co-medications like mycophenolate mofetil or efavirenz [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No antagonism has been demonstrated in vitro with other antifungals (azoles, amphotericin B) or various antibacterial classes [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current PLLR rules]. There are no available human data. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) using 3–5 times the clinical exposure showed no adverse embryofetal outcomes, though maternal toxicity (histamine release in rats) was noted.
- **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk is unknown. It is present in rat milk. Consider the developmental benefits of breastfeeding alongside the clinical need for the drug and potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggests potential for decreased sperm motility and morphology at high doses; however, the clinical significance in humans is unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required. While 42% of trial participants were $\ge$65 years, data are insufficient to determine if they respond differently than younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically relevant effects on pharmacokinetics were observed. No adjustment is required, including for patients on hemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients with Child-Pugh Class B or C impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the synthesis of 1,3-$\beta$-D-glucan, an essential component of the fungal cell wall, via inhibition of the 1,3-$\beta$-D-glucan synthase enzyme complex.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (88–94% in patients); volume of distribution is approximately 67 L.
    - **Metabolism:** No hepatic metabolism observed; not a clinically relevant substrate for CYP450.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted via feces (74% as rezafungin). Terminal half-life is approximately 152 hours, supporting weekly dosing.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits concentration-dependent fungicidal activity against *Candida* species. It does not prolong the QTc interval at 3.5 times the loading dose [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Trial 1 (ReStore):** A randomized, double-blind trial compared rezafungin (400 mg load/200 mg weekly) to caspofungin (70 mg load/50 mg daily). 
    - **Primary Outcome:** Day 30 all-cause mortality was 23.7% for rezafungin vs. 21.3% for caspofungin (difference 2.4%; 95% CI: -9.7, 14.4).
    - **Global Cure (Day 14):** 59.1% for rezafungin vs. 60.6% for caspofungin.
- **Trial 2 (STRIVE):** A dose-finding exploratory study providing supportive safety and efficacy data for candidemia and invasive candidiasis [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Stable for up to 24 hours when stored at 5°C to 25°C.
- **Infusion Solution:** Stable for up to 48 hours when stored at 5°C to 25°C. Do not freeze [US].
