# Editorial Product Information: Ritlecitinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and US (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of official Product Information from the listed jurisdictions, designed as a comprehensive reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ritlecitinib (as ritlecitinib tosylate).
- **Chemical Class:** Kinase inhibitor; irreversible inhibitor of Janus kinase 3 (JAK3) and the tyrosine kinase expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma (TEC) family.
- **Brand Name:** **LITFULO**.
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:**
  - Hard capsules: 50 mg of ritlecitinib (equivalent to 80.13 mg [US] or 80.128 mg [Australian] ritlecitinib tosylate).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Alopecia Areata:** Treatment of severe alopecia areata in adults and adolescents 12 years of age and older.
- **Limitations of Use:** Not recommended for use in combination with other JAK inhibitors, biologic immunomodulators, cyclosporine, or other potent immunosuppressants [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents $\ge$ 12 years):** 50 mg orally once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
  - May be taken with or without food.
  - Capsules must be swallowed whole; do not crush, split, or chew.
- **Treatment Duration:** 
  - Consideration should be given to discontinuing patients who show no evidence of therapeutic benefit after 36 weeks [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** 
  - If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible unless it is less than 8 hours before the next scheduled dose. In that case, the missed dose should be skipped, and the regular schedule resumed.
- **Pre-treatment Evaluations:**
  - Screen for tuberculosis (TB) and viral hepatitis (B and C) before initiation.
  - Perform baseline absolute lymphocyte count (ALC) and platelet count.
  - Ensure all immunisations, including prophylactic herpes zoster, are up to date [Australian, US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ritlecitinib or any of its excipients.
- **Active Serious Infection:** Including tuberculosis [Australian-specific contraindication].
- **Severe Hepatic Impairment:** Child-Pugh Class C [Australian-specific contraindication; [US] notes it is "Not Recommended"].
- **Pregnancy and Breastfeeding:** [Australian-specific contraindication; [US] provides warnings against use].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]:** 
  - **Serious Infections:** Increased risk of serious bacterial, fungal, viral, and opportunistic infections (e.g., TB, shingles) leading to hospitalisation or death.
  - **Mortality:** Higher rate of all-cause mortality observed with other JAK inhibitors in specific populations.
  - **Malignancy:** Lymphomas and lung cancers reported with JAK inhibitors.
  - **Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events (MACE):** Higher rate of MACE (cardiovascular death, MI, stroke) reported with other JAK inhibitors.
  - **Thrombosis:** Increased incidence of pulmonary embolism (PE), deep vein thrombosis (DVT), and arterial thrombosis.
- **Haematologic Abnormalities:** 
  - **Monitoring:** Perform ALC and platelet counts at 4 weeks post-initiation and thereafter per routine management.
  - **Initiation Threshold:** Do not start if ALC < 500 cells/mm³ or platelets < 100,000 cells/mm³.
  - **Interruption/Discontinuation:** Interrupt if ALC < 500 cells/mm³; resume once above this value. Discontinue if platelets < 50,000 cells/mm³.
- **Neurological Events:** Ritlecitinib-related axonal dystrophy was observed in animal studies. Discontinue if unexplained neurological symptoms occur [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Liver enzyme elevations (ALT/AST $\ge$ 5x ULN) have been reported. Monitor at baseline and per routine management.
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Reported in patients with diabetes receiving JAK inhibitors. Monitor blood glucose and advise patients of symptoms [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (10.8%), diarrhoea (10.0%) [Australian, US].
- **Common (1–10%):** 
  - Acne (6.2%)
  - Rash (5.4%)
  - Urticaria (4.6%)
  - Folliculitis (3.1%)
  - Pyrexia (3.1%)
  - Dizziness (2.3%)
  - Atopic dermatitis (2.3%)
  - Stomatitis [US: 1.5%]
  - Herpes zoster [US: 1.5%]
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** 
  - Serious infections (0.66 per 100 patient-years) [US].
  - Malignancy (0.37 per 100 patient-years, excluding NMSC) [US].
  - Thromboembolic events (PE reported in 1 subject in trials).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
  - **CYP3A Inhibitors (e.g., Itraconazole):** Increased ritlecitinib exposure (15%), not clinically significant; no dose adjustment required.
  - **CYP3A Inducers (e.g., Rifampin):** Decreased ritlecitinib exposure (44%). Coadministration with strong CYP3A inducers is not recommended [US].
- **Effect of Ritlecitinib on Other Drugs:**
  - **CYP3A Substrates (e.g., Midazolam):** Ritlecitinib is a moderate CYP3A inhibitor; increases exposure of substrates (2.7-fold). Exercise caution and consider monitoring/dose adjustment for sensitive substrates.
  - **CYP1A2 Substrates (e.g., Caffeine):** Ritlecitinib is a moderate CYP1A2 inhibitor; increases exposure (2.7-fold). Monitor and adjust dose of substrates (e.g., theophylline) as needed.
  - **OCT1 Substrates (e.g., Sumatriptan):** Ritlecitinib may increase exposure; caution is advised [Australian].
- **Live Vaccines:** Avoid use during or immediately prior to ritlecitinib therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Australian Category D:** Ritlecitinib is contraindicated during pregnancy [Australian].
  - **US Status:** No lettered category; data are insufficient to identify drug-associated risks. Animal studies showed fetotoxicity and malformations at $\ge$ 49 times the MRHD. A pregnancy registry is available ([US]).
- **Breastfeeding:** 
  - **Australian:** Contraindicated during breastfeeding.
  - **US:** Not recommended. Advise women not to breastfeed during treatment and for 14 hours after the last dose (approximately 6 elimination half-lives).
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed effects on male rat fertility (preimplantation loss) at 55 times MRHD, but no effects on spermatogenesis. No effects on female fertility were observed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for adolescents $\ge$ 12 years. Safety and efficacy in children < 12 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 65 years):** No dose adjustment required. Caution is advised due to a higher baseline risk of infection. Data in patients $\ge$ 75 years are unavailable [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
  - **Mild to Severe (eGFR < 30 mL/min):** No dose adjustment required.
  - **ESRD/Transplant:** Not studied and not recommended.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
  - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** No dose adjustment required.
  - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended [US]; Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Irreversibly inhibits JAK3 and the TEC kinase family. It blocks the ATP-binding site, preventing cytokine-induced STAT phosphorylation (JAK3) and inhibiting immune receptor signalling (TEC).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability ~64%. Peak plasma concentration ($T_{max}$) reached within 1 hour. High-fat meals decrease $C_{max}$ by 32% but do not have a clinically significant impact on total exposure.
  - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution ~74 L. Plasma protein binding ~14%.
  - **Metabolism:** Mediated by multiple pathways including Glutathione S-transferase (GST) and CYP enzymes (3A, 2C8, 1A2, 2C9). No single route contributes > 25% to clearance.
  - **Elimination:** Primary elimination via metabolism. 66% of dose excreted in urine and 20% in faeces. Mean terminal half-life: 1.3 to 2.3 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study (Trial AA-I):** A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in 718 patients $\ge$ 12 years with $\ge$ 50% scalp hair loss (SALT score $\ge$ 50).
- **Primary Endpoint:** Proportion of subjects achieving SALT $\le$ 10 (90% or more scalp hair coverage) at Week 24.
- **Outcomes:** 
  - **Ritlecitinib 50 mg:** 13.4% achieved SALT $\le$ 10 vs. 1.5% for placebo.
  - **SALT $\le$ 20:** 23.0% for ritlecitinib 50 mg vs. 1.6% for placebo.
  - Efficacy was consistent between adults and adolescents.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian]. [US] specifies 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F), with excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
- **Special Conditions:** Store in original container to protect from light. Contains a desiccant; do not eat.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States:** Rx-only.
