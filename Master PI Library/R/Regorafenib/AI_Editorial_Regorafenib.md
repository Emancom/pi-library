# Editorial Product Information: Regorafenib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of regorafenib for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the local approved PI when prescribing in a specific region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Regorafenib (as monohydrate); a multikinase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **STIVARGA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EX05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 40 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bayer Australia Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Colorectal Cancer (CRC):** Treatment of patients previously treated with fluoropyrimidine-, oxaliplatin-, and irinotecan-based chemotherapy, an anti-VEGF therapy, and, if RAS wild type, an anti-EGFR therapy [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumours (GIST):** Treatment of patients with unresectable or metastatic GIST who progressed on or are intolerant to prior treatment with imatinib and sunitinib [Australian].
- **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC):** Treatment of patients previously treated with sorafenib [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 160 mg (four 40 mg tablets) orally once daily for 21 days, followed by a 7-day break to complete a 28-day cycle [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole with water.
    - Administration must occur at the same time each day following a low-fat meal (ideally breakfast) containing less than 30% fat [Australian].
- **Dose Modifications:**
    - Adjustments should occur in 40 mg (one tablet) steps.
    - Lowest recommended daily dose: 80 mg.
    - Maximum daily dose: 160 mg [Australian].
- **Dose Interruption/Discontinuation:** Required for symptomatic Grade 2 hypertension, any Grade 3 or 4 adverse reactions, or surgical procedures. Permanent discontinuation is required for hypertensive crisis, GI perforation/fistula, RPLS, or severe/persistent hepatotoxicity [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken on the same day as soon as remembered. Two doses should not be taken on the same day to make up for a missed dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to regorafenib or any of the excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Hepatotoxicity [Australian]:** Severe drug-induced liver injury with fatal outcomes has been reported. Liver function tests (ALT, AST, and bilirubin) must be monitored before treatment initiation and every two weeks for the first two months. Monthly monitoring is required thereafter.
- **Infections:** Increased risk of infection, including fatal cases. Therapy should be interrupted for worsening infection events [Australian].
- **Haemorrhage:** Increased incidence of bleeding events (respiratory, GI, or genitourinary). Monitor blood counts and coagulation parameters, especially in patients on anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin) [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation/Fistula:** Reported including fatal cases; permanent discontinuation is recommended if these occur [Australian].
- **Cardiac Ischaemia and Infarction:** Increased risk observed. Therapy should be interrupted for new or acute onset ischaemia and only resumed if benefits outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Arterial Hypertension:** Onset typically occurs in the first cycle. Do not initiate therapy until blood pressure is controlled. Monitor BP weekly for the first 6 weeks [Australian].
- **Reversible Posterior Leukoencephalopathy Syndrome (RPLS):** Discontinue if signs (seizures, headache, visual disturbances) are confirmed by brain imaging [Australian].
- **Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA):** Rare cases reported. Discontinuation is required if TMA is diagnosed [Australian].
- **Wound Healing:** Regorafenib may interfere with wound healing. Stop treatment at least 2 weeks prior to scheduled surgery [Australian].
- **Dermatological Toxicity:** Hand-foot skin reaction (HFSR/PPE) and rash are very common. Management includes keratolytic creams and dose modifications [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Asthenia/fatigue, pain, HFSR/PPE, decreased appetite, diarrhoea, infection, hypertension, dysphonia, rash, fever, and mucosal inflammation [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Stomatitis, weight loss, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia, headache, proteinuria, hypokalaemia, and hypothyroidism [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Tremor, gastroesophageal reflux, gastroenteritis, nail disorder, and myocardial infarction [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, and gastrointestinal fistula [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Severe liver injury, haemorrhage, GI perforation, and hypertensive crisis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Interactions:**
    - **Strong Inhibitors (e.g., Ketoconazole, Clarithromycin):** Increase regorafenib exposure (AUC) by ~33% and decrease active metabolite exposure by ~90%. Avoid concomitant use [Australian].
    - **Strong Inducers (e.g., Rifampicin, St John’s Wort, Phenytoin):** Decrease regorafenib exposure by ~50% and increase metabolite M-5 exposure. Avoid concomitant use [Australian].
- **UGT1A1 and UGT1A9 Substrates:** Regorafenib and its M-2 metabolite inhibit glucuronidation. Co-administration with irinotecan (a UGT1A1 substrate) increases SN-38 exposure by ~44% [Australian].
- **BCRP Substrates:** Co-administration with rosuvastatin (5 mg) increased rosuvastatin AUC 3.8-fold. Monitor patients closely for increased toxicity of BCRP substrates (e.g., methotrexate, atorvastatin) [Australian].
- **Antibiotics:** Neomycin (non-absorbable) reduces the exposure of active metabolites M-2 and M-5 by ~76–86% by interfering with enterohepatic circulation; the clinical significance is unknown [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Regorafenib is suspected to cause fetal harm based on its mechanism of action and animal studies (teratogenicity and embryofetal deaths). Effective contraception is required during treatment and for at least 8 weeks following completion [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established in humans. Animal data show excretion in milk. Breastfeeding must be discontinued during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Based on animal data, regorafenib has the potential to adversely affect male and female reproduction [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents <18 years not established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No general dose adjustment required. However, cardiac disorder events were more frequent in patients ≥75 years (20.5% vs 10.4%) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** No dose recommendation; close monitoring required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not studied; use not recommended [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe impairment. Not studied in end-stage renal disease [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A multikinase inhibitor blocking kinases involved in angiogenesis (VEGFR1-3, TIE2), oncogenesis (KIT, RET, RAF-1, BRAF), metastasis (VEGFR3, PDGFR, FGFR), and tumour immunity (CSF1R) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Mean peak plasma levels reached at 3–4 hours. Bioavailability 69–83%. Absorption is optimal with a low-fat breakfast [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** High protein binding (99.5%). Undergoes enterohepatic circulation [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP3A4 (oxidative) and UGT1A9 (glucuronidation). Active metabolites M-2 and M-5 reach steady-state concentrations similar to the parent drug [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life: parent/M-2 (20–30 hours); M-5 (~60 hours). 71% excreted in faeces; 19% in urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CORRECT (Metastatic CRC):** Randomized 2:1 (regorafenib vs placebo). Regorafenib significantly improved Overall Survival (OS) (6.4 vs 5.0 months, HR 0.77, p=0.005) and Progression-Free Survival (PFS) (1.9 vs 1.7 months, HR 0.49, p<0.000001) [Australian].
- **GRID (GIST):** Randomized 2:1. Regorafenib significantly increased PFS (4.8 vs 0.9 months, HR 0.27, p<0.000001) [Australian].
- **RESORCE (HCC):** Randomized 2:1. Regorafenib improved OS (10.6 vs 7.8 months, HR 0.62, p=0.000017) and PFS (3.1 vs 1.5 months, HR 0.45, p<0.000001) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C. Keep the bottle tightly closed with the desiccant inside. Once opened, tablets must be discarded after 28 days [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
