# Editorial Product Information: Romosozumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of romosozumab for use by healthcare professionals. It aims to consolidate clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved documentation for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Romosozumab (humanised IgG2 monoclonal antibody).
- **Chemical Class:** Sclerostin inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Evenity** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M05BX06.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 105 mg/1.17 mL solution for injection in a single-use pre-filled syringe (PFS). [Australian: Pre-filled autoinjector (AI) also registered but not currently available].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amgen Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Postmenopausal Osteoporosis:** Treatment of osteoporosis in postmenopausal women at high risk of fracture [Australian].
- **Male Osteoporosis:** Treatment to increase bone mass in men with osteoporosis at high risk of fracture [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Dosage:** 210 mg administered once monthly for 12 doses.
- **Administration Instructions:** The 210 mg dose requires two separate 105 mg subcutaneous injections administered consecutively.
- **Injection Sites:** Abdomen, thigh, or upper arm. Rotate injection sites; do not inject into areas where skin is tender, bruised, red, or indurated.
- **Duration of Therapy:** Limited to 12 monthly doses. Efficacy and safety beyond 12 months have not been established.
- **Post-Therapy Transition:** After completing the 12-month course, transition to an antiresorptive osteoporosis therapy is required to preserve bone mass gains.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be administered as soon as possible. Subsequent doses should then be scheduled monthly from the date of the last injection.
- **Preparation:** Allow the product to reach room temperature (approx. 30 minutes) before injecting. Do not warm by other means.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Cardiovascular History:** History of myocardial infarction (MI) or stroke [Australian].
- **Calcium Metabolism:** Uncorrected hypocalcaemia.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to romosozumab, Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO)-derived proteins, or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myocardial Infarction and Stroke:** Clinical trials showed an increased risk of serious cardiovascular events (MI and stroke) compared to controls. Before initiating therapy, evaluate the patient's individual fracture risk over the next year alongside cardiovascular risk factors (e.g., hypertension, diabetes, smoking, age, established CV disease). Romosozumab should be discontinued if an MI or stroke occurs during treatment.
- **Hypocalcaemia:** Transient hypocalcaemia may occur. Existing hypocalcaemia must be corrected prior to initiation. All patients should receive adequate calcium and vitamin D supplementation. 
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Clinically significant reactions including angioedema, erythema multiforme, and urticaria have been reported. Anaphylactic reactions require immediate discontinuation and appropriate medical management.
- **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ):** Occurs rarely. A dental examination and preventative dentistry should be considered for patients with risk factors prior to starting treatment.
- **Atypical Femoral Fracture:** Rare, low-energy fractures of the femoral shaft have been reported. Patients presenting with new or unusual thigh, hip, or groin pain should be evaluated for incomplete femur fracture.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Safety data are based on 12-month pooled data from postmenopausal women and men with osteoporosis.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Viral upper respiratory tract infection, Arthralgia |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache, Cough, Muscle spasms, Neck pain, Peripheral edema, Injection site reactions (mostly pain and erythema), Rash, Dermatitis |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Myocardial infarction, Stroke, Hypocalcaemia, Urticaria |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Angioedema, Erythema multiforme |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Cardiovascular events (MI, stroke), osteonecrosis of the jaw, and atypical femoral fractures are the most clinically significant serious risks.
- **Immunogenicity:** Binding anti-romosozumab antibodies were detected in 18.1% of postmenopausal women; 0.8% developed neutralising antibodies. No impact on efficacy or safety was observed in the presence of these antibodies [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted.
- **Laboratory Tests:** No known interactions with laboratory or diagnostic tests.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. There are no studies in pregnant women. Animal studies in rats showed skeletal malformations (syndactyly, polydactyly) at very high exposures (30x clinical dose). Use is not recommended in pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk is unknown. A risk-benefit assessment is required to decide whether to discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug, considering the mother’s clinical need.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effect on male or female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients <18 years of age.
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required. No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required. Patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR 15–29 mL/min/1.73 m²) or those receiving dialysis are at higher risk of developing hypocalcaemia; monitoring of calcium levels is highly recommended.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No clinical studies have been conducted in this population.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Romosozumab is a humanised IgG2 monoclonal antibody that binds and inhibits sclerostin. Sclerostin is a negative regulator of bone formation. By inhibiting sclerostin, romosozumab exerts a dual effect: it increases bone formation and decreases bone resorption.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases the bone formation marker P1NP (peaking at 2 weeks) and decreases the bone resorption marker CTX (maximal reduction at 2 weeks). These effects are reversible upon discontinuation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is approximately 81%. Tmax is reached in 5 days (range 2–7 days). Steady state is achieved by month 3.
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is approx. 3.92 L.
    - **Metabolism:** The metabolic pathway has not been characterised; as a monoclonal antibody, it is expected to be degraded via catabolic pathways into small peptides and amino acids.
    - **Elimination:** Clearance is non-linear and decreases with increasing dose. Effective half-life is approximately 12.8 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ARCH Study (Active-controlled):** Comparison with alendronate in 4093 postmenopausal women. Romosozumab for 12 months followed by alendronate significantly reduced the incidence of new vertebral, clinical, non-vertebral, and hip fractures compared to alendronate alone.
- **FRAME Study (Placebo-controlled):** Comparison with placebo in 7180 postmenopausal women. Romosozumab significantly reduced the risk of new vertebral fractures by 73% at month 12. Risk reduction persisted through month 24 after transitioning to denosumab.
- **BRIDGE Study (Male Osteoporosis):** Comparison with placebo in 245 men. Romosozumab significantly increased lumbar spine BMD by 12.1% at 12 months compared to 1.2% for placebo.
- **STRUCTURE Study (Bisphosphonate Transition):** Evaluated women transitioning from bisphosphonates. Romosozumab increased BMD significantly more than teriparatide over 12 months.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Room Temperature Stability:** If removed from the refrigerator, the product must be kept in the original carton at room temperature (up to 25°C) and used within 30 days or discarded.
- **Special Handling:** Do not freeze. Do not shake.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only [Australian].
