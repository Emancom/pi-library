# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory and clinical data.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** **Levocarnitine Injection, USP** (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Injection Method:** Administer as a slow bolus injection over 2 to 3 minutes [US].
- **ESRD Patients:** The dose should be administered into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Metabolic Crisis:** In severe cases, a loading dose may be given, followed by the equivalent dose over the next 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL in PVC bags [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** Pre-therapy plasma carnitine levels should be obtained. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Dose Adjustment (ESRD):** Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of IV administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately and initiate appropriate medical treatment [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### Clinical Precautions
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Dosing):** While this editorial covers the IV formulation, it is noted that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirement:** Regular monitoring of plasma carnitine levels is essential to ensure therapeutic levels and avoid toxicity [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
The following events were reported in clinical trials of ESRD patients at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), rhinitis (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).

### Serious and Postmarketing Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (reported in both pre-existing and non-pre-existing cases) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis (less frequent) [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labelling, but studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to the dosing recommendations in Section 3; the metabolic disorder dosing applies to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically for ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed that trough levocarnitine levels increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While carnitine concentrations increased, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
- **Packaging:** Supplied in cartons of 10 x 5 mL vials [US].
