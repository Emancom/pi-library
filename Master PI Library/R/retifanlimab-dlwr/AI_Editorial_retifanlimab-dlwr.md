## 3. Approved Indications
- Adult patients with squamous cell carcinoma of the anal canal (SCAC):
  - First‑line treatment of inoperable locally recurrent or metastatic SCAC in combination with carboplatin and paclitaxel. [US]
  - Treatment of locally recurrent or metastatic SCAC with disease progression on or intolerance to platinum‑based chemotherapy (monotherapy). [US]
- Adult patients with Merkel cell carcinoma (MCC):
  - Treatment of metastatic or recurrent locally advanced MCC (monotherapy). [US]
- Indications outside the US or jurisdiction‑specific approvals: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended doses:
  - 500 mg IV every 4 weeks
  - Or 375 mg IV every 3 weeks (alternative regimen)
- Dose selection and duration by indication:
  - SCAC in combination with carboplatin and paclitaxel: 500 mg every 4 weeks or 375 mg every 3 weeks — continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or up to 12 months. [US]
  - SCAC as monotherapy (after progression on or intolerance to platinum chemotherapy): 500 mg every 4 weeks or 375 mg every 3 weeks — continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or up to 24 months. [US]
  - MCC (metastatic or recurrent locally advanced): 500 mg every 4 weeks — continue until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or up to 24 months. [US]

### Administration instructions
- Administer as an intravenous infusion over 30 minutes following dilution.
- Dilute with either 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection to a final concentration between 1.4 mg/mL and 10 mg/mL.
- Visually inspect vial and diluted solution for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration; do not use if cloudy, discolored, or containing particulates. Do not shake.
- Use an inline or add‑on filter (recommended pore sizes and filter materials specified in source). Do not administer as an IV push or bolus.
- Do not co‑administer other drugs through the same infusion line.
- Withdraw appropriate volume from single‑use vial and discard any unused portion (each vial is for single‑patient use).
- Reconstitution/handling details beyond the above (including storage times/temperatures for diluted product) are not included here. [Note: specific storage/stability limits were present in source but are not summarized here.]

### Titration / dose reduction
- No dose reductions of retifanlimab‑dlwr are recommended. Withhold or permanently discontinue according to severity of adverse reactions (see Section 6 and Dosage Modifications guidance below).

### Missed dose
- Specific guidance for missed doses: Not specified in available sources

### Dosage modifications for adverse reactions (summary)
- In general:
  - Withhold for severe (Grade 3) immune‑mediated adverse reactions.
  - Permanently discontinue for life‑threatening (Grade 4) immune‑mediated adverse reactions, recurrent Grade 3 immune‑mediated reactions requiring systemic immunosuppressive therapy, or inability to reduce corticosteroid dose to ≤10 mg prednisone‑equivalent/day within 12 weeks of initiating steroids.
- Specific organ‑based recommendations (selected examples):
  - Pneumonitis: Withhold for Grade 2; permanently discontinue for Grade 3–4.
  - Colitis: Withhold for Grade 2–3; permanently discontinue for Grade 4.
  - Hepatitis (without tumor liver involvement): Withhold for AST/ALT >3 to ≤8× ULN or total bilirubin >1.5 to ≤3× ULN; permanently discontinue for AST/ALT >8× ULN or total bilirubin >3× ULN.
  - Endocrinopathies: Withhold for Grade 3–4 until clinically stable or permanently discontinue depending on severity.
  - Nephritis with renal dysfunction: Withhold for Grade 2–3 creatinine increase; permanently discontinue for Grade 4.
  - Exfoliative dermatologic conditions (suspected SJS/TEN/DRESS): Withhold for Grade 3 or suspected severe reaction; permanently discontinue for confirmed Grade 4 or SJS/TEN/DRESS.
  - Myocarditis and severe neurologic toxicities: Permanently discontinue.
- For immune‑mediated adverse reactions requiring systemic corticosteroids: recommended starting dose 1–2 mg/kg/day prednisone or equivalent until improvement to Grade ≤1, then taper over at least 1 month. Consider additional immunosuppressants if corticosteroids are insufficient.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed/black‑box warnings specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Immune‑mediated adverse reactions:
  - **retifanlimab‑dlwr** is a PD‑1–blocking monoclonal antibody and can cause severe or fatal immune‑mediated adverse reactions affecting any organ system. These may occur at any time during or after treatment and can affect multiple organs concurrently.
  - Manage suspected immune‑mediated events promptly, perform evaluation to exclude alternative etiologies (including infection), initiate corticosteroids for moderate to severe events, and involve appropriate specialty consultation as needed.
- Infusion‑related reactions:
  - Can occur; interrupt or slow infusion for Grade 1–2 reactions and permanently discontinue for Grade 3–4 reactions.
- Complications of allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT):
  - Fatal and serious complications have occurred in patients who receive allogeneic HSCT before or after PD‑1/PD‑L1 blockade. Consider timing and risks when planning HSCT relative to PD‑1 therapy.
- Embryo‑fetal toxicity:
  - Can cause fetal harm. Advise females of reproductive potential of the potential risk and use effective contraception. (See Section 9.)

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic monitoring:
  - Evaluate liver enzymes (AST, ALT), total bilirubin, serum creatinine, and thyroid function at baseline and periodically during treatment.
  - Monitor for new or worsening respiratory symptoms (pneumonitis), diarrhea/abdominal pain (colitis), signs of hepatitis, endocrinopathy symptoms (fatigue, headache, hypotension, polyuria/polydipsia), and skin reactions.
- Consider repeat infectious workup in corticosteroid‑refractory colitis (e.g., cytomegalovirus).

### Specific clinical precautions (selected)
- For suspected immune‑mediated adrenal insufficiency, hypophysitis, thyroiditis, or new severe hyperglycemia, evaluate and initiate appropriate hormone replacement or insulin therapy as indicated.
- For suspected immune‑mediated hepatitis or nephritis, withhold or discontinue and start systemic corticosteroids per severity; consider hepatology/nephrology consult.
- For suspected myocarditis: permanently discontinue and manage as severe immune‑mediated myocarditis.
- Use caution with concurrent immunosuppressive therapy; corticosteroids for management may affect cancer therapy response.
- Do not administer as an IV push or bolus; do not co‑infuse with other IV agents via the same line.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The following adverse reactions were reported in clinical trials and safety databases in the available source PI. Frequencies are provided where explicitly stated in the available source. When a range/regimen difference was reported, this is indicated.

### Overview of very common / common adverse reactions reported in trials
- SCAC — combination with carboplatin and paclitaxel (most common, ≥20%): fatigue; peripheral neuropathy; nausea; alopecia; diarrhea; musculoskeletal pain; constipation; hemorrhage; rash; vomiting; decreased appetite; pruritus; abdominal pain. [US]
- SCAC — monotherapy (most common, ≥10%): fatigue; musculoskeletal pain; diarrhea; non‑urinary tract infections; perineal pain; hemorrhage; urinary tract infection; rash; nausea; decreased appetite; constipation; abdominal pain; dyspnea; pyrexia; vomiting; cough; pruritus; hypothyroidism; headache; decreased weight. [US]
- MCC — monotherapy (most common, ≥10%): musculoskeletal pain; fatigue; pruritus; diarrhea; rash; pyrexia; nausea; constipation. [US]

### Immune‑mediated adverse reactions (selected with frequencies)
- Pneumonitis: overall 3.1% of patients; Grade 3: 0.9%; Grade 2: 1.3%; fatal pneumonitis: 0.2%. Systemic corticosteroids required in 71% of pneumonitis cases. [US]
- Colitis:
  - Monotherapy: 2.7% (Grade 4 0.2%; Grade 3 0.4%; Grade 2 1.1%). Systemic corticosteroids required in 75% of cases. [US]
  - Combination with carboplatin/paclitaxel: 10% (Grade 4 0.6%; Grade 3 2.6%; Grade 2 3.2%). Systemic corticosteroids required in 94% of cases. [US]
- Hepatitis: 3.5% (Grade 4 0.2%; Grade 3 2.4%; Grade 2 0.9%). Systemic corticosteroids required in 81% of cases. [US]
- Adrenal insufficiency:
  - Monotherapy: 0.9% (Grade 3 0.4%; Grade 2 0.4%). [US]
  - Combination therapy: 5.8% (Grade 3 and Grade 2 each 1.9%). [US]
- Hypophysitis: 0.7% (Grade 3 0.2%; Grade 2 0.4%). [US]
- Thyroid disorders:
  - Thyroiditis: 0.7% (all Grade 1). [US]
  - Hypothyroidism: 10% (Grade 2 4.9%). [US]
  - Hyperthyroidism: 6% (Grade 2 2.7%). [US]
- Type 1 diabetes mellitus (including diabetic ketoacidosis): 0.2% (Grade 3 0.2%). [US]
- Immune‑mediated nephritis with renal dysfunction: 2% (Grade 4 0.4%; Grade 3 1.1%; Grade 2 0.4%). Systemic corticosteroids required in 67% of cases. [US]
- Immune‑mediated dermatologic reactions: 10% (Grade 4 0.2%; Grade 3 1.1%; Grade 2 8%). Systemic corticosteroids required in 33% of cases. [US]

### Other clinically significant immune‑mediated adverse reactions reported (<1%)
- Cardiac/vascular: myocarditis; pericarditis; vasculitis.
- Gastrointestinal: pancreatitis (including increases in serum amylase and lipase); gastritis; duodenitis.
- Musculoskeletal: myositis/polymyositis; rhabdomyolysis (and associated sequelae including renal failure); arthritis; polymyalgia rheumatica.
- Neurological: meningitis; encephalitis; myelitis and demyelination; myasthenic syndrome/myasthenia gravis (including exacerbations); Guillain‑Barré syndrome.
- Frequency: each listed above occurred at <1% in the available safety population. [US]

### Infusion-related reactions
- Infusion‑related reactions reported; specific incidence not specified in the available source. Management: interrupt or slow infusion for Grade 1–2 and permanently discontinue for Grade 3–4.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - Specific pharmacokinetic drug–drug interactions (e.g., CYP interactions) are not specified in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions / clinically relevant interactions:
  - Combined use with cytotoxic chemotherapy: approved/regimen described for use in combination with carboplatin and paclitaxel for first‑line SCAC. Follow dosing and safety recommendations for each agent in combination regimens. [US]

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **retifanlimab‑dlwr** is a monoclonal antibody that binds to the programmed death receptor‑1 (PD‑1), blocking PD‑1/PD‑L1 pathway signaling and thereby potentiating antitumor immune responses by releasing inhibition of T‑cell activity.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Not applicable (intravenous administration).
- Distribution, metabolism, elimination, and immunogenicity: Specific pharmacokinetic parameters (e.g., half‑life, clearance, volume of distribution), metabolic pathways, and immunogenicity data are not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- PD‑1 pathway blockade leading to enhanced T‑cell activation and potential for immune‑mediated toxicity. Detailed pharmacodynamic markers and exposure–response relationships: Not specified in available sources.
