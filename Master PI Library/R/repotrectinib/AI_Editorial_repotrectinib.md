# Editorial Product Information: Repotrectinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Regulatory Agencies:** Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA), Australia; Food and Drug Administration (FDA), United States.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information from the aforementioned agencies. It is intended as a clinically accurate reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) or Prescribing Information required for clinical practice within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Repotrectinib
- **Chemical Class:** Kinase inhibitor; macrocyclic tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI).
- **Brand Name:** **AUGTYRO**
- **ATC Code:** L01EX28
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **40 mg hard capsules:** White, opaque, Size 0, imprinted with "REP 40". [TGA/FDA]
    - **160 mg hard capsules:** Blue, opaque, Size 0, imprinted with "REP 160". [TGA/FDA]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Bristol-Myers Squibb Australia Pty Ltd [TGA]
    - E.R. Squibb & Sons, L.L.C. [FDA]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **ROS1-positive Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** Monotherapy for the treatment of adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic ROS1-positive NSCLC. [TGA/FDA]
- **NTRK-positive Solid Tumors:** Treatment of adult and paediatric patients 12 years of age and older with solid tumors that have a neurotrophic tyrosine receptor kinase (*NTRK*) gene fusion, are locally advanced or metastatic (or where surgical resection is likely to result in severe morbidity), and have progressed following treatment or have no satisfactory alternative therapy. [FDA-approved; not specified in TGA source].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Paediatrics $\ge$ 12 years)
- **Initiation Phase:** 160 mg orally once daily for 14 days.
- **Maintenance Phase:** Increase to 160 mg orally twice daily.
- **Duration:** Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules must be swallowed whole. Do not open, crush, chew, or dissolve.
- May be taken with or without food.
- **Grapefruit:** Avoid consumption of grapefruit or grapefruit juice during treatment.

### Missed Dose and Vomiting
- If a dose is missed, it should be skipped if not taken within a reasonable timeframe; do not double the next dose.
- If vomiting occurs after administration, the dose should not be repeated; resume treatment at the next scheduled time.

### Dose Modifications for Adverse Reactions
Reductions should be implemented in the following sequence:
1. **First Reduction:** 120 mg (once or twice daily, depending on current schedule).
2. **Second Reduction:** 80 mg (once or twice daily, depending on current schedule).
3. **Discontinuation:** If unable to tolerate 80 mg twice daily.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to repotrectinib or any excipients in the formulation (e.g., sulfites) [TGA]. 
- **FDA Note:** The FDA PI states "None" under the contraindications section.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects:** Broad-spectrum CNS reactions occur frequently (approx. 77% of patients), including dizziness, ataxia, cognitive disorders (memory impairment, confusion), and mood disorders. Dizziness is the most common (over 60%). Patients should be advised not to drive or operate machinery until they know how the drug affects them.
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** Serious and potentially fatal cases have occurred. Monitor for new or worsening respiratory symptoms. Withhold for suspected ILD and permanently discontinue if confirmed.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevations in ALT, AST, and bilirubin are common. Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) every 2 weeks for the first month, then monthly thereafter.
- **Myalgia and Creatine Phosphokinase (CPK) Elevation:** Monitor CPK levels at baseline and every 2 weeks during the first month for patients reporting muscle pain or weakness.
- **Hyperuricaemia:** Repotrectinib may increase serum uric acid levels. Monitor levels prior to initiation and periodically during treatment. Initiate urate-lowering therapy as clinically indicated.
- **Skeletal Fractures:** Increased risk of fractures, which may occur at sites of disease or following minor trauma. Evaluate patients promptly for signs of fracture (e.g., pain, deformity).
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential before initiation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (61–65%), dysgeusia (51–54%), peripheral neuropathy (49–50%), ataxia (28%), cognitive impairment (24–25%), headache (19%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Constipation (37–38%), nausea (18–20%), diarrhea (12–14%), vomiting (12% [FDA]).
- **Respiratory:** Dyspnea (30%), cough (17–18%).
- **General:** Fatigue (25–30%), edema (15%), decreased appetite (11% [FDA]).
- **Musculoskeletal:** Muscular weakness (20–22%), myalgia (12–13%).
- **Metabolic/Other:** Increased weight (16%), vision disorders (11–12%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Pneumonia (3.4–6.3%), dyspnea (3.1%), pleural effusion (2.8–3.4%), and hypoxia (2.6–3.1%).
- **Fatalities (4.1%):** Reported causes include cardiac arrest, sudden death, respiratory failure, and disseminated intravascular coagulation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Effect of CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Avoid concomitant use with strong or moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole), as they significantly increase repotrectinib exposure and toxicity risk. Discontinue inhibitors for 3–5 half-lives before starting repotrectinib.
- **Effect of CYP3A4 Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use with strong or moderate CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampin), as they markedly decrease repotrectinib plasma concentrations and efficacy.
- **Effect of Repotrectinib on Other Drugs:** Repotrectinib is a **moderate CYP3A4 inducer** [TGA] / **CYP3A4 inducer** [FDA]. It may reduce the efficacy of CYP3A4 substrates, particularly hormonal contraceptives and drugs with narrow therapeutic indices.
- **P-gp Inhibitors:** Avoid concomitant use; may increase repotrectinib exposure.
- **Transporters:** Repotrectinib may inhibit P-gp, BCRP, OATP1B1, MATE1, and MATE2-K.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **TGA Category D:** Drugs which have caused, are suspected to have caused or may be expected to cause, an increased incidence of human fetal malformations or irreversible damage.
    - **FDA:** Advise of potential fetal risk. Repotrectinib is teratogenic in animals at subclinical exposures.
- **Contraception:** 
    - **Females:** Use effective non-hormonal contraception during treatment and for **2 months** after the final dose.
    - **Males:** Use effective contraception (condoms) during treatment and for **4 months** after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on presence in human milk. Because of potential serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child, discontinue breastfeeding during treatment and for **10 days** after the final dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **FDA:** Safety and efficacy established for NTRK-positive solid tumors in patients $\ge$ 12 years. Not established for ROS1-positive NSCLC or in patients < 12 years.
    - **TGA:** Safety and efficacy in patients < 18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No clinically meaningful differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients $\ge$ 65 years and younger patients. No dose adjustment required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment (eGFR 30–90 mL/min). Not established for severe impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min) or dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild impairment. Not established for moderate (Total Bilirubin > 1.5–3x ULN) or severe (Total Bilirubin > 3x ULN) impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Repotrectinib inhibits ROS1 and the tropomyosin receptor kinases (TRKs) TRKA, TRKB, and TRKC. It is designed to bind within the ATP-binding pocket even in the presence of solvent front mutations (e.g., ROS1 G2032R) that confer resistance to other TKIs.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is approx. 45.7%. Median Tmax is 2–3 hours. 
    - **Food Effect:** No clinically significant effect with high-fat meals.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is 95.4%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Time-dependent due to autoinduction of CYP3A4. Steady-state terminal half-life is approx. 40 hours. Excretion is primarily fecal (88.8%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **TRIDENT-1 Study:** A multi-cohort, open-label, phase 1/2 trial.
    - **ROS1-positive NSCLC:** In TKI-naïve patients (n=71), the Overall Response Rate (ORR) was 79% (95% CI: 68, 88) with a median Duration of Response (DOR) of 34.1 months. In patients previously treated with one ROS1 TKI (n=56), the ORR was 38% (95% CI: 25, 52).
    - **NTRK-positive Solid Tumors [FDA]:** In TKI-naïve patients (n=40), the ORR was 58%. In TKI-pretreated patients (n=48), the ORR was 50%.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **TGA:** Store below 25°C.
- **FDA:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
- **Handling:** Keep in original container. Do not use if capsules are broken, cracked, or damaged.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States:** Rx-only.
