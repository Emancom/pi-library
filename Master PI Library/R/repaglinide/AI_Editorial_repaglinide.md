# Editorial Product Information: Repaglinide

*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** The following Product Information (PI) document served as the sole source for this editorial:
    *   United States (US FDA): Repaglinide Tablets (distributed by Aurobindo Pharma USA / Bryant Ranch Prepack).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis intended for use by healthcare professionals. It integrates clinical data into a concise, structured format.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial overview and does not replace the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Repaglinide.
*   **Chemical Class:** Glinide (meglitinide analogue); non-sulfonylurea insulin secretagogue.
*   **Brand Names:** Prandin [US-Original Reference Brand; not explicitly named in the provided prepack label].
*   **ATC Code:** A10BX02.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **Tablets (Oral):** 0.5 mg (white to off-white), 1 mg (yellow), 2 mg (peach/mottled) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aurobindo Pharma Limited (distributed by Bryant Ranch Prepack) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM):** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults [US].
*   **Limitations of Use:** 
    *   Not for the treatment of type 1 diabetes mellitus or diabetic ketoacidosis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
*   **Administration Timing:** Doses must be taken orally within 30 minutes before each meal. Repaglinide may be dosed 2, 3, or 4 times daily depending on the patient's meal pattern [US].
*   **Dose-Meal Relationship:** Patients should be instructed to **skip the dose** if a meal is skipped and **add a dose** if an extra meal is consumed to minimize the risk of hypoglycemia [US].

### Starting Doses
*   **HbA1c < 8%:** 0.5 mg before each meal [US].
*   **HbA1c ≥ 8%:** 1 mg or 2 mg before each meal [US].

### Titration and Maximum Dose
*   **Titration:** Dosage should be adjusted based on glycemic response (Fasting Plasma Glucose). Doses may be doubled at each step up to a maximum of 4 mg per meal. At least one week should elapse between dose adjustments to assess response [US].
*   **Maximum Daily Dose:** 16 mg [US].

### Missed Dose
*   If a meal is skipped, the corresponding dose should be omitted [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Gemfibrozil:** Concomitant administration is strictly contraindicated due to a significant increase in repaglinide exposure [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to repaglinide or any inactive tablet ingredients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypoglycemia:** All glinides can cause hypoglycemia. Risk is increased by meal-skipping, strenuous exercise, alcohol ingestion, or renal/hepatic impairment. Severe cases may be life-threatening or result in seizures/coma [US].
*   **Serious Cardiovascular Reactions (Combination with NPH-Insulin):** Repaglinide is **not indicated** for use in combination with NPH-insulin. Clinical trials showed a higher rate of serious myocardial ischemia in patients using this combination compared to insulin alone [US].
*   **Macrovascular Outcomes:** Conclusive evidence of macrovascular risk reduction has not been established for repaglinide [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of HbA1c and periodic blood glucose (especially during stress or dose titration) is required [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (Incidence ≥ 5%)
The following frequencies are based on US clinical trials:
*   **Hypoglycemia:** Very common (>10%); occurred in 31% of patients in trials (vs. 7% placebo) [US].
*   **Upper Respiratory Infection:** Common (16%) [US].
*   **Headache:** Common (11%) [US].
*   **Sinusitis:** Common (6%) [US].
*   **Arthralgia:** Common (6%) [US].
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (5%), Diarrhea (5%) [US].
*   **Back Pain:** Common (5%) [US].

### Infrequent and Postmarketing Reactions
*   **Cardiovascular:** Serious cardiovascular events (including ischemia) occurred in 4% of patients in controlled trials [US].
*   **Weight Gain:** Average gain was 3.3% in patients not previously treated with sulfonylureas [US].
*   **Hepatobiliary:** Elevated liver enzymes; postmarketing reports of severe hepatic dysfunction including jaundice and hepatitis [US].
*   **Dermatologic/Allergic:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, alopecia, anaphylactoid reactions [US].
*   **Hematologic:** Hemolytic anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
*   **CYP2C8 and CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Drugs such as clarithromycin, ketoconazole, and itraconazole increase repaglinide exposure. Dose reduction and frequent glucose monitoring may be required [US].
*   **CYP2C8 and CYP3A4 Inducers:** Drugs such as rifampin, barbiturates, and carbamazepine may decrease repaglinide exposure. Dose increases may be necessary [US].
*   **OATP1B1 Inhibitors:** Repaglinide is a substrate for hepatic uptake transporter OATP1B1 [US].

### Significant Specific Interactions
*   **Gemfibrozil:** Increases repaglinide AUC by 8.1-fold. **Do not co-administer** [US].
*   **Clopidogrel:** Increases repaglinide AUC by 3.9 to 5.1-fold. Avoid concomitant use; if unavoidable, limit total repaglinide to 4 mg/day and initiate at 0.5 mg pre-prandially [US].
*   **Cyclosporine:** Increases repaglinide AUC by 2.5-fold. Limit total repaglinide to 6 mg/day [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
*   **Increased Hypoglycemic Effect:** ACE inhibitors, Beta-blockers, MAOIs, NSAIDs, salicylates, and sulfonamides [US].
*   **Decreased Hypoglycemic Effect (Hyperglycemia Risk):** Atypical antipsychotics, corticosteroids, diuretics, estrogens, oral contraceptives, phenothiazines, and thyroid hormones [US].
*   **Masking Hypoglycemia:** Beta-blockers, clonidine, and reserpine may blunt the autonomic signs of hypoglycemia [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified in new labeling format]. Limited data from case reports have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects. However, poorly controlled diabetes in pregnancy poses significant risks to the mother and fetus. Animal data showed skeletal deformations at high exposures (4x human dose) during lactation/gestation [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. While human data are absent, the drug is present in animal milk, and there is a theoretical risk of hypoglycemia in the infant [US].
*   **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at doses >60 times the human exposure [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between those >65 years and younger subjects. However, greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:**
    *   **Mild to Moderate (CrCl 40-80 mL/min):** No initial dose adjustment required [US].
    *   **Severe (CrCl 20-40 mL/min):** Initiate at 0.5 mg before each meal and titrate carefully [US].
    *   **CrCl < 20 mL/min or Hemodialysis:** Not studied [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Repaglinide exposure is higher and more prolonged in patients with chronic liver disease. Use with caution; allow longer intervals between dose adjustments [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Repaglinide stimulates insulin release from pancreatic beta cells by closing ATP-dependent potassium channels. This depolarizes the cell, opening calcium channels and inducing insulin secretion. The effect is glucose-dependent and diminishes at low glucose concentrations [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; $T_{max}$ within 1 hour. Bioavailability is 56%. Co-administration with food decreases $C_{max}$ and AUC by 20% and 12.4%, respectively [US].
*   **Distribution:** High protein binding (>98%) [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Completely metabolized by oxidative biotransformation (CYP2C8 and CYP3A4) and direct conjugation [US].
*   **Elimination:** 90% recovered in feces (mostly as metabolites) and 8% in urine. Elimination half-life is approximately 1 hour [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Monotherapy:** In a 24-week trial, repaglinide reduced HbA1c by 1.7% to 2.1% in treatment-naïve patients compared to placebo [US].
*   **Combination with Metformin:** Showed statistically significant improvements in HbA1c and FPG compared to either monotherapy, with evidence of dose-sparing effects [US].
*   **Combination with Thiazolidinediones:** Combination with pioglitazone or rosiglitazone resulted in greater HbA1c reduction than monotherapy, but with increased risks of weight gain and peripheral edema [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Handling:** Protect from moisture and keep bottles tightly closed. Dispense in tight containers with safety closures [US].
