# Editorial Product Information: Capmatinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdiction's Product Information (PI): 
    - **US (FDA)**: TABRECTA (capmatinib) tablets, revised March 2024.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of capmatinib for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational use by healthcare professionals and is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the local PI for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Capmatinib (present as capmatinib dihydrochloride monohydrate).
- **Chemical Class**: Kinase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name**: **TABRECTA** [US].
- **ATC Code**: L01EX17.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Film-coated tablets: 150 mg (pale orange brown, ovaloid, debossed with ‘DU’ and ‘NVR’) and 200 mg (yellow, ovaloid, debossed with ‘LO’ and ‘NVR’) [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)**: For the treatment of adult patients with metastatic NSCLC whose tumors have a mutation that leads to mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET) exon 14 skipping, as detected by a regulatory-approved test [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Patient Selection**: Presence of a MET exon 14 skipping mutation must be confirmed in tumor or plasma specimens prior to initiation [US]. 
- **Standard Dosage**: 400 mg taken orally twice daily [US].
- **Administration**:
    - May be taken with or without food.
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not break, crush, or chew.
- **Dose Modifications for Adverse Reactions**:
    - **First reduction**: 300 mg twice daily.
    - **Second reduction**: 200 mg twice daily.
    - **Discontinuation**: Permanently discontinue if 200 mg twice daily cannot be tolerated.
- **Missed Dose**: If a dose is missed or vomited, the patient should not take a compensatory dose but should wait until the next scheduled dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis**: Can be fatal. Monitor for new or worsening pulmonary symptoms (e.g., dyspnea, cough, fever). Permanently discontinue if ILD/pneumonitis is confirmed.
- **Hepatotoxicity**: Increased transaminases and bilirubin have occurred. Monitor liver function tests (ALT, AST, total bilirubin) prior to start, every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then monthly.
- **Pancreatic Toxicity**: Elevations in amylase and lipase have been reported. Monitor these levels at baseline and regularly during treatment.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions (pyrexia, chills, pruritus, rash, hypotension) have occurred. Withhold or discontinue based on severity.
- **Risk of Photosensitivity**: Potential for ultraviolet (UV) sensitivity. Advise patients to limit direct UV exposure and use sunscreen/protective clothing.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity**: Based on mechanism and animal data, can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential before starting.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **General**: Edema (59%), Fatigue (34%), Pyrexia (14%), Weight decreased (11%).
- **Gastrointestinal**: Nausea (46%), Vomiting (28%), Constipation (19%), Diarrhea (19%).
- **Musculoskeletal**: Musculoskeletal pain (40%).
- **Respiratory**: Dyspnea (25%), Cough (21%), Pneumonia (13%).
- **Metabolic**: Decreased appetite (21%).
- **Dermatologic**: Rash (13%).
- **Nervous System**: Dizziness (13%).

### Common (1–10%)
- Pruritus, cellulitis, urticaria, acute pancreatitis.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Serious adverse reactions occurred in 53% of patients. The most frequent (≥2%) included dyspnea, pneumonia, pleural effusion, and musculoskeletal pain [US].
- Fatalities (0.5%) were reported due to pneumonitis or other causes.

### Laboratory Abnormalities (≥20%)
- Decreased albumin, increased creatinine, increased ALT/AST, increased amylase/lipase, increased alkaline phosphatase, increased GGT, and decreased lymphocytes/hemoglobin [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong/Moderate CYP3A Inducers**: Avoid concomitant use (e.g., rifampicin, efavirenz) as they significantly decrease capmatinib exposure and may reduce efficacy [US].
- **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors**: Closely monitor for increased adverse reactions if coadministered with strong CYP3A inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole).
- **CYP1A2 Substrates**: Capmatinib may increase exposure to CYP1A2 substrates (e.g., caffeine). Reduce substrate dosage if minimal concentration changes lead to serious toxicity.
- **Transporter Substrates (P-gp, BCRP, MATE1/2K)**: Capmatinib may increase the concentration of substrates for these transporters (e.g., digoxin, rosuvastatin). Reduce substrate dosage as per their respective PI if necessary.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Category**: Not specified under current labeling rules, but documented risk of fetal harm. 
    - Animal studies showed malformations at exposures below the human clinical dose. Advise patients of potential risk.
- **Contraception**: Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for **1 week** after the final dose. Males with female partners of reproductive potential should also use effective contraception for **1 week** post-dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Advise women not to breastfeed during treatment and for **1 week** after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility**: No dedicated human studies; no effects on reproductive organs were observed in general toxicology studies in rats or monkeys.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Geriatric Use**: No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose adjustment for mild (CLcr 60–89 mL/min) or moderate (CLcr 30–59 mL/min) impairment. Not studied in severe renal impairment (CLcr 15–29 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No dose adjustment for mild, moderate, or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A, B, or C).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Capmatinib is a kinase inhibitor targeting MET, including the variant produced by exon 14 skipping. It inhibits MET phosphorylation and downstream signaling, leading to decreased cancer cell growth and survival.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: >70% absorbed. Peak plasma levels (Tmax) in 1–2 hours. High-fat meals increase AUC by 46% but are not considered clinically significant for dosing.
    - **Distribution**: 96% protein bound. Volume of distribution at steady state is 164 L.
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily via CYP3A4 and aldehyde oxidase.
    - **Elimination**: Mean elimination half-life is 6.5 hours. Excretion is mainly fecal (78%) and renal (22%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **GEOMETRY mono-1**: A multicenter, non-randomized, open-label study (N=160 with MET exon 14 skipping).
    - **Treatment-naïve patients**: Overall Response Rate (ORR) 68%; Median Duration of Response (DOR) 16.6 months.
    - **Previously treated patients**: ORR 44%; Median DOR 9.7 months.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
- Keep in original package with the desiccant to protect from moisture.
- **Important**: Discard any remaining tablets **6 weeks** after first opening the bottle [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States**: Rx-only.
