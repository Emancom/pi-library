# Editorial Product Information: citric + magnesium + sodium

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **citric + magnesium + sodium**
- Chemical class:
  - Combination stimulant and osmotic laxative (sodium picosulfate = stimulant laxative; citric acid + magnesium carbonate hydrate → magnesium citrate = osmotic laxative)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration:
  - Oral administration (solution prepared from sachet)
  - Administration via nasogastric intubation described

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval:
  - 28 April 2021 [Australian]
- Date of last revision/update of source product information:
  - 6 January 2025 [Australian]
- Withdrawal, suspension or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.
- Registration/approval dates or PI revision dates for jurisdictions other than Australian: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Bowel emptying and cleansing by total gastrointestinal tract perfusion in preparation for gastrointestinal examination (e.g. colonoscopy, barium enema), prior to intravenous pyelograms (IVP) or colorectal surgery.
- Indicated for use in adults and children aged 9 years and over.
- Use for other indications: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (per sachet composition)
- Each sachet contains:
  - sodium picosulfate 10.3 mg
  - magnesium carbonate hydrate 7.4 g
  - citric acid 12.2 g
- Each sachet is intended to be dissolved in approximately 250 mL water to produce a solution for ingestion.

### Recommended dosing regimens
- Two-sachet total dose regimens (adult and children ≥9 years):
  - Single-day regimen (example):
    - Day before procedure: 1st sachet at ~15:00; 2nd sachet at ~21:00.
  - Split-dose regimen:
    - Day before procedure: 1st sachet at ~19:00.
    - Day of procedure: 2nd sachet approx. 2–5 hours before procedure.
  - Note: Split-dose regimens are recommended when procedure is in the afternoon and provide superior cleansing for morning procedures when administered over two days.

- Three-sachet total dose regimens (adult and children ≥9 years where clinically indicated):
  - Single-day regimen (example):
    - Day before procedure: 1st sachet ~13:00, 2nd sachet ~17:00, 3rd sachet ~21:00.
  - Split-dose regimen:
    - Day before procedure: 1st sachet ~15:00–17:00, 2nd sachet ~21:00.
    - Day of procedure: 3rd sachet approx. 2–5 hours before procedure.

- Paediatric dosing:
  - A two-sachet course should be adequate for children from 9 years of age.
  - Contraindicated in children below 9 years.

### Preparation of solution and administration instructions
- Dissolve the contents of one sachet by slowly adding the powder to approximately 250 mL of water at ambient temperature; solution will effervesce and may become warm—stir gently until effervescence ceases.
- The prepared solution should be ingested slowly but completely.
- The solution should be ingested within 24 hours of reconstitution.
- During the day of preparation and until procedure, patients should consume recommended clear fluids (see below) and are advised to maintain hydration by drinking at least one 250 mL glass of recommended clear fluids each hour until bedtime, in addition to the water taken with the preparation.
- It is recommended patients follow a modified diet (e.g. low-fibre) up until they take the medication; upon starting the medication, only recommended clear fluids should be consumed.
- Patients are generally advised to cease taking any fluids 2 hours prior to the procedure.

### Nasogastric administration
- Administration via nasogastric intubation is described with careful observation to ensure hydration and prevention of aspiration.
- Infuse prepared solution at a rate described as 20–30 mL/minute.
- The source document describes infusing 1.2–1.8 L of prepared solution each hour, as per oral administration; if gastrointestinal distension, bloating or pain develops, slow or temporarily stop administration.

### Missed doses and titration
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.
- Titration schedules: Not specified in available sources.

### Recommended clear fluids (examples)
- Water
- Fat-free clear soups (strained)
- Broth/bouillon
- Pulp-free fruit juices (apple, pear, grape)
- Black tea or coffee (no milk)
- Electrolyte-replacement drinks
- Commercial high-energy, fat-free, milk-free nutritional supplements
- Carbonated beverages
- Clear fruit cordials (lemon, lime)
- Plain jelly, sorbet, plain boiled sweets, gums/jubes
- Avoid red or purple-coloured fluids; sugar, salt and sweetener may be used.

## 5. Contraindications
List of contraindications (all jurisdictions combined as stated in available sources):
- Clinically significant renal impairment.
- Gastrointestinal obstruction.
- Gastric retention.
- Bowel perforation (frank or suspected).
- Toxic megacolon.
- Toxic colitis.
- Ileus.
- Presence of a stoma.
- Severe dehydration.
- Hypersensitivity to any component of the product.
- Use in children below 9 years of age.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / prominent warning
- **Life‑threatening dehydration and/or electrolyte disturbances may occur in at‑risk groups.** [Australian]

### General warnings and precautions
- Administration may cause transient hypovolaemia; ensure adequate fluid intake or replacement.
- Exercise caution in debilitated patients and those with severe ulcerative colitis, pre-existing electrolyte disturbances, heart disease (including congestive heart failure), dehydration, or diabetes.
- Use with care in elderly patients; they are at greater risk of dehydration and electrolyte depletion and must receive adequate fluids during administration.
- Not recommended in patients with clinically significant renal impairment; patients with renal disease or impaired renal function should be monitored closely.
- Administration should be undertaken with caution in patients with impaired gag reflex, those who are unconscious or semi‑conscious, or those prone to regurgitation or aspiration. Nasogastric administration requires careful observation.
- If administered by nasogastric tube, bloating, distension or abdominal pain may occur; slow or temporarily stop administration if these symptoms develop.
- Caution and monitoring are advised for patients receiving calcium channel blockers, diuretics or other medications that may affect serum electrolytes or exacerbate volume depletion.
- Use in hepatic impairment: No data available in available sources.
- Effects on laboratory tests: Not specified in available sources.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor hydration status and fluid balance in all patients, particularly the elderly, debilitated, or those with cardiac or renal impairment.
- Monitor electrolytes (sodium, potassium, chloride, calcium) in patients at risk of electrolyte disturbances and in suspected overdose.
- Patients on medications that affect electrolytes (e.g., diuretics) should have electrolyte monitoring during and after use.
- Diabetic patients: monitor blood glucose and consider adjustment of antidiabetic therapy because recommended liquid diets may affect glycaemic control.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Oral medications, particularly sustained-release products, short half-life drugs or drugs with a narrow therapeutic index, taken within 1 hour of commencing the bowel preparation may be flushed from the gastrointestinal tract and not absorbed — consider timing of other oral medications.
- Low‑dose oral contraceptives may be rendered ineffective if taken with the preparation; advise alternative contraception as clinically appropriate.
- The efficacy of sodium picosulfate depends on colonic bacterial conversion; concurrent antibiotics may reduce effectiveness.

### Overdose
- Overdose may result in dehydration. Monitor calcium, potassium, chloride and sodium levels and correct electrolyte imbalances with appropriate fluid replacement.
- For management of overdose/poisoning: local poison information procedures should be followed. (Exact regional contact details not specified in this unified document.)

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Oral medications taken within 1 hour of starting the preparation may be flushed and not absorbed (notably sustained-release formulations and drugs with short half-lives or narrow therapeutic windows).
- Antibiotics may reduce the effectiveness of sodium picosulfate because sodium picosulfate requires colonic bacterial hydrolysis to its active metabolite.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions / clinically important interactions
- Concomitant use with medications that alter electrolyte balance (e.g., diuretics, certain antihypertensives such as calcium channel blockers) may increase risk of clinically significant electrolyte disturbances and volume depletion; monitor and adjust therapy as appropriate.
- Diabetic medications: liquid diet and reduced oral intake may alter glycaemic control; diabetic patients may require adjustment of antidiabetic therapy.
- Oral contraceptives: low‑dose combined oral contraceptives may have reduced effectiveness when taken with the preparation (insufficient gastrointestinal residence time for absorption).

### Management notes
- Consider holding or re-timing oral medications with narrow therapeutic windows around the bowel preparation; provide alternative dosing advice as clinically appropriate.
- Monitor electrolytes and fluid status when co-administered with diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or other agents that affect renal function or electrolytes.
- Advise female patients on low-dose oral contraceptives of potential loss of contraceptive efficacy and discuss alternative contraception during the relevant cycle if applicable.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Use in pregnancy: It is not known whether the product can cause fetal harm or affect reproductive capacity. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
- Pregnancy categorisation: Not specified in available sources (no jurisdiction-specific pregnancy category provided).

### Breastfeeding
- Excretion in human milk: The medication is unlikely to be excreted in breast milk because the active agents exert a local action in the gut and are not absorbed systemically (sodium picosulfate is not systemically absorbed).
- Recommendation: Use with caution; consider clinical need vs potential risk.

### Fertility
- Effects on fertility: No fertility studies have been conducted; not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Approved for use in children aged 9 years and older.
- Contraindicated in children below 9 years of age.
- Recommended total dose for many paediatric patients: two sachets considered adequate for children ≥9 years (adjustment by healthcare professional possible).

### Elderly
- Exercise caution in elderly patients because of increased risk of dehydration and electrolyte depletion; ensure adequate fluid intake and consider electrolyte monitoring.

### Renal impairment

- Patients with kidney disease or impaired renal function should be monitored closely if the product is used (monitor electrolytes and renal function).

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Sodium picosulfate is a stimulant laxative that is converted by colonic bacterial enzymes to an active metabolite that directly stimulates the colonic mucosa to induce peristalsis.
- Citric acid reacts with magnesium carbonate hydrate to form magnesium citrate in situ; magnesium citrate acts as an osmotic laxative, drawing water into the bowel and producing a watery stool to cleanse the bowel.
- Typical onset: induction of watery stool usually within approximately 3 hours.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Sodium picosulfate is not absorbed systemically.
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism: Sodium picosulfate is hydrolysed by colonic bacterial sulfatase to the active compound bis(p‑hydroxy‑phenyl)‑pyridyl‑2‑methane.
- Excretion: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Combined stimulant (colonic peristalsis) and osmotic (increased luminal water) effects result in effective bowel cleansing.

- Carcinogenicity: No carcinogenic studies have been conducted (not specified).
