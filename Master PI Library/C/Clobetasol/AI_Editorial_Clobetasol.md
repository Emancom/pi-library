# Editorial Product Information: Clobetasol Propionate (Topical Shampoo)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the local approved PI before prescribing or administering this medication in their specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Clobetasol propionate (a very potent Group IV/Class IV corticosteroid).
- **Brand Name(s):** **CLOBEX** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** D07AD01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Topical Shampoo:** 500 micrograms/mL (0.05% w/v) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical (scalp only).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Galderma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Scalp Psoriasis:** Topical treatment of moderate to severe scalp psoriasis in adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Apply once daily directly to the dry scalp.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - The shampoo should be massaged well into the affected lesions.
    - **Maximum Daily Dose:** 7.5 mL (measured via the provided cup) [Australian].
    - **Contact Time:** The product must remain in place, uncovered, for **15 minutes** before rinsing with water.
    - Hands must be washed thoroughly after application.
    - Regular shampoo may be used after the 15-minute period if needed to facilitate washing.
    - **Interactions with Hair Dye:** The product must be thoroughly rinsed to avoid potential hair colour changes.
- **Treatment Duration:** Limited to a maximum of 4 consecutive weeks. Once clinical results are achieved, treatment should be tapered or transitioned to alternative therapies.
- **Reassessment:** If no clinical improvement is observed within 4 weeks, the diagnosis should be reassessed.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to clobetasol propionate or any excipients.
- **Infectious Conditions:** Skin areas affected by bacterial, mycobacterial, viral (e.g., herpes simplex, zoster, varicella), fungal, or parasitic infections.
- **Specific Pathologies:** Skin tuberculosis, syphilitic skin lesions (lues), or ulcerous wounds.
- **Site-Specific Restrictions:** Application to the eyes or eyelids is contraindicated due to risks of glaucoma and cataracts.
- **Age Restriction:** Contraindicated in children under 2 years of age [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **HPA Axis Suppression:** As a very potent corticosteroid, systemic absorption can cause reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycemia, and glucosuria. Risk increases with prolonged use, large volumes, or occlusive dressings. 
- **Treatment Limits:** Do not exceed 4 weeks of continuous use or 50 mL per week. Patients exceeding these limits require close monitoring for HPA suppression.
- **Withdrawal Effects:** Abrupt discontinuation can lead to acute adrenal insufficiency.
- **Site Restrictions:** Intended for the scalp only. Do not apply to the face, intertriginous areas (axillae, genitoanal regions), or erosive skin surfaces due to the risk of atrophy and telangiectasia.
- **Visual Disturbances:** Both systemic and topical corticosteroid use may lead to visual disturbances. Patients reporting blurred vision should be evaluated for cataracts, glaucoma, or central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR).
- **Specific Dermatological Conditions:** Not recommended for patients with acne vulgaris, rosacea, or perioral dermatitis.
- **Serious Complications:** Rare cases of osteonecrosis, necrotizing fasciitis, and systemic immunosuppression (e.g., Kaposi’s sarcoma) have been reported with long-term use beyond recommended doses.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequently reported adverse reaction is a skin burning sensation (approx. 5%) [Australian].

| Body System | Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Skin and Subcutaneous Tissue** | **Common** (1–10%) | Skin burning sensation, acne/folliculitis |
| | **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Skin pain/discomfort, irritation, pruritus, oedema, telangiectasia, psoriasis aggravation, alopecia, dry skin, urticaria, skin atrophy, skin tightness |
| **Eye Disorders** | **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Eye stinging/burning, eye irritation, ocular tight sensation, ocular discomfort |
| **Nervous System** | **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Headache |

- **Post-Marketing Reports:** Blurred vision, glaucoma, hypersensitivity, erythema, rash, and allergic contact dermatitis.
- **Class Effects:** Prolonged use of potent steroids may cause striae, purpura, hypertrichosis, and pigmentation changes.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No specific interaction studies have been performed.
- **Management:** To avoid potential interactions with hair dyes (colour changes), the shampoo must be thoroughly rinsed from the hair.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3.** Animal studies show reproductive toxicity and teratogenicity (cleft palate, skeletal abnormalities) at doses lower than the human topical dose. There are no adequate human trials. Use should be avoided unless clearly necessary.
- **Breastfeeding:** Systemic corticosteroids are excreted into breast milk. It is unknown if topical clobetasol results in significant milk transfer. Use should be avoided in breastfeeding women unless clearly necessary.
- **Fertility:** Subcutaneous administration in rats reduced embryo viability and maternal reproductive capacity at doses of 25–50 µg/kg/day, but did not affect mating performance.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children and adolescents under 18 years of age. Contraindicated in children under 2 years. Children are at higher risk of HPA axis suppression and growth retardation due to a larger skin surface area to body mass ratio.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific studies performed; no specific dose adjustments provided in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific studies performed. Patients with severe liver dysfunction should be treated with caution and monitored closely for systemic side effects.
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific studies performed. Patients with severe diabetes mellitus should be treated with caution due to potential systemic effects on glucose metabolism.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Clobetasol propionate induces phospholipase A2 inhibitory proteins (lipocortins), which inhibit the release of arachidonic acid. This reduces the biosynthesis of potent inflammatory mediators such as prostaglandins and leukotrienes.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Very low topical absorption when used as directed (15-minute rinse-off). In vitro studies show only 0.1% of the dose reaches the epidermis.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma concentrations are generally negligible; only 1 of 126 subjects in clinical trials had a quantifiable concentration (0.43 ng/mL).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** In five Phase III trials, 4 weeks of daily CLOBEX shampoo (15-minute application) was significantly more effective than vehicle. 
- **Comparative Data:**
    - Superior to calcipotriol 50 µg/mL solution [Australian].
    - Superior to coal tar 1% solution [Australian].
    - Found non-inferior to clobetasol propionate 0.05% gel (applied daily without rinsing) [Australian].
- **Safety Study:** A specific ultrasound study of 13 patients showed no skin thinning after 4 weeks of treatment.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** 4 weeks after the first opening of the container.
- **Packaging:** High-density polyethylene (HDPE) bottles of 30 mL, 60 mL, or 125 mL.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (S4) – Prescription Medicine.
