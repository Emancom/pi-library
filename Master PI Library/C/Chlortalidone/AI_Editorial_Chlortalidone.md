# Editorial Product Information: Chlortalidone

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of chlortalidone, synthesising clinical information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive prescribing guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Chlortalidone (a sulfonamide-derived benzothiadiazine/thiazide-related diuretic).
- **Brand Names:** **Hygroton** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C03BA04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 25 mg tablets (pale orange, speckled, round, flat with bevelled edges; imprinted "CW" and scored) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amdipharm Mercury (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Essential Arterial Hypertension:** Indicated as primary therapy or in combination with other antihypertensives, provided creatinine clearance (CrCl) is > 30 mL/min [Australian].
- **Chronic Heart Failure:** Treatment of stable, mild-to-moderate disease (NYHA Functional Class II or III) in patients with CrCl > 30 mL/min [Australian].
- **Edema/Ascites:** Treatment of ascites resulting from liver cirrhosis in stable patients under rigorous clinical supervision [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Therapy should be initiated at the lowest possible dose and titrated based on individual patient response to maximise efficacy while minimising adverse effects. Doses should ideally be taken as a single dose in the morning with food.

### Adult Dosing
- **Hypertension:**
    - Range: 12.5 mg to 50 mg daily.
    - Starting dose: 12.5 mg or 25 mg daily (25 mg is typically sufficient for maximum hypotensive effect). 
    - Full effect is usually reached within 3 to 4 weeks. 
- **Stable Chronic Heart Failure (Class II/III):**
    - Starting dose: 25 mg to 50 mg daily.
    - Maintenance: 12.5 mg to 50 mg daily, or 25 mg to 50 mg every other day.
    - Severe cases (Class IV) not tolerating loop diuretics: 50 mg every other day may be initiated [Australian].
- **Oedema of Specific Origin:**
    - Use lowest effective dose for limited periods; maximum dose 50 mg/day [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Children:** Use the lowest effective dose. Reported doses include an initial dose of 0.5 to 1 mg/kg every 48 hours, up to a maximum of 1.7 mg/kg every 48 hours [Australian].

### Administration Notes
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Combination Therapy:** When adding ACE inhibitors or Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers (ARBs), the chlortalidone dose should be reduced or discontinued for 2–3 days prior to starting the new agent to prevent excessive hypotension [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Renal/Hepatic Status:** Anuria, severe renal failure (CrCl < 30 mL/min), and hepatic failure [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to chlortalidone, other sulfonamide derivatives, or any tablet excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Refractory hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, and hypercalcaemia [Australian].
- **Metabolic:** Symptomatic hyperuricaemia (history of gout or uric acid calculi) [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Hypertension during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Endocrine:** Untreated Addison’s disease [Australian].
- **Other:** Conditions with enhanced potassium loss (e.g., salt-losing nephropathies) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [Australian].
- **Choroidal Effusion/Acute Glaucoma:** As a sulfonamide derivative, chlortalidone can cause idiosyncratic reactions resulting in choroidal effusion, acute transient myopia, and acute angle-closure glaucoma. Symptoms (decreased vision, ocular pain) occur within hours to weeks of initiation. Discontinue immediately if these symptoms occur [Australian].
- **Electrolyte Monitoring:** Thiazides can cause hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hyponatraemia, and hypercalcaemia. Monitoring is essential, particularly in the elderly, patients with cirrhosis/ascites, or those with nephrotic syndrome [Australian].
- **Metabolic Effects:** May elevate serum uric acid and lipids (cholesterol, triglycerides). Glucose tolerance may be impaired; avoid as first-line therapy in patients with overt diabetes or hypercholesterolaemia [Australian].
- **Azotaemia:** May be precipitated in patients with renal disease; effects may be cumulative [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse drug reactions (ADRs) are often dose-dependent.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Hypokalaemia, hyperuricaemia, hyperlipidaemia (mainly at higher doses). |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Hyponatraemia, hypomagnesaemia, hyperglycaemia, decreased appetite; Dizziness; Urticaria/rash; Orthostatic hypotension (aggravated by alcohol/sedatives); Muscular weakness; Erectile dysfunction. |
| **Rare** (0.1–1%) | Thrombocytopaenia, leucopaenia, agranulocytosis, eosinophilia; Paraesthesia, headache; Visual impairment; Intrahepatic cholestasis, jaundice; Photosensitivity; Glycosuria; Cardiac arrhythmia. |
| **Very Rare** (<0.1%) | Hypochloraemic alkalosis; Non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema; Pancreatitis; Tubulointerstitial nephritis; Vasculitis. |
| **Not Known** | Choroidal effusion, acute myopia, acute angle-closure glaucoma. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Lithium:** Diuretics increase blood lithium levels; paradoxically, they may exert an antidiuretic effect in lithium-induced polyuria.
- **Antihypertensives/Muscle Relaxants:** Potentiates the action of non-depolarising relaxants, curare derivatives, and other antihypertensives (e.g., beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors).
- **Hypokalaemic Agents:** Risk of severe hypokalaemia is increased when combined with corticosteroids, ACTH, beta-2 agonists, amphotericin B, or carbenoxolone.
- **NSAIDS (e.g., Indometacin):** May reduce diuretic and antihypertensive efficacy; potential for renal function deterioration in at-risk patients.
- **Digitalis:** Thiazide-induced hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia increases the risk of digitalis-induced arrhythmias.
- **Cytotoxic Agents (e.g., Cyclophosphamide, Methotrexate):** Reduced renal excretion may enhance myelosuppressive effects.
- **Bile Acid Sequestrants (e.g., Colestyramine):** Decreases chlortalidone absorption.
- **Others:** Potentiates hyperglycaemic effects of diazoxide; increases risk of hypercalcaemia with Vitamin D or calcium salts.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category C.** Diuretics may cause placental hypoperfusion and are suspected of causing harmful effects on the fetus (e.g., neonatal thrombocytopaenia, electrolyte disturbances). **Contraindicated** for hypertension in pregnancy; avoid for other indications unless no safer alternative exists [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Chlortalidone passes into breast milk (approx. 4% of maternal blood level). Avoid use in nursing mothers for safety reasons [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited data; refer to section 3 for specific weight-based dosing [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Elimination is slower compared to young adults, though absorption is unaffected. Require close medical observation and electrolyte monitoring [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Thiazide diuretics lose efficacy when CrCl is < 30 mL/min; loop diuretics are preferred in these patients [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with extreme caution. Minor fluid/electrolyte shifts may precipitate hepatic coma, particularly in patients with cirrhosis [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits NaCl reabsorption by antagonising the Na+-Cl- cotransporter in the early distal renal tubule. This increases delivery of Na+ and water to the collecting tubule, promoting K+ and H+ secretion. It also promotes calcium reabsorption [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Diuresis begins within 2–3 hours, peaks at 4–24 hours, and can persist for 48–72 hours [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability ~64%; peak blood concentrations at 8–12 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Highly bound to erythrocyte carbonic anhydrase (only 1.4% free in plasma at steady state); 76% plasma protein binding (albumin). Crosses the placenta.
    - **Metabolism/Excretion:** Elimination half-life ~50 hours. 70% excreted in urine and faeces, primarily unchanged [Australian].
