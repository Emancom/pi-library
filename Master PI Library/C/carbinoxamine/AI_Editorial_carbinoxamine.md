# Editorial Product Information: Carbinoxamine

## Editorial Note
- The information contained in this editorial was synthesized from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of carbinoxamine for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved labeling for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Carbinoxamine maleate.
- **Chemical Class:** H1-receptor antagonist (ethanolamine derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Noted as Carbinoxamine Maleate Extended-Release Oral Suspension (manufactured for Neos Therapeutics, LP) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Extended-release oral suspension:** 4 mg carbinoxamine maleate per 5 mL (equivalent to 2.86 mg of carbinoxamine) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Neos Therapeutics, LP; manufactured by Tris Pharma, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Carbinoxamine is indicated for the symptomatic treatment of the following conditions in adults and pediatric patients aged 2 years and older [US]:
- Seasonal and perennial allergic rhinitis.
- Vasomotor rhinitis.
- Allergic conjunctivitis caused by inhalant allergens and foods.
- Mild, uncomplicated allergic skin manifestations of urticaria and angioedema.
- Dermatographism.
- As adjunctive therapy for anaphylactic reactions (following the control of acute manifestations with epinephrine and other standard measures).
- Amelioration of the severity of allergic reactions to blood or plasma.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Administration Instructions
- Dosage must be individualized based on the severity of the condition and patient response.
- Start at the lower end of the dosing range and titrate upward as tolerated.
- **Note:** The suspension must be shaken well before each use.
- **Device Warning:** Use an accurate milliliter measuring device; household teaspoons are not accurate and may lead to dosing errors [US].

### 3.2 Adults and Adolescents (12 years and older)
- **Recommended Dose:** 7.5 mL to 20 mL (6 mg to 16 mg) orally every 12 hours [US].

### 3.3 Pediatric Patients (2 to 11 years of age)
Dosing is approximately 0.2 mg/kg/day to 0.4 mg/kg/day, administered in divided doses every 12 hours [US]:
- **2 to 3 years:** 3.75 mL to 5 mL (3 mg to 4 mg) every 12 hours.
- **4 to 5 years:** 3.75 mL to 10 mL (3 mg to 8 mg) every 12 hours.
- **6 to 11 years:** 7.5 mL to 15 mL (6 mg to 12 mg) every 12 hours.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Age:** Children younger than 2 years of age (due to reports of pediatric mortality) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to carbinoxamine maleate or any inactive ingredients (e.g., sodium metabisulfite) [US].
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Patients taking monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Pediatric Mortality:** Deaths have been reported in children under 2 years of age associated with carbinoxamine-containing products. Use is strictly contraindicated in this age group [US].
- **Somnolence and Impaired Mental Alertness:** Significant drowsiness may occur. Patients should avoid hazardous activities requiring mental alertness (e.g., driving, operating machinery) until response is determined.
- **Anticholinergic Effects:** Use with caution in patients with the following conditions:
    - Increased intraocular pressure or narrow-angle glaucoma.
    - Hyperthyroidism.
    - Cardiovascular disease or hypertension.
    - Stenosing peptic ulcer or pyloroduodenal obstruction.
    - Symptomatic prostatic hypertrophy or bladder neck obstruction [US].
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** The formulation contains sodium metabisulfite, which may cause anaphylaxis or severe asthmatic episodes in susceptible individuals [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequency data is not provided in the source; however, reactions are categorized by clinical impact and organ system.

### 6.1 Most Common Reactions
- Sedation, sleepiness, dizziness, disturbed coordination, epigastric distress, and thickening of bronchial secretions.

### 6.2 Serious and Other Identified Reactions
- **Body as a Whole:** Urticaria, rash, anaphylactic shock, photosensitivity, excessive perspiration, chills, dryness of mouth/nose/throat.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension, headache, palpitations, tachycardia, extrasystoles.
- **Central Nervous System:** Fatigue, confusion, restlessness, excitation, nervousness, tremor, irritability, insomnia, euphoria, paresthesia, blurred vision, diplopia, vertigo, tinnitus, acute labyrinthitis, hysteria, neuritis, convulsions.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Anorexia, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation.
- **Hematologic:** Hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis.
- **Respiratory:** Tightness of chest, wheezing, nasal stuffiness.
- **Urogenital:** Urinary frequency, difficult urination, urinary retention, early menses.
- **Laboratory:** Increase in uric acid levels.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Contraindicated. MAOIs prolong and intensify the anticholinergic (drying) effects of antihistamines [US].
- **Alcohol and CNS Depressants:** Avoid concomitant use. Alcohol, hypnotics, sedatives, and tranquilizers have additive effects on CNS depression and may further impair mental alertness [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Noted as Category C in older systems [US]. Decades of antihistamine use have not identified a clear risk of major birth defects; however, no carbinoxamine-specific data or animal reproduction studies are available. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Carbinoxamine is likely present in breast milk. Antihistamines may cause drowsiness or irritability in infants and carry a risk of respiratory depression in neonates.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies have not been conducted to evaluate the effects on fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness for patients ≥ 2 years are established based on bioequivalence to immediate-release products. Paradoxical excitation may occur in younger children. Contraindicated in those < 2 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of dizziness, sedation, hypotension, and confusion. Initiate therapy at the lower end of the dosing range and monitor closely [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive H1-receptor antagonist. It exhibits significant anticholinergic (atropine-like) and sedative properties.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Antihistamines compete with histamine for receptor sites on effector cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** In an extended-release form, 16 mg is bioequivalent to two 8 mg immediate-release doses given 6 hours apart.
    - **Food Effect:** None observed.
    - **Cmax:** 28.7 ng/mL (single 16 mg dose) to 72.9 ng/mL (steady-state).
    - **Tmax:** Approximately 5.6 to 6.7 hours for the ER suspension.
    - **Elimination:** Mean plasma half-life is 17.0 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Effectiveness was established based on demonstration of bioequivalence to the immediate-release reference product [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure. Shake well before use.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
