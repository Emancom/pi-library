# Editorial Product Information: Certolizumab pegol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI when prescribing in a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Certolizumab pegol
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant, humanised antibody Fab' fragment conjugated to polyethylene glycol (PEG); Tumour Necrosis Factor-alpha (TNF-$\alpha$) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Cimzia** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L04AB05
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 200 mg/mL solution for injection in a single-use pre-filled syringe or pre-filled pen (AutoClicks) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** UCB Pharma [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):** Treatment of moderate to severe active RA in adults, either in combination with methotrexate (MTX) or as monotherapy (if MTX is contraindicated or not tolerated). Indicated to reduce the rate of progression of joint damage when given with MTX. Also indicated for severe, active, and progressive RA in MTX-naïve adults [Australian].
- **Psoriatic Arthritis (PsA):** Treatment of active PsA in adults with inadequate response to prior disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD) therapy [Australian].
- **Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS):** Treatment of active AS in adults with inadequate response or intolerance to at least one nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) [Australian].
- **Non-radiographic Axial Spondyloarthritis (nr-axSpA):** Treatment of adults with active nr-axSpA showing objective signs of inflammation (elevated CRP or MRI changes) and inadequate response/intolerance to NSAIDs [Australian].
- **Plaque Psoriasis:** Treatment of moderate to severe chronic plaque psoriasis in adults who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment should be initiated and supervised by specialist physicians experienced in the relevant therapeutic areas.

### Loading Dose
- **All Indications:** 400 mg (given as two 200 mg subcutaneous injections) at Week 0, Week 2, and Week 4 [Australian].

### Maintenance Dose
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** 200 mg every 2 weeks. Alternatively, 400 mg every 4 weeks is considered safe and effective [Australian].
- **Psoriatic Arthritis:** 200 mg every 2 weeks. Alternatively, 400 mg every 4 weeks [Australian].
- **Ankylosing Spondylitis & nr-axSpA:** 200 mg every 2 weeks or 400 mg every 4 weeks. After 1 year of sustained remission, a reduced dose of 200 mg every 4 weeks may be considered [Australian].
- **Plaque Psoriasis:** 200 mg every 2 weeks. A dose of 400 mg every 2 weeks may be considered for patients with insufficient response or those weighing $\ge$ 90 kg [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer via subcutaneous injection into the thigh or abdomen.
- Patients may self-inject after proper training and physician determination of appropriateness.
- **Clinical Review:** Reconsider therapy if no evidence of benefit is seen within the first 12 weeks (RA, PsA, AS, nr-axSpA) or 16 weeks (Plaque Psoriasis) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to certolizumab pegol or any excipients.
- Active tuberculosis (TB) or other severe infections (e.g., sepsis, opportunistic infections).
- Concurrent administration with **anakinra** (IL-1 receptor antagonist).
- Moderate to severe heart failure (NYHA Class III/IV) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Serious Infections:** Increased risk of serious bacterial, mycobacterial (TB), invasive fungal, viral, and parasitic infections. Some have been fatal. Discontinue if a new serious infection develops.
- **Tuberculosis:** Monitor for reactivation or new infection. Evaluate all patients for latent and active TB prior to initiation. Start prophylaxis for latent TB before therapy begins [Australian].
- **HBV Reactivation:** Monitor chronic carriers of Hepatitis B. Discontinue if reactivation occurs.
- **Malignancies:** Lymphoma and other malignancies, including fatal cases in children/adolescents treated with TNF-blockers, have been reported. A risk for hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTCL) exists, primarily in young adult males with inflammatory bowel disease.
- **Skin Cancers:** Melanoma and Merkel cell carcinoma reported; periodic skin exams recommended.
- **Neurological Events:** Rare cases of demyelinating disease (Multiple Sclerosis, Guillain-Barré syndrome). Exercise caution in patients with pre-existing CNS disorders.
- **Haematologic Events:** Infrequent reports of pancytopenia, leukopenia, and thrombocytopenia. Advise patients to seek immediate medical attention for signs of blood dyscrasias.
- **Congestive Heart Failure:** Use with caution in mild heart failure (NYHA I/II); contraindicated in mod-severe cases.
- **Latex Sensitivity:** The pre-filled syringe/pen needle shield contains 7% natural rubber latex derivative [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** Certolizumab pegol may cause erroneously elevated activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) assay results in certain tests (e.g., PTT-Lupus Anticoagulant, STA-PTT). No evidence of *in vivo* coagulation effects [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Baseline and periodic TB screening.
- Periodic skin examinations for cancer.
- Clinical and laboratory monitoring for HBV carriers.
- Signs of infection during and for up to 5 months after treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequencies in Clinical Trials
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Infections (specifically in RA population) [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Upper respiratory tract infections, nasopharyngitis, urinary tract infection, herpes simplex, sinusitis, back pain, abdominal pain, injection site reactions, pyrexia, rash, hypertension, ALT/AST increased, eosinophilia, headache [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Sepsis, TB, fungal infections, solid organ tumours, anaemia, lymphadenopathy, electrolyte imbalance, anxiety, mood disorders, visual disorders, tinnitus, vertigo, palpitations, asthma, nausea, stomatitis, alopecia, psoriasis (new onset or worsening) [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Gastrointestinal tumours, melanoma, pancytopenia, sarcoidosis, thyroid disorders, suicide attempt, seizure, pericarditis, cerebrovascular accident, interstitial lung disease, cholelithiasis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome [Australian]. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Sepsis, opportunistic infections, TB reactivation, lymphoma, and worsening heart failure [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anakinra / Abatacept:** Concurrent use is not recommended due to an increased risk of serious infections without added clinical benefit.
- **Methotrexate (MTX):** No significant effect on the pharmacokinetics of MTX; no significant effect of MTX on certolizumab pegol pharmacokinetics [Australian].
- **Other DMARDs:** No significant interactions noted with NSAIDs, corticosteroids, or analgesics [Australian].
- **Live Vaccines:** Should not be administered concurrently with certolizumab pegol [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Data from >1,000 prospective pregnancies suggest no increased risk of malformations. Due to its structure (lack of Fc region), there is minimal to no placental transfer of certolizumab pegol from mother to infant.
- **Breastfeeding:** Considered compatible. A multicentre study of 17 lactating women showed no to minimal transfer into breast milk (<1% of maternal dose reaching the infant) [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies and a small study in 20 healthy males showed no apparent effect on fertility or semen quality [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required. Population PK showed no effect of age. However, a higher incidence of infections was observed in subjects $\ge$ 65 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No effect of mild impairment on creatinine clearance was found. Insufficient data for dosing recommendations in moderate to severe impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Specific studies have not been performed; the effect of hepatic impairment on pharmacokinetics is unknown [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A recombinant, humanised Fab' fragment neutralises soluble and membrane-bound human TNF-$\alpha$ with high affinity. It lacks a fragment crystallisable (Fc) region, meaning it does not fix complement or induce antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) *in vitro* [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability ~80%. Peak plasma concentrations attained between 54–171 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution (V/F) ~8.01 L (RA) and 4.71 L (Psoriasis).
    - **Metabolism:** PEGylation delays elimination by decreasing renal clearance and proteolysis. Fab' is conjugated to a 40 kDa PEG.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life ~14 days for all doses. Clearance increases in the presence of anti-certolizumab antibodies [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RA (RA-I to RA-IV):** Demonstrated significant ACR20/50/70 responses compared to placebo. RA-I showed inhibition of structural damage progression over 52 weeks [Australian].
- **PsA (PsA001):** Demonstrated superior ACR20 response and improvement in physical function and skin manifestations (PASI) at Week 12 and 24 compared to placebo [Australian].
- **AS (AS001):** Showed significant improvement in ASAS20/40 responses and reduction in spinal inflammation via MRI [Australian].
- **nr-axSpA (AS0006):** Achieved significant ASAS40 response at Week 12 compared to placebo, maintained through Week 52 [Australian].
- **Plaque Psoriasis (CIMPASI-1/2, IMPACT):** Significant improvements in PGA "clear/almost clear" and PASI 75/90 responses compared to placebo and Enbrel (IMPACT) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Light Protection:** Keep in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Room Temperature Allowance:** If necessary, may be stored at room temperature (up to 25°C) for a single period of up to 10 days. Once stored at room temperature, it must not be returned to the refrigerator and must be discarded if not used within 10 days [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4** (Prescription Animal Remedy) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription only medicine.
