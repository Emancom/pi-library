# Editorial Product Information: Clozapine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: "Australian Product Information – AKM CLOZAPINE"
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines mandated in a specific jurisdiction. Prescribers must consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI and mandatory monitoring registry requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: **Clozapine** (Dibenzo[b,e][1,4]diazepine derivative).
- **Chemical Class**: Atypical antipsychotic agent.
- **Brand Names**: AKM CLOZAPINE [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: N05AH02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Uncoated tablets: 25 mg, 50 mg, 100 mg, and 200 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Treatment-Resistant Schizophrenia**: Indicated only for patients with schizophrenia who are non-responsive to, or intolerant of, other antipsychotic drugs [Australian].
    - *Non-responsiveness*: Lack of clinical improvement despite adequate doses of at least two different classes of antipsychotics for reasonable durations.
    - *Intolerance*: Inability to achieve benefit due to severe, untreatable neurological adverse effects (extrapyramidal side effects or tardive dyskinesia).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Individualisation**: Dosage must be adjusted to the lowest effective dose.
- **Initiation**: 12.5 mg once or twice on the first day, followed by 25 mg once or twice on the second day [Australian].
- **Titration**: If well tolerated, daily dose may be increased in 25–50 mg increments to reach 300 mg/day within 2–3 weeks. Subsequent increases of 50–100 mg may be made at half-weekly or weekly intervals [Australian].
- **Therapeutic Range**: Usually 200–450 mg/day (divided doses; larger portion at bedtime).
- **Maximum Dose**: 600 mg/day standard; up to 900 mg/day for selected patients [Australian].
- **Maintenance**: 150–300 mg/day. Doses ≤200 mg may be taken as a single evening dose.

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food.

### Restarting Therapy
- If the interval since the last dose exceeds two days, treatment must be restarted at 12.5 mg once or twice on the first day [Australian].

### Discontinuation (Ending Therapy)
- **Planned**: Gradual reduction over 1–2 weeks to avoid cholinergic rebound (sweating, headache, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea).
- **Abrupt**: Immediate monitoring for psychotic recurrence and cholinergic rebound is required.

## 4. Contraindications
- History of drug-induced granulocytopenia or agranulocytosis [Australian].
- Impaired bone marrow function or bone marrow disorders [Australian].
- Inability to undergo regular blood tests [Australian].
- Uncontrolled epilepsy [Australian].
- Circulatory collapse or CNS depression of any cause [Australian].
- Drug intoxication, comatose conditions, or alcoholic/toxic psychoses [Australian].
- Severe cardiac disease (e.g., myocarditis) [Australian].
- Severe renal disease [Australian].
- Severe hepatic disease (active disease with nausea/jaundice, progressive disease, or hepatic failure) [Australian].
- Paralytic ileus [Australian].
- Hypersensitivity to **clozapine** or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [Australian]
- **Gastrointestinal Hypomotility**: Severe reactions (paralytic ileus, faecal impaction, intestinal obstruction) can lead to hospitalisation, surgery, and death.
- **Myocarditis/Cardiomyopathy/Pericarditis**: Potentially fatal cases reported, primarily in the first month. Persistent tachycardia at rest, chest pain, or heart failure symptoms require immediate referral to a cardiologist.

### Hematologic Monitoring (Agranulocytosis)
- **Mandatory Monitoring**: Treatment is limited to patients with a normal baseline leucocyte count (WBC ≥3.5x10⁹/L and normal ANC) [Australian].
- **Schedule**: WBC and ANC must be monitored weekly for the first 18 weeks, then at least monthly thereafter, and for 4 weeks after discontinuation [Australian].
- **Interruption**: If ANC falls below 1.5x10⁹/L, **clozapine** must be discontinued immediately.

### General Precautions
- **Seizures**: Dose-dependent risk; caution in patients with a history of epilepsy.
- **Orthostatic Hypotension**: Risk of syncope and cardiac arrest, particularly during rapid titration.
- **Metabolic Changes**: Significant weight gain, hyperlipidaemia, and hyperglycaemia (including ketoacidosis/coma) may occur.
- **VTE**: Risk of venous thromboembolism; avoid immobilisation.
- **Dementia-related Psychosis**: Increased risk of mortality in elderly patients; **clozapine** is not recommended in this population [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Sedation/drowsiness (40%), dizziness, tachycardia, constipation, hypersalivation. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Weight gain, leucopenia/neutropenia, eosinophilia, leucocytosis, blurred vision, headache, tremor, rigidity, akathisia, ECG changes, hypertension, nausea/vomiting, elevated liver enzymes, urinary incontinence/retention. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Agranulocytosis, Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS), falls. |
| **Rare** (0.01–0.1%) | Myocarditis, pericarditis, circulatory collapse, arrhythmias, dysphagia, hepatitis, cholestatic jaundice, diabetes mellitus/impaired glucose tolerance, respiratory depression, thromboembolism. |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Cardiomyopathy, cardiac arrest, tardive dyskinesia, fulminant hepatic necrosis, intestinal obstruction/faecal impaction, tubulointerstitial nephritis, priapism, sudden unexplained death. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions**:
    - **Bone Marrow Depressants**: Avoid concurrent use.
    - **Benzodiazepines**: Increased risk of circulatory collapse [Australian].
    - **Anticholinergics**: Potentiates risk of GI hypomotility and toxicity.
    - **CNS Depressants/Alcohol**: Enhanced sedative effects.
    - **Epinephrine**: **Clozapine** may reverse the vasopressor effect (beta-agonist activity predominates).

- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP450)**:
    - **CYP1A2 Inhibitors** (e.g., Fluvoxamine, Caffeine, Ciprofloxacin): Increase **clozapine** levels.
    - **CYP1A2 Inducers** (e.g., Tobacco smoke, Omeprazole): Decrease **clozapine** levels. Sudden smoking cessation can cause a toxic rise in levels.
    - **CYP3A4 Inhibitors** (e.g., Erythromycin, Azole antifungals): May increase levels.
    - **CYP3A4 Inducers** (e.g., Carbamazepine, Phenytoin, Rifampicin, St John's Wort): Decrease **clozapine** levels.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category C** [Australian]. Neonates exposed in the third trimester are at risk of extrapyramidal symptoms or withdrawal. Use only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding**: **Clozapine** is excreted in human milk. Reports of sedation and agranulocytosis in infants exist. Mothers should not breastfeed [Australian].
- **Fertility**: Animal data show inconsistent effects (testicular atrophy in rats but no effect on fertility at lower doses) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric**: Safety and efficacy in children/adolescents <16 years not established [Australian].
- **Elderly**: Start with 12.5 mg; restrict increments to 25 mg/day. High risk of orthostatic hypotension, tachycardia, and anticholinergic effects [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: Caution required; dose reduction may be necessary in significant impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Regular liver function monitoring required. Discontinue if jaundice or significant enzyme elevation occurs.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Atypical antipsychotic with weak dopamine D1 and D2 receptor blockade but potent noradrenolytic, anticholinergic, antihistaminic, and antiserotonergic properties [Australian]. It produces minimal prolactin elevation.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - *Absorption*: 90–95% absorbed; food does not affect the rate/extent.
    - *Bioavailability*: 50–60% due to first-pass metabolism.
    - *Distribution*: 95% protein bound.
    - *Metabolism*: Almost completely metabolised; desmethyl metabolite has limited activity.
    - *Elimination*: Biphasic; mean terminal half-life is ~14 hours (range 8–29 hours). 50% excreted in urine, 30% in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy**: Clinical data indicate that approximately one-third of treatment-resistant patients show clinically relevant improvement [Australian]. Efficacy is noted for both positive and negative symptoms of schizophrenia.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Conditions**: Keep in a cool, dry place and protect from light.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: **S4** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Requirements**: Use is typically restricted to registries (e.g., Clozapine Patient Monitoring System) to ensure mandatory blood testing compliance.
