# Editorial Product Information: Clomifene

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) documentation from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Clomid (clomifene citrate) 50 mg tablets.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is intended as a clinical reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.
- **Note on Sources**: At the time of this editorial, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis. Clinical recommendations and regulatory classifications are attributed accordingly.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Clomifene citrate (a triarylethylene compound).
- **Brand Names**: Clomid [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: G03BG02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: 
    - Tablet: 50 mg clomifene citrate [Australian].
    - Appearance: Beige, scored on one side, engraved with "M" inside two concentric circles on the other [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Vitalion Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Ovulatory Failure**: Treatment of ovulatory dysfunction in carefully selected infertile women who desire pregnancy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **First Course**: 50 mg (one tablet) daily for five days.
- **Second Course (if required)**: If ovulation is not achieved after the first course, a dose of 100 mg daily (as a single dose) for five days may be administered.
- **Maximum Dosage**: Doses should not exceed 100 mg daily, nor should the duration of the course exceed five days [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing**: In patients with spontaneous or progestin-induced bleeding, therapy should be initiated on or around the fifth day of the cycle. In patients with no recent uterine bleeding, therapy may start at any time [Australian].
- **Duration of Therapy**: Long-term cyclic therapy is not recommended. Treatment is generally limited to a total of approximately 6 cycles. If ovulation does not occur after three courses of 100 mg, further treatment is not recommended, and the patient should be re-evaluated [Australian].
- **Coitus Timing**: Patients should be advised to time coitus to coincide with expected ovulation, which typically occurs 6–12 days after a course of clomifene [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Liver Disease**: Known liver disease or a history of hepatic dysfunction [Australian].
- **Hormone-Dependent Tumours**: Presence of such neoplasms [Australian].
- **Abnormal Uterine Bleeding**: Bleeding of undetermined origin [Australian].
- **Pregnancy**: Clomifene must not be administered during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Ovarian Cysts**: Presence of an ovarian cyst (excluding polycystic ovary syndrome), as enlargement may occur [Australian].
- **Visual Disorders**: History of significant vision disorders related to prior or current clomifene use [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Ovarian Hyperstimulation Syndrome (OHSS)**: OHSS has been reported, occasionally in severe forms (e.g., pericardial effusion, renal failure, thromboembolism). Patients should report pelvic pain, weight gain, or abdominal distension immediately. If OHSS or significant ovarian enlargement occurs, clomifene should be withheld until the ovaries return to pre-treatment size [Australian].
- **Visual Symptoms**: Blurring, spots, or flashes (scintillating scotomata) may occur. These are usually reversible but may be permanent, particularly with high doses or prolonged use. Treatment must be discontinued permanently if visual symptoms occur, and an ophthalmological evaluation performed [Australian].
- **Ovarian Cancer Risk**: Some data suggest an increased risk of ovarian cancer with clomifene, especially in nulligravid women or with prolonged use [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Pelvic Examination**: Mandatory before each treatment course to exclude ovarian enlargement or cyst formation [Australian].
- **Pregnancy Testing**: Must be performed before each course to avoid inadvertent administration during early pregnancy [Australian].
- **Lipid Profile**: Monitoring of plasma triglycerides may be necessary in patients with a history of hyperlipidaemia or those receiving higher/prolonged doses [Australian].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Uterine Fibroids**: Use with caution due to the potential for fibroid enlargement [Australian].
- **Driving/Machinery**: Visual symptoms, dizziness, or fatigue may impair the ability to drive or operate machinery, especially in variable lighting [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally dose-related and more frequent at higher dosages.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Ovarian enlargement (13.6%), Vasomotor flushes (10.4%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal-pelvic discomfort (5.5%), Nausea/vomiting (2.2%), Breast discomfort (2.1%), Visual symptoms (1.5%), Headache (1.3%), Intermenstrual spotting/menorrhagia (1.3%) |
| **Uncommon/Rare** | Seizures, stroke, syncope, tachycardia, pancreatitis, acute abdomen |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Ovarian malignancies, anaphylaxis, angioedema, hypertriglyceridemia, endometriosis exacerbation, alopecia, mood disturbances (anxiety, depression) |

**Serious Adverse Effects**: Severe OHSS, irreversible visual loss, and ectopic pregnancy are high-risk complications [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions**: Interactions with other medicinal products have not been documented in available sources [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Classification**: [Australian: Category B3].
- **Risk**: Clomifene is contraindicated in pregnancy. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed foetal damage at high doses.
- **Multiple Pregnancy**: There is a significantly increased risk of multiple births (approx. 8%). In a large series, twins occurred in 6.9%, triplets in 0.5%, and quadruplets/quintuplets in <0.5%.
- **Pregnancy Wastage**: Fetal loss/abortion rates are reported at approximately 21.4% [Australian].

### Breastfeeding
- **Safety**: It is unknown if clomifene is excreted in human milk. It may reduce lactation [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: No data available; not indicated for this population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: Contraindicated in patients with liver dysfunction. No specific renal dosing provided in available sources [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Clomifene stimulates the release of pituitary gonadotrophins (FSH and LH), which in turn induces the maturation of the ovarian follicle and development of the corpus luteum [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Approximately 70% of appropriately selected patients will ovulate, and approximately 30% will achieve pregnancy [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Readily absorbed after oral administration.
    - **Metabolism/Excretion**: Excreted primarily via faeces (approx. 42%) and urine (approx. 8%). Traces may remain in the body for up to 53 days, potentially exposing an early embryo if conception occurs in the treatment cycle [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary**: Specific pivotal trial names were not provided in the source PI. Data regarding success rates (70% ovulation/30% pregnancy) are derived from clinical investigations involving a broad range of anovulatory diagnoses [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Packaging**: Available in PVC/Al blister packs [Australian].
