# Editorial Product Information: colistimethate sodium

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **colistimethate sodium**
- Chemical class: Polypeptide antibiotic
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous (IV) and intramuscular (IM)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval: 20 September 1991 [Australian]
- Date of last revision/update of source PI: 05 July 2024 [Australian]
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of acute or chronic infections due to susceptible gram-negative bacilli, particularly when caused by susceptible strains of:
  - Pseudomonas aeruginosa
  - Enterobacter aerogenes
  - Escherichia coli
  - Klebsiella pneumoniae
- May be used empirically to initiate therapy in serious infections suspected to be due to gram‑negative organisms, pending culture and sensitivity results.
- Not indicated for infections due to Proteus or Neisseria.
- Jurisdictional differences in indications: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adults and children)
- Usual total daily dose: 2.5–5 mg colistin (as colistimethate sodium) per kg per day, divided into 2 to 4 doses for patients with normal renal function.
- **Maximum daily dose should not exceed 5 mg/kg/day with normal renal function.** (critical warning)

### Renal impairment — dose modification (adults)
The following dosing modifications are provided as suggested schedules according to degree of renal impairment. Units and values reflect the original PI presentation.

- Renal function: Normal
  - Plasma creatinine: ~0.7–1.2 mg/100 mL
  - Urea clearance: 80–100% of normal
  - Unit dose: 100–150 mg
  - Frequency: 4 to 2 times/day
  - Total daily dose: ~300 mg
  - Approx. daily dose: ~5.0 mg/kg/day

- Renal function: Mild impairment
  - Plasma creatinine: ~1.3–1.5 mg/100 mL
  - Urea clearance: 40–70% of normal
  - Unit dose: 75–115 mg
  - Frequency: 2 times/day
  - Total daily dose: 150–230 mg
  - Approx. daily dose: 2.5–3.8 mg/kg/day

- Renal function: Moderate impairment
  - Plasma creatinine: ~1.6–2.5 mg/100 mL
  - Urea clearance: 25–40% of normal
  - Unit dose: 66–150 mg
  - Frequency: 2 or 1 times/day
  - Total daily dose: 133–150 mg
  - Approx. daily dose: ~2.5 mg/kg/day

- Renal function: Considerable impairment
  - Plasma creatinine: ~2.6–4.0 mg/100 mL
  - Urea clearance: 10–25% of normal
  - Unit dose: 100–150 mg
  - Frequency: every 36 hours
  - Total daily dose: ~100 mg
  - Approx. daily dose: ~1.5 mg/kg/day

Note: The suggested unit dose range is 2.5–5 mg/kg; in renal impairment the dosing interval should be increased and total daily dose reduced in proportion to renal function. Monitor renal function and adjust dosing accordingly.

### Administration instructions
- Reconstitution: Reconstitute vial with 2.0 mL water for injections to provide the labelled colistin content (example: 150 mg in 2 mL). Swirl gently to avoid frothing. Use as soon as practicable after reconstitution; single‑patient use only.
- Intravenous administration:
  - Intermittent (direct) administration: Slowly inject one-half of the total daily dose over 3–5 minutes every 12 hours.
  - Continuous infusion technique described: Inject one-half of the total daily dose slowly over 3–5 minutes, then add the remaining half to an appropriate infusion solution and administer as a continuous infusion.
  - Recommended infusion solutions (limited data available): 0.9% sodium chloride; 5% glucose in 0.9% NaCl; 5% glucose in water; 5% glucose in 0.45% NaCl; 5% glucose in 0.225% NaCl; lactated Ringer’s solution; 10% invert sugar solution. There are insufficient data to recommend use with other infusion solutions.
  - Suggested infusion rate with normal renal function: approximately 5–6 mg/hr for the infusion component; reduce the infusion rate in the presence of renal impairment according to degree of impairment.
  - Any infusion solution containing colistimethate sodium should be freshly prepared and used for no longer than 24 hours.
- Intramuscular administration: Dosing same as IV (2.5–5 mg/kg/day in divided doses); respiratory arrest has been reported after IM administration — use with caution.
- Additional administration notes:
  - Observe patients carefully for neuromuscular weakness and respiratory compromise; adjust dose or discontinue if toxicity develops.
  - If therapy is discontinued because of toxicity and a life‑threatening infection persists, therapy may be reinstituted at a lower dose after drug levels have declined.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to colistimethate sodium or any component of the formulation.
- **Myasthenia gravis** — colistimethate sodium is contraindicated because it is known to reduce presynaptic acetylcholine release and may exacerbate neuromuscular transmission impairment. [Australian]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed/Prominent warnings
- **Maximum daily dose should not exceed 5 mg/kg/day in patients with normal renal function.** (critical)
- **Risk of nephrotoxicity and neurotoxicity, including neuromuscular blockade, muscle weakness and apnoea.** Dose reduction and careful monitoring required in renal impairment.

### General warnings and precautions
- Renal function:
  - Colistimethate sodium is primarily eliminated by the kidneys. Use with caution in patients with impaired renal function.
  - Administration in excess of renal excretory capacity can produce high serum levels, further renal impairment and a cycle leading to acute renal failure and increased systemic toxicity.
  - Monitor urine output, blood urea nitrogen (BUN) and serum creatinine. Decreased urine output or rising BUN/creatinine are early indicators of nephrotoxicity; discontinue drug if these occur.
- Neuromuscular effects:
  - Colistimethate sodium reduces acetylcholine release at the presynaptic neuromuscular junction and can potentiate neuromuscular blockade. This may present as muscle weakness or respiratory depression/apnoea.
  - Use caution with concomitant agents that impair neuromuscular transmission (see Section 8).
  - If apnoea occurs: provide assisted respiration, oxygen and consider calcium chloride injections as needed.
- Drug interactions:
  - Concomitant use with other drugs that can potentiate neuromuscular blockade (aminoglycosides, polymyxins, curariform muscle relaxants etc.) increases risk of respiratory depression and should be avoided or used only with extreme caution.
- Use in specific conditions:
  - Do not use in patients with myasthenia gravis (contraindicated).
  - Consider age-related decline in renal function when dosing elderly patients.
- Reconstitution and administration:
  - Use reconstituted solution promptly and for single‑patient use only.
  - Prepare infusion solutions freshly; do not store prepared infusion for more than 24 hours.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic monitoring of renal function (urine output, BUN, serum creatinine).
- Monitor respiratory status and neuromuscular strength, particularly when used with other potentially neurotoxic or neuromuscular-blocking drugs.
- Observe for signs of neurotoxicity (circumoral paresthesia, paresthesia of extremities or tongue, dizziness, slurred speech) and dermatologic reactions (pruritus, urticaria).

### Specific clinical precautions
- Patients should be warned not to drive or operate hazardous machinery if experiencing transient neurological disturbances (e.g., dizziness, slurred speech).
- Use with extreme caution in the presence of curariform muscle relaxants and other neuromuscular-blocking agents.
- In life‑threatening situations where therapy was stopped for toxicity, re‑initiation may be considered at a reduced dose after drug levels decline.

- Serious adverse reactions highlighted:
  - Respiratory arrest and apnoea (may be associated with neuromuscular blockade and renal impairment).
  - Dose-related nephrotoxicity potentially progressing to acute renal failure.
- Reversibility:
  - Nephrotoxicity manifestations have been reported to be reversible following discontinuation of therapy in the clinical reports included in the source.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacodynamic interactions (clinically important)
- Agents that potentiate neuromuscular blockade — avoid concomitant use where possible or use only with extreme caution and close monitoring:
  - Aminoglycosides and related antibiotics reported to interfere with neuromuscular transmission: kanamycin, streptomycin, dihydrostreptomycin, neomycin, polymyxin. (These should not be given concomitantly except with greatest caution.)
  - Curariform muscle relaxants / neuromuscular blockers: ether, tubocurarine, succinylcholine, gallamine, decamethonium, sodium citrate — potentiate neuromuscular blocking effect; extreme caution advised.
- Macrolides and fluoroquinolones:
  - Co‑treatment with macrolides (e.g., azithromycin, clarithromycin) or fluoroquinolones (e.g., norfloxacin, ciprofloxacin) should not be used in patients with myasthenia gravis due to risk of exacerbating weakness in that population. [Australian]

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- No specific pharmacokinetic interactions are described in the available source beyond the interaction potential described above and the primary renal elimination pathway.

### Management notes
- Avoid combinations known to potentiate neuromuscular blockade; if combination is necessary, monitor respiratory function and neuromuscular strength closely and be prepared to provide respiratory support.
- Monitor renal function when used with other nephrotoxic drugs.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category: Category B2 [Australian].
- Safety during human pregnancy is not established. Colistimethate sodium has been used in pregnancy (third trimester) for bacteriuria and urinary infections.
- Animal data: Evidence of possible embryotoxic and teratogenic effects in pregnant rabbits; exercise caution when using in women of child‑bearing potential.
- Placental transfer: Colistimethate sodium crosses the placenta; fetal blood levels of approximately 1 mcg/mL have been reported following intravenous maternal administration.
- Clinical recommendation: Use in pregnancy only if benefit justifies potential risk; consider alternative agents where appropriate.

### Breastfeeding
- No data available in the source on excretion into human breast milk or effects on the breastfed infant. Safety during lactation: Not specified in available sources.

### Fertility
- Effects on fertility: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Dosing guidance in the source applies to adults and children: 2.5–5 mg/kg/day in 2–4 divided doses for patients with normal renal function.
- Some pharmacokinetic statements in the source indicate comparable serum half-life in adults and children (see Section 11).
- Other paediatric-specific safety or efficacy data: Not specified in available sources.

### Elderly use
- The source states "No data available" for specific elderly use; however, it emphasises that age‑related decline in renal function should be taken into account and dosing adjusted accordingly.

### Renal impairment
- Colistimethate sodium is renally cleared and dosing interval and/or total dose should be reduced in proportion to the degree of renal impairment. Refer to the dosing modification table in Section 4.
- Monitor urine output, BUN and serum creatinine; discontinue if renal function deteriorates.

### Hepatic impairment
- Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- **Colistimethate sodium** is a polypeptide antibiotic with bactericidal activity against certain gram‑negative bacilli. It acts by disrupting the bacterial cell membrane (mechanistic details beyond this are not specified in the available source).

### Microbiology / Spectrum
- Active against susceptible strains of:
  - Pseudomonas aeruginosa
  - Enterobacter aerogenes
  - Escherichia coli
  - Klebsiella pneumoniae

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption / distribution:
  - After a single dose equivalent to colistin 150 mg (IM or IV), higher serum levels are reached at about 10 minutes following IV administration; serum concentrations decline with an observed half‑life of approximately 2–3 hours after IV or IM administration in adults and children (including premature infants).
  - Colistimethate sodium is transferred across the placenta; fetal blood levels of approximately 1 mcg/mL were observed after maternal IV administration.
- Elimination:
  - The drug is eliminated mainly by renal excretion.
  - Urine concentrations after IV administration: average urine levels approximately 270 mcg/mL at 2 hours and ~15 mcg/mL at 8 hours; after IM administration urine levels ranged ~200 to ~25 mcg/mL over similar time points.
- Metabolism: Not specified in available sources.
- Other pharmacokinetic parameters (e.g., volume of distribution, clearance rates by eGFR): Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Bactericidal activity against listed gram‑negative pathogens; clinically used particularly for urinary tract infections due to susceptible Pseudomonas aeruginosa and for infections caused by gram‑negative bacilli resistant to broad‑spectrum agents.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- The available PI states that colistimethate sodium has been clinically effective in acute and chronic urinary tract infections caused by susceptible Pseudomonas aeruginosa and in infections due to other susceptible gram‑negative bacilli. Detailed pivotal trial design, randomisation, comparator arms, primary outcomes, and statistical results are not provided in the available source.
- No additional clinical trial summaries or primary outcome data are specified in available sources.
