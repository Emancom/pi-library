# Editorial Product Information: Ceritinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ceritinib product information, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ceritinib (free base)
- **Chemical Class:** Anaplastic Lymphoma Kinase (ALK) inhibitor; antineoplastic agent
- **Brand Name(s):** **Zykadia** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01XE28
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Hard capsules, 150 mg. The capsules are opaque white and opaque blue, marked with "LDK 150MG" (cap) and "NVR" (body) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **ALK-positive Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** Treatment of patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC that is anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK)-positive [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 450 mg (three 150 mg capsules) taken orally once daily. 
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Must be taken **with food** (ranging from a snack to a full meal) at the same time each day.
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole with water; do not chew or crush.
    - If vomiting occurs, do not take an additional dose; continue with the next scheduled dose.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, skip the dose if the next dose is due in less than 12 hours. Otherwise, take the missed dose and resume the normal schedule.
- **Dose Adjustments for Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs):** 
    - Adjustments should be made in decrements of 150 mg. 
    - If a patient is unable to tolerate a minimum dose of 150 mg daily with food, the drug should be permanently discontinued.
    - **Dose Reduction Schedule:**
        - First reduction: 300 mg daily.
        - Second reduction: 150 mg daily.
- **Management of Specific Toxicities:**
    - **Hepatotoxicity:** Withhold for ALT/AST >5x ULN until recovery to ≤3x ULN, then reinitiate at a reduced dose. Permanently discontinue for ALT/AST >3x ULN with total bilirubin >2x ULN.
    - **ILD/Pneumonitis:** Permanently discontinue for any grade of treatment-related interstitial lung disease.
    - **QT Prolongation:** Withhold for QTc >500 msec until <481 msec, then reinitiate at a reduced dose. Discontinue for QTc >500 msec or >60 msec change from baseline with serious arrhythmia.
    - **Hyperglycaemia:** Withhold until controlled, then reduce dose. If control cannot be achieved with medical management, discontinue.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to ceritinib or any excipients (including sulfites) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: QT Interval Prolongation** [Australian]: Ceritinib can cause concentration-dependent prolongation of the QT interval. This may lead to an increased risk of ventricular tachyarrhythmias (e.g., Torsade de pointes) or sudden death.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Drug-induced liver injury occurred in clinical trials. Monitor ALT, AST, and total bilirubin monthly and as clinically indicated.
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** Severe, life-threatening, or fatal ILD has occurred. Monitor for pulmonary symptoms and exclude other causes.
- **Bradycardia:** Ceritinib can cause symptomatic or life-threatening bradycardia. Avoid use with other agents known to cause bradycardia (e.g., beta-blockers, digoxin). Monitor heart rate and blood pressure regularly.
- **Gastrointestinal Toxicity:** Diarrhoea, nausea, and vomiting are very common (reported in ~77% of patients at the 450 mg dose with food). Manage with anti-diarrhoeals, anti-emetics, or fluid replacement.
- **Hyperglycaemia:** Monitor fasting serum glucose prior to initiation and periodically thereafter.
- **Pancreatic Toxicity:** Pancreatitis (including fatalities) has been reported. Monitor lipase and amylase prior to treatment and periodically.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are based on a pool of patients treated with 750 mg fasted; however, GI toxicity is reduced with the 450 mg fed regimen.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, fatigue, abdominal pain, decreased appetite, weight loss, constipation, rash, anaemia, blood creatinine increase, oesophageal disorder, liver laboratory abnormalities. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vision disorders (blurred vision, photopsia), bradycardia, pericarditis, pneumonitis, hepatotoxicity, abnormal liver function tests, renal failure/impairment, QT prolongation, lipase/amylase increase. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pancreatitis. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Pneumonia and respiratory failure (leading to discontinuation in >0.5% of cases) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, voriconazole). Avoid concurrent use. If unavoidable, reduce the ceritinib dose by approximately one-third. After stopping the inhibitor, resume the pre-inhibitor ceritinib dose.
- **Strong CYP3A Inducers:** (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine, St. John’s Wort). Avoid concurrent use as they significantly decrease ceritinib plasma concentrations.
- **Gastric Acid-Reducing Agents:** PPIs, H2-antagonists, and antacids may reduce ceritinib bioavailability due to pH-dependent solubility. Use caution; the effect is most pronounced with PPIs.
- **CYP3A and CYP2C9 Substrates:** Ceritinib inhibits CYP3A and CYP2C9. Avoid co-administration with substrates that have a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., midazolam, cyclosporin, warfarin). If unavoidable, consider dose reduction of the substrate.
- **Transporters:** Ceritinib is a substrate of P-glycoprotein (P-gp). P-gp inhibitors may increase ceritinib concentrations.
- **Food/Grapefruit:** Avoid grapefruit and grapefruit juice, which may inhibit CYP3A in the gut wall.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. Based on its mechanism of action and animal data, ceritinib may cause fetal harm. Skeletal anomalies were observed in animal studies at doses significantly lower than human exposure. Women of childbearing potential should use highly effective contraception during treatment and for 3 months after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if ceritinib is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, women should be advised not to breastfeed during treatment.
- **Fertility:** The potential for infertility in humans is unknown; animal studies have not specifically assessed fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use (≥65 years):** No specific dose adjustment is required based on limited safety and efficacy data. No data available for patients over 85 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild or moderate: No dose adjustment required. 
    - Severe: Use with caution as there is no clinical experience in this population.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild (Child-Pugh A) or Moderate (Child-Pugh B): No dose adjustment required.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Reduce the dose by approximately one-third.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ceritinib is a highly selective, potent inhibitor of ALK. It inhibits autophosphorylation of ALK and its downstream signalling proteins, thereby inhibiting the proliferation of ALK-dependent cancer cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak levels (Cmax) are reached in 4–6 hours (fasted). Absolute bioavailability is estimated at ≤58%.
    - **Food Effect:** Bioavailability increases with food. The 450 mg fed dose provides similar systemic exposure to the 750 mg fasted dose.
    - **Distribution:** 97% protein bound. Crosses the blood-brain barrier in animal models.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via faeces (68% as unchanged drug). Terminal half-life is 31–41 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ASCEND-4:** A Phase III trial in previously untreated ALK-positive NSCLC patients showed a median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) of 16.6 months for ceritinib vs 8.1 months for chemotherapy (HR 0.55).
- **ASCEND-5:** A Phase III trial in patients previously treated with crizotinib showed a median PFS of 5.4 months for ceritinib vs 1.6 months for chemotherapy (HR 0.49).
- **ASCEND-8:** A dose-optimisation study demonstrated that 450 mg taken with food provided comparable efficacy and systemic exposure to 750 mg fasted, with significantly improved gastrointestinal tolerability [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- Keep in the original container to protect from moisture [Australian].
