# Editorial Product Information: Cerliponase alfa

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **cerliponase alfa** for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cerliponase alfa (recombinant human tripeptidyl peptidase-1 [rhTPP1]).
- **Chemical Class:** Hydrolytic lysosomal N-terminal exopeptidase.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Brineura** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AB17.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Solution for injection: 150 mg of cerliponase alfa in 5 mL (30 mg/mL).
    - Supplied as a pack containing two vials of cerliponase alfa and one vial of flushing solution (5 mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intracerebroventricular (ICV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** BioMarin Pharmaceutical Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Neuronal Ceroid Lipofuscinosis Type 2 (CLN2):** Indicated for the treatment of CLN2 disease, also known as tripeptidyl peptidase 1 (TPP1) deficiency [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Standard Dose:** 300 mg administered once every other week.
- **Pre-treatment:** Administration of antihistamines (with or without antipyretics) is recommended 30 to 60 minutes prior to starting the infusion.

### Paediatric Dosing
Posology for patients under 2 years of age is based on estimated brain mass:
| Age Group | Dose (Every Other Week) | Volume of Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Birth to < 6 months | 100 mg | 3.3 mL |
| 6 months to < 1 year | 150 mg | 5 mL |
| 1 year to < 2 years | 200 mg (first 4 doses); 300 mg (subsequent) | 6.7 mL (first 4 doses); 10 mL (subsequent) |
| 2 years and older | 300 mg | 10 mL |

### Administration Instructions
- **Setting:** Must be administered by trained healthcare professionals in a clinical setting.
- **Route:** ICV use only via a surgically implanted reservoir and catheter. The first dose should occur at least 5 to 7 days after device implantation.
- **Infusion Rate:** 2.5 mL/hour for both the drug and the subsequent flushing solution. Total infusion time is approximately 2 to 4.5 hours.
- **Preparation:** Thaw vials at room temperature for approximately 60 minutes. Do not shake or re-freeze. A 0.2 μm inline filter is mandatory.
- **Device Patency:** Verify patency by withdrawing 0.5 mL to 1 mL of CSF before infusion. Do not return this CSF to the patient.

### Dose Adjustments
- **Intolerance:** The dose may be reduced by 50% and/or the rate slowed.
- **Hypersensitivity:** If interrupted, restart at half the initial rate once symptoms resolve.
- **Increased Intracranial Pressure (ICP):** Slow or interrupt infusion if signs of increased ICP (headache, nausea, vomiting, decreased mental state) occur, particularly in children under 3 years.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Anaphylaxis:** Life-threatening hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients if re-challenge is unsuccessful.
- **Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) Shunts:** Prohibited in CLN2 patients.
- **Device Failure:** Do not administer if there are signs of acute ICV access device leakage, failure, or infection.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Device-Related Complications:** 
    - Risk of device-related infections, including meningitis (sub-clinical or clinical). CSF samples should be routinely monitored for infection.
    - Material degradation of the reservoir typically occurs after 4 years of use; replacement should be considered by this time.
- **Clinical Monitoring:**
    - Monitor vital signs before, during, and after infusion.
    - **ECG Monitoring:** Perform during infusion in patients with a history of bradycardia or heart disease. Regular 12-lead ECGs are required every 6 months in all patients.
- **Anaphylaxis:** Symptoms may include hives, dyspnoea, stridor, and hypotension. Appropriate medical support must be available during administration.
- **Sodium Content:** Contains 17.42 mg of sodium per vial, which should be considered for patients on a controlled sodium diet [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions were evaluated in 38 patients over up to 309 weeks.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Pyrexia (84%), Vomiting (63%), Seizures (45%), Epilepsy (40%), Hypersensitivity (37%), Myoclonus (26%), Abdominal pain (21%), Device-related infection (24%), Irritability (16%), Headache (13%), CSF Pleocytosis (18%) |
| **Common (1-10%)** | Bradycardia (5%), Rash (5%), Urticaria (5%), Status epilepticus (8%), Clonic convulsion (3%), Feeling jittery (5%) |
| **Product Issues** | Needle issues (29%), Device leakage (18%), Device malfunction (13%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Convulsions are a common manifestation of CLN2 but may also be related to treatment (4% of events deemed drug-related). Anaphylactic reactions have occurred in both clinical and post-marketing settings.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Interaction Studies:** None performed.
- **Mechanism:** As a zymogen activated in lysosomes with minimal systemic exposure, cerliponase alfa is unlikely to interact with other medicinal products.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian: Category B2]. There are no data in pregnant women. Animal studies are lacking. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if cerliponase alfa is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment.
- **Fertility:** No human or animal data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited data in children <1 year of age. Increased peripheral exposure has been noted in children <3 years but without a change in the safety profile.
- **Elderly Use:** No data available.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Cerliponase alfa is a protein degraded via peptide hydrolysis; impaired hepatic or renal function is not expected to affect pharmacokinetics.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A recombinant form of human TPP1. It is a zymogen (proenzyme) activated in the lysosome. Once active, it cleaves tripeptides from the N-terminus of proteins. Lack of TPP1 leads to neurodegeneration in CLN2.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Restores enzyme activity to slow the loss of ambulation and speech.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Vz = 435 mL (exceeds typical CSF volume, suggesting tissue distribution).
    - **Metabolism:** Proteolysis.
    - **Elimination:** t½ = 7.35 hours (in CSF). Renal clearance is a minor pathway.
    - **Note:** No evidence of drug accumulation in CSF or plasma was observed with every-other-week dosing.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study (190-201/202):** A study of 24 patients (ages 3–9) showed that 87% of patients did not have an unreversed 2-point decline in the ML (Motor-Language) score over 48 weeks.
- **Comparison to Natural History:** Treated patients showed a median time to a 2-point decline of 272 weeks, compared to 49 weeks in untreated historical controls (Hazard Ratio 0.14, p < 0.0001).
- **Study 190-203:** In children <3 years, treatment showed a delay in disease onset and attenuation of ML score decline compared to age-matched controls.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store upright in a freezer at -25°C to -15°C.
- **Protection:** Store in the original package to protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to packaging [Australian].
- **Stability After Thawing:** Once thawed, use immediately. Vials may be stored at 2-8°C for up to 24 hours if not used immediately.
