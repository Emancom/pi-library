# Editorial Product Information: Collagenase clostridium histolyticum

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **collagenase clostridium histolyticum** for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines used for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Collagenase clostridium histolyticum (a combination of bacterial collagenases AUX-I and AUX-II).
- **Chemical Class:** Microbial collagenase (proteinase).
- **Brand Name:** **XIAFLEX** [US].
- **ATC Code:** M09AB02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilized powder for injection; 0.9 mg per single-use vial.
- **Route of Administration:** Intralesional injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Endo USA, Inc. / Endo Biologics Limited [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Dupuytren’s Contracture:** Treatment of adult patients with a palpable cord.
- **Peyronie’s Disease:** Treatment of adult men with a palpable plaque and curvature deformity of at least 30 degrees at the start of therapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Must be reconstituted with the supplied diluent (containing calcium) before use.
- Administration must be performed by a healthcare provider experienced in injection procedures of the hand (for Dupuytren's) or male urological diseases (for Peyronie's).

### Dupuytren’s Contracture
- **Dose:** 0.58 mg per injection into the palpable cord.
- **Procedure:** Up to two joints/cords in the same hand may be treated during a single visit.
- **Finger Extension Procedure:** If contracture persists, perform 24–72 hours post-injection to facilitate cord disruption.
- **Retreatment:** Injections and extension procedures may be repeated up to 3 times per cord at approximately 4-week intervals.

### Peyronie’s Disease
- **Dose:** 0.58 mg per injection into the target plaque.
- **Cycle:** One treatment cycle consists of two injections (1–3 days apart) followed by a penile modeling procedure (1–3 days after the second injection).
- **Course:** Up to 4 treatment cycles may be administered at 6-week intervals.
- **Discontinuation:** Stop treatment if curvature is reduced to <15 degrees after any cycle.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of hypersensitivity to **XIAFLEX** or other therapeutic collagenases [US].
- **Urethral Involvement:** Treatment of Peyronie’s plaques that involve the penile urethra [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]: Corporal Rupture (Penile Fracture).** Intralesional injection can cause serious penile injury, including corporal rupture or severe hematoma. Signs include a "popping" sound, sudden detumescence, or severe ecchymosis. Evaluation for surgical intervention is required if these occur.
- **REMS Program [US]:** Due to the risk of corporal rupture, the drug is available for Peyronie's disease only through a restricted Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) program.
- **Tendon Rupture (Dupuytren's):** Improper injection into tendons, nerves, or blood vessels can cause permanent injury, including tendon rupture or ligament damage. Use caution near the PIP joint of the fifth finger.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and severe allergic reactions may occur as the product contains foreign proteins.
- **Coagulation Disorders:** Use with caution in patients with abnormal coagulation or those taking anticoagulants (except low-dose aspirin $\le$ 150 mg/day) within 7 days of injection.
- **Acute Post-Injection Back Pain:** May occur in Peyronie's treatment, typically resolving within 15 minutes.
- **Syncope:** Vasovagal syncope or presyncope can occur post-injection, often triggered by pain.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency in Dupuytren’s Contracture
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Edema peripheral (hand swelling), contusion, injection site hemorrhage, injection site reaction, pain in extremity, tenderness, lymphadenopathy. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Skin laceration, lymph node pain, erythema, axillary pain. |

### Frequency in Peyronie’s Disease
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Penile hematoma, penile swelling, penile pain, penile ecchymosis. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Blood blister, penile blister, genital pruritus, painful erection, erectile dysfunction, procedural pain. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Tendon rupture, corporal rupture, severe injection site hematoma, skin and soft tissue necrosis, and anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants:** Increased risk of bleeding/bruising at the injection site. **XIAFLEX** should be used with caution in patients receiving concomitant anticoagulants (excluding low-dose aspirin) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted with other medicinal products.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category B]. Animal studies (rats) showed no harm to the fetus at 11 times the MRHD. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women; use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if the drug is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients <18 years of age have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients $\ge$ 65 years and younger patients.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (systemic exposure is minimal to non-quantifiable).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lysis of collagen deposits via enzymatic hydrolysis. The two collagenases (AUX-I and AUX-II) work synergistically to hydrolyse Type I and Type III collagen, the primary components of Dupuytren's cords and Peyronie's plaques.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Results in the disruption of cords/plaques, leading to improved range of motion and reduced curvature.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Not quantifiable in systemic circulation after Dupuytren's injection. Minimal, short-lived levels (Cmax <29–71 ng/mL) may be detected 10 minutes post-penile injection.
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Not specified in available sources; however, systemic levels fall below quantification limits within 30 minutes.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dupuytren’s Contracture:** Two randomized, double-blind trials showed significant reduction in contracture (to $\le$ 5 degrees) in 44–64% of **XIAFLEX** patients vs. 5–7% of placebo patients after up to 3 injections.
- **Peyronie’s Disease:** Two randomized trials (Studies 1 and 2) demonstrated a mean percent change in curvature deformity of approximately 34% with **XIAFLEX** compared to 18–21% with placebo at 52 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials/Diluent:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F).
- **Do not freeze.**
- **Reconstituted Solution:** May be kept at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) for up to 1 hour or refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 4 hours prior to use.
