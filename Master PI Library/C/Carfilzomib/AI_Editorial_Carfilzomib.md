# Editorial Product Information: Carfilzomib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, intended to consolidate clinical and regulatory data into a single, concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines. Prescribers should consult the current approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Carfilzomib (a modified tetrapeptidyl epoxide/proteasome inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **Kyprolis** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XG02.
- **Dosage Form and Strengths:** Sterile, white to off-white lyophilised powder for injection in single-use vials: 10 mg, 30 mg, and 60 mg. Upon reconstitution, the solution contains 2 mg/mL of carfilzomib [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only. **Do not administer as a bolus.**
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amgen Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Kyprolis** is indicated for the treatment of patients with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM) who have received at least one prior therapy, as part of a combination regimen with:
- Dexamethasone;
- Lenalidomide and dexamethasone;
- Daratumumab and dexamethasone; OR
- Isatuximab and dexamethasone [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- **Calculation:** Dose is based on baseline Body Surface Area (BSA). For patients with a BSA > 2.2 m², the dose should be calculated using 2.2 m².
- **Weight Adjustments:** Dose changes are only required if weight fluctuates by > 20%.
- **Duration:** Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

### Regimen Overview [Australian]
| Regimen | Starting Dose (Cycle 1, Day 1) | Target Dose (Day 8+) | Frequency | Infusion Time |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **With Lenalidomide + Dex** | 20 mg/m² | 27 mg/m² | Twice weekly | 10 mins |
| **With Dex OR Daratumumab + Dex** | 20 mg/m² | 56 mg/m² | Twice weekly | 30 mins |
| **With Dex OR Daratumumab + Dex** | 20 mg/m² | 70 mg/m² | Once weekly | 30 mins |
| **With Isatuximab + Dex** | 20 mg/m² | 56 mg/m² | Twice weekly | 30 mins |

### Administration Instructions
- **Hydration:** Adequate hydration is mandatory before and after Cycle 1 doses to reduce risks of tumour lysis syndrome (TLS) and renal toxicity. Recommended: 30 mL/kg/day oral fluids for 48 hours prior to Day 1, plus 250–500 mL IV fluids before and after each Cycle 1 dose.
- **Pre-medication:** Dexamethasone must be administered 30 minutes to 4 hours before **Kyprolis** to reduce infusion-related reactions.
- **Prophylaxis:** Consider antiviral prophylaxis to decrease the risk of herpes zoster reactivation. Thromboprophylaxis is recommended when combined with lenalidomide or thalidomide-based regimens.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to carfilzomib or any excipients (sulfobutyl betadex sodium, citric acid, sodium hydroxide).
- When used in combination, the contraindications for co-administered agents (e.g., lenalidomide, daratumumab) also apply [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiac Disorders:** New or worsening cardiac failure (e.g., CHF, pulmonary oedema), myocardial ischaemia, and infarction have occurred. Fatal cardiac arrest has been reported within 24 hours of administration. Monitor for volume overload; patients ≥ 75 years and Asian patients are at increased risk [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS), interstitial lung disease, and pneumonitis have occurred. Fatalities are reported.
- **Pulmonary Hypertension:** Reported in approximately 2% of patients. 
- **Acute Renal Failure:** Reported more frequently in advanced RRMM. Monitor SCr/CrCl monthly.
- **Infusion Reactions:** May occur up to 24 hours post-dose. Symptoms include fever, chills, arthralgia, myalgia, and chest tightness.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Particularly in patients with high tumour burden. Ensure adequate hydration.
- **Thrombocytopenia/Haemorrhage:** Haemorrhagic events (GI, pulmonary, intracranial) are reported, often associated with cyclical thrombocytopenia (nadir on Day 8 or 15).
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Significant risk of DVT and PE.
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Rare; discontinue if suspected.
- **HBV Reactivation:** Test all patients for Hepatitis B before initiation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are based on pooled clinical study data (n=3878) [Australian].

- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - Anaemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, lymphopenia, leukopenia.
    - Fatigue, pyrexia, peripheral oedema, asthenia.
    - Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, constipation, abdominal pain.
    - Dyspnoea, cough.
    - Hypertension.
    - Insomnia, dizziness, headache, peripheral neuropathy.
    - Hypokalaemia, hyperglycaemia, decreased appetite.
    - Increased blood creatinine.
- **Common (1–10%):** Sepsis, pneumonia, urinary tract infection, pulmonary embolism, pulmonary hypertension, acute kidney injury, hepatic failure, and deep vein thrombosis.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Tumour lysis syndrome, PRES, ARDS, GI perforation.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Haemolytic uraemic syndrome (HUS), thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by peptidase and epoxide hydrolase activities. Cytochrome P450 (CYP) mediated metabolism plays a minor role.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Carfilzomib is unlikely to be affected by CYP inhibitors or inducers. It did not affect the pharmacokinetics of oral midazolam (a CYP3A probe) in clinical studies.
- **Transporters:** Carfilzomib is a P-gp substrate and an OATP1B1 inhibitor in vitro, but clinically significant interactions are unlikely due to rapid IV clearance.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Efficacy may be reduced during treatment; additionally, carfilzomib increases VTE risk, making hormonal contraceptives less ideal [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian: Category C]. No data in pregnant women. Animal studies show embryo-fetal toxicity (rabbits) at doses lower than human therapeutic levels. Not teratogenic in rats/rabbits at tested doses. 
- **Contraception:**
    - **Females:** Must use effective contraception during and for one month after treatment.
    - **Males:** Must use effective contraception during and for three months after treatment if the partner is pregnant or of childbearing potential.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established; excretion in milk is unknown. Breastfeeding should be discontinued or treatment withheld [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children (<18 years) have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** Higher incidence of certain adverse events (e.g., cardiac failure, fatal TEAEs) in patients ≥ 75 years compared to younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No starting dose adjustment required for mild, moderate, or severe impairment (including chronic dialysis). Administer dose *after* dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No starting dose adjustment required for mild or moderate impairment. Not studied in severe hepatic impairment; monitor liver enzymes monthly.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Irreversible tetrapeptide epoxyketone proteasome inhibitor. It binds selectively to the N-terminal threonine active sites of the 20S proteasome (the proteolytic core of the 26S proteasome).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Suppression of chymotrypsin-like (CT-L) activity was observed 1 hour post-dose. Inhibition is maintained for ≥ 48 hours after the first dose of each week.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Vd at steady state (20 mg/m²) is 22 L. Plasma protein binding is ~97%.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly and extensively metabolised into inactive peptide fragments and a diol. 
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is ≤ 1 hour. Clearance (151–263 L/h) exceeds hepatic blood flow, indicating significant extrahepatic clearance. <1% excreted as parent drug in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ASPIRE (PX-171-009):** Compared KRd (Kyprolis, Lenalidomide, Dexamethasone) vs Rd. Median PFS was 26.3 months (KRd) vs 17.6 months (Rd).
- **ENDEAVOR (2011-003):** Compared Kd (56 mg/m² twice weekly) vs bortezomib/dexamethasone (Vd). Median PFS was 18.7 months (Kd) vs 9.4 months (Vd).
- **CANDOR (0160275):** Compared KDd (56 mg/m² twice weekly, daratumumab, dexamethasone) vs Kd. KDd showed a 37% reduction in risk of progression/death.
- **IKEMA (EFC15246):** Compared Isa-Kd (Isatuximab, Kyprolis, Dexamethasone) vs Kd. Isa-Kd showed a 46.9% reduction in risk of progression/death.
- **A.R.R.O.W. (20140355):** Demonstrated that once-weekly Kd (70 mg/m²) provided superior PFS (11.2 months) compared to twice-weekly Kd (27 mg/m²) (7.6 months) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze. Store in original carton to protect from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Use as soon as practicable. Stable for 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C or 4 hours below 25°C.
- **Diluted Solution (in IV bag):** Stability is similar to reconstituted solution. Light protection is not required for the diluted product [Australian].
