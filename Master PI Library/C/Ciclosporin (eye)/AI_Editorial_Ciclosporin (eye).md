# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory instructions into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN/USP)
- **Chemical Class**: Carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) involved in fatty acid metabolism.
- **Brand Names**: Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism)**: For the acute and chronic management of patients with genetic metabolic defects that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose**: The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration**: Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis**: A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Monitoring**: It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels (target: 35 to 60 µmol/L) and baseline blood chemistries prior to therapy, with weekly and monthly monitoring thereafter [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration**: Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment**: Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40 to 50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

### Administration Notes
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discolouration prior to use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity**: None known at the time of publication, though hypersensitivity reactions have been reported (see Warnings and Precautions) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, there may be an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures [US].
- **Monitoring**: In ESRD patients, supplemental carnitine increases plasma concentrations, but the long-term effect on clinical outcomes in this specific population has not been fully determined [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD Trials)
The following effects were reported in clinical trials involving patients on chronic hemodialysis, regardless of causality [US]:
- **Body as a Whole**: Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), injection site reaction (33%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), infection (15%).
- **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive**: Diarrhea (16%), nausea (8%), vomiting (15%).
- **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Neurological**: Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%).

### Post-marketing Experience
- **Neurologic**: Seizures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity**: Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].
- **Gastrointestinal**: Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: [US: Category not specified in new format]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human surface area dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The benefits of carnitine supplementation to the mother should be weighed against potential risks of excess intake to the infant [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: See Dosage and Administration; dosing is weight-based (mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment**: The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination**: Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses post-dialysis increased mean trough levocarnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L.
- **Dose-Ranging Data**: A study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility**: Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC plastic bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL at room temperature [US].
