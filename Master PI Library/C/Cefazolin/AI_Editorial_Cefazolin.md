# Editorial Product Information: Cefazolin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, legally binding PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local guidelines and the current approved PI for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefazolin (as Cefazolin sodium), a semi-synthetic first-generation cephalosporin antibiotic.
- **Brand Names:** Cefazolin-AFT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01DB04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Powder for injection: 500 mg, 1 g, and 2 g vials [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Cefazolin is indicated for the treatment of serious infections caused by susceptible organisms, including:
- **Respiratory Tract Infections:** Due to *S. pneumoniae*, *Klebsiella* sp., *H. influenzae*, *S. aureus*, and group A beta-haemolytic streptococci.
- **Genitourinary Tract Infections:** Due to *E. coli*, *P. mirabilis*, *Klebsiella* sp., and certain strains of *Enterobacter* and *Enterococci*.
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** Due to *S. aureus*, group A beta-haemolytic streptococci, and other streptococcal strains.
- **Bone and Joint Infections:** Due to *S. aureus*.
- **Septicaemia:** Due to *S. pneumoniae*, *S. aureus*, *P. mirabilis*, *E. coli*, and *Klebsiella* sp.
- **Endocarditis:** Due to *S. aureus* and group A beta-haemolytic streptococci.

*Editorial Note:* While effective in eradicating streptococci from the nasopharynx, there is currently no data establishing efficacy in the subsequent prevention of rheumatic fever [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Mild Gram-positive infections:** 250 mg to 500 mg every 8 hours.
- **Mild to moderate infections (Respiratory/GU):** 500 mg to 1 g every 12 hours.
- **Moderate to severe infections:** 500 mg to 1 g every 6 to 8 hours.
- **Maximum dose:** Up to 6 g/day has been used in life-threatening infections such as endocarditis [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Intramuscular (IM):** Reconstitute with Water for Injections or 0.9% Sodium Chloride. Inject into a large muscle mass.
- **Direct Intravenous (IV):** Dilute reconstituted dose in a minimum of 10 mL Water for Injections; inject slowly over 3 to 5 minutes.
- **IV Infusion:** May be administered via intermittent or continuous infusion in compatible fluids (e.g., 0.9% NaCl, 5% or 10% Dextrose, Lactated Ringer’s).
- **Incompatibility:** **Must NOT** be administered intrathecally; reports of severe CNS toxicity and seizures have occurred with this route [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Cephalosporin Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to the cephalosporin group.
- **Penicillin Allergy:** Previous experience of a major/immediate (Type I) allergy to penicillin.
- **Diluent Contraindication:** Lidocaine (lignocaine) must not be used as a diluent for IM injection in patients with known hypersensitivity to lidocaine [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity and Cross-Reactivity:** Careful inquiry regarding previous reactions to beta-lactams is essential. Partial cross-allergenicity exists between penicillins and cephalosporins. 
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** Including SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP. Discontinue immediately if suspected.
- **Neurotoxicity:** Risk of seizures and encephalopathy (symptoms: myoclonus, lethargy, disorientation) is increased in the elderly and those with renal impairment if dosages are not appropriately adjusted.
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Disease (CDAD):** May range from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis. Avoid drugs that delay peristalsis (e.g., opiates) if CDAD is suspected.
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms.
- **History of GI Disease:** Use with caution in patients with a history of gastrointestinal disorders.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common:** Eosinophilia (most common blood disorder) [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, anorexia, and oral candidiasis.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rash, drug fever, vulvar pruritus, and itching.
- **Haematological:** Neutropenia, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, and positive Coombs’ test.
- **Hepatic/Renal:** Transient rises in AST, ALT, alkaline phosphatase, and urea without clinical evidence of organ impairment.
- **Local Reactions:** Phlebitis (IV site) and pain or induration (IM site).
- **Serious Effects:** Seizures and encephalopathy (primarily in overdose or renal failure); SCAR [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** Competitively inhibits renal tubular secretion, leading to increased and prolonged cefazolin blood levels.
- **Aminoglycosides:** Potential for additive nephrotoxicity; monitor renal function closely if co-administered.
- **Warfarin:** Cefazolin may enhance the anticoagulant effect; monitor INR/Prothrombin time and adjust warfarin dose as needed.
- **Live Typhoid Vaccine:** Antibiotic activity may interfere with the vaccine's immunological response; allow at least 24 hours between the last antibiotic dose and vaccination [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B1 [Australian]:** Studies in animals are limited but show no evidence of fetal damage. Cefazolin readily crosses the placenta (cord blood levels are 25–33% of maternal levels). Safety in human pregnancy trials is not established.
- **Breastfeeding:** Cefazolin is excreted in breast milk in very low concentrations. Use with caution [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in provided sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Safety:** Not established for premature infants or neonates under 1 month of age.
    - **Dosing:** 25 to 50 mg/kg/day divided into 3 or 4 doses for mild-to-moderate infections; up to 100 mg/kg/day for severe infections [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data provided, though increased risk of neurotoxicity is noted due to age-related renal decline.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose reduction is required based on creatinine clearance (CrCl).
    - **Adult Adjustment (post-loading dose of 500 mg):**
        - CrCl 40–70 mL/min: Use mild-to-moderate dosing (12-hourly).
        - CrCl 20–40 mL/min: Reduced dose (12-hourly).
        - CrCl 5–20 mL/min: Further reduced dose (24-hourly).
        - CrCl < 5 mL/min: Minimal maintenance dose (24-hourly) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak serum levels occur roughly 1 hour after IM injection.
    - **Distribution:** Readily enters inflamed synovial fluid and crosses the placental barrier. Present in bile (unless obstruction is present).
    - **Metabolism:** Not significantly metabolised.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted unchanged in the urine; 56–89% of an IM dose is recovered within 6 hours. Half-life is approximately 1.4 hours (IV) to 1.8 hours (IM).
- **Microbiology:** Active against *S. aureus* (methicillin-susceptible), *S. pneumoniae*, *E. coli*, *P. mirabilis*, and *Klebsiella* sp. Most strains of *Enterococcus*, *Pseudomonas*, and methicillin-resistant *Staphylococci* (MRSA) are resistant [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data was detailed in the source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vial Storage:** 
    - 500 mg and 1 g: Store below 25°C.
    - 2 g: Store below 30°C.
- **Light Protection:** Vials should be protected from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Stable for 24 hours when stored under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C). Do not freeze. Discard any unused portion after 24 hours [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
