# Editorial Product Information: Clomipramine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: 
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single reference.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Clomipramine hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Tricyclic antidepressant (TCA); dibenzazepine derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Anafranil** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 25 mg sugar-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmaco (Australia) Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depression** [Australian].
- **Obsessive-Compulsive Disorders (OCD) and Phobias** in adults [Australian].
- **Cataplexy** associated with narcolepsy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
Dosage should be individualised, using the lowest effective dose. Plasma concentrations do not stabilise for 7 to 14 days after initiation or dose changes.

### Depression, OCD, and Phobias
- **Initial:** 25 mg two or three times daily.
- **Titration:** Increase stepwise (e.g., 25 mg every few days) based on tolerability to a target of 100 mg to 150 mg (4 to 6 tablets) daily.
- **Maintenance:** Adjust to an average of 50 mg to 100 mg (2 to 4 tablets) daily once improvement is established.

### Cataplexy associated with Narcolepsy
- **Daily Dose:** 25 mg to 75 mg. 
- **Administration Note:** Nocturnal dosing should be avoided if the medication exacerbates insomnia.

### Discontinuation
Abrupt cessation should be avoided due to potential withdrawal symptoms. Doses should be tapered gradually after long-term use.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to clomipramine, any tablet excipients, or cross-sensitivity to other tricyclic antidepressants in the dibenzazepine group.
- Concomitant use with **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs)**, or use within 14 days before or after an irreversible MAOI or within 14 days before moclobemide (a reversible MAOI) [Australian].
- Acute and recovery stages following myocardial infarction.
- Congenital long QT syndrome.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Clinical Worsening and Suicidality
- **Suicide Risk:** The risk of suicide is inherent in depression and may persist until significant remission occurs. 
- **Monitoring:** Close surveillance is required for clinical worsening and emergence of suicidality, particularly during the first few months of therapy or during dose adjustments.
- **Age-Specific Risk:** Pooled analyses of short-term trials show an increased risk of suicidal thinking and behaviour in children, adolescents, and young adults (ages 18–24). Risk was reduced in adults aged 65 and older.
- **Precursor Symptoms:** Anxiety, agitation, panic attacks, insomnia, and mania may be precursors to emerging suicidality.

### Cardiovascular Effects
- **QTc Prolongation:** There is a risk of QTc prolongation and Torsades de pointes, particularly at supra-therapeutic doses or when co-administered with drugs that increase clomipramine concentrations (e.g., SSRIs).
- **Pre-existing Conditions:** Caution is required in patients with cardiovascular insufficiency, conduction disorders (AV block grades I to III), or arrhythmias. ECG monitoring is recommended in these patients and the elderly.
- **Hypokalaemia:** Must be treated before initiating clomipramine.

### Neurological and Psychiatric Effects
- **Seizure Threshold:** Clomipramine lowers the seizure threshold in a dose-dependent manner. Caution is advised in patients with epilepsy or brain damage.
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** A risk exists when co-administered with other serotonergic agents (SSRIs, SNRIs, lithium).
- **Activation of Mania/Psychosis:** TCAs may precipitate mixed/manic episodes in patients with bipolar disorder or activate psychosis in patients with schizophrenia.
- **Panic Disorder:** A paradoxical increase in anxiety may occur at the start of treatment, usually subsiding within two weeks.

### General Precautions
- **Anticholinergic Effects:** Caution in narrow-angle glaucoma, urinary retention, and chronic constipation (risk of paralytic ileus, especially in the elderly/bedridden).
- **Dental:** Increased incidence of dental caries has been reported.
- **Ocular:** Decreased lacrimation may damage corneal epithelium in contact lens users.
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of blood counts (risk of agranulocytosis), hepatic enzymes, and renal function is recommended during prolonged treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often dose-related and may not always correlate with plasma levels.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Drowsiness, fatigue, dizziness, tremor, headache, myoclonus, restlessness; blurred vision, accommodation disorder; nausea, dry mouth, constipation; weight increase, increased appetite; hyperhidrosis; micturition disorder; libido disorder, erectile dysfunction. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Confusion, disorientation, hallucinations (especially in elderly/Parkinson’s), anxiety, mania, insomnia, nightmares; speech disorders, paraesthesia, muscle hypertonia, memory impairment; tachycardia, orthostatic hypotension, palpitations; tinnitus; vomiting, abdominal distress, diarrhoea, anorexia; transaminase/alkaline phosphatase increase; allergic dermatitis, photosensitivity, pruritus; hot flushes; yawning; galactorrhoea, breast enlargement. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Convulsions, ataxia; arrhythmias, increased blood pressure; activation of psychotic symptoms. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Glaucoma. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | SIADH; leucopenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia; conduction disorders (QRS widening, prolonged PR/QTc), bundle-branch block, Torsades de pointes, cardiomyopathy, heart failure, MI, stroke; paralytic ileus; hepatitis (with or without jaundice); anaphylactic reactions; peripheral neuropathy, Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS); urinary retention; allergic alveolitis; purpura; alopecia, hyperpyrexia, oedema. |
| **Unknown Frequency** | Serotonin syndrome, extrapyramidal symptoms (akathisia, tardive dyskinesia), rhabdomyolysis (as a complication of NMS), ejaculation failure/delay, increased blood prolactin. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Risk of hypertensive crisis, hyperpyrexia, and serotonin syndrome. A 14-day washout is mandatory (24 hours for moclobemide initiation after the MAOI, but 14 days if clomipramine was used first) [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Agents:** (SSRIs, SNRIs, lithium) Increased risk of serotonin syndrome.
- **CNS Depressants:** Potentiation of effects of alcohol, barbiturates, and benzodiazepines.
- **Anticholinergics:** Potentiation of effects on eye, bladder, and bowel; risk of delirium.
- **Sympathomimetic Amines:** Potentiation of cardiovascular effects (e.g., adrenaline, noradrenaline).
- **Antihypertensives:** Clomipramine may reduce the effects of clonidine, guanethidine, and methyldopa.

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** (e.g., terbinafine, quinidine, propafenone, fluoxetine, paroxetine) Increase clomipramine concentrations. Terbinafine co-administration requires dose adjustment.
- **CYP1A2, CYP2C19, and CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., fluvoxamine, cimetidine) Increase clomipramine concentrations. Fluvoxamine may increase clomipramine levels 4-fold.
- **CYP Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, barbiturates) Decrease clomipramine concentrations by accelerating metabolism. 
- **Valproate:** May increase serum levels of clomipramine.
- **Cigarette Smoking:** Can decrease clomipramine plasma concentrations by 2-fold.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Monitoring is recommended if using high-dose oestrogen (50 mcg daily).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Category:** **Category C** [Australian].
- **Clinical Data:** TCAs are not associated with increased birth defects. However, clomipramine should be avoided unless benefits outweigh fetal risks.
- **Neonatal Effects:** Withdrawal/toxicity symptoms (dyspnoea, lethargy, irritability, convulsions, hyper/hypotonia) have occurred in newborns.
- **Management:** Withdraw clomipramine gradually at least 7 weeks before the expected delivery date if possible.

### Breastfeeding
- Clomipramine passes into human milk. Due to potential risks, infants should be weaned or the medication should be gradually withdrawn.

### Fertility
- No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric Use
- **Depression/Psychiatric Disorders:** Not established and not recommended for children and adolescents under 18 years [Australian]. 

### Elderly Use
- Elderly patients are more sensitive to anticholinergic, cardiovascular, and neurological effects.
- **Dosing:** Start with 25 mg daily; gradually increase to 50 mg to 75 mg daily over approximately 10 days.

### Renal and Hepatic Impairment
- Use with caution in patients with severe hepatic or renal disease; periodic monitoring of organ function is recommended.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Clomipramine inhibits the neuronal re-uptake of noradrenaline (NA) and serotonin (5-HT). Serotonin re-uptake inhibition is the dominant component. It also possesses alpha1-adrenolytic, anticholinergic, antihistaminic, and antiserotoninergic properties.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Completely absorbed; bioavailability <50% due to extensive first-pass metabolism.
- **Distribution:** 97.6% protein bound. Volume of distribution is ~12 L/kg.
- **Metabolism:** Primarily demethylated (via CYP3A4, CYP2C19, CYP1A2) to the active metabolite **N-desmethylclomipramine**. Both are hydroxylated via CYP2D6.
- **Elimination:** Two-thirds excreted in urine (as conjugates), one-third in faeces. Parent drug half-life is 21 hours (range 12–36h); metabolite half-life is 13–25 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Specific pivotal trials are not detailed in the available PI. 
- Safety data includes pooled analyses of 24 short-term trials involving 4,400 children and adolescents for various psychiatric indications, which helped define suicidality risks [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- Protect from moisture.
- Keep out of reach of children.
