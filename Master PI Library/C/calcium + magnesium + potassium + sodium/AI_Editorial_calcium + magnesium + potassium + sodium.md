# Editorial Product Information: calcium + magnesium + potassium + sodium

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **calcium + magnesium + potassium + sodium**
- Chemical class: Oxybate salts (oxybate / gamma-hydroxybutyrate [GHB] salts of calcium, magnesium, potassium and sodium)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Most recent revision date noted in available source: 7/2025 (specific jurisdictional attributions not provided in source excerpts)
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources beyond the presence of a restricted distribution program (REMS) for safe use/mitigation of risks

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of cataplexy in patients with narcolepsy aged 7 years and older.
- Treatment of excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) in patients with narcolepsy aged 7 years and older.
- Treatment of idiopathic hypersomnia (IH) in adults.
- Jurisdictional notes:
  - Pediatric narcolepsy indication applies to patients 7 years of age and older (documented approval in available sources).  
  - Adult idiopathic hypersomnia indication described for adults (documented in available sources).

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General principles
- The active moiety is oxybate (gamma-hydroxybutyrate); dosing is expressed in grams (g) of total nightly oxybate salts.
- Doses should be prepared just prior to bedtime, diluted with approximately 1/4 cup (≈60 mL) water in the provided container, and consumed while in bed. Patients should lie down immediately after dosing.
- Administer at least 2 hours after eating.
- Solutions prepared following dilution should be consumed within 24 hours.
- Patients often fall asleep rapidly; many fall asleep within 5–15 minutes of dosing.
- Two doses must never be taken at one time.

### Adult patients with narcolepsy (cataplexy or EDS)
- Recommended starting dose: 4.5 g per night, administered orally as two equal doses (2.25 g at bedtime and 2.25 g 2.5–4 hours later).
- Titration: Increase by up to 1.5 g per night per week (for example, 0.75 g increments per dose) based on efficacy and tolerability.
- Recommended dosage range: 6 g to 9 g per night (total nightly dose). Doses above 9 g/night have not been studied and ordinarily should not be administered.
- Doses may be equal or unequal between bedtime and the second dose.

### Pediatric patients with narcolepsy (7 years of age and older)
- Administration: Twice nightly dosing regimen.
- Insufficient information to provide specific dosing recommendations for patients who weigh less than 20 kg.
- Weight-based guidance (recommended starting dose, weekly titration increments, and maximum per-night doses by weight band):
  - 20 kg to <30 kg:
    - Initial: ≤1.0 g at bedtime and ≤1.0 g 2.5–4 h later
    - Maximum weekly increase: 0.5 g per dose
    - Maximum recommended dose: 3.0 g at bedtime and 3.0 g 2.5–4 h later (total up to 6.0 g/night)
  - 30 kg to <45 kg:
    - Initial: ≤1.5 g at bedtime and ≤1.5 g 2.5–4 h later
    - Maximum weekly increase: 0.5 g per dose
    - Maximum recommended dose: 3.75 g at bedtime and 3.75 g 2.5–4 h later (total up to 7.5 g/night)
  - ≥45 kg:
    - Initial: ≤2.25 g at bedtime and ≤2.25 g 2.5–4 h later
    - Maximum weekly increase: 0.75 g per dose
    - Maximum recommended dose: 4.5 g at bedtime and 4.5 g 2.5–4 h later (total up to 9.0 g/night)
- For patients who sleep more than 8 hours per night, the first dose may be taken at bedtime or after an initial period of sleep.
- Doses higher than 9 g per night have not been studied and should not be administered.

### Adult patients with idiopathic hypersomnia (IH)
- Two acceptable regimens: twice nightly or once nightly (adults).
- Twice-nightly regimen:
  - Starting nightly dose: ≤4.5 g per night divided into two doses (e.g., 2.25 g + 2.25 g).
  - Titration increments: ≤1.5 g per night per week (divided into two doses).
  - Maximum total nightly dose: 9 g (divided).
- Once-nightly regimen:
  - Starting nightly dose: ≤3.0 g as a single bedtime dose.
  - Titration increments: ≤1.5 g per night per week.
  - Maximum total nightly dose as a single dose: 6 g.
- During titration the regimen (once vs twice nightly) may be changed based on efficacy and tolerability.
- Doses higher than 9 g per night (or single-dose administrations higher than 6 g) have not been studied and should not be administered.

### Transitioning from sodium-only oxybate products
- For patients switching from other oxybate (sodium oxybate) formulations to **calcium + magnesium + potassium + sodium**, initiate at the same total nightly dose and dosing regimen (gram-for-gram) on the first night, then titrate based on efficacy and tolerability.

### Hepatic impairment
- Recommended starting dose in patients with hepatic impairment: one-half of the original dose per night, administered orally divided into two doses. (Titrate as clinically indicated.)

### Co-administration with divalproex sodium
- When initiating divalproex sodium in patients on a stable oxybate regimen, reduce the oxybate dose by at least 20% at concomitant initiation.
- When initiating oxybate in patients already taking divalproex, start oxybate at a lower dose and titrate based on response and tolerability.

### Missed dose instructions
- If dosing twice nightly and the second dose is missed, skip that second dose and resume the usual regimen the next night.
- Two doses must never be taken at the same time.

## 5. Contraindications
- Concomitant use with alcohol. (Absolute contraindication.)
- Concomitant use with sedative-hypnotics. (Absolute contraindication.)
- Succinic semialdehyde dehydrogenase deficiency. (Absolute contraindication.)
- Other contraindications explicitly listed in the source PI excerpts: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings / highest-risk warnings
- **Central nervous system depression**: **calcium + magnesium + potassium + sodium** is a CNS depressant and can cause clinically significant respiratory depression and obtundation at recommended doses.
- **Abuse and misuse**: The active moiety (oxybate / gamma-hydroxybutyrate) has known abuse liability; illicit GHB is associated with seizures, respiratory depression, decreased consciousness, coma, and death.
- Availability is limited to a restricted distribution program (REMS) because of the risks of CNS depression and abuse/misuse. Prescribers, dispensers and patients must comply with REMS requirements.

### General warnings and precautions
- CNS depression and respiratory depression:
  - Use caution with concurrent CNS depressants (opioids, benzodiazepines, sedating antidepressants/antipsychotics, sedating antiepileptics, muscle relaxants, general anesthetics, alcohol, illicit depressants).
  - Concomitant use may increase risk of respiratory depression, profound sedation, hypotension, syncope, and death.
  - Consider dose reduction or discontinuation of one or more CNS depressants if combination is necessary. Consider interrupting oxybate during short-term opioid use (e.g., perioperative).
- Sleep-disordered breathing:
  - Increased central apneas and clinically relevant oxygen desaturation events have been observed; in some studies significant oxygen desaturation occurred and sleep apnea severity worsened in some patients.
  - Exercise caution and monitor patients with preexisting respiratory compromise or sleep-disordered breathing.
- Psychiatric effects:
  - Depression, suicidal ideation and behavior can occur; monitor for emergent or worsening depression and suicidality.
  - Other neuropsychiatric reactions (confusion, hallucinations, paranoia, psychosis, aggression, agitation, anxiety) have been reported; monitor closely.
- Parasomnias:
  - Sleepwalking and other parasomnias have been reported.
- Abuse, misuse and diversion:
  - Evaluate patients for history of substance abuse and monitor for drug-seeking behavior, dose escalation, or other signs of misuse.
  - Discontinue therapy if abuse is suspected.
- Driving and hazardous activities:
  - Counsel patients not to engage in hazardous activities requiring full mental alertness or motor coordination (e.g., driving, operating machinery, flying) for at least 6 hours after dosing and until they are reasonably certain the drug does not adversely affect them.
- Special populations:
  - Geriatric patients: monitor for impaired motor and/or cognitive function.
  - Pediatric patients: monitor growth and behavior per standard clinical practice.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor respiratory status in patients at risk for respiratory compromise.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of depression, suicidality, and other psychiatric adverse reactions.
- Monitor for signs of misuse, abuse or diversion.
- Monitor for parasomnias and sleep-disordered breathing changes (consider polysomnography if clinically indicated).

## 7. Adverse Effects

- Overall most frequently reported adverse reactions in adults with narcolepsy or IH (reported as ≥5% in clinical studies): nausea, headache, dizziness, anxiety, insomnia, decreased appetite, hyperhidrosis, vomiting, diarrhea, dry mouth, parasomnia, somnolence, fatigue, tremor.
- In pediatric studies with oxybate (same active moiety), adverse reactions reported ≥5% included: nausea, enuresis, vomiting, headache, decreased weight, decreased appetite, dizziness, and sleepwalking.
- Serious adverse reactions and events reported: clinically significant respiratory depression, coma, death (reported with overdose/illicit use), suicides and suicide attempts in oxybate clinical experience, seizures, profound CNS depression requiring respiratory support, clinically significant oxygen desaturation events and central sleep apneas.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant CNS depressants (opioids, benzodiazepines, sedating antidepressants/antipsychotics, sedating antiepileptics, general anesthetics, muscle relaxants, alcohol, and illicit CNS depressants):
  - Increased risk of respiratory depression, profound sedation, hypotension, syncope, and death.
  - Avoid concomitant use when possible; if coadministration is necessary, consider dose reductions, increased monitoring or discontinuation of one agent.
  - If short-term opioid therapy is required, consider interruption of oxybate treatment.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Divalproex sodium:
  - When divalproex is initiated in patients taking oxybate, reduce the oxybate dose by at least 20% at the outset.
  - When initiating oxybate in patients already receiving divalproex, start oxybate at a lower dose and titrate cautiously.
- Other specific CYP- or transporter-mediated interactions: Not specified in available sources.

### Management notes
- Counsel patients to avoid alcohol and sedative-hypnotics while taking this product.
- Monitor carefully for additive CNS and respiratory depression with any CNS depressant.
- Consider dose adjustment of oxybate when initiating or discontinuing divalproex sodium.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Approved for narcolepsy (cataplexy and EDS) in patients 7 years of age and older.
- Dosing is weight-based as described in Section 4. Insufficient data are available for children under 7 years and for children <20 kg (insufficient information to provide specific dosing recommendations).
- Pediatric adverse reactions: in pediatric studies with oxybate, common events included nausea, enuresis, vomiting, headache, decreased weight, decreased appetite, dizziness, and sleepwalking.

### Geriatric use
- Clinical experience limited; monitor elderly patients for impaired motor and cognitive function and increased susceptibility to CNS depressant effects.

### Renal impairment
- Specific dosing adjustments by renal function (eGFR/stage) are not specified in the available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Recommended starting dose in hepatic impairment: one-half of the original nightly dose, given divided into two doses. Adjust further based on clinical response and tolerability.

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Overview of key study populations and outcomes (as reported in available sources)
- Adult narcolepsy studies: Clinical trials evaluated treatment of cataplexy and excessive daytime sleepiness; many trial participants were concomitantly receiving CNS stimulants. Reported efficacy outcomes are summarized in full trial publications and product labeling; specific numerical efficacy endpoints are not fully detailed in the available source excerpts.
- Pediatric narcolepsy study: Evaluated safety and efficacy in patients ≥7 years of age; commonly reported adverse reactions included nausea, enuresis, vomiting, headache, weight decreased, decreased appetite, dizziness, and sleepwalking.
- Idiopathic hypersomnia study(s): Demonstrated benefit with both twice-nightly and once-nightly regimens in adults; dosing regimens and titration recommendations were derived from these studies.
- Safety signals from trials and postmarketing: Increased central apneas and clinically significant oxygen desaturation events observed in some patients; severe CNS depression requiring respiratory support occurred in some adult patients; psychiatric adverse events including depression, suicidality and psychosis were observed in clinical development and postmarketing experience.

- Trial design details, primary/secondary endpoints and quantitative efficacy results: Not specified in available sources beyond the headings and safety outcome summaries noted above.
