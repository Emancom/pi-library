# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory information into a clinically actionable format.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only the US (FDA) source was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with an inborn error of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - Loading Dose: In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) via infusion or IV injection.
    - Maintenance: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Titration: Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately and do not re-administer if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures may occur [US].
- **Thromboembolic Events/Bleeding:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when used with warfarin [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Metabolic Disorders: Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly/monthly, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
    - ESRD: Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) levels (normal range 40 to 50 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Seizures.

### Frequency Data (ESRD Population)
The following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% in clinical trials, regardless of causality [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%) |

*Note: Transient nausea, vomiting, and gastritis have been observed. Body odor may occur with carnitine supplementation [US].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Concurrent use may result in an increased INR. Healthcare professionals should monitor INR levels upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound; therefore, it is unlikely to interact with drugs through displacement from plasma proteins [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned, but traditionally aligned with Category B]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the maternal benefits of treatment. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have been established for metabolic disorders and ESRD; dosing is weight-based (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources; generally, dose selection should be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Intravenous:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis.
    - **Oral Precautions:** Chronic administration of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for beta-oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific acidopathies by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates the metabolic consequences of carnitine loss due to dialysis and reduced renal synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies (ESRD):** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportionate increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
