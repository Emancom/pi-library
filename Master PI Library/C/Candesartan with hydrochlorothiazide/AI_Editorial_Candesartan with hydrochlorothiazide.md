# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)** via Baxter Healthcare Corporation. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized, clinically oriented overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This document serves as an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing and dispensing in any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - *Loading Dose:* In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - *Frequency:* Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - *Maximum Dose:* Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - *Starting Dose:* 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - *Administration:* Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - *Adjustments:* Dose reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Plasma Concentrations:** Prior to starting therapy, plasma carnitine levels should be measured. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry and vital signs.
- **Target Range:** Free carnitine plasma concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the product contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the provided source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, sometimes within minutes of administration. These are reported most frequently in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon signs of a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Renal Impairment/Oral Metabolites:** While the IV formulation bypasses primary GI metabolism, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function can lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of plasma carnitine levels and clinical status is essential to guide therapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, headache, injection site reaction, pain, fever, flu syndrome |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, abdominal pain, vomiting, nausea |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, paresthesia |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].
- **Other Notable Effects:** Body odor and gastritis have been reported, though frequency is difficult to estimate due to underlying pathology [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified in source]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate, well-controlled human studies. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Animal data (cows) show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated for metabolic disorders; refer to the mg/kg dosing guidelines provided in Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in source; general clinical monitoring is advised.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Note that dialysis effectively removes levocarnitine from plasma [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dose adjustments provided in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma carnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses three times weekly showed a dose-proportional increase in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine concentrations increased, effects on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population were not determined during these trials [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
