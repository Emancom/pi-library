# Editorial Product Information: Carbachol

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by an editorial team to provide a comprehensive overview of carbachol-based ophthalmic therapies. It integrates clinical data and safety guidelines from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) regarding intraocular carbachol (0.01%) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) regarding topical carbachol/brimonidine tartrate (2.75%/0.1%). 

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Carbachol**

This PI covers two distinct formulations:
*   **Intraocular Solution:** Carbachol 150 micrograms/1.5 mL (0.01%) [TGA].
*   **Topical Ophthalmic Solution:** A fixed-dose combination containing carbachol 2.75% (27.5 mg/mL) and brimonidine tartrate 0.1% (1 mg/mL) [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Surgical Miosis:** For intraocular use to induce miosis during ophthalmic surgery [TGA].
*   **Presbyopia:** For the topical treatment of presbyopia in adult patients [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
Carbachol is contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity to carbachol, brimonidine tartrate (if applicable), or any of the excipients in the formulation [TGA, FDA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Ocular Effects
*   **Retinal Detachment:** Rare instances of retinal tears and detachment have been reported with the use of miotics. A thorough retinal examination is recommended for all patients prior to starting therapy, particularly those with pre-existing retinal disease [TGA, FDA].
*   **Iritis and Inflammation:** Carbachol may exacerbate existing ocular inflammation or increase surgically induced inflammation. Use with caution in patients with a history of iritis [TGA, FDA].
*   **Visual Impairment:** Miotics may cause accommodative spasm, blurred vision, and difficulty with dark adaptation. Patients should be cautioned against driving or operating machinery if vision is not clear, especially in poor illumination or at night [TGA, FDA].
*   **Corneal Health:** Post-operative corneal clouding, bullous keratopathy, and corneal degeneration have been observed following intraocular use [TGA].

### Systemic Cautions
Due to the potential for systemic absorption, carbachol should be used with caution in patients with the following conditions:
*   Acute cardiac failure or coronary insufficiency [TGA, FDA].
*   Bronchial asthma [TGA].
*   Peptic ulcer disease [TGA].
*   Hyperthyroidism [TGA].
*   Gastrointestinal spasm or urinary tract obstruction [TGA].
*   Parkinson’s disease [TGA].
*   Depression, Raynaud's phenomenon, or thromboangiitis obliterans [FDA].

### Other Precautions
*   **Latex Allergy:** For certain intraocular formulations, the vial stopper may contain natural rubber (latex), which can trigger severe allergic reactions [TGA].
*   **Contamination:** To prevent eye injury or contamination of topical solutions, avoid touching the dispensing tip to any surface [FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Surgical Miosis (Intraocular)
*   **Route:** Anterior chamber instillation.
*   **Dose:** Gently instill a maximum of 0.5 mL into the anterior chamber using an atraumatic cannula.
*   **Timing:** Administration may occur before or after securing sutures. Peak miosis typically occurs within 2 to 5 minutes [TGA].

### Presbyopia (Topical)
*   **Route:** Topical ophthalmic instillation.
*   **Dose:** One drop in each eye once daily.
*   **Administration:** Remove contact lenses before use; wait 10 minutes before reinserting. If using other topical ophthalmic drugs, space administrations by at least 5 minutes [FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Ocular Reactions
*   **Common (>5%):** Eye pain or irritation upon instillation, visual impairment [FDA].
*   **Uncommon/Rare:** Increased intraocular pressure, corneal oedema, anterior chamber inflammation, ocular hyperaemia, and blurred vision [TGA].
*   **Post-Surgical:** Persistent bullous keratopathy and iritis following cataract extraction [TGA].

### Systemic Reactions
*   **Nervous System:** Headache is a frequently reported side effect (uncommon to common depending on the route) [TGA, FDA].
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Vomiting, abdominal cramps, and epigastric distress [TGA].
*   **Other:** Sweating, flushing, and tightness in the urinary bladder [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
While no specific interactions have been described for intraocular use [TGA], the following interactions are noted for topical therapy involving carbachol/brimonidine [FDA]:
*   **Antihypertensives/Cardiac Glycosides:** Potential for additive blood pressure reduction.
*   **CNS Depressants:** Possible additive effect with alcohol, barbiturates, or sedatives.
*   **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs):** May interfere with pupil constriction and blunt the hypotensive effects of alpha-agonists.
*   **MAO Inhibitors:** May interfere with the metabolism of brimonidine, potentially increasing systemic side effects like hypotension.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **TGA Category B2:** Animal studies have not shown harmful effects, but human data is lacking. Use is preferable to avoid unless the benefit justifies the potential fetal risk [TGA].
*   **FDA Data:** Animal studies using 3 times the maximum human dose showed no teratogenic effects in rats. Maternal toxicity (reduced weight/food consumption) was observed in rabbits, though without fetal effects [FDA].

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if carbachol is excreted in human milk. Caution is advised, and the developmental benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug and potential infant exposure [TGA, FDA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Following topical administration, carbachol reaches quantifiable plasma concentrations in some subjects, with a mean Cmax of 1.02 ng/mL and a Tmax of 4.42 hours [FDA].
*   **Distribution:** Brimonidine is approximately 29% bound to plasma proteins; data for carbachol is unavailable [FDA].
*   **Metabolism/Excretion:** Brimonidine is extensively metabolized by the liver and excreted primarily via urine (87% within 120 hours). Specific metabolic and excretion data for carbachol in humans has not been fully established [FDA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Carbachol is a potent cholinergic (parasympathomimetic) agent. Its primary action is the induction of miosis through the constriction of the iris sphincter and the ciliary body [TGA]. 

In the treatment of presbyopia, carbachol creates a "pinhole effect" by constricting the pupil, which increases the depth of focus and improves near visual acuity. When combined with brimonidine (an alpha-adrenergic agonist), the iris dilator muscle is blocked and the ciliary muscle is relaxed, which enhances the selectivity of the pupillary effect and increases the bioavailability of carbachol in the aqueous humor [FDA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **General:** Store below 25°C (77°F) [TGA, FDA].
*   **Specifics:** Do not freeze [TGA]. For topical single-dose vials, store in the original foil pouch to protect from light; use immediately upon opening and discard any remaining contents [FDA].
