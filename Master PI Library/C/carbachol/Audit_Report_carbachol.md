# Originality Audit Report: carbachol

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 72/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 6
- **Overall Assessment:** Concern

## Verbatim Copied Passages
1.  **Editorial Location:** Section 4, "Retinal Detachment"
    - **Editorial Text:** "Rare instances of retinal tears and detachment have been reported with the use of miotics. ... a thorough retinal examination is recommended for all patients prior to starting therapy"
    - **Source Match:** Document 2 (FDA), Section 5.2: "Rare cases of retinal tear and detachment have been reported with miotics. ... Therefore, examination of the retina is advised in all patients prior to initiation of therapy."

2.  **Editorial Location:** Section 5, "Surgical Miosis (Intraocular)"
    - **Editorial Text:** "No more than one-half mL should be gently instilled into the anterior chamber ... It may be instilled before or after securing sutures. Miosis is usually maximal within two to five minutes"
    - **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 4.2: "No more than one-half mL should be gently instilled into the anterior chamber ... It may be instilled before or after securing sutures. Miosis is usually maximal within two to five minutes after application."

3.  **Editorial Location:** Section 5, "Presbyopia (Topical)"
    - **Editorial Text:** "Remove contact lenses before use; wait 10 minutes before reinserting. If using other topical ophthalmic drugs, space administrations by at least 5 minutes"
    - **Source Match:** Document 2 (FDA), Section 17 (and 2.2): "Advise patients to remove contact lenses prior to the instillation ... Wait 10 minutes after dosing before reinserting contact lenses ... medicines must be administered at least 5 minutes apart."

4.  **Editorial Location:** Section 9, "Pharmacokinetics"
    - **Editorial Text:** "mean Cmax of 1.02 ng/mL and a Tmax of 4.42 hours"
    - **Source Match:** Document 2 (FDA), Section 12.3: "with Cmax and Tmax up to 1.02 ng/mL and 4.42 hours respectively."

5.  **Editorial Location:** Section 10, "Mechanism of Action"
    - **Editorial Text:** "blocks contraction of the iris dilator muscle and relaxes tonic contraction of the ciliary muscle, enhancing selectivity for the pupil and increasing bioavailability of carbachol in the aqueous humor."
    - **Source Match:** Document 2 (FDA), Section 12.1: "blocks contraction of the iris dilator muscle and relaxes tonic contraction of the ciliary muscle, enhancing selectivity for the pupil and increasing bioavailability of carbachol in the aqueous humor."

6.  **Editorial Location:** Section 11, "Storage Conditions"
    - **Editorial Text:** "store in the original foil pouch to protect from light; use immediately upon opening and discard any remaining contents"
    - **Source Match:** Document 2 (FDA), Section 16: "store the single-dose vials in the foil pouch away from light until ready for use. Discard opened single-dose vials and any remaining contents immediately after use."

## Close Paraphrases
- **Section 4 (Visual Impairment):** The warning about driving in poor illumination closely follows the wording of FDA Section 5.1 and TGA Section 4.7.
- **Section 6 (Adverse Effects):** The list of systemic reactions (sweating, flushing, tightness in the urinary bladder) is a direct lift of the list in TGA Section 4.8, though slightly reordered.
- **Section 8 (Pregnancy):** The summary of rabbit maternal toxicity (reduced weight/food consumption) in the FDA data section is very close to the wording in FDA Section 8.1.

## Originality Strengths
- **Structural Synthesis:** The editorial successfully creates a new structure that distinguishes between intraocular (TGA) and topical (FDA) formulations, which are listed separately in the sources.
- **Comparative Analysis:** Section 10 (Mechanism of Action) and Section 1 (Product Name) do a good job of explicitly labeling which data belongs to which regulatory body, creating a "unified" view that does not exist in the source files.
- **Harmonized Tables:** Section 7 (Drug Interactions) synthesizes complex FDA bullet points into a more concise, readable summary.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Consolidation:** The editorial removes brand names (Miostat, Yuvezzi) to focus on the active ingredient, carbachol, as a class.
- **Contextual Labeling:** The writer added [TGA] and [FDA] tags to help the reader identify the source of specific regulatory requirements.
- **Omission of Technicalities:** The editorial correctly omits NDC numbers, manufacturer addresses, and specific Phase 3 trial names (BRIO I, BRIO II) to maintain a professional editorial tone.
- **Merged Safety Profiles:** The "Systemic Cautions" section combines specific warnings from both agencies into a single, unified checklist.

## Recommendation
**REVISION NEEDED**

While the editorial is an excellent synthesis of two different products, it relies too heavily on verbatim strings for clinical instructions (Dosage) and scientific descriptions (Pharmacokinetics and Mechanism of Action). 

**Specific Changes Required:**
1.  **Section 5 (Dosage):** Rewrite the instillation instructions for both surgical and topical use in original language (e.g., instead of "before or after securing sutures," use "regardless of suture status").
2.  **Section 10 (Mechanism of Action):** The second paragraph is almost entirely a verbatim lift from the FDA PI. This must be summarized or rewritten (e.g., explain the "pinhole effect" using different terminology like "increased depth of field via miosis").
3.  **Section 11 (Storage):** Rephrase the instructions regarding the foil pouch and disposal of single-use vials to avoid matching the FDA source text.
