# Editorial Product Information: Cobimetinib

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of cobimetinib for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cobimetinib (as hemifumarate).
- **Chemical Class:** Orally available inhibitor of MEK1 and MEK2 tyrosine-threonine kinases.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Cotellic** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EE02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 20 mg film-coated tablets (white, round, debossed with "COB" on one side).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **BRAF V600 Mutation-Positive Melanoma:** Indicated for use in combination with vemurafenib (Zelboraf) for the treatment of patients with unresectable or metastatic melanoma. Tumour status must be confirmed by a validated BRAF V600 mutation test prior to initiation.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Dosing:** The recommended dose is 60 mg (three 20 mg tablets) taken orally once daily.
- **Dosing Cycle:** Treatment follows a 28-day cycle: 21 consecutive days of treatment followed by a 7-day treatment break.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be swallowed whole with water and may be taken with or without food.
- **Duration of Treatment:** Therapy should continue until the patient no longer derives clinical benefit or develops unacceptable toxicity.
- **Missed Doses:** If a dose is missed, it may be taken up to 12 hours before the next scheduled dose.
- **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after administration, an additional dose should not be taken that day; treatment should resume the following day as prescribed.
- **Dose Modifications:** 
    - Reductions should occur in 20 mg increments (from 60 mg to 40 mg, then to 20 mg). 
    - Once a dose is reduced, it should not be re-escalated. 
    - General guidance for Grade 2 (intolerable) or Grade 3/4 toxicities involves treatment interruption, then restarting at a lower dose upon recovery to Grade ≤1.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to cobimetinib or any of its excipients (including lactose monohydrate).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haemorrhage:** Major haemorrhagic events, including cerebral and gastrointestinal bleeding, have occurred. Use with caution in patients with brain metastases or those on concomitant anticoagulants/antiplatelets.
- **Serous Retinopathy:** Fluid accumulation within retinal layers (chorioretinopathy/retinal detachment) is a known risk of MEK inhibitors. Perform ophthalmologic examination for new/worsening visual disturbances. Withhold treatment if diagnosed; resume only after improvement to Grade ≤1.
- **Left Ventricular Dysfunction:** Reductions in LVEF have been reported. Evaluate LVEF prior to treatment, one month after initiation, and every 3 months thereafter.
- **Liver Laboratory Abnormalities:** Increases in ALT, AST, and ALP are common. Monitor LFTs before initiation, monthly during treatment, or more frequently if indicated.
- **Rhabdomyolysis and CPK Elevations:** Asymptomatic and symptomatic elevations in creatine phosphokinase (CPK) have occurred. Monitor CPK and creatinine levels monthly. If rhabdomyolysis is diagnosed, interrupt treatment and monitor CPK until resolution.
- **Dermatological Reactions:** Severe rash and photosensitivity may occur. Patients should be advised to use broad-spectrum UVA/UVB protection and protective clothing.
- **Lactose Intolerance:** Each tablet contains 36 mg of lactose monohydrate.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are based on the combination of cobimetinib and vemurafenib in clinical trials.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea (60%), Nausea (41%), Vomiting (24%), Stomatitis.
- **Skin:** Photosensitivity (47%), Rash (40%), Pruritus (20.6%), Dermatitis acneiform (14%).
- **Systemic:** Pyrexia (28%), Peripheral oedema (15.3%), Hypertension (15%), Chills (10%).
- **Laboratory/Investigational:** Increased CPK (70%), Increased ALT/AST/ALP/GGT, Anaemia, Lymphopenia.

### Common (1–10%)
- **Ocular:** Chorioretinopathy (13%), Vision blurred (10%), Retinal detachment (9%).
- **Metabolic:** Dehydration, Hyponatremia, Hyperglycaemia, Hypophosphatemia.
- **Vascular/Respiratory:** Haemorrhage (various sites), Pneumonitis.
- **Neoplasms:** Basal cell carcinoma.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Major Haemorrhage:** Including cerebral haemorrhage.
- **Cardiac:** Significant LVEF decrease.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Rhabdomyolysis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A Inhibitors:** Cobimetinib is a CYP3A substrate. Co-administration with potent CYP3A inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole) can increase cobimetinib AUC by ~7-fold. Avoid potent or moderate CYP3A inhibitors.
- **CYP3A Inducers:** Avoid co-administration with potent or moderate CYP3A inducers; these are expected to significantly decrease cobimetinib exposure.
- **Effect on Other Drugs:** 
    - **CYP Substrates:** Cobimetinib does not clinically alter the pharmacokinetics of midazolam (CYP3A) or dextromethorphan (CYP2D6).
    - **Transporters:** Cobimetinib is a substrate of P-gp but not an inhibitor. It is a weak-to-moderate inhibitor of BCRP and weakly inhibits OATP1B1, OATP1B3, and OCT1 in vitro, though the clinical relevance is unknown.
- **Acid-Reducing Agents:** Drugs that increase gastric pH (e.g., proton pump inhibitors) do not affect cobimetinib absorption.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. Not recommended. Animal studies indicate embryolethality and foetal malformations (great vessels and skull). 
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use two effective forms of contraception during treatment and for at least 3 months after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if cobimetinib is excreted in human milk. A risk to the infant cannot be excluded; a decision must be made to discontinue breastfeeding or the drug.
- **Fertility:** Effect on human fertility is unknown. Degenerative changes in reproductive tissues were noted in animal studies (rats/dogs).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established. A trial in patients aged 2-17 showed no new safety signals but exposure was lower than in adults.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for patients ≥65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild-to-moderate renal impairment (CrCl 30–89 mL/min). Data are insufficient for patients with severe renal impairment; use with caution.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended for patients with any degree of hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh A, B, or C). 

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Cobimetinib is a selective, allosteric inhibitor of MEK1 and MEK2. These kinases are upstream regulators of the MAPK pathway, which promotes cell proliferation. Combining cobimetinib with vemurafenib (a BRAF inhibitor) provides more potent inhibition of MAPK reactivation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median Tmax of 2.4 hours; absolute bioavailability 45.9%. No food effect.
    - **Distribution:** 94.8% plasma protein bound. Volume of distribution is ~806 L in cancer patients.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A oxidation and UGT2B7 glucuronidation.
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is 43.6 hours. Excreted primarily in faeces (94% recovery within 17 days).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** No clinically significant effect on QT interval prolongation.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **coBRIM (Study GO28141):** A Phase III trial comparing cobimetinib + vemurafenib vs. placebo + vemurafenib in BRAF V600-positive metastatic melanoma.
    - **Primary Outcome:** Median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) was 9.9 months for the combination vs. 6.2 months for vemurafenib alone (HR 0.51; p < 0.0001). 
    - **Overall Survival:** 22.3 months (combination) vs. 17.4 months (monotherapy).
- **BRIM7 (Study NO25395):** Phase Ib study showing an objective response rate (ORR) of 87% in BRAF inhibitor-naïve patients and 15% in patients who had previously progressed on vemurafenib.
