# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the prescribing information for levocarnitine injection, intended for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for regional legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP). It is a carrier molecule essential for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. 
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not specified in provided source).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administration must be via the intravenous route [US].
- Parenteral products should be visually inspected for discoloration or particulate matter prior to use [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration Method:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and must not be less frequent than every 6 hours [US].
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily maintenance typically remains at 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administered as a 2 to 3 minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma carnitine levels. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment if plasma concentrations are high [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to therapy [US].
- Recommended monitoring includes weekly and monthly assessment of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- No known contraindications are specified in the available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and laryngeal edema, have been reported [US].
- These may occur within minutes of administration, particularly in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Treatment should be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].

### Neurologic Precautions (Seizures)
- Seizures have been reported in patients receiving levocarnitine, including those with and without a prior history of seizure activity [US].
- In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].

### General Precautions
- The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Effect (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, pharyngitis, rhinitis |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common; less frequent effects include body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concurrently with warfarin [US].
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not formally categorised by letter in the source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of fetal harm or impaired fertility. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after administration, the risks of excess carnitine intake in a human infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug should be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight as described in the Dosage and Administration section [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety of high-dose *oral* carnitine in renal failure is not established due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical data from 9-week and 24-week studies in ESRD patients undergoing hemodialysis demonstrated that intravenous doses of 20 mg/kg significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations compared to baseline. Trough levels remained stable or increased over the trial periods [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the specific effect of supplemental carnitine on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Preservation:** Formulation contains no preservatives; any unused portion of a single-dose vial must be discarded [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in provided source.
