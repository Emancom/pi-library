# Editorial Product Information: Crizotinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for specific legal and regulatory requirements. At the time of this publication, documents from the US (FDA), European (EMA), and other agencies were not provided for comparison.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Crizotinib (AAN). 
- **Chemical Class:** Aminopyridine-based inhibitor of receptor tyrosine kinases.
- **Brand Name:** **XALKORI** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01ED01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Hard gelatin capsules in 200 mg (white opaque body, pink opaque cap) and 250 mg (pink opaque body and cap) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **ALK-positive NSCLC:** Treatment of patients with anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK)-positive advanced non-small cell lung cancer [Australian].
- **ROS1-positive NSCLC:** Treatment of patients with ROS1-positive advanced non-small cell lung cancer [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** 250 mg taken orally twice daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Capsules must be swallowed whole and may be taken with or without food. Treatment should continue as long as clinical benefit is maintained [Australian].
- **Testing Requirements:** Use of an accurate and validated assay for ALK or ROS1 status is mandatory for patient selection [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered unless it is less than 6 hours before the next scheduled dose. Do not double doses [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustments for Toxicity:** 
  - **First reduction:** 200 mg twice daily.
  - **Second reduction:** 250 mg once daily.
  - **Discontinuation:** Permanently stop if the 250 mg once-daily dose cannot be tolerated [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to crizotinib or any excipients used in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Drug-induced hepatotoxicity with fatal outcomes has occurred (0.1%). Monitor LFTs (ALT, AST, bilirubin) every 2 weeks for the first 2 months, then monthly [Australian].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)/Pneumonitis:** Severe or fatal ILD has been reported (2%). Monitor for pulmonary symptoms and permanently discontinue if treatment-related ILD is diagnosed [Australian].
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** Caution in patients with a history of or predisposition to QTc prolongation. Monitor electrolytes and ECGs in at-risk patients [Australian].
- **Bradycardia:** Usually asymptomatic but can be symptomatic. Avoid use with other bradycardic agents (e.g., beta-blockers, certain calcium channel blockers, digoxin) [Australian].
- **Cardiac Failure:** Severe and fatal cases have been reported; monitor for signs and symptoms of heart failure [Australian].
- **Visual Effects:** Common (63%). Severe visual loss (best corrected vision < 6/60) requires treatment discontinuation and ophthalmological evaluation [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Use with caution in patients with risks such as diverticulitis or GI metastases [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on a dataset of 1722 patients [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Drug Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Vision disorder (63%), Nausea (57%), Diarrhoea (54%), Vomiting (51%), Oedema (47%), Constipation (43%), Elevated transaminases (32%), Fatigue (30%), Decreased appetite (30%), Dizziness (26%), Neuropathy (25%), Neutropenia (22%), Dysgeusia (21%), Leucopenia (15%), Bradycardia (13%), Rash (13%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Increased blood creatinine (8%), Dyspepsia (8%), Increased alkaline phosphatase (7%), Prolonged QT (4%), Renal cyst (3%), Syncope (3%), Interstitial lung disease (3%), Oesophagitis (2%), Decreased testosterone (2%), Cardiac failure (1%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hepatic failure (<1%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Hepatotoxicity, ILD/pneumonitis, QT prolongation, and cardiac failure are highlighted as the most significant clinical risks [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
  - **CYP3A Inhibitors:** Coadministration with strong CYP3A inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin) increases crizotinib exposure and should be avoided. Avoid grapefruit/grapefruit juice [Australian].
  - **CYP3A Inducers:** Coadministration with strong CYP3A inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St. John’s wort) significantly decreases crizotinib exposure and should be avoided [Australian].
  - **CYP3A Substrates:** Crizotinib is a moderate inhibitor of CYP3A. Exercise caution or avoid coadministration with CYP3A substrates that have narrow therapeutic indices (e.g., alfentanil, fentanyl, cyclosporin, sirolimus, ergotamine) [Australian].
  - **Transporters:** Crizotinib inhibits P-gp, OCT1, and OCT2 *in vitro*, potentially increasing concentrations of their substrates [Australian].
- **Gastric pH:** While crizotinib solubility is pH-dependent, coadministration with esomeprazole did not result in a clinically meaningful change in exposure [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. May cause fetal harm. Reduced fetal body weights were seen in animal studies. 
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 90 days after the final dose. Male patients with pregnant partners or partners of childbearing potential must use condoms [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if crizotinib is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggest potential impairment of both male and female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy compared to younger patients; no starting dose adjustment required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
  - **Mild/Moderate (CLcr 30–90 mL/min):** No starting dose adjustment [Australian].
  - **Severe (CLcr <30 mL/min) not requiring dialysis:** Reduce dose to 250 mg once daily. May increase to 200 mg twice daily after 4 weeks based on tolerability [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
  - **Mild:** No starting dose adjustment required [Australian].
  - **Moderate:** Consider starting dose of 200 mg twice daily [Australian].
  - **Severe:** Starting dose should not exceed 250 mg once daily [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective small-molecule inhibitor of ALK, HGFR (c-Met), ROS1, and RON receptor tyrosine kinases. It inhibits phosphorylation and modulates kinase-dependent phenotypes, inducing apoptosis in tumour cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability 43%. Peak plasma concentrations in 4–6 hours. Steady state reached in 15 days [Australian].
  - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (91%). Volume of distribution is 1,772 L [Australian].
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4/5 [Australian].
  - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 42 hours. Primary excretion is via faeces (63%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **First-line ALK-positive NSCLC (Study 1014):** A Phase 3 trial showed crizotinib was superior to platinum-based chemotherapy. Median PFS was 10.9 months for crizotinib vs 7.0 months for chemotherapy (HR 0.45, p<0.0001). ORR was 74% vs 45% [Australian].
- **Previously treated ALK-positive NSCLC (Study 1007):** A Phase 3 trial compared crizotinib to docetaxel or pemetrexed. Median PFS was 7.7 months for crizotinib vs 3.0 months for chemotherapy (HR 0.49, p<0.0001) [Australian].
- **ROS1-positive NSCLC (Study 1001):** A single-arm study showed an ORR of 72% and a median duration of response of 24.7 months [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Medicine)** [Australian].
