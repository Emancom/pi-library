# Editorial Product Information: Carbimazole

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of carbimazole, synthesising official product information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for the most current legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Carbimazole (an imidazole antithyroid agent).
- **Brand Names:** Neo-Mercazole [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H03BA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 5 mg tablets (pale pink, circular, biconvex) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amdipharm Mercury (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hyperthyroidism:** Used as definitive therapy to induce permanent remission in primary or secondary thyrotoxicosis [Australian].
- **Surgical Preparation:** Pre-operative management prior to thyroidectomy [Australian].
- **Adjunctive Therapy:** Treatment before and after radioactive iodine administration [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
The objective of therapy is to achieve a euthyroid state quickly and then titrate down to a maintenance dose.

- **Initial Dosing:** 
    - **Mild cases:** 15 mg to 20 mg daily in divided doses [Australian].
    - **Moderate cases:** 30 mg daily in divided doses [Australian].
    - **Severe cases:** 40 mg to 45 mg daily, with a maximum of 60 mg daily in divided doses [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dosing:** Once symptoms are controlled, the dose should be reduced to 10 mg to 15 mg daily. Treatment typically continues for 12 months up to 2 years to secure remission [Australian].

### Special Administration Instructions
- **Surgical Preparation:** Carbimazole should be administered until the patient is euthyroid. For the final two weeks preceding a thyroidectomy, it should be co-administered with iodide [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Patients should be monitored monthly for the first year, then every 3 to 6 months [Australian].

### Missed Doses
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to carbimazole, its active metabolite thiamazole (methimazole), or any excipients (e.g., lactose, sucrose) [Australian].
- History of acute pancreatitis following administration of carbimazole or thiamazole [Australian].
- Serious pre-existing haematological conditions [Australian].
- Severe hepatic insufficiency [Australian].
- Retrosternal goitre [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Agranulocytosis:** Fatal cases have been reported. Patients must be warned to stop the medication and seek immediate medical attention for sore throat, fever, malaise, mouth ulcers, or bruising. Blood cell counts are required immediately if infection is suspected [Australian].
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** If pancreatitis occurs, carbimazole must be stopped immediately and permanently avoided in that patient [Australian].
- **Vasculitis:** ANCA-positive vasculitis has been reported, involving the skin (leukocytoclastic vasculitis), kidneys, lungs, and CNS. Therapy must be discontinued if vasculitis is suspected [Australian].
- **Hepatic Monitoring:** Therapy should be ceased if signs of liver dysfunction (anorexia, pruritus, upper abdominal pain) occur [Australian].
- **Tracheal Obstruction:** Caution is required in patients with intrathoracic goitre; high doses may increase thyroid size and worsen airway obstruction [Australian].
- **Patient Compliance:** Not recommended for patients unable to comply with regular monitoring or instructions [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
All toxic reactions generally occur within the first 8 weeks of treatment; reactions are less common at doses $\leq$ 20 mg daily.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** | Nausea, headache, arthralgia, mild gastric distress, mild skin rashes, pruritus. |
| **Rare** | Pancytopaenia, aplastic anaemia. |
| **Very Rare** | Haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopaenia, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, generalised dermatitis. |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Acute pancreatitis, insulin autoimmune syndrome (severe hypoglycemia), myopathy (monitor CPK), hair loss, loss of sense of taste, neuritis, cholestatic jaundice, hepatitis. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Bone marrow depression (neutropaenia, agranulocytosis), ANCA-positive vasculitis, and multi-system hypersensitivity (affecting lungs, liver, and kidneys) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants:** Carbimazole acts as a vitamin K antagonist; its use may intensify the effects of oral anticoagulants. Conversely, as hyperthyroidism resolves to a euthyroid state, anticoagulant requirements may change [Australian].
- **Theophylline:** Serum levels may increase and lead to toxicity as the patient becomes euthyroid unless the dose is reduced [Australian].
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** Serum levels may increase as the metabolic rate normalises; dose reduction may be necessary [Australian].
- **Beta-blockers:** Clearance of beta-blockers with a high extraction ratio may decrease as thyrotoxicosis is treated, necessitating dose adjustment [Australian].
- **Prednisolone:** Clearance may be increased when co-administered with carbimazole [Australian].
- **Erythromycin:** Carbimazole may inhibit the metabolism of erythromycin, leading to reduced clearance [Australian].
- **Cross-allergy:** There is a risk of cross-allergy between carbimazole, thiamazole, and propylthiouracil [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** Carbimazole is suspected to cause congenital malformations, particularly in the first trimester. 
    - **Risks:** Aplasia cutis congenita, craniofacial defects (choanal atresia), exomphalos, and ventricular septal defects.
    - **Management:** Use only if the benefit outweighs the risk and propylthiouracil is unsuitable. Use the lowest effective dose; avoid additional thyroid hormone administration. In the final trimester, doses should ideally not exceed 15 mg twice daily. Carbimazole should be discontinued 3–4 weeks before delivery [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Carbimazole is concentrated in breast milk [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No specific data available in the source PI [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No special dosage regimen is required, though there is a reported higher risk of fatal neutrophil dyscrasia in patients aged 65 and over [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in mild-to-moderate impairment. Contraindicated in severe hepatic insufficiency [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Carbimazole is a pro-drug. It blocks the organic binding of iodine by inhibiting the iodination of tyrosine and inhibits the peroxidase enzyme. It does not interfere with the uptake of iodine by the thyroid [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed from the GI tract.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly and completely converted to the active metabolite, methimazole (thiamazole).
    - **Distribution:** Crosses the placenta and enters breast milk.
    - **Elimination:** Peak plasma concentration of methimazole occurs 1 hour post-dose. Plasma half-life is 3–6 hours. Primarily excreted in the urine [Australian].
