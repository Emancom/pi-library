# Editorial Product Information: Ceftobiprole

## Editorial Note
- The information contained in this editorial is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ceftobiprole, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ceftobiprole medocaril sodium [US].
- **Chemical Class:** Cephalosporin antibacterial [US].
- **Brand Name:** **ZEVTERA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilized powder for reconstitution in single-dose vials containing 667 mg of ceftobiprole medocaril sodium (equivalent to 500 mg of ceftobiprole) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Distributed by La Jolla Pharmaceutical Company [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**ZEVTERA** is indicated for the treatment of the following infections:
- **Staphylococcus aureus bloodstream infections (bacteremia) (SAB):** Including adult patients with right-sided infective endocarditis caused by methicillin-susceptible (MSSA) and methicillin-resistant (MRSA) isolates [US-approved].
- **Acute bacterial skin and skin structure infections (ABSSSI):** In adult patients caused by susceptible isolates of *S. aureus* (MSSA/MRSA), *Streptococcus pyogenes*, and *Klebsiella pneumoniae* [US-approved].
- **Community-acquired bacterial pneumonia (CABP):** In adult and pediatric patients (aged 3 months to <18 years) caused by susceptible isolates of *S. aureus* (MSSA), *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Haemophilus parainfluenzae*, *Escherichia coli*, and *Klebsiella pneumoniae* [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing [US]
Administer via intravenous infusion over 2 hours at a concentration of 2.67 mg/mL.
- **SAB:** 667 mg every 6 hours (Days 1 to 8), then 667 mg every 8 hours (from Day 9). Total duration: up to 42 days.
- **ABSSSI:** 667 mg every 8 hours. Duration: 5 to 14 days.
- **CABP:** 667 mg every 8 hours. Duration: 5 to 14 days.

### Pediatric Dosing (CABP only) [US]
Administer via intravenous infusion over 2 hours.
- **12 to <18 years:** 13.3 mg/kg (max 667 mg) every 8 hours. Concentration: 2.67 mg/mL.
- **3 months to <12 years:** 20 mg/kg (max 667 mg) every 8 hours. Concentration: 5.33 mg/mL.
- **Duration:** 7 to 14 days.

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with 10 mL of Sterile Water for Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection [US].
- **Dilution:** Must be further diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Dextrose (see Section 12 for storage of diluted solutions) [US].
- **Incompatibilities:** Must NOT be mixed with calcium-containing solutions. Do not co-administer through the same line as other drug products [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known history of severe hypersensitivity to ceftobiprole or other members of the cephalosporin class [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Increased Mortality in VABP:** An increase in mortality was observed in a subgroup of patients with ventilator-associated bacterial pneumonia (VABP) in clinical trials. **ZEVTERA** is not approved for VABP [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reactions and serious skin reactions may occur. Discontinue immediately if a reaction is suspected [US].
- **Seizures and CNS Reactions:** Cephalosporins have been associated with seizures, encephalopathy, and myoclonia. Risk is increased in patients with a history of epilepsy or renal impairment. If CNS reactions occur, evaluate for discontinuation [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all systemic antibacterials; severity can range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis [US].
- **Drug-Resistant Bacteria:** Prescribing without a proven/strongly suspected infection increases the risk of developing resistant organisms [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (Adults)
Frequencies vary by indication in clinical trials [US]:
- **SAB ($\ge$4%):** Anemia, nausea, hypokalemia, vomiting, increased hepatic enzymes/bilirubin, diarrhea, increased blood creatinine, hypertension, leukopenia, pyrexia.
- **ABSSSI ($\ge$2%):** Nausea, diarrhea, headache, injection site reaction, rash, vomiting, dysgeusia.
- **CABP ($\ge$2%):** Nausea, increased hepatic enzymes, vomiting, diarrhea, headache, rash, insomnia, abdominal pain, phlebitis, hypertension, dizziness.

### Common Adverse Reactions (Pediatrics, CABP)
- **Frequencies $\ge$2%:** Vomiting, headache, increased hepatic enzymes, diarrhea, infusion site reaction, phlebitis, pyrexia [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Seizures and status epilepticus.
- *C. difficile* infection.
- Agranulocytosis and aplastic anemia (reported post-marketing outside the US) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pitavastatin:** Co-administration decreased pitavastatin $C_{max}$ (22%) and $AUC$ (14%). Ceftobiprole is an inhibitor of OATP1B1 and OATP1B3 [US].
- **Drug-Laboratory Test Interactions:**
    - **Dipstick Tests:** May result in false-positive results for urine protein, ketones, or occult blood [US].
    - **Serological Testing:** Potential for a positive direct Coombs' test [US].
    - **Urinary Glucose:** May cause false-positive results with certain tests [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** There are no available data in pregnant women to evaluate drug-associated risks. Animal studies (rats/monkeys) showed no evidence of fetal malformations at doses near the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data are available on the presence of ceftobiprole in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. In rats, ceftobiprole is excreted in milk at low concentrations (approx. 20% of maternal plasma levels) [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility or early fetal development was observed in rats at doses up to 1.4 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
### Pediatric Use
- Approved for CABP in patients $\ge$3 months. Safety and effectiveness not established for CABP <3 months, or for SAB/ABSSSI in any pediatric age group [US].

### Geriatric Use
- No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between patients $\ge$65 years and younger patients. Dose adjustment should be based on renal function [US].

### Renal Impairment [US]
Dose adjustments are required for patients with $CL_{CR} < 50$ mL/min.

| Indication | $CL_{CR}$ (mL/min) | Adult Dose | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SAB** | 30 to <50 | 667 mg | Q8H (Days 1-8), then Q12H |
| | 15 to <30 | 333 mg | Q8H (Days 1-8), then Q12H |
| | <15 (inc. HD) | 333 mg | Q24H |
| **ABSSSI/CABP**| 30 to <50 | 667 mg | Q12H |
| | 15 to <30 | 333 mg | Q12H |
| | <15 (inc. HD) | 333 mg | Q24H |

- **Augmented Renal Clearance ($CL_{CR} > 150$ mL/min):** Increase dose frequency to 667 mg every 6 hours [US].
- **Hemodialysis:** Administer dose *after* the completion of the dialysis session [US].

### Hepatic Impairment
- The effect of hepatic impairment on ceftobiprole pharmacokinetics is unknown [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal action via inhibition of bacterial cell wall synthesis. Ceftobiprole binds with high affinity to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs), including PBP2a in MRSA and PBP2x/2b in penicillin-resistant *S. pneumoniae* [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** 16% protein bound; Vss is 18 L [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Minimally metabolized; ceftobiprole medocaril is a prodrug rapidly converted to the active moiety ceftobiprole [US].
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal (83% unchanged in urine). Half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is approximately 3.3 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SAB (Trial 1):** A randomized, double-blind trial compared ceftobiprole to daptomycin (plus optional aztreonam). Overall success at Day 70 was 69.8% for ceftobiprole vs 68.7% for the comparator [US].
- **ABSSSI (Trial 2):** Compared ceftobiprole to vancomycin plus aztreonam. Early clinical response (48-72h) was 91.3% for ceftobiprole vs 88.1% for the comparator [US].
- **CABP (Trial 3):** Compared ceftobiprole to ceftriaxone (plus optional linezolid). Clinical cure rates at test-of-cure were 76.4% for ceftobiprole vs 79.3% for the comparator [US].
- **Pediatric CABP (Trial 5):** A safety-focused study in patients 3 months to <18 years confirmed similar clinical response rates to standard-of-care cephalosporins [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Keep in the original carton to protect from light [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** If not diluted immediately, may be stored refrigerated for up to 24 hours or at room temperature for up to 1 hour [US].
- **Diluted Infusion Solution:**
    - **2.67 mg/mL (in NS or D5W):** Stable for 6 hours at 25°C or 94 hours at 2°C to 8°C [US].
    - **5.33 mg/mL (in D5W):** Stable for 6 hours at 25°C or 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C [US].
- **Do not freeze.** [US].

- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison schedule/classification not specified in terms of S-ratings used in other jurisdictions [US].
