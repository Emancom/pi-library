# Editorial Product Information: Ciprofloxacin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** To provide a harmonised editorial overview of ciprofloxacin for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ciprofloxacin hydrochloride (a synthetic carboxyquinolone derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Fluoroquinolone antibiotic.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Ciprofloxacin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01MA02 (Not explicitly stated in source; provided for clinical reference).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Tablets:** 250 mg, 500 mg, and 750 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Ciprofloxacin is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by susceptible organisms in the following conditions [Australian]:
- **Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs):** Including complicated and uncomplicated cases.
- **Respiratory Tract:** Bronchial infections. Note: Not recommended for *Streptococcus pneumoniae* due to inadequate efficacy [Australian].
- **Sexually Transmitted Infections:** Gonorrhoeal urethritis and cervicitis.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastroenteritis (infectious diarrhoea).
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections.**
- **Bone and Joint Infections.**
- **Prostatitis:** Chronic bacterial prostatitis of mild to moderate severity.
- **Inhalational Anthrax (Post-exposure):** To reduce disease incidence or progression following exposure to aerosolized *Bacillus anthracis*.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Urinary Tract Infections:** 250 mg every 12 hours. For complicated infections or less susceptible organisms (e.g., *Enterococcus faecalis*), 500 mg every 12 hours [Australian].
- **Bronchial, Skin, and Skin Structure Infections:** 500 mg every 12 hours. Severe/complicated cases: 750 mg every 12 hours [Australian].
- **Bone and Joint Infections:** 750 mg every 12 hours [Australian].
- **Gastroenteritis:** 500 mg every 12 hours [Australian].
- **Acute Uncomplicated Gonorrhoeal Urethritis:** 250 mg as a single dose [Australian].
- **Chronic Bacterial Prostatitis:** 250 mg to 500 mg every 12 hours [Australian].
- **Inhalational Anthrax (Post-exposure):** 500 mg every 12 hours for 60 days [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed with liquid.
- **Chelation:** Avoid administration with multivalent cation-containing products (e.g., calcium, magnesium, aluminium, iron), antacids, or highly buffered drugs (e.g., certain antiretrovirals). Administer ciprofloxacin 1–2 hours before or at least 4 hours after these agents [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Take as soon as remembered. If nearly time for the next dose, skip the missed dose. Do **not** double the dose to compensate [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to ciprofloxacin, other quinolones (including nalidixic acid), or any excipients [Australian].
- **Tizanidine Co-administration:** Concurrent use is strictly contraindicated due to the risk of severe hypotension and sedation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Serious Disabling Reactions):** Fluoroquinolones are associated with disabling and potentially irreversible serious adverse reactions involving the nervous system, musculoskeletal system, and psychiatric health. Discontinue the drug immediately at the first sign of these reactions [Australian].
- **Tendonitis and Tendon Rupture:** Risk is increased in the elderly, those with renal impairment, solid organ transplant recipients, and patients on concomitant corticosteroids. Discontinue if pain or inflammation occurs [Australian].
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects:** May cause tremors, restlessness, confusion, and very rarely, seizures. Use with extreme caution in patients with known CNS disorders (e.g., epilepsy, severe cerebral arteriosclerosis) [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Reactions:** Risk of toxic psychosis, hallucinations, depression, and suicidal ideation/behaviour, sometimes occurring after the first dose [Australian].
- **Cardiac Disorders:** Associated with QT interval prolongation. Avoid in patients with risk factors like hypokalaemia, bradycardia, or concomitant use of Class IA/III antiarrhythmics [Australian].
- **Aortic Aneurysm and Dissection:** Epidemiologic studies show an increased risk, especially in the elderly. Use only after careful benefit-risk assessment in at-risk patients [Australian].
- **Dysglycaemia:** Risk of both hypoglycaemia and hyperglycaemia, particularly in elderly diabetic patients on oral hypoglycaemic agents or insulin [Australian].
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhoea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibiotics; severity can range from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data
- **Common (1–10%):** Nausea, diarrhoea [Australian].
- **Uncommon to Rare (<1%):** Agitation, vomiting, rash, restlessness, tremor, headache, dizziness, pruritus, vaginitis, abdominal pain, bad taste, palpitations, light-headedness, and fever [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Musculoskeletal:** Achilles tendon rupture, arthralgia.
- **Neurological:** Convulsive seizures, peripheral neuropathy (paresthesia, hypoesthesia), increased intracranial pressure.
- **Psychiatric:** Psychotic reactions, suicidal ideation.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Hepatic necrosis, life-threatening hepatic failure.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylactic shock, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN).
- **Vascular:** Aortic aneurysm/dissection rupture.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Tizanidine:** Contraindicated (CYP1A2 inhibition leads to increased tizanidine levels) [Australian].
- **Theophylline:** Concomitant use can lead to serious and fatal reactions (cardiac arrest, seizures) due to elevated theophylline levels. Monitor serum levels [Australian].
- **Cations/Antacids:** Significant reduction in absorption due to chelation [Australian].
- **Vitamin K Antagonists (e.g., Warfarin):** May augment anticoagulant effects; monitor INR frequently [Australian].
- **NSAIDs:** High doses of quinolones combined with some NSAIDs may provoke convulsions [Australian].
- **Methotrexate:** Inhibition of renal tubular transport may increase methotrexate toxicity [Australian].
- **Duloxetine/Ropinirole/Clozapine:** Increased plasma concentrations of these drugs when co-administered [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Reproduction studies in animals showed no teratogenicity, but quinolones are known to cause arthropathy in immature animals. Use only if potential benefit outweighs potential fetal risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Ciprofloxacin is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or avoid the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended in pre-pubertal children due to the risk of arthropathy in weight-bearing joints, **except** for inhalational anthrax (post-exposure). Anthrax dose: 15 mg/kg per dose (max 500 mg) every 12 hours [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of tendon disorders and QT prolongation. Use caution and adjust for renal function [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustments required based on creatinine clearance (CrCl) [Australian]:
    - **CrCl 31–60 mL/min/1.73m²:** Max oral dose 1000 mg/day.
    - **CrCl ≤30 mL/min/1.73m²:** Max oral dose 500 mg/day.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dose adjustments provided, but use with caution due to reports of hepatic necrosis [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal agent that inhibits DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II) and topoisomerase IV, interfering with bacterial DNA replication, transcription, and repair [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (bioavailability ~70%). Peak plasma levels reached in 1–2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed with low protein binding (20–40%).
    - **Metabolism:** Partially metabolised into four metabolites with less activity than the parent drug.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (40–50% as unchanged drug). Elimination half-life is approximately 4 hours in normal renal function [Australian].
