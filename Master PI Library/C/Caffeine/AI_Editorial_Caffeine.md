# Editorial Product Information: Caffeine Citrate

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical practice in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Caffeine citrate.
- **Chemical Class:** Methylxanthine.
- **Brand Names:** **Cafnea** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06BC01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 40 mg/2 mL (20 mg/mL) caffeine citrate, equivalent to 10 mg/mL caffeine base [Australian].
    - **Oral Solution:** 25 mg/5 mL (5 mg/mL) caffeine citrate, equivalent to 2.5 mg/mL caffeine base [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (infusion) and Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Phebra Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Apnoea of Prematurity:** Indicated for the short-term treatment of apnoea of prematurity (AOP) in infants with a gestational age of 28 to less than 33 weeks [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Neonatal)
Dosing is expressed as caffeine citrate. **Note:** The dose of caffeine base is half the dose of caffeine citrate (e.g., 20 mg caffeine citrate = 10 mg caffeine base).

- **Loading Dose:** 20 mg/kg body weight administered via intravenous syringe infusion pump over 30 minutes.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 5 mg/kg once daily. Maintenance begins 24 hours after the loading dose.
- **Dose Adjustment:** If apnoea persists, the maintenance dose may be increased to a maximum of 10 mg/kg once daily. Doses should be adjusted weekly based on changes in body weight [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Intravenous:** Maintenance doses should be infused over 10 minutes.
- **Oral:** Administered via Cafnea Oral Solution once the infant is tolerating full enteral feeds.
- **Preparation:** Solutions are preservative-free and intended for single use in one patient only. Discard any remaining residue [Australian].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible with 0.9% sodium chloride solution [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a known history of hypersensitivity to caffeine or citrate [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Differential Diagnosis:** Prior to therapy, other causes of apnoea (e.g., sepsis, anaemia, CNS disorders, metabolic disturbances, or obstructive apnoea) must be ruled out or treated [Australian].
- **Seizure Disorders:** Caffeine is a CNS stimulant; use with caution in infants with known seizure disorders, as overdoses have been associated with seizures [Australian].
- **Necrotising Enterocolitis (NEC):** Clinical trials have noted the development of NEC in treated neonates. Monitor patients carefully for clinical signs of NEC [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Caffeine increases heart rate, stroke volume, and left ventricular output. Use with caution in infants with underlying cardiovascular disease [Australian].
- **Gastro-oesophageal Reflux:** Caffeine may increase gastric acid secretion and relax the lower oesophageal sphincter, potentially exacerbating gastro-oesophageal reflux [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Baseline serum caffeine levels should be measured if the mother consumed significant caffeine prior to delivery. In cases of hepatic or renal impairment, serum levels must be monitored to avoid toxicity [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies reported in controlled clinical trials (e.g., Erenberg et al.) compare caffeine citrate against placebo:

| Adverse Event | Caffeine Citrate (%) | Placebo (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Constipation | 17.4 | 20.5 |
| Injection site reaction | 8.7 | 12.8 |
| Rash | 8.7 | 7.7 |
| Perinatal disorder (feeding intolerance) | 8.7 | 5.1 |
| Anaemia | 6.5 | 17.9 |
| Gastrointestinal (GERD, dilated bowel) | 4.3 | 7.7 |

**Other reported effects (frequency not specified):**
- **CNS:** Irritability, restlessness, jitteriness, tremors, and unexplained seizures.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, tachypnoea.
- **Metabolic:** Hypoglycaemia, hyperglycaemia, and temporary reduction in weight gain.
- **Renal:** Increased urine flow, increased creatinine clearance, and increased excretion of sodium and calcium [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP1A2 Interactions:** Caffeine is primarily metabolised by CYP1A2. Potential interactions exist with drugs that inhibit or are substrates for this enzyme. 
- **Decreased Caffeine Elimination:** Documented in adults with co-administration of mexiletine, cimetidine, fluvoxamine, enoxacin, ciprofloxacin, and fluconazole [Australian].
- **Increased Caffeine Elimination:** Co-administration with phenytoin may increase caffeine clearance [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Antagonism:** Caffeine antagonises the sedative effects of benzodiazepines.
- **Other Methylxanthines:** Concurrent use of theophylline or aminophylline is not recommended [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category: Not applicable/Not specified]. Note: Caffeine readily crosses the placenta.
- **Breastfeeding:** [Australian: Not applicable]. If mothers are consuming caffeine-containing fluids, this must be considered when determining the neonate's dose, as caffeine is excreted in breast milk.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies suggest neonatal exposure does not pose a hazard to future fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated specifically for pre-term infants (gestational age 28 to <33 weeks) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. Serum caffeine monitoring and dose adjustments are required to prevent toxicity [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution. Serum caffeine monitoring and dose adjustments are required [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Not applicable for the indicated condition [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Caffeine is a centrally acting respiratory stimulant. It increases respiratory rate, reduces the frequency of apnoeic episodes, and may increase lung compliance in ventilator-dependent infants [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases heart rate and may increase cerebral blood flow in some cases. It does not significantly change mean arterial blood pressure [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and complete after oral administration; Tmax 30 mins to 2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributed; Volume of distribution (Vd) 0.8–0.9 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal in preterm infants; approximately 3–8% converted to theophylline. CYP1A2 pathways do not mature until approximately 4 months postnatal age.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted unchanged in urine (>85%). In preterm neonates, the half-life is significantly prolonged (80–120 hours) compared to adults [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy Study (Erenberg et al.):** A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of 87 preterm infants (28–32 weeks). Caffeine citrate (20 mg/kg loading, 5 mg/kg maintenance) was significantly more effective than placebo in achieving a 50% reduction in apnoeic episodes (68.9% vs 43.2%) and eliminating apnoea within 5 days (24.4% vs 0%) [Australian].
- **Safety Study (Schmidt et al.):** A large multinational study (2006 infants) showed caffeine therapy was associated with reduced rates of cerebral palsy and cognitive delay when assessed at 18–21 months, though it caused a temporary reduction in weight gain [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Sterile and preservative-free. For single use only; discard any unused portion [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
