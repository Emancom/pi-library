# Editorial Product Information: Ceftibuten

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ceftibuten, synthesised for healthcare professionals to facilitate clinical decision-making. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. The clinical details provided herein reflect the evidence and regulatory approvals from the United States (FDA). Other regional PIs (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) were not provided for this synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ceftibuten (as ceftibuten dihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Semisynthetic cephalosporin antibiotic.
- **Brand Names:** **CEDAX** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Capsules:** 400 mg [US].
    - **Oral Suspension:** 90 mg/5 mL (after reconstitution) [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sciele Pharma, Inc. (A Shionogi Company) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Ceftibuten is indicated for the treatment of mild-to-moderate infections caused by susceptible strains of designated microorganisms in the following conditions [US]:
- **Acute Bacterial Exacerbations of Chronic Bronchitis (AECB):** Caused by *Haemophilus influenzae* (including β-lactamase-producing strains), *Moraxella catarrhalis* (including β-lactamase-producing strains), or *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (penicillin-susceptible strains only) [US].
- **Acute Bacterial Otitis Media (ABOM):** Caused by *Haemophilus influenzae* (including β-lactamase-producing strains), *Moraxella catarrhalis* (including β-lactamase-producing strains), or *Streptococcus pyogenes* [US].
- **Pharyngitis and Tonsillitis:** Caused by *Streptococcus pyogenes* [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Adults and Adolescents (≥12 years):** 400 mg once daily for 10 days [US].
- **Pediatric Patients (6 months to 12 years):** 9 mg/kg once daily (maximum of 400 mg per day) for 10 days [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- Dosage remains consistent across approved indications for both adult and pediatric populations, provided the duration is 10 days [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Oral Suspension:** Must be administered at least 2 hours before or 1 hour after a meal [US].
- **Capsules:** While food delays the time to peak concentration and decreases the extent of absorption slightly, the PI does not strictly mandate fasting for the capsule formulation, though it notes bioequivalence to the suspension [US].
- **Reconstitution (Suspension):** The powder should be loosened by tapping the bottle, then water added in two portions, shaking well after each aliquot [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known allergy/hypersensitivity to ceftibuten or the cephalosporin class of antibiotics [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Before initiating therapy, clinicians must verify the patient's history of reactions to cephalosporins, penicillins, or other drugs. Cross-hypersensitivity among beta-lactam antibiotics occurs in up to 10% of penicillin-allergic patients. If an allergic reaction occurs, therapy must be discontinued immediately [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents, including ceftibuten. Severity ranges from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis. Discontinuation of ceftibuten and initiation of targeted *C. difficile* treatment may be required in moderate-to-severe cases [US].
- **Emergence of Resistant Bacteria:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms. 
- **Seizure Risk:** As with other cephalosporins, there is a risk of triggering seizures, particularly in patients with renal impairment where dosage was not appropriately reduced [US].
- **Specific Clinical Precautions:** 
    - Efficacy against *S. pneumoniae* in ABOM was found to be 23% less than comparators in trials; clinicians should use ceftibuten only when adequate coverage against this pathogen has been previously addressed or is not the primary concern [US].
    - Efficacy against *M. catarrhalis* in AECB trials was 22% less than control [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience
Adverse events reported in multiple-dose trials (n=1092 adults; n=772 pediatrics) [US]:

| Frequency | Adults (≥1%) | Pediatric Patients (≥1%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea (4%), Headache (3%), Diarrhea (3%), Dyspepsia (2%), Dizziness (1%), Abdominal pain (1%), Vomiting (1%) | Diarrhea (4%), Vomiting (2%), Abdominal pain (2%), Loose stools (2%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anorexia, constipation, dry mouth, dyspnea, dysuria, fatigue, flatulence, rash, urticaria, vaginitis | Agitation, anorexia, dehydration, diaper dermatitis, dizziness, fever, headache, insomnia, irritability, rash |

*Note: The incidence of diarrhea in pediatric patients ≤2 years old (8%) was higher than in those >2 years old (2%) [US].*

### Serious and Post-marketing Adverse Effects
- **Post-marketing Experience:** Aphasia, jaundice, melena, psychosis, serum sickness-like reactions, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, and toxic epidermal necrolysis [US].
- **Cephalosporin-Class Effects:** Anaphylaxis, drug fever, renal dysfunction, toxic nephropathy, hepatic cholestasis, aplastic anemia, and hemolytic anemia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Theophylline:** Intravenous theophylline pharmacokinetics were not altered by ceftibuten. The effect on oral theophylline is unknown [US].
- **Antacids/H2-Receptors:** A single dose of liquid antacid did not affect bioavailability. However, ranitidine (150 mg twice daily) increased the ceftibuten Cmax by 23% and AUC by 16% [US].
- **Laboratory Test Interactions:** Cephalosporins may induce a false-positive direct Coombs' test. While ceftibuten did not cause positive reactions in vitro at concentrations up to 40 µg/mL, clinicians should be aware of this class potential [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category B.** Animal studies (rats and rabbits) showed no evidence of fetal harm at doses up to 8.6 and 1.5 times the human dose, respectively. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women; use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if ceftibuten is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised when administered to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in rats at oral doses up to 2000 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in infants under 6 months of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics are similar to younger adults, though accumulation may reach 40% at steady state. Dose adjustment should be based on renal function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustment is required for patients with CrCl < 50 mL/min [US]:

| Creatinine Clearance (mL/min) | Recommended Dosing Schedule [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **>50** | 400 mg every 24h (Normal dose) |
| **30–49** | 200 mg every 24h |
| **5–29** | 100 mg every 24h |
| **Hemodialysis** | 400 mg capsule (or 9 mg/kg suspension) administered at the end of each session |

- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ceftibuten is bactericidal. It acts by binding to essential target proteins of the bacterial cell wall, leading to the inhibition of cell-wall synthesis [US].
- **Microbiology:** Stable against most plasmid-mediated beta-lactamases; however, it is unstable against chromosomally-mediated cephalosporinases (e.g., those produced by *Bacteroides, Citrobacter, Enterobacter*) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; Cmax reached in approximately 2–2.6 hours. Bioavailability is affected by food (decreased AUC/Cmax in suspension) [US].
    - **Distribution:** 65% protein bound. Penetration into bronchial secretions and middle-ear fluid is approximately 15–37% and 70% of plasma levels, respectively [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily exists as *cis*-ceftibuten; 10% is converted to the less active *trans*-isomer [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted renally; 95% of radioactivity recovered in urine or feces [US]. Plasma half-life is approximately 2–2.4 hours in healthy adults [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **AECB:** Three trials showed clinical equivalence to comparators. Eradication rates for *H. influenzae* were 73% vs 72% for control. Eradication of *S. pneumoniae* was 66% vs 70% for control [US].
- **ABOM:** Four trials showed equivalence to comparators in overall clinical outcome, though eradication of *S. pneumoniae* was lower (65% vs 88% for control) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Capsules:** Store between 2°C and 25°C (36°F–77°F) [US].
- **Oral Suspension (Powder):** Store between 2°C and 25°C (36°F–77°F) [US].
- **Oral Suspension (Reconstituted):** Stable for 14 days when stored in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C (36°F–46°F) [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified in available sources.
