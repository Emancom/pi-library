# Editorial Product Information: Chlorothiazide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for information purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Chlorothiazide (a benzothiadiazide diuretic).
- **Chemical Class:** Sulfonamide derivative.
- **Brand Names:** **Diuril** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Suspension:** 250 mg per 5 mL (contains 0.4% alcohol) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Salix Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Edema:** As adjunctive therapy for edema associated with:
    - Congestive heart failure.
    - Hepatic cirrhosis.
    - Corticosteroid and estrogen therapy.
    - Renal dysfunction (e.g., nephrotic syndrome, acute glomerulonephritis, and chronic renal failure) [US].
- **Hypertension:** Used as the primary therapeutic agent or as an adjunct to enhance the efficacy of other antihypertensive drugs in more severe cases [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Therapy must be individualised to patient response. Use the minimum effective dose.
- **Edema:** 500 mg to 1,000 mg (10 mL to 20 mL) once or twice daily. Intermittent therapy (e.g., alternate days or 3–5 days per week) may reduce electrolyte imbalance [US].
- **Hypertension:** Starting dose is 500 mg to 1,000 mg daily as a single or divided dose. Rarely, some patients may require up to 2,000 mg daily in divided doses [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Shake the oral suspension well before use [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Anuria** [US].
- **Hypersensitivity** to chlorothiazide or other sulfonamide-derived medications [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Renal Disease:** Use with caution; may precipitate azotemia. Cumulative effects may occur in patients with renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Minor electrolyte shifts may precipitate hepatic coma; use with caution in progressive liver disease [US].
- **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Patients should be monitored for clinical signs of fluid or electrolyte imbalance (hyponatremia, hypochloremic alkalosis, hypomagnesemia, and hypokalemia). Serum electrolytes should be checked periodically [US].
- **Metabolic Effects:**
    - **Hyperuricemia** may occur or acute gout may be precipitated [US].
    - **Hyperglycemia** may occur; latent diabetes may become manifest. Adjustments to insulin or oral hypoglycaemics may be required [US].
    - **Calcium/Magnesium:** Thiazides decrease calcium excretion and increase magnesium excretion [US].
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):** Potential for exacerbation or activation of SLE has been reported [US].
- **Sensitivity Reactions:** May occur regardless of a history of allergy or bronchial asthma [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following reactions are reported, listed by system but not by numerical frequency [US]:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiovascular** | Orthostatic hypotension (potentiated by alcohol, barbiturates, or narcotics) |
| **Digestive** | Pancreatitis, jaundice (intrahepatic cholestatic), diarrhea, vomiting, sialadenitis, cramping, gastric irritation, nausea, anorexia |
| **Hematologic** | Aplastic anemia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia |
| **Hypersensitivity** | Anaphylaxis, necrotizing angiitis (vasculitis), respiratory distress (pneumonitis, pulmonary edema), photosensitivity, fever, urticaria, rash, purpura |
| **Metabolic** | Electrolyte imbalance, hyperglycemia, glycosuria, hyperuricemia |
| **Nervous System** | Vertigo, paresthesias, dizziness, headache, restlessness |
| **Skin** | Erythema multiforme, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, exfoliative dermatitis (including toxic epidermal necrolysis), alopecia |
| **Other** | Weakness, muscle spasm, transient blurred vision, xanthopsia, impotence |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Alcohol, Barbiturates, Narcotics:** Potentiation of orthostatic hypotension [US].
- **Antidiabetic Agents:** Dosage adjustment of insulin or oral agents may be necessary [US].
- **Other Antihypertensives:** Additive effect or potentiation [US].
- **Cholestyramine and Colestipol Resins:** May bind thiazides and reduce gastrointestinal absorption [US].
- **Corticosteroids/ACTH:** Increased risk of electrolyte depletion, particularly hypokalemia [US].
- **Pressor Amines (e.g., Norepinephrine):** Possible decreased response to amines, though not sufficient to preclude use [US].
- **Skeletal Muscle Relaxants (Nondepolarizing):** Possible increased responsiveness to relaxants (e.g., tubocurarine) [US].
- **Lithium:** Generally should not be given with diuretics. Diuretics reduce renal clearance of lithium, creating a high risk of toxicity [US].
- **NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors):** May reduce diuretic and antihypertensive effects. In patients with compromised renal function (elderly or volume-depleted), co-administration with ACE inhibitors or ARBs may lead to further renal deterioration, including acute renal failure [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Thiazides cross the placental barrier and appear in cord blood. Routine use for normal pregnancy is inappropriate due to hazards such as fetal/neonatal jaundice and thrombocytopenia. Use only if clearly needed for pathologic edema [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Chlorothiazide is excreted in breast milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal models (rats) at doses up to 1–1.5 times the maximum human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - **General Dosage:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg daily in single or two divided doses.
    - **Infants (<6 months):** May require up to 30 mg/kg daily in two divided doses.
    - **Maximum Doses:** 375 mg/day (infants up to 2 years); 1,000 mg/day (children 2–12 years) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Start at the low end of the dosing range. Greater caution is required due to higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution. If progressive renal impairment becomes evident, consider withholding or discontinuing therapy [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with extreme caution due to the risk of hepatic coma [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Chlorothiazide inhibits electrolyte reabsorption in the distal renal tubules. It increases the excretion of sodium and chloride in roughly equal amounts. Natriuresis is accompanied by some loss of potassium and bicarbonate [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of action occurs within 2 hours of oral administration, peaks at 4 hours, and lasts 6 to 12 hours [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Not metabolised.
    - **Elimination:** Rapidly eliminated unchanged in the urine.
    - **Half-life:** Plasma half-life is 45 to 120 minutes [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Data for pediatric use is supported by empiric evidence and published literature rather than formal well-controlled trials. Geriatric data is limited in pivotal trials [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Keep container tightly closed. Store at room temperature, 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F). Protect from freezing (–20°C) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
