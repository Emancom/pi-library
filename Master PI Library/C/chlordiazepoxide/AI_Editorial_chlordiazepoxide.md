# Editorial Product Information: Chlordiazepoxide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of chlordiazepoxide for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Chlordiazepoxide hydrochloride (a benzodiazepine derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Librium (Reference listed drug); marketed generically in the [US].
- **ATC Code:** N05BA02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Capsules:** 5 mg, 10 mg, and 25 mg [US].
    - **Injectable:** Information regarding a parenteral form is mentioned for acute indications, though not the primary focus of the provided source [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral; Intramuscular (IM) [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Generically distributed by various entities, including RemedyRepack Inc. and Teva [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anxiety Disorders:** Management of anxiety disorders or short-term relief of anxiety symptoms [US].
- **Acute Alcohol Withdrawal:** Management of withdrawal symptoms associated with acute alcoholism [US].
- **Preoperative Apprehension:** Relief of apprehension and anxiety prior to surgical procedures [US].
- **Note:** The effectiveness of chlordiazepoxide for long-term use (exceeding 4 months) has not been systematically evaluated. Periodic reassessment of treatment utility is required [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Dosage must be individualised based on diagnosis and patient response [US].
- **Mild to Moderate Anxiety:** 5 mg or 10 mg, 3 or 4 times daily [US].
- **Severe Anxiety:** 20 mg or 25 mg, 3 or 4 times daily [US].

### Indication-Specific Dosing
- **Acute Alcohol Withdrawal:** 
    - **Oral:** Initial dose of 50 mg to 100 mg, followed by repeated doses as needed until agitation is controlled (maximum 300 mg per day), then reduced to maintenance levels [US].
    - **Parenteral:** Usually used initially for acute symptoms [US].
- **Preoperative Apprehension:** 
    - **Oral:** 5 mg to 10 mg, 3 or 4 times daily on days preceding surgery [US].
    - **IM:** 50 mg to 100 mg, 1 hour prior to surgery [US].

### Special Populations
- **Geriatric/Debilitated Patients:** 5 mg, 2 to 4 times daily [US].

### Discontinuation or Dosage Reduction
To mitigate the risk of withdrawal reactions, a gradual taper is required. If withdrawal symptoms occur, the taper should be paused or the dose increased to the previous level before decreasing more slowly [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to chlordiazepoxide hydrochloride [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Risks from Concomitant Use with Opioids:** Simultaneous use may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death. Reserve for cases where alternative treatments are inadequate.
- **Abuse, Misuse, and Addiction:** Use of chlordiazepoxide exposes users to risks of addiction, which can lead to overdose or death. 
- **Dependence and Withdrawal Reactions:** Continued use may lead to physical dependence. Abrupt discontinuation can precipitate life-threatening withdrawal reactions.

### General Warnings
- **CNS Depression:** May impair mental and physical abilities (e.g., driving, operating machinery). Effects are additive with alcohol and other CNS depressants [US].
- **Paradoxical Reactions:** Excitement, stimulation, and acute rage have been reported, particularly in psychiatric patients and hyperactive aggressive pediatric patients [US].
- **Neonatal Sedation and Withdrawal:** Use late in pregnancy can cause respiratory depression, sedation, and withdrawal symptoms in the neonate [US].
- **Psychiatric Cautions:** Use with caution in patients with impending depression or suicidal tendencies; protective measures may be necessary [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Porphyria:** Exercise caution in patients with this disease [US].
- **Blood Coagulation:** Variable effects on blood coagulation have been reported very rarely in patients taking oral anticoagulants [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
- Drowsiness [US]
- Ataxia [US]
- Confusion [US]

### Infrequent/Rare Adverse Effects
- **Neurological:** Syncope, extrapyramidal symptoms, changes in EEG patterns (low-voltage fast activity) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal/Metabolic:** Nausea, constipation, edema, jaundice, hepatic dysfunction [US].
- **Dermatological:** Skin eruptions [US].
- **Genitourinary:** Minor menstrual irregularities, changes in libido [US].
- **Haematological:** Blood dyscrasias, including agranulocytosis [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Respiratory depression [US]
- Delirium and seizures (associated with withdrawal or abuse) [US]
- Suicidal ideation and behavior [US]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Opioids:** Increased risk of fatal respiratory depression and profound sedation [US].
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with alcohol, barbiturates, and other psychotropic agents [US].
- **MAO Inhibitors and Phenothiazines:** Known to potentially potentiate the effects of chlordiazepoxide [US].
- **Oral Anticoagulants:** Rare reports of variable effects on blood coagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not explicitly lettered in the [US] source, but carries significant warnings.
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Crosses the placenta. Use in late pregnancy involves risks of neonatal sedation and withdrawal syndrome.
    - **Registry:** A National Pregnancy Registry for Psychiatric Medications exists for monitoring outcomes [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding is not recommended. Chlordiazepoxide has a long half-life and may accumulate in breast milk, potentially causing sedation and poor feeding in infants [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed decreased fertilization rates and viability of offspring at very high doses (100 mg/kg) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Under 6 years:** Not recommended due to limited clinical experience [US].
    - **Over 6 years:** Initiation at the lowest dose (5 mg, 2 to 4 times daily) is required; monitor for paradoxical reactions [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Recommended to limit dosage to the smallest effective amount (initially 10 mg or less per day) to prevent ataxia or oversedation [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Observe usual precautions; periodic liver function tests are advisable during protracted treatment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Chlordiazepoxide has antianxiety, sedative, and appetite-stimulating properties. It is believed to act on the limbic system of the brain and blocks EEG arousal from the brain stem reticular formation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Reaches peak blood levels in several hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Crosses the placenta [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Long-acting; half-life is between 24 and 48 hours [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted in the urine; 1-2% unchanged, 3-6% as a conjugate. Plasma levels decline slowly over several days post-discontinuation [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Pivotal trial data were not detailed in the provided source. The clinical value is described as being established through "extensive clinical evidence" as the prototype benzodiazepine [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Schedule IV controlled substance (CIV); Rx-only.
