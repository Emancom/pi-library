# Editorial Product Information: coagulation factor xiii (human)

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **coagulation factor xiii (human)**
- Chemical class: Plasma‑derived coagulation factor concentrate (Factor XIII A2B2 complex)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous only

## 2. Regulatory History
- First registration/approval date (stated): First U.S. approval 2011.  
- Date of last revision/update of source PI (stated): Revised 9/2020 [US].  
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.  
- Registrations/updates in other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Routine prophylactic replacement therapy for patients (adult and pediatric) with congenital Factor XIII (FXIII) deficiency to reduce the risk of bleeding.
- Peri‑operative management of surgical bleeding in adult and pediatric patients with congenital FXIII deficiency.
- Any jurisdictional differences in approved indications: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult and pediatric)
- Recommended routine prophylactic dose: 40 International Units (IU) per kg body weight intravenously every 28 days. Administer at a rate not to exceed 4 mL per minute.
- Potency is expressed in IU and should be determined by a validated FXIII activity assay referenced to the current International Standard for Blood Coagulation Factor XIII, Plasma.

### Dose adjustment (target trough)
- Target trough FXIII activity: 5% to 20% (measured by a validated FXIII activity assay).
- Suggested dose adjustment steps (measured trough result):
  - One trough level < 5%: increase dose by 5 IU/kg.
  - Trough level 5% to 20%: no change.
  - Two consecutive trough levels > 20%: decrease dose by 5 IU/kg.
  - One trough level > 25%: decrease dose by 5 IU/kg.

### Peri‑operative management (surgical bleeding)
- Individualize dosing for peri‑operative management based on:
  - Patient’s FXIII activity level
  - Type of surgery
  - Clinical response
- Examples of dose adjustment in relation to time since last prophylactic dose:
  - Within 7 days: an additional dose may not be needed.
  - 8–21 days: an additional partial or full dose may be needed based on FXIII activity level.
  - 21–28 days: a full prophylactic dose is usually required.

### Reconstitution and administration instructions
- Use aseptic technique. Ensure vial and diluent at room temperature prior to reconstitution.
- Transfer the diluent into the product vial using a sterile transfer device; allow the product to dissolve by gentle swirling. Do not shake.
- Use a separate sterile transfer device for each product vial.
- Draw the reconstituted product into a sterile syringe and infuse intravenously at a rate not to exceed 4 mL per minute.
- If multiple vials are required for a dose, pool vial contents into a single syringe using appropriate sterile technique.
- The reconstituted product contains no preservative and must be used within 4 hours after reconstitution. Do not refrigerate or freeze the reconstituted solution. Discard partially used vials.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known anaphylactic or severe systemic reactions to human plasma‑derived products.  
- Other jurisdiction‑specific contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/warning statements
- No branded boxed warning specified in available sources; however, the following critical warnings are emphasized: risk of hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis, potential for development of inhibitory antibodies, thrombotic risk, and risk of transmission of infectious agents because product is plasma‑derived.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, have occurred. If signs or symptoms of anaphylaxis or severe hypersensitivity occur (urticaria, rash, chest tightness, wheeze, hypotension), discontinue administration immediately and institute appropriate treatment.
- **Immunogenicity:** Development of inhibitory (neutralizing) antibodies to FXIII has been observed. Presence of inhibitors may present as inadequate clinical or laboratory response. If expected FXIII activity levels are not achieved or breakthrough bleeding occurs, test for FXIII inhibitory antibodies.
- **Thromboembolic risk:** Thrombotic and thromboembolic events have been reported. Monitor patients with known thrombotic risk factors and use clinical judgment regarding thrombosis risk.
- **Transmission of infectious agents:** Because the product is manufactured from pooled human plasma, it may carry a risk of transmission of viral agents, variant Creutzfeldt‑Jakob disease (vCJD) agent, and theoretically Creutzfeldt‑Jakob disease (CJD) agent, as well as unknown or emerging pathogens. Manufacturers apply donor screening and validated virus‑reduction steps, but residual risk cannot be excluded.
- **Laboratory monitoring:** Monitor trough FXIII activity levels during treatment. If bleeding control is inadequate or expected peak levels are not reached, investigate for inhibitory antibodies and consider laboratory confirmation of FXIII activity.

### Monitoring requirements
- Regular measurement of trough FXIII activity (to guide dosing adjustments).
- Monitor for clinical signs of thrombosis and hypersensitivity reactions.
- If breakthrough bleeding or inadequate recovery, perform assays to detect FXIII inhibitory antibodies.
- Report any infections potentially transmitted by coagulation products to appropriate pharmacovigilance authorities.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use caution in patients with prior reactions to plasma‑derived products.
- Consider dose adjustment in pediatric patients because of shorter half‑life and faster clearance (see Section 10).
- If neutralizing antibodies are detected, manage according to specialist hematology guidance (examples in clinical experience include interruption of potentially immunomodulating concomitant therapies and plasmapheresis).

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Formal pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic interaction studies: Not specified in available sources.
- Clinical observations:
  - A case report in clinical study data noted concomitant interferon therapy in a patient who developed neutralizing antibodies to FXIII; interferon was discontinued and plasmapheresis performed with subsequent resolution of detectable neutralizing antibodies. This is an observational report and does not establish a causal drug–drug interaction.
- Clinically significant interactions and management:
  - No specific interaction contraindications or routine interaction monitoring requirements are specified in available sources.
  - Monitor clinical response when initiating or stopping concomitant immunomodulatory therapies as they may influence immune response to plasma‑derived products (observational data only).

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Animal reproduction studies: Not conducted for this product (not specified).
- Clinical considerations:
  - Disease‑associated risk: Recurrent miscarriage is a known complication of congenital FXIII deficiency. Observational data cited indicate a miscarriage rate of ~91% in pregnancies without routine FXIII prophylaxis versus ~11% in pregnancies with routine FXIII prophylaxis.
- Jurisdictional pregnancy category: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Safety in lactation: No information available on presence of coagulation factor xiii (human) in human milk, effects on breastfed infant, or effects on milk production.
- Clinical recommendation: Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding along with the mother’s need for therapy and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed child.

### Fertility

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Clinical data: In clinical development programs, subjects <16 years were included. A pharmacokinetic subset showed that patients <16 years had a shorter half‑life and faster clearance compared with adults.
  - Reported paediatric pharmacokinetic parameters (for patients <16 years): mean half‑life ≈ 5.7 ± 1.00 days; clearance ≈ 0.29 ± 0.12 mL/hr/kg.
  - Adult comparator values: half‑life ≈ 7.1 ± 2.74 days; clearance ≈ 0.22 ± 0.07 mL/hr/kg.
- Dosing considerations: Dose adjustments may be needed for patients <16 years based on measured FXIII activity and clinical response.
- Age ranges studied: clinical studies included neonates (1 month to <2 years), children (2 to 11 years), and adolescents (12 to <16 years) in the development programs.

### Elderly use
- Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects ≥65 years to determine whether they respond differently from younger subjects.
- Recommendation: Use caution when selecting dose for elderly patients; consider starting at the low end of the dosing range and titrate based on clinical response and laboratory monitoring.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **coagulation factor xiii (human)** provides exogenous Factor XIII, an A2B2 plasma glycoprotein complex. Activated FXIII (FXIIIa) is a transglutaminase that cross‑links fibrin α‑ and γ‑chains, stabilizing and strengthening the fibrin clot and rendering it more resistant to fibrinolysis. FXIIIa also cross‑links α‑2‑plasmin inhibitor to fibrin, further protecting clot integrity.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Administration temporarily replaces deficient FXIII activity in patients with congenital deficiency and reduces bleeding tendency by improving clot stability.
- Standard coagulation clotting time tests may be normal in FXIII deficiency; specific activity assays and qualitative clot stability tests are used to assess FXIII activity and function.

### Pharmacokinetics (clinical data)
- Key findings from pharmacokinetic study (n=13; ages 5–42 years):
  - Mean increase in FXIII activity after dosing (third dose) ≈ 83% (range 48–114% over baseline).
  - PK parameters (baseline‑adjusted, Berichrom assay method in source — described here as validated FXIII activity assay):
    - AUC0‑inf (units·hr/mL): 184.0 ± 65.78
    - Cmax (units/mL): 0.9 ± 0.20
    - Cmin (units/mL): 0.05 ± 0.05
    - Tmax (hr): 1.7 ± 1.44
    - Half‑life: 6.6 ± 2.29 days (mean)
    - Clearance (CL): 0.25 ± 0.09 mL/hr/kg
    - Volume of distribution at steady state (Vss): 51.1 ± 12.61 mL/kg
    - Mean residence time (MRT): 10.0 ± 3.45 days
- Pediatric pharmacokinetic differences: shorter half‑life and increased clearance in subjects <16 years (see Section 10).

### Viral safety/manufacturing (relevant to pharmacology/manufacturing)
- Product manufactured from pooled human plasma with multi‑step viral reduction/inactivation processes described in manufacturer data, including solvent/detergent/adsorption steps, ion‑exchange chromatography, validated heat treatment at +60°C for 10 hours, and sequential nanofiltration (20 nm filters). In vitro virus reduction data in source material report substantial log reduction factors for both enveloped and non‑enveloped model viruses; cumulative virus reduction factors varied by virus type in experimental validation.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Pharmacokinetic study (pivotal for approval)
- Design: 12‑week prospective, open‑label, multicenter pharmacokinetic and safety study in subjects with congenital FXIII deficiency.
- Population: 13 evaluable subjects (ages 5–42 years; children and adults included).
- Dosing: 40 IU/kg intravenously every 28 days for three doses (infusion rate up to ~250 units per minute in study description; routine recommended infusion rate not to exceed 4 mL/min).
- Key outcomes:
  - Mean increase in FXIII activity after third dose ≈ 83%.
  - 12/13 subjects (92%) attained trough FXIII levels ≥ 5% (measured by validated FXIII activity assay), supporting the chosen prophylactic regimen.

### Postmarketing/efficacy study
- Design: Prospective, multicenter, open‑label study to verify clinical benefit and correlate trough FXIII activity with bleeding outcomes.
- Population: 41 subjects received routine prophylactic treatment (40 IU/kg every 28 days) for 52 weeks; included pediatric subjects (44% <16 years).
- Key outcomes:
  - Incidence of spontaneous bleeding episodes requiring treatment was evaluated.
  - None of the five subjects who experienced spontaneous bleeding episodes required treatment with an FXIII‑containing product.
  - An annualized spontaneous bleeding rate of 0 episodes per subject per year was reported in the treated cohort, compared to a historical annualized bleeding rate of 2.5 episodes per subject per year in patients treated on‑demand (historical data for congenital FXIII deficiency).

### Immunogenicity observation
- One reported case of neutralizing antibodies to FXIII in a subject receiving long‑term prophylaxis (approximately ten years of treatment) who was receiving concomitant interferon therapy for hepatitis C. The subject developed bruising and declining post‑infusion FXIII levels; neutralizing antibodies were detected, interferon discontinued, plasmapheresis performed, and inhibitors became undetectable within a month with recovery of FXIII levels and resumption of prior regimen. This represents an observed clinical case and informs cautions regarding immunogenicity.

- Additional clinical trial details, patient numbers, and infusion counts: Across 12 clinical studies described in source material, a total of 188 subjects were enrolled (108 subjects <16 years of age) and approximately 4,314 infusions were administered in the studies summarized in source materials.
