# Editorial Product Information: Calaspargase pegol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulations and the current official PI for their respective region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Calaspargase pegol-mknl (an asparagine-specific enzyme).
- **Chemical Class:** Escherichia coli-derived L-asparagine amidohydrolase conjugated with monomethoxypolyethylene glycol (mPEG).
- **Brand Name:** **ASPARLAS** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** L01XX02 (not explicitly stated in the source but corresponding to the pharmacological class).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection: 3,750 units/5 mL (750 units/mL) clear, colorless, preservative-free solution in a single-dose vial [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Servier Pharmaceuticals LLC [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia (ALL):** Indicated as a component of a multi-agent chemotherapeutic regimen for the treatment of ALL in paediatric and young adult patients aged 1 month to 21 years [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Pediatric and Young Adult):** The recommended dose is 2,500 units/m² administered intravenously.
- **Dosing Frequency:** Administer no more frequently than every 21 days [United States].
- **Premedication:** To reduce the risk and severity of infusion and hypersensitivity reactions, premedicate patients 30–60 minutes prior to administration with:
    - Acetaminophen.
    - An H-1 receptor blocker (e.g., diphenhydramine).
    - An H-2 receptor blocker (e.g., famotidine).
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Dilute the dose in 100 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection.
    - Administer via intravenous infusion over a period of 1 hour.
    - Do not infuse other drugs through the same intravenous line.
    - Do not shake or freeze the drug.
- **Monitoring:** Monitor patients at least weekly for bilirubin, transaminases, and glucose levels until recovery from the therapy cycle [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
**ASPARLAS** is contraindicated in patients with:
- A history of serious hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, to pegylated L-asparaginase.
- A history of serious thrombosis during prior L-asparaginase therapy.
- A history of serious pancreatitis related to previous L-asparaginase treatment.
- A history of serious haemorrhagic events during previous L-asparaginase therapy.
- Severe hepatic impairment [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Grade 3 and 4 reactions, including anaphylaxis, have occurred in 7% to 21% of patients. Observe patients for one hour post-infusion in a setting with resuscitation equipment. Discontinue permanently for serious reactions [United States].
- **Pancreatitis:** Reported in 12% to 16% of patients. Discontinue if pancreatitis is suspected; permanently discontinue if clinical pancreatitis is confirmed. Monitor serum amylase and lipase.
- **Thrombosis:** Serious thrombotic events (e.g., sagittal sinus thrombosis) have been reported (9% to 12%). Discontinue for severe or life-threatening thrombosis.
- **Haemorrhage:** Associated with increased PT/PTT and hypofibrinogenemia. Discontinue for severe haemorrhage and evaluate coagulation parameters.
- **Hepatotoxicity (including Veno-Occlusive Disease [VOD]):** Severe, life-threatening, and fatal cases of VOD have occurred. Monitor liver tests frequently, especially if the patient shows rapid weight gain, fluid retention, or painful hepatomegaly [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are reported as Grade ≥3 (severe) as collected in clinical trials.

**Serious Adverse Effects:** One fatal reaction (multi-organ failure) was reported in the setting of chronic pancreatitis [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specifically detailed in the available source.
- **Clinical Management:** While formal drug interaction studies have not been conducted, the depletion of asparagine by **ASPARLAS** can affect the toxicity or efficacy of other chemotherapeutic agents (e.g., methotrexate or vincristine) if administered concurrently; however, specific management notes were not provided in the source document.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: No lettered category provided, but risk of fetal harm is noted]. Animal studies indicate potential for asparagine depletion to cause harm to the offspring. Advise females of reproductive potential to use effective non-hormonal contraception during treatment and for 3 months after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of the drug in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child, breastfeeding is advised against during treatment and for 3 months after the last dose [United States].
- **Fertility:** Carcinogenicity, mutagenicity, and impairment of fertility studies have not been conducted with this agent [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in patients aged 1 month to <17 years. Use in patients up to 21 years is supported by evidence from clinical trials [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources (the indication is limited to patients ≤ 21 years).
- **Renal Impairment:** The impact of renal impairment on the pharmacokinetics of the drug is unknown.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment. The impact of mild to moderate impairment on pharmacokinetics is unknown [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Calaspargase pegol-mknl is an enzyme that catalyses the conversion of L-asparagine into aspartic acid and ammonia. This deprives leukemic cells (which often lack asparagine synthetase) of the exogenous asparagine required for survival and protein synthesis [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Maintains plasma asparagine concentrations below the limit of quantification for more than 18 days following a single dose of 2,500 units/m².
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Mean Cmax:** 1.62 U/mL.
    - **Elimination Half-life (t1/2):** 16.1 days.
    - **Clearance:** 0.147 L/day.
    - **Volume of Distribution (Vss):** 2.96 L [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study DFCI 11-001:** An open-label, randomized trial in 237 children and adolescents with ALL or lymphoblastic lymphoma. Patients received **ASPARLAS** 2,500 units/m² every 3 weeks or pegaspargase every 2 weeks. The complete remission rate in the **ASPARLAS** arm for B-cell lineage ALL was 98%.
- **Study AALL07P4:** Evaluated the maintenance of nadir serum asparaginase activity (NSAA). 99% of patients maintained NSAA >0.1 U/mL at weeks 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30 [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F).
- **Unopened Vials:** May be stored at room temperature (15°C to 25°C) for no more than 48 hours.
- **Diluted Solution:** May be stored for up to 4 hours at room temperature or up to 24 hours if refrigerated.
- **Protection:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze or shake [United States].
