# Editorial Product Information: Clobetasone Butyrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Clobetasone butyrate (a corticosteroid).
- **Brand Names:** **Eumovate** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Topical cream containing 0.05% w/w clobetasone butyrate [Australian]. 
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Short-term treatment (up to 7 days):** Indicated for milder forms of eczema, dermatitis, and other skin conditions that are responsive to topical steroids [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adults and Children (12 years and older)
- **Application:** A thin film should be applied to the affected area and gently rubbed in twice daily. Use only enough to cover the site [Australian].
- **Duration:** Treatment should be limited to a maximum of 7 days. Therapy must be discontinued if the condition resolves within this period.
- **Clinical Review:** Patients should consult a physician if the condition worsens, fails to improve within 7 days, or if improvement is noted but further treatment beyond the 7-day limit is required [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Hands should be washed following application unless the hands are the area being treated.
- Patients should be cautioned against using excessive quantities or applying the medication to a single area for prolonged periods.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **clobetasone butyrate** or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].
- **Inappropriate Skin Conditions:**
    - Rosacea, acne vulgaris, or perioral dermatitis.
    - Pruritus in the absence of a rash.
- **Infections:**
    - Untreated bacterial infections (e.g., cellulitis, folliculitis, impetigo).
    - Fungal infections (e.g., tinea, athlete’s foot, jock itch).
    - Viral infections (e.g., herpes simplex, chickenpox, shingles, vaccinia).
    - Parasitic infestations (e.g., scabies).
- **Tissue Integrity:** Do not apply to broken, infected, or inflamed skin near chronic ulcers. Topical steroids are contraindicated for use on skin ulcers, cuts, or abrasions as they may impair wound healing [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [Australian].
- **Systemic Absorption:** Significant systemic absorption can occur if the medication is used for more than a few days, applied to large surface areas, used on damaged/thin skin (such as the face or intertriginous areas), or used under occlusion [Australian].
- **Hypercortolism:** Prolonged or extensive use may lead to manifestations of Cushing’s syndrome (hypercortolism) and hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression.
- **Visual Disturbances:** Both systemic and topical corticosteroid use have been linked to visual disturbances. Patients reporting blurred vision or other symptoms should be referred to an ophthalmologist to evaluate for cataracts, glaucoma, or central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR) [Australian].
- **Site-Specific Cautions:** Avoid application to the face, groin, genitals, or between the toes. Avoid contact with the eyes.
- **Impaired Circulation:** Should not be used on skin with impaired circulation (e.g., stasis ulcers) due to the risk of prolonged vasoconstriction.
- **Infection Risk:** Topical steroids may predispose the site to local infection. If an inflammatory lesion becomes infected, appropriate antimicrobial therapy is required; spread of infection necessitates the withdrawal of the corticosteroid [Australian].
- **Skin Damage:** Prolonged use can cause epidermal atrophy and dermal damage, potentially leading to permanent atrophic striae, telangiectasia, and discolouration [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Systemic adverse effects are more frequent in children, with prolonged use, or under occlusion.

### Very Rare (<0.01%)
- **Infections:** Opportunistic infections.
- **Immune System:** Hypersensitivity (local reactions including erythema, rash, pruritus, urticaria, or allergic contact dermatitis).
- **Endocrine:** HPA axis suppression, Cushingoid features (e.g., moon face, central obesity), delayed weight gain/growth retardation in children, osteoporosis, glaucoma, hyperglycaemia, hypertension, and decreased endogenous cortisol [Australian].

### Rare (0.01% to <0.1%)
- **Skin/Subcutaneous:** Skin atrophy, pigmentation changes, hypertrichosis, exacerbation of underlying symptoms, bruising, and rosacea [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Corticosteroids:** Concomitant use with other topical or systemic corticosteroids (including over-the-counter hydrocortisone) should be avoided due to the increased risk of drug interactions and systemic effects [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Co-administration with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., **ritonavir**, **itraconazole**) may inhibit corticosteroid metabolism, potentially leading to increased systemic exposure [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B3. Animal studies have demonstrated fetal abnormalities (cleft palate, intrauterine growth retardation). Human data are limited. Use should only be considered if the benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the fetus, using the minimum amount for the shortest duration [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been established. It is unknown if topical administration results in detectable levels in breast milk. If used, the cream must not be applied to the breasts to prevent accidental ingestion by the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Human data regarding the effects of topical corticosteroids on fertility are unavailable [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in children under 12 years requires medical supervision. Children are more susceptible to systemic toxicity and HPA axis suppression due to a higher surface-area-to-weight ratio and immature skin barrier. Corticosteroids may inhibit linear bone growth and epiphyseal maturation in this population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not identify specific differences in response compared to younger patients; however, the higher prevalence of renal or hepatic impairment in the elderly may delay elimination if systemic absorption occurs [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** In cases of systemic absorption, impaired metabolism or elimination may increase the risk of toxicity. Use the minimum quantity for the shortest possible duration [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Clobetasone butyrate** is a corticosteroid with high topical activity and low systemic activity. It reduces erythema and pruritus associated with inflammatory skin conditions [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The cream base possesses moisturising properties [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Human data on distribution, metabolism, and excretion are lacking. Absorption studies using a 0.05% ointment (30g dose) showed peak plasma levels of 0.6 ng/mL within three hours, declining to below 0.1 ng/mL after 72 hours. Cream formulations are generally less occlusive than ointments and are expected to have similar or lower absorption rates [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data were specified in the available source documents [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Keep out of reach of children. In case of accidental ingestion, contact professional assistance or a poisons information centre immediately.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification:** Pharmacist Only Medicine (Schedule 3).
