# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (US FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory information for **levocarnitine injection**.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive guidance.
*   **Note on Source Discrepancy:** While the user's prompt referenced a Cladribine document, the provided source material is the official US PI for Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation). This editorial is based strictly on the provided Levocarnitine documentation.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not specified in available source).
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution for injection [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    *   **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    *   **Frequency:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours. Daily maintenance doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used.
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    *   **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   **Adjustment:** Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Monitoring
*   **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Normal free carnitine concentrations should be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
*   **ESRD Monitoring:** Initiation of therapy is often prompted by trough levels below 40–50 µmol/L.

### Administration Instructions
*   **Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
*   **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of intravenous administration. Therapy should be discontinued immediately upon signs of severe hypersensitivity [US].
*   **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
*   **Metabolite Accumulation:** In patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD, chronic high-dose oral administration (not intravenous) may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, the following events occurred at a frequency **≥5%** (regardless of causality):
*   **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), infection (15%), fever (7%), abdominal pain (9%).
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%), arrhythmia (2%).
*   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), rhinitis (9%), dyspnea (7%).
*   **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).
*   **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%), depression (5%).

### Post-Marketing Experience
*   **Neurologic:** Seizures.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis (less frequent).

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
*   **Management:** Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended in patients receiving warfarin therapy after starting levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Classification:** Not explicitly letter-coded in the provided text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. 
    *   **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of carnitine following administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the child must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients. (See Section 3).
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, the safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    *   **Elimination:** Following intravenous administration (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Bioavailability:** In healthy volunteers, levocarnitine tablets and oral solutions were found to be bioequivalent. Absolute bioavailability (corrected for endogenous levels) was approximately 15–16% [US].
*   **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly showed that trough concentrations (initially ~20 µmol/L) rose to between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week of therapy and remained stable or increased over 8 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Cautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
*   **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (standard US prescription drug).
