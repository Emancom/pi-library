# Editorial Product Information: Cyproterone Acetate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of cyproterone acetate for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cyproterone acetate (a steroidal antiandrogen with progestogenic activity).
- **Brand Names:** Androcur [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 50 mg round, white, scored tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amdipharm Mercury (Australia) Pty Ltd (An ADVANZ PHARMA company) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
### Women
- Treatment of moderately severe to severe signs of androgenisation, including:
    - Moderately severe to severe hirsutism [Australian].
    - Moderately severe to severe androgen-dependent alopecia [Australian].
    - Moderately severe to severe acne and/or seborrhoea associated with other androgenic features [Australian].

### Men
- **Sexual Deviations:** Reduction of sexual drive in men with sexual deviations [Australian].
- **Inoperable Prostatic Carcinoma:**
    - Suppression of "flare" during initial LHRH (luteinising hormone-releasing hormone) analogue therapy [Australian].
    - Long-term palliative treatment where LHRH analogues or surgery are ineffective, contraindicated, or not tolerated [Australian].
    - Management of hot flushes in patients treated with LHRH analogues or following orchidectomy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- Tablets should be taken with liquid after a meal [Australian].

### Women
- **Women of Childbearing Potential:** Treatment must begin on Day 1 of the menstrual cycle. 
    - **Hirsutism:** 50 mg daily from Day 1 to Day 10 of the cycle. Maintenance doses as low as 10 mg daily for 10 days per month may be sufficient once a response is achieved [Australian].
    - **Severe Androgenisation (Acne/Alopecia):** 100 mg daily from Day 1 to Day 10 [Australian].
    - **Note:** A progestogen-oestrogen combined oral contraceptive must be taken concurrently to ensure contraception and cycle stability [Australian].
- **Postmenopausal or Hysterectomised Patients:** 25 mg to 50 mg once daily for 21 days, followed by a 7-day tablet-free interval [Australian].

### Men
- **Sexual Deviations:** Initially 50 mg twice daily. Dose may be increased to 100 mg twice daily or 100 mg three times daily for short periods. Maintenance should use the lowest effective dose (often 25 mg twice daily). Dose reductions should be gradual over several weeks [Australian].
- **Prostate Carcinoma (Flare Suppression):** 100 mg twice daily alone for 5–7 days, followed by 100 mg twice daily for 3–4 weeks in combination with an LHRH agonist [Australian].
- **Prostate Carcinoma (Palliative):** 100 mg two to three times daily. Do not interrupt treatment or reduce dose upon remission [Australian].
- **Hot Flushes:** 50 mg once to three times daily, titrated up to 100 mg three times daily if required [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, it should be disregarded; patients should not take a double dose. For women on cyclical therapy, a missed dose may lead to intermenstrual bleeding and, if combined with a contraceptive, may reduce contraceptive efficacy [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **General:** Liver diseases, Dubin-Johnson syndrome, Rotor syndrome, previous or existing liver tumours (unless due to metastases from prostate cancer), presence or history of meningioma, wasting diseases (except inoperable prostate cancer), severe chronic depression, existing thromboembolic processes, sickle-cell anaemia, and hypersensitivity to the drug [Australian].
- **Specific to Women:** Pregnancy and lactation; history of jaundice, persistent pruritus, or herpes gestationis during a previous pregnancy [Australian].
- **Specific to Men (Sexual Deviation):** Severe diabetes with vascular changes [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Direct hepatic toxicity (jaundice, hepatitis, hepatic failure) has been reported, including fatalities at doses ≥100 mg. Most fatal cases occurred in men with advanced prostate cancer. Liver function tests (LFTs) must be performed at baseline and regular intervals [Australian].
- **Meningioma:** The risk of single or multiple meningiomas increases with long-term use (years) and cumulative doses ≥25 mg/day. If diagnosed, cyproterone must be permanently discontinued [Australian].
- **Thromboembolism:** Patients with a history of arterial or venous thrombotic events or advanced malignancies are at increased risk [Australian].
- **Adrenocortical Function:** High doses may suppress adrenocortical function; regular monitoring is recommended [Australian].
- **Diabetes:** Requires strict medical supervision as insulin or oral antidiabetic requirements may change [Australian].
- **Anemia:** Red blood cell counts should be checked regularly [Australian].
- **Shortness of Breath:** High-dose treatment may cause a sensation of breathlessness [Australian].
- **Osteoporosis:** Long-term androgen deprivation in men may lead to reduced bone density [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Tiredness, weight increase, diminished libido (men), erectile dysfunction, reversible inhibition of spermatogenesis, ovulation inhibition [Australian] |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Depressed mood, restlessness (temporary), headache, nausea, gastrointestinal complaints, mastodynia, irregular menstrual cycles, hot flushes, sweating, gynaecomastia (men), hepatic toxicity [Australian] |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Libido decreased (women), rash [Australian] |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Hypersensitivity reactions, libido increased (women) [Australian] |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Benign and malignant liver tumours, intra-abdominal haemorrhage, osteoporosis (men), tachycardia, galactorrhoea [Australian] |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Hepatotoxicity, thromboembolic events, and the development of meningiomas [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Metabolic Pathway:** Cyproterone acetate is metabolised primarily by **CYP3A4** [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir) may increase cyproterone levels [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St. John’s wort) may reduce cyproterone levels [Australian].
- **Statins:** Risk of statin-associated myopathy or rhabdomyolysis is increased when co-administered with high-dose cyproterone, as they share the CYP3A4 pathway [Australian].
- **Diabetes Medications:** Requirements for insulin or oral antidiabetics may change [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** May diminish the drive-reducing effect of cyproterone in men [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Contraindicated. Use during the hormone-sensitive differentiation phase of foetal genital organs (after approx. Day 45) may lead to feminisation of male foetuses [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Small amounts of the drug are excreted in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In men, spermatogenesis is inhibited but usually recovers within several months of discontinuation. Pre-treatment spermatograms are recommended for men of procreative age [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for use in children or adolescents under 18 years. Must not be given before the conclusion of puberty due to potential negative impacts on growth and endocrine function [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No data suggesting a need for dose adjustment [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No data suggesting a need for dose adjustment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with liver disease [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive inhibition of androgens at target organs (e.g., prostate). It also exerts a progestogenic effect that reduces gonadotropin release, leading to diminished production of testicular androgens [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Completely absorbed; peak serum levels reached in ~3 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Primarily bound to serum albumin; free fraction is ~3.5–4% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 to the 15ß-hydroxy derivative [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Terminal half-life is ~44 hours. Approximately 30% excreted in urine and 58% in faeces [Australian].
