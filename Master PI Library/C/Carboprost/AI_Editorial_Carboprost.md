# Editorial Product Information: Carboprost

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis was prepared based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority:
    *   Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **carboprost** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the local approved PI for prescribing and legal requirements within their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Carboprost (as trometamol).
*   **Chemical Class:** Synthetic 15-methyl analogue of dinoprost (prostaglandin F2α).
*   **Brand Names:** **Carboprost Dr. Reddy’s** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** G02AD04 (Not specified in available source).
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection containing 250 µg/mL carboprost (as trometamol) [Australian].
*   **Route of Administration:** Deep intramuscular (IM) injection only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr Reddy’s Laboratories (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Postpartum Haemorrhage:** Indicated for the treatment of postpartum haemorrhage due to uterine atony that has not responded to conventional management strategies [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Initial Dose:** 250 µg (1.0 mL) administered by deep intramuscular injection.
*   **Subsequent Dosing:** If required, additional doses of 250 µg may be administered.
*   **Interval:** The standard interval between doses is approximately 1.5 hours. In severe clinical scenarios, the interval may be reduced at the physician's discretion but must **not be less than 15 minutes**.
*   **Maximum Total Dose:** The cumulative dose should not exceed 2 mg (equivalent to 8 doses) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
*   Visual inspection for particulate matter and discolouration is required before administration.
*   **Intravenous (IV) administration is strictly prohibited** [Australian].
*   The product is for single-use only; any remaining residue must be discarded.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Hypersensitivity to carboprost trometamol or any excipients (including benzyl alcohol).
*   Acute pelvic inflammatory disease.
*   Active cardiac, pulmonary, renal, or hepatic disease.
*   **Pregnancy** (Carboprost is contraindicated for use during pregnancy) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
*   **Strict Adherence to Dosing:** **Carboprost** is a potent oxytocic agent and must be used only as recommended.
*   **Setting of Use:** Should only be administered by trained personnel in hospitals or clinics with specialised obstetric units.
*   **Labour Induction:** This preparation is not indicated and **must not be used** for the induction of labour [Australian].
*   **Cardiovascular Collapse:** Extremely rare cases of cardiovascular collapse have been reported with prostaglandin use.
*   **Benzyl Alcohol Toxicity:** Contains benzyl alcohol, which is associated with "Gasping Syndrome" and death in premature infants. Caution is required if high volumes are used, especially in patients with renal or hepatic impairment due to the risk of metabolic acidosis [Australian].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
*   **Uterine Scarring:** Use with caution in patients with a previously compromised or scarred uterus.
*   **Chorioamnionitis:** Patients with chorioamnionitis may exhibit an inhibitory effect on uterine response to carboprost.
*   **Cardiopulmonary Status:** Decreases in maternal arterial oxygen content have been observed. Patients with pre-existing cardiopulmonary disease should be monitored closely and provided supplemental oxygen if necessary.
*   **Other Conditions:** Use with caution in patients with a history of:
    *   Asthma, glaucoma, or raised intra-ocular pressure.
    *   Hypertension or hypotension.
    *   Anaemia, jaundice, diabetes, or epilepsy.

### Monitoring
*   Monitor blood pressure (4–5.1% of patients in trials experienced moderate hypertension) [Australian].
*   Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally transient and reversible upon discontinuation. Most effects are related to the drug's contractile action on smooth muscle.

| Body System | Adverse Effect | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea | Very Common (>10%) |
| | Nausea, Vomiting | Common (1–10%) |
| | Abdominal cramp, pain, Retching | Uncommon (0.1–1%) |
| **General** | Fever | Common (1–10%) |
| | Chills, flushing, sweating, chest pain, asthenia, thirst | Uncommon (0.1–1%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Elevated blood pressure | Common (1–10%) |
| | Tachycardia, Palpitations | Uncommon (0.1–1%) |
| **Respiratory** | Dyspnoea, Choking sensation, Epistaxis, Dry throat | Uncommon (0.1–1%) |

**Post-Marketing Experience (Serious/Other):**
*   **Immune System:** Anaphylactic reaction, anaphylactic shock, angioedema.
*   **Endocrine:** Thyrotoxic crisis.
*   **Psychiatric:** Anxiety, nervousness.
*   **Musculoskeletal:** Blepharospasm.
*   **Skin:** Rash.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Oxytocic Agents:** **Carboprost** may potentiate the effects of other oxytocics. Simultaneous or sequential use is not recommended; if necessary, patients must be carefully monitored [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Contraindicated during pregnancy. Administration during pregnancy can cause irreversible fetal damage, death, and inability to sustain pregnancy. Carboprost crosses the placenta.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if carboprost is excreted in human milk. Animal studies showed reversible failure to lactate. It is recommended that **breastfeeding be avoided for at least 6 hours** following administration [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** No human data available. High-dose animal studies (3x human dose) showed no effect on rat fertility, though similar compounds are known to disrupt fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established. Presence of benzyl alcohol poses a fatal risk ("Gasping Syndrome") to premature infants [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** No data available [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in active renal disease. Use with caution in patients with a history of renal impairment.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in active hepatic disease. Use with caution in patients with a history of impaired liver function.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** A synthetic analogue of prostaglandin F2α that stimulates the myometrium to contract, mimicking normal postpartum uterine activity. This provides haemostasis at the placental site. It also stimulates gastrointestinal smooth muscle and may cause bronchoconstriction or increased blood pressure at higher doses.
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits a more prolonged uterine stimulant action than dinoprost.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Following a 250 µg IM dose, peak plasma concentrations occur between 15 and 60 minutes, ranging from approximately 2700 to 3100 picograms/mL [Australian].
    *   **Distribution:** Known to cross the placenta.
    *   **Elimination:** Plasma clearance rates suggest the drug is cleared sufficiently to allow breastfeeding after 6 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Study #7055:** An uncontrolled, multicentre trial (n=115) in the USA for refractory postpartum haemorrhage. Haemostasis was achieved in 87.8% of patients, with 73.3% requiring only a single dose.
*   **Post-marketing Study:** A 12-month epidemiological study (n=333) in the USA. Successful control of haemorrhage was achieved in 83.8% of cases, with 81.4% requiring only one dose [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store between 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate).
*   **Handling:** Do not freeze.
*   **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
