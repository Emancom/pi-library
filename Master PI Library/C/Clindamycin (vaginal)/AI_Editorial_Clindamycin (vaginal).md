# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single, coherent reference.
*   **Prescriber Caution:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with metabolic disorders resulting in secondary deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    *   *Severe Metabolic Crisis:* A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    *   *Maintenance:* Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours. 
    *   *Maximum Dose:* The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). 
    *   *Administration:* Administered as a 2 to 3-minute slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
*   Dose adjustments in dialysis patients should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are supratherapeutic [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
*   Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Known Hypersensitivity:** There are no known contraindications listed in the available source; however, see Warnings regarding hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a known seizure history, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
*   **Monitoring:** 
    *   Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before therapy.
    *   Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
*   **Oral Levocarnitine Caution:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High oral doses in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (Frequency ≥5%)
Data from double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients indicate the following (regardless of causality):
*   **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), abdominal pain (9%), asthenia (9%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%).
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%).
*   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).
*   **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
*   **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   **Neurologic:** Seizures.
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, melena (uncommon) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
    *   *Management:* Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins, reducing the likelihood of protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. 
    *   *Recommendation:* Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
*   **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, yeast) were negative. Long-term carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for inborn errors of metabolism is weight-based (50 mg/kg), including for infants [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. However, **oral** levocarnitine is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily feces).
    *   **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Total body clearance is 4.00 L/h [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels at 12 and 24 weeks. However, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population remains to be determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
*   **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
*   **Stability:** When mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
*   **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
