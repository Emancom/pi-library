# Editorial Product Information: Captopril

## Editorial Note
- Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of captopril for use by healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory information into a single clinical reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Captopril, a specific competitive inhibitor of angiotensin I-converting enzyme (ACE).
- **Chemical Class:** Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) Inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** Captopril-Reach [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C09AA01 [Not explicitly stated in source; provided for clinical reference].
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 5 mg/mL oral solution [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Reach Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** For the treatment of hypertension, alone or in combination with other agents (particularly thiazide diuretics) [Australian].
- **Myocardial Infarction:** To improve survival following myocardial infarction in clinically stable patients with left ventricular dysfunction (ejection fraction ≤40%) and to reduce the incidence of overt heart failure and subsequent hospitalisation [Australian].
- **Heart Failure:** For the treatment of heart failure, typically in conjunction with diuretics and/or digitalis [Australian].
- **Diabetic Nephropathy:** For the treatment of diabetic nephropathy in patients with Type 1 insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
- **Administration:** Should be taken one hour before meals [Australian].
- **Oral Solution Use:** Intended for initial dose titration where fractional doses are required; not intended for extended therapy. The solution must be mixed with up to 100 mL of a suitable beverage (water, juice, tea, coffee, or cola) and consumed immediately [Australian].
- **Individualisation:** Dosage must be individualised and titrated based on patient response.

### Hypertension
- **Initial Dose:** 12.5 mg [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** May be increased to 25 mg twice daily. If required after 2–4 weeks, the dose may be increased to 50 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Severe/Refractory Hypertension:** A starting dose of 6.25–12.5 mg may be used in patients on high-dose diuretics or with severe hypertension. Daily doses should not normally exceed 75 mg twice daily [Australian].

### Myocardial Infarction
- **Initiation:** As early as 3 days post-infarct [Australian].
- **Initial Dose:** 6.25 mg [Australian].
- **Titration:** Increase to 25 mg three times daily over several days, with a final target of 50 mg three times daily over several weeks [Australian].

### Heart Failure
- **Initiation:** Must be started under close medical supervision [Australian].
- **Initial Dose:** 6.25 mg three times daily. In high-risk patients (sodium depletion/high-dose diuretics), a 2.5 mg starting dose may minimise the first-dose hypotensive effect [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** Generally 25 mg to 75 mg twice daily. Maximum daily dose should not exceed 150 mg [Australian].

### Diabetic Nephropathy
- **Standard Dosing:** 75 mg to 100 mg daily in divided doses [Australian].
- **Microalbuminuria:** 50 mg twice daily has been shown to attenuate disease progression [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of previous hypersensitivity to captopril or any excipients [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated in all trimesters [Australian].
- **Angioedema:** History of hereditary/idiopathic angioedema or angioedema associated with previous ACE inhibitor treatment [Australian].
- **Dual Blockade:** Concomitant use with aliskiren-containing products in patients with diabetes or renal impairment (GFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m²) [Australian].
- **Neprilysin Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with sacubitril/valsartan. Captopril must not be initiated until 36 hours after the last dose of sacubitril/valsartan [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neutropenia/Agranulocytosis:** Risk is highest in patients with renal impairment or collagen vascular disease (e.g., SLE, scleroderma). Monitor WBC counts prior to therapy and every two weeks for the first three months in high-risk patients [Australian].
- **Angioedema:**
    - **Head and Neck:** May occur at any time during therapy (most common in the first week). Can be life-threatening if it involves the larynx/glottis [Australian].
    - **Intestinal:** Rare; presents as abdominal pain and should be considered in differential diagnosis [Australian].
- **Hypotension:** May occur following the first dose, especially in volume- or salt-depleted patients. Correct volume depletion before initiation if possible [Australian].
- **Renal Function:** Evaluation of the patient should always include assessment of renal function. Proteinuria may occur (usually in those with pre-existing renal disease or on doses >150 mg/day) [Australian].
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Risk increases with renal impairment, diabetes, and use of potassium-sparing diuretics or potassium supplements. Monitor serum electrolytes [Australian].
- **Insulin Autoimmune Syndrome (IAS):** Reported rarely; may cause severe hypoglycaemia [Australian].
- **Hepatic Effects:** ACE inhibitors are rarely associated with a syndrome of cholestatic jaundice progressing to fulminant hepatic necrosis [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies based on clinical trials of ~7000 patients.*

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypotension (2%), Rash (3.8% in normal renal function; 13.1% in renal impairment), Sleep disorders, Taste disturbances (1.6% at ≤150 mg/day; 7.3% at >150 mg/day) [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Tachycardia, chest pain, palpitations, Angina pectoris, Myocardial infarction, Raynaud's, CHF, Proteinuria, Renal insufficiency/failure, Polyuria/Oliguria [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Angioedema (1 in 1000), Stomatitis, Glossitis, Hepatitis, Pancreatitis, Paraesthesia, Bronchospasm [Australian]. |

- **Cough:** Persistent, non-productive cough reported in 0.5–2% of patients (up to 15% in some ACE inhibitor studies) [Australian].
- **Serious Reactions:** Neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anaphylactoid reactions (especially during high-flux dialysis or desensitisation), and severe hypotension [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Dual Blockade of RAAS:** Increased risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia, and renal impairment when used with ARBs or aliskiren [Australian].
- **Sacubitril/Valsartan:** Increased risk of angioedema; strictly contraindicated [Australian].
- **mTOR Inhibitors/Vildagliptin:** Increased risk of angioedema (e.g., sirolimus, everolimus) [Australian].
- **Diuretics:** Additive hypotensive effect [Australian].
- **Potassium-Sparing Agents/Supplements:** Risk of significant hyperkalaemia [Australian].
- **Lithium:** Increased serum lithium levels and toxicity risk [Australian].
- **NSAIDs:** May reduce antihypertensive effect and increase risk of renal impairment (especially with concomitant diuretics) [Australian].
- **Gold (Sodium Aurothiomalate):** Nitritoid reactions (flushing, nausea, hypotension) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian].** ACE inhibitors can cause foetal and neonatal injury (skull hypoplasia, anuria, renal failure) and death when used in pregnancy. If pregnancy is detected, discontinue captopril immediately [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Captopril is excreted in breast milk at concentrations ≤1% of maternal blood. Due to unknown effects on the infant, breastfeeding should generally be interrupted [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established. Limited experience suggests weight-based dosing is comparable to adults. Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk. Premature neonates are at particular risk of oliguria and unpredictable hypotension [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in the primary source; however, caution is advised due to higher likelihood of renal impairment and concomitant drug use [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Reduce initial daily dosage and use smaller titration increments. Captopril is removed by haemodialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** ACE inhibitors should be discontinued if jaundice or marked elevations in hepatic enzymes occur [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive inhibition of ACE prevents the conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II, reducing vasoconstriction and aldosterone secretion. It also inhibits the degradation of bradykinin [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak levels at 0.5–1 hour. Bioavailability is ~75%, but food reduces absorption by 25–40% [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** ~30% protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised, primarily to captopril dimer [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in urine (40% unchanged, 35% metabolites). Elimination half-life is 1–2 hours, increasing significantly with renal impairment [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SAVE Trial:** In 2,231 patients with LVD post-MI, captopril reduced all-cause mortality (19% risk reduction) and CHF hospitalisation (20% reduction) [Australian].
- **Diabetic Nephropathy:** In type 1 diabetics with proteinuria (≥500 mg/day), captopril (25 mg TID) reduced the risk of doubling serum creatinine and the combined endpoint of ESRD or death by 51% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any remaining oral solution 21 days after the bottle is first opened [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Poison Schedule S4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
