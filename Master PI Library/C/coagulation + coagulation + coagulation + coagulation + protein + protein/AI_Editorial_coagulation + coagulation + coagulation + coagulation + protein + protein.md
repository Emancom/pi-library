# Editorial Product Information: coagulation + coagulation + coagulation + coagulation + protein + protein

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **coagulation + coagulation + coagulation + coagulation + protein + protein**
- Chemical class: Human plasma‑derived prothrombin complex concentrate containing vitamin K‑dependent coagulation factors (II, VII, IX, X) and vitamin K‑dependent anticoagulant proteins (Protein C, Protein S).
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous injection/infusion only.
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form physical descriptions, or PBS/schedule listings.

## 2. Regulatory History

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment and perioperative prophylaxis of bleeding in acquired deficiency of the prothrombin complex coagulation factors (e.g., deficiency caused by vitamin K antagonist therapy or overdose of vitamin K antagonists) when rapid correction of the deficiency is required.
- Use for other indications (congenital isolated deficiencies, routine prophylaxis) is not recommended where specific factor concentrates are available; use of specific factor products is preferred in congenital deficiency of individual vitamin K‑dependent factors.
- Jurisdictional differences in approved indications: Not specified in available sources beyond the above indication.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Dosage should be individualized according to severity of deficiency, location and extent of bleeding, and clinical condition. Treatment should be initiated under supervision of a clinician experienced in coagulation disorders.
- Dose selection and intervals should account for differing circulating half‑lives of the individual coagulation factors contained in the product.
- Monitoring (specific factor assays and/or global tests such as INR) is required to guide dosage.

### Dosing by indication — urgent reversal of vitamin K antagonist (example dose table)
- Pre‑treatment INR 2.0–3.9:
  - Approximate dose: 1 mL/kg reconstituted product
  - Approximate dose (factor IX): 25 IU/kg
- Pre‑treatment INR 4.0–6.0:
  - Approximate dose: 1.4 mL/kg
  - Approximate dose (factor IX): 35 IU/kg
- Pre‑treatment INR > 6.0:
  - Approximate dose: 2 mL/kg
  - Approximate dose (factor IX): 50 IU/kg
- Dosing is based on body weight up to but not exceeding 100 kg. For patients >100 kg, recommended maximum single doses:
  - INR 2.0–3.9: maximum 2500 IU (factor IX)
  - INR 4.0–6.0: maximum 3500 IU (factor IX)
  - INR >6.0: maximum 5000 IU (factor IX)
- Correction of VKA‑induced impairment of haemostasis is commonly achieved approximately 30 minutes after injection.
- Simultaneous administration of vitamin K should be considered because vitamin K effect typically takes 4–6 hours to manifest.
- Repeated dosing for urgent reversal of vitamin K antagonists is not supported by clinical data and is therefore not recommended.

### Administration instructions
- The reconstituted product should be administered by slow intravenous injection/infusion via a separate injection/infusion line.
- Maximum recommended infusion rate: not to exceed 3 IU/kg body weight/minute; maximum 210 IU/minute (approximately 8 mL/minute).
- The reconstituted solution must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration; do not use if cloudy or containing deposits or if a clot/gel has formed.
- Reconstitution and withdrawal must be performed under aseptic conditions. Use immediately after reconstitution (product contains no antimicrobial preservative). Although physico‑chemical stability of the reconstituted solution has been demonstrated for up to 24 hours at room temperature, it is recommended to use immediately and to discard any unused solution.
- Pooling of multiple vials for a single infusion via an appropriate infusion device is permitted if required.
- Do not dilute the reconstituted product.

### Reconstitution (summary)
- Bring diluent to room temperature.
- Use an appropriate vial transfer device (Mix2Vial™ style described in the source): attach to diluent vial, transfer diluent into product vial, gently swirl until dissolved; do not shake.
- Withdraw solution into sterile syringe using aseptic technique; avoid drawing blood into the syringe containing product (risk of clot formation).
- Use single patient, single occasion only.

### Titration schedules
- Titration should be guided by repeated coagulation measurements (INR or specific factor assays) and by clinical response. Specific titration schedules are not provided in the available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List of all contraindications reported in available sources:
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance (human prothrombin complex) or to any of the excipients.
- Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC): prothrombin complex preparations should only be used after termination of the consumptive state; as such DIC is a contraindication unless consumptive state is terminated.
- Known history of Heparin‑Induced Thrombocytopenia (HIT) — product contains heparin.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed/Highlighted warnings
- **Risk of thrombosis and disseminated intravascular coagulation**: administration may be associated with thromboembolic events, including fatal outcomes. Use only when clinically indicated and monitor closely.

### General warnings and precautions
- Seek specialist advice from clinicians experienced in coagulation disorder management.
- In patients on vitamin K antagonists, use only when rapid correction is required (eg major bleeding, emergency surgery). For non‑urgent situations, consider withholding/reducing vitamin K antagonist dose or giving vitamin K.
- Patients treated with vitamin K antagonists may have underlying hypercoagulable conditions; replacement of coagulation factors may exacerbate thrombosis risk.
- Use with caution, and only if the potential benefit outweighs risk, in patients with:
  - History of coronary heart disease or myocardial infarction
  - Liver disease
  - Peri‑ or post‑operative patients
  - Neonates
  - Patients at risk of thromboembolic phenomena or DIC
  - Patients with simultaneous inhibitor deficiency
- Product may not be suitable for patients with recent (within prior 3 months) thromboembolic events, myocardial infarction, DIC, cerebrovascular accident, transient ischaemic attack, unstable angina, or severe peripheral vascular disease (study population exclusions noted).
- Hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis/anaphylactic shock, have been observed; stop administration immediately if such reactions occur and institute appropriate emergency measures.
- Replacement therapy can lead to development of inhibitory antibodies to one or more prothrombin complex factors; consider specialist referral if poor clinical response suggests inhibitors.
- Heparin content: product contains up to 200 IU heparin per 100 mL reconstituted solution — consider when interpreting heparin‑sensitive clotting tests and in patients with recent heparin exposure.
- Sodium content: product contains up to 343 mg sodium (~15 mmol) per 100 mL reconstituted solution — account for in patients on sodium‑restricted diets.
- Pathogen safety: product is manufactured from human plasma with donor selection, screening, pasteurisation (60°C for 10 hours) and nanofiltration steps; however, the risk of transmission of infectious agents cannot be completely excluded. Consider hepatitis A and B vaccination for patients receiving repeated/regular plasma‑derived products.
- Record product name and batch number each time product is administered to maintain traceability.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor INR and/or specific coagulation factor levels before and after administration to guide dosing and assess response.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of thrombosis and DIC.
- In patients receiving products containing heparin, consider heparin exposure history and monitor platelet counts for HIT (platelet count drop >50% or new thromboembolic complications may indicate HIT).
- Monitor vital signs and observe for allergic reactions during and after infusion.
- Monitor for clinical signs of fluid overload, particularly in patients at risk (cardiac/renal impairment).

### Specific clinical precautions
- In patients with DIC, replacement of coagulation factors should only be done after consumptive state is controlled (eg underlying cause treated, antithrombin III normalised).
- Resumption of anticoagulation after reversal should be considered as soon as clinically appropriate to reduce risk from the underlying indication for anticoagulation.

## 7. Adverse Effects

- All adverse effects listed in the available sources are included below. Frequencies are as reported in clinical trial tables where available (percentages reflect the reported trial incidence in the product group) or as frequency categories when provided in consolidated safety tables. The frequency convention used in the source: very common (≥1/10), common (≥1/100 and <1/10), uncommon (≥1/1,000 and <1/100), rare (≥1/10,000 and <1/1,000), very rare (<1/10,000), not known (cannot be estimated).

### Summary of important serious adverse effects
- Thromboembolic events (arterial and venous), including myocardial infarction, cerebral ischaemia/stroke, deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, microthrombosis (may be fatal).
- Disseminated intravascular coagulation.
- Anaphylactic and severe hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylactic shock.
- Development of inhibitory antibodies to coagulation factors.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacodynamic interaction: **Neutralises the anticoagulant effect of vitamin K antagonists (VKAs)** — administration reverses VKA anticoagulation; concomitant vitamin K should be considered to sustain correction once VKA reversal is required.
- Laboratory interactions: product contains heparin; clotting tests sensitive to heparin may be affected following administration. Heparin content must be taken into account when interpreting such assays.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Clinically significant interactions and management:
  - When reversing VKA therapy, consider both immediate reversal with this product and administration of vitamin K for sustained reversal; monitor INR to guide further therapy.
  - Be cautious with other pro‑thrombotic therapies or pre‑existing prothrombotic conditions; weigh benefits vs thrombosis risk.
- Jurisdiction‑specific interactions: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- Replacement therapy with vitamin K‑dependent coagulation factors II, VII, IX and X (and proteins C and S) restores components of the prothrombin complex and corrects coagulation deficits, thereby neutralising the anticoagulant effect of vitamin K antagonists.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Specific pharmacokinetic parameters (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination) are not specified in available sources.
- The constituent coagulation factors have differing circulating half‑lives; dosing intervals should consider these differences.
- Correction of anticoagulation is commonly observed approximately 30 minutes after intravenous administration.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Specific activity of factor IX in the reconstituted product: 2.5 IU per mg total protein (as specified for the product composition).
- Total protein content of reconstituted solution: 6–14 mg/mL.
- Activities are measured against current WHO standards.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Two pivotal randomized controlled trials (prospective, randomized, open‑label, active‑controlled, multicentre, non‑inferiority design) are reported in the available sources:
  - Acute major bleeding trial:
    - Enrolled 212 subjects total; 103 treated with the product and 109 with plasma.
    - Ages ranged from 26 to 96 years.
    - Reported safety data include adverse event profiles and thromboembolic events; product group experienced possible TEEs in 9/103 (8.7%) when tabulating certain events; overall TEE incidence figures presented in safety tables.
  - Urgent surgery / invasive procedures trial:
    - Enrolled 176 subjects total; 88 treated with the product and 88 with plasma.
    - Ages ranged from 27 to 94 years.
    - Safety data and TEE rates presented similarly.
- Primary efficacy outcomes and statistical endpoints are not specified in available sources; trials are described as non‑inferiority against plasma.
- Reported trial safety findings include the adverse event frequencies listed in Section 7, and the trials formed the basis for safety summaries (including thromboembolic event rates and other adverse events).
