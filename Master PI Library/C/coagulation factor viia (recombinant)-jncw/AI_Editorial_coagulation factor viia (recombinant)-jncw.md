# Editorial Product Information: coagulation factor viia (recombinant)-jncw

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **coagulation factor viia (recombinant)-jncw**
- Chemical class: Recombinant activated coagulation Factor VII (protein biologic) produced by recombinant DNA technology
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2020.  
- Last revision/update of the available PI: November 2025 (as stated in the source).  
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.  
- Registration/approval dates for jurisdictions other than the United States: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment and control of bleeding episodes in adults and adolescents 12 years of age and older with hemophilia A or B with inhibitors.  
  - Note: Indication as stated in the available source applies to the United States. Approvals in other jurisdictions are not specified in available sources.
- Limitations of use:
  - **Not indicated** for treatment of congenital factor VII deficiency.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General principles
- For intravenous use only after reconstitution. Initiate treatment as soon as a bleeding event occurs. Dose, frequency and duration should be guided by clinical response and hemostasis assessment. Laboratory coagulation assays (PT/INR, aPTT, FVII:C) do not reliably predict clinical hemostatic effectiveness.
- Consider alternative treatments if adequate control of bleeding is not achieved within 24 hours of the first administration.

### Recommended dosing (adult and adolescent ≥12 years)
- Mild or moderate bleeding (joint, superficial muscle, soft tissue, mucous membrane):
  - Option A: 75 mcg/kg repeated every 3 hours until hemostasis is achieved.
  - Option B: Initial single dose 225 mcg/kg. If hemostasis is not achieved within 9 hours after a 225 mcg/kg initial dose, additional 75 mcg/kg doses may be administered every 3 hours as needed.
- Severe bleeding (life- or limb-threatening hemorrhage; iliopsoas or deep muscle with neurovascular compromise; retroperitoneal; intracranial; gastrointestinal):
  - Initial dose 225 mcg/kg.
  - If needed, repeat dosing 6 hours after the initial dose with 75 mcg/kg every 2 hours until hemostasis is achieved.
- After achieving hemostasis: continue dosing as clinically indicated to support healing and prevent re-bleeding; base duration on bleeding site, severity, and concomitant therapies; weigh thrombotic risk before additional dosing.

### Reconstitution (practical clinical instructions)
- Use aseptic technique. Bring product and diluent to room temperature before reconstitution.
- The specified diluent volume corresponding to vial potency (as stated in the source) produces a final concentration of approximately 1 mg/mL after reconstitution:
  - 1 mg vial: 1.1 mL sterile water for injection
  - 2 mg vial: 2.2 mL sterile water for injection
  - 5 mg vial: 5.2 mL sterile water for injection
- Inject the entire volume of diluent into each vial and mix gently (do not shake) until the powder is fully dissolved. The reconstituted solution is clear to slightly opaque; do not use if particulate matter remains or if discoloration is observed.
- After reconstitution, each mL contains approximately 1 mg (1000 mcg) coagulation factor viia (recombinant)-jncw.
- Do not freeze reconstituted solution or store in a syringe.
- Infuse the reconstituted solution within 4 hours of reconstitution; discard any unused product after 4 hours.

### Administration
- Administer as an intravenous bolus infusion over 2 minutes or less (rapid bolus). Do not mix with other infusion solutions.
- Use a syringe sufficient to deliver the prescribed dose. Inspect visually prior to administration.

### Titration / dose adjustment
- Adjust dosing based on clinical response and hemostasis evaluation.
- Reduce dose or discontinue treatment if there is laboratory confirmation of intravascular coagulation or clinical thrombosis, depending on patient condition.
- Dose adjustment may be needed if other procoagulant therapies were recently administered.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known allergy to rabbits or rabbit proteins (exposure can result in severe hypersensitivity).  
- Severe hypersensitivity reaction to coagulation factor viia (recombinant)-jncw or any component of the product.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning
- **WARNING: THROMBOSIS** — Serious arterial and venous thrombotic events may occur following administration of **coagulation factor viia (recombinant)-jncw**. [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- Thrombosis:
  - Serious arterial and venous thrombosis can occur with Factor VIIa-containing products including this product.
  - Patients with additional risk factors for thrombosis (history of atherosclerotic disease, coronary artery disease, cerebrovascular disease, prior thromboembolism, crush injury, septicemia) or those receiving concomitant procoagulant concentrates (activated prothrombin complex concentrate [aPCC] or non-activated PCC) may have an increased risk.
  - Monitor patients for signs and symptoms of thrombosis or activation of the coagulation system. If intravascular coagulation is confirmed or clinical thrombosis occurs, reduce dose or stop treatment based on clinical judgment.
- Hypersensitivity and infusion-related reactions:
  - Hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, may occur. Symptoms can include urticaria, itching, rash, dyspnea, oropharyngeal swelling, chest tightness, wheeze, dizziness, syncope, and hypotension.
  - Patients with known IgE-based hypersensitivity to casein may be at higher risk.
  - Discontinue and treat according to standard clinical practice if hypersensitivity occurs.
- Neutralizing antibodies:
  - Neutralizing antibodies to Factor VIIa may develop. If expected hemostatic response is not achieved, consider antibody testing as clinically indicated.
- Laboratory tests:
  - PT/INR, aPTT and FVII:C do not necessarily correlate with clinical response; do not rely solely on these parameters to guide therapy.
- Use in patients with co-administration of other procoagulants:
  - Concomitant use with activated prothrombin complex concentrates is associated with elevated risk of serious thrombotic events; use caution and monitor closely.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for clinical signs of thrombosis and for activation of the coagulation system.
- Observe for hypersensitivity or infusion-related reactions during and after administration.
- Clinical hemostasis assessment should guide dosing and duration rather than routine coagulation assay normalization.

Avoid simultaneous administration; if co-administration is considered, monitor closely for thrombosis and coagulation activation. [General statement from available source]

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and effectiveness established in patients ≥12 years of age for treatment and control of bleeding episodes. Limited clinical data were collected in adolescents (≥12 to <18 years; five patients treated for 79 mild/moderate bleeding episodes while <18).

### Elderly use

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **coagulation factor viia (recombinant)-jncw** is an activated recombinant analogue of human Factor VIIa. In the presence of calcium and phospholipids, Factor VIIa forms a complex with tissue factor (TF) to activate Factor X to Factor Xa, thereby bypassing the need for Factors VIII or IX and promoting thrombin generation and fibrin clot formation. Factor VIIa may also activate coagulation on activated platelet surfaces in a TF-independent manner.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Demonstrates dose- and concentration-dependent effects on coagulation laboratory measures including shortening of aPTT and PT, increased thrombin generation (with platelets), and increased maximum clot firmness on ROTEM-FIBTEM testing.
- Routine coagulation tests (PT/INR, aPTT, FVII:C) do not reliably predict clinical hemostatic effectiveness.

### Pharmacokinetics
- A pharmacokinetic evaluation was performed in an open-label, randomized dose-comparison study in 28 adult patients (ages 18–75) with hemophilia A with or without inhibitors who were not actively bleeding; single intravenous doses of 75 mcg/kg or 225 mcg/kg were studied.
- Detailed PK parameters (e.g., Cmax, AUC, clearance, half-life) are not specified in the available source text provided here.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Two clinical studies (Study 1 and Study 2) contributed to safety and efficacy data described in the available source:
  - Total of 42 patients with hemophilia A or B with or without inhibitors received the product.
  - Study 1: 27 patients treated at 75 mcg/kg and 225 mcg/kg dose levels.
  - Study 2: 15 patients treated at escalating dose levels (25 mcg/kg, 75 mcg/kg, 225 mcg/kg).
  - Adolescent subgroup (≥12 to <18 years): five patients contributed data (treated for 79 mild/moderate bleeding episodes while under 18); hemostatic efficacy in this subgroup was comparable to the overall population.
  - A separate pediatric trial in patients aged 6 months to <12 years did not demonstrate effectiveness.
- Primary outcomes and full trial design details (randomization, blinding, primary efficacy endpoints and statistical results) are not fully specified in the available source text provided here.
