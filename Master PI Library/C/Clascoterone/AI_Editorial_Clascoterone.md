# Editorial Product Information: Clascoterone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Clascoterone (an androgen receptor inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** WINLEVI [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Topical cream containing 10 mg of clascoterone per gram (1% w/w) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharma ANZ Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acne Vulgaris:** Indicated for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in patients aged 12 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Paediatrics $\ge$ 12 years):** Apply a thin, uniform layer twice daily, once in the morning and once in the evening.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - The entire area prone to acne should be treated; "spot treating" individual lesions is discouraged for optimal efficacy.
    - Skin should be cleansed and gently dried before application.
    - Hands must be washed both before and after applying the cream.
    - Avoid application to mucous membranes (eyes, lips, mouth, or nose). If contact occurs, rinse thoroughly with water.
    - Do not apply to broken skin, including cuts, abrasions, eczematous skin, or sunburned areas.
- **Missed Dose:** Apply the next dose at the scheduled time. Do not double the dose to compensate for a missed application.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to clascoterone or any excipients in the formulation (e.g., cetyl alcohol, propylene glycol) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal (HPA) Axis Suppression:** Clascoterone is structurally related to non-androgen corticosteroids. Reversible HPA axis suppression has been observed during and after treatment, particularly under maximal use conditions (high dose or large surface area). If suppression occurs, withdrawal of the drug should be attempted.
- **Local Skin Reactions:** Users may experience erythema, scaling, pruritus, or stinging. Use of concomitant potentially irritating topical products (medicated soaps, abrasive cleansers, or products with high alcohol/astringent concentrations) should be limited.
- **Systemic Absorption:** Risk increases with use over large surface areas, prolonged use, or the use of occlusive dressings.
- **Monitoring:** The product is subject to additional monitoring in some jurisdictions to allow quick identification of new safety information [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Local skin reactions were the most common adverse events, occurring at rates similar to the vehicle control in clinical trials.

| Adverse Event | Frequency (WINLEVI 1%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Erythema/redness (12.2%), Scaling/dryness (10.5%), Pruritus (7.7%), Stinging/burning (4.2%), Edema (3.6%), Striae rubrae (2.5%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Skin atrophy (1.6%), Nasopharyngitis (1.4%), Telangiectasia (1.2%) |

- **Serious/Systemic Effects:**
    - **Hyperkalemia:** Shifts from normal to elevated potassium levels were observed in 3.7% of treated patients (compared to 3.9% for vehicle) in early-phase studies [Australian].
    - **Paediatric-specific:** Headache was reported in 1.3% of adolescent patients [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** In vitro studies suggest clascoterone does not inhibit major CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, and 3A4) at clinically relevant concentrations.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No formal clinical drug interaction studies have been conducted. Caution is advised when using other irritating topical acne therapies concurrently.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Clascoterone should not be used during pregnancy. Animal studies (subcutaneous administration) have shown malformations (omphalocele, cerebral ventricle dilation) and embryofetal lethality at doses several times the human equivalent topical dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of clascoterone in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. The clinical need for the mother should be weighed against potential risks to the infant.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed increased pre-implantation loss and reduced sperm counts at high subcutaneous doses (35 times the human topical dose). No effect was seen at lower doses (7 times the human dose).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in patients aged 12 to 18 years. Use in children under 12 is not recommended due to insufficient data and a higher observed frequency of HPA axis suppression in younger children during maximal use studies [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include patients $\ge$ 65 years. Dosing should be cautious, generally starting at the low end of the range, reflecting higher frequencies of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dosing adjustments are provided in available sources, though caution in the elderly is noted regarding these functions.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A topical androgen receptor inhibitor. It competes with androgens, specifically dihydrotestosterone (DHT), for binding to androgen receptors within the sebaceous gland and hair follicles, thereby limiting the pro-acneogenic effects of these hormones.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Steady-state plasma concentrations are reached by Day 5 of twice-daily application.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is approximately 84–89%.
    - **Metabolism:** The primary metabolite is cortexolone (an intermediate in glucocorticoid synthesis with weak glucocorticoid activity).
    - **Elimination:** Excretion pathways in humans are not yet fully characterised.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Two identical Phase 3, multicentre, randomised, double-blind, vehicle-controlled trials enrolled 1,440 patients (9 years and older, though the indication is for $\ge$ 12 years) with moderate to severe acne.
- **Primary Outcomes:** At Week 12, "IGA Success" (defined as $\ge$ 2-point reduction in Investigator’s Global Assessment and a score of 0 or 1) was significantly higher in the clascoterone group (18.8% and 20.9%) compared to vehicle (8.7% and 6.6%). Significant reductions in both inflammatory and non-inflammatory lesion counts were also achieved.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Prior to Dispensing:** Store in a refrigerator between 2°C and 8°C. Do not freeze [Australian].
- **After Dispensing:** Patients should store the product at room temperature (below 25°C). Do not freeze.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused product 6 months after the date of dispensing [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:**
    - **Australian:** Schedule 4 (S4) – Prescription Only Medicine.
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription only [Australian].
