# Editorial Product Information: Cetuximab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information from other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source materials for this specific synthesis; however, references to specific international data (e.g., Japanese post-marketing surveillance) contained within the provided document are included where clinically relevant.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing or administering this medication in a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cetuximab (rmc)
- **Chemical Class:** Chimaeric monoclonal antibody (IgG1 subclass)
- **Brand Name(s):** **Erbitux** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01FE01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Solution for intravenous infusion (sterile, preservative-free).
  - 5 mg/mL concentration.
  - Available in 20 mL (100 mg) and 100 mL (500 mg) single-use vials [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion via pump, gravity drip, or syringe pump.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Metastatic Colorectal Cancer (mCRC):**
- Treatment of patients with EGFR-expressing, RAS wild-type mCRC:
  - In combination with infusional 5-fluorouracil/folinic acid plus irinotecan (FOLFIRI).
  - In combination with irinotecan in patients refractory to first-line chemotherapy.
  - In combination with FOLFOX as first-line therapy.
  - As monotherapy in patients who have failed or are intolerant to both oxaliplatin- and irinotecan-based regimens.

**Squamous Cell Cancer of the Head and Neck (SCCHN):**
- In combination with radiation therapy for locally advanced disease.
- In combination with platinum-based chemotherapy for recurrent and/or metastatic disease.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
**Cetuximab** requires premedication with an antihistamine and a corticosteroid at least one hour prior to the first dose. Premedication is recommended for all subsequent doses.

**Weekly Regimen:**
- **Initial dose:** 400 mg/m² body surface area (BSA) administered as a 120-minute infusion.
- **Subsequent weekly doses:** 250 mg/m² BSA administered as a 60-minute infusion.

**Every Two Weeks Regimen:**
- **Initial and subsequent doses:** 500 mg/m² BSA administered as a 120-minute infusion.

### Administration Instructions
- The maximum infusion rate must not exceed 10 mg/min.
- **Cetuximab** must be administered under the supervision of a physician experienced in antineoplastic therapy.
- Patients must be monitored during the infusion and for at least one hour post-infusion.
- A separate infusion line must be used; the line must be flushed with sterile 0.9% sodium chloride at the conclusion of the infusion.

### Dose Modifications
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRR):** 
  - Mild/Moderate (Grade 1-2): Reduce the infusion rate. Maintain the lower rate for subsequent infusions.
  - Severe (Grade 3-4): Stop infusion immediately and discontinue treatment permanently.
- **Skin Reactions:** 
  - If a severe (Grade 3) reaction occurs, withhold treatment for up to 2 weeks. 
  - If resolved to Grade 2, resume at the same dose (1st occurrence), 80% of original dose (2nd occurrence), or 60% of original dose (3rd occurrence).
  - Discontinue permanently if a 4th severe reaction occurs or if the reaction does not resolve to Grade 2 during the treatment interruption.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known severe (Grade 3 or 4) hypersensitivity to **cetuximab**.
- Combination with oxaliplatin-containing chemotherapy in patients with mutant RAS mCRC or unknown RAS status [Australian].
- Contraindications for any concomitantly used chemotherapy or radiation therapy must also be observed.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs):** Severe IRRs (including anaphylaxis) may occur commonly and can be fatal. Some reactions are linked to preformed IgE antibodies (e.g., in patients with a history of red meat allergy or tick bites). Resuscitation equipment must be available.
- **Respiratory Disorders:** Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD) has been reported, including fatal cases. While the majority were noted in Japanese post-marketing surveillance, all patients should be monitored for new or worsening pulmonary symptoms. Discontinue if ILD is diagnosed.
- **Skin Reactions:** Occur in >80% of patients, typically presenting as acne-like rash. Secondary superinfections (e.g., *S. aureus*) may lead to cellulitis or necrotising fasciitis.
- **Electrolyte Disturbances:** Progressive hypomagnesaemia is frequent and may be severe. Hypokalaemia and hypocalcaemia (especially when combined with platinum-chemo) also occur. Monitor electrolytes prior to and periodically during treatment.
- **Cardiovascular Disorders:** Increased frequency of severe cardiovascular events has been observed. Caution is required in patients with poor performance status or pre-existing cardiac disease, particularly when used with fluoropyrimidines.
- **Eye Disorders:** Keratitis and ulcerative keratitis have been reported. Immediate ophthalmological consultation is required for suggestive symptoms.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
  - Skin reactions (80%+); hypomagnesaemia; mild-to-moderate infusion-related reactions; mucositis (sometimes leading to epistaxis); increased liver enzymes (AST, ALT, AP).
- **Common (1–10%):** 
  - Severe infusion-related reactions (sometimes fatal); dehydration; hypocalcaemia; anorexia; headache; diarrhoea; nausea; vomiting; fatigue; hand-foot syndrome (when used with fluoropyrimidines).
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** 
  - Blepharitis; keratitis; pulmonary embolism; deep vein thrombosis; cardiac ischaemia/myocardial infarction (when used with fluoropyrimidines).
- **Rare (<0.1%):** 
  - Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD).
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** 
  - Stevens-Johnson syndrome/Toxic epidermal necrolysis.
- **Frequency Not Known:** 
  - Aseptic meningitis; superinfection of skin lesions; severe neutropenia/leukopenia (when used with platinum-based chemo).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** 
  - **With Fluoropyrimidines:** Increased frequency of cardiac ischaemia and hand-foot syndrome compared to fluoropyrimidines alone.
  - **With Platinum-based chemotherapy:** Increased frequency of severe leukopenia/neutropenia and associated infectious complications (febrile neutropenia, sepsis).
  - **With Radiation Therapy (Head/Neck):** Increased frequency of typical radiation toxicities (mucositis, radiation dermatitis).
  - **With Capecitabine and Oxaliplatin (XELOX):** May increase the frequency of severe diarrhoea.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No specific pharmacokinetic interaction studies with other medicines have been detailed in the provided sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. EGFR is involved in fetal development. Animal studies show placental transfer and increased incidence of abortion. **Cetuximab** is not recommended during pregnancy. Effective contraception is required during and for 2 months after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established. It is recommended that women do not breastfeed during treatment and for 2 months after the last dose.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in monkeys showed impairment of menstrual cycling. Human data are not available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Effectiveness in patients <18 years has not been established. Phase I data showed no new safety signals.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required in patients ≥65 years. However, experience is limited in those ≥75 years. Elderly patients with pre-existing cardiac disease are at higher risk for adverse events.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Only patients with adequate function have been studied (Creatinine ≤1.5x, Transaminases ≤5x, and Bilirubin ≤1.5x upper limit of normal). Use in severe impairment is not specified.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A chimaeric IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds to the Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor (EGFR) with higher affinity than endogenous ligands. This binding inhibits receptor phosphorylation and downstream signalling, leading to inhibited cell growth, induction of apoptosis, and reduced neo-vascularisation. It also mediates Antibody-Dependent Cellular Cytotoxicity (ADCC).
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
  - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately equivalent to the vascular space (2.9 L/m²).
  - **Elimination:** Exhibits non-linear pharmacokinetics. Clearance plateaus at doses >200 mg/m². Half-life is 70 to 100 hours at the target dose. Stable serum levels are reached after 3 weeks of monotherapy.
  - **Metabolism:** Biodegradation into small peptides and amino acids.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CRYSTAL Study (mCRC):** FOLFIRI + **Cetuximab** significantly improved Progression-Free Survival (PFS) and Overall Survival (OS) in RAS wild-type patients compared to FOLFIRI alone.
- **OPUS Study (mCRC):** FOLFOX4 + **Cetuximab** improved Objective Response Rate (ORR) in RAS wild-type patients but showed a negative benefit-risk balance in RAS mutant patients.
- **EXTREME Study (SCCHN):** **Cetuximab** + platinum/5-FU significantly increased PFS and OS (median increase of 2.3 and 2.7 months respectively) in recurrent/metastatic disease.
- **EMR 62 202-006 (SCCHN):** **Cetuximab** + Radiation Therapy significantly improved locoregional control (24.4 vs 14.9 months) and OS (49.0 vs 29.3 months) in locally advanced disease.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze.
- **In-use stability:** Chemical and physical stability has been demonstrated for 48 hours at 25°C. To reduce microbiological risk, it should be used immediately; if not, it can be held at 2°C to 8°C for no more than 24 hours.
