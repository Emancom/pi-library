# Editorial Product Information: Chlorambucil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Chlorambucil (an aromatic nitrogen mustard derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Bifunctional alkylating agent.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Leukeran** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01AA02 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for the active ingredient).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 2 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
The following indications are approved in the **Australian** jurisdiction:
- Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL).
- Waldenström's macroglobulinaemia (regarded as a treatment of choice).
- Hodgkin's disease.
- Specific forms of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma.
- Advanced ovarian adenocarcinoma.
- Advanced breast cancer (significant effect observed in a proportion of patients).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- **Leukeran** must only be administered under the supervision of clinicians experienced in cytotoxic therapy.
- **Dosing Instruction:** Tablets should be taken daily on an empty stomach (at least one hour before or three hours after a meal) [Australian].
- **Handling:** Tablets must not be divided. If the outer coating is intact, there is no risk in handling; however, standard cytotoxic handling guidelines should be followed.

### Specific Indications [Australian]
- **Hodgkin’s Disease:** 0.2 mg/kg/day for 4–8 weeks when used as monotherapy. Often used in combination regimens.
- **Non-Hodgkin’s Lymphoma:** Initially 0.1 to 0.2 mg/kg/day for 4–8 weeks; maintenance is achieved via reduced daily doses or intermittent courses.
- **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia:** Therapy usually begins once symptoms appear or bone marrow function is impaired. Initial dose: 0.15 mg/kg/day until the leucocyte count drops to 10,000/mcL. Treatment may resume 4 weeks later at 0.1 mg/kg/day.
- **Waldenström’s Macroglobulinaemia:** 6 to 12 mg daily until leucopenia occurs, followed by 2 to 8 mg daily indefinitely.
- **Ovarian Carcinoma:** 0.2 mg/kg/day for 4–6 weeks as monotherapy.
- **Advanced Breast Cancer:** 0.2 mg/kg/day for 6 weeks. May be used in combination with prednisolone, or with methotrexate, 5-fluorouracil, and prednisolone.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** To chlorambucil or any excipients [Australian].
- **Prior Resistance:** Should not be used in patients whose disease has demonstrated prior resistance to the agent [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cytotoxicity:** Chlorambucil is an active cytotoxic agent.
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** May cause irreversible bone marrow suppression. Blood counts must be closely monitored. Neutrophil counts may continue to fall for 10 days or more after the final dose [Australian].
- **Seizure Risk:** Increased risk in children with nephrotic syndrome, patients on high pulse-dosing, and those with a history of epilepsy or head trauma.
- **Secondary Malignancies:** Associated with increased risk of acute secondary haematologic malignancies (leukaemia and myelodysplastic syndrome), particularly after long-term use.
- **Vaccinations:** Immunisation with live organism vaccines is not recommended in immunocompromised hosts due to infection risk [Australian].
- **Stem Cell Transplantation:** Long-term use should be avoided in patients who are potential candidates for autologous stem cell transplantation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Purine Nucleoside Analogues:** (e.g., fludarabine, pentostatin, cladribine) have shown increased chlorambucil cytotoxicity *in vitro*; clinical significance is undetermined [Australian].
- **Phenylbutazone:** Animal studies suggest phenylbutazone may enhance toxicity, potentially necessitating a dose reduction of chlorambucil [Australian].
- **Live Vaccines:** Increased risk of infection; contraindicated/not recommended [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Potential for spontaneous abortion, foetal loss, and birth defects. Use should be avoided, especially during the first trimester.
- **Breastfeeding:** Mothers receiving chlorambucil should not breastfeed [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May suppress ovarian function/cause amenorrhoea. Azoospermia may occur; it is estimated that a total dose of ≥400 mg is required for this effect. Recovery of spermatogenesis varies [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Adequate contraceptive measures are advised when either partner is receiving treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Used for Hodgkin’s disease and non-Hodgkin’s lymphomas. Dosing regimens are similar to adult protocols [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific studies. Recommended to monitor renal and hepatic function closely and initiate therapy at the low end of the dosing range [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires close monitoring for additional myelosuppression associated with azotaemia [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Primarily metabolised in the liver. Dose reduction should be considered in cases of severe/gross hepatic dysfunction, though specific dosing guidelines are unavailable [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Acts as a bifunctional alkylating agent. It forms a highly reactive ethylenimonium radical, leading to cross-linkage between DNA strands and interference with replication.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and completely absorbed from the GI tract. Peak plasma levels occur 0.25–2 hours post-dose.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via beta-oxidation of the butyric acid side chain. The major active metabolite is **phenylacetic acid mustard (PAAM)**.
    - **Distribution:** 99% protein-bound (specifically albumin).
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is approximately 1.3 hours for chlorambucil and 1.8 hours for PAAM. Urinary excretion is low [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate) [Australian].
- **Stability:** Do not freeze. Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** 36 months [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
