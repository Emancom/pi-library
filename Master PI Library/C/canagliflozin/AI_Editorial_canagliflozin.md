# Editorial Product Information: Canagliflozin

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **canagliflozin** for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory guidance.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Canagliflozin (as hemihydrate).
*   **Chemical Class:** Sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor.
*   **Brand Name(s):** **INVOKANA** [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   100 mg film-coated tablets: Yellow, capsule-shaped, debossed with "CFZ" and "100" [US].
    *   300 mg film-coated tablets: White, capsule-shaped, debossed with "CFZ" and "300" [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Janssen Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Glycemic Control:** As an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults and pediatric patients aged 10 years and older with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) [US].
*   **Cardiovascular Risk Reduction:** To reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), including cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, and nonfatal stroke, in adults with T2DM and established cardiovascular disease [US].
*   **Renal and Heart Failure Protection:** To reduce the risk of end-stage kidney disease (ESKD), doubling of serum creatinine, cardiovascular death, and hospitalization for heart failure in adults with T2DM and diabetic nephropathy with albuminuria >300 mg/day [US].
*   **Limitations of Use:** Not recommended for T2DM patients with an eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m² for glycemic control, nor for patients with type 1 diabetes mellitus [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
*   **Adults and Pediatrics ($\ge$10 years):** The recommended starting dose is 100 mg taken once daily, before the first meal of the day [US].
*   **Dose Titration:** For patients requiring additional glycemic control who are tolerating 100 mg and have an eGFR $\ge$60 mL/min/1.73 m², the dose may be increased to 300 mg once daily [US].
*   **Renal and CV Risk Reduction:** For indications other than glycemic control in adults, the recommended dose is 100 mg once daily [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Assess renal function and volume status before initiation. Correct volume depletion if present [US].
*   **Surgery:** Withhold **canagliflozin** at least 3 days prior to major surgery or procedures associated with prolonged fasting to minimize ketoacidosis risk [US].
*   **Missed Dose:** Take as soon as possible. Do not double the dose to make up for a missed one [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** History of serious hypersensitivity reactions to **canagliflozin**, including anaphylaxis or angioedema [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA):** Risk is significantly increased in T1DM; however, fatal cases have occurred in T2DM. DKA may occur even with blood glucose <250 mg/dL. Monitor for metabolic acidosis and discontinue if suspected [US].
*   **Lower Limb Amputation:** An increased risk of lower limb amputations (primarily toe and midfoot) was observed in the CANVAS and CANVAS-R trials. Monitor for infections or ulcers; consider discontinuation if these occur [US].
*   **Volume Depletion:** Osmotic diuresis may cause symptomatic hypotension or acute kidney injury (AKI). Risk is higher in the elderly, patients with renal impairment, or those on loop diuretics [US].
*   **Genitourinary Infections:** Increased risk of urinary tract infections (including urosepsis and pyelonephritis) and genital mycotic infections.
*   **Fournier’s Gangrene:** Necrotizing fasciitis of the perineum has been reported. Immediate evaluation is required for patients presenting with genital/perineal pain, erythema, or swelling with fever [US].
*   **Bone Fractures:** Increased risk observed as early as 12 weeks after initiation. Consider fracture risk factors before starting therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects ($\ge$5%) [US]
*   Female genital mycotic infections (10.6%–11.6%)
*   Urinary tract infections (4.4%–5.9%)
*   Increased urination (4.6%–5.1%)

### Other Adverse Effects (2%–5%) [US]
*   Male genital mycotic infections
*   Thirst
*   Constipation
*   Nausea
*   Vulvovaginal pruritus

### Serious and Postmarketing Adverse Effects
*   Anaphylaxis and angioedema.
*   Acute Kidney Injury (AKI).
*   Necrotizing fasciitis of the perineum (Fournier’s gangrene).
*   Euglycemic ketoacidosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **UGT Enzyme Inducers (e.g., Rifampin, Phenytoin, Phenobarbital, Ritonavir):** These decrease **canagliflozin** exposure.
    *   *Management:* Increase **canagliflozin** dose to 200 mg (if tolerating 100 mg) or 300 mg (if eGFR $\ge$60) [US].
*   **Digoxin:** **Canagliflozin** increases digoxin AUC and Cmax. Monitor digoxin levels [US].
*   **Lithium:** SGLT2 inhibitors may decrease serum lithium concentrations. Monitor lithium levels more frequently [US].
*   **Insulin and Secretagogues:** Increased risk of hypoglycemia. A lower dose of insulin or secretagogue may be required [US].
*   **Laboratory Test Interference:** 
    *   Positive urine glucose tests (due to mechanism of action).
    *   Unreliable 1,5-anhydroglucitol (1,5-AG) assays [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Not recommended during the second and third trimesters. Juvenile animal studies showed adverse renal effects (pelvic and tubule dilatations) at exposures 0.5-times the human clinical dose [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Animal data shows **canagliflozin** is excreted in milk (milk/plasma ratio 1.40). Risk to the developing human kidney is possible [US].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies showed minor alterations in sperm parameters and slightly fewer corpora lutea at high doses (approx. 14–18 times human exposure), but no effect on the ability to mate [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Pediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for glycemic control in T2DM patients $\ge$10 years. Not established for patients <10 years or for CV/renal risk reduction indications [US].
*   **Geriatric Use:** Higher incidence of adverse reactions related to volume depletion and hypotension [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:**
    *   **eGFR $\ge$60:** No dose adjustment.
    *   **eGFR 30 to <60:** Maximum dose is 100 mg once daily [US].
    *   **eGFR <30:** Initiation not recommended. Patients already on 100 mg with albuminuria >300 mg/day may continue therapy to reduce renal/CV risk [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild or moderate (Child-Pugh A or B). Not recommended for severe impairment (Child-Pugh C) due to lack of clinical experience [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits SGLT2 in the proximal renal tubules, reducing reabsorption of filtered glucose and lowering the renal threshold for glucose (RTG), thereby increasing urinary glucose excretion. It also increases sodium delivery to the distal tubule, reducing intraglomerular pressure [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Bioavailability:** ~65%.
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily via O-glucuronidation (UGT1A9 and UGT2B4) to inactive metabolites. Minimal CYP3A4 metabolism (~7%).
    *   **Elimination:** Half-life of 10.6–13.1 hours. Excreted in feces (41.5%–51.7%) and urine (~33%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **CANVAS/CANVAS-R:** Large integrated analysis (N=10,134) in adults with T2DM and high CV risk. **Canagliflozin** reduced the risk of MACE (HR 0.86; 95% CI: 0.75, 0.97) but showed an increased risk of lower limb amputations and bone fractures [US].
*   **CREDENCE:** Renal outcomes trial (N=4,401) in adults with T2DM and diabetic nephropathy. **Canagliflozin** 100 mg significantly reduced the risk of the primary composite endpoint (ESKD, doubling of creatinine, or renal/CV death) with an HR of 0.70 (95% CI: 0.59, 0.82) [US].
*   **Pediatric Study:** A 52-week trial in patients aged 10 to 17 years showed a placebo-corrected HbA1c reduction of -0.73% at week 26 [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
*   Keep out of reach of children.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Prescription-only (Rx-only).
