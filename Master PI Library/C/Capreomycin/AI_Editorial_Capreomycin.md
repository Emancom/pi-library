# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the available clinical data into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Adult and Paediatric:** Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Maintenance Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### Dosing for ESRD on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### General Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Recommended weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target range 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** While the US PI states "None known," it includes warnings regarding serious hypersensitivity reactions reported post-administration [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occurred within minutes of injection [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety of **oral** levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Clinical monitoring should include regular assessment of plasma carnitine levels and overall clinical condition [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Clinical Trial Experience
In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials of ESRD patients on chronic hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality):
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting (common), abdominal pain (9%), diarrhoea (16%), gastritis (less frequent) [US].
- **Body as a Whole:** Headache (22%), infection (15%), pain (30%), fever (7%), asthenia (9%), flu syndrome (25%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%) [US].
- **Injection Site:** Injection site reaction (33%) [US].
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hypervolemia (5%) [US].
- **Other:** Body odor has been reported as a less frequent adverse reaction [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis/Hypersensitivity:** Laryngeal edema and bronchospasm.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category B]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk, the risks to the infant from excess intake must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, Saccharomyces, Schizosaccharomyces) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 (Dosage and Administration). Clinical data suggests the drug is used for inborn errors of metabolism in this population [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Levocarnitine is easily removed by dialysis. Specific dosing is provided for ESRD patients. Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral formulations are used in patients with severely compromised renal function [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, carnitine deficiency may be related to reduced intake, reduced renal synthesis, and dialytic losses. Supplemental levocarnitine increases plasma concentrations.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein bound. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg after dialysis showed that trough levels increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy. While the ability to increase carnitine concentrations was demonstrated, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial, as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's in PVC bags (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison schedule not applicable (Legend drug).
