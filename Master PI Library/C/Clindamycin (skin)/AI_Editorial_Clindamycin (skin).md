# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **US (Food and Drug Administration)**. 
- *Note: While the user context mentioned an Australian source, the provided document is a US FDA-approved label (evidenced by USP standards, NDC numbering, and US-based manufacturer details). No Australian TGA documentation was provided for comparison.*
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Sterile injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours). 
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives.

### Monitoring
- **Baseline:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating parenteral therapy.
- **Routine:** Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **General Precautions:** Chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]); however, the clinical impact of this via the intravenous route is not fully characterised [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology of metabolic disorders or ESRD.

### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in Clinical Trials)
| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain (12%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Fever (7%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Depression (5%), Paresthesia (5%) |

### Serious/Other Notable Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis occur less frequently.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (post-marketing reports) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in the source [US].
    - **Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess intake must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by mg/kg; see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. The safety of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine is not established in renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. It is readily removed by hemodialysis [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis, 20 mg/kg administered three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean plasma levocarnitine concentrations from 20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to trough levels between 54 and 180 µmol/L.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses three times weekly showed a dose-proportional increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) when stored in PVC bags at room temperature [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison Schedule: Not applicable.
