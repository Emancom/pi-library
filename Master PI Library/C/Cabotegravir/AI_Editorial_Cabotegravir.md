# Editorial Product Information: Cabotegravir

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for definitive guidance in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cabotegravir (as sodium for tablets; as free acid for injection).
- **Chemical Class:** Integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI).
- **Brand Name:** **APRETUDE** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AJ04 (Inhibitors of HIV integrase).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 30 mg cabotegravir (as sodium) [Australian].
    - **Prolonged-release suspension for injection:** 600 mg cabotegravir in a 3 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral; Gluteal intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ViiV Healthcare [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pre-exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP):** Indicated for use in at-risk adults and adolescents (weighing at least 35 kg and aged at least 12 years) to reduce the risk of sexually acquired HIV-1 infection [Australian].
    - **Requirement:** Individuals must have a documented negative HIV-1 test immediately prior to initiation and before each subsequent injection.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents $\ge$ 35 kg)
The regimen may begin directly with injections or follow an optional oral lead-in phase to assess tolerability.

#### Oral Lead-in (Optional)
- **Dose:** 30 mg once daily for at least 28 days [Australian].
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food.

#### Intramuscular Injection Schedule
- **Initiation Injections:** A single 600 mg (3 mL) gluteal IM injection at Month 1 and Month 2. If an oral lead-in was used, the first injection should occur on the last day of oral dosing or within 3 days thereafter [Australian].
- **Continuation Injections:** 600 mg (3 mL) gluteal IM injection administered every 2 months. 
- **Flexibility:** Injections may be administered up to 7 days before or after the scheduled date.

### Administration Instructions
- **Injection Site:** For gluteal intramuscular use only. Do not administer intravenously.
- **BMI Considerations:** Needle length must be sufficient to reach the gluteus muscle; clinicians should consider the patient's Body Mass Index (BMI).
- **Preparation:** Follow specific "Instructions for Use" to prevent leakage during preparation.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- **Oral Tablets:** Take the missed dose as soon as possible.
- **Injections:** Adherence is critical. If a visit is missed, clinically reassess and re-confirm a negative HIV-1 status.
    - **Oral Bridge:** If a delay of >7 days is unavoidable, oral cabotegravir (30 mg daily) may replace one scheduled injection for up to 2 months [Australian].
    - **Resumption:** If the time since the last injection is $\le$ 3 months, resume 600 mg as soon as possible. If >3 months, the initiation phase (two injections one month apart) must be restarted [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **HIV-1 Status:** Known positive status or unknown HIV-1 status [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to cabotegravir or any excipients (e.g., lactose in tablets).
- **Concomitant Medications:** Co-administration with the following potent UGT1A1 inducers, which significantly decrease cabotegravir plasma concentrations:
    - **Anticonvulsants:** Carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, phenobarbital, phenytoin.
    - **Antimycobacterials:** Rifampicin, rifapentine.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **HIV-1 Resistance Risk:** There is a significant risk of developing resistance if HIV-1 is acquired shortly before or during treatment, or during the "pharmacokinetic tail" after discontinuation. Frequent HIV testing (at least every 3 months) is mandatory [Australian].
- **Comprehensive Prevention Strategy:** Cabotegravir is not 100% effective and must be used as part of a strategy including safe sex practices, condom use, and regular STI screening.
- **Long-acting Properties:** Residual concentrations may persist in systemic circulation for 12 months or longer after a single injection. This must be considered if the drug is discontinued or if pregnancy occurs.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Reported in patients with and without pre-existing liver disease. Monitor liver transaminases; discontinue if hepatotoxicity is suspected.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Discontinue immediately if signs of systemic hypersensitivity (e.g., severe rash, fever, organ dysfunction, facial oedema) occur.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are categorised by frequency. Injection site reactions (ISRs) are the most characteristic effect of the injectable formulation.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site pain, headache, diarrhoea, transaminase increased (ALT/AST). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Depression, insomnia, abnormal dreams, dizziness, nausea, abdominal pain, flatulence, vomiting, rash, myalgia, fatigue, malaise, pyrexia, injection site reactions (nodule, induration, swelling, bruising, erythema). |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypersensitivity (including urticaria and angioedema), suicidal ideation/attempt (especially with history of depression), weight increased, vasovagal reactions. |
| **Very Rare (<0.1%)** | Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis [Australian]. |

*Note: ISRs generally decrease in severity and frequency over time. 2% of trial participants discontinued due to ISRs [Australian].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Profile
- **Metabolism:** Primarily mediated by UGT1A1 (minor UGT1A9 contribution). 
- **Transporters:** Substrate of P-gp and BCRP, but clinical relevance is low due to high permeability.

### Clinically Significant Interactions
- **UGT1A1 Inducers:** Rifampicin decreases cabotegravir AUC by 59%; use is contraindicated.
- **Rifabutin:** While rifabutin does not significantly affect oral cabotegravir, it reduces injection concentrations. If used concomitantly with injections, the schedule must be adjusted to monthly 600 mg doses [Australian].
- **Antacids:** Polyvalent cations (magnesium, aluminium, calcium) can reduce oral absorption. Administer antacids at least 2 hours before or 4 hours after oral cabotegravir. This interaction is not relevant to the injection.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** No clinically relevant interaction; no dose adjustment required.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1.** 
    - Human data are limited. Animal studies showed no teratogenicity but revealed reduced survival in rat offspring at high doses. 
    - Use only if the benefit outweighs the potential risk. Due to the long half-life, fetal exposure can occur for up to 12 months after the last injection.
- **Breastfeeding:** Secretion into human milk is expected (based on animal data) but not confirmed. Breastfeeding is recommended only if the benefit justifies the risk to the infant.
- **Fertility:** No human data available; no effects observed in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Safety and efficacy not established in children <12 years or weighing <35 kg [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No dose adjustment required; however, data for patients >65 years are limited.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild to severe impairment (CrCl $\ge$ 15 mL/min). Not studied in patients on dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild or moderate impairment (Child-Pugh A or B). Not studied in severe impairment (Child-Pugh C).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Cabotegravir binds to the integrase active site, blocking the strand transfer step of retroviral DNA integration.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Oral $T_{max}$ is 3 hours. Injection exhibits absorption-limited ("flip-flop") kinetics with a median $T_{max}$ of 7 days.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>99%). Present in the genital tract and rectal tissues.
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life of 41 hours (oral) vs. 5.6 to 11.5 weeks (injection). Primarily eliminated via metabolism and faecal excretion (47% unchanged). Renal elimination is minimal (<1%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **HPTN 083:** A randomised, double-blind study in HIV-uninfected men and transgender women who have sex with men. Cabotegravir demonstrated a 66% reduction in incident HIV-1 infection compared to daily oral TDF/FTC [Australian].
- **HPTN 084:** A randomised, double-blind study in HIV-uninfected cisgender women. Cabotegravir demonstrated an 88% reduction in incident HIV-1 infection compared to daily oral TDF/FTC [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C. 
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. 
- **In-use Stability:** Once drawn into a syringe, the suspension should be used as soon as possible but may be stored for up to 2 hours at room temperature. Discard if 2 hours are exceeded [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring in Australia to allow quick identification of new safety information.
