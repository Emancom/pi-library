# Editorial Product Information: Carbamide Peroxide

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial Product Information (PI) has been developed using documentation provided for the **US (FDA)** jurisdiction, specifically the OTC Drug Facts labeling for the **Quality Choice Earwax Removal Kit**.
- **Purpose:** This document serves as a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. It synthesises available data into a clinical format for quick reference.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official Product Information or local regulatory labeling required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Carbamide peroxide 6.5% (equivalent to hydrogen peroxide and urea complex).
- **Brand Names:** Quality Choice Earwax Removal Kit [US].
- **ATC Code:** S02DC (Otolologicals, Other).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Topical otic solution containing 65 mg of carbamide peroxide per 1 mL (6.5% w/v) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical (Otic).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Chain Drug Marketing Association, Inc. (Labeler); Bell Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (Registrant) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Earwax Removal:** Indicated for occasional use as an aid to soften, loosen, and remove excessive or impacted cerumen (earwax) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children >12 years)
- **Dosage:** Instill 5 to 10 drops into the affected ear(s) [US].
- **Frequency:** Twice daily for up to 4 days, or as directed by a physician [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Tilt the head sideways and instill drops.
- The applicator tip should not enter the ear canal [US].
- Maintain the head tilt or place a cotton plug in the ear to keep drops in the canal for several minutes [US].
- **Post-Treatment:** Any remaining wax after the 4-day treatment period may be removed by gently flushing the ear with warm water using a soft rubber bulb syringe [US].

## 4. Contraindications
Clinical sources indicate that carbamide peroxide should not be used in patients with the following conditions without prior medical consultation:
- Perforation (hole) of the eardrum [US].
- Recent ear surgery [US].
- Presence of ear drainage, discharge, or active ear pain [US].
- Rash or irritation in the ear canal [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Route Restriction:** For otic (ear) use only; avoid contact with the eyes [US].
- **Duration of Use:** Patients should stop use and consult a physician if symptoms persist or if the product needs to be used for more than 4 days [US].
- **Mechanical Effects:** The product foams upon contact with earwax due to the release of oxygen. Patients should be advised that a crackling sound may be heard, which is a normal occurrence [US].
- **Dizziness:** Patients experiencing dizziness should consult a healthcare provider before use [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Local Irritation:** Uncommon instances of ear irritation or rash may occur [US].
- **Sensory:** Associated crackling sound in the ear due to oxygen release (expected pharmacodynamic effect) [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Not specified in available source documents; however, excessive earwax remaining after use or persistent pain warrants medical evaluation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Management:** Concurrent use of other otic preparations should be discussed with a healthcare professional to avoid potential dilution or chemical interference.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not specified in available sources. (Note: Systemic absorption of carbamide peroxide following otic administration is negligible).
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Fertility:** Data not available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - **Children >12 years:** Refer to adult dosing [US].
    - **Children <12 years:** A physician should be consulted before use [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustments are anticipated due to the topical nature and minimal systemic absorption of the active ingredient.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Carbamide peroxide is an oxidizing agent. When applied to the ear, it releases oxygen, which acts to mechanically soften and break up cerumen. The anhydrous glycerin base further aids in softening the wax [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Topical otic application generally results in negligible systemic absorption.
    - **Metabolism:** Breaks down into urea and hydrogen peroxide; hydrogen peroxide further decomposes into water and oxygen.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The primary effect is the foaming action (oxygen release) which facilitates the removal of impacted wax.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Key pivotal trial data is not provided in the OTC Monograph labeling documents. Approval is based on the established safety and efficacy profile of carbamide peroxide 6.5% under the US FDA OTC Monograph [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 15°–30°C (59°–86°F) [US].
- **Protection:** Protect the bottle from excessive heat and direct sunlight. Keep the bottle in the outer carton [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the cap on the bottle when not in use. Do not use if the tamper-evident safety seal is broken or missing [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a Human OTC Drug (OTC Monograph Drug M014) [US].
- **Australia:** Not specifically covered by the provided US source document, though carbamide peroxide is typically unscheduled or S2 depending on concentration and pack size in the Australian Poison Standard.
