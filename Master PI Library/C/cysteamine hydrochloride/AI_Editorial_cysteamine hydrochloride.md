# Editorial Product Information: cysteamine hydrochloride

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **cysteamine hydrochloride**
- Chemical name / descriptor: mercaptamine (cysteamine) hydrochloride
- Chemical/therapeutic group: cystine‑depleting agent; ophthalmologicals, other ophthalmologicals
- ATC code: S01XA21
- Route(s) of administration: ocular (topical ophthalmic)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first approval/registration stated in available source: 28 January 2022.
- Date of last PI revision stated in available source: 16 February 2026.
- Withdrawal/suspension/restriction history: Not specified in available sources.
- Additional jurisdictional registration dates: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of corneal cystine deposits in adults and children with cystinosis.
  - Paediatric approval: may be used from 2 years of age (see Section 10).  
  - Use in children <2 years: safety and efficacy not established (no data available).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adults and paediatric ≥ 2 years)
- Recommended dose: one drop in each eye 4 times daily during waking hours.
- Recommended interval between instillations: approximately 4 hours.
- Maximum dose: do not exceed 4 drops per eye per day.
- Dose reduction: dose may be decreased progressively depending on ophthalmic findings (e.g. corneal cystine crystals, photophobia) to a minimum total daily dose of 1 drop in each eye.
- Missed dose: if an instillation is missed, continue with the next scheduled dose; do not double doses to make up for the missed one.

### Administration instructions
- For ocular use only.
- Before first use, bring the product to room temperature to facilitate administration. After first opening, keep the dropper bottle at room temperature.
- To avoid contamination, do not touch the dropper tip to the eyelids, surrounding area, or any surface.
- Follow the manufacturer’s instructions for opening the primary container and attaching the dropper applicator. Discard the dropper bottle after 7 days of use.
- If other topical ocular medicinal products are used concomitantly, allow an interval of ten minutes between successive applications; eye ointments should be administered last.
- Contact lenses: remove soft contact lenses prior to instillation and wait at least 15 minutes before reinserting contact lenses.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **cysteamine hydrochloride** (active substance) or to any of the excipients listed in the product formulation.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Contains benzalkonium chloride: may cause ocular irritation and has been associated with punctate keratopathy and/or toxic ulcerative keratopathy. Monitor patients for ocular surface toxicity.
- Contact lenses: benzalkonium chloride can discolour soft contact lenses; contact with soft contact lenses should be avoided. Patients should remove lenses before administration and wait at least 15 minutes before reinsertion.
- Do not discontinue therapy abruptly: accumulation of corneal cystine crystals increases if treatment is stopped.
- Paediatric use: safety and efficacy in children <2 years have not been established; no data available.
- Visual disturbances: temporary blurred vision or other visual disturbances may occur at instillation and can affect ability to drive or operate machinery. Patients should wait until vision clears before driving or using machines.

### Monitoring requirements
- Ocular monitoring for signs of irritation, corneal epitheliopathy, or other ocular adverse effects is recommended.
- Ophthalmic examination (e.g. assessment of corneal cystine crystals, photophobia) may guide dose adjustments.

### Specific clinical precautions
- When used concomitantly with oral cysteamine, consider cumulative systemic exposure: ocular administration contributes negligibly to systemic exposure (see Section 11), but precautions are advised when combined with oral therapy.
- Pregnancy/lactation precautions: see Section 9.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The adverse reaction profile below includes all reactions reported in clinical trials and post‑marketing programmes from the available source. Frequencies are presented using the following definitions when provided in the source: very common (≥1/10), common (≥1/100 to <1/10). Frequencies for less common categories were not specified beyond those listed.

- Paediatric population: frequency, type and severity of adverse reactions reported were the same as in adults.

### Adverse effects by System Organ Class and frequency

| System Organ Class | Very common (≥1/10) | Common (≥1/100 to <1/10) |
|---|---:|---|
| Eye disorders | eye pain; vision blurred; eye irritation; ocular hyperaemia; eye pruritus; lacrimation increased; eye deposit | abnormal sensation in eye; dry eye; foreign body sensation in eye; eyelid oedema; eyelid irritation; visual impairment; hordeolum |

- Serious adverse effects: Not specifically enumerated as distinct events in the available source beyond the categories above.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Interaction studies have not been performed.
- Pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic interactions: systemic exposure after ocular administration is anticipated to be low; the recommended total daily ocular dose is approximately 0.4% of the highest recommended oral daily dose of cysteamine. Therefore, clinically significant interactions with orally administered medicinal products are not anticipated.
- Concomitant use with oral cysteamine: although ocular systemic contribution is negligible, take precautions when oral cysteamine is used concurrently (see pregnancy/lactation and preclinical safety sections).

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category (as stated in available source): **Category B3** [Australian].
- Data and recommendations:
  - Systemic exposure following ocular administration is negligible; no effects during pregnancy and breastfeeding are anticipated from ocular use alone.
  - Teratology studies with oral cysteamine in rats at various oral doses showed embryofetotoxicity and teratogenicity (cleft palate, kyphosis, ventricular septal defects, microcephaly, exencephaly). The potential risk to humans is unknown.
  - Oral cysteamine should not be used during pregnancy, particularly during the first trimester, unless clearly necessary.
  - If pregnancy is diagnosed or planned, reconsider therapy and advise the patient of possible teratogenic risk associated with systemic/oral cysteamine.

### Breastfeeding
- Cysteamine excretion in human milk: not specified in available sources.
- Recommendation: women receiving ocular cysteamine together with oral cysteamine should not breast‑feed (based on animal data for oral exposure).

### Fertility
- Human data: not available.
- Animal data: oral dosing in rats at 375 mg/kg/day (≥~2000 times the recommended human ocular dose on a body surface area basis) reduced fertility and offspring survival; no effects on fertility at 75 mg/kg/day (≥~400 times the recommended ocular dose on a body surface area basis).

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- May be used from 2 years of age at the same dose as adults.
- Safety and efficacy have not been established in children <2 years (no data).

### Elderly use
- Specific dose adjustments or cautions for elderly patients: Not specified in available sources.

### Renal impairment
- Dose adjustments by eGFR or renal stage: Not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Dose adjustments by severity of hepatic impairment: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Cysteamine** reduces corneal cystine crystal accumulation by acting as a cystine‑depleting agent: it converts cystine to cysteine and to cysteine‑cysteamine mixed disulfides, which decreases intralysosomal cystine accumulation.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Reduction of corneal cystine crystals and associated symptomatic improvement (e.g. photophobia) observed in clinical studies using in‑vivo confocal microscopy scoring.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Human pharmacokinetic studies following ocular administration: not performed.
- Systemic absorption: likely occurs similarly to other topically administered ocular products, but systemic exposure is anticipated to be low.
- The recommended daily ocular dose is no more than approximately 0.4% of the highest recommended daily oral dose of cysteamine in any age group; therefore systemic exposure after ocular dosing is considered negligible relative to oral dosing.

### Preclinical safety data
- Genotoxicity: cysteamine showed no evidence of mutagenic potential in bacterial gene mutation assays. In vitro assays showed clastogenic effects in some cell systems (rat liver cells and human lymphocytes), and sister chromatid exchanges in Chinese hamster cells but not in human lymphocytes. A mouse micronucleus test was negative.
- Carcinogenicity: no long‑term animal carcinogenicity studies available for cysteamine in the sources provided.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Two clinical studies are described in the available source documents.

### OCT‑1 study
- Design: single‑arm clinical trial including children and adults; 5‑year follow‑up.
- Participants: 8 patients (children and adults).
- Dosing: median frequency ~4 instillations/day with dose adjustments per ocular exam.
- Outcomes:
  - After 30 days and at a median frequency of 4 instillations/day, an average 30% decrease in in‑vivo confocal microscopy (IVCM) total score (quantifying corneal cystine crystals) was observed.
  - A mean decrease ~30% compared with baseline was maintained over time with a median dosing regimen of 3 drops/eye/day (range 1–3) in 7 of 8 patients.
  - Photophobia tended to improve over time.
  - No patients discontinued treatment during the 5‑year follow‑up.

### CHOC study
- Design: randomised, multi‑centre, open‑label, active‑controlled phase III trial.
- Participants: 32 patients enrolled; 15 patients received cysteamine ophthalmic solution (per available data).
- Dose regimen: 4 drops/eye/day for 90 days.
- Primary efficacy endpoint: IVCM total score.
- Outcomes:
  - A trend to a lower IVCM total score in the cysteamine arm at day 30; mean decrease of ~40% in the cysteamine arm confirmed at day 90.
  - Superiority of cysteamine ocular solution compared with the control arm (cysteamine hydrochloride 0.10%) was demonstrated for IVCM total score (p<0.0001; 95% CI 2.11–5.58).
  - Superiority was also demonstrated for investigator‑rated photophobia compared with the control arm (p=0.0048; 95% CI 0.23–1.14).

### Paediatric exposure in trials
- Across the clinical studies and additional programme data, 15 paediatric patients were exposed to cysteamine ocular solution; 3 subjects were <6 years old (including one 2‑year‑old and one 3‑year‑old). Safety and efficacy outcomes in the paediatric population were reported as similar to adults.

## 13. Overdosage
- Overdose with ocular administration is unlikely.
- In case of accidental ingestion, implement monitoring and symptomatic management.
