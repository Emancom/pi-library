# Editorial Product Information: Cannabidiol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of cannabidiol (Epidyolex) for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cannabidiol (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Antiepileptic.
- **Brand Name(s):** **EPIDYOLEX** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N03AX24.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral solution containing 100 mg/mL of cannabidiol [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Jazz Pharmaceuticals ANZ Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Lennox-Gastaut Syndrome (LGS):** Adjunctive therapy for seizures associated with LGS in patients aged 2 years and older [Australian].
- **Dravet Syndrome (DS):** Adjunctive therapy for seizures associated with DS in patients aged 2 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric $\ge$ 2 years)
- **Starting Dose:** 2.5 mg/kg taken twice daily (5 mg/kg/day) for one week.
- **Maintenance Dose:** After one week, increase to 5 mg/kg twice daily (10 mg/kg/day).
- **Maximum Recommended Dose:** 10 mg/kg twice daily (20 mg/kg/day), based on clinical response and tolerability.

### Administration and Titration
- **Titration:** Dose increases beyond 10 mg/kg/day should occur in weekly increments of 2.5 mg/kg twice daily (5 mg/kg/day).
- **Food Effect:** Food can significantly increase plasma levels. **EPIDYOLEX** must be taken consistently either with or without food (including ketogenic diets) to minimise variability.
- **Administration:** Use the provided calibrated oral syringes (1 mL or 5 mL). Round the calculated dose to the nearest graduated increment.

### Discontinuation and Missed Doses
- **Discontinuation:** Withdraw gradually to prevent increased seizure frequency. In clinical trials, the dose was reduced by approximately 10% per day for 10 days.
- **Missed Doses:** Do not compensate for missed doses; resume the regular schedule. If doses are missed for more than 7 days, re-titration is required.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to cannabidiol or any excipients (e.g., sesame oil, ethanol).
- Patients with baseline transaminase elevations > 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) AND bilirubin > 2 times the ULN [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatocellular Injury:** Dose-related elevations of ALT and AST typically occur within the first two months of treatment. Risk is significantly higher with concomitant **valproate** use and to a lesser extent **clobazam**.
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Baseline: Obtain ALT, AST, and total bilirubin.
    - Routine: Monitor at 1, 3, and 6 months post-initiation, then periodically.
    - Intensified: For patients on valproate or with baseline LFT elevations, monitor at 2 weeks, 1 month, 2 months, 3 months, and 6 months.
- **Somnolence and Sedation:** Common early in treatment; potentiated by CNS depressants (alcohol, clobazam).
- **Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation:** As with all antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), monitor for new or worsening depression or suicidal thoughts.
- **Sesame Oil:** The formulation contains refined sesame oil, which may rarely cause severe allergic reactions.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are presented by frequency based on placebo-controlled clinical trials:

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Somnolence, decreased appetite, diarrhoea, vomiting, pyrexia, fatigue. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Pneumonia, bronchitis, nasopharyngitis, UTI, irritability, insomnia, aggression, agitation, lethargy, tremor, drooling, cough, rash, increased AST/ALT/GGT, weight decreased. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Sustained transaminase elevations > 5x ULN. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Hepatocellular injury (transaminase elevations), pneumonia, and haematologic abnormalities (decreased haemoglobin/haematocrit).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4/CYP2C19 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, St. John’s wort) can decrease cannabidiol and active metabolite (7-OH-CBD) levels by 30-60%.
- **Clobazam:** Cannabidiol inhibits CYP2C19, leading to a 3- to 4-fold increase in the active metabolite **N-desmethylclobazam**. This increases the risk of somnolence; clobazam dose reduction may be necessary.
- **Everolimus (and other mTOR/Calcineurin inhibitors):** Cannabidiol increases everolimus exposure by ~2.5-fold via P-gp inhibition. Monitor blood levels and reduce dose as needed.
- **UGT Substrates:** Cannabidiol may inhibit UGT1A9 and UGT2B7 (e.g., lamotrigine, morphine, lorazepam).

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Valproate:** Concomitant use significantly increases the incidence of transaminase elevations (up to 23% in patients taking both clobazam and valproate).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian] **Category B2**. Limited data in pregnant women. Animal studies show embryofetal mortality and developmental toxicity at high doses. Should not be used unless the benefit clearly outweighs the risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown. Due to high protein binding and animal toxicity data, breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment.
- **Fertility:** No human data available; animal studies in rats showed no adverse effects on fertility at doses up to 250 mg/kg/day.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for LGS and DS in patients $\ge$ 2 years. Safety/efficacy not established for patients < 6 months [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data in patients > 55 years. Start at the low end of the dosing range due to higher frequency of decreased organ function.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild to severe impairment. No experience in end-stage renal disease.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No adjustment.
    - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Lower starting, maintenance, and max dose (Max: 10 mg/kg/day).
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Use only if benefit outweighs risk; significant dose reduction required (Max: 4 mg/kg/day) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Not fully elucidated; does not act through cannabinoid receptors. It reduces neuronal hyper-excitability by modulating intracellular calcium via **GPR55** and **TRPV-1** channels, and inhibiting adenosine uptake via **ENT-1**.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ 2.5–5 hours at steady state. 
    - **Distribution:** > 94% protein bound (mainly to albumin). Large volume of distribution (20,000–42,000 L).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism via CYP2C19, CYP3A4, and UGT (1A7, 1A9, 2B7). Active metabolite: 7-OH-CBD.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life 56–61 hours. Primarily excreted in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **LGS Trials (GWPCARE3 & 4):** Adjunctive therapy in patients (mean age 15) showed a median reduction in drop seizures of 37.2%–43.9% (at 10–20 mg/kg/day) compared to 17.2%–21.8% for placebo.
- **DS Trials (GWPCARE1 & 2):** Adjunctive therapy in patients (mean age 9) showed a median reduction in convulsive seizures of 38.9%–48.7% compared to 13.3%–26.9% for placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Use within **12 weeks** after first opening the bottle [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Amber glass bottle with child-resistant cap.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** [Australian] **Schedule 4** (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring (Black Triangle Scheme) in Australia to identify new safety information.
