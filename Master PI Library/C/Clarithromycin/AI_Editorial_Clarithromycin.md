# Editorial Product Information: Clarithromycin

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the clinical and regulatory profiles of **clarithromycin**, designed for use by healthcare professionals as a consolidated reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Clarithromycin
- **Chemical Class:** Semi-synthetic macrolide antibiotic
- **Brand Names:** Clarithromycin Sandoz [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** J01FA09
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 250 mg and 500 mg [Australian]
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
**Clarithromycin** is indicated for the treatment of mild to moderately severe infections caused by susceptible organisms.

**Adults and Children >12 years:**
- **Respiratory Tract:** Acute streptococcal pharyngitis; Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) due to *C. pneumoniae*, *M. pneumoniae*, *L. pneumophila*, and *S. pneumoniae*; Acute bacterial exacerbation of chronic bronchitis (AECB).
- **Skin and Soft Tissue:** Uncomplicated infections due to *S. aureus* or *S. pyogenes*.
- **Mycobacterial Infections:** Treatment of disseminated or localised infections (*M. avium*, *M. intracellulare*) and skin infections (*M. chelonae*).
- **Prophylaxis:** Prevention of disseminated *M. avium* complex (MAC) in HIV-infected adults with CD4 counts <75 cells/mm³.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Combination therapy for *H. pylori* eradication in peptic ulcer disease.

**Children (<12 years):**
- Acute streptococcal pharyngitis and tonsillitis.
- Community-acquired pneumonia.
- Skin and skin structure infections (e.g., impetigo).
- Disseminated or localised mycobacterial infections (MAC).
- Acute otitis media (AOM).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children >12y)
- **Standard Infections:** 250 mg twice daily.
- **Severe Infections:** 500 mg twice daily (e.g., *Legionella* infections).
- **Duration:** Typically 7 to 14 days.

### Indication-Specific Dosing
- **Mycobacterial Infections (Treatment):** 500 mg twice daily in combination with other antimycobacterial agents.
- **MAC Prophylaxis:** 500 mg twice daily.
- **H. pylori Eradication:** 500 mg twice daily in combination with other antibiotics and a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) for 7–14 days.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be taken with or without food.
- Food intake may increase the rate and extent of absorption, particularly for the 500 mg strength [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specifically detailed in source; standard clinical practice suggests taking as soon as remembered unless near the next dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to macrolides or any excipients.
- **Cardiac/Electrolytes:** 
    - History of QT prolongation (congenital or acquired) or ventricular cardiac arrhythmia (including *torsades de pointes*).
    - Uncorrected electrolyte disturbances (hypokalaemia or hypomagnesaemia).
- **Severe Organ Impairment:** Patients with severe hepatic failure combined with renal impairment.
- **Drug-Drug Interactions (Strictly Contraindicated):**
    - **QT Prolonging Agents:** Astemizole, terfenadine, cisapride, domperidone, pimozide.
    - **Ergot Alkaloids:** Ergotamine, dihydroergotamine (risk of ergot toxicity).
    - **Strong CYP3A4 Substrates:** Ivabradine, oral midazolam, lomitapide, ticagrelor, ranolazine.
    - **Statins:** Lovastatin or simvastatin (increased risk of rhabdomyolysis).
    - **Other:** Colchicine.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Events:** Macrolides are associated with prolonged cardiac repolarization and QT interval. Use with caution in patients with coronary artery disease, heart failure, bradycardia, or those taking other QT-prolonging medicines.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Hepatic dysfunction (increased enzymes, hepatitis, cholestasis) has been reported. Discontinue immediately if signs of hepatitis (anorexia, jaundice, dark urine) occur.
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhoea (CDAD):** May range from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis. Consider this diagnosis in all patients presenting with diarrhoea post-antibiotic use.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** Including Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, and DRESS. Discontinue therapy immediately at the first sign of skin rash.
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Exacerbation of symptoms has been reported.
- **Resistance:** Macrolide resistance in *S. pneumoniae* and *S. pyogenes* is emerging; sensitivity testing is recommended for CAP and SSTIs.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Nervous System** | Headache, dizziness | Insomnia, anxiety, tremor |
| **Skin** | Rash, pruritus | Urticaria |
| **Special Senses** | Dysgeusia | Tinnitus, hearing disturbances |
| **Infections** | Vaginal infection | Candidiasis |

### Serious Adverse Effects (Post-Marketing)
- **Cardiac:** *Torsades de pointes*, ventricular tachycardia, fibrillation.
- **Hepatic:** Hepatic failure (rarely fatal).
- **Musculoskeletal:** Rhabdomyolysis (often in the context of drug interactions).
- **Renal:** Interstitial nephritis, renal failure.
- **Haematological:** Thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
**Clarithromycin** is a potent inhibitor of **CYP3A4** and the efflux transporter **P-glycoprotein (P-gp)**.

- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** May increase concentrations of alprazolam, carbamazepine, ciclosporin, digoxin, sildenafil, theophylline, valproate, and warfarin.
- **Statins:** Concurrent use with simvastatin/lovastatin is contraindicated; caution with other statins (use lowest dose).
- **Oral Anticoagulants:** Increased risk of serious haemorrhage. Frequent INR monitoring is required for warfarin; caution with DOACs (dabigatran, rivaroxaban, apixaban).
- **Hypoglycaemic Agents:** Risk of significant hypoglycaemia when used with insulin or certain oral agents (e.g., repaglinide, pioglitazone).
- **CYP3A Inducers:** Rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, and St John’s Wort may decrease clarithromycin levels to sub-therapeutic concentrations.
- **Bi-directional Interactions:** Significant increases in levels of both drugs can occur with atazanavir, itraconazole, and saquinavir.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Clarithromycin should not be used in pregnant women except in clinical circumstances where no alternative therapy is appropriate. Animal studies have shown embryo-foetal toxicity at doses producing plasma levels 2–17 times higher than human therapeutic levels.
- **Breastfeeding:** Clarithromycin is excreted in human breast milk (estimated at 1.7% of the maternal weight-adjusted dose). Safety has not been established; use with caution.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no evidence of impaired fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Tablets have not been studied in children <12 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is required unless significant renal impairment is present.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **CrCl <30 mL/min:** Reduce dose by half (e.g., 250 mg once daily, or 250 mg twice daily for severe infections).
    - Maximum treatment duration is 14 days in this population [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Exercise caution. No dose adjustment is required if renal function is normal; however, it is contraindicated in severe hepatic failure if combined with renal impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits protein synthesis by binding to the 50S ribosomal subunit of susceptible bacteria.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits a post-antibiotic effect. The primary metabolite, **14-OH-clarithromycin**, is also microbiologically active and may be more potent against certain organisms like *H. influenzae*.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~50%. 
    - **Metabolism:** Undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism to the 14-OH metabolite.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 20% (250 mg dose) to 30% (500 mg dose) is excreted unchanged in the urine. The half-life is dose-dependent (3–4 hours at 250 mg BID; 5–7 hours at 500 mg BID).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **H. pylori:** Triple therapy (clarithromycin, amoxicillin, omeprazole) achieved an 88% eradication rate compared to 55% for dual therapy [Australian].
- **MAC Prophylaxis:** In HIV patients with CD4 ≤100 cells/mm³, clarithromycin reduced the risk of developing MAC or death by 37% compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Handling:** Keep in the original blister pack to protect from light/moisture.
