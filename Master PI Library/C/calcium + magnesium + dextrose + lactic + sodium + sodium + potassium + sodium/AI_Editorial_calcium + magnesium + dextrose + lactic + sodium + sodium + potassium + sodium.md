# Editorial Product Information: calcium + magnesium + dextrose + lactic + sodium + sodium + potassium + sodium

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **calcium + magnesium + dextrose + lactic + sodium + sodium + potassium + sodium**
- Chemical class: Electrolyte and carbohydrate-containing parenteral replacement/buffer solution for renal replacement therapy (combination of mineral electrolytes, buffer precursor lactate and/or bicarbonate, and dextrose)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous, as an extracorporeal replacement solution administered into the extracorporeal circuit during Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction:
  - PRISMASOL formulation family: Initial U.S. approval 2006 [US]
  - PHOXILLUM formulation family: Initial U.S. approval 2015 [US]
- Date of last revision/update of source PI: Revised 5/2025 [US]
- Withdrawal/suspension/restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Other jurisdictions' registration dates: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated in pediatric and adult patients for use as a replacement solution in Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT) to replace plasma water and electrolytes removed by ultrafiltration and to correct electrolyte and acid–base imbalances. [US]
- May be used when CRRT is used to remove dialyzable substances in cases of drug poisoning. [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult and pediatric)
- Dosing is individualized. The selection of solution variant, mode of CRRT, flow rates, and duration of replacement therapy must be established by the treating physician based on the patient’s clinical condition, electrolyte concentrations (including phosphate), acid–base and glucose balance.
- Multiple preformulated replacement solutions with differing electrolyte and buffer contents are supplied; each mixture (after mixing) yields a 5 L solution (250 mL compartment A + 4750 mL compartment B). Specific post‑mix concentrations for available formulations are provided below.

### Dosing by indication
- Replacement therapy in CRRT: Administered as a replacement solution into the extracorporeal circuit. Can be given pre‑filter (pre‑dilution), post‑filter (post‑dilution), or both, per clinician choice and CRRT modality.
- Drug poisoning when CRRT is used: Use as replacement fluid per CRRT prescription to support fluid and electrolyte balance while extracorporeal removal of dialyzable toxins is performed.

### Administration instructions
- Use only with extracorporeal dialysis equipment appropriate for CRRT.
- Inspect solution visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Bags are supplied as two compartments separated by a peel seal (small compartment A ~250 mL; large compartment B ~4750 mL). Mix immediately prior to use by opening the peel seal to combine compartments and mixing thoroughly.
- After mixing, the solution should be used promptly. Mix additives before connecting the bag to the extracorporeal circuit.
- The replacement line may be connected via the designated connectors; ensure secure, leak‑free connections and free flow of fluid.
- The solution may be warmed to 37°C (98.6°F) to enhance patient comfort — use dry heat only. Do not heat in water baths or microwave ovens. After warming, verify clarity and absence of particulates.
- Aseptic technique must be used for handling and when adding medications/additives.

### Addition of drugs/additives
- After mixing, drugs may be added via the injection connector to the large compartment (B). Use aseptic technique and mix thoroughly. Do not use if color change or precipitate forms after addition.
- Phosphate:
  - For formulations in the PRISMASOL family: Up to 1.2 mmol/L phosphate may be added to the bag as potassium phosphate or sodium phosphate. Total potassium concentration in the final PRISMASOL solution must not exceed 4 mEq/L. If total potassium is already 4 mEq/L, use sodium phosphate to add phosphate.
  - For PHOXILLUM formulations: Phosphate up to 0.2 mmol/L may be added; total phosphate concentration should not exceed 1.2 mmol/L. Use sodium phosphate if adding phosphate to the bag.
- In general, administer drugs other than phosphate through a different access line when possible.

### Titration and missed doses
- Titration: Adjust formulation selection, flow rates, and supplementation based on ongoing monitoring of electrolytes, acid–base status, glucose and fluid balance. No fixed titration schedules specified in available sources.
- Missed dose: Not applicable in the context of continuous replacement therapy; if therapy is interrupted, resume per clinical judgement and monitor electrolytes and acid–base status. Specific instructions for missed doses are not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed warnings**: Not specified in available sources.
- General warnings and precautions:
  - Individualize therapy based upon the patient’s clinical condition, fluid status, electrolytes, acid–base and glucose balance.
  - These replacement solutions can alter electrolytes and fluid status and may result in hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypophosphatemia, changes in calcium (including increased ionized calcium for calcium‑containing solutions), and other electrolyte disturbances.
  - PHOXILLUM formulations contain hydrogen phosphate, a weak acid; this may increase the risk of metabolic acidosis in some patients.
  - Solutions containing dextrose can affect blood glucose and may result in hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia depending on dextrose content; monitor glucose and adjust antidiabetic therapy as required.
  - Use only with CRRT‑appropriate extracorporeal equipment.
  - Mix compartments immediately prior to use; do not use if the overwrap or seals are damaged or if solution is discolored or contains particulates.
  - Aseptic technique should be used throughout handling and administration.
- Monitoring requirements:
  - Monitor hemodynamic status and fluid inputs/outputs continuously as clinically indicated.
  - Monitor serum electrolytes (including potassium, phosphate, calcium, magnesium, sodium, chloride), acid–base status (arterial/venous blood gas as indicated) and bicarbonate or lactate levels as appropriate.
  - Monitor blood glucose regularly during and after therapy when dextrose‑containing solutions are used.
  - Monitor for signs of metabolic acidosis, hypotension, and fluid overload or depletion.
- Specific clinical precautions:
  - When regional anticoagulation with citrate is used, citrate contributes to buffer load and can reduce plasma ionized calcium levels; select formulation and monitor calcium accordingly.
  - Adjust formulation or supplemental electrolytes if abnormalities occur; consider changing replacement solution or dialysate, supplementing electrolytes, or adjusting flow rates to correct abnormalities.
  - Do not use if precipitation or insoluble complexes are observed after addition of drugs.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - CRRT can remove dialyzable drugs, reducing their blood concentrations. Monitor plasma concentrations and clinical effect of drugs that are dialyzable during CRRT and adjust dosing as needed.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Use of citrate anticoagulation can interact with calcium balance — citrate chelates calcium leading to reduced plasma ionized calcium and contributes to overall buffer load; select replacement formulation and monitor calcium and acid–base status accordingly. [US]
- Clinically significant interactions and management:
  - Monitor levels of drugs known to be removed by CRRT (e.g., certain antimicrobials, anticonvulsants, immunosuppressants); dose adjustments or therapeutic drug monitoring may be required.
  - When adding phosphate salts to the replacement solution, account for added potassium load if using potassium phosphate; avoid total potassium >4 mEq/L in PRISMASOL formulations. Use sodium phosphate if potassium already at upper permitted concentration.
- Other interactions: Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - Risk summary: These replacement solutions are pharmacologically inactive electrolyte/buffer/dextrose solutions. There are no adequate and well‑controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal reproductive studies have not been conducted with these specific solutions. Appropriate administration with monitoring of fluid, electrolyte, acid–base and glucose balance is not expected to cause fetal harm. [US]
  - Clinical considerations: Maintenance of normal maternal acid–base status and electrolyte balance is important for fetal well‑being; monitor and correct abnormalities accordingly.
  - Pregnancy category/class: Not specified in available sources.
- Breastfeeding:
  - Risk summary: Components of these solutions are excreted in human milk. Appropriate administration with monitoring of maternal fluid, electrolyte, acid–base and glucose balance is not expected to harm a nursing infant. [US]
- Fertility: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- These solutions are pharmacologically inactive replacement fluids composed of electrolytes, bicarbonate (or bicarbonate precursor lactate) and dextrose. Electrolyte concentrations are chosen to replace or maintain plasma electrolyte levels and to replenish water removed during hemofiltration/hemodiafiltration. Bicarbonate or lactate acts as an alkalinizing buffer (lactate being metabolized to bicarbonate) to help restore acid–base balance.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption/distribution/elimination:
  - Distribution and elimination of electrolytes, bicarbonate and dextrose from these solutions depend on the patient's clinical condition, metabolic status, residual renal function and ongoing physiologic losses. Elimination is driven by CRRT settings, native renal function (if any), and tissue handling/metabolism (e.g., lactate metabolism to bicarbonate).
- Metabolism:
  - Lactate in the solution serves as a bicarbonate precursor via metabolic conversion to bicarbonate in the liver and other tissues.
- Pharmacodynamics:
  - Administration alters plasma electrolyte concentrations, acid–base status and glucose depending on formulation and infusion/regeneration rates.
