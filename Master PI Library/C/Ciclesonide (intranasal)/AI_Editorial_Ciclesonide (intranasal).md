# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial information is derived from the official Product Information (PI) and labeling for **Levocarnitine Injection** provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport. 
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is **50 mg/kg** administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US]. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. 
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours. 
    - Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to **300 mg/kg** have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is **10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg** (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. 
    - Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on plasma levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable and compatible for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with **0.9% Sodium Chloride** or **Lactated Ringer’s** at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
- **Monitoring:** Pre-therapy plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known based on current manufacturer documentation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit assessment and close monitoring [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral and IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine Caution):** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted renally [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:

| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Chest pain (12%), Accidental injury (10%), Asthenia (9%), Abdominal pain (9%), Fever (7%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Palpitations (5%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Dyspepsia (5%), Anorexia (5%) |

### Serious and Postmarketing Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis; body odor (less frequent) [US].
- **Immunologic:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when co-administered with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned in current labeling format]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA-based) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, no adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. Levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase in dairy cows following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant. Discontinuation of nursing or treatment may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were observed in standard tests (*Salmonella typhimurium*, *S. cerevisiae*, *S. pombe*). Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (see Section 3). In premature infants and newborns, secondary deficiency is defined as plasma concentrations below age-related normal levels [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Information not specified in available source documents.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 for ESRD-specific dosing. Note precaution regarding accumulation of metabolites with oral formulations in patients with severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Information not specified in available source documents.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].
    - **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies in ESRD:** Trials in ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg after dialysis showed that trough concentrations reached steady state (54 to 180 µmol/L) within one week. Trough levels remained stable or increased over 24 weeks of therapy.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** In a study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg, mean trough concentrations at 24 weeks were 148 ± 50, 240 ± 60, and 456 ± 162 µmol/L, respectively [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations in ESRD is established, the specific effect of supplementation on clinical outcomes (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
