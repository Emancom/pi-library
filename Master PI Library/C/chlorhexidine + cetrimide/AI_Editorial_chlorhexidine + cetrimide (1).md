# Editorial Product Information: chlorhexidine + cetrimide

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **chlorhexidine + cetrimide**
- Chemical class:
  - Chlorhexidine: biguanide antiseptic/disinfectant
  - Cetrimide: quaternary ammonium compound (cationic surfactant; disinfectant/detergent)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: topical irrigation / external wound irrigation only
- Excipients specified in available sources: gluconic acid, water for injections, sodium hydroxide, sunset yellow FCF

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval:
  - [Australian] First approval recorded as 4 September 1996
- Date(s) of last revision/update of source PI:
  - [Australian] Revision dated 12 September 2023
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- For jurisdictions other than Australian: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indication listed in available sources:
  - Cleansing and irrigation of skin and dirty or infected wounds where an added surfactant (detergent) effect is required.
- Indication limitations:
  - Intended for external wound cleansing/irrigation rather than general wound management because cetrimide may prolong wound healing.
- Jurisdictional differences in indications: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Concentrations provided in source documents (for topical irrigation):
  - Chlorhexidine gluconate 0.015% (0.015% w/v) with cetrimide 0.15% (0.15% w/v)
  - Chlorhexidine gluconate 0.05% (0.05% w/v) with cetrimide 0.5% (0.5% w/v)
- Specific numeric volume dosing per wound: Not specified in available sources (other than examples of single-unit ampoules in regional packaging, which are not included here)

### Dosing by indication
- No indication-specific dose regimens (e.g., surgical irrigation volume, frequency) are specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Rinse the area to be cleaned with water first.
- Apply the minimum amount of irrigation solution necessary to cover the wound area; wash gently.
- Rinse the area thoroughly after application.
- Apply to wound as necessary.
- Discard any remaining solution after use (single-use only).
- Not for injection; for external irrigation only.
- Do not use for irrigation of the brain, meninges, eyes, or perforated eardrum.

### Titration schedules: Not specified in available sources
### Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to chlorhexidine or cetrimide (both listed).
- Do not use to irrigate the brain or meninges.
- Do not use in the eyes.
- Do not use to irrigate a perforated eardrum.
- For injection: explicitly contraindicated (not for injection).
- Not isotonic and is haemolytic — therefore unsuitable for situations requiring isotonic irrigation or internal use.
- Any additional contraindications by other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed warnings specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- For external use only; not for injection.
- Not isotonic and haemolytic — avoid use in indications that require isotonic solutions or where haemolysis risk is relevant.
- Cetrimide may delay wound healing; restrict use to infection control and wound cleansing rather than routine wound management.
- Use with caution in neonates, particularly in premature infants, as chlorhexidine may cause irritation or chemical burns.
- Discontinue use if irritative skin reactions or hypersensitivity occur.

### Monitoring requirements
- Specific monitoring protocols (laboratory or clinical monitoring schedules) are not specified in available sources.
- Observe wounds for impaired healing or signs of local irritation/hypersensitivity.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid application to mucous membranes of brain, meninges, eyes, or middle ear if tympanic membrane is perforated.
- Consider risk of delayed wound healing when choosing antiseptic for routine wound care.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Chlorhexidine: antiseptic/disinfectant active against a broad range of vegetative Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, some viruses and some fungi; more active against Gram-positive organisms. Ineffective against bacterial spores at room temperature; acid-fast bacteria are inhibited but not killed. Activity is greatest at neutral to slightly acidic pH and may be reduced by blood and other organic matter.
- Cetrimide: quaternary ammonium cationic surfactant with detergent/emulsifying properties and bactericidal action against Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms, though higher concentrations are required to kill Gram-negative bacteria. It provides combined detergent and antibacterial properties useful for cleansing dirty or infected wounds but is relatively ineffective against bacterial spores, acid-fast bacteria, viruses and fungi.
