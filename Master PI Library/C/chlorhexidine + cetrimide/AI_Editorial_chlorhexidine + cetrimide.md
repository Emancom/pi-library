# Editorial Product Information: chlorhexidine + cetrimide

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **chlorhexidine + cetrimide**
- Chemical class:
  - Chlorhexidine: biguanide antiseptic/disinfectant
  - Cetrimide: quaternary ammonium cationic surfactant (detergent)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Topical irrigation/cleansing of skin and wounds (external use only).

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval: 4 September 1996 [Australian].
- Date of most recent PI revision/update: 12 September 2023 [Australian].
- Withdrawal, suspension, restriction history: Not specified in available sources.
- If registration dates in other jurisdictions are required: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Cleansing and irrigating the skin and dirty or infected wounds where an added surfactant (detergent) effect is required.
- Use should be restricted to infection control and wound cleansing rather than routine/general wound management because of potential effects on wound healing.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to either **chlorhexidine** or **cetrimide**.
- Do not use to irrigate the brain or meninges.
- Do not use in the eyes.
- Do not use to irrigate a perforated eardrum.
- Use for injection is contraindicated (product is for external irrigation only).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- For external use only. Not for injection.
- The solution is not isotonic and is haemolytic; avoid mucosal/inner-ear/sterile body-cavity exposure when contraindicated.
- Cetrimide may prolong the wound healing process; restrict use to infection control and cleansing rather than routine wound care/long-term wound management.
- Chlorhexidine can cause irritation and has been associated with chemical burns, particularly in neonates and premature infants — use with caution in these populations.
- Chlorhexidine has been associated with hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis. Discontinue use if signs of hypersensitivity occur.
- Activity of chlorhexidine may be reduced by blood or other organic matter and is most active at neutral to slightly acidic pH.

### Monitoring requirements
- No specific laboratory or clinical monitoring requirements are specified in available sources.
- Monitor for signs of local irritation, chemical burn, or hypersensitivity/allergic reaction during and after use; discontinue use if such reactions occur.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use with care in neonates, particularly premature infants (risk of irritation/chemical burns).
- Restrict use to intended external wound/skin irrigation indications; avoid use in body sites specified under contraindications.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action / Pharmacodynamics
- Chlorhexidine:
  - Acts as an antiseptic and disinfectant with activity against a wide range of vegetative Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, some viruses, and some fungi.
  - Ineffective against bacterial spores at room temperature; acid-fast bacteria are inhibited but not killed.
  - More active against Gram-positive organisms than Gram-negative; some Pseudomonas and Proteus spp. are relatively less susceptible.
  - Most active at neutral or slightly acidic pH; activity can be reduced by the presence of blood or other organic material.
- Cetrimide:
  - A quaternary ammonium cationic surfactant with detergent and emulsifying properties.
  - Exhibits bactericidal activity against Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms, though higher concentrations are required to kill many Gram-negative bacteria.
  - Useful as a detergent/antiseptic adjunct for cleansing dirty or infected wounds.
  - Relatively ineffective against bacterial spores, acid-fast bacteria, many viruses and fungi.
