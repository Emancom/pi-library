# Editorial Product Information: Cinacalcet

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to their local regulatory authority's current approved documentation.
- **Note on Sources:** As only the Australian PI was provided for this synthesis, jurisdictional comparisons are limited. All clinical recommendations are attributed to the [Australian] source.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cinacalcet hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Calcimimetic agent; a phenylpropylamine derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** AKM Cinacalcet [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H05BX01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 30 mg, 60 mg, and 90 mg (as hydrochloride) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Hyperparathyroidism (HPT):** Treatment of biochemical manifestations in patients with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) receiving dialysis. It should be used as adjunctive therapy [Australian].
- **Parathyroid Carcinoma:** Treatment of hypercalcaemia in affected patients [Australian].
- **Primary Hyperparathyroidism:** Treatment of biochemical manifestations in patients where parathyroidectomy is clinically inappropriate or not a treatment option [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Tablets should be swallowed whole and not divided [Australian].
- **Food Effect:** Cinacalcet should be taken with food or shortly after a meal to maximize bioavailability [Australian].

### Adult Dosing by Indication [Australian]
- **Secondary HPT (Dialysis Patients):**
    - Starting Dose: 30 mg once daily.
    - Titration: Every 2 to 4 weeks to a maximum of 180 mg once daily.
    - Target: Achieve parathyroid hormone (PTH) levels between 1.5 to 5 times the upper limit of normal.
    - Assessment: PTH levels should be measured at least 12 hours after a dose.
- **Parathyroid Carcinoma and Primary HPT:**
    - Starting Dose: 30 mg twice daily.
    - Titration: Every 2 to 4 weeks using sequential doses (30 mg BID, 60 mg BID, 90 mg BID, then 90 mg three or four times daily) as required to normalise serum calcium levels.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to cinacalcet or any excipients (e.g., lactose monohydrate) [Australian].
- **Hypocalcaemia:** Treatment must not be initiated if serum calcium is below the lower limit of the normal range [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serum Calcium:** Significant reductions in serum calcium can occur. Life-threatening events and fatal outcomes related to hypocalcaemia have been reported [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation and Arrhythmias:** Decreased serum calcium can prolong the QT interval, potentially leading to ventricular arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Seizures:** The threshold for seizures is lowered by significant reductions in serum calcium. Seizures (primarily generalised or tonic-clonic) have been observed in clinical trials [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Bleeding:** Cases of upper GI bleeding have been reported. Patients with risk factors (gastritis, oesophagitis, ulcers, severe vomiting) should be monitored closely [Australian].
- **Hypotension/Heart Failure:** Idiosyncratic cases of hypotension and worsening heart failure have been reported in patients with impaired cardiac function [Australian].
- **Adynamic Bone Disease:** May develop if PTH levels are suppressed below 100 pg/mL. If this occurs, reduce the dose of cinacalcet or vitamin D sterols [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** 
    - Measure serum calcium within 1 week of initiation or dose adjustment.
    - Once maintenance is reached, monitor calcium monthly for secondary HPT, or every 2–3 months for carcinoma/primary HPT.
    - For secondary HPT, monitor PTH 1 to 4 weeks after dose adjustments and every 1 to 3 months thereafter [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency and Severity [Australian]
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea (31%), vomiting (27%), diarrhoea (21%), myalgia (15%), headache (16%), dizziness (10%), hypocalcaemia.
- **Common (1–10%):** Dyspnoea, cough, hypertension, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, anorexia, asthenia, fatigue, fever, peripheral oedema, back pain, rash, and hypersensitivity reactions.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Seizures.
- **Rare/Very Rare:** GI bleeding, angioedema, and urticaria (post-marketing).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Severe hypocalcaemia (associated with tetany, seizures, and arrhythmias).
- Upper gastrointestinal bleeding.
- Worsening heart failure/hypotension.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions [Australian]
- **Effect of Cinacalcet on Other Drugs:**
    - **CYP2D6 Inhibition:** Cinacalcet is a potent inhibitor of CYP2D6. Dose adjustments may be necessary for co-administered drugs with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., flecainide, vinblastine, thioridazine, tricyclic antidepressants) or those primarily metabolised by CYP2D6 (e.g., metoprolol).
    - **Desipramine:** Exposure increased ~3.6-fold.
    - **Amitriptyline:** Exposure increased by ~20%.
- **Effect of Other Drugs on Cinacalcet:**
    - **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, erythromycin, itraconazole) may increase cinacalcet exposure (~2-fold with ketoconazole). Dose adjustment may be required when starting or stopping these agents.
    - **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Agents like rifampicin or St. John’s Wort may decrease cinacalcet concentrations.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Calcium-lowering medications:** Concurrent use increases the risk of hypocalcaemia; monitor serum calcium closely [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B3** [Australian]. 
    - Animal studies show cinacalcet crosses the placental barrier. There was no evidence of teratogenicity, but decreased foetal body weight occurred at maternally toxic doses. 
    - Use in pregnancy only if potential benefits outweigh risks to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if cinacalcet is excreted in human milk. Due to the high milk-to-plasma ratio in animal studies, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of mating or fertility in rat studies at exposures up to 2x human levels [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for paediatric patients. Safety and efficacy have not been established. A fatal case of severe hypocalcaemia was reported in a paediatric clinical trial [Australian].
- **Geriatric Use:** No clinically relevant differences in pharmacokinetics or safety/efficacy observed in patients >65 years; no dose adjustment required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics are comparable across varying degrees of renal function; no dose adjustment necessary [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Exposure (AUC) is 2 to 4 times higher in moderate to severe impairment. Monitor PTH and serum calcium very closely during titration [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Cinacalcet is a calcimimetic that increases the sensitivity of the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) on the parathyroid gland to extracellular calcium. This leads to a direct reduction in PTH secretion and a subsequent decrease in serum calcium and phosphorus levels [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics [Australian]:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability ~25%. Cmax reached in 2–6 hours. Bioavailability increases by 50–80% when taken with food.
    - **Distribution:** Large volume of distribution (~1000 L); ~97% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by CYP3A4, CYP1A2, and CYP2D6. Metabolites are inactive.
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic decline; terminal half-life of 30–40 hours. 80% recovered in urine, 15% in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Secondary HPT:** Three 6-month Phase 3 studies in dialysis patients showed significant reductions in iPTH, calcium-phosphorus product (Ca x P), calcium, and phosphorus. Approximately 40% of cinacalcet patients achieved iPTH ≤ 26.5 pmol/L compared to <7% in placebo groups [Australian].
- **EVOLVE Trial:** A study of 3,883 dialysis patients failed to meet its primary objective of demonstrating a reduction in the risk of all-cause mortality or major cardiovascular events (HR 0.93; 95% CI: 0.85, 1.02) [Australian].
- **Parathyroid Carcinoma/Primary HPT:** Open-label studies showed significant reductions in serum calcium, with approximately 62% (carcinoma) and 88% (primary HPT) of patients achieving a reduction of at least 0.25 mmol/L [Australian].
