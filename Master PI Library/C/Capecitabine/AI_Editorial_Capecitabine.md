# Editorial Product Information: Capecitabine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from Australia (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of capecitabine for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Capecitabine (a fluoropyrimidine carbamate).
- **Brand Name(s):** Capecitabine-DRLA [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 150 mg (light peach, marked "150" and "RDY") and 500 mg (peach, marked "500" and "RDY") [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr Reddy’s Laboratories (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Colon Cancer:** Adjuvant treatment for patients with Dukes’ stage C and high-risk stage B colon cancer, as monotherapy or in combination with oxaliplatin [Australian].
- **Colorectal Cancer:** Treatment of advanced or metastatic colorectal cancer [Australian].
- **Oesophagogastric Cancer:** First-line treatment of advanced oesophagogastric cancer in combination with a platinum-based regimen [Australian].
- **Breast Cancer:** 
    - Monotherapy for locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer after failure of taxanes and anthracyclines (or if contraindicated) [Australian].
    - In combination with docetaxel for locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer after failure of prior anthracycline-containing chemotherapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Administration:** Tablets must be swallowed with water within 30 minutes after the end of a meal [Australian].
- **Monotherapy (Colon, Colorectal, Breast Cancer):** 1250 mg/m² twice daily (morning and evening) for 14 days, followed by a 7-day rest period, in 3-week cycles [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:**
    - **Breast Cancer:** 1250 mg/m² twice daily for 2 weeks (plus docetaxel 75 mg/m² every 3 weeks) [Australian].
    - **Colorectal/Adjuvant Colon Cancer:** 1000 mg/m² twice daily for 2 weeks (plus oxaliplatin 130 mg/m² every 3 weeks) [Australian].
    - **Oesophagogastric (Triplet):** 625 mg/m² twice daily as a continuous regimen (with epirubicin and platinum) [Australian].
    - **Gastric (Doublet):** 1000 mg/m² twice daily for 2 weeks (with cisplatin 80 mg/m² every 3 weeks) [Australian].

### Dosing Modifications
- Doses are calculated based on Body Surface Area (BSA).
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources. However, omitted doses for toxicity are not replaced [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to capecitabine or any excipients [Australian].
- History of severe or unexpected reactions to fluoropyrimidine therapy or known hypersensitivity to 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) [Australian].
- Known dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) deficiency [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min) [Australian].
- Concomitant treatment with sorivudine or its analogues (e.g., brivudine) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None listed in provided Australian PI.
- **Diarrhoea:** Can be severe. Therapy must be interrupted if Grade 2 (4–6 stools/day) or higher occurs until resolution. Standard antidiarrhoeals (e.g., loperamide) are recommended [Australian].
- **Dehydration:** Must be prevented or corrected at onset. Dehydration may lead to acute renal failure, which can be fatal [Australian].
- **Hand-Foot Syndrome:** Also known as palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia. Grade 2 or 3 events require treatment interruption and subsequent dose reduction [Australian].
- **Cardiotoxicity:** Similar to 5-FU; includes myocardial infarction, angina, dysrhythmias, and cardiac arrest. Higher risk in patients with pre-existing coronary artery disease [Australian].
- **Dihydropyrimidine Dehydrogenase (DPD) Deficiency:** Patients with complete deficiency are at high risk of life-threatening toxicity and must not be treated. Partial deficiency requires a reduced starting dose [Australian].
- **Hyperbilirubinaemia:** Interrupt treatment if bilirubin > 3.0 x ULN or hepatic aminotransferases > 2.5 x ULN [Australian].
- **Skin Reactions:** Severe reactions like Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) have been reported; permanent discontinuation is required if these occur [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequencies (Monotherapy)
| Body System | Very Common (≥ 10%) | Common (1–10%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea (13% G3/4), Vomiting, Nausea, Stomatitis, Abdominal pain | Constipation, Dyspepsia, Upper abdominal pain |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | Hand-foot syndrome (17% G3/4), Dermatitis | Rash, Alopecia, Erythema, Dry skin |
| **General** | Fatigue, Lethargy | Pyrexia, Asthenia, Weakness |
| **Metabolism** | Anorexia | Dehydration, Decreased appetite |
| **Nervous System** | Paraesthesia | Dysgeusia, Headache, Dizziness |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Not specified | Hyperbilirubinaemia (G3/4: 1%) |

### Serious/Other Adverse Effects
- **Haematologic:** Neutropenia, anaemia, and thrombocytopenia [Australian].
- **Post-Marketing:** Rare reports of hepatic failure, cholestatic hepatitis, lacrimal duct stenosis, and toxic leukoencephalopathy [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Sorivudine/Brivudine:** Contraindicated due to inhibition of DPD, which increases 5-FU toxicity. A 4-week washout is required after sorivudine before starting capecitabine [Australian].
- **Coumarin Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** Increased INR and bleeding events. Monitor PT/INR regularly and adjust anticoagulant dose [Australian].
- **Cytochrome P450 2C9:** Capecitabine may inhibit CYP2C9; caution with substrates like phenytoin (increased phenytoin levels reported) [Australian].
- **Leucovorin (Folinic Acid):** Enhances the pharmacodynamics and toxicity of capecitabine [Australian].
- **Antacids:** Small increase in plasma concentrations of capecitabine and one metabolite; no effect on major metabolites [Australian].
- **Food:** Decreases rate and extent of absorption; however, PI recommends administration with food for safety and efficacy [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Capecitabine may cause foetal harm. Animal studies show teratogenicity and embryolethality [Australian].
    - Women of childbearing potential should avoid pregnancy during treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if capecitabine is excreted in human milk. Discontinuation of breastfeeding is recommended during therapy due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data show impairment of fertility in both males and females; effects in females were reversible after a drug-free period [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in persons < 18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥ 65 years):** Higher incidence of Grade 3/4 adverse reactions, particularly when used in combination with docetaxel. Patients ≥ 60 years in combination with docetaxel should start at 75% of the capecitabine dose (950 mg/m² twice daily) [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Moderate (CrCl 30–50 mL/min):** Reduce starting dose to 75% [Australian].
    - **Severe (CrCl < 30 mL/min):** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Monitor closely in patients with mild to moderate impairment due to liver metastases. No initial dose adjustment is specified for these patients, but severe impairment has not been studied [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Capecitabine is a non-cytotoxic prodrug. It is selectively activated to the cytotoxic moiety, 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), by the enzyme thymidine phosphorylase, which is found at higher levels in many tumours compared to normal tissues [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 5-FU metabolites cause cell injury via two mechanisms: inhibition of thymidylate synthase (disrupting DNA synthesis) and incorporation into RNA (disrupting RNA processing) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and extensive; 95.5% recovered in urine [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Low protein binding (mainly albumin) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Three-step enzymatic conversion: Liver (carboxylesterase) → Liver/Tumour (cytidine deaminase) → Tumour (thymidine phosphorylase) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primary metabolite FBAL excreted in urine. Half-life is approximately 0.75–0.85 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **XACT Trial:** Adjuvant treatment in Dukes’ stage C colon cancer showed capecitabine was at least equivalent to 5-FU/leucovorin (Mayo regimen) in disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) [Australian].
- **NO16968 (XELOX):** In stage III colon cancer, capecitabine plus oxaliplatin was superior to bolus 5-FU/leucovorin [Australian].
- **Metastatic Colorectal:** Two Phase III trials showed capecitabine monotherapy equivalent to 5-FU/leucovorin in time to progression and OS, with superior objective response rates [Australian].
- **REAL-2 Trial:** In oesophagogastric cancer, capecitabine-based regimens were non-inferior to 5-FU-based regimens regarding OS [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources (refer to packaging) [Australian].
