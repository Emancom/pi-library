# Editorial Product Information: Ciclosporin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following official Product Information (PI) document:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Australian Product Information – APO-CICLOSPORIN (Ciclosporin) capsules.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ciclosporin for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory data into a clinically relevant reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing software relevant to a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Ciclosporin (INN, BAN, AAN); Ciclosporine (USAN).
- **Chemical Class**: Cyclic polypeptide consisting of 11 amino acids (calcineurin inhibitor).
- **Brand Names**: APO-CICLOSPORIN [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: L04A D01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Soft gelatin capsules containing a microemulsion pre-concentrate: 25 mg, 50 mg, and 100 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Ciclosporin is indicated for:
- **Organ Transplantation**: Prevention of graft rejection in kidney, liver, and heart allogeneic transplantation [Australian].
- **Nephrotic Syndrome**: Induction and maintenance of remission. Note: It is not a first-line agent and should be used only when steroids and cytostatic drugs have failed or are inappropriate, and where renal function is unimpaired [Australian].
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis**: Treatment of severe, active disease in patients where classical slow-acting antirheumatic agents (including methotrexate) are ineffective or inappropriate [Australian].
- **Psoriasis**: Treatment of severe psoriasis where conventional therapy is ineffective or inappropriate, and the disease significantly impacts quality of life [Australian].
- **Atopic Dermatitis**: Treatment of severe cases when other treatments are ineffective or inappropriate [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
- **Administration**: Total daily doses must always be given in two divided doses. Capsules must be swallowed whole [Australian].
- **Formulation Note**: APO-CICLOSPORIN is a microemulsion pre-concentrate. Absorption is less influenced by food or diurnal rhythm compared to conventional formulations, though monitoring is required when switching between brands [Australian].

### Specific Dosing Guidelines [Australian]
- **Organ Transplantation**: 
    - Initial: 10–15 mg/kg (within 12 hours before surgery), maintained for 1–2 weeks post-operatively.
    - Maintenance: Gradually reduced to 2–6 mg/kg per day.
- **Nephrotic Syndrome**: 
    - Adults: 5 mg/kg/day; Children: 6 mg/kg/day (if renal function is normal). 
    - If baseline renal function is impaired, initial dose should not exceed 2.5 mg/kg/day. 
    - Max dose: 5 mg/kg/day (adults); 6 mg/kg/day (children).
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis**: 
    - First 6 weeks: 3 mg/kg/day. If response is inadequate, increase by 0.5–1.0 mg/kg/day at 1–2 month intervals (Max: 5 mg/kg/day).
- **Psoriasis**: 
    - Initial: 2.5 mg/kg/day. May increase gradually if no improvement after one month (Max: 5 mg/kg/day).
- **Atopic Dermatitis**: 
    - Range: 2.5–5 mg/kg/day. If no response at 2.5 mg/kg within 2 weeks, increase rapidly to 5 mg/kg/day.

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Trough Levels**: Whole blood trough levels (C0) are the robust parameter for monitoring, especially in transplant patients [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustments**: For non-transplant indications, doses must be reduced by 25–50% if serum creatinine increases >30% above baseline in more than one measurement [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known allergy to ciclosporin or any excipients [Australian].
- **Non-Transplant Indications (General)**: Uncontrolled hypertension, uncontrolled infection, and primary/secondary immunodeficiency (excluding autoimmune diseases or selective IgA deficiency) [Australian].
- **Nephrotic Syndrome**: Impaired baseline renal function (serum creatinine >200 micromol/L in adults; >140 micromol/L in children) [Australian].
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis/Psoriasis/Atopic Dermatitis**: Any degree of renal impairment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Expert Management**: Should only be prescribed by physicians experienced in immunosuppressive therapy [Australian].
- **Malignancies**: Increased risk of lymphomas and skin cancers. Patients must avoid excessive UV exposure [Australian].
- **Infections**: Increased susceptibility to bacterial, fungal, parasitic, and viral infections (e.g., Polyomavirus associated nephropathy (PVAN), BK virus, and Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML)) [Australian].
- **Nephrotoxicity**: Both acute (dose-dependent, reversible) and chronic (structural changes like interstitial fibrosis) may occur. Chronic changes must be differentiated from graft rejection in transplant patients [Australian].
- **Hypertension**: Occurs in up to 50% of transplant patients. Requires regular monitoring and management with antihypertensives (avoiding those that interact with ciclosporin levels) [Australian].
- **Biochemical Changes**: May cause hyperkalaemia (potentially life-threatening), hypomagnesaemia, hyperuricaemia/gout, and hyperlipidaemia [Australian].
- **Special Excipients**: Contains **ethyl lactate**, which metabolises to ethanol. This must be considered in patients with liver disease, epilepsy, alcoholism, or in pregnancy/lactation [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are usually dose-dependent and respond to dose reduction.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects [Australian] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Hyperlipidaemia, hypertension, tremor, fatigue, headache, burning sensation (extremities), gingival hypertrophy, gastrointestinal disturbances (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain), impaired renal function, hirsutism. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Anorexia, hyperkalaemia, hyperuricaemia, hypomagnesaemia, flushing, paraesthesia, convulsions, acne, rash, pyrexia, oedema, leukopenia, anaemia, muscle cramps, myalgia. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Confusion, encephalopathy (including PRES), visual hallucinations, lethargy, agitation, thrombocytopenia, hearing loss, tinnitus, weight loss, gynaecomastia. |
| **Rare** (0.01–0.1%) | Hyperglycaemia, motor polyneuropathy, pancreatitis, micro-angiopathic haemolytic anaemia, HUS, muscle weakness, myopathy. |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Optic disc oedema (benign intracranial hypertension). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors** (Increase levels): Azole antifungals, macrolide antibiotics (erythromycin, clarithromycin), diltiazem, verapamil, amiodarone, grapefruit juice, and oral contraceptives [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers** (Decrease levels): Rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, barbiturates, and St John’s Wort [Australian].
- **P-gp Substrates**: Ciclosporin increases levels of aliskiren, dabigatran, and bosentan; co-administration should be avoided or approached with caution [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Nephrotoxic Synergy**: Increased risk with aminoglycosides, amphotericin B, ciprofloxacin, NSAIDs (especially diclofenac), methotrexate, and vancomycin [Australian].
- **Hyperkalaemia**: Risk increased with potassium-sparing diuretics, ACE inhibitors, and ARBs [Australian].
- **Statins**: Ciclosporin increases the risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Category C [Australian]**: Not teratogenic in animals but associated with embryo/fetotoxic effects at maternally toxic doses. 
    - In humans, risk of premature delivery and foetal growth retardation exists. Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk. Not recommended in non-transplant indications if alternatives exist [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: Ciclosporin passes into breast milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No data available in source documents [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: 
    - Approved for transplantation and nephrotic syndrome (age ≥1 year) [Australian].
    - Not recommended for other non-transplant indications in patients under 16 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: Experience is limited. Cautious dosing is required, usually starting at the low end of the range, due to higher frequency of decreased renal/hepatic function [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: Requires close monitoring. In non-transplant patients, impairment is often a contraindication for initiation (see Section 4) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: May lead to significantly increased ciclosporin blood levels; monitoring and dose reduction are necessary [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Potent immunosuppressive agent. It acts specifically and reversibly on lymphocytes by blocking resting lymphocytes in the G0 or G1 phase of the cell cycle. It inhibits the production and release of lymphokines, including interleukin-2 (IL-2/T-cell growth factor) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Peak plasma concentrations (Tmax) occur 1–2 hours. Bioavailability is approximately 29% higher with microemulsion formulations compared to older forms [Australian].
    - **Distribution**: Largely outside the blood volume (Vd 3.5 L/kg). 90% protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism**: Extensively metabolised by CYP3A4 to approximately 15 metabolites [Australian].
    - **Elimination**: Primarily biliary. Terminal half-life ranges from 6.3 hours (healthy) to 20.4 hours (severe liver disease) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Atopic Dermatitis**: Supported by two double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trials and one parallel-group study (86 patients total). A dose of 5 mg/kg/day was effective in reducing the area and severity of skin disease, as well as improving sleep and itch scores [Australian].
- **General**: Registration was supported by bioequivalence and pharmacokinetic studies comparing APO-CICLOSPORIN to the reference formulation (Sandimmun) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Conditions**: Store in the original container to protect from light. Do not use if the blister seal is broken [Australian].
- **Shelf Life**: Refer to the product packaging or public summary of the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
