# Editorial Product Information: ceftazidime + avibactam

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **ceftazidime + avibactam**
- Chemical class:
  - Ceftazidime: third-generation cephalosporin (beta-lactam antibacterial)
  - Avibactam: non‑β‑lactam β‑lactamase inhibitor (diazabicyclooctane)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous infusion only
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form physical descriptions, or regional schedule listings.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Adult indications:
  - Treatment of complicated intra‑abdominal infection (cIAI) — to be used in combination with metronidazole.  
  - Treatment of complicated urinary tract infection (cUTI), including pyelonephritis.  
  - Treatment of hospital‑acquired pneumonia (HAP), including ventilator‑associated pneumonia (VAP).
- Paediatric indications:
  - Treatment of cIAI (in combination with metronidazole) and cUTI (including pyelonephritis) in infants and paediatric patients from ≥ 3 months to < 18 years.
- Additional guidance:
  - Consider official guidance on appropriate antibacterial use.
  - When Gram‑positive pathogens and/or anaerobes are known or suspected contributors, use ceftazidime + avibactam together with antibacterial agent(s) active against those pathogens.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended standard adult regimen: ceftazidime 2000 mg + avibactam 500 mg administered every 8 hours by intravenous infusion over 2 hours.

### Dosing by indication (adult)
- Complicated intra‑abdominal infection (cIAI): every 8 hours; infusion time 2 hours; duration 5–14 days. Used in clinical trials in combination with metronidazole.
- Complicated urinary tract infection (cUTI), including pyelonephritis: every 8 hours; infusion time 2 hours; duration 5–10 days (total duration may be extended to 14 days in patients with bacteraemia). Total course may include IV followed by appropriate oral therapy where clinically appropriate.
- Hospital‑acquired pneumonia (including VAP): every 8 hours; infusion time 2 hours; duration 7–14 days.

### Renal impairment — adult dosage adjustments (estimated creatinine clearance (CrCL) by Cockcroft‑Gault)
- CrCL ≥ 51 to ≤ 80 mL/min: no dosage adjustment required.
- CrCL 31–50 mL/min: ceftazidime 1000 mg + avibactam 250 mg every 8 hours; infusion 2 hours.
- CrCL 16–30 mL/min: ceftazidime 750 mg + avibactam 187.5 mg every 12 hours; infusion 2 hours.
- CrCL 6–15 mL/min: ceftazidime 750 mg + avibactam 187.5 mg every 24 hours; infusion 2 hours.
- End‑stage renal disease (ESRD) including on haemodialysis: ceftazidime 750 mg + avibactam 187.5 mg every 48 hours; infusion 2 hours.
- Note: For patients with changing renal function, estimate CrCL at least daily and adjust dosing accordingly.

### Haemodialysis / renal replacement therapy
- Both ceftazidime and avibactam are removed by haemodialysis. When haemodialysis is performed, administer dose after completion of haemodialysis on dialysis days.
- Insufficient data to make specific dosage recommendations for continuous veno‑venous haemofiltration (CVVH) or peritoneal dialysis — clinical judgment required.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dosage adjustment required for hepatic impairment; close clinical monitoring advised.

### Elderly
- No routine dosage adjustment required solely on the basis of age; renal function should guide dosing.

### Paediatric dosing
- Approved age range in available sources: ≥ 3 months to < 18 years.
- General paediatric dosing (per available data):
  - For cIAI (used in trials with metronidazole): patients 6 months to < 18 years — ceftazidime 50 mg/kg + avibactam 12.5 mg/kg every 8 hours (maximum single dose ceftazidime 2000 mg + avibactam 500 mg); infusion 2 hours. Duration 7–14 days.
  - For cUTI including pyelonephritis: patients ≥ 3 months to < 6 months — ceftazidime 40 mg/kg + avibactam 10 mg/kg every 8 hours; infusion 2 hours. Duration 7–14 days.
  - Note: There is no clinical data in children 3 months to 2 years for some indications; dosing recommendations for that subgroup may be based on pharmacokinetic modelling.
- Paediatric renal impairment (age 2 years to < 18 years; CrCL estimated using Schwartz bedside formula, mL/min/1.73 m2):
  - CrCL 31–50: 25 mg/kg ceftazidime + 6.25 mg/kg avibactam every 8 hours (to a maximum of 1000 mg/250 mg); infusion 2 hours.
  - CrCL 16–30: 18.75 mg/kg ceftazidime + 4.75 mg/kg avibactam every 12 hours (to a maximum of 750 mg/187.5 mg); infusion 2 hours.
  - CrCL 6–15: dosing frequency listed as every 24 hours with infusion 2 hours; specific mg/kg dose not specified in available sources.
  - ESRD including on haemodialysis: dosing frequency listed as every 48 hours with infusion 2 hours; specific mg/kg dose not specified in available sources.
  - There is insufficient information to recommend dosing for paediatric patients < 2 years old with creatinine clearance < 50 mL/min/1.73 m2.
  - Insufficient clinical data to make specific dosing recommendations for children < 18 years undergoing haemofiltration or peritoneal dialysis.

### Preparation and administration
- Administer by intravenous infusion over 120 minutes (2 hours).
- Reconstitution and dilution steps are required prior to administration; use aseptic technique. (Detailed reconstitution/dilution instructions and in‑use stability parameters: Not specified in full in available sources for inclusion here.)
- Parenteral preparations should be visually inspected for particulate matter prior to administration.
- Each single‑use vial should be used for a single patient only; discard any residue.
- Total time between start of reconstitution and completion of preparation of infusion should not exceed 30 minutes (as provided in available sources).

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to ceftazidime, avibactam, or any excipient.
- Hypersensitivity to any cephalosporin antibacterial agent.
- Severe hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis, severe skin reaction) to any other type of β‑lactam antibacterial agent (penicillins, monobactams, carbapenems).
- Any additional regional contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed warnings specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity reactions:
  - Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity reactions (including anaphylaxis) have been reported. Discontinue immediately and initiate emergency measures if such reactions occur.
  - Before therapy, ascertain history of hypersensitivity to ceftazidime, other cephalosporins, or other β‑lactams. Exercise caution if there is a history of non‑severe hypersensitivity to penicillins, monobactams, or carbapenems.
- Clostridioides (Clostridium) difficile–associated diarrhoea:
  - Reported with ceftazidime + avibactam; can range from mild to life‑threatening. Consider this diagnosis in patients with diarrhoea during or after therapy. Discontinue drug and consider specific therapy for C. difficile. Avoid antiperistaltic agents.
- Nephrotoxicity:
  - Concomitant use with other nephrotoxic agents (e.g., aminoglycosides, potent diuretics such as furosemide) may adversely affect renal function.
- Direct antiglobulin test (DAGT/Coombs) seroconversion and haemolytic anaemia:
  - Development of a positive direct antiglobulin test has been commonly observed in clinical studies (range in Phase 3 studies: approximately 3.2% to 20.8% among patients with a negative baseline Coombs and at least one follow‑up test). Although no associated haemolysis was evidenced in these studies, drug‑induced immune haemolytic anaemia cannot be excluded. Investigate patients who develop anaemia during or after treatment.
- Severe cutaneous adverse reactions:
  - Stevens‑Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS), and acute generalised exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP) have been reported with beta‑lactam antibiotics. Discontinue immediately if SCAR is suspected.
- Neurotoxicity:
  - Cephalosporin‑associated neurotoxicity (encephalopathy, seizures, myoclonus) has been reported. Risk factors include advanced age, renal impairment, pre‑existing CNS disorders, and intravenous administration. Consider withdrawing therapy if neurotoxicity occurs.
- Spectrum limitations:
  - Ceftazidime has limited activity against most Gram‑positive organisms and anaerobes; add agents active against these organisms when appropriate.
  - Avibactam inhibits many β‑lactamases (Ambler class A and class C; some class D) but does not inhibit class B (metallo‑β‑lactamases) and is not active against many class D enzymes.
- Overgrowth of non‑susceptible organisms:
  - Prolonged use may result in selection and overgrowth of non‑susceptible organisms (e.g., Enterococci, fungi).
- Sodium content:
  - Each dose contains a clinically relevant sodium load from avibactam sodium and excipients (amount per dose: Not specified in available sources in mg; monitor in patients on controlled sodium diets).
- Use in renal impairment:
  - Dose reduction is required in moderate and severe renal impairment per dosing table above. Monitor renal function closely; creatinine clearance can change rapidly early in infection treatment.
- Paediatric dosing considerations:
  - Safety and efficacy not established in infants < 3 months. Care with volume calculations in infants 3 to < 12 months to avoid dosing errors.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic assessment of renal function (e.g., serum creatinine, estimated creatinine clearance) — at least daily in patients with changing renal function.
- Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity and severe cutaneous adverse reactions.
- Monitor for diarrhoea and consider C. difficile testing as indicated.
- Monitor for neurological signs (confusion, myoclonus, seizures).
- Monitor complete blood count and haemolysis parameters if anaemia or positive Coombs test is suspected.
- Monitor fluid/electrolyte balance and sodium intake in patients on controlled sodium diets.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Avibactam is an in vitro substrate of renal organic anion transporters OAT1 and OAT3; probenecid (an OAT inhibitor) inhibits this uptake in vitro by 56%–70% and therefore has the potential to alter avibactam elimination. Co‑administration with probenecid is not recommended (clinical interaction studies have not been conducted).
- Avibactam showed no significant in vitro inhibition of cytochrome P450 enzymes. Neither avibactam nor ceftazidime showed in vitro CYP induction at clinically relevant concentrations; therefore CYP‑mediated interactions are unlikely.
- Avibactam and ceftazidime do not inhibit major renal or hepatic transporters at clinically relevant exposures in vitro; interaction potential via these mechanisms is considered low.
- Both ceftazidime and avibactam are removed by haemodialysis.

### Pharmacodynamic and other interactions
- No clinical interaction between ceftazidime and avibactam was observed; no interaction observed between ceftazidime/avibactam and metronidazole in clinical data.
- Chloramphenicol demonstrated in vitro antagonism with ceftazidime and other cephalosporins; avoid combination due to possible antagonism in vivo.
- Concomitant treatment with high doses of cephalosporins and nephrotoxic medicinal products (e.g., aminoglycosides, potent diuretics such as furosemide) may increase risk of renal impairment.
- Clinical significance of other interactions: Not specified in available sources.

### Management notes
- Avoid co‑administration with probenecid when possible.
- Avoid combining with chloramphenicol due to potential antagonism.
- Monitor renal function closely if used with other nephrotoxic agents and adjust dosing according to renal function.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Approved for use in infants and paediatric patients ≥ 3 months to < 18 years for cIAI (in combination with metronidazole) and cUTI (including pyelonephritis) per available sources.
- Safety and efficacy not established in infants < 3 months.
- Careful dose/volume calculation required for infants (particularly ages 3 to < 12 months) to avoid overdosing.

### Elderly
- No dosage adjustment required solely on age; renal function should guide dosing. Elderly may have increased risk of neurotoxicity and renal impairment — monitor accordingly.

### Renal impairment
- Dose reductions required according to estimated CrCL as specified in Section 4 (adult) and paediatric adjustments where provided. Monitor renal function frequently; adjust dosing promptly if renal function changes.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dosage adjustment required; close clinical monitoring recommended.

### Dialysis and renal replacement
- Haemodialysis: both agents are dialysable; administer dose after haemodialysis.
- Haemofiltration/peritoneal dialysis: insufficient data to provide specific dosing recommendations.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Ceftazidime**: bactericidal third‑generation cephalosporin that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to penicillin‑binding proteins (activity principally against Gram‑negative organisms, including Pseudomonas spp.; limited activity against most Gram‑positive organisms and anaerobes).
- **Avibactam**: a non‑β‑lactam β‑lactamase inhibitor that protects ceftazidime from hydrolysis by many β‑lactamases — including Ambler class A and class C enzymes and some class D enzymes — but does not inhibit class B (metallo‑β‑lactamases) and is not active against many class D enzymes.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Administered intravenously; systemic exposure dependent on IV dosing.
- Distribution: Not fully specified in available sources.
- Metabolism: Avibactam shows no significant cytochrome P450 inhibition or induction in vitro; metabolic pathways not fully specified in available sources.
- Elimination:
  - Both ceftazidime and avibactam are primarily renally eliminated.
  - Both agents are removed by haemodialysis.
  - Avibactam is an in vitro substrate of renal OAT1 and OAT3 transporters; inhibition of these transporters (e.g., by probenecid) may alter elimination.
- Pharmacokinetic changes in special populations (renal impairment, elderly): Dose adjustments recommended for renal impairment; no routine dose change for hepatic impairment or elderly except as dictated by renal function.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Avibactam restores activity of ceftazidime against organisms producing susceptible β‑lactamases (class A and C; some D). Not active against class B β‑lactamase producers.
- Ceftazidime alone has limited Gram‑positive and anaerobic activity; combination therapy may be needed.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key trial information included in available sources:
  - In clinical trials for cIAI, ceftazidime + avibactam was used in combination with metronidazole.
  - Phase 3 studies reported direct antiglobulin (Coombs) seroconversion rates in patients who were negative at baseline and had at least one follow‑up test; observed range approximately 3.2%–20.8%.
- Detailed pivotal trial designs, populations, primary endpoints, efficacy outcomes, and safety incidence tables beyond the DAGT seroconversion range are not specified in available sources.
