# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** US (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized editorial synthesis of provided Product Information (PI) for healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinically accurate overview of the drug's profile based on the available regulatory documentation.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
*   **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
*   **General Dose:** Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
*   **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
*   **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
*   **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; frequency should never be less than every 6 hours.
*   **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
*   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
*   **Timing:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
*   **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### General Administration Notes
*   **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L).
*   **Inspection:** Parenteral products must be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL.

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
*   **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
*   **TMA/TMAO Accumulation:** While not evaluated for the injection, chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of plasma carnitine levels and clinical status is required to ensure therapeutic efficacy and safety.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specifically lettered, but descriptive]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled human studies exist. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
*   **Fertility:** No long-term animal studies have evaluated carcinogenic potential. Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, S. cerevisiae) were negative [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight (see Section 3).
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral administration is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    *   **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normal plasma free carnitine levels are 40–50 µmol/L; dialysis patients often present with levels as low as 6–20 µmol/L [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Dialysis Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough concentrations reached 68.4 ± 26.1 µmol/L after a single dose and 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Study:** A study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks.
*   **Clinical Outcomes:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the specific impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
