# Editorial Product Information: Cefaclor

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals to provide a clear, clinically accurate summary of the drug's profile.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefaclor (as monohydrate)
- **Chemical Class:** Semi-synthetic second-generation cephalosporin antibiotic
- **Brand Name(s):** **Ceclor** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Powder for oral liquid: 125 mg/5 mL and 250 mg/5 mL [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
**Cefaclor** is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by susceptible organisms, including:
- **Lower Respiratory Tract Infections:** Pneumonia, bronchitis, and acute exacerbations of chronic bronchitis [Australian].
- **Upper Respiratory Tract Infections:** Pharyngitis, tonsillitis, and otitis media [Australian].
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** Including impetigo [Australian].
- **Urinary Tract Infections:** Pyelonephritis and cystitis [Australian].

*Note: Penicillin remains the drug of choice for streptococcal infections and rheumatic fever prophylaxis. While cefaclor is effective in eradicating nasopharyngeal streptococci, data regarding the prevention of rheumatic fever are currently unavailable [Australian].*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adults
- **General Dosing:** 250 mg every 8 to 12 hours [Australian].
- **Bronchitis and Pneumonia:** 250 mg administered 3 times daily [Australian].
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** 250 mg administered 2 to 3 times daily [Australian].
- **Severe Infections/Less Susceptible Organisms:** Doses may be doubled to 500 mg every 8 hours. The maximum daily dose is 2 g [Australian].

### Children
- **Mild to Moderate Infections:** 20 mg/kg/day in divided doses every 8 hours (Maximum: 1 g/day) [Australian].
- **Serious Infections and Otitis Media:** 40 mg/kg/day in divided doses every 8 to 12 hours (Maximum: 2 g/day) [Australian].
- **Specific Conditions:** For streptococcal pharyngitis, tonsillitis, and impetigo, twice-daily (12-hourly) dosing is considered effective [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution (Oral Liquid):** 
    - 125 mg/5 mL: Add 60 mL of water in two portions; shake well after each addition.
    - 250 mg/5 mL: Add 45 mL of water in two portions; shake well after each addition.
- **Duration:** For beta-haemolytic streptococcal infections, therapy must continue for at least 10 days [Australian].
- **Food Effect:** Cefaclor is well-absorbed regardless of food intake; however, administration with food reduces peak plasma concentrations by 25% to 50% [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to the cephalosporin class of antibiotics [Australian].
- History of a major/severe allergic reaction to penicillin [Australian].
- Hypersensitivity to any excipients in the formulation (e.g., sugars, erythrosine) [Australian].
- **Paediatrics:** Contraindicated in infants under one month of age [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Allergic Reactions:** Partial cross-allergenicity exists between penicillins and cephalosporins. Serious and occasionally fatal anaphylaxis has occurred. If an allergic reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately and initiate emergency treatment (e.g., adrenaline, oxygen, steroids) [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and DRESS have been reported. Discontinue treatment if SCAR is suspected [Australian].
- **Pseudomembranous Colitis:** *Clostridium difficile*-associated diarrhoea (CDAD) may occur during or several weeks after therapy. Avoid drugs that delay peristalsis (e.g., opiates) [Australian].
- **Neurotoxicity:** Risk of encephalopathy, seizures, and myoclonus, particularly in elderly patients or those with renal impairment [Australian].
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms [Australian].
- **Bleeding Disorders:** All cephalosporins may cause hypoprothrombinemia and potential bleeding [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** May cause false-positive results for urine glucose (using Benedict’s, Fehling’s, or Clinitest) and a positive direct Coombs test [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (1–10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea (most frequent) [Australian].
- **Immune System:** Morbilliform eruptions, pruritus, and urticaria [Australian].

### Rare (<0.1%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, pseudomembranous colitis [Australian].
- **Hepatobiliary:** Transient hepatitis, cholestatic jaundice, slight elevations in AST/ALT [Australian].
- **Renal:** Reversible interstitial nephritis, elevations in serum urea or creatinine [Australian].
- **Nervous System:** Reversible hyperactivity, nervousness, insomnia, confusion, dizziness, hallucinations, and somnolence [Australian].
- **Haematological:** Eosinophilia, transient lymphocytosis, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and haemolytic anaemia [Australian].

### Jurisdiction-Specific Frequency Notes
- **Serum-sickness-like reactions:** Characterised by erythema multiforme/rash and arthritis/arthralgia. These are reported more frequently in children (0.055%) than in adults (<0.01%) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antacids:** Aluminium hydroxide or magnesium hydroxide-containing antacids diminish cefaclor absorption if taken within 1 hour of administration [Australian].
- **Probenecid:** Inhibits renal tubular secretion, leading to increased and prolonged cefaclor serum concentrations [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Rare reports of increased prothrombin time with or without clinical bleeding [Australian].
- **Vitamin K:** Cephalosporins may inhibit Vitamin K synthesis; prophylactic Vitamin K therapy is recommended for prolonged use in malnourished or seriously ill patients [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Animal studies (rats/mice) showed minor skeletal malformations at high doses. Safety in human pregnancy is not established; use only if essential [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Cefaclor is excreted in breast milk in small amounts (0.16 to 0.21 µg/mL). The effect on nursing infants is unknown; exercise caution [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment of fertility; human data are unavailable [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not established for infants <1 month. Serum-sickness-like reactions occur more frequently in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** In patients >65 years with normal renal function, no dosage adjustment is necessary despite slightly higher peak plasma concentrations [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Administer with caution. Dosage adjustments are usually not required in moderate to severe impairment as the half-life only increases to 2.3–2.8 hours in anuria [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to lack of documented clinical experience in this population [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis [Australian].
- **Microbiology:** Active against *Staphylococci* (excluding MRSA), *S. pyogenes*, *S. pneumoniae*, *E. coli*, *P. mirabilis*, *Klebsiella* spp., *H. influenzae*, *N. gonorrhoeae*, and *M. catarrhalis* [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma levels (7–23 µg/mL) reached within 30–60 minutes in fasting subjects [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** No evidence of metabolism in humans [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Plasma half-life is 40–60 minutes in healthy subjects [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trials:** No specific pivotal trial data summaries were provided in the available source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Dry Powder:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Suspension:** Store under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C). Do not freeze. Discard any unused portion after 14 days [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
