# Editorial Product Information: Clocortolone Pivalate

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of clocortolone pivalate, synthesized for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary intended for clinical reference and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Clocortolone pivalate (a medium-potency synthetic corticosteroid).
- **Chemical Class:** Topical corticosteroid; 9-chloro-6α-fluoro-11β, 21-dihydroxy-16αmethylpregna-1, 4-diene-3, 20-dione 21-pivalate.
- **Brand Names:** Clocortolone Pivalate Cream USP, 0.1% [US].
- **ATC Code:** D07AB (Corticosteroids, moderately potent (group II)).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Cream, 0.1% (containing 1 mg clocortolone pivalate per gram of cream) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharmaceutical Industries, Inc. / Taro Pharmaceutical Industries Ltd. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Corticosteroid-Responsive Dermatoses:** Indicated for the relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations associated with dermatoses that respond to topical corticosteroids [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Apply a thin film of the cream sparingly to the affected areas three times daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Gently rub the cream into the skin.
    - This medication is for external use only. 
    - Avoid contact with the eyes [US].
- **Occlusive Dressings:** 
    - May be utilized for the management of psoriasis or other recalcitrant skin conditions [US].
    - If a secondary skin infection develops, occlusive therapy must be discontinued and appropriate antimicrobial treatment initiated [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a known history of hypersensitivity to clocortolone pivalate or any components of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **HPA Axis Suppression:** Systemic absorption of topical corticosteroids can produce reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression. This may lead to clinical glucocorticosteroid insufficiency upon withdrawal.
- **Systemic Effects:** Manifestations of Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycemia, and glucosuria have been reported following systemic absorption during topical treatment.
- **Risk Augmentation:** Factors increasing systemic absorption include:
    - Application to large surface areas.
    - Prolonged duration of use.
    - Application under occlusive dressings.
- **Monitoring:** Patients receiving large doses or under occlusion should be periodically evaluated for HPA axis suppression via the ACTH stimulation test or urinary free cortisol test [US].
- **Local Irritation:** If irritation or sensitization occurs, discontinue use and initiate appropriate therapy.
- **Concomitant Infection:** In the presence of dermatological infections, use an appropriate antifungal or antibacterial agent. If the infection does not resolve promptly, discontinue the corticosteroid until the infection is controlled [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Local adverse reactions are reported infrequently but occur more often with the use of occlusive dressings. The following are listed in approximate decreasing order of occurrence [US]:
- **Common/Infrequent:** Burning, itching, irritation, dryness, folliculitis, hypertrichosis, acneform eruptions, hypopigmentation.
- **Associated with Occlusion/Prolonged Use:** Perioral dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis, skin maceration, secondary infection, skin atrophy, striae, miliaria.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Management:** Systemic interactions are less likely with topical application unless significant systemic absorption occurs via damaged skin or occlusive dressings.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** While specific letter categories are being phased out, the US PI notes that corticosteroids are generally teratogenic in laboratory animals at low systemic doses. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Avoid extensive use, large amounts, or prolonged treatment in pregnant patients [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if topical administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable quantities in breast milk. Caution should be exercised when clocortolone is administered to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies to evaluate the effect of topical clocortolone on fertility have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Children are more susceptible to HPA axis suppression and Cushing’s syndrome than adults due to a higher skin surface area-to-body weight ratio.
    - Reported effects in children include linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, and low plasma cortisol levels. 
    - **Intracranial Hypertension:** Manifestations such as bulging fontanelles, headaches, and bilateral papilledema have been reported [US].
    - **Administration:** Limit use to the minimum amount required for efficacy. Parents should be advised against using tight-fitting diapers or plastic pants on children treated in the diaper area (as these act as occlusive dressings) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, absorbed corticosteroids are primarily metabolized in the liver and excreted by the kidneys [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Topical corticosteroids possess anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. The exact mechanism of anti-inflammatory activity in the skin remains unclear [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Percutaneous absorption is determined by the vehicle, epidermal integrity, and use of occlusive dressings. Inflammation or skin disease increases absorption.
    - **Distribution:** Once absorbed, corticosteroids are bound to plasma proteins in varying degrees.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted by the kidneys and, to a lesser extent, in the bile [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **General Findings:** Comparative efficacy of topical corticosteroids is often predicted using vasoconstrictor assays, where a correlation between vasoconstrictor potency and therapeutic efficacy has been suggested [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Keep the tube tightly closed [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx Only (Prescription only).
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
