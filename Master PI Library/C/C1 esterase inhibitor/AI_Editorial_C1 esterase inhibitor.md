# Editorial Product Information: Human C1 esterase inhibitor

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory nuances into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing and dispensing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Human C1 esterase inhibitor (highly purified, plasma-derived concentrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Serine protease inhibitor (serpin).
- **Brand Name:** **Berinert IV** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B06AC01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Freeze-dried powder for injection supplied with Water for Injections for reconstitution [Australian].
    - **500 IU presentation:** 500 IU per vial (reconstituted to 50 IU/mL in 10 mL) [Australian].
    - **1500 IU presentation:** 1500 IU per vial (reconstituted to 500 IU/mL in 3 mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** CSL Behring (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hereditary Angioedema (HAE):** Indicated for the treatment of acute attacks in patients with HAE [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose for both adult and paediatric populations is **20 IU per kg body weight** [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administration should be initiated under the supervision of a physician experienced in treating C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency [Australian].
    - Reconstitution must be performed using aseptic technique with the provided Mix2Vial™ filter transfer set [Australian].
    - **Berinert IV 500 IU:** Administer via slow intravenous injection at a rate of approximately 4 mL/minute [Australian].
    - **Berinert IV 1500 IU:** Administer as a bolus intravenous injection [Australian].
- **Self-Administration:** Home treatment or self-administration may be considered if deemed appropriate by the treating physician, provided the patient or carer has received adequate training on intravenous infusion and the management of adverse reactions [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not applicable as the product is indicated for the treatment of acute attacks.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of the excipients (glycine, sodium citrate, sodium chloride) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis:** Allergic-type reactions are possible. If symptoms of hypersensitivity or anaphylaxis occur (e.g., urticaria, chest tightness, wheezing, hypotension), administration must be discontinued immediately and appropriate emergency treatment initiated. Prophylactic antihistamines and corticosteroids may be considered for patients with a known tendency toward allergies [Australian].
- **Laryngeal Oedema:** Patients experiencing acute laryngeal attacks require particularly careful monitoring and should be advised to seek urgent hospital treatment immediately, even after receiving treatment, due to the risk of airway obstruction [Australian].
- **Pathogen Safety:** As a product derived from human plasma, the risk of transmitting infectious agents (e.g., HIV, HBV, HCV, HAV, or Parvovirus B19) cannot be totally excluded. Standard measures include donor screening, testing of plasma pools for viral markers, and dedicated inactivation/removal steps during manufacturing (pasteurisation, hydrophobic interaction chromatography, and nanofiltration) [Australian].
- **Sodium Content:** Contains up to 486 mg of sodium per 100 mL of solution. This should be considered for patients on a controlled sodium diet [Australian].
- **Thrombosis Risk:** Thrombotic events have been reported in off-label use (Capillary Leak Syndrome) or at very high doses (exceeding 90 IU/kg) in neonates or young children [Australian].
- **Capillary Leak Syndrome:** Treatment with **Berinert IV** is not advised for this condition [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Frequency:** Overall adverse reactions are considered rare (≥ 1/10,000 to < 1/1,000) [Australian].
- **Common Adverse Events (Clinical Trials):**
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhoea [Australian].
    - **Nervous System:** Dysgeusia, headache [Australian].
    - **General:** Injection site pain/redness, chills, fever, muscle spasms [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - An increase in the severity of pain associated with HAE was reported as a serious adverse event in trials [Australian].
    - Allergic/anaphylactic-type reactions and potential for thrombosis (at supra-therapeutic doses) are high-priority clinical concerns [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No formal interaction studies have been conducted. The interaction of **Berinert IV** with other medicinal products is currently not established [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Safety in human pregnancy has not been established in controlled clinical trials. Clinical experience suggests no negative impact on the mother or child. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [Australian].
    - *Jurisdictional Note:* A specific pregnancy letter category (e.g., Category B or C) is not specified in available sources [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety during lactation is not established. Use only if clearly indicated [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No specific studies have been conducted on the effects of this product on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** The 20 IU/kg dose applies to paediatric patients. While safety and efficacy were not systematically evaluated in children, clinical trial data showed no apparent differences in safety or efficacy profiles compared to adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy in the elderly population (>65 years) have not been established [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments for renal or hepatic impairment are not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** C1 esterase inhibitor is a major inhibitor of the classical complement pathway, blocking the activation of C1r and C1s. It also regulates the contact (kinin), coagulation, and fibrinolytic systems by inhibiting kallikrein and factors XIIa and XIa [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Cmax:** Reached within 0.8 hours post-administration [Australian].
    - **Half-life:** The median half-life is approximately 36.1 hours in adults and 32.9 hours in children [Australian].
    - **In vivo recovery (IVR):** 86.7% (higher in children at 98.2%) [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration replaces the missing or dysfunctional protein, relieving HAE symptoms by preventing the spontaneous activation of the complement and kinin-generating systems [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Phase III Study:** A multinational, double-blind study (n=124) evaluated 10 IU/kg, 20 IU/kg, and placebo for acute abdominal or facial HAE attacks.
    - **Results:** The 20 IU/kg dose significantly reduced the median time to onset of relief to 30 minutes compared to 90 minutes for placebo (p = 0.0025) [Australian]. 
    - The 10 IU/kg dose did not demonstrate a significant clinical difference from placebo [Australian].
- **Extension Study:** Evaluated 1,085 HAE attacks (including laryngeal). The median time to initial relief for laryngeal attacks was 0.25 hours, with complete resolution in a median of 8.38 hours [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light and do not freeze [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Product:** Does not contain an antimicrobial preservative.
    - **500 IU presentation:** Use within 24 hours if stored at 2–8°C [Australian].
    - **1500 IU presentation:** Use within 8 hours if stored at 2–8°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Unscheduled [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription only medicine.
