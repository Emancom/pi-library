# Editorial Product Information: Calcium Acetate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of calcium acetate for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesizes clinical data, regulatory warnings, and administration guidelines into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Calcium acetate (Phosphate binder)
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in source [US: Distributed by American Health Packaging]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Capsule: 667 mg [US]
    - (Note: 667 mg of calcium acetate is equivalent to 169 mg or 8.45 mEq of elemental calcium) [US]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** American Health Packaging [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hyperphosphatemia in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the reduction of serum phosphorus levels in patients with ESRD [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):**
    - **Initial Dose:** 2 capsules (1334 mg) administered with each meal [US].
    - **Titration:** Increase dosage gradually depending on serum phosphorus levels. Titration should occur every 2 to 3 weeks until the target phosphorus level is reached, provided hypercalcemia does not occur [US].
    - **Maintenance Dose:** Most patients require 3 to 4 capsules with each meal [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole; do not chew [US].
    - Must be taken concurrently with meals to bind dietary phosphate [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypercalcemia:** Use is strictly contraindicated in patients with existing hypercalcemia [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None reported in available sources.
- **Hypercalcemia Risk:**
    - Patients with ESRD may develop hypercalcemia. Concomitant use of calcium supplements or calcium-based antacids should be avoided [US].
    - **Severe Hypercalcemia (Ca >12 mg/dL):** Associated with confusion, delirium, stupor, and coma. Requires emergency measures, including acute hemodialysis and discontinuation of the drug [US].
    - **Mild Hypercalcemia (10.5 to 11.9 mg/dL):** May be asymptomatic or present as nausea, vomiting, anorexia, and constipation. Management involves dose reduction or temporary discontinuation of calcium acetate and Vitamin D therapy [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Serum calcium should be monitored twice weekly during the early dose-adjustment phase [US].
    - The serum calcium-phosphorus (Ca x P) product should be maintained below 55 mg²/dL² [US].
- **Vascular/Soft Tissue Calcification:** Chronic hypercalcemia may lead to ectopic calcification. The long-term impact on the progression of vascular calcification has not been fully determined [US].
- **Digitalis Toxicity:** Hypercalcemia induced by calcium acetate may aggravate digitalis toxicity [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily related to the mechanism of action and the risk of calcium absorption.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Hypercalcemia (12.6%), Nausea, Vomiting [US] |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Not specified (Nausea/Vomiting specifically noted as "occasional" in clinical studies) [US] |
| **Frequency Unknown** | Pruritus (isolated cases, may indicate allergy), Dizziness, Edema, Weakness [US] |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Mechanism:** Calcium acetate may bind to drugs with anionic functions (e.g., carboxyl or hydroxyl groups), reducing their bioavailability [US].
- **Specific Interactions:**
    - **Fluoroquinolones (e.g., Ciprofloxacin):** Co-administration of 2.7 g of calcium acetate can decrease ciprofloxacin bioavailability by approximately 50% [US].
    - **Tetracyclines:** Bioavailability may be significantly decreased [US].
- **Management:** 
    - Administer interacting oral medications at least **one hour before** or **three hours after** calcium acetate [US].
    - For drugs with a narrow therapeutic range, monitor blood levels closely [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C [US]:** No animal reproduction studies or adequate controlled human studies have been conducted. Maintenance of normal calcium levels is vital; hypercalcemia during pregnancy increases risks of stillbirth, preterm delivery, and neonatal hypocalcemia/hypoparathyroidism [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Calcium acetate is excreted in human milk. It is not expected to harm the infant if maternal calcium levels are appropriately monitored [US].
- **Fertility:** No fertility studies have been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in pediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not identify differences between elderly and younger patients. Dosing should be cautious, typically starting at the lower end of the range due to higher frequencies of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically for ESRD; no specific dose adjustments provided other than titration based on serum phosphorus and calcium levels [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** When taken with meals, calcium acetate combines with dietary phosphate to form an insoluble calcium phosphate complex, which is subsequently excreted in the feces [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces serum phosphorus levels, which helps prevent secondary hyperparathyroidism in ESRD patients [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Systemically absorbed up to 40% under fasting conditions and 30% under non-fasting conditions [US].
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy Data:**
    - In a 12-week open-label study (n=91), calcium acetate (average final dose 3.4 tablets per meal) reduced serum phosphorus by 30% (p<0.01) [US].
    - A double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study (n=69) showed a 19% decrease in serum phosphorus over two weeks compared to placebo (p<0.01) [US].
- **Safety Data:** Hypercalcemia (>11 mg/dL) was reported in 16% of patients in a 3-month study [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Do not use if the blister is torn or broken [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison/Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
