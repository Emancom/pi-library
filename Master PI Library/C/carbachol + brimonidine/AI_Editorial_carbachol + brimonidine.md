# Editorial Product Information: carbachol + brimonidine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **carbachol + brimonidine**
- Chemical class:
  - Carbachol — cholinergic (muscarinic) agonist (miotic)
  - Brimonidine (tartrate) — alpha-adrenergic receptor agonist
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Topical ophthalmic

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dose: Instill one drop in each eye once daily.

### Strengths
- Product strength described in available sources: carbachol 2.75% (27.5 mg/mL) combined with brimonidine tartrate 0.1% (1 mg/mL). (Presented here for clinical dosing reference.)

### Administration instructions
- Remove contact lenses prior to instillation. Wait at least 10 minutes after dosing before reinserting contact lenses.
- If more than one topical ophthalmic medication is being used, administer each medication at least 5 minutes apart.
- One single-dose vial may be used to dose both eyes. Discard any remaining contents and single-dose vial immediately after use.
- To prevent eye injury or contamination, avoid touching the tip of the vial to the eye, eyelids, or any other surface.
- Advise patients that vision may be temporarily blurred or dim following instillation; caution regarding driving or operating machinery if vision is not clear.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to carbachol, brimonidine (tartrate), or any component of the formulation (including excipients).
- No additional jurisdiction-specific contraindications are specified in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Blurred vision / accommodative spasm: Miotic agents may cause accommodative spasm and transient blurred vision. Advise patients not to drive or operate machinery if vision is not clear. Exercise caution with night driving or activities performed in poor illumination.
- Temporary dim or dark vision: Patients may experience transient dim or dark vision following instillation.
- Risk of retinal tear or detachment: Rare cases of retinal tear and detachment have been reported with miotic therapy. Patients with preexisting retinal disease may be at increased risk. Examination of the retina is advised for all patients prior to initiating therapy. Instruct patients to seek immediate medical attention for sudden onset of flashes, floaters, or loss of vision.
- Iritis/ocular inflammation: Use with caution in patients with active iritis. Miotics may exacerbate sequelae of ocular inflammation (e.g., posterior or anterior synechiae); use is not recommended when iritis is present.
- Potentiation of vascular insufficiency: May potentiate syndromes associated with vascular insufficiency. Use with caution in patients with depression, cerebral or coronary insufficiency, Raynaud's phenomenon, orthostatic hypotension, or thromboangiitis obliterans.
- Potential for eye injury or contamination: Avoid contact between the vial tip and eye or adnexa. Discard single-dose vial after use.

### Monitoring requirements
- Retinal examination prior to initiation of therapy (recommended).
- Advise patients to report immediately new flashes, floaters, or loss of vision.
- Monitor for systemic signs of hypotension, especially when used concomitantly with antihypertensive agents or in patients with cardiovascular disease.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Contact lens wear: remove lenses prior to instillation and wait 10 minutes before reinsertion.
- Concomitant topical ocular medications: administer at least 5 minutes apart.
- Consider systemic effects in susceptible patients (see Drug Interactions and Overdosage).

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Antihypertensive agents and cardiac glycosides: Brimonidine may reduce blood pressure; concomitant use with antihypertensives or cardiac glycosides may enhance hypotensive effects. Exercise caution and monitor blood pressure as clinically indicated.
- CNS depressants: Concomitant use with central nervous system depressants (e.g., alcohol, opiates, barbiturates, sedatives, anesthetics) may result in additive CNS depressant effects. Consider clinical monitoring and appropriate counsel.
- Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs): TCAs have been reported to blunt the hypotensive effect of systemic clonidine; it is unknown whether concurrent use with this ophthalmic combination affects pupil constriction or systemic hemodynamics. Use with caution.
- Monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors: MAO inhibitors may theoretically interfere with the metabolism/uptake of brimonidine and potentially increase systemic side effects such as hypotension. Exercise caution.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Brimonidine is extensively metabolized by the liver; agents that substantially alter hepatic metabolism may theoretically affect systemic brimonidine exposure. Specific interaction studies with carbachol + brimonidine are not reported in available sources.

### Management notes
- Review concomitant medications that lower blood pressure and monitor signs/symptoms of hypotension.
- Consider additional monitoring or alternative therapies in patients on multiple agents that can potentiate hypotension or CNS depression.
- No specific dose adjustments for drug–drug interactions are defined in the available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Human data: No adequate and well-controlled studies of carbachol + brimonidine in pregnant women are available.
- Animal data: Topical ocular administration in rats and rabbits throughout organogenesis did not produce teratogenic effects at dose levels up to approximately three times the maximum recommended human ocular dose (MRHOD) based on body surface area scaling. In rabbits at ~3× MRHOD, maternal toxicity (reduced food consumption, body weight change, and morbidity in some animals) was observed without fetal effects; maternal effects resolved after dosing cessation.
- Recommendation: Administer during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the mother and fetus.
- Pregnancy category/classification systems (jurisdictional): Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- No information on the presence of carbachol or brimonidine in human milk, effects on the breastfed infant, or effects on milk production.
- Systemic exposures after topical ocular administration are low; however, it is unknown whether measurable amounts would be present in breast milk.
- Clinical decision: Consider the benefits of breastfeeding, the mother's clinical need for therapy, and potential adverse effects on the breastfed child.
- Formal breastfeeding risk categories: Not specified in available sources.

### Fertility
- Fertility studies for carbachol or the fixed combination were not conducted.
- A reproduction and fertility study with brimonidine tartrate in rats demonstrated no adverse effects on male or female fertility at oral doses up to 1 mg/kg.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- The fixed-dose combination produces pupillary constriction (miosis) and a pinhole effect that increases depth of focus and improves near visual acuity in presbyopia.
- Carbachol (cholinergic agonist) stimulates contraction of the iris sphincter and ciliary body (miotic effect).
- Brimonidine (alpha-adrenergic agonist) inhibits contraction of the iris dilator muscle and relaxes tonic ciliary muscle contraction, augments pupillary selectivity, and increases ocular bioavailability of carbachol.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Pupil size reduction under mesopic conditions was significantly greater with the combination than with either active monotherapy (in comparative single-dose study) and greater than vehicle in randomized studies, with onset noted within 30 minutes and effect sustained for several hours (see Clinical Trials).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Systemic exposures after topical ocular administration are generally low. In a subset of subjects after one drop in each eye, brimonidine plasma was quantifiable in 5 of 14 subjects (Cmax up to 0.066 ng/mL; Tmax ~0.52 hours). Carbachol plasma was quantifiable in 6 of 14 subjects (Cmax up to 1.02 ng/mL; Tmax up to ~4.42 hours).
- Distribution: Brimonidine is approximately 29% bound to plasma proteins in healthy subjects. Protein binding of carbachol not studied in available sources.
- Metabolism: Brimonidine is extensively metabolized by the liver. Metabolism of carbachol has not been characterized in humans in available sources.
- Elimination: Urinary excretion is a major route for brimonidine and its metabolites (approximate urinary recovery after an oral radioactive dose: ~74% in urine within 120 hours). Excretion of carbachol has not been studied in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Overview
- Two pivotal Phase 3 randomized, double-masked controlled studies in adults aged approximately 45–80 years with presbyopia demonstrated efficacy for improvement in near visual acuity following topical administration of the fixed-dose combination.

### Study BRIO I (single-dose crossover)
- Design: Single-dose, randomized, double-masked crossover study.
- Participants: ~182 participants received single administrations of the combination, carbachol monotherapy, and brimonidine monotherapy with washout between treatments.
- Purpose: Evaluate superiority of the combination over each active monotherapy after a single dose.
- Key findings (proportion achieving gain ≥3 lines [15 letters] in binocular uncorrected near visual acuity [BUCNVA] without loss of ≥1 line [5 letters] in binocular uncorrected distance visual acuity [BUCDVA]):
  - Hour 0.5: Combination 27.0% vs brimonidine 10.8% (p<0.01) and carbachol 24.9% (p=0.54)
  - Hour 1: Combination 49.4% vs brimonidine 22.7% (p<0.01) and carbachol 35.0% (p<0.01)
  - Hour 2: Combination 51.1% vs brimonidine 26.7% (p<0.01) and carbachol 39.8% (p<0.01)
  - Hour 4: Combination 32.0% vs brimonidine 22.2% (p=0.02) and carbachol 21.6% (p=0.01)
  - Hour 6: Combination 22.5% vs brimonidine 14.8% (p=0.04) and carbachol 13.7% (p<0.01)
  - Hour 8: Combination 13.5% vs brimonidine 12.5% (p=0.73) and carbachol 8.6% (p=0.10)

### Study BRIO II (randomized, vehicle-controlled; single-dose and long-term safety)
- Design: Randomized, double-masked, vehicle-controlled study with single-dose efficacy assessment and a long-term safety portion (once-daily dosing for up to 6 months with a subsequent 6-month open-label extension).
- Participants: 436 randomized for single-dose efficacy (218 combination, 218 vehicle); long-term safety evaluated in additional participants (total receiving combination in safety portion: 358).
- Population included participants who were post–refractive surgery and/or pseudophakic.
- Key findings (proportion achieving gain ≥3 lines in BUCNVA without loss ≥1 line in BUCDVA):
  - Hour 0.5: Combination 19.4% vs vehicle 1.8% (p<0.01)
  - Hour 1: Combination 38.2% vs vehicle 3.7% (p<0.01)
  - Hour 2: Combination 34.1% vs vehicle 3.7% (p<0.01)
  - Hour 4: Combination 20.3% vs vehicle 2.8% (p<0.01)
  - Hour 6: Combination 13.8% vs vehicle 2.8% (p<0.01)
  - Hour 8: Combination 10.2% vs vehicle 3.2% (p=0.01)

### Safety observations in trials
- Across studies (total ~536 patients exposed), the most frequently reported adverse reactions included eye irritation on instillation and headache (reported in ~10–16% of participants), and eye pain on instillation and visual impairment (reported in ~5–7% of participants). Most adverse events were mild and transient.

### Long-term safety
- Long-term exposure data were collected up to 12 months (6-month dosing plus 6-month open-label extension in BRIO II). No overarching differences in safety or effectiveness between older and younger adults were observed in available trial data.
