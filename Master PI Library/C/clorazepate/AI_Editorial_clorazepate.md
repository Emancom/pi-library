# Editorial Product Information: Clorazepate Dipotassium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a clinically relevant format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulations and the current approved PI for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Clorazepate dipotassium (a benzodiazepine).
- **Brand Names:** Gen-Xene (historical), various generics [US]. 
- **ATC Code:** N05BA05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets:** 3.75 mg, 7.5 mg, and 15 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharmaceutical Industries, Inc. / Taro Pharmaceuticals U.S.A., Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anxiety Disorders:** Management of anxiety disorders or short-term relief of anxiety symptoms. Treatment of anxiety associated with everyday stress usually does not require an anxiolytic [US].
- **Acute Alcohol Withdrawal:** Symptomatic relief of acute alcohol withdrawal [US].
- **Partial Seizures:** Adjunctive therapy in the management of partial seizures [US].
- **Long-term Use:** Effectiveness for anxiety (beyond 4 months) has not been systematically evaluated. Periodic reassessment of the drug's usefulness for the individual patient is required [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Anxiety:** The usual daily dose is 30 mg administered in divided doses. The dosage should be adjusted gradually within the range of 15 mg to 60 mg daily based on patient response [US].
- **Bedtime Administration:** Alternatively, a single 15 mg dose may be given at bedtime as an initial dose [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Acute Alcohol Withdrawal (Specific 4-Day Regimen) [US]:**
    - **Day 1:** 30 mg initially, followed by 30 mg to 60 mg in divided doses.
    - **Day 2:** 45 mg to 90 mg in divided doses.
    - **Day 3:** 22.5 mg to 45 mg in divided doses.
    - **Day 4:** 15 mg to 30 mg in divided doses.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequently reduce the daily dose to 7.5 mg to 15 mg and discontinue as soon as the patient is stable. The maximum recommended total daily dose is 90 mg.
- **Adjunctive Therapy for Seizures [US]:**
    - **Adults (>12 years):** Maximum initial dose is 7.5 mg three times daily. Increase by no more than 7.5 mg per week. Maximum dose: 90 mg/day.
    - **Children (9–12 years):** Maximum initial dose is 7.5 mg twice daily. Increase by no more than 7.5 mg per week. Maximum dose: 60 mg/day.

### Administration Instructions
- To minimize drowsiness in seizure patients, recommended initial dosages and titration increments should not be exceeded [US].
- **Discontinuation:** To reduce the risk of withdrawal reactions, use a gradual taper to discontinue or reduce the dosage. If withdrawal symptoms occur, consider pausing the taper or increasing the dose to the previous level before decreasing more slowly [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to clorazepate dipotassium [US].
- Acute narrow-angle glaucoma [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Risks from Concomitant Use with Opioids:** Simultaneous use of benzodiazepines and opioids may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death. Reserve concomitant prescribing for patients with inadequate alternative treatment options; limit dosages and durations to the minimum required.
- **Abuse, Misuse, and Addiction:** Use of clorazepate exposes users to risks of abuse and addiction, which can lead to overdose or death. Assess risk before prescribing and monitor throughout treatment.
- **Dependence and Withdrawal Reactions:** Continued use may lead to clinically significant physical dependence. Abrupt discontinuation or rapid dose reduction may precipitate life-threatening withdrawal reactions.

### General Warnings
- **CNS Depression:** Patients should be cautioned against engaging in hazardous occupations requiring mental alertness (e.g., operating machinery or driving) [US].
- **Suicidal Behavior and Ideation:** Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behavior. Monitor patients for new or worsening depression and unusual changes in mood [US].
- **Neonatal Sedation and Withdrawal Syndrome:** Use late in pregnancy can result in sedation (respiratory depression, lethargy, hypotonia) and/or withdrawal symptoms (hyperreflexia, irritability, tremors) in the neonate [US].
- **Psychotic Reactions:** Not recommended for use in depressive neuroses or psychotic reactions [US].

### Monitoring
- Periodic blood counts and liver function tests are recommended during prolonged therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are listed by descending order of occurrence where frequency data is not specified.
- **Common:** Drowsiness (most frequent) [US].
- **Less Common:** Dizziness, various gastrointestinal complaints, nervousness, blurred vision, dry mouth, headache, and mental confusion [US].
- **Other Reported Effects:** Insomnia, transient skin rashes, fatigue, ataxia, genitourinary complaints, irritability, diplopia, depression, tremor, slurred speech, and decrease in systolic blood pressure [US].
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Abnormal liver and kidney function tests; decrease in hematocrit [US].
- **Serious Effects:** Respiratory depression, suicidal ideation, and acute withdrawal reactions (including seizures) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Opioids:** Increased risk of fatal respiratory depression and profound sedation [US].
- **CNS Depressants (Alcohol, Barbiturates, Narcotics, Phenothiazines, MAOIs, Antidepressants):** Potentiation of sedative effects. Animal data indicates clorazepate increases the inhibitory effects of chlorpromazine and prolongs sleep time after hexobarbital or ethanol [US].
- **Antacids:** Concurrent administration of antacids at therapeutic levels does not significantly influence clorazepate bioavailability [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in the current labeling, but warnings describe risks of neonatal sedation and withdrawal syndrome [US]. 
    - **Human Data:** Observational studies do not report a clear association with major birth defects, though early studies suggested risks with other benzodiazepines [US].
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Benzodiazepines cross the placenta. Monitor neonates exposed in utero for sedation and withdrawal symptoms [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Clorazepate and its active metabolite, nordiazepam, are present in breast milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended due to potential for infant sedation and poor feeding [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no alteration in fertility indices or reproductive capacity [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Not recommended for children under 9 years of age [US].
    - Seizure dosing established for ages 9–12 (see Section 3) [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** 
    - Elderly or debilitated patients may be especially sensitive to benzodiazepine effects.
    - **Anxiety Dosing:** Start at 7.5 mg to 15 mg daily [US].
    - Dosing should be initiated at the lower end of the range and adjusted gradually to preclude ataxia or excessive sedation [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Observe usual precautions; dose adjustments may be necessary as clorazepate is metabolized in the liver and excreted by the kidneys [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** As a benzodiazepine, it exerts depressant effects on the central nervous system, likely through the enhancement of GABAergic neurotransmission [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Clorazepate is a pro-drug, rapidly decarboxylated in the stomach to its primary active metabolite, **nordiazepam**. Nordiazepam is further hydroxylated in the liver to conjugated oxazepam [US].
    - **Half-life:** The apparent plasma half-life of nordiazepam is approximately 40 to 50 hours [US].
    - **Protein Binding:** Nordiazepam is 97% to 98% protein bound [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal; 62–67% excreted in urine and 15–19% in feces within 10 days [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits tranquilizing and sedative effects. Studies in healthy men showed no toxic effects with single daily doses as high as 120 mg [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Long-term Management:** Effectiveness for anxiety (over 4 months) has not been assessed by systematic clinical studies.
- **Seizure Disorders:** Long-term studies in patients with epilepsy have shown continued therapeutic activity.
- **Suicidality:** Data derived from a pooled analysis of 199 placebo-controlled trials of 11 different antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) showed a relative risk of 1.8 for suicidal behavior/ideation compared to placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from moisture. Keep bottle tightly closed. Dispense in a USP tight, light-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a **Schedule IV (CIV)** controlled substance under the Controlled Substances Act [US].
- **Prescription Status:** Prescription only (Rx only) [US].
