# Editorial Product Information: Clindamycin and Tretinoin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Clindamycin (as phosphate) and Tretinoin.
- **Chemical Class:** Lincosamide antibiotic and Retinoid (Vitamin A derivative).
- **Brand Names:** **Acnatac** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** D10AF51.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Topical aqueous translucent yellow gel containing clindamycin phosphate 1.2% w/w (equivalent to 1% w/w clindamycin) and tretinoin 0.025% w/w [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical (dermatological use only).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acne Vulgaris:** Indicated for the topical treatment of acne vulgaris in patients 12 years of age or older when comedones, papules, and pustules are present [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents $\ge$ 12 years):** Apply a small amount (approximately a pea-sized portion) once daily at night.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Wash the affected area with mild soap and dry thoroughly before application.
    - Dot the gel onto the affected area and rub in gently to cover the entire site (e.g., the whole face).
    - Avoid contact with eyes, eyelids, lips, and nostrils.
    - Wash hands thoroughly after application.
- **Treatment Duration:** Continuous use should not exceed 12 weeks without clinical re-evaluation. Therapeutic effects may take several weeks to become apparent [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Patients should wait until the next scheduled dose and not apply a double amount to compensate for a missed application [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to clindamycin, tretinoin, lincomycin, or any excipients (including hydroxybenzoates) [Australian].
- History of regional enteritis, ulcerative colitis, or antibiotic-associated colitis [Australian].
- Personal or familial history of skin cancer [Australian].
- History of acute eczema, rosacea, or perioral dermatitis [Australian].
- Pustular and deep cystic nodular acne (acne conglobata and acne fulminans) [Australian].
- **Pregnancy** or women planning a pregnancy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Site of Application:** Strictly for dermatological use. Do not apply to oral, ophthalmic, intranasal, or intravaginal mucosa. Avoid abraded or eczematous skin.
- **Colitis Risk:** While systemic absorption is negligible for this topical formulation, antibiotic-associated colitis (CDAD) has been reported with topical clindamycin. Discontinue immediately if significant diarrhea or abdominal cramps occur [Australian].
- **Photosensitivity:** Tretinoin increases susceptibility to UV radiation. Patients must minimise sun exposure, avoid sun lamps/beds, and use SPF 30+ sunscreen and protective clothing. Treatment should be suspended in patients with active sunburn [Australian].
- **Irritation:** Over-application may cause severe redness or stinging. If irritation occurs, reduce frequency or temporarily discontinue use [Australian].
- **Atopic Subjects:** Use with caution in patients with atopy [Australian].
- **Resistance:** Long-term clindamycin use may lead to bacterial resistance or fungal overgrowth. Cross-resistance may occur with erythromycin or lincomycin [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily dermatological. Frequencies below are based on clinical trial data [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nasopharyngitis, headache, dry skin, upper respiratory tract infection, pharyngolaryngeal pain. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Acne flare, erythema, seborrhoea, photosensitivity, pruritus, rash, skin exfoliation, sunburn, application site reactions (burning, dermatitis, dryness). |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypersensitivity, hypothyroidism, eye irritation, gastroenteritis, nausea, dermatitis, skin bleeding, depigmentation, and application site erosion/discolouration. |

- **Paediatric Population:** The incidence of dry skin may be slightly higher in adolescents (12–17 years) compared to adults [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Erythromycin:** Do not use concomitantly; in vitro studies show antagonism between clindamycin and erythromycin [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blockers:** Use with caution; clindamycin possesses neuromuscular blocking properties that may enhance the effects of these agents [Australian].
- **Vitamin K Antagonists (e.g., Warfarin):** Increased coagulation tests (PT/INR) and/or bleeding have been reported. Monitor coagulation parameters frequently if used concurrently [Australian].
- **Topical Keratolytics:** Use with caution alongside sulfur, salicylic acid, benzoyl peroxide, or resorcinol. Allow the effects of peeling agents to subside before starting this therapy [Australian].
- **Drying Agents:** Medicated cleansers, abrasive soaps, and products with high alcohol concentrations should be used with caution due to cumulative drying effects [Australian].
- **Corticosteroids:** Avoid concomitant topical treatment [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Retinoids may cause foetal malformations or irreversible damage. 
    - Contraindicated in pregnancy and in women not using effective contraception [Australian].
    - Contraception must be maintained during treatment and for **one month** after discontinuation [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Use is not recommended. It is unknown if tretinoin or clindamycin are secreted in human milk following topical application, though systemic clindamycin and oral retinoids are known to be excreted in breast milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show no impact on fertility at clinical doses, but systemically administered tretinoin is known to affect fertility severely [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in children under 12 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients over 65 years [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not expected to result in systemic exposure of concern due to low topical absorption. However, use with caution and individualise decisions in severe cases [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Clindamycin:** Binds to the 50S ribosomal subunit of bacteria, inhibiting protein synthesis. Primarily bacteriostatic. Active against *Propionibacterium acnes*.
    - **Tretinoin:** Comedolytic and anti-inflammatory. Decreases follicular epithelial cell cohesiveness and increases turnover, aiding the extrusion of comedones.
    - **Combination:** Tretinoin enhances the penetration of clindamycin into the follicle [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Percutaneous absorption is minimal. Plasma clindamycin levels generally remain below 3.5 ng/mL. Tretinoin levels remain within endogenous ranges [Australian].
    - **Metabolism/Excretion:** Not specified in available sources for the fixed-dose combination.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Three Phase III double-blind studies (N=4,550) compared the combination gel to clindamycin alone, tretinoin alone, and vehicle. 
- **Primary Outcomes:** The combination showed significant superiority in reducing inflammatory, non-inflammatory, and total lesion counts at Week 12 compared to monotherapies and vehicle [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C. 
- Do not refrigerate or freeze. 
- **Shelf Life:** Discard 3 months after the first opening of the tube [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification:** Schedule 4 (S4) – Prescription Only Medicine.
