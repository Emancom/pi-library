# Editorial Product Information: Cetirizine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of cetirizine hydrochloride for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cetirizine hydrochloride (a carboxylic acid derivative and human metabolite of hydroxyzine).
- **Chemical Class:** H1-receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** **ALZENE** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in provided source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 10 mg film-coated tablets (capsule-shaped, with breakline) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd trading as Viatris [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Cetirizine is indicated for adults and children aged 6 years and over for the relief of:
- **Seasonal Allergic Rhinitis (Hayfever):** Management of symptoms including sneezing, rhinorrhoea, post-nasal discharge, nasal pruritus, and ocular symptoms (pruritus, tearing, and redness).
- **Perennial Allergic Rhinitis:** Management of symptoms including sneezing, rhinorrhoea, post-nasal discharge, nasal pruritus, and ocular symptoms.
- **Chronic Idiopathic Urticaria:** Treatment of uncomplicated skin manifestations; significantly reduces the frequency, severity, and duration of hives and associated pruritus [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adults and Children over 12 Years
- **Recommended Initial Dose:** 10 mg once daily.
- **Adjustment:** Dose may be increased to a maximum of 20 mg daily if a sufficient clinical response is not achieved.
- **Timing:** May be taken with or without food; administration time can be adjusted to suit patient needs.

### Children 6 to 12 Years
- **Recommended Dose:** 5 mg (half-tablet) twice daily.
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be divided via the breakline to facilitate 5 mg dosing.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to cetirizine hydrochloride, the parent compound (hydroxyzine), or any excipients (including lactose) [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment, defined as a creatinine clearance (CrCl) of less than 10 mL/min [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Activities Requiring Mental Alertness:** While studies often show no effect on cognitive function or motor performance at standard doses, some individuals may experience drowsiness. Caution is advised when driving or operating dangerous machinery.
- **Epilepsy/Seizure Disorders:** Caution is recommended as CNS stimulation may occur with antihistamines, particularly in paediatric populations.
- **Hepatic Effects:** Reversible elevations of liver transaminases have been observed without accompanying jaundice or hepatitis.
- **Urticaria and Food Allergy:** Patients with chronic urticaria should be advised to investigate potential dietary triggers.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Clinical trial data (pooled US and European studies) indicate that sedation/somnolence is the most common adverse event, occurring at a rate of 14.3% compared to 7.6% for placebo.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache (10.2%), Dry mouth (5.0%), Fatigue (3.4%), Nausea (2.1%), Dizziness (2.0%), Pharyngitis (1.4%), Insomnia (1.2%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Dyspepsia, weight increase (0.4% in controlled trials) |
| **Rare (<0.1%) / Post-Marketing** | Anaphylaxis, cholestasis, hepatitis, glomerulonephritis, haemolytic anaemia, convulsions, orofacial dyskinesia, severe hypotension, thrombocytopenia, aggressive reaction, stillbirth |

*Note: Infrequent events (<1%) reported include tachycardia/palpitations, abdominal pain, micturition disorders (polyuria, retention), and respiratory distress (bronchospasm, dyspnoea) [Australian].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No specific data available in the provided source.
- **General Note:** Clinical studies showed minimal CNS depressant effects at doses up to 20 mg, though concurrent use with other CNS depressants should be approached with caution.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. Animal reproduction studies (mice, rats, rabbits) showed no teratogenicity, though post-natal development was inhibited in mice at high doses (96 mg/kg). Clinical data are insufficient; use only if benefits clearly outweigh potential risks to the mother and foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Approximately 3% of a dose is excreted in milk (animal data). Use in breastfeeding mothers is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in provided source.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children 6 years and older. Younger children exhibit more rapid clearance (half-life ~5 hours).
- **Elderly Use:** Generally well-tolerated in patients ≥65 years. Dosage should be adjusted based on renal function. For moderate renal impairment in the elderly, a starting dose of 5 mg/day is recommended [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance is reduced in proportion to renal dysfunction. In patients with renal insufficiency, the dose should be reduced to half the recommended daily dose (e.g., 5 mg daily). Contraindicated in severe impairment (<10 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically addressed in the source, though transaminase elevations are noted as a rare precaution.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A potent and selective peripheral H1-receptor antagonist. It lacks significant anticholinergic or antiserotoninergic activity in animal models.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Inhibits the "wheal and flare" response to histamine for at least 24 hours following a single dose.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma levels (~329 ng/mL for 10 mg dose) reached in approximately 1 hour. Food slows the rate of absorption (lower Cmax, delayed Tmax) but does not affect the total extent of absorption (AUC).
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is 93%. Apparent volume of distribution is 0.45 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Minimally metabolised in the liver; approximately 60% of the dose is excreted unchanged in the urine.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is approximately 7 hours in adults, increasing to ~20 hours in patients on dialysis.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific pivotal trial summaries provided in the source document.
- **Clinical Observations:** Pooled data from US and European trials confirmed the efficacy of cetirizine 5–20 mg in treating urticaria and allergic rhinitis.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Container Type:** PVC/PVDC/Al blister packs.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
