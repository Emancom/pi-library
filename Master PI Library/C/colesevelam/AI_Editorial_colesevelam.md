# Editorial Product Information: Colesevelam Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized editorial overview of colesevelam hydrochloride, synthesized for healthcare professionals.
*   **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. The information provided is based solely on the source material provided; clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for specific prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Colesevelam hydrochloride.
*   **Chemical Class:** Bile acid sequestrant; non-absorbed, polymeric lipid-lowering and glucose-lowering agent.
*   **Brand Names:** **Welchol** (Reference listed drug); generic versions available [US].
*   **ATC Code:** C10AC04 (Not specified in available source).
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    *   **Film-coated tablets:** 625 mg [US].
    *   **Oral suspension:** Mentioned as recommended for pediatric use, but specific strengths not detailed in the provided tablet PI [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ascend Laboratories, LLC; Repackaged by Bryant Ranch Prepack [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Primary Hyperlipidemia:** As an adjunct to diet and exercise to reduce elevated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) in adults [US].
*   **Heterozygous Familial Hypertriglyceridemia (HeFH):** As an adjunct to diet and exercise to reduce LDL-C in boys and postmenarchal girls, aged 10 to 17 years, who have failed to reach LDL-C targets despite adequate lifestyle modification [US].
*   **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus:** As an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults [US].

**Limitations of Use:**
*   Not for the treatment of type 1 diabetes or diabetic ketoacidosis [US].
*   Not studied in Fredrickson Type I, III, IV, and V dyslipidemias [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
*   **Baseline Testing:** Serum triglyceride (TG) levels must be obtained prior to initiation [US].
*   **Adult Dosing (Hyperlipidemia and Type 2 Diabetes):** 3.75 grams daily [US].
*   **Pediatric Dosing (HeFH, ages 10–17):** 3.75 grams daily [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Tablets:** 6 tablets taken once daily or 3 tablets taken twice daily [US].
*   **Requirement:** Must be taken with a meal and liquid [US].
*   **Note:** For patients with difficulty swallowing, the oral suspension formulation is recommended to avoid risk of esophageal obstruction [US].

### Dosing with Statin Therapy
*   Colesevelam may be administered at the same time as a statin or dosed apart [US].
*   Lipid levels should be monitored within 4 to 6 weeks of starting therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   Serum triglyceride levels > 500 mg/dL [US].
*   History of hypertriglyceridemia-induced pancreatitis [US].
*   History of bowel obstruction [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypertriglyceridemia and Pancreatitis:** Colesevelam can increase serum TG. Hypertriglyceridemia is a known cause of acute pancreatitis. Patients with baseline TG > 300 mg/dL are at higher risk and require frequent monitoring. Therapy should be discontinued if TG exceeds 500 mg/dL or if symptoms of pancreatitis occur [US].
*   **Gastrointestinal Obstruction:** Due to its constipating effects, the drug is not recommended for patients with gastroparesis, GI motility disorders, or major GI surgery. Patients should seek medical attention for severe constipation or abdominal pain [US].
*   **Fat-Soluble Vitamin Deficiencies:** May decrease absorption of vitamins A, D, E, and K. Patients susceptible to vitamin K deficiency (e.g., those on warfarin or with malabsorption) are at increased risk. Supplemental vitamins should be taken at least 4 hours before colesevelam [US].
*   **Drug Interactions:** Due to binding potential, drugs not specifically tested for interaction should be considered for administration 4 hours prior to colesevelam [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience
Adverse reactions occurring at an incidence of ≥2% and more frequently than placebo are listed below.

**Primary Hyperlipidemia (Adults):**
*   **Common (1–10%):** Dyspepsia (8.3%), Nausea (4.2%), Accidental injury (3.7%), Asthenia (3.6%), Pharyngitis (3.2%), Rhinitis (3.2%).
*   **Very Common (>10%):** Constipation (11.0%).

**Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (Adults):**
*   **Common (1–10%):** Constipation (6.5%), Hypoglycemia (3.4%), Dyspepsia (2.8%), Nausea (2.6%), Hypertension (2.6%), Back pain (2.3%).

**Pediatric HeFH (Ages 10–17):**
*   **Common (1–10%):** Nasopharyngitis (6.2%), Headache (3.9%), Fatigue (3.9%), Creatine Phosphokinase increase (2.3%), Rhinitis (2.3%), Vomiting (2.3%).

### Post-marketing Experience
Additional reactions identified include:
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Fecal impaction, pancreatitis, abdominal distension, hemorrhoid exacerbation, dysphagia, and esophageal obstruction.
*   **Hepatobiliary:** Increased transaminases.
*   **Neurological:** Increased seizure activity (in patients taking phenytoin) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
Colesevelam primarily interacts by reducing the absorption of concomitant medications.

### Reduced Exposure of Concomitant Medication
The following should be administered **at least 4 hours prior** to colesevelam:
*   **Narrow Therapeutic Index Drugs:** Cyclosporine (34% reduction in AUC).
*   **Antiepileptics:** Phenytoin (reports of reduced levels/seizures).
*   **Thyroid Replacement:** Levothyroxine (22% reduction in AUC).
*   **Oral Contraceptives:** Ethinyl estradiol/Norethindrone (may reduce efficacy).
*   **Antidiabetics:** Sulfonylureas (Glimepiride, Glipizide, Glyburide).
*   **Antihypertensives:** Olmesartan medoxomil (39% reduction in AUC).
*   **Anticoagulants:** Warfarin (reports of reduced INR; monitor INR frequently upon initiation and periodically).
*   **Vitamins:** Oral fat-soluble vitamin supplements (A, D, E, K).

### Increased Exposure of Concomitant Medication
*   **Metformin Extended-Release (ER):** AUC increased by 44% when co-administered. Monitor glycemic control [US].

### No Significant Interaction
Bioavailability of the following was not significantly affected: Aspirin, atenolol, digoxin, enalapril, fenofibrate, lovastatin, metoprolol, pioglitazone, quinidine, rosiglitazone, sitagliptin, valproic acid, and warfarin (in specific controlled studies) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Colesevelam is not absorbed systemically; therefore, maternal use is not expected to result in fetal exposure. However, it may reduce the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins, which are critical in pregnancy. Animal studies at doses up to 50 times the MRHD showed no evidence of harm. No adequate human studies are available [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not systemically absorbed by the mother; breastfeeding is not expected to result in infant exposure to the drug [US].
*   **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in rat studies at doses up to 50 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Pediatric Use:**
    *   **HeFH:** Safety and efficacy established for ages 10 to 17 years. Not recommended for children < 10 years or premenarchal girls [US].
    *   **Type 2 Diabetes:** Safety and efficacy **not established**; a 6-month study in patients aged 10–17 failed to demonstrate effectiveness [US].
*   **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between subjects ≥65 years and younger subjects, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** In Type 2 Diabetes trials, no significant differences in safety or efficacy were observed in patients with mild (CrCl 50–80 mL/min) or moderate (CrCl 30–50 mL/min) impairment. Data for severe impairment is unavailable [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
*   **Hyperlipidemia:** Colesevelam is a non-absorbed, lipid-lowering polymer that binds bile acids in the intestine, preventing their reabsorption. This depletes the bile acid pool, signaling the liver to upregulate cholesterol 7-α-hydroxylase to convert more cholesterol into bile acids. This results in increased LDL receptor activity and increased clearance of LDL-C from the blood [US].
*   **Type 2 Diabetes:** The exact mechanism for improving glycemic control is unknown [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Not absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract [US].
*   **Distribution:** Limited to the gastrointestinal tract [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Not metabolized systemically; does not interfere with Cytochrome P450 enzymes [US].
*   **Elimination:** Primarily fecal; <0.05% of a radiolabeled dose was excreted in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Monotherapy (Hyperlipidemia):** In a 24-week trial, colesevelam (3.8g–4.5g daily) reduced LDL-C by 15% to 18% [US].
*   **Combination Therapy (Hyperlipidemia):** Adding colesevelam (2.3g–3.8g) to stable statin therapy resulted in an additional 8% to 16% reduction in LDL-C [US].
*   **Type 2 Diabetes:** In a monotherapy trial, colesevelam reduced HbA1c by a placebo-corrected 0.27%. In add-on trials (with metformin, sulfonylureas, or insulin), the placebo-corrected reduction in HbA1c was approximately 0.5% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F) [US].
*   **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
*   **Protection:** Protect from moisture [US].
