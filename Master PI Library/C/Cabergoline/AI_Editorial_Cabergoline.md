# Editorial Product Information: Cabergoline

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference. 
- **Prescribing Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory requirements for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the current approved PI in their specific region for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cabergoline (a dopaminergic ergoline derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Cabaser** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 1 mg oral tablet: White, oval, concave, scored, engraved '7' and '01' [Australian].
    - 2 mg oral tablet: White, oval, concave, scored, engraved '7' and '02' [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Parkinson’s Disease:** Management of the signs and symptoms of Parkinson’s Disease [Australian].
- **Note:** Treatment must be initiated under specialist supervision [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Administration:** Administer as a single daily dose, preferably with food to improve tolerability. Food does not significantly affect absorption [Australian].
- **Initial Titration:** 
    - **Monotherapy:** 0.5 mg daily.
    - **Adjunctive (with Levodopa):** 1.0 mg daily.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Increments of 0.5 mg to 1.0 mg should be made at weekly or bi-weekly intervals until the optimal balance of efficacy and side effects is reached. In patients on levodopa, the levodopa dose may be gradually decreased as cabergoline is increased [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** The typical therapeutic range is 2 mg to 3 mg per day [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** Safety and efficacy of doses higher than the recommended therapeutic range have not been established [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to cabergoline, other ergot alkaloids, or any excipients.
- History of pulmonary, pericardial, or retroperitoneal fibrotic disorders.
- Anatomical evidence of cardiac valvulopathy (e.g., echocardiographic evidence of valve leaflet thickening, restriction, or stenosis) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiac Valvulopathy and Fibrosis:** Cabergoline is associated with fibrotic reactions and cardiac valvulopathy involving one or more valves (aortic, mitral, and tricuspid). These effects are associated with cumulative doses. Treatment must be discontinued if new or worsened valvular pathology is detected.
- **Somnolence and Sudden Sleep Onset:** Associated with sudden sleep episodes during daily activities, sometimes without warning. Caution is required when driving or operating machinery. Dosage reduction or termination may be necessary if these occur [Australian].
- **Postural Hypotension:** May occur, particularly during the first few days of therapy. Monitor patients also taking blood pressure-lowering medications [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Effects:** A history of psychiatric illness (especially psychosis) increases the risk of depressive adverse events.
- **Impulse Control Disorders:** Pathological gambling, increased libido, hypersexuality, compulsive spending, and punding have been reported. These are generally reversible upon dose reduction [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **Baseline:** Cardiovascular evaluation (including echocardiogram), ESR, lung function/chest X-ray, and renal function.
    - **During Treatment:** Regular monitoring for progressive fibrosis. Echocardiogram should occur within 3–6 months of initiation, then every 6–12 months based on clinical assessment [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Dopamine Antagonists:** Concomitant use with phenothiazines, butyrophenones, thioxanthines, or metoclopramide is not recommended as they may reduce cabergoline’s therapeutic effect [Australian].
- **Macrolide Antibiotics:** (e.g., erythromycin) May increase systemic bioavailability and risk of adverse effects [Australian].
- **Ergot Alkaloids:** Concomitant use during long-term treatment is not recommended [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Potential for additive symptomatic hypotension [Australian].
- **Levodopa/Selegiline:** No pharmacokinetic interactions observed [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Cabergoline crosses the placenta in rats. Animal studies show no teratogenic effects at non-maternally toxic doses. 
    - **Recommendation:** Exclude pregnancy before initiation. Discontinue one month before intended conception due to the drug's long half-life. If conception occurs during therapy, discontinue immediately upon confirmation [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Cabergoline inhibits/suppresses lactation due to its dopamine-agonist properties. Puerperal women should be advised not to breastfeed [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Inhibited in female rats; no effect on male rat fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been investigated in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** CNS adverse events are reported more frequently in the elderly. No specific dose adjustment is provided, but titration should be cautious [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Kinetics are not modified by renal insufficiency [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh score >10):** Associated with increased AUC. Dose regimens should be modified in these patients [Australian].
    - **Mild/Moderate:** Pharmacokinetics do not appear significantly affected [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A potent, long-lasting dopamine D2 receptor agonist. It acts directly on pituitary lactotrophic cells to decrease prolactin and stimulates D2 receptors in the striatum to improve motor deficits in Parkinson's disease [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; Tmax 0.5–4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 41–42% plasma protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised; main metabolite is 6-allyl-8b-carboxy-ergoline.
    - **Elimination:** Long elimination half-life (63–68 hours in healthy volunteers; 79–115 hours in hyperprolactinemic patients). Excreted primarily in faeces (72%) and urine (20%) [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Prolactin-lowering effect is dose-related and persists for 7–28 days after a single dose [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adjunctive Therapy:** A Phase III trial (n=188) demonstrated a 15% improvement in Unified Rating Scale for Parkinson’s Disease (UPDRS) and a 18% reduction in levodopa requirements compared to placebo.
- **Monotherapy:** A Phase III trial in newly diagnosed patients (n=419) showed that cabergoline significantly delayed the onset of motor complications compared to levodopa. The relative risk of motor complications was 54% lower in the cabergoline group [Australian].
