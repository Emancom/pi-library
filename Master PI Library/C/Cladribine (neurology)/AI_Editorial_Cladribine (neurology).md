# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided for the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Note on Jurisdictional Scope:** While the user instructions referenced multiple potential sources, only the United States PI (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) was provided for analysis. Therefore, this document reflects United States regulatory positions and clinical data.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinical synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring amino acid derivative; a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [United States].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). 
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [United States].

### Monitoring and Adjustments
- **Plasma Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels before starting therapy. Subsequent weekly and monthly monitoring is advised. 
- **Target Levels:** Plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [United States].
- **ESRD Adjustments:** Dose adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [United States].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no listed contraindications in the available source document [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [United States].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical treatment initiated [United States].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects listed below are based on clinical trials in patients with chronic hemodialysis.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), Pain (21–35%), Flu syndrome (15–29%), Chest pain (6–15%), Headache (3–37%), Infection (10–24%), Abdominal pain (5–21%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%), Rhinitis (6–11%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%).

### Common (1–10%)
- **General:** Asthenia, Fever, Back pain, Accidental injury.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, Palpitations, Arrhythmia, Vascular disorder.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Hypervolemia, Weight increase/decrease, Peripheral edema.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Anorexia, Constipation, Dyspepsia, Melena.
- **Other:** Anemia, Insomnia, Paresthesia, Pruritus, Rash, Eye disorder, Taste perversion, Urinary tract infection [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. 
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored closely after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not specifically lettered, but descriptive]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy for the treatment of metabolic disorders and ESRD carnitine deficiency appear consistent with adult populations; dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg or 10–20 mg/kg) [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source; clinical trial data primarily focused on general ESRD and metabolic populations.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For ESRD patients, the **intravenous** route is indicated to avoid metabolite accumulation [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities caused by the accumulation of toxic organic acids (acylCoA compounds) [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed that mean pre-dialysis levocarnitine concentrations rose from 20 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-dependent increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [United States].
