# Editorial Product Information: Cefixime

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of cefixime for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data into a single reference point.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefixime (as trihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Semisynthetic cephalosporin antibacterial.
- **Brand Names:** Cefixime Tablets (Rising Pharma Holdings, Inc.) [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01DD08 (Note: Not explicitly stated in the source but standard for this class).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Film-coated, scored tablets:** 400 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Rising Pharma Holdings, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Cefixime is indicated for the treatment of adults and pediatric patients weighing more than 45 kg or older than 12 years for the following infections when caused by susceptible isolates:
- **Uncomplicated Urinary Tract Infections:** Caused by *Escherichia coli* and *Proteus mirabilis* [US].
- **Pharyngitis and Tonsillitis:** Caused by *Streptococcus pyogenes* [US]. 
    - *Editorial Note:* While effective for eradication from the nasopharynx, data on the prevention of rheumatic fever are unavailable [US].
- **Acute Exacerbations of Chronic Bronchitis:** Caused by *Streptococcus pneumoniae* and *Haemophilus influenzae* [US].
- **Uncomplicated Gonorrhea (cervical/urethral):** Caused by *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* (including penicillinase-producing isolates) [US].

**Antimicrobial Stewardship:** Therapy should be reserved for infections proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible bacteria to maintain efficacy and prevent resistance [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Pediatrics >45 kg or >12 years)
- **Standard Dose:** 400 mg daily [US].
- **Dosing Regimen:** May be administered as a single 400 mg tablet once daily or split into 200 mg (one-half tablet) every 12 hours [US].
- **Uncomplicated Gonorrhea:** A single oral dose of 400 mg is recommended [US].
- **Treatment Duration:** For *S. pyogenes* infections, therapy must continue for at least 10 days [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be administered without regard to food [US].
- **Bioequivalence Note:** Tablets are not bioequivalent to the oral suspension; the suspension results in higher peak blood levels. Tablets should not be substituted for suspension in the treatment of otitis media [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **cefixime** or other cephalosporins [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, including fatalities, have been reported. Before initiation, verify history of hypersensitivity to cephalosporins, penicillins, or other drugs. Use with caution in penicillin-sensitive patients due to documented cross-reactivity (up to 10%). Discontinue immediately if an allergic reaction occurs [US].
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents, ranging from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis. Monitor for diarrhea occurring up to two months post-therapy [US].
- **Coagulation Effects:** Cephalosporins may cause a decrease in prothrombin activity. Patients at higher risk include those with renal or hepatic impairment, poor nutritional status, prolonged antimicrobial therapy, or those on anticoagulant therapy. Monitor prothrombin time and administer exogenous Vitamin K if indicated [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Particularly in patients with renal impairment where dosage was not reduced. Discontinue therapy if seizures occur [US].
- **Development of Drug-Resistant Bacteria:** Use in the absence of a proven/suspected bacterial infection is unlikely to provide benefit and increases resistance risk [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trials Experience (Tablet Formulation)
The most common adverse reactions reported in U.S. trials were gastrointestinal (30% of adult patients). 

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Diarrhea | 16% |
| Nausea | 7% |
| Loose/Frequent Stools | 6% |
| Abdominal Pain | 3% |
| Dyspepsia | 3% |
| Flatulence | 4% |

### Post-marketing and Other Adverse Effects (Incidence <2%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Pseudomembranous colitis [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Skin rashes, urticaria, drug fever, pruritus, angioedema, facial edema, Erythema multiforme, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, and serum sickness-like reactions [US].
- **Hepatic:** Transient elevations in SGPT (ALT), SGOT (AST), alkaline phosphatase; hepatitis, jaundice [US].
- **Renal:** Transient elevations in BUN or creatinine; acute renal failure [US].
- **Central Nervous System:** Headaches, dizziness, seizures [US].
- **Hemic and Lymphatic:** Transient thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, neutropenia, prolonged prothrombin time, elevated LDH, pancytopenia, agranulocytosis, and eosinophilia [US].
- **Other:** Genital pruritus, vaginitis, candidiasis, toxic epidermal necrolysis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Carbamazepine:** Elevated carbamazepine levels have been reported when co-administered. Plasma concentration monitoring is recommended [US].
- **Warfarin and Anticoagulants:** Increased prothrombin time (with or without bleeding) has been reported [US].
- **Drug/Laboratory Test Interactions:**
    - **Urine Ketones:** False-positive reactions may occur with nitroprusside tests [US].
    - **Urine Glucose:** False-positive reactions may occur with Clinitest®, Benedict's, or Fehling's solutions. Use enzymatic glucose oxidase tests (e.g., Clinistix®) instead [US].
    - **Coombs Test:** A false-positive direct Coombs test may occur [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Available human data from observational studies/case reports have not established a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. Animal reproduction studies (mice/rats) at doses up to 40–80 times the human dose showed no evidence of fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on cefixime in human milk. Cefixime is present in animal milk (steady-state concentrations in dams 70 times greater than nursing pups). Consider the developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother’s clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** In rats, no effect on mating behavior or fertility was detected at doses up to 1000 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in infants under 6 months have not been established. Patients >12 years or >45 kg should receive adult dosing [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy between elderly and younger patients were observed. While AUC is 40% higher in the elderly, dose adjustment is not required based on age alone [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustment is required for patients with CrCl <60 mL/min [US].

### Dosage in Renal Failure (Adults) [US]
| Creatinine Clearance (mL/min) | 400 mg Tablet Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| ≥ 60 | Normal dose |
| 21 to 59 (or hemodialysis) | 400 mg tablet is "Not Appropriate" |
| ≤ 20 (or continuous peritoneal dialysis) | 0.5 tablet (200 mg) daily |

*Note: Neither hemodialysis nor peritoneal dialysis removes significant amounts of cefixime from the body [US].*

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis. Stable in the presence of many beta-lactamase enzymes [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 40% to 50% absorbed orally. Tmax is increased by ~0.8 hours when taken with food [US].
    - **Distribution:** Protein binding is ~65% (concentration-independent). Adequate CSF levels are not achieved [US].
    - **Metabolism:** No evidence of *in vivo* metabolism [US].
    - **Elimination:** ~50% excreted unchanged in urine within 24 hours. Serum half-life averages 3 to 4 hours (up to 9 hours in some patients) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Specific pivotal trial summaries for the tablet formulation were not detailed in the source beyond the establishment of the gastrointestinal adverse effect profile and comparison studies for otitis media (which favoured suspension over the tablet due to peak blood levels) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** Dispense in a bottle with child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
