# Editorial Product Information: Cycloserine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction:
    - **US (FDA)**: Cycloserine Capsules USP (Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories, Inc. / Cerovene, Inc.)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cycloserine (D-Cycloserine; (R)-4-amino-3-isoxazolidinone).
- **Chemical Class:** Broad-spectrum antibiotic (produced by *Streptomyces orchidaceus*).
- **Brand Names:** Available as generic **Cycloserine** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J04AB01 (Not explicitly specified in source, provided for reference).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 250 mg capsules [US]. 
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Distributed by Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Tuberculosis (TB):** Treatment of active pulmonary and extrapulmonary tuberculosis (including renal disease). It is indicated only when the causative organisms are susceptible and primary medications (isoniazid, rifampin, ethambutol, streptomycin) have proved inadequate or are not tolerated. 
    - *Note:* Must be used in conjunction with other effective antituberculosis agents, not as monotherapy.
- **Urinary Tract Infections (UTI):** Treatment of acute UTI caused by susceptible strains of gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria. 
    - *Note:* Should be considered only when conventional therapy has failed and susceptibility is demonstrated.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Usual Dosage:** 500 mg to 1 g daily, administered in divided doses. 
- **Initial Titration:** The standard starting dose is 250 mg twice daily (at 12-hour intervals) for the first two weeks.
- **Maximum Dose:** Do not exceed 1 g daily.
### Administration Instructions
- Dosage should be monitored and adjusted based on blood levels to maintain therapeutic, non-toxic concentrations.
### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
Administration is contraindicated in patients with:
- Hypersensitivity to **Cycloserine**.
- Epilepsy.
- Depression, severe anxiety, or psychosis.
- Severe renal insufficiency.
- Excessive concurrent use of alcohol.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None listed in provided sources.
### General Warnings and Precautions
- **CNS Toxicity:** Discontinue or reduce dosage if the patient develops allergic dermatitis or symptoms of central nervous system (CNS) toxicity (e.g., convulsions, psychosis, somnolence, confusion, hyperreflexia, headache, tremor, vertigo, paresis, or dysarthria).
- **Narrow Therapeutic Index:** The ratio of toxic dose to effective dose is small. Toxicity is closely related to blood levels above 30 mcg/mL.
- **Chronic Alcoholism:** Risk of convulsions is significantly increased in chronic alcoholics.
- **Anemia:** Use has been associated with Vitamin B12 and/or folic acid deficiency, megaloblastic anemia, and sideroblastic anemia. If anemia develops, appropriate therapy should be initiated.
### Monitoring Requirements
- **Blood Levels:** Monitor levels at least weekly for patients with reduced renal function, those receiving >500 mg/day, or those showing signs of toxicity. Maintain levels below 30 mcg/mL.
- **Organ Function:** Monitor hematologic, renal, liver function, and blood levels throughout therapy.
- **Susceptibility Testing:** Establish organism susceptibility to **Cycloserine** and other agents in the regimen before and during treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most reactions are dose-related (occurring more frequently at >500 mg daily) or relate to hypersensitivity.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous System** | Convulsions, drowsiness, somnolence, headache, tremor, dysarthria, vertigo, confusion, memory loss, psychosis (potentially with suicidal tendencies), character changes, aggression, hyperirritability, paresis, hyperreflexia, paresthesia, major/minor clonic seizures, coma. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Sudden congestive heart failure (reported at doses of 1–1.5 g daily). |
| **Skin/Allergic** | Skin rash (apparently not dose-related), allergic dermatitis. |
| **Hepatic/Misc.** | Elevated serum transaminase (especially in patients with pre-existing liver disease). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Ethionamide:** Concurrent administration may potentiate neurotoxic side effects.
- **Isoniazid:** May result in an increased incidence of CNS effects, such as dizziness or drowsiness; monitor for toxicity and adjust dosage if necessary.
- **Alcohol:** Incompatible with **Cycloserine**, particularly at higher doses; increases the risk of epileptic episodes.
- **Pyridoxine:** While many neurotoxic effects of overdosage can be treated with 200–300 mg of pyridoxine daily, its value in *preventing* toxicity from standard doses has not been proven.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C:** Animal studies (rats) showed no teratogenicity at doses up to 100 mg/kg/day, but there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** **Cycloserine** is excreted in breast milk (approaching serum concentrations). Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** A two-generation rat study showed no impairment in the first mating, but somewhat lower fertility in the second mating.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** Dose selection should be cautious, generally starting at the low end of the range. Monitor renal function closely as the drug is substantially excreted by the kidneys.
- **Renal Impairment:** Risk of toxic reactions is higher. Weekly blood level monitoring is mandatory for patients with reduced renal function. **Cycloserine** is contraindicated in severe renal insufficiency.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; elevated transaminases may occur, particularly in those with pre-existing disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits bacterial cell-wall synthesis in susceptible gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria by competing with D-alanine.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract.
    - **Distribution:** Peak blood levels occur in 4 to 8 hours. Widely distributed; concentrations in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), pleural fluid, fetal blood, and breast milk approach serum levels.
    - **Metabolism:** Approximately 35% is metabolized to unknown substances.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 65% is excreted unchanged in the urine within 72 hours. Half of the drug is eliminated in 12 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal trial data is not detailed in the available product information; approval is based on established historical use and susceptibility data for *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at controlled room temperature between 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Packaging:** Typically supplied in 250 mg capsules in blister cards [US].
