# Editorial Product Information: Cefdinir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefdinir (an extended-spectrum, semi-synthetic, third-generation cephalosporin).
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Generic: Cefdinir Capsules, USP) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral Capsules: 300 mg [US].
    - Oral Suspension: (Referenced in pharmacokinetics but specific strength for this product listing is 300 mg capsules) [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Northwind Health Company, LLC (Distributed by Ascend Laboratories, LLC) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Cefdinir is indicated for the treatment of mild to moderate infections caused by susceptible strains of designated microorganisms [US]:

**Adults and Adolescents (≥13 years):**
- Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP)
- Acute Exacerbations of Chronic Bronchitis (AECB)
- Acute Maxillary Sinusitis
- Pharyngitis and Tonsillitis
- Uncomplicated Skin and Skin Structure Infections (USSSI)

**Pediatric Patients (6 months through 12 years):**
- Acute Bacterial Otitis Media
- Pharyngitis and Tonsillitis
- Uncomplicated Skin and Skin Structure Infections (USSSI)

*Editorial Note: Cefdinir is effective in eradicating S. pyogenes from the oropharynx, but has not been studied for the prevention of rheumatic fever; only intramuscular penicillin is demonstrated for this purpose [US].*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents ≥13 years)
The total daily dose for all infections is 600 mg. Dosing can be administered as 600 mg once daily (q24h) or 300 mg twice daily (q12h) [US].
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia:** 300 mg q12h for 10 days.
- **AECB:** 300 mg q12h for 5–10 days OR 600 mg q24h for 10 days.
- **Acute Maxillary Sinusitis:** 300 mg q12h OR 600 mg q24h for 10 days.
- **Pharyngitis/Tonsillitis:** 300 mg q12h for 5–10 days OR 600 mg q24h for 10 days.
- **Uncomplicated SSSI:** 300 mg q12h for 10 days.

### Administration Instructions
- May be administered without regard to food [US].
- **Timing with Interacting Substances:** Cefdinir should be taken at least 2 hours before or after the administration of antacids (aluminum or magnesium-containing) or iron supplements (including multivitamins containing iron) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Cephalosporin Hypersensitivity:** Cefdinir is contraindicated in patients with a known allergy to the cephalosporin class of antibiotics [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Before initiation, check for previous reactions to cefdinir, cephalosporins, or penicillins. Cross-hypersensitivity among $\beta$-lactam antibiotics occurs in up to 10% of penicillin-allergic patients. Serious reactions may require emergency intervention [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-associated diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterials, ranging from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis. Discontinue if CDAD is suspected [US].
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms [US].
- **History of Colitis:** Prescribe with caution in patients with a history of gastrointestinal disease [US].
- **Seizure Potential:** Like other cephalosporins, seizures may be triggered, particularly in patients with renal impairment where dosing was not appropriately reduced [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse events observed in clinical trials were mild and self-limiting [US].

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhea (15%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vaginal moniliasis (4% of women), Nausea (3%), Headache (2%), Abdominal pain (1%), Vaginitis (1% of women) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Rash (0.9%), Dyspepsia (0.7%), Flatulence (0.7%), Vomiting (0.7%), Dizziness (0.3%), Insomnia (0.2%), Pruritus (0.2%) |

### Serious/Postmarketing Effects
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, exfoliative dermatitis, facial/laryngeal edema [US].
- **Hepatic:** Acute hepatitis, cholestasis, fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure, jaundice [US].
- **Hematologic:** Pancytopenia, granulocytopenia, hemolytic anemia, idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura [US].
- **Other:** Acute renal failure, rhabdomyolysis, and a "reddish stool" phenomenon due to a non-absorbable complex formed with iron in the GI tract [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antacids (Al or Mg-containing):** Reduces Cmax and AUC of cefdinir by ~40%. Separate dosing by at least 2 hours [US].
- **Iron Supplements/Iron-Fortified Foods:** Reduces cefdinir absorption by up to 80% (therapeutic iron) or 31% (multivitamins with 10mg iron). Separate dosing by at least 2 hours [US].
- **Probenecid:** Inhibits renal excretion, resulting in an approximate doubling of AUC and a 50% increase in elimination half-life [US].
- **Laboratory Tests:**
    - False-positive for ketones in urine (using nitroprusside).
    - False-positive for glucose in urine (using Clinitest®, Benedict’s, or Fehling’s solution; enzymatic glucose oxidase tests are recommended).
    - Positive direct Coombs’ test [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category B]. No evidence of teratogenicity in animal studies at 70x human doses. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women; use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Following a single 600 mg dose, cefdinir was not detected in human breast milk [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility or reproductive performance was observed in rats at doses up to 1000 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in neonates and infants <6 months have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Dose adjustment is not necessary based on age alone, provided renal function is adequate. Geriatric patients may experience lower rates of diarrhea compared to younger adults [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Adults (CrCl <30 mL/min):** Reduce dose to 300 mg once daily [US].
    - **Pediatrics (CrCl <30 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$):** 7 mg/kg (up to 300 mg) once daily [US].
    - **Hemodialysis:** Cefdinir is removed by dialysis (63%). Recommended dose is 300 mg (or 7 mg/kg) at the end of each session, then every other day thereafter [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied; however, as cefdinir is predominantly renally eliminated, dose adjustment is not expected to be required [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal activity via inhibition of bacterial cell wall synthesis. Cefdinir is stable in the presence of several $\beta$-lactamase enzymes [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is 2–4 hours. Absolute bioavailability is ~21% for capsules and 25% for suspension [US].
    - **Distribution:** Vd is 0.35 L/kg in adults; 60–70% protein bound. Penetrates skin blister fluid, tonsil tissue, sinus tissue, and lung tissue. CSF penetration data are unavailable [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Not appreciably metabolized; activity is due to the parent drug [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal excretion. Mean plasma half-life is 1.7 (±0.6) hours in healthy adults [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia:** A US study comparing cefdinir (300 mg BID) to cefaclor (500 mg TID) showed equivalent clinical cure rates (80% vs 79%). A European study comparing cefdinir to amoxicillin/clavulanate showed cefdinir clinical cure rates of 80% vs 89% for the comparator [US].
- **Pharyngitis/Tonsillitis:** In US studies, 10 days of cefdinir (once or twice daily) was found superior to 10 days of penicillin qid for S. pyogenes eradication (91-94% vs 70-83%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from light and moisture [US].
