# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- This document serves as an editorial reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing and dispensing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion. 
- **Acute Management (Metabolic Crisis):** A loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided and administered every 3 or 4 hours (and no less frequently than every 6 hours). Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirements [United States].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administration should occur as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [United States].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Levocarnitine injection is compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL. Mixed solutions are stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when stored in PVC bags [United States].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitoring should be weekly or monthly and include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [United States].
- **ESRD:** Initiation may be prompted by low trough (pre-dialysis) carnitine levels (<40–50 µmol/L). Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical treatment initiated [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in the frequency or severity of events has been observed [United States].
- **Oral Formulation Restriction in Renal Impairment:** Note that the safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported in clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis (incidence ≥5% regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Events (≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, dyspepsia, anorexia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash |

**Other notable effects:**
- **GI Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis.
- **Body Odor:** A "fishy" body odor has been reported [United States].
- **Serious Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation or after dose adjustments of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned letter category in provided source.
  - Animal reproductive studies (rats and rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after administration, the risks to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal treatment [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations (50 mg/kg in metabolic disorders) apply to paediatric patients. Secondary deficiency in newborns and premature infants is defined by plasma levels below age-related norms [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided in the source; dosing should generally follow weight-based protocols as per other populations.
- **Renal Impairment:** This product is specifically indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis. Dose adjustments are guided by pre-dialysis trough carnitine concentrations [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy (ATP) production. In patients with organic acidemias, it promotes the excretion of toxic acyl-CoA esters as acylcarnitine [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces).
  - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. In dialysis patients, levocarnitine is easily removed by the procedure [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical studies demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels typically stabilised or increased over 8 to 24 weeks. While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is established, the long-term impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation does not contain a preservative [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx only).
