### Editorial Note
This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by synthesizing regulatory data for products containing cyanocobalamin from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). This document merges clinical data from both injectable B-complex formulations [TGA] and oral multivitamin/mineral supplements [FDA]. 

Healthcare professionals should note that while cyanocobalamin is the primary focus, the source documents describe it within the context of combination therapies. Where instructions or risks differ based on the route of administration or regional regulatory stance, the source agency is cited in brackets.

# Product Information: Cyanocobalamin

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Active Ingredient:** Cyanocobalamin (Vitamin B12)

This PI covers cyanocobalamin in both parenteral (injection) and oral (chewable tablet) delivery systems. Depending on the specific formulation, the medication may be administered as a high-potency B-complex injection [TGA] or as a comprehensive oral nutritional supplement containing vitamins, minerals, and folic acid [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
Cyanocobalamin is indicated for the treatment and prevention of Vitamin B12 deficiency states. Specific clinical applications include:

*   **Pernicious Anemia:** Treatment of anemia resulting from a lack of intrinsic factor or inadequate B12 absorption [TGA].
*   **Rapid Saturation:** For patients requiring immediate replenishment of B-group vitamins [TGA].
*   **Malabsorption and Dietary Deficiency:** Management of deficiencies secondary to alcoholism, gastrectomy, gastrointestinal pathologies, or inadequate diet, particularly in elderly or debilitated patients [TGA, FDA].
*   **Neurological Support:** Treatment of peripheral neuritis associated with B-vitamin deficiencies [TGA].
*   **Prophylaxis:** To ensure nutritional requirements are met and to prevent the development of deficiencies in at-risk populations [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to cyanocobalamin, cobalt, or any other excipients in the formulation [TGA, FDA].
*   **Folate Deficiency:** Cyanocobalamin must not be used to treat megaloblastic anemia that is primarily caused by a folate deficiency [TGA].
*   **Secondary Ingredients:** In products containing dexpanthenol, use is contraindicated in patients with hemophilia or mechanical gastrointestinal obstruction (ileus) [TGA].
*   **Hypervitaminosis:** Pre-existing B-vitamin toxicity [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Anaphylaxis:** Parenteral administration carries a rare risk of severe allergic reactions and anaphylactic shock. Clinical monitoring is essential during and immediately following injection. If anaphylaxis occurs, administration must cease immediately, and emergency treatment (e.g., adrenalin, corticosteroids, antihistamines) should be initiated [TGA].
*   **Masking of Diagnosis:** Folic acid doses exceeding 0.1 mg daily may produce a hematologic response in patients with B12 deficiency while allowing irreversible neurological damage to progress. Indiscriminate use of cyanocobalamin may obscure the precise diagnosis of the underlying anemia [TGA, FDA].
*   **Iron Toxicity:** In oral combination products containing iron, accidental overdose is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under six years of age. Keep such products strictly out of reach of children [FDA].
*   **Sensitivity Testing:** In patients with a known history of allergies, an intradermal test dose may be considered before starting parenteral therapy [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Cyanocobalamin should be administered under the guidance of a licensed medical practitioner.

*   **Parenteral (Injection):** Administer via slow deep intramuscular (intragluteal) injection. A typical regimen involves weekly injections for 2 to 6 weeks, or daily in severe cases until acute symptoms resolve. For patient comfort, the solution should be warmed to body temperature prior to use. It is intended for single use only; any residual solution must be discarded [TGA].
*   **Oral (Chewable Tablet):** The standard adult dose is one tablet taken orally once or twice daily, or as directed by a physician [FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Allergic Reactions:** Rare instances of hypersensitivity, including skin rashes and, in extreme cases, anaphylaxis [TGA, FDA].
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Oral formulations containing various B-vitamins and minerals may cause heartburn, nausea, or abdominal pain [TGA, FDA].
*   **Systemic Effects:** High-dose nicotinamide (if present in B-complex) may cause headaches, fatigue, or difficulty focusing [TGA].
*   **Diagnostic Interference:** Riboflavin components may cause a bright yellow discoloration of the urine, which is harmless but may interfere with certain laboratory tests [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Levodopa:** Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6), often co-administered with B12, can reduce the efficacy of levodopa. This interaction is mitigated if a dopa-decarboxylase inhibitor is used concurrently [TGA].
*   **General Medications:** Patients should be advised to consult their physician before combining cyanocobalamin supplements with other prescription medications [FDA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** While B-vitamins are essential, high-potency supplements should be used with caution. In Australia, the injection is exempt from standard pregnancy classification [TGA]. In the US, practitioners are advised to exercise caution and oversee use in pregnant patients [FDA].
*   **Lactation:** Data regarding high-dose parenteral B12 in breastfeeding are limited; oral supplementation is generally considered safe under medical supervision [TGA, FDA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
Upon injection, the fraction of cyanocobalamin retained in the blood and tissues is converted into the active coenzyme forms: **mecobalamin** (co-methylcobalamin) and **adenosylcobalamin**. 
*   **Distribution:** B12 is stored primarily in the liver.
*   **Conversion:** Mecobalamin is essential for neurological function and folate recycling, while adenosylcobalamin is vital for energy production within the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle. 
*   **Excretion:** B12 is excreted via the kidneys and bile; however, hydroxocobalamin is generally retained better than cyanocobalamin [TGA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Cyanocobalamin serves as a precursor to essential coenzymes required for:
1.  **DNA Synthesis and Hematopoiesis:** Acting as a cofactor for methionine synthase, it facilitates the recycling of folate, which is necessary for DNA synthesis and the prevention of megaloblastic anemia.
2.  **Neurological Integrity:** It is required for the maintenance of myelin sheaths and normal nerve function.
3.  **Metabolism:** It participates in the conversion of methylmalonyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA, allowing fats and proteins to enter the TCA cycle for cellular energy production [TGA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Parenteral:** Store below 25°C and protect from light to prevent degradation of the vitamins [TGA].
*   **Oral:** Store at controlled room temperature (20°C to 25°C), with brief excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C. Protect from excessive heat and moisture, as contact with water may cause surface discoloration of tablets [FDA].
