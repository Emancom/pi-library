# Editorial Product Information: Cefepime

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized, clinically focused editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Prescriber Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in your specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefepime (as hydrochloride monohydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Semi-synthetic, broad-spectrum cephalosporin antibiotic for parenteral administration.
- **Brand Name(s):** Cefepime-AFT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Powder for injection in vials containing 500 mg, 1 g, and 2 g [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Intramuscular (IM).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Adults [Australian]:**
- Lower respiratory tract infections (LRTIs), including pneumonia and bronchitis.
- Urinary tract infections (UTIs), including complicated (pyelonephritis) and uncomplicated infections.
- Skin and skin structure infections.
- Intra-abdominal infections, including peritonitis and biliary tract infections.
- Gynaecological infections.
- Septicaemia.
- Empiric treatment of febrile neutropenic patients.
- Surgical prophylaxis in intra-abdominal surgery (must be administered in combination with metronidazole).

**Paediatrics (Over 2 months of age) [Australian]:**
- Pneumonia.
- Urinary tract infections (complicated and uncomplicated).
- Skin and skin structure infections.
- Septicaemia.
- Empiric treatment of febrile neutropenic patients.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children >12 years or >40 kg)
The standard adult dosage is 1 g IV or IM every 12 hours. Duration of therapy is typically 7–10 days, though severe infections may require longer treatment [Australian].

| Severity of Infection | Dose and Route | Interval |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mild to moderate UTI | 500 mg – 1 g IV/IM | q12h |
| Mild to moderate (non-UTI) | 1 g IV/IM | q12h |
| Severe infections | 2 g IV | q12h |
| Very severe/Life-threatening | 2 g IV | q8h |

### Surgical Prophylaxis (Adults)
- **Dose:** 2 g IV (30-minute infusion) starting 60 minutes before the initial incision.
- **Combination:** A single 500 mg IV dose of metronidazole should follow the cefepime infusion.
- **Note:** Cefepime and metronidazole are incompatible; lines must be flushed between infusions [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (2 months to 12 years, ≤40 kg)
- **Standard Infections:** 500 mg/kg q12h (q8h for severe infections).
- **Febrile Neutropenia:** 50 mg/kg q8h.
- **Route:** IV is preferred; IM experience is limited and not recommended in this population [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Injection:** Reconstitute with 5 or 10 mL of 5% Glucose or 0.9% NaCl and inject slowly over 3–5 minutes.
- **IV Infusion:** Administer over approximately 30 minutes.
- **IM Injection:** Reconstitute with Sterile Water for Injections, 0.9% NaCl, 5% Glucose, or 0.5%–1.0% lignocaine hydrochloride. Inject into a large muscle mass [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known immediate hypersensitivity to cefepime, L-arginine, cephalosporins, penicillins, or other beta-lactam antibiotics [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neurotoxicity:** Serious events including reversible encephalopathy (confusion, hallucinations, stupor, coma), myoclonus, and seizures (including non-convulsive status epilepticus) have occurred. Most cases were reported in patients with renal impairment receiving doses exceeding recommendations [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Cross-reactivity with penicillins may occur. Serious reactions require immediate emergency treatment with adrenaline and supportive care [Australian].
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhoea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibiotics; severity ranges from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Including SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP. Discontinue immediately if suspected [Australian].
- **Renal Monitoring:** Function should be monitored carefully, especially when co-administered with aminoglycosides or potent diuretics [Australian].
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms.

## 6. Adverse Effects
**Common (>1% to 10%):**
- Gastrointestinal: Diarrhoea (1.2%) [Australian].
- Hypersensitivity: Rash (1.8%) [Australian].
- Local Reactions: Phlebitis at IV site (2.9%), pain/inflammation at IM site (2.6%) [Australian].
- Laboratory: Positive Coombs' test without haemolysis (18.7%), elevated ALT (3.6%), elevated AST (2.5%) [Australian].

**Uncommon (0.1% to 1%):**
- Nausea, vomiting, oral moniliasis, colitis.
- Pruritus, urticaria, erythema.
- Headache, fever, vaginitis.

**Rare (<0.1%):**
- Anaphylaxis, seizures, abdominal pain, constipation, dizziness, paraesthesia, dyspnoea [Australian].

**Serious Post-marketing Effects:**
- Encephalopathy, renal failure, agranulocytosis, anaphylactic shock [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Aminoglycosides:** Concomitant use increases the risk of nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity. Renal function must be monitored [Australian].
- **Potent Diuretics (e.g., Furosemide):** May increase the risk of nephrotoxicity [Australian].
- **Metronidazole:** Physically incompatible; do not mix in the same container [Australian].
- **Lab Interactions:** May cause a false-positive reaction for urinary glucose when using copper reduction tests (e.g., Benedict's solution) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Reproduction studies in animals showed no evidence of fetal harm. However, use in pregnant women only if clearly needed as human studies are inadequate.
- **Breastfeeding:** Cefepime is excreted in human milk in very low concentrations (<0.01% of a 1 g dose). Caution should be exercised [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility observed in rat studies at two-fold human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety not established in infants <2 months. Dosing for children >40 kg should follow adult guidelines [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher risk of toxicity due to age-related decline in renal function. Dosage adjustment is required if renal function is compromised [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Maintenance dose must be adjusted for CrCl ≤ 50 mL/min [Australian].

**Adult Renal Maintenance Dosing Table [Australian]:**
| CrCl (mL/min) | Recommended Maintenance Dose Adjustment |
| :--- | :--- |
| > 50 | No adjustment (e.g., 2g q8h, 2g q12h, 1g q12h, 500mg q12h) |
| 30 – 50 | Dose q24h (except 2g q8h becomes 2g q12h) |
| 11 – 29 | 500 mg – 2 g q24h (depending on original dose) |
| ≤ 10 | 250 mg – 1 g q24h |
| Haemodialysis | 500 mg q24h (1 g loading dose on Day 1) |

- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is necessary [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal agent; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis. It has high resistance to hydrolysis by many beta-lactamases and rapid penetration into Gram-negative cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Completely absorbed following IM administration.
    - **Distribution:** Low protein binding (~16.4%); distributes into bile, peritoneal fluid, sputum, and prostate.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolized to N-methylpyrrolidine, which is converted to N-oxide.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 85% recovered unchanged in urine; elimination half-life is ~2 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Surgical Prophylaxis (Trial AI411-230):** A randomized, multi-centre, open-label study (n=615) compared 2 g cefepime (+ metronidazole) to ceftriaxone (+ metronidazole) in elective colorectal surgery. Cefepime showed a success rate (absence of infection) of 75%, comparable to the ceftriaxone group [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Dry State:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Should be used immediately to avoid microbial contamination. If delayed, store at 2–8°C for a maximum of 24 hours [Australian].
- **Note:** The powder and reconstituted solution may darken on storage; this does not indicate loss of potency [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
