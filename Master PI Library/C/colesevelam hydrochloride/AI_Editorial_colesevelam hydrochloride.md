# Editorial Product Information: colesevelam hydrochloride

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **colesevelam hydrochloride**
- Chemical class: Bile acid sequestrant (anion-exchange polymer)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2000 [US]
- Date of last revision/update reported in provided source: January 2024 [US]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Registration/approval dates for jurisdictions other than the United States: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Reduction of elevated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) as an adjunct to diet and exercise in adults with primary hyperlipidemia.
- Reduction of LDL-C in boys and postmenarchal girls 10 to 17 years of age with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) who are unable to reach LDL-C targets despite adequate diet and lifestyle modification.  
- Improvement of glycemic control as an adjunct to diet and exercise in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus.  
- Limitations of use: **Not for treatment of type 1 diabetes or diabetic ketoacidosis.** Not studied in Fredrickson Types I, III, IV, and V dyslipidemias.

(Indications and limitations above are those described in the available U.S. product information. Indication status in other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended daily dose for adults: 3.75 grams daily.

### Dosing by indication
- Primary hyperlipidemia (adults): 3.75 g daily.
- Primary hyperlipidemia (boys and postmenarchal girls 10–17 years with HeFH): 3.75 g daily.
- Type 2 diabetes mellitus (adults): 3.75 g daily.

### Administration instructions
- Take with a meal and liquid.
- Tablet dosing options (when tablets used): equivalent to 3.75 g daily (examples in clinical documents: 6 tablets once daily or 3 tablets twice daily). (Tablet-size considerations: use oral suspension for patients who have difficulty swallowing.)
- Oral suspension dosing: one 3.75 g packet once daily. To prepare, empty entire packet into a glass or cup; add approximately 1 cup (8 ounces) of water, fruit juice, or diet soft drink; stir well and drink. Do not take the powder in dry form.
- For pediatric patients (boys and postmenarchal girls 10–17 years), oral suspension is recommended due to tablet size.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Concomitant lipid therapy
- May be dosed at the same time as a statin or apart from a statin. Monitor lipid levels 4–6 weeks after initiation.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Serum triglyceride (TG) concentrations greater than 500 mg/dL.
- History of hypertriglyceridemia-induced pancreatitis.
- History of bowel obstruction.

(These contraindications are those stated in the available U.S. product information. Contraindication differences in other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- **Hypertriglyceridemia and risk of pancreatitis**: Colesevelam can increase serum triglyceride concentrations. Obtain baseline lipid parameters including triglycerides before initiating therapy and monitor periodically thereafter. Discontinue colesevelam if TG levels exceed 500 mg/dL. Instruct patients to stop the drug and seek prompt medical attention for signs or symptoms of acute pancreatitis (e.g., severe abdominal pain with or without nausea/vomiting).
  - Clinical-trial findings (median placebo-corrected TG changes): monotherapy and various add-on regimens demonstrated median increases in TG (examples): monotherapy ~9.7% increase; as add-on to metformin ~5%; to pioglitazone ~11%; to sulfonylureas ~18%; to insulin ~22%. Patients with baseline TG >300 mg/dL may have greater increases and may require more frequent monitoring.
- **Gastrointestinal obstruction and related events**: Cases of bowel obstruction, fecal impaction, dysphagia, and esophageal obstruction have occurred postmarketing. Because of constipating potential, colesevelam is not recommended in patients with gastroparesis, other gastrointestinal motility disorders, or in patients with prior major gastrointestinal tract surgery at risk of bowel obstruction. Instruct patients to discontinue and seek medical attention for severe abdominal pain or severe constipation.
- **Vitamin K and fat-soluble vitamin absorption**: Colesevelam may decrease absorption of fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K. Patients susceptible to deficiency (e.g., on warfarin, malabsorption syndromes) may be at increased risk. Patients taking oral vitamin supplements should separate dosing by at least 4 hours (take vitamins ≥4 hours prior to colesevelam).
- **Drug absorption interactions**: Colesevelam reduces gastrointestinal absorption of certain concomitant medications. Administer drugs with known interactions at least 4 hours prior to colesevelam. Consider similar spacing for other drugs not formally tested, particularly those with a narrow therapeutic index.
- **Phenylketonuria (PKU)**: Oral suspension contains aspartame-derived phenylalanine (27 mg phenylalanine per 3.75 g packet). Consider total daily phenylalanine intake before prescribing suspension to patients with PKU.
- **Tablet size caution**: Tablet size may cause dysphagia or esophageal obstruction in some patients; use oral suspension for patients with swallowing difficulty.

### Monitoring requirements
- Lipid panel including triglycerides: obtain before initiation and periodically thereafter; recheck lipids 4–6 weeks after initiation for LDL-C effect.
- For patients on warfarin: monitor International Normalized Ratio (INR) frequently upon initiation and periodically thereafter.
- For coadministration with narrow therapeutic index drugs (e.g., cyclosporine, phenytoin): consider therapeutic drug monitoring or dose/timing adjustment; administer such agents ≥4 hours prior to colesevelam.
- For patients coadministered metformin extended release: monitor glycemic control.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of gastrointestinal obstruction and pancreatitis.
- Monitor liver enzymes if clinically indicated (postmarketing reports of increased transaminases).

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid use in patients with serum TG >500 mg/dL or with history of hypertriglyceridemia-induced pancreatitis (contraindicated).
- Not recommended in patients at increased risk of bowel obstruction (e.g., severe motility disorders, prior major GI surgery).
- Take precautions for possible altered absorption of concomitant oral therapies; separate dosing by ≥4 hours when appropriate.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### General interaction principles
- Colesevelam binds bile acids in the gastrointestinal tract and reduces gastrointestinal absorption of certain coadministered oral medications; spacing of dosing and monitoring are recommended.
- For drugs with known interactions, administer those drugs at least 4 hours prior to colesevelam to reduce the risk of decreased absorption. Consider similar spacing for other oral drugs, especially those with a narrow therapeutic index.

### Interactions shown to decrease exposure of concomitant medications (and management)
- Cyclosporine: decrease in exposure observed; administer cyclosporine ≥4 hours prior to colesevelam; monitor drug levels when appropriate.
- Phenytoin: postmarketing reports of decreased phenytoin levels and increased seizure activity; administer phenytoin ≥4 hours prior to colesevelam.
- Thyroid hormone replacement (levothyroxine): decreased exposure observed; postmarketing reports of elevated TSH; administer thyroid hormone replacement ≥4 hours prior to colesevelam and monitor thyroid function.
- Warfarin: postmarketing reports of reduced INR; monitor INR frequently when initiating colesevelam and periodically thereafter.
- Oral contraceptives containing ethinyl estradiol and norethindrone: decreased exposure observed; administer oral contraceptive ≥4 hours prior to colesevelam.
- Olmesartan medoxomil: decreased exposure observed; administer olmesartan medoxomil ≥4 hours prior to colesevelam.
- Sulfonylureas (glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide): decreased exposure observed in vivo studies; administer sulfonylureas ≥4 hours prior to colesevelam.
- Oral vitamin supplements (fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K): decreased absorption possible; take vitamins ≥4 hours prior to colesevelam.

### Interactions shown to increase exposure of concomitant medications
- Metformin extended release (ER): in vivo interaction showed increased exposure when coadministered with colesevelam ER; monitor patients' glycemic control when coadministered.

### Other interaction considerations
- Because of potential decreased absorption of drugs not explicitly tested, consider administering critical concomitant oral medications at least 4 hours prior to colesevelam, particularly agents with narrow therapeutic indices.
- Reported clinical interaction consequences include: seizure exacerbation (phenytoin), reduced INR (warfarin), and elevated TSH (levothyroxine).

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Risk summary: Colesevelam is not absorbed systemically following oral administration and maternal use is not expected to result in fetal systemic exposure. Available human data are limited and insufficient to determine a drug-associated risk of major congenital malformations or miscarriage.
- Animal data: In rats and rabbits exposed during organogenesis at doses approximately 8 and 5 times, respectively, the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) on a mg/m^2 basis, no evidence of maternal or fetal toxicity was observed. In rats, no adverse effects on offspring survival and development were observed at approximately 5 times the MRHD.
- Effect on fat-soluble vitamin absorption in pregnancy: Not known—colesevelam may decrease fat-soluble vitamin absorption; there are no data on this effect in pregnant women.
- Clinical recommendation: If pregnancy occurs during therapy, discuss the limited potential for clinical benefit and the absence of evidence for fetal risk; consider discontinuation if clinically appropriate.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Indication: Reduction of LDL-C in boys and postmenarchal girls aged 10–17 years with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) who fail to reach LDL-C targets with diet and lifestyle modification.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **colesevelam hydrochloride** is a bile acid sequestrant (non-absorbable polymer) that binds bile acids in the gastrointestinal tract, reducing enterohepatic recirculation of bile acids and thereby lowering LDL-C. (Mechanistic characterization as a bile acid sequestrant is provided in the product information.)

### Pharmacokinetics
- Systemic absorption: Not absorbed systemically following oral administration (i.e., minimal systemic absorption anticipated).
- Distribution, metabolism, elimination (quantitative PK parameters such as Cmax, Tmax, half-life, clearance, protein binding): Not specified in available sources.
- Effect on absorption of concomitant oral drugs: Colesevelam reduces gastrointestinal absorption of some medications; shown to decrease exposure of several coadministered drugs and to increase exposure of metformin ER in vivo.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Lowers LDL-C; also has been shown to improve glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus in randomized trials. Effects on triglycerides: colesevelam can increase serum triglyceride concentrations (median increases reported in clinical trials vary by regimen).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key trial program summaries available in the product information include studies in primary hyperlipidemia, pediatric HeFH, and type 2 diabetes mellitus.

### Primary hyperlipidemia trials
- Seven double-blind, placebo-controlled trials enrolling 807 patients (age range 18–86 years) evaluated colesevelam at doses from 1.5 to 4.5 g/day for 4–24 weeks (total exposure ~199 patient-years). Adverse reaction profile included constipation (11.0% vs 7.0% placebo), dyspepsia (8.3% vs 3.5%), and nausea (4.2% vs 3.9%), among others.

### Pediatric HeFH trial
- One 8-week double-blind, placebo-controlled study in boys and postmenarchal girls aged 10–17 years with HeFH (n=194). Doses ranged 1.9–3.8 g daily. Adverse events reported more frequently with colesevelam included nasopharyngitis (6.2% vs 4.6%), headache (3.9% vs 3.1%), fatigue (3.9% vs 1.5%), increased creatine phosphokinase (2.3% vs 0%), rhinitis, and vomiting. An 18-week open-label extension reported similar adverse events.

### Type 2 diabetes mellitus trials
- Six double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (five add-on combination trials and one monotherapy) of 12–26 weeks enrolled patients with type 2 diabetes. A total of 1,022 patients received colesevelam (intended dose 3.8 g/day; mean exposure 20 weeks; total exposure ~393 patient-years). Baseline mean HbA1c ~8.2%. Colesevelam improved glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes (indication); adverse reactions included constipation (6.5% vs 2.2% placebo), hypoglycemia (3.4% vs 3.1%), dyspepsia (2.8% vs 1.0%), and nausea (2.6% vs 1.6%). Median changes in triglycerides differed by regimen with placebo-corrected median increases as noted in Section 6.

- Laboratory outcomes: In diabetes trials, post-treatment median fasting TG was 180 mg/dL (colesevelam) vs 162 mg/dL (placebo). Fasting TG ≥500 mg/dL occurred in 0.9% of colesevelam-treated vs 0.7% placebo; TG ≥1000 mg/dL occurred in 0.6% of colesevelam-treated vs 0.3% placebo.

- Cardiovascular safety signals: Serious cardiovascular adverse reactions occurred in 2.2% of colesevelam-treated vs 1.0% placebo in diabetes trials; events were heterogeneous and clinical significance was not established.

### Notes on interpretation
- Clinical-trial adverse-event rates are context-specific; caution when extrapolating to broader populations. Trials excluded patients with baseline fasting serum TG >500 mg/dL.
