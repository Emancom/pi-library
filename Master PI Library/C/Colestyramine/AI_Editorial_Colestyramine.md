# Editorial Product Information: Colestyramine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, intended to consolidate clinical information into a concise, professional reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Colestyramine (as the chloride salt of a basic anion exchange resin).
- **Brand Names:** **Questran Lite** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C10AB01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Powder for oral solution. Available in sachets containing 4 g of colestyramine (in 4.7 g powder) or 8 g of colestyramine (in 9.4 g powder) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Primary Hypercholesterolemia:** Adjunctive therapy to diet for the reduction of elevated serum cholesterol in patients with primary hypercholesterolemia (elevated LDL). It may be used in combined hypercholesterolemia and hypertriglyceridemia, though it is not indicated when hypertriglyceridemia is the primary concern [Australian].
- **Pruritus:** Relief of pruritus associated with partial biliary obstruction [Australian].
- **Bile Acid Diarrhoea:** Relief of diarrhoea associated with ileal resection or ileal disease (cholerrhoeic enteropathy) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** 4 g daily (morning or evening).
- **Titration:** Increase the dose over 2 to 4 weeks to the required maintenance level.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 12 to 16 g of colestyramine per day is usually effective [Australian].
- **Maximum Dosing:** If doses exceed 24 g/day, patients must be closely monitored for increased adverse effects.

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Colestyramine **must never be taken in its dry form**. It must be mixed with water, fruit juice, highly fluid soups, or pulpy fruits (e.g., apple puree).
- **Mixing:** Place the powder on the surface of 100–150 mL of liquid (for a 4 g sachet) or 200–300 mL (for an 8 g sachet). Stir vigorously and drink immediately. Rinse the glass with additional fluid to ensure the full dose is consumed.
- **Timing with Other Meds:** To prevent absorption interference, other medications should be taken at least **one hour before** or **four to six hours after** colestyramine [Australian].

### Clinical Response
- For bile acid-induced diarrhoea, a clinical response should be evident within 3 days; otherwise, alternative therapy should be considered [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to colestyramine or any excipients [Australian].
- Complete biliary obstruction (where no bile is secreted into the intestine) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Phenylketonuria (PKU):** **Questran Lite** contains aspartame, providing 16.8 mg of phenylalanine per 4 g dose. Use with caution in patients with PKU [Australian].
- **Underlying Disorders:** Before starting therapy, secondary causes of hypercholesterolemia (e.g., hypothyroidism, diabetes mellitus, nephrotic syndrome) should be identified and treated.
- **Vitamin Deficiencies:** Chronic use may interfere with the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, K). Long-term therapy may require daily supplementation of water-miscible or parenteral vitamins A and D [Australian].
- **Bleeding Tendencies:** Vitamin K deficiency-induced hypoprothrombinaemia may occur, increasing bleeding risk. This is particularly hazardous for patients on anticoagulants. Folic acid deficiency has also been reported [Australian].
- **Hyperchloraemic Acidosis:** Because colestyramine is a chloride form of resin, prolonged use may lead to hyperchloraemic acidosis, particularly in children and smaller patients [Australian].
- **Constipation:** Colestyramine may exacerbate pre-existing constipation. Dosage should be reduced if impaction is a risk, particularly in patients with symptomatic coronary artery disease [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (>10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Constipation (major complaint). It may be severe and sometimes leads to faecal impaction or haemorrhoidal bleeding. Risk factors include high doses (>24 g/day) and age >60 years [Australian].

### Less Common (0.1–1%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain/distension, flatulence, nausea, vomiting, heartburn, diarrhoea, anorexia, dyspepsia, and steatorrhoea.
- **Haematological:** Bleeding tendencies (hypoprothrombinaemia), ecchymosis, and anaemia.
- **Other:** Rash, irritation of the skin/tongue/perianal area, hyperchloraemic acidosis, and osteoporosis.

### Rare (<0.1%)
- Intestinal obstruction (post-marketing reports include paediatric fatalities) [Australian].

### Events of Uncertain Causality
- Liver function abnormalities, biliary colic, calcified gallbladder, and various cardiovascular events (angina, myocardial infarction) have been reported but are not necessarily drug-related [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
Colestyramine is an anion exchange resin and has a high affinity for acidic materials, though it may also bind neutral or basic substances.
- **Delayed/Reduced Absorption:** Includes but is not limited to:
    - Warfarin
    - Phenylbutazone
    - Chlorothiazide
    - Tetracycline and Phenobarbital
    - Thyroid and Levothyroxine preparations
    - Digitalis (Discontinuing colestyramine in a patient stabilised on digitalis can lead to toxicity)
    - Inorganic iron
- **Enterohepatic Recirculation:** May interfere with drugs like estrogens that undergo enterohepatic cycling [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category B2** [Australian].
- **Clinical Recommendation:** Safety in pregnancy has not been established. The risk of fat-soluble vitamin deficiency must be weighed against benefits. Physiological hyperlipidaemia of pregnancy generally does not require treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Use with caution; interference with vitamin absorption in the mother may impact the nutritional quality of breast milk [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Experience is limited and a formal dosage schedule is not established. Use small initial doses and monitor for hyperchloraemic acidosis [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Patients >60 years are at higher risk of severe constipation [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, use is contraindicated in complete biliary obstruction.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Colestyramine is a non-absorbed resin that binds bile acids in the intestine, forming an insoluble complex excreted in the faeces. This prevents reabsorption and leads to increased oxidation of cholesterol to bile acids in the liver.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Results in decreased LDL-cholesterol. Serum triglycerides may increase or remain unchanged [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract; it is unaffected by digestive enzymes and excreted entirely in the faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C [Australian].
- Shelf life is specified on the individual product packaging.
