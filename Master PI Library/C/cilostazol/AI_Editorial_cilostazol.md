# Editorial Product Information: Cilostazol

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) documents originating from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of cilostazol for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Prescribing Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cilostazol
- **Chemical Class:** Quinolone derivative; Phosphodiesterase III (PDE III) inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** Cilostazol Tablets, USP (AvKARE) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 50 mg and 100 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AvKARE [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Intermittent Claudication:** Indicated to reduce symptoms of intermittent claudication, as evidenced by an objective increase in maximal walking distance [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The standard recommended dose is **100 mg taken twice daily** [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Doses should be administered at least 30 minutes before or 2 hours after breakfast and dinner. Taking cilostazol with a high-fat meal significantly increases absorption (Cmax increases by approximately 90%) [US].
- **Dose Adjustments for Interactions:** The dose should be reduced to **50 mg twice daily** when co-administered with:
    - Strong or moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, erythromycin, diltiazem).
    - CYP2C19 inhibitors (e.g., ticlopidine, fluconazole, omeprazole) [US].
- **Clinical Response:** Therapeutic effects may be observed within 2 to 4 weeks; however, treatment for up to 12 weeks may be necessary to realize the full benefit. 
- **Discontinuation Criteria:** If symptoms remain unimproved after 3 months of therapy, discontinuation of cilostazol should be considered [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Heart Failure:** Contraindicated in patients with heart failure of any severity. Cilostazol is a PDE III inhibitor; drugs in this class have demonstrated decreased survival in patients with Class III-IV heart failure [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to cilostazol or any component of the formulation (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (US):** **Contraindicated in Heart Failure Patients.** PDE III inhibitors have caused decreased survival in patients with NYHA Class III-IV heart failure compared to placebo. 
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** 
    - May induce tachycardia, palpitations, tachyarrhythmia, or hypotension.
    - Patients with ischemic heart disease may be at increased risk for exacerbations of angina pectoris or myocardial infarction.
    - **Left Ventricular Outflow Tract (LVOT) Obstruction:** Reported in patients with a sigmoid-shaped interventricular septum; clinicians should monitor for new systolic murmurs [US].
- **Hematologic Reactions:** Rare cases of thrombocytopenia or leukopenia progressing to agranulocytosis have been reported. Agranulocytosis is generally reversible upon discontinuation. Periodic monitoring of platelet and white blood cell counts is advised [US].
- **Hemostatic Disorders:** Cilostazol inhibits platelet aggregation. Use should be avoided in patients with active pathologic bleeding or known hemostatic disorders [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP450):**
    - **Strong/Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., Ketoconazole, erythromycin, diltiazem, grapefruit juice). Increase cilostazol exposure. Reduce dose to 50 mg BID [US].
    - **CYP2C19 Inhibitors:** (e.g., Omeprazole). Increases exposure to the 3,4-dehydro-cilostazol active metabolite. Reduce dose to 50 mg BID [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Aspirin:** Short-term co-administration increases inhibition of ADP-induced platelet aggregation by 22–37% compared to aspirin alone. Long-term co-administration (up to 325 mg/day) did not show a significant increase in hemorrhagic effects in clinical trials [US].
    - **Warfarin:** Cilostazol does not appear to inhibit the metabolism or pharmacologic effects of R- and S-warfarin after a single dose, though the effects of chronic co-administration are unknown [US].
- **Lifestyle Interactions:**
    - **Smoking:** Decreases cilostazol exposure by approximately 20% [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C]. Cilostazol demonstrated teratogenicity in rat studies at doses >5 times the human MRHD. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Cilostazol is excreted in the milk of rats. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was noted in rats at high doses, although a reversible contraceptive effect was observed in female mice [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between elderly (≥65 years) and younger patients. Pharmacokinetics are not significantly affected by age [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. While severe renal impairment increases metabolite levels, the total pharmacologic activity remains similar. Cilostazol is not efficiently removed by dialysis (95–98% protein bound) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild:** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate to Severe:** Not studied; no dosing recommendations can be provided [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Cilostazol and its metabolites inhibit PDE III, leading to increased levels of cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) in platelets and blood vessels. This results in the reversible inhibition of platelet aggregation and vasodilation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability unknown. High-fat meals significantly increase Cmax and AUC.
    - **Distribution:** 95% to 98% protein bound (predominantly albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized by hepatic CYP3A4 and CYP2C19. Two active metabolites exist: 3,4-dehydro-cilostazol (4–7 times more active than parent) and 4'-trans-hydroxy-cilostazol [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primary route is urinary (74%); terminal half-life is approximately 11 to 13 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** In eight randomized, placebo-controlled trials (n=2,274), cilostazol 100 mg BID improved maximal walking distance by 28% to 100% relative to baseline. Improvement was observed within 2 to 4 weeks [US].
- **Mortality:** A Phase IV trial (n=1,439) in patients with intermittent claudication and no heart failure found no increase in mortality risk compared to placebo over a median of 18 months [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
