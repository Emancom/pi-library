# Editorial Product Information: Cefditoren Pivoxil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference. 
- **Important:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefditoren pivoxil (a semi-synthetic, broad-spectrum cephalosporin antibiotic; prodrug of cefditoren).
- **Brand Name(s):** Spectracef [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01DD16.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Film-coated tablets, 200 mg and 400 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured by Tedec-Meiji Farma, S.A. (Spain); Distributed by Vansen Pharma Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Cefditoren pivoxil is indicated for the treatment of mild-to-moderate infections in adults and adolescents ($\ge 12$ years of age) caused by susceptible strains of designated microorganisms in the following conditions [US]:
- **Acute Bacterial Exacerbation of Chronic Bronchitis:** Caused by *Haemophilus influenzae* (including $\beta$-lactamase-producing strains), *Haemophilus parainfluenzae*, *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (penicillin-susceptible strains only), or *Moraxella catarrhalis*.
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia:** Caused by *H. influenzae*, *H. parainfluenzae*, *S. pneumoniae* (penicillin-susceptible), or *M. catarrhalis*.
- **Pharyngitis/Tonsillitis:** Caused by *Streptococcus pyogenes*.
- **Uncomplicated Skin and Skin-Structure Infections:** Caused by *Staphylococcus aureus* (including $\beta$-lactamase-producing strains) or *Streptococcus pyogenes*.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents $\ge 12$ years)
Dosing is administered twice daily (BID) and varies by indication [US]:

| Indication | Dose | Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Community-Acquired Pneumonia | 400 mg BID | 14 days |
| Acute Bacterial Exacerbation of Chronic Bronchitis | 400 mg BID | 10 days |
| Pharyngitis / Tonsillitis | 200 mg BID | 10 days |
| Uncomplicated Skin and Skin-Structure Infections | 200 mg BID | 10 days |

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be taken **with meals** to enhance oral absorption [US].
- To minimize the development of drug-resistant bacteria, therapy should only be used for infections proven or strongly suspected to be bacterial.

### Missed Dose
- Not specifically addressed in available sources; however, standard clinical practice suggests taking the missed dose as soon as remembered unless it is nearly time for the next dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Cephalosporin Allergy:** Known hypersensitivity to cefditoren pivoxil, other cephalosporins, or any component of the formulation [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency:** Patients with clinical carnitine deficiency or inborn errors of metabolism that may result in clinically significant carnitine deficiency (cefditoren causes renal excretion of carnitine) [US].
- **Milk Protein Hypersensitivity:** Spectracef tablets contain **sodium caseinate**. Use is contraindicated in patients with hypersensitivity to milk protein (distinct from lactose intolerance) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Cross-hypersensitivity with penicillins occurs in approximately 10% of penicillin-allergic patients. Serious acute reactions may require emergency intervention [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-associated diarrhoea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all systemic antibacterial agents, ranging from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency:** Cefditoren is not recommended for prolonged antibiotic treatment (several months), as pivalate-containing compounds can cause clinical carnitine deficiency. Short-term treatment (10–14 days) is associated with a transient decrease in serum carnitine (approx. 30–46%), which typically returns to normal within 7–10 days of discontinuation [US].
- **Prothrombin Activity:** Cephalosporins may be associated with a fall in prothrombin activity, particularly in patients with renal/hepatic impairment, poor nutritional status, or those on anticoagulant therapy [US].
- **Seizure Potential:** Several cephalosporins have been implicated in triggering seizures, especially in patients with renal impairment when doses were not adjusted [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse events reported in clinical trials were mild and self-limiting [US].

### Common Adverse Effects (Incidence $\ge 1\%$)
| Adverse Event | 200 mg BID | 400 mg BID |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Diarrhoea** | 11% | 15% |
| **Nausea** | 4% | 6% |
| **Vaginal Moniliasis** | 3% | 6% |
| **Headache** | 3% | 2% |
| **Abdominal Pain** | 2% | 2% |
| **Dyspepsia** | 1% | 2% |
| **Vomiting** | 1% | 1% |

### Less Common and Serious Effects ($< 1\%$ to $> 0.1\%$)
- **Serious:** Allergic reactions, anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, acute renal failure, pseudomembranous colitis, and eosinophilic pneumonia [US].
- **Other:** Abnormal dreams, coagulation time increased, hyperglycemia, leukopenia, and thrombocytopenia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antacids/H2-Receptor Antagonists:** Co-administration with antacids (magnesium/aluminum) or intravenous famotidine reduces cefditoren absorption (AUC decreased by 11% and 22%, respectively). Concurrent use is not recommended [US].
- **Probenecid:** Increases plasma exposure (Cmax by 49%, AUC by 122%) and extends the half-life of cefditoren [US].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Cefditoren does not significantly affect the pharmacokinetics of ethinyl estradiol [US].
- **Laboratory Test Interactions:** 
    - May induce a positive direct Coombs’ test.
    - May cause a false-positive reaction for glucose in urine using copper reduction tests (e.g., Benedict’s or Fehling’s solution) but not with enzyme-based tests [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category B]. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no teratogenicity at high doses. However, no adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Cefditoren was detected in rat breast milk. Caution is advised when administering to nursing women as many drugs are excreted in human milk [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rats at doses up to 24 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is **not recommended** for patients under 12 years of age. Safety and efficacy (including carnitine effects) have not been established for children [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required for geriatric patients with normal renal function (relative to age) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild (CrCl 50–80 mL/min):** No dose adjustment required [US].
    - **Moderate (CrCl 30–49 mL/min):** Maximum dose of 200 mg BID [US].
    - **Severe (CrCl < 30 mL/min):** 200 mg once daily (QD) [US].
    - **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Appropriate dose has not been determined [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild or moderate impairment (Child-Pugh Class A or B). Use in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C) has not been studied [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs). Cefditoren is stable against various $\beta$-lactamases [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Prodrug hydrolyzed to active cefditoren by esterases. Absolute bioavailability is ~14% (fasted) and increases significantly (up to 70% increase in AUC) when taken with a high-fat meal [US].
    - **Distribution:** ~88% protein bound (primarily to albumin). Negligible penetration into red blood cells [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Not appreciably metabolized after the initial hydrolysis of the pivoxil ester [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal excretion. Terminal half-life is approximately 1.6 hours in healthy adults [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Clinical trial data involved 4,834 adult and adolescent patients treated with recommended doses. The overall incidence of adverse events, particularly diarrhoea, increased with the higher 400 mg BID dose compared to 200 mg BID. In CAP trials, no adverse events were attributed specifically to the observed transient decreases in serum carnitine [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15°–30°C (59°–86°F) [US].
- **Protection:** Protect from light and moisture.
- **Dispensing:** Use tight, light-resistant containers [US].
