# Editorial Product Information: Colchicine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of colchicine product information, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Colchicine (Chemical class: Alkaloid)
- **Brand Names:** ARX-COLCHICINE [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** M04AC01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 500 microgram (0.5 mg) uncoated tablets [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Limited [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Gout:** Relief of pain in acute gout flares. Colchicine is generally considered second-line therapy and should be reserved for cases where non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are contraindicated, have failed, or have caused unacceptable side effects [Australian].
- **Note:** Colchicine is not a general analgesic and is ineffective for other types of pain [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing (Acute Gout)
- **Initial Dose:** 1 mg (2 x 500 mcg tablets) at the first sign of a flare.
- **Follow-up Dose:** 0.5 mg (1 tablet) one hour later.
- **Maximum Dose:** 1.5 mg total over a one-hour period.
- **Repetition:** A course of treatment should not be repeated within 3 days [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Therapy must be stopped immediately if gastrointestinal symptoms (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain) occur, regardless of whether joint pain has subsided [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Organ Impairment:** Patients with severe renal or hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Drug-Drug Interactions:** Patients with renal or hepatic impairment who are concurrently taking a P-glycoprotein (P-gp) inhibitor or a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor [Australian].
- **Combined Disease:** Patients with concurrent hepatic and renal disease [Australian].
- **Other Medical Conditions:**
    - Serious cardiac or gastrointestinal disorders [Australian].
    - Blood dyscrasias [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to colchicine or any tablet excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- **Paediatrics:** Children under 2 years of age [Australian].
- **Hereditary Conditions:** Patients with rare galactose intolerance, total lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Narrow Therapeutic Index:** Colchicine has a narrow therapeutic window. The distinction between a therapeutic and toxic dose is poorly defined. Fatalities have been reported at therapeutic doses as low as 6 to 7 mg [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Toxicity:** Early signs of toxicity include nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea. Patients must be instructed to cease treatment immediately at the onset of these symptoms [Australian].
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** Prolonged use or high doses can cause significant haematological effects, including leukopenia and thrombocytopenia. Dental work should be deferred if blood counts are low [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Toxicity:** Risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis is increased in the elderly and those with renal/hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic complete blood counts are recommended during long-term therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally dose-related.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect | Frequency/Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea | Very common; occurs in ~80% of patients at maximal doses [Australian] |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Haemorrhagic gastroenteritis, paralytic ileus, stomatitis | Associated with toxic doses [Australian] |
| **Haematological** | Anaemia, leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis | Associated with long-term use or overdose [Australian] |
| **Neuromuscular** | Myopathy, reversible muscular weakness, rhabdomyolysis | Risk increased in renal/hepatic impairment [Australian] |
| **Dermatological** | Alopecia, rashes, urticaria, dermatitis | Rare; alopecia may occur post-overdose [Australian] |
| **Reproductive** | Reversible azoospermia, oligospermia | Reported in clinical literature [Australian] |
| **Neurological** | Peripheral neuropathy, dizziness, drowsiness | Associated with prolonged administration [Australian] |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions (P-gp and CYP3A4)
Colchicine is a substrate for both CYP3A4 and P-gp.
- **Strong CYP3A4/P-gp Inhibitors:** (e.g., Clarithromycin, Erythromycin, Itraconazole, Ketoconazole, Ritonavir, Atazanavir). Concomitant use increases colchicine plasma levels and risk of fatal toxicity. Use is contraindicated in patients with renal or hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., Verapamil, Diltiazem). May increase colchicine exposure. Dose reduction or interruption of colchicine may be required [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Cyclosporin:** Increased risk of myopathy, rhabdomyolysis, and nephrotoxicity [Australian].
- **NSAIDs/Phenylbutazone:** Increased risk of gastrointestinal ulceration, haemorrhage, and bone marrow depression [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** Colchicine may increase sensitivity to opiates, sedatives, and alcohol [Australian].
- **Acidifying/Alkalinising Agents:** Action is inhibited by acidifying agents (e.g., ammonium chloride) and potentiated by alkalinising agents (e.g., sodium bicarbonate) [Australian].
- **Vitamin B12:** Colchicine may induce reversible malabsorption of Vitamin B12 [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category D. Colchicine crosses the placenta and has shown teratogenic and foetotoxic effects in animal models. It should be avoided in pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Colchicine is excreted into human breast milk at concentrations similar to maternal serum. While limited data showed no adverse effects in infants over 6 months, it is generally recommended that breastfeeding be avoided during therapy [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May adversely affect spermatogenesis in humans [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Safety and efficacy not established; contraindicated in children under 2 years [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Increased susceptibility to cumulative toxicity and myopathy. 
    - Patients weighing <50 kg or with impairment should observe a maximum cumulative dose of 1.5 mg over three days [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **CrCl $\le$ 50 mL/min:** Recommended to reduce dosage by half.
    - **CrCl $\le$ 10 mL/min:** Use is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Close monitoring required; dose reduction or increased intervals may be necessary [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Not fully understood in gout. It inhibits leukocyte chemotaxis and phagocytosis, reducing the inflammatory response to urate crystals. It also arrests cell division in metaphase by interfering with the mitotic spindle [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** 25% to 50%.
    - **Peak Plasma Time:** Within 2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** High concentrations found in leucocytes, kidneys, liver, and spleen.
    - **Metabolism:** Partially deacetylated in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (80–90%); 10–20% excreted in urine.
    - **Half-life:** ~4.4 hours (normal renal function) to 18.8 hours (renal dysfunction) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Container:** HDPE bottle of 30 tablets [Australian].
