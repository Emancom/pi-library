## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients where these errors result in deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Administered every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered in clinical practice [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow 2–3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **General Monitoring:** 
    - **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** It is recommended to obtain levels prior to therapy and monitor weekly/monthly. The plasma free carnitine concentration should ideally be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
    - Monitor blood chemistries, vital signs, and overall clinical condition [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology (ESRD or metabolic errors).
- **Common (>10%) [ESRD Trial Data]:**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33%–59%), pain (30%–49%), headache (22%), flu syndrome (25%).
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%–35%), vomiting (15%–21%), nausea (8%–12%), abdominal pain (9%–21%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18%–21%), hypotension (14%–19%), chest pain (12%–15%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%–33%), cough increase (9%–18%), dyspnea (7%–19%).
- **Other Reported Effects:** Body odor, gastritis, and transient nausea/vomiting [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, seizures, and laryngeal edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concurrently with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not explicitly lettered in source, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentration after exogenous administration. Weigh risks to the child against maternal benefits; consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg weight; safety and efficacy in children follow adult guidelines for the indicated metabolic conditions [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Oral Levocarnitine Warning:** High doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
    - **IV Use:** Core indication is for ESRD patients on dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Corrects carnitine deficiency (defined as <20 µmol/L at one week post-term). In ESRD, it addresses losses due to dialysis and reduced renal synthesis [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine over 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis showed that trough levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While carnitine levels increase, the definitive effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, arrhythmias) has not been determined in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
