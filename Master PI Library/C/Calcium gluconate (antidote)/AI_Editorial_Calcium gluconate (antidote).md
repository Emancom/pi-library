# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine injection** for use by healthcare professionals.
- This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. The clinical data provided herein is based on the source material provided for Baxter Healthcare Corporation’s **levocarnitine** injection.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]; Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** A loading dose is often employed in patients presenting with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours. 
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to initiating parenteral therapy.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### Compatibility and Stability
- Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations: 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution):** While this PI refers to the injection, it is noted that chronic high-dose **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concurrently with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category Not Specified; traditionally consistent with Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, no adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to Section 3 for dosing. Safety and efficacy for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily used to treat deficiency in ESRD. Safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following a single 20 mg/kg IV bolus, the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg levocarnitine three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
