# Editorial Product Information: Cabozantinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the Australian (TGA) regulatory authority.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **cabozantinib** for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, safety profiles, and administration guidelines.
- **Prescriber Notice:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cabozantinib (as (S)-malate).
- **Chemical Class:** Multikinase inhibitor; small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI).
- **Brand Name(s):** **CABOMETYX** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EX07.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Film-coated tablets: 20 mg (yellow, round), 40 mg (yellow, triangle), and 60 mg (yellow, oval) [Australian]. 
  - *Note: Cabometyx tablets are not bioequivalent to cabozantinib capsules (Cometriq) [Australian].*
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ipsen Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC):**
  - Monotherapy for advanced RCC in treatment-naïve adults with intermediate or poor risk [Australian].
  - Monotherapy for advanced RCC in adults following prior vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF)-targeted therapy [Australian].
  - In combination with **nivolumab** for the first-line treatment of advanced RCC [Australian].
- **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC):** Monotherapy for HCC in adults previously treated with sorafenib [Australian].
- **Differentiated Thyroid Carcinoma (DTC):** Monotherapy for adults and paediatric patients ($\ge$ 12 years) with locally advanced or metastatic DTC that progressed following prior VEGFR-targeted therapy and who are radioactive iodine (RAI) refractory or ineligible [Australian].
- **Neuroendocrine Tumours (NET):** Treatment of adults with locally advanced/unresectable or metastatic, well-differentiated extra-pancreatic (epNET) or pancreatic (pNET) neuroendocrine tumours who progressed on at least one prior systemic therapy (other than somatostatin analogues) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Monotherapy (RCC, HCC, DTC, NET):** 60 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy (RCC):** 40 mg once daily in combination with nivolumab (240 mg every 2 weeks or 480 mg every 4 weeks) [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (DTC $\ge$ 12 years)
Dosing is weight-based [Australian]:
- $\ge$ 40 kg: 60 mg once daily.
- < 40 kg: 40 mg once daily.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not crush [Australian].
- Administer on an empty stomach: Patients should not eat for at least 2 hours before and 1 hour after dose administration [Australian].

### Dose Modifications
- **General Principle:** Toxicity management involves treatment interruption and/or dose reduction.
- **Monotherapy Reductions:** From 60 mg $\rightarrow$ 40 mg $\rightarrow$ 20 mg daily [Australian].
- **Combination (with Nivolumab) Reductions:** From 40 mg $\rightarrow$ 20 mg daily $\rightarrow$ 20 mg every other day [Australian].
- **Paediatric (< 40kg) Reductions:** From 40 mg $\rightarrow$ 20 mg daily $\rightarrow$ 20 mg every other day [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Do not take a missed dose if the next dose is due in less than 12 hours [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **cabozantinib** or any excipients (including lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Perforation and Fistula:** Serious, sometimes fatal, GI perforations and fistulas have been reported. Use with extreme caution in patients with inflammatory bowel disease or GI tumour infiltration [Australian].
- **Haemorrhage:** Severe and fatal haemorrhages have occurred. Avoid in patients at high risk for severe bleeding [Australian].
- **Thromboembolic Events:** Increased risk of myocardial infarction, stroke, and venous thromboembolism (including pulmonary embolism). Discontinue if acute MI or clinically significant thromboembolic complications develop [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** Blood pressure must be well-controlled before initiation and monitored regularly. Hypertensive crisis requires permanent discontinuation [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Abnormalities in ALT, AST, and bilirubin are frequent. Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) before initiation and periodically [Australian].
- **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ):** Perform oral examinations prior to and during therapy. Maintain good oral hygiene [Australian].
- **Wound Complications:** Interruption of treatment is required at least 28 days prior to scheduled surgery [Australian].
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Rare; discontinue if suspected [Australian].
- **Cardiac Failure:** Monitor for signs/symptoms of cardiac failure throughout treatment [Australian].
- **QT Interval:** Use caution in patients with a history of QT prolongation or those taking antiarrhythmics [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequencies (Monotherapy Pool)
- **Very Common ($\ge$ 10%):** Diarrhoea, fatigue, nausea, vomiting, decreased appetite, palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia syndrome (PPES), hypertension, weight loss, dysgeusia, constipation, AST/ALT/ALP increased, anaemia, and thrombocytopenia [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Dehydration, hypomagnesaemia, hypokalaemia, proteinuria, dysphonia, dyspnoea, abdominal pain, and mucosal inflammation [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Convulsions, acute myocardial infarction, hypertensive crisis, and GI perforation [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Pneumonia, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, pulmonary embolism, and hyponatraemia were identified as common serious ADRs in RCC patients [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir) increase **cabozantinib** plasma exposure. Approach co-administration with caution [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St. John’s Wort) significantly decrease exposure. Chronic use should be avoided [Australian].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Substrates:** **Cabozantinib** may increase plasma concentrations of P-gp substrates (e.g., fexofenadine, digoxin, dabigatran etexilate) [Australian].
- **MRP2 Inhibitors:** May increase **cabozantinib** plasma levels [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Potential for plasma protein displacement; monitor INR in patients on warfarin [Australian].
- **Gastric pH Modifiers:** Co-administration with PPIs (e.g., esomeprazole) does not result in clinically significant changes in exposure [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Animal studies demonstrate embryofoetal lethality and teratogenic effects.
- **Contraception:** Effective contraception is required for both male and female patients and their partners during therapy and for at least 4 months after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment and for at least 4 months after the final dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Based on animal data, male and female fertility may be compromised [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established for patients < 18 years, except in DTC ($\ge$ 12 years). Not recommended for children < 12 years with DTC [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No specific dose adjustment recommended for patients $\ge$ 65 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution in mild/moderate impairment. Not recommended for use in severe renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild impairment. Use with caution in moderate impairment (Child-Pugh B). Not recommended in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits multiple receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) involved in tumour growth, angiogenesis, and bone remodelling, specifically MET, VEGFR1-3, AXL, RET, ROS1, TYRO3, MER, KIT, TRKB, FLT3, and TIE-2 [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations reached in 3–4 hours; high-fat meals increase Cmax and AUC [Australian].
  - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound ($\ge$ 99.7%) [Australian].
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4 [Australian].
  - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 110 hours. Excreted via faeces (54%) and urine (27%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RCC (METEOR):** Demonstrated significant improvement in PFS (7.4 vs 3.8 months) and OS (21.4 vs 16.5 months) compared to everolimus in patients previously treated with VEGFR-TKIs [Australian].
- **RCC (CABOSUN):** In treatment-naïve intermediate/poor risk patients, **cabozantinib** showed superior PFS compared to sunitinib (8.6 vs 5.3 months) [Australian].
- **HCC (CELESTIAL):** Demonstrated improved OS (10.2 vs 8.0 months) vs placebo in patients previously treated with sorafenib [Australian].
- **DTC (COSMIC-311):** Showed significant PFS benefit (11.0 vs 1.9 months) in RAI-refractory patients who progressed on prior VEGFR therapy [Australian].
- **NET (CABINET):** Significant PFS improvement observed in both pNET (13.8 vs 4.5 months) and epNET (8.5 vs 4.0 months) cohorts compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Keep in original container; shelf life information is available on the public ARTG summary [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
