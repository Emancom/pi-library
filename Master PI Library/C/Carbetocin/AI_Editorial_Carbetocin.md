# Editorial Product Information: Carbetocin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating available regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Carbetocin (a synthetic, long-acting octapeptide analogue of oxytocin).
- **Brand Names:** Carbetocin WKT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection, 100 micrograms/mL. Packaged in 1 mL clear Type I glass pre-filled syringes [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV). 
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Wockhardt Bio Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prevention of uterine atony and excessive bleeding:** Indicated following the delivery of an infant by either caesarean section or vaginal delivery [Australian-approved].
- **Administration Timing:** Must only be administered after the delivery of the infant [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** A single dose of 100 micrograms (1 mL).
- **Administration Instructions:** Administered as a slow intravenous bolus injection over 1 minute.
- **Timing by Delivery Type:**
    - **Caesarean Section:** Administer after delivery of the infant, either before or after delivery of the placenta [Australian].
    - **Vaginal Delivery:** Administer after delivery of the infant for the active management of the third stage of labour [Australian].
- **Repeat Dosing:** Carbetocin should be administered as a single dose only. No safety or efficacy data exist for repeat doses following delivery [Australian].
- **Additional Treatment:** If uterine tone remains inadequate or excessive bleeding persists, other uterotonic agents (e.g., oxytocin or ergometrine) should be initiated according to local clinical guidelines [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy/Induction of Labour:** Contraindicated prior to the delivery of the infant for any reason, including elective or medical induction of labour. Due to its long duration of action, carbetocin-induced contractions cannot be stopped by discontinuing the medication [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to oxytocin or carbetocin [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Patients with coronary artery disease, valvular heart disease, cardiomyopathy, or heart failure [Australian].
- **Paediatrics:** Not intended for use in children [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Uterine Response:** Some patients may not respond adequately to a single dose. Retained placental fragments, coagulopathy, or genital tract trauma must be ruled out in cases of persistent bleeding [Australian].
- **Eclampsia/Pre-eclampsia:** Patients must be closely monitored for blood pressure changes [Australian].
- **Fluid Balance:** Use with caution in conditions where rapid addition to extracellular water is hazardous (e.g., overburdened cardiovascular system) [Australian].
- **Specific Clinical Cautions:**
    - **Neurologic:** Use cautiously in patients with epilepsy or migraine [Australian].
    - **Respiratory:** Use cautiously in patients with asthma [Australian].
    - **Antidiuretic Effect:** While significant effects are not expected at recommended doses, hyponatremia and water intoxication should be considered due to the structural similarity to oxytocin [Australian].
- **Placental Retention:** Theoretical risk of partial placental retention or "trapping" if administered before the placenta is delivered [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed with carbetocin are generally similar in type and frequency to those seen with oxytocin.

### Frequency Data (from 122-patient placebo-controlled study)
| Adverse Event | Frequency (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (61%), Pruritus (48%), Hypotension (45%), Vomiting (41%), Flushing (34%), Abdominal pain (27%), Feeling of warmth (19%), Anaemia (17%), Tremors (16%), Back pain (13%), Dizziness (13%), Incisional abnormality (11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache (9%), Sweating (8%), Fever (6%), Tachycardia (5%), Insomnia (3%), Chills (3%), Metallic taste (2%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects (Post-marketing):** Anaphylactic reactions and shock, cardiac arrhythmias, and cardiac arrest [Australian].
- **Cardiac Disorders (Frequency Unknown):** Bradycardia, myocardial ischaemia, and QT prolongation have been reported (noting these are also reported with the structurally related oxytocin) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** While no specific carbetocin interactions are reported, it is expected to share interactions common to oxytocin [Australian].
- **Vasoconstrictors/Anaesthesia:** Severe hypertension has been reported when oxytocin followed the prophylactic use of a vasoconstrictor in conjunction with caudal block anaesthesia [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Contraindicated during pregnancy. It induces uterine contractions and may cause premature or hypertonic labour.
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts (approx. 50 times lower than plasma levels) cross into breast milk. Carbetocin is expected to be rapidly degraded by peptidases in the infant gastrointestinal tract and is not considered a significant safety concern [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not intended for use in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Not recommended for use in elderly patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Limited data available; use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Limited data available; use with caution [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Carbetocin is a long-acting synthetic analogue of oxytocin. It binds to oxytocin receptors in the uterine myometrium with similar affinity to the natural peptide, causing potent uterine contractions in the pregnant and immediate postpartum uterus [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - **Onset:** Rapid; firm contractions are obtained within 2 minutes in ~90% of patients [Australian].
    - **Duration:** Approximately 1 hour for IV bolus and 2 hours for IM injection [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is ~80% following IM administration [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Follows a 2-compartment model; distribution half-life is approximately 5.5 minutes [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily non-renal routes [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 41 minutes. Only 0.7% is excreted unchanged by the kidneys [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Elective Caesarean Section:** A multicentre, double-blind trial (n=122) showed carbetocin (100 mcg IV) was significantly more effective than placebo in preventing the need for additional oxytocic therapy (13% vs 72%). A second large trial (n=694) compared carbetocin to an 8-hour oxytocin infusion, finding a lower incidence of additional intervention in the carbetocin group (5% vs 10%; p=0.031) [Australian].
- **Vaginal Delivery:** Meta-analyses and trials suggest carbetocin is at least as effective as oxytocin infusion or the oxytocin/ergometrine combination (Syntometrine) in reducing the need for uterine massage and additional uterotonics [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Refrigerate; **do not freeze** [Australian].
- **Handling:** Keep pre-filled syringes in the outer carton to protect from light [Australian].
- **Product Stability:** Only clear, particle-free solutions should be used [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Schedule 4 (S4) - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
