# Editorial Product Information: Carboplatin

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Carboplatin, a platinum-containing antineoplastic agent (analogue of cisplatin).
- **Brand Names:** Carboplatin Accord [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01BC02 [Not specified in source, but standard for carboplatin].
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Solution for injection: 10 mg/mL.
    - Vial sizes: 50 mg/5 mL, 150 mg/15 mL, 450 mg/45 mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Accord Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Carboplatin** is indicated for the treatment of:
- Advanced stage ovarian cancer of epithelial origin.
- Small cell lung carcinoma.
- Carcinoma of the head and neck.
- Carcinoma of the testis.
- Paediatric cerebral tumours.
- Soft tissue sarcoma.
- Neuroblastoma.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Previously Untreated Adults (Normal Renal Function):** 400 mg/m² administered as a single IV infusion over 15 to 60 minutes.
- **Cycle Frequency:** Therapy should not be repeated until four weeks have elapsed since the previous course.
- **Dose Adjustments:** An initial dose reduction of 20% to 25% is recommended for patients with risk factors such as advanced age, poor performance status, or extensive prior myelosuppressive therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- **Equipment Warning:** Do **NOT** use needles, syringes, catheters, or IV administration sets containing aluminium parts. Carboplatin reacts with aluminium to form a black precipitate, which reduces potency.
- **Dilution:** Ready-to-use solution (10 mg/mL) may be further diluted in 5% glucose. Dilution in 0.9% sodium chloride is **not recommended** due to slow degradation.
- **Handling:** As a cytotoxic agent, preparation must be performed by trained personnel in designated areas using protective clothing (gloves, eye shields). Pregnant personnel should avoid handling.

### Monitoring
- Weekly blood counts are required to determine the haematologic nadir and guide future dose adjustments.

## 4. Contraindications
**Carboplatin** is contraindicated in patients with:
- Severe myelosuppression.
- Pre-existing severe renal impairment (though adjustment may allow use in mild impairment).
- History of severe allergic reactions to carboplatin, other platinum-containing compounds, or mannitol.
- Severe bleeding.
- **Pregnancy and Lactation** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Specialist Supervision:** Administration must be overseen by a physician experienced in cytotoxic therapy, with adequate diagnostic and treatment facilities available.
- **Narrow Therapeutic Index:** Toxicity is common and often necessary for therapeutic effect.
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** Dose-limiting toxicity (leucopenia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia). Risk is increased in patients with renal impairment or those receiving concurrent myelosuppressive therapy. Nadirs typically occur at days 14–28 for WBCs and days 14–21 for platelets.
- **Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis:** Allergic reactions (rash, hypotension, bronchospasm) may occur within minutes of administration. 
    - **Kounis Syndrome:** There are reports of hypersensitivity progressing to acute allergic coronary arteriospasm (Kounis syndrome), which can result in myocardial infarction.
- **Neurotoxicity and Ototoxicity:** More common in patients >65 years or those previously treated with cisplatin. Ototoxicity is cumulative.
- **Haemolytic-Uremic Syndrome (HUS):** A potentially fatal side effect. Discontinue at the first sign of microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia.
- **Other Conditions:** Cases of hepatic veno-occlusive disease, Reversible Posterior Leukoencephalopathy Syndrome (RPLS), and Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS) have been reported.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by system and frequency where available:

| System Organ Class | Very Common (>10%) | Common (1–10%) / Uncommon |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Blood/Lymphatic** | Anaemia (59%), Leucopenia (55%), Thrombocytopenia (32%) | Haemolytic anaemia (sometimes fatal), HUS |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea/Vomiting (53%), Nausea only (25%) | Diarrhoea (6%), Constipation (3%), Mucositis |
| **Renal/Urinary** | Decreased CrCl (25%), Elevated Uric Acid (25%), Elevated BUN (16%) | Acute renal failure (rare) |
| **Metabolic** | Decreased Mg (37%), Decreased K (16%) | Decreased Ca (5%), Decreased Na |
| **Nervous System** | | Peripheral neuropathy (6%), Dysgeusia (<1%) |
| **Ear/Labyrinth** | Subclinical hearing loss (15%) | Clinical ototoxicity/Tinnitus (1%) |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Elevated ALP (30%), Elevated AST (15%) | Elevated Bilirubin (4%), Veno-occlusive disease |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Myelosuppression-related infections (sepsis/septic shock), haemorrhage, anaphylaxis, and secondary malignancies (APL/AML/MDS) reported years after therapy.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Aluminium:** Physical incompatibility; forms a black precipitate.
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Concurrent use with aminoglycosides or other nephrotoxic medicines increases the risk of renal toxicity and ototoxicity.
- **Phenytoin/Fosphenytoin:** Carboplatin may decrease serum phenytoin levels, potentially leading to breakthrough seizures.
- **Vaccines:** Avoid live attenuated vaccines in immunocompromised patients due to the risk of serious or fatal infection. Response to killed/inactivated vaccines may be diminished.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Carboplatin is embryotoxic and mutagenic. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for at least 7 months after therapy. Men should not father a child during and for at least 4 months after treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Carboplatin and its metabolites are identified in human milk. Breastfeeding is **contraindicated** during therapy and for **one month** following the final dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May cause reduction in oogenesis and spermatogenesis; fertility preservation advice should be sought prior to treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated for paediatric cerebral tumours and neuroblastoma. Specific dosage recommendations are not provided in the source PI; clinicians are advised to refer to current literature.
- **Elderly Use:** Patients >65 years are more susceptible to peripheral neuropathy and may have decreased renal/haematopoietic reserve.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage must be adjusted based on Creatinine Clearance (CrCl):
    - **CrCl >40 mL/min:** 400 mg/m²
    - **CrCl 20–39 mL/min:** 250 mg/m²
    - **CrCl 0–19 mL/min:** 150 mg/m²
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically detailed, though hepatotoxicity is reported at high doses.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A platinum analogue that creates DNA intrastrand and interstrand crosslinks, interfering with DNA synthesis and leading to cytotoxicity.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Low initial protein binding (0–29% in first 4 hours); increases to 85–89% by 24 hours.
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic decay. Initial half-life (free platinum) ~90 minutes; terminal half-life ~6 hours.
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal (glomerular filtration). Approximately 65% is eliminated in urine within 24 hours (32% as unchanged drug).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Protection:** Do not freeze. Protect from light.
- **Stability:** Once diluted in 5% glucose, the solution is stable for 24 hours if stored at 2–8°C. As it contains no antimicrobial agent, it is for single use in one patient only.
