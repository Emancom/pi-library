# Editorial Product Information: cinnarizine + dimenhydrinate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **cinnarizine + dimenhydrinate**
- Chemical class: Not specified in available sources
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Short‑term, symptomatic treatment of vertigo of various causes in adults who have not responded to alternative treatments.  
- Duration of treatment is generally limited; reassessment is recommended if treatment beyond four weeks is being considered.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dosing regimen: one tablet three times daily. (Each tablet in the referenced formulation contains cinnarizine 20 mg + dimenhydrinate 40 mg.)
- Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration required to control symptoms. The recommended dose should not be exceeded.

### Dosing by indication
- Not specified in available sources beyond the general indication for symptomatic vertigo.

### Administration instructions
- Swallow tablets whole with some liquid after meals to minimise gastric irritation.
- In general, treatment duration should not exceed four weeks without reassessment of risk/benefit.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **cinnarizine**, **dimenhydrinate**, **diphenhydramine**, other antihistamines of similar structure, or to any excipients.
- Severe renal impairment: creatinine clearance ≤ 25 mL/min.
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- Angle‑closure glaucoma.
- Convulsions (seizure disorders).
- Suspicion of raised intracranial pressure.
- Alcohol abuse.
- Urinary retention due to urethroprostatic disorders.
- Children under 6 years of age (risk of fatal respiratory depression and serious psychiatric/CNS events).
- The product should not be given to children or adolescents under 18 years of age (safety and efficacy not established).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / critical warnings
- **Contraindicated in children under 6 years** due to risk of fatal respiratory depression and serious psychiatric/CNS events.  
- **Use is contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment and in patients with creatinine clearance ≤ 25 mL/min.**

### General warnings and precautions
- Extrapyramidal reactions: extrapyramidal effects including tremor, dyskinesia, parkinsonism, tardive dyskinesia and akathisia have been reported rarely. Risk factors include older age (> 60 years), female sex, high cumulative dose (> 150 mg/day of cinnarizine) and prolonged exposure. Symptoms may appear after weeks to years of exposure and can begin after drug cessation; some cases have been irreversible. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration; discontinue immediately if abnormal involuntary movements or parkinsonian features occur.
- Do not administer to patients with pre‑existing extrapyramidal movement disorders (e.g. Parkinson’s disease).
- Use with caution in hypotensive patients (product does not markedly reduce blood pressure but may aggravate hypotension).
- Use with caution in conditions that may be worsened by anticholinergic effects: raised intra‑ocular pressure, pyloro‑duodenal obstruction, prostatic hypertrophy with urinary retention, hypertension, hyperthyroidism, severe coronary heart disease.
- Hepatic impairment: contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment; plasma concentrations of parent drugs and half‑lives may increase with hepatic dysfunction.
- Renal impairment: contraindicated when creatinine clearance ≤ 25 mL/min; exercise caution in mild to moderate renal impairment.
- Elderly: no routine dose adjustment specified, but increased age may increase risk of extrapyramidal adverse events.
- Paediatric use: contraindicated under 6 years; safety and efficacy not established for children and adolescents < 18 years — product should not be used in patients under 18 years.
- Treatment duration: generally should not exceed four weeks; reassess risk/benefit for longer therapy.
- Effects on ability to drive/use machines: may cause drowsiness, particularly at treatment initiation; patients should be cautioned not to drive or operate machinery if affected.

### Monitoring requirements
- Reassess therapy if treatment extends beyond four weeks.
- Monitor for early signs of extrapyramidal reactions and discontinue if they occur.
- Consider monitoring hepatic and renal function when clinically indicated, particularly in patients with known impairment (no formal monitoring schedule specified in available sources).

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The most frequently observed adverse reactions in clinical trials were somnolence (drowsiness, tiredness, fatigue, daze), dry mouth and headache. These were usually mild and often resolved within a few days.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Both components are extensively metabolised by hepatic cytochrome P450 enzymes.
- In vitro data indicate cinnarizine metabolism involves CYP2D6 (predominant), CYP2B6, CYP2C9 and CYP1A2. Diphenhydramine/dimenhydrinate is also metabolised in part by CYP2D6 and is a known inhibitor of CYP2D6.
- Co‑administration with CYP2D6 substrates may lead to interactions; use caution with agents metabolised by CYP2D6 such as tricyclic antidepressants, selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, neuroleptics, some beta‑blockers and certain anti‑arrhythmics (management: consider monitoring for increased effects or adverse reactions and dose adjustment as appropriate).
- Dimenhydrinate contains 8‑chlorotheophylline (a caffeine derivative) which is metabolised by CYP1A2; it may inhibit or potentiate the effects of drugs metabolised via CYP1A2 (e.g. diltiazem, verapamil, olanzapine, oral contraceptives, omeprazole) — this interaction is described as possible.
- Dimenhydrinate is dialyzable; however haemoperfusion with activated charcoal is considered a more effective elimination measure in overdose. Dialysability of cinnarizine is unknown.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Additive sedative effects with other central nervous system depressants including alcohol, barbiturates, narcotic analgesics and tranquillisers — advise patients to avoid alcohol and use caution with sedatives.
- Anticholinergic and sedative effects may be potentiated by monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs).
- Procarbazine may enhance the effects of cinnarizine/dimenhydrinate.
- Enhanced effects with antihypertensive agents, ephedrine, and other anticholinergic agents (e.g. atropine, tricyclic antidepressants).
- Concomitant use with medicines that prolong the QT interval (e.g. Class Ia and Class III anti‑arrhythmics) should be avoided.
- Cinnarizine may oppose the effects of betahistine; concomitant administration with betahistine should be avoided.
- May mask ototoxic symptoms associated with aminoglycoside antibiotics and may interfere with the response to cutaneous allergy tests.

### Clinically significant interactions — management notes
- Avoid co‑administration with strong CYP2D6 substrates when possible; if required, monitor closely for enhanced effects or toxicity.
- Avoid alcohol and other CNS depressants or counsel patients regarding increased sedation.
- Do not combine with betahistine.
- Avoid concomitant QT‑prolonging medicines when possible.
- Consider alternative antimotion/vertigo therapy in patients on multiple interacting medications.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category (as stated in available sources): Category B2 [Australian].
- Clinical recommendations: use is not recommended during pregnancy.
- Basis and animal data: limited human data; animal studies inadequate or limited but available data do not indicate increased teratogenic risk. At maternal‑toxic doses in rats, cinnarizine decreased litter size, increased resorptions and reduced pup birth weight. Cinnarizine was detected in the fetus in guinea pig studies (fetal exposure < 25% of maternal dose). Dimenhydrinate/diphenhydramine teratogenic risk considered low in available animal studies. Dimenhydrinate is suspected to have oxytocic effects and may shorten labour.

### Breastfeeding
- Both cinnarizine and dimenhydrinate are excreted in human breast milk.
- Recommendation: not recommended during breastfeeding.

### Fertility
- Effects on fertility are not known from available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Contraindicated in children under 6 years (due to risk of fatal respiratory depression and psychiatric/CNS events).
- Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents under 18 years have not been established; product should not be given to patients under 18 years.

### Elderly
- No specific dose adjustment is mandated in available sources.
- Patients > 60 years are at increased risk of cinnarizine‑related extrapyramidal adverse events; use caution and the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration.

### Renal impairment
- Contraindicated in severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance ≤ 25 mL/min).
- Use with caution in mild to moderate renal impairment. (Diphenhydramine is reported to be completely excreted renally; patients with severe renal impairment were excluded from clinical development.)

### Hepatic impairment
- Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment.
- Use with caution in mild to moderate hepatic impairment. Both active components are extensively metabolised by hepatic CYP enzymes and plasma concentrations and elimination half‑lives may increase in hepatic dysfunction (increased exposure shown for diphenhydramine in cirrhosis).

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- The two components have complementary central and peripheral actions on the vestibular system:
  - Dimenhydrinate (a chlorotheophylline salt of diphenhydramine) is a potent antagonist at histamine H1 receptors and also blocks muscarinic acetylcholine receptors; it is thought to exert predominantly central effects at vestibular nuclei and related brainstem centers.
  - Cinnarizine is an antagonist at histamine H1 and H4 receptors and at dopamine D2 receptors; it is a less potent inhibitor of pressure‑sensitive potassium channels and serotonin 5‑HT2 receptors. Cinnarizine mainly acts peripherally on the labyrinth.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Combined, the two agents provide dual mechanisms targeting peripheral vestibular structures (cinnarizine) and central vestibular pathways (dimenhydrinate), intended to reduce vertigo symptoms and associated vegetative symptoms (e.g. nausea).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Not specified in available sources (no Tmax or bioavailability data provided).
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism: Both components are extensively metabolised by hepatic CYP enzymes. Cinnarizine metabolism involves CYP2D6 (predominant), CYP2B6, CYP2C9 and CYP1A2. Dimenhydrinate/diphenhydramine is metabolised via CYP pathways and can inhibit CYP2D6.
- Elimination: Diphenhydramine (component of dimenhydrinate) is reported to be excreted renally; dimenhydrinate is dialyzable but haemoperfusion with activated charcoal is more effective for elimination in overdose. No data on cinnarizine dialysability. Half‑life data are not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- The efficacy and safety of the fixed‑dose combination (cinnarizine 20 mg + dimenhydrinate 40 mg, administered three times daily) were evaluated in a series of randomized controlled clinical trials summarized in multiple publications. In total, nine randomized controlled trials conducted in adult patients with vertigo (central, peripheral or mixed origin) were reported; overall about 1,235 adult patients were enrolled across studies with mean ages in the early 50s, majority female.
- Key pivotal trials:
  - Multicentre, double‑blind, randomized, parallel‑group trial comparing the fixed‑dose combination (FDC) to monotherapy with cinnarizine 20 mg and dimenhydrinate 40 mg (each given three times daily). Primary endpoint: change from baseline in mean vertigo score (MVS) at 4 weeks (MVS derived from patient‑rated intensity of 12 vertigo symptoms on a 5‑point VAS). Results: FDC produced significantly greater reduction in MVS at week 1 and week 4 compared with either monotherapy; a larger proportion of patients on FDC were symptom‑free at week 4 versus monotherapy.
  - Multicentre, double‑blind, randomized, four‑arm trial comparing FDC to higher‑dose monotherapies (cinnarizine 50 mg, dimenhydrinate 100 mg) and placebo. Primary endpoint: change in MVS at 4 weeks. Results: FDC demonstrated significantly greater reduction in MVS at week 4 than both higher‑dose monotherapies and placebo. The proportion of patients with complete resolution of vertigo at week 4 was higher with FDC than with monotherapies or placebo.
- Across trials, secondary outcomes included vegetative symptom scores (e.g. nausea), balance tests (Unterberger, Romberg, lateral sway) and nystagmus assessments; improvements in nausea were more pronounced with the FDC in some studies. Most supportive RCTs used similar endpoints and 4‑week treatment durations.
- Safety summary in trials: the FDC had an adverse event profile dominated by sedation/somnolence, dry mouth and headache; overall tolerability assessments were recorded by participants and investigators.

## 13. Overdose
- Clinical features of overdose may include drowsiness, dizziness, ataxia, anticholinergic signs (dry mouth, flushed skin, mydriasis, urinary retention), tachycardia, pyrexia, headache, and in larger ingestions convulsions, hallucinations, excitement, respiratory depression, hypertension, tremor and coma. In children, overdose manifestations may include excitation, ataxia, incoordination, athetosis, hallucinations and reversible cognitive deficits; adults may become drowsy and may progress to coma.
- Management:
  - Treatment is primarily supportive and symptomatic.
  - Monitor respiratory and circulatory function; control body temperature as pyrexia may occur.
  - Cramp‑like symptoms may be treated with short‑acting barbiturates where appropriate.
  - In pronounced central anticholinergic toxicity, physostigmine may be given after an appropriate test dose; recommended dosing (as per available sources): 0.03 mg/kg IV slowly (adults max 2 mg; children max 0.5 mg) — administer with caution and appropriate monitoring.
  - Dimenhydrinate is dialyzable; haemoperfusion with activated charcoal provides more effective elimination in overdose. Dialysability of cinnarizine is unknown.
  - For local poison information and management guidance: contact local poisons information services. [Australian] Poisons Information Centre: 13 11 26 (as stated in available sources).
