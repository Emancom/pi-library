# Editorial Product Information: Ceftizoxime

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ceftizoxime sodium (a sterile, semisynthetic, broad-spectrum, beta-lactamase resistant third-generation cephalosporin).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Cefizox** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01DD07.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Lyophilized powder for injection (vials): 500 mg, 1 gram, 2 grams [US].
    - Piggyback vials for IV admixture: 1 gram, 2 grams [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) or Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Fujisawa Healthcare, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Ceftizoxime is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by susceptible strains of designated microorganisms in the following conditions [US-approved]:
- **Lower Respiratory Tract Infections:** e.g., caused by *Klebsiella* spp., *E. coli*, *H. influenzae*, *S. aureus*, *S. pneumoniae*.
- **Urinary Tract Infections:** Including those caused by *P. aeruginosa*, *Proteus* spp., and *Serratia* spp.
- **Gonorrhea:** Uncomplicated cervical and urethral infections (*N. gonorrhoeae*).
- **Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID):** Note: Does not cover *Chlamydia trachomatis*; additional coverage must be added if suspected.
- **Intra-Abdominal Infections:** Including peritonitis.
- **Septicemia.**
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections.**
- **Bone and Joint Infections.**
- **Meningitis:** Caused by *H. influenzae* and *S. pneumoniae*.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
The typical adult dose is **1 to 2 grams every 8 to 12 hours**.

| Type of Infection | Daily Dose (Grams) | Frequency and Route |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Uncomplicated UTI | 1 g | 500 mg q12h (IM/IV) |
| Other Sites (Moderate) | 2–3 g | 1 g q8–12h (IM/IV) |
| Severe or Refractory | 3–6 g | 1 g q8h or 2 g q8–12h (IM/IV) |
| Life-Threatening | 9–12 g | 3–4 g q8h (IV) |

*Note: For life-threatening infections, doses up to 2 grams every 4 hours have been administered [US].*

### Specific Indication Dosing
- **Uncomplicated Gonorrhea:** 1 gram IM as a single dose.
- **PID:** 2 grams every 8 hours IV (Total 6 g/day).

### Administration Instructions
- **IM Injection:** Inject into a large muscle mass. Doses of 2 grams should be divided and given at two separate sites.
- **IV Bolus:** Administer slowly over 3 to 5 minutes.
- **IV Infusion:** Dilute in 50–100 mL of compatible fluid (e.g., 0.9% NaCl, D5W, LR) and administer over a specified duration.
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstituted solutions are stable for 24 hours at room temperature and 96 hours when refrigerated (5ºC).

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to ceftizoxime sodium or the cephalosporin class of antibiotics [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Cross-sensitivity between beta-lactam antibiotics (penicillins and cephalosporins) occurs in up to 10% of penicillin-allergic patients. Discontinue immediately if an allergic reaction occurs.
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents; severity may range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis.
- **Prothrombin Activity:** Cephalosporins may lead to a fall in prothrombin activity. Risk factors include renal/hepatic impairment, poor nutrition, and concurrent anticoagulant therapy. Monitor prothrombin time and administer Vitamin K if needed.
- **Seizure Activity:** Particularly in patients with renal impairment where dosage has not been appropriately reduced.
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Ceftizoxime is generally well tolerated.

| Frequency | System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–5%)** | Local | Injection site pain, burning, phlebitis (IV), induration. |
| | Hypersensitivity | Rash, pruritus, fever. |
| | Hepatic | Transient AST, ALT, and alkaline phosphatase elevation. |
| | Hematologic | Transient eosinophilia, thrombocytosis; positive Coombs test. |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | GI | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea. |
| | Renal | Transient BUN and creatinine elevation. |
| | Hematologic | Anemia, leukopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia. |
| | Urogenital | Vaginitis. |
| **Rare/Class Effect** | Systemic | Anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** Concurrent administration slows renal tubular secretion, resulting in higher and more prolonged serum ceftizoxime levels [US].
- **Aminoglycosides:** While not specifically reported with ceftizoxime, the combination of other cephalosporins and aminoglycosides has been associated with increased nephrotoxicity. Renal function should be monitored during concurrent use.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **US Category B.** Reproduction studies in rats and rabbits showed no evidence of fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Labor and Delivery:** Safety has not been established.
- **Breastfeeding:** Ceftizoxime is excreted in human milk in low concentrations. Caution is advised when administering to nursing women.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric Use
- Safety and efficacy have not been established for infants under 6 months of age.
- **Dosing (6 months+):** 50 mg/kg every 6 to 8 hours. Total daily dose may reach 200 mg/kg (not to exceed the maximum adult dose).

### Geriatric Use
- Clinical studies did not identify specific differences between elderly and younger patients. Dose selection should be cautious, typically starting at the lower end of the range, due to higher frequencies of renal or hepatic impairment.

### Renal Impairment
Dosage modification is required based on creatinine clearance (CrCl). An initial loading dose of 500 mg–1 gram is recommended.

| CrCl (mL/min) | Renal Function | Dose for Less Severe Infection | Dose for Life-Threatening Infection |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 79–50 | Mild | 500 mg q8h | 0.75–1.5 g q8h |
| 49–5 | Moderate to Severe | 250–500 mg q12h | 0.5–1 g q12h |
| 4–0 | Dialysis Patients | 500 mg q48h or 250 mg q24h | 0.5–1 g q48h or 0.5 g q24h |

*Note: In dialysis patients, dosing should be timed to occur at the end of the dialysis session.*

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Protein Binding:** Approximately 30%.
    - **Metabolism:** Not metabolized.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted virtually unchanged by the kidneys within 24 hours.
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 1.7 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Achieves therapeutic levels in CSF (inflamed meninges), bile, bone, heart, and peritoneal fluid.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** The source document indicates clinical efficacy in serious infections including meningitis (caused by *H. influenzae* and *S. pneumoniae*), septicemia, and intra-abdominal infections. It is noted as effective in debilitated, immunosuppressed, or neutropenic patients. Specific pivotal trial identifiers were not provided in the source documentation.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unreconstituted Powder:** Store at controlled room temperature (15°C to 30°C / 59°F to 86°F) and protect from excessive light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature or 96 hours if refrigerated at 5°C (41°F).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as **Rx-only** (Prescription only).
