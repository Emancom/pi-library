# Editorial Product Information: Conjugated Estrogens / Bazedoxifene

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for full prescribing details and legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Conjugated Estrogens (CE) and Bazedoxifene (as acetate).
- **Chemical Class:** Estrogen and Selective Estrogen Receptor Modulator (SERM) / Estrogen Agonist/Antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **DUAVEE** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Film-coated tablets containing 0.45 mg conjugated estrogens and 20 mg bazedoxifene (equivalent to 22.6 mg bazedoxifene acetate) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Inc. / Wyeth Pharmaceuticals LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**DUAVEE** is indicated for the treatment of postmenopausal women who possess a uterus for the following:
- **Vasomotor Symptoms:** Treatment of moderate to severe symptoms (e.g., hot flushes) associated with menopause [US].
- **Osteoporosis Prevention:** Prevention of postmenopausal osteoporosis [US]. 
    - *Limitation of Use:* When used solely for osteoporosis prevention, it should be reserved for women at significant risk, and non-estrogen therapies should be carefully considered [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One tablet (0.45 mg CE / 20 mg bazedoxifene) taken orally once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole and may be taken without regard to meals [US]. 
- **Duration of Therapy:** Use for the shortest duration consistent with treatment goals and individual risks. Therapy should be re-evaluated periodically to determine continued necessity [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered, unless it is nearly time for the next scheduled dose. Two doses should not be taken at the same time [US].
- **Supplementation:** Women treated for osteoporosis prevention should ensure adequate intake of supplemental calcium and vitamin D if dietary intake is insufficient [US].

## 4. Contraindications
**DUAVEE** is contraindicated in women with:
- Undiagnosed abnormal uterine bleeding [US].
- Known, suspected, or a history of breast cancer [US].
- Known or suspected estrogen-dependent neoplasia [US].
- Active or history of venous thromboembolism (DVT, pulmonary embolism) [US].
- Active or history of arterial thromboembolism (e.g., stroke, myocardial infarction) [US].
- Hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema) to estrogens, bazedoxifene, or any excipients [US].
- Known hepatic impairment or disease [US].
- Known protein C, protein S, or antithrombin deficiency, or other thrombophilic disorders [US].
- Pregnancy or potential for pregnancy [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Endometrial Cancer:** Unopposed estrogen increases the risk of endometrial cancer in women with a uterus. **DUAVEE** contains bazedoxifene to reduce the risk of endometrial hyperplasia, but clinical surveillance for abnormal bleeding is essential.
- **Cardiovascular Disorders:** Estrogen therapy should not be used for the prevention of cardiovascular disease. The WHI study reported increased risks of stroke and DVT.
- **Probable Dementia:** Estrogen therapy should not be used for the prevention of dementia. The WHIMS study reported an increased risk in postmenopausal women $\ge$ 65 years of age.

### General Warnings
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Discontinue immediately if VTE is suspected. If feasible, discontinue 4–6 weeks prior to major surgery or during periods of prolonged immobilization [US].
- **Malignant Neoplasms:** Beyond endometrial cancer, clinicians must monitor for breast and ovarian cancer. While CE (0.625 mg) alone did not increase invasive breast cancer in the WHI study, the risk with **DUAVEE** is unknown [US].
- **Gallbladder Disease:** Estrogens increase the risk of gallbladder disease requiring surgery [US].
- **Visual Abnormalities:** Discontinue pending examination if sudden partial or complete loss of vision, proptosis, diplopia, or migraine occurs [US].
- **Hypertriglyceridemia:** Estrogens may elevate plasma triglycerides, potentially leading to pancreatitis in women with pre-existing hypertriglyceridemia [US].
- **Monitoring:** Monitor thyroid function in women on thyroid replacement therapy, as estrogens increase thyroid-binding globulin (TBG) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions with an incidence $\ge$ 5% and more common than placebo in clinical trials include:

| Adverse Reaction | DUAVEE % (N=1224) | Placebo % (N=1069) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Muscle spasms | 9% | 6% |
| Nausea | 8% | 5% |
| Diarrhea | 8% | 5% |
| Dyspepsia | 7% | 6% |
| Abdominal pain upper | 7% | 5% |
| Oropharyngeal pain | 7% | 6% |
| Dizziness | 5% | 3% |
| Neck pain | 5% | 4% |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Include VTE, stroke, myocardial infarction, endometrial malignancy, and gallbladder disease [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with strong inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole) increases exposure to bazedoxifene (approx. 40%) and CE [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Inducers (e.g., St. John's Wort, phenobarbital, rifampin) may decrease estrogen plasma concentrations, potentially reducing efficacy or altering uterine bleeding profiles [US].
- **UGT Inducers:** Bazedoxifene is metabolised by UGT enzymes. Inducers (e.g., rifampin, phenytoin) may decrease bazedoxifene exposure, potentially increasing the risk of endometrial hyperplasia [US].
- **Atorvastatin:** No clinically significant pharmacokinetic interaction was observed when co-administered with bazedoxifene [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **DUAVEE** is contraindicated. Estrogen/SERM exposure during pregnancy may cause fetal harm. Animal data for bazedoxifene show reduced foetal weights and skeletal anomalies at maternally toxic doses [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Estrogens are excreted in human milk and can reduce milk production. It is unknown if bazedoxifene is excreted in human milk [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate that bazedoxifene may adversely affect female fertility; however, human clinical fertility studies have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for use in children [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not recommended for women $>$ 75 years of age. An increased risk of probable dementia was noted in women $\ge$ 65 years [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The pharmacokinetics of **DUAVEE** have not been evaluated in this population; use is not recommended [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated. Bazedoxifene exposure (AUC) increases significantly in mild (Child-Pugh A, 143%), moderate (Child-Pugh B, 109%), and severe (Child-Pugh C, 268%) impairment [US].
- **Body Mass Index (BMI):** Systemic exposure to bazedoxifene and CE is lower in obese subjects (BMI $\ge$ 30). This may lead to decreased protection against endometrial hyperplasia [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** CE provides estrogenic effects to alleviate menopausal symptoms and prevent bone loss. Bazedoxifene acts as an estrogen antagonist in the uterus, reducing the risk of CE-induced endometrial hyperplasia without the need for a progestin [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bazedoxifene absolute bioavailability is ~6%. CE components are well-absorbed from the GI tract. Food (high-fat meal) increases bazedoxifene AUC by 25% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Bazedoxifene is 98–99% protein-bound. CE components circulate bound to SHBG and albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** CE is metabolised by CYP3A4. Bazedoxifene undergoes extensive metabolism via UGT enzymes, primarily to bazedoxifene-5-glucuronide [US].
    - **Elimination:** CE components are excreted in urine. Bazedoxifene is primarily eliminated via biliary excretion into the feces (~85%) [US]. Half-lives: Total estrone ~17 hours; Bazedoxifene ~30 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Vasomotor Symptoms (Study 3):** A 12-week study (n=318) showed significant reductions in the frequency and severity of hot flushes compared to placebo at Weeks 4 and 12 [US].
- **Osteoporosis (Studies 1 & 2):** 24-month and 12-month trials demonstrated significant increases in bone mineral density (BMD) at the lumbar spine and total hip compared to placebo in postmenopausal women [US].
- **Endometrial Safety:** In 2-year studies, the incidence of endometrial hyperplasia or malignancy was $<$ 1% in women treated with **DUAVEE** [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Special Handling:** Dispense in the original package to protect from moisture. Do not remove tablets from blisters until immediately before use. After opening the foil pouch, the product must be used within 60 days [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine (Rx only) [US].
