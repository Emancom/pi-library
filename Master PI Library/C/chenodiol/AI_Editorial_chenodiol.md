# Editorial Product Information: Chenodiol

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA) 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of chenodiol product information for healthcare professionals. It synthesizes regulatory data to provide a concise, clinically relevant reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Chenodiol (also known as chenodeoxycholic acid).
- **Chemical Class:** Primary bile acid.
- **Brand Names:** CTEXLI [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A05AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 250 mg [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mirum Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cerebrotendinous Xanthomatosis (CTX):** Indicated for the treatment of CTX in adult patients [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosage:** The recommended dose is 250 mg administered three times daily [United States].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets should be swallowed whole.
    - May be administered with or without food [United States].
- **Pre-treatment Assessment:** Baseline liver transaminases (ALT, AST) and total bilirubin must be obtained prior to initiation [United States].
- **Monitoring and Titration:**
    - Monitor liver transaminases and total bilirubin annually and as clinically indicated [United States].
    - **Dose Modification:** Treatment must be interrupted if ALT/AST levels exceed 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or if total bilirubin exceeds 2 times ULN. Therapy may resume once levels return to baseline. If abnormalities persist or recur, permanent discontinuation should be considered [United States].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, patients should skip the missed dose and resume the regular schedule. A double dose should not be taken [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Chenodiol is associated with potential liver injury. Risks may be elevated in patients with pre-existing liver disease or bile duct abnormalities.
- **Lithocholic Acid Sulfation:** Patients who are "poor sulfators" of lithocholic acid (a metabolite of chenodiol) may be at an increased risk of serum aminotransferase elevations [United States].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** Patients should be advised to report symptoms of hepatic dysfunction (e.g., jaundice, dark urine, abdominal pain, fatigue, pruritus). Immediate discontinuation is required if clinical signs of hepatotoxicity occur [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reaction data is primarily derived from a crossover trial in patients with CTX (Trial 1).

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhea (36%), Headache (21%), Abdominal pain (14%), Constipation (14%), Hypertension (14%), Muscular weakness (14%), Upper respiratory tract infection (14%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | ALT elevations >3x ULN (7%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Clinically significant hepatotoxicity [United States].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Hypersensitivity reactions (including facial swelling, pruritus, rash, and urticaria) have been reported [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Bile Acid Sequestering Agents (e.g., cholestyramine, colestipol):** May decrease the intestinal absorption and efficacy of chenodiol. Concomitant use should be avoided [United States].
- **Aluminum-based Antacids:** May reduce the absorption of chenodiol. Concomitant use should be avoided [United States].
- **Coumarin and Derivatives:** Chenodiol may enhance the anticoagulant effect, leading to prolonged prothrombin time and hemorrhage risk. If co-administration is necessary, monitor prothrombin time closely and adjust anticoagulant dosage as required [United States].
- **In vitro findings:** Chenodiol may upregulate CYP3A4 mRNA and inhibit OATP1B1 and OATP1B3; however, the clinical significance is currently unknown [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (Current US labeling uses Risk Summary).
    - **Summary:** Published case reports over decades have not identified an increased risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. However, animal studies in non-human primates at human-equivalent doses resulted in serious hepatic, renal, and adrenal lesions in fetuses [United States]. 
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no available data regarding the presence of chenodiol in human or animal milk, or its effects on milk production or the breastfed infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need and potential risks [United States].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats at 0.2 to 0.6 times the recommended human dose did not show impairment of fertility [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in pediatric patients [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include a sufficient number of patients aged 65 and older to determine whether they respond differently than younger subjects [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; pre-existing liver disease increases the risk of hepatotoxicity [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** In CTX, a deficiency in sterol 27-hydroxylase (CYP27A1) disrupts bile acid synthesis. Chenodiol replaces deficient endogenous chenodeoxycholic acid, expands the bile acid pool, restores farnesoid X receptor (FXR) activation, and downregulates CYP7A1. This suppresses the production of atypical bile acids and alcohols (e.g., cholestanol and 23S-pentol) [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median Tmax is 3 hours (range 0.5–8 hours).
    - **Distribution:** Resides mainly in the enterohepatic circulation. Volume of distribution is 0.36 L/kg. Plasma protein binding is ~98%.
    - **Metabolism:** Converted in the liver to taurine and glycine conjugates. In the colon, bacteria convert it to lithocholic acid (partially hepatotoxic).
    - **Elimination:** Total apparent clearance is 20 L/h. Excreted primarily in feces (80% as lithocholate) [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Trial 1 (NCT 04270682):** A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover withdrawal study in 14 patients (aged 16–55).
- **Primary Outcomes:** Treatment with chenodiol 250 mg TID resulted in significant reductions in plasma cholestanol (estimated treatment difference -8.5 µg/mL) and urine 23S-pentol (estimated treatment difference -29,321 ng/mL) compared to placebo [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [United States].
- **Special Handling:** Keep out of reach of children.

- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
