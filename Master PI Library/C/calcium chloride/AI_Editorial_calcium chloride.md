# Editorial Product Information: Calcium Chloride-based Haemofiltration and Replacement Solutions

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from official regulatory documents provided by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) for ACCUSOL 35 and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for PRISMASOL and PHOXILLUM. Information has been integrated to provide the most comprehensive clinical guidance, with specific regional differences noted in brackets where applicable.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
This PI covers sterile, non-pyrogenic electrolyte solutions used for continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT). The primary active moieties include:

*   **Calcium chloride dihydrate**
*   **Magnesium chloride hexahydrate**
*   **Sodium chloride**
*   **Sodium bicarbonate**
*   **Potassium chloride** (in specific formulations)
*   **Glucose monohydrate / Dextrose anhydrous** (in specific formulations)
*   **Sodium phosphate dibasic dihydrate** (specific to PHOXILLUM [FDA])

These products are typically supplied as two-chamber bags that must be mixed immediately prior to administration to ensure a clear, physiological replacement fluid.

## 2. Approved Indications
These solutions are indicated for use in adult and pediatric patients [FDA] as a replacement solution in Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT). Specific clinical applications include:
*   Treatment of acute kidney injury (AKI) of any origin [TGA].
*   Replacement of plasma volume removed via ultrafiltration.
*   Correction of electrolyte and acid-base imbalances.
*   Management of drug poisoning where the toxin is a dialyzable substance [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
Contraindications may vary depending on the specific electrolyte concentration of the formulation used:
*   **General:** Known hypersensitivity to any of the solution components [FDA].
*   **Electrolyte-Dependent [TGA]:** 
    *   Hypokalemia (for potassium-free or low-potassium formulations).
    *   Hyperkalemia (for formulations with higher potassium concentrations).
    *   Metabolic alkalosis.
*   **CRRT-Specific [TGA]:**
    *   Renal failure accompanied by extreme protein catabolism where CRRT cannot sufficiently manage uremic symptoms.
    *   Insufficient blood flow or arterial pressure at the vascular access site.
    *   Clinical states with a high risk of hemorrhage (e.g., systemic anticoagulation).

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Clinical Monitoring
Strict monitoring of hemodynamic status, fluid balance (weight), and serum electrolytes is essential. Particular attention must be paid to levels of phosphate, calcium, magnesium, and potassium to prevent severe imbalances [FDA, TGA]. Acid-base status (blood pH and bicarbonate) must be regularly assessed.

### Fluid and Electrolyte Abnormalities
Hyper- or hypohydration may occur if fluid balance is not precisely calculated. This can lead to congestive cardiac failure or circulatory collapse. If calcium carbonate precipitation is observed in the solution or tubing, the lines must be replaced immediately [TGA].

### Glucose Management
Treatment may significantly impact blood glucose levels. Hypoglycemia has been reported with the use of glucose-free solutions [TGA]. Diabetic patients may require frequent monitoring and adjustment of antidiabetic medications [FDA].

### Administration Precautions
Solutions must be mixed thoroughly before use. If the inter-chamber seal is not opened and only the buffer chamber is infused, severe metabolic alkalosis may result [TGA]. Replacement fluids should be warmed to approximately body temperature (37°C) using appropriate equipment; they must never be infused below room temperature [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Administration Instructions
Solutions are for intravenous use only and must be administered under the direction of a physician experienced in CRRT. They are infused via the arterial or venous lines of the extracorporeal circuit in pre-dilution mode, post-dilution mode, or a combination of both [FDA, TGA].

### Dosing Considerations
The dosage must be individualized based on the patient’s clinical status, body weight, and metabolic requirements. 
*   **Adult Dosing [TGA]:** A typical effluent volume is 20 – 35 mL/kg/h to achieve a delivered dose of 20 – 25 mL/kg/h. The maximum effluent volume is 75 L/day.
*   **Pediatric Dosing [FDA]:** Safety and effectiveness have been established in pediatric patients from newborns to 17 years of age.
*   **Duration:** The maximum intended period of use for a single CRRT line is 72 hours [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions may be related to the CRRT procedure or the replacement solution itself:
*   **Metabolic:** Electrolyte disturbances (hypophosphatemia, hypoglycemia, hypokalemia), metabolic alkalosis, or acidosis.
*   **Vascular/Cardiac:** Hypotension, hypertension, fluid imbalance (hypervolemia or hypovolemia).
*   **Procedural:** Bleeding, clotting of the circuit, infection at the access site, and air embolism (associated with precipitate formation) [TGA].
*   **General:** Muscle cramps, nausea, vomiting, lethargy, and headache (often signs of alkalosis).

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Dialyzable Drugs:** CRRT may reduce blood concentrations of drugs, particularly those with low protein binding and a small volume of distribution. Dosage adjustments may be necessary [FDA, TGA].
*   **Cardiac Glycosides:** There is an increased risk of digitalis-associated arrhythmias if hypokalemia occurs during treatment [TGA].
*   **Vitamin D/Calcium:** Concomitant use of Vitamin D analogues or calcium-containing medicines increases the risk of hypercalcemia [TGA].
*   **Citrate:** When used as an anticoagulant, citrate contributes to the total buffer load and can lower plasma ionized calcium [FDA].
*   **Additional Buffers:** Concomitant sodium bicarbonate administration increases the risk of metabolic alkalosis [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
There are no adequate clinical data on the use of these solutions in pregnant women. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. Use should only occur after a careful assessment of risks and benefits [TGA]. Maintaining normal acid-base balance is considered vital for fetal well-being [FDA].

### Breastfeeding
The components of these solutions (electrolytes and glucose) are naturally occurring in human milk. No harm to nursing infants is anticipated if the mother’s fluid and electrolyte balance is appropriately managed [FDA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Bioavailability:** As these solutions are administered intravenously, bioavailability is 100%.
*   **Distribution:** Electrolytes and bicarbonate are distributed according to osmotic and concentration gradients between the extracellular and intracellular compartments. Glucose distribution is facilitated by insulin.
*   **Metabolism:** The active substances are physiological components of plasma and are not metabolized, with the exception of glucose [TGA].
*   **Elimination:** Excretion occurs primarily through the CRRT effluent, though natural elimination via the kidneys (if residual function exists), gut, and skin also contributes [TGA, FDA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
These solutions are pharmacologically inactive [FDA]. They act by replacing water and essential electrolytes (Na+, K+, Mg2+, Ca2+, Cl-) removed during hemofiltration or hemodiafiltration. The bicarbonate in the solution acts as a physiological buffer, restoring acid-base balance and increasing the buffering capacity of the blood to correct metabolic acidosis associated with renal failure [TGA, FDA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **General Storage:** Store between 20°C and 25°C [FDA]. TGA guidelines allow storage below 30°C. 
*   **Specific Restrictions:** Do not freeze or expose to excessive heat. Do not use the product if a precipitate has formed or if the container is damaged.
*   **In-use Stability:** Once the overwrap is removed and the solutions are mixed, the product is stable for 24 hours (including the duration of treatment) [FDA, TGA]. For microbiological safety, use immediately after mixing.
