# Editorial Product Information: Clopidogrel and Aspirin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following provided jurisdiction: 
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Clopidogrel (as hydrogen sulfate) and Aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid).
- **Chemical Class:** Clopidogrel is a thienopyridine-class platelet aggregation inhibitor; Aspirin is a salicylate-class non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) with antiplatelet activity.
- **Brand Names:** APX-Clopidogrel/Aspirin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B01AC30.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablet: 75 mg clopidogrel / 100 mg aspirin [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd; Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS):** Intended for the continuation of therapy in patients already initiated on separate clopidogrel and aspirin components for:
    - **Unstable Angina or Non-ST Elevation Myocardial Infarction (UA/NSTEMI):** To prevent early and long-term atherothrombotic events (MI, stroke, vascular death, or refractory ischaemia), regardless of whether the patient underwent surgical or percutaneous revascularisation [Australian].
    - **ST-Segment Elevation Acute Myocardial Infarction (STEMI):** To prevent atherothrombotic events and reduce mortality in patients eligible for thrombolytic therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** One tablet (75 mg/100 mg) taken once daily with adequate water [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Should be taken with or after food to mitigate gastric irritation caused by aspirin [Australian].
- **Initiation in ACS:** This fixed-dose combination (FDC) is used *after* an initial clopidogrel loading dose (typically 300 mg) in combination with aspirin (75 mg to 325 mg) [Australian].
- **Duration of Therapy:**
    - **UA/NSTEMI:** Evidence supports use for at least 12 months [Australian].
    - **STEMI:** Use beyond four weeks has not been specifically studied for the combination in this setting [Australian].
- **Specific Dosing Considerations:** If maintenance aspirin doses >100 mg are required, clopidogrel and aspirin must be administered as separate products [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to clopidogrel, salicylates, or any product excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- Active pathological bleeding (e.g., peptic ulcer, intracranial haemorrhage, or haemophilia) [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment [Australian].
- Breastfeeding [Australian].
- Third trimester of pregnancy [Australian].
- **Aspirin-specific contraindications:**
    - Hypersensitivity to NSAIDs.
    - Syndrome of asthma with rhinitis and/or nasal polyps.
    - Pre-existing mastocytosis (risk of severe hypersensitivity reactions including shock) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bleeding Risk:** Both agents prolong bleeding time. Use with extreme caution in patients with an increased risk of bleeding from trauma or surgery. Discontinue 7 days prior to elective surgery if an antiplatelet effect is not desired [Australian].
- **Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura (TTP):** Reported very rarely; requires prompt treatment including plasmapheresis [Australian].
- **CYP2C19 Pharmacogenetics:** Clopidogrel is a prodrug. Poor metabolisers of CYP2C19 (genotype-dependent) exhibit reduced antiplatelet response. Consider alternative strategies or higher clopidogrel doses (600 mg loading/150 mg maintenance) in these patients, though clinical outcome data for higher doses are limited [Australian].
- **G6PD Deficiency:** Requires close medical supervision due to the risk of aspirin-induced haemolysis [Australian].
- **Reye’s Syndrome:** Aspirin is associated with this rare but fatal condition in children and adolescents; use in patients under 18 is not established [Australian].
- **Acquired Haemophilia:** Reported following clopidogrel use; suspected if isolated aPTT prolongation occurs [Australian].
- **Kounis Syndrome:** Allergic vasospastic angina has been reported in the context of aspirin hypersensitivity [Australian].
- **DRESS Syndrome:** Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms has been reported with aspirin [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>1%)** | Dyspepsia, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, bruising/purpura, epistaxis. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Headache, dizziness, paraesthesia, nausea, gastritis, flatulence, constipation, vomiting, gastric/duodenal ulcers, rash, pruritus, leucopenia, decreased platelets. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Vertigo, neutropenia. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | TTP, aplastic anaemia, pancytopenia, agranulocytosis, anaphylactoid reactions, serum sickness, confusion, hallucinations, taste disturbances, vasculitis, hypotension, bronchospasm, hepatitis, acute liver failure, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN). |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Intracranial haemorrhage (0.1–0.5% in major trials), gastrointestinal haemorrhage, and fatal bleeding [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Oral Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** Not recommended as it may increase bleeding intensity [Australian].
- **NSAIDs / COX-2 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use increases the risk of occult GI blood loss and is generally not recommended [Australian].
- **CYP2C19 Inhibitors:** Strong or moderate inhibitors (e.g., omeprazole, esomeprazole) reduce the active metabolite of clopidogrel. If a PPI is necessary, pantoprazole is preferred as it has less inhibitory effect [Australian].
- **CYP2C19 Inducers (e.g., Rifampicin):** May increase clopidogrel active metabolite levels and bleeding risk [Australian].
- **SSRIs:** Increased bleeding risk due to effects on platelet activation [Australian].
- **Methotrexate:** Aspirin inhibits renal clearance, potentially leading to bone marrow toxicity [Australian].
- **Valproic Acid:** Salicylates may displace valproic acid from protein binding, increasing serum levels [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Increases the risk of GI injury and may alter clopidogrel metabolism [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C.** 
    - Should not be used in the first two trimesters unless clinically essential. 
    - Contraindicated in the **third trimester** due to risks of premature closure of the ductus arteriosus, prolonged labour, and maternal/neonatal bleeding [Australian].
    - Use at ~20 weeks gestation or later may cause fetal renal dysfunction leading to oligohydramnios [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Clopidogrel and salicylates are excreted in breast milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Clopidogrel showed no effect on rat fertility. Aspirin has shown antispermatogenic effects in animal models [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in subjects under 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required. Note higher plasma levels of clopidogrel metabolites in patients ≥75 years, though this does not translate to different antiplatelet effects [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. Use with caution in mild-to-moderate impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. Use with caution in moderate hepatic disease due to potential bleeding diatheses [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Clopidogrel:** Irreversibly binds to the P2Y12 platelet receptor, inhibiting ADP-mediated activation of the GPIIb/IIIa complex [Australian].
    - **Aspirin:** Irreversibly inhibits cyclo-oxygenase (COX-1), blocking thromboxane A2 production [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Clopidogrel is rapidly absorbed (at least 50%). Aspirin is rapidly hydrolysed to salicylic acid; food does not significantly affect aspirin bioavailability [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Clopidogrel is a prodrug metabolized via two pathways: esterase-mediated hydrolysis (85% to inactive carboxylic acid) and CYP450-mediated (primarily CYP2C19) to its active thiol metabolite [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Clopidogrel half-life is ~6 hours; 50% excreted in urine and 46% in faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CURE:** Clopidogrel + aspirin (N=6244) vs placebo + aspirin (N=6287) in UA/NSTEMI. Primary endpoint (CV death/MI/stroke) occurred in 9.3% vs 11.4% (p=0.00009). Benefits were seen within hours and maintained for 12 months [Australian].
- **CLARITY:** Clopidogrel (300 mg load/75 mg daily) + aspirin vs placebo + aspirin in STEMI (N=3491). Significant reduction (36%) in the odds of the primary endpoint (occluded artery/death/MI) [Australian].
- **COMMIT:** Large trial (N=45,852) in STEMI. Clopidogrel 75 mg + aspirin reduced the relative risk of death from any cause by 7% (p=0.029) and the combined endpoint of re-infarction/stroke/death by 9% (p=0.002) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Container:** PA/Al/PVC/Al blister packs [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
