# Editorial Product Information: Corticorelin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of corticorelin, synthesized for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. The information provided is based solely on the source documents available at the time of writing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Corticorelin ovine triflutate (a synthetic 41-amino acid peptide analogue of ovine corticotropin-releasing hormone [oCRH]).
- **Chemical Class:** Corticotropin-releasing hormone analogue.
- **Brand Names:** **Acthrel** [US].
- **ATC Code:** H01AA03.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilized powder for injection (100 mcg/vial) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ferring Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Differential Diagnosis of Cushing's Syndrome:** Indicated for use in differentiating between pituitary and ectopic sources of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) production in patients with established ACTH-dependent Cushing’s syndrome [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** A single intravenous dose of 1 mcg/kg body weight [US].
- **Dose Limitation:** Doses exceeding 1 mcg/kg are not recommended as they do not provide additional diagnostic benefit and are associated with increased adverse effects [US].

### Test Methodology
- **Baseline Sampling:** Draw venous blood samples 15 minutes before and immediately prior to administration. The average of these two samples defines the ACTH baseline [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as an intravenous infusion over a 30- to 60-second interval [US].
- **Post-Administration Sampling:** Draw venous blood samples at 15, 30, and 60 minutes post-injection [US].
- **Timing:** If repeated testing is required, it should be performed at the same time of day as the original test to account for circadian rhythms of ACTH and cortisol [US].

### Reconstitution Instructions
- Reconstitute with 2 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, USP.
- **Method:** Do not shake the vial; roll it gently to dissolve the powder to avoid bubble formation.
- **Final Concentration:** 50 mcg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a known history of hypersensitivity to ovine corticorelin or any excipients (ascorbic acid, lactose, or cysteine hydrochloride monohydrate) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Dose-Dependent Cardiovascular Effects:** Adverse reactions such as transient tachycardia, hypotension, and loss of consciousness are dose-dependent. These risks are significantly higher at doses >1 mcg/kg [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Acute reactions including urticaria, facial/laryngeal oedema, dyspnea, and wheezing have been reported. Immediate discontinuation and appropriate medical intervention are required if these occur [US].
- **Administration Rate:** To reduce the severity of adverse effects, the drug should be administered as a slow 30-second infusion rather than a rapid bolus [US].
- **Diagnostic Limitations:** Approximately 5% to 10% of patients with Cushing’s disease may show false-negative responses to the corticorelin test, potentially leading to a misdiagnosis of ectopic ACTH production [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally transient and dose-dependent.
- **Common (>10%):** 
    - Facial flushing (timing coincides with injection; can last up to 4 hours in some subjects).
- **Frequency Not Specified:**
    - **Respiratory:** Dyspnea, wheezing, "chest compression," or an urge to take a deep breath.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Transient tachycardia, hypotension, loss of sinus rhythm.
    - **Neurological:** "Absence-like" loss of consciousness (lasting 10 seconds to 5 minutes).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - **Cardiac:** Asystole (reported in patients with HPA axis disturbances).
    - **Neurological:** Grand mal seizure (reported in a patient with a known seizure diathesis who had discontinued anticonvulsants) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Dexamethasone:** Pretreatment with dexamethasone inhibits or blunts the plasma ACTH response to corticorelin [US].
- **Heparin:** The use of heparin to maintain cannula patency is not recommended due to a potential interaction that may cause severe hypotension and loss of sinus rhythm [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned under current labeling, but document states use "only if clearly needed"]. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if corticorelin is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies evaluating the effect of corticorelin on fertility have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited data available. Dosages of 1 mcg/kg have been used in children with hypothalamic/pituitary deficiencies or tumors. No significant differences in test response compared to adults have been observed [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Corticorelin is a potent stimulator of ACTH release from the anterior pituitary. In patients with Cushing's disease (pituitary adenoma), the corticotrophs typically remain responsive to CRH stimulation despite high cortisol levels, whereas ectopic ACTH-producing tumors are generally unresponsive [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** IV administration causes a rapid increase in plasma ACTH (peak at 10–15 mins) followed by a near-parallel increase in plasma cortisol (peak at 30–60 mins) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Mean volume of distribution is 6.2 ± 0.5 L. It does not appear to bind specifically to circulating plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolic clearance rate is approximately 95 ± 11 L/m²/day.
    - **Elimination:** Follows a biexponential decay curve. Fast component half-life: 11.6 ± 1.5 minutes; Slow component half-life: 73 ± 8 minutes [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Clinical evaluation of ~300 patients with Cushing's disease showed a hyper-response to corticorelin despite high basal cortisol levels.
- In 32 patients with ectopic ACTH secretion, high basal levels of ACTH and cortisol were generally not further stimulated by corticorelin, although rare instances of response were noted [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Lyophilized Powder:** Store refrigerated between 2°C and 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Protect from light [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Stable for up to 8 hours when stored under refrigerated conditions. Discard any unused portion [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **US:** Rx-only (Prescription only); Diagnostic Use Only.
