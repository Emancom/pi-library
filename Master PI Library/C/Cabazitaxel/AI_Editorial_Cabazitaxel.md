# Editorial Product Information: Cabazitaxel

## Editorial Note
*   This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    *   **Australian (TGA)**: Cabazitaxel Accord (Cabazitaxel) Concentrated Injection.
*   **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing information specific to the jurisdiction in which a healthcare professional is practicing.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient**: Cabazitaxel (a semi-synthetic derivative of a natural taxoid).
*   **Chemical Class**: Taxane (Antineoplastic agent).
*   **Brand Name(s)**: Cabazitaxel Accord [Australian].
*   **ATC Code**: L01CD04.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 60 mg/3 mL (20 mg/mL) concentrated injection for intravenous infusion. [Australian]
*   **Route of Administration**: Intravenous (IV).
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Accord Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Metastatic Castration-Resistant Prostate Cancer (mCRPC)**: Indicated in combination with prednisone or prednisolone for the treatment of patients with mCRPC previously treated with a docetaxel-containing regimen. [Australian-approved]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Recommended Dose**: 20 mg/m² administered as a 1-hour intravenous infusion every 3 weeks. [Australian]
*   **Optional High Dose**: A dose of 25 mg/m² may be used in select patients at the discretion of the treating physician. [Australian]
*   **Concomitant Therapy**: Must be administered with oral prednisone or prednisolone (10 mg daily throughout treatment). [Australian]

### Premedication
To reduce the risk and severity of hypersensitivity, premedicate at least 30 minutes prior to every infusion with:
*   Intravenous Antihistamine (e.g., dexchlorpheniramine 5 mg or diphenhydramine 25 mg).
*   Intravenous Corticosteroid (e.g., dexamethasone 8 mg).
*   Intravenous H2 antagonist (e.g., ranitidine).
*   Antiemetic prophylaxis is recommended (oral or IV). [Australian]

### Dosage Adjustments for Adverse Reactions
| Adverse Reaction | Dosage Modification |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Prolonged Grade ≥3 Neutropenia** (>1 week despite G-CSF) | Delay until ANC >1.5 x 10⁹/L, then reduce dose by one level. |
| **Febrile Neutropenia / Neutropenic Infection** | Delay until resolution and ANC >1.5 x 10⁹/L, then reduce dose by one level. |
| **Grade ≥3 Diarrhoea** (or persistent) | Delay until resolution, then reduce dose by one level. |
| **Grade ≥2 Peripheral Neuropathy** | Delay until improvement, then reduce dose by one level. |

*   **Dose reduction levels**: Reduce from 25 mg/m² to 20 mg/m², then to 15 mg/m². If the patient is already at 20 mg/m², reduce to 15 mg/m². [Australian]

### Preparation and Administration Instructions
*   **Direct Dilution**: The 20 mg/mL concentrate does NOT require an intermediate dilution step. [Australian]
*   **Final Concentration**: Dilute with 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose to a final concentration of 0.10 mg/mL to 0.26 mg/mL.
*   **Equipment**: Use **PVC-free** infusion containers and **polyurethane-free** infusion sets.
*   **Filtration**: Must use an in-line filter (0.22 micrometre nominal pore size).
*   **Handling**: Cytotoxic agent; use gloves. Pregnant staff should not handle the product. [Australian]

## 4. Contraindications
*   History of severe hypersensitivity to cabazitaxel, other polysorbate 80-containing drugs, or any excipients. [Australian]
*   Baseline neutrophil count ≤ 1,500/mm³. [Australian]
*   Severe hepatic impairment (Total Bilirubin > 3 x ULN). [Australian]
*   Pregnancy and breastfeeding. [Australian]
*   Concomitant vaccination with yellow fever vaccine. [Australian]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Bone Marrow Suppression**: Neutropenia is the most common adverse reaction. Deaths due to infection have occurred. Monitor Complete Blood Count (CBC) **weekly** during Cycle 1 and before every subsequent cycle. [Australian]
*   **G-CSF Prophylaxis**: Recommended to limit neutropenia severity. Primary prophylaxis should be considered for high-risk patients (age >65, poor performance status, prior febrile neutropenia, or extensive radiation). [Australian]
*   **Hypersensitivity**: Monitor closely, especially during the first and second infusions. Discontinue immediately if severe reactions (hypotension, bronchospasm, generalised rash) occur. [Australian]
*   **Gastrointestinal Effects**: Nausea, vomiting, and severe diarrhoea may occur. Rehydration and anti-diarrhoeals/anti-emetics are essential. Deaths related to diarrhoea and electrolyte imbalance (hypokalaemia) have been reported. [Australian]
*   **Renal Disorders**: Renal failure (sometimes fatal) has been reported in association with sepsis, dehydration, and obstructive uropathy. Monitor creatinine at baseline and with every CBC. Discontinue if renal failure ≥ Grade 3 occurs. [Australian]
*   **Respiratory Disorders**: Interstitial pneumonia/pneumonitis and Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) have been reported (some fatal). Discontinue if new/worsening pulmonary symptoms develop. [Australian]
*   **Cardiac Arrhythmias**: Tachycardia and atrial fibrillation have been reported. [Australian]

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are based on the TROPIC study (n=371) comparing cabazitaxel 25 mg/m² + prednisone vs mitoxantrone.

### Very Common (>10%)
*   **Haematological**: Neutropenia (93.5%; Grade ≥3: 81.7%), Anaemia (97.3%), Leukopenia (95.7%), Thrombocytopenia (47.4%).
*   **Gastrointestinal**: Diarrhoea (46.6%), Nausea (34.2%), Vomiting (22.6%), Constipation (20.5%), Abdominal pain (11.6%).
*   **General**: Fatigue (36.7%), Asthenia (20.5%), Pyrexia (12.1%), Anorexia (15.9%).
*   **Other**: Haematuria (16.7%), Dyspnoea (11.9%), Back pain (16.2%), Arthralgia (10.5%), Dysgeusia (11.1%), Alopecia (10%).

### Common (1–10%)
*   **Infections**: Urinary tract infection (7.3%), Sepsis (1.1%), Septic shock (1.1%).
*   **Neurological**: Peripheral neuropathy (8.1%), Dizziness (8.1%), Headache (7.5%).
*   **Renal**: Acute renal failure (2.2%), Dysuria (6.7%).
*   **Cardiac**: Atrial fibrillation (1.1%), Tachycardia (1.6%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Neutropenic sepsis and septic shock (fatalities reported).
*   Severe dehydration and renal failure.
*   Interstitial Lung Disease. [Australian]

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **CYP3A4 Inhibitors**: Cabazitaxel is primarily metabolised by CYP3A (80% to 90%). Strong CYP3A inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir) increase cabazitaxel exposure and should be avoided. If co-administration is necessary, consider a dose reduction. [Australian]
*   **CYP3A4 Inducers**: Strong inducers (e.g., phenytoin, rifampin, St John's Wort) decrease cabazitaxel concentrations and should be avoided. [Australian]
*   **OATP1B1 Substrates**: Cabazitaxel inhibits OATP1B1 in vitro. Risk of interaction with substrates (e.g., statins, valsartan) is possible during the infusion and up to 20 minutes post-infusion. [Australian]
*   **Vaccinations**: Avoid live or live-attenuated vaccines due to the risk of serious/fatal infections in immunocompromised patients. [Australian]

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy**: **Australian Category D**. Cabazitaxel is genotoxic and can cause foetal harm. It is not recommended during pregnancy. [Australian]
*   **Contraception**: 
    *   **Males**: Should use effective contraception during treatment and for **6 months** after the final dose. Ejaculate contact with others must be prevented.
*   **Breastfeeding**: Excretion in human milk is unknown (confirmed in rat milk). Breastfeeding is contraindicated. [Australian]
*   **Fertility**: Male reproductive toxicity (tubular atrophy) observed in animal studies. Men are advised to seek sperm conservation advice before treatment. [Australian]

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy not established. [Australian]
*   **Elderly (≥65 years)**: No specific dose adjustment is required. However, elderly patients are more likely to experience fatigue, asthenia, pyrexia, dizziness, UTI, and dehydration. Patients ≥65 years had higher rates of Grade ≥3 neutropenia (86.3% vs 73.3%). [Australian]
*   **Renal Impairment**: No adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment. Data is limited for end-stage renal disease (CLCR <15 mL/min); treat with caution. [Australian]
*   **Hepatic Impairment**:
    *   **Mild** (Bilirubin >1 to ≤1.5 x ULN or AST >1.5 x ULN): Dose should be limited to 20 mg/m².
    *   **Moderate** (Bilirubin >1.5 to ≤3.0 x ULN): Maximum tolerated dose is 15 mg/m².
    *   **Severe** (Bilirubin >3 x ULN): Contraindicated. [Australian]

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action**: Disrupts the microtubular network by binding to tubulin and promoting assembly into microtubules while simultaneously inhibiting disassembly. This stabilises microtubules and inhibits mitotic and interphase cellular functions. [Australian]
*   **Pharmacokinetics**:
    *   **Distribution**: Very high volume of distribution (Vss = 4870 L). 89–92% plasma protein bound (albumin and lipoproteins).
    *   **Metabolism**: Extensively hepatic (≥95%), mainly via CYP3A4/5. Seven metabolites detected in plasma.
    *   **Elimination**: Terminal half-life is 95 hours. Primarily excreted in faeces (76%) as metabolites; renal excretion accounts for 3.7% of the dose. [Australian]

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **TROPIC Study**: A Phase III trial (n=755) in mCRPC post-docetaxel. Cabazitaxel 25 mg/m² + prednisone demonstrated significantly longer median overall survival (15.1 months) compared to mitoxantrone + prednisone (12.7 months), representing a 30% reduction in risk of death (HR 0.70; p<0.0001). [Australian]
*   **EFC11785 Study**: A non-inferiority study (n=1200) comparing 20 mg/m² and 25 mg/m². The 20 mg/m² dose demonstrated non-inferiority in overall survival and a significantly better safety profile (lower rates of Grade 3-4 neutropenia). [Australian]

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Concentrate Vial**: Store below 25°C. **Do not refrigerate.** [Australian]
*   **Diluted Infusion Solution**:
    *   Should be used immediately.
    *   If necessary, may be stored for **8 hours** at room temperature (below 30°C) or **24 hours** under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C).
*   **Crystallisation**: The infusion solution is supersaturated and may crystallise; discard if precipitate is observed. [Australian]
