# Editorial Product Information: Carglumic acid

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidance before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Carglumic acid (structural analogue of N-acetylglutamate).
- **Brand Name:** CARBAGLU [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Dispersible tablet, 200 mg. The tablets are white, elongated, and feature three score marks, allowing for division into equal halves or quarters [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral (including nasogastric/oral syringe administration).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Recordati Rare Diseases Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Carglumic acid is indicated for the treatment of:
- Hyperammonaemia due to **N-acetylglutamate synthase (NAGS)** primary deficiency [Australian].
- Hyperammonaemia due to **Organic Acidaemias (OA)**, including:
    - Isovaleric acidaemia (IVA) [Australian].
    - Methylmalonic acidaemia (MMA) [Australian].
    - Propionic acidaemia (PA) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
Treatment must be initiated under the supervision of a physician experienced in metabolic disorders. Acute symptomatic hyperammonaemia is a life-threatening emergency; therapy may begin as early as the first day of life.

### General Dosing
- **Initial Dose (Acute Hyperammonaemia):** 100 mg/kg/day to 250 mg/kg/day [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dose:** Titrate individually to maintain normal plasma ammonia levels for age.
- **Frequency:** The total daily dose should be divided into 2 to 4 doses.
- **Rounding:** For adults, round the total daily dose to the nearest 100 mg (half tablet).

### Responsiveness Testing
It is recommended to test individual responsiveness before long-term use:
- **Comatose Children:** Start with 100–250 mg/kg/day; ammonia levels should normalise within a few hours.
- **Moderate Hyperammonaemia:** Administer 100–200 mg/kg/day for 3 days with constant protein intake; assess ammonia levels before and 1 hour after meals.

### Administration Instructions
**CARBAGLU tablets must not be swallowed whole or crushed.**
- **Oral (Adults):** Disperse each 200 mg tablet in 5–10 mL of water. Take immediately. The suspension has a slightly acidic taste and contains undissolved particles; the container should be rinsed with additional water to ensure the full dose is consumed. Administer before meals or feedings.
- **Oral Syringe (Paediatrics):** Mix each tablet in 2.5 mL water (80 mg/mL concentration). Draw into a syringe and administer immediately. Flush with 1–2 mL of additional water.
- **Nasogastric (NG) Tube:** Mix as above (80 mg/mL for paediatrics or in 5–10 mL for adults) and administer immediately. Flush the tube with water after administration.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to carglumic acid or any excipients [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding** is contraindicated during the use of carglumic acid [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Management:** Hyperammonaemia may require additional interventions, such as haemodialysis, to remove the ammonia burden and prevent brain injury or death.
- **Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of plasma ammonia and amino acid levels is essential. Additionally, clinical response, neurological, cardiac, renal, hepatic, and haematological status should be assessed.
- **Cardiotoxicity and Hepatotoxicity:** Due to limited data, a definitive association cannot be determined; however, monitoring of cardiac and hepatic function is advised [Australian].
- **Nutritional Management:** Protein restriction may be necessary during acute phases, while protein supplementation may be indicated during maintenance.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### NAGS Deficiency (Retrospective Study, N=23)
Adverse events regardless of causality occurring in ≥13% of patients:
- **Gastrointestinal:** Vomiting (26%), Abdominal pain (17%), Diarrhoea (13%).
- **General:** Pyrexia (17%), Asthenia (9%).
- **Infections:** Tonsillitis (17%), Ear infection (13%), Nasopharyngitis (13%), Infection (13%).
- **Haematological:** Anaemia (13%).
- **Neurological:** Headache (13%).

### Organic Acidaemias (Retrospective Study, N=57)
Adverse events regardless of causality:
- **Haematological:** Anaemia (5.3%), Thrombocytopenia (5.3%).
- **General:** Pyrexia (5.3%), Hyperthermia (3.5%), Hypothermia (3.5%), Condition aggravated (3.5%).
- **Other:** Sepsis (3.5%), Hyperglycaemia (3.5%).

**Spontaneous Reports:** The most frequently reported spontaneous adverse events include vomiting, diarrhoea, and rash.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic:** No specific clinical interaction studies have been performed. In vitro studies indicate that carglumic acid is **not** an inducer of CYP1A1/2, 2B6, or 3A4/5 and is **not** an inhibitor of CYP1A2, 2A6, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, or 3A4/5 [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at high doses (up to 2000 mg/kg/day) showed no adverse embryofoetal effects.
- **Breastfeeding:** [Contraindicated in Australia]. It is unknown if carglumic acid is excreted in human milk. It is excreted in the milk of lactating rats, where it was associated with increased neonatal mortality and impaired weight gain.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effects on male or female fertility at doses significantly higher than the maximum recommended human dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (100–250 mg/kg/day) and suitable for use from the first day of life.
- **Elderly Use:** No data are available for this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage reduction is required based on absolute GFR (not normalised to body surface area).
    - **Moderate (GFR 30–59 mL/min):** Initial 50–125 mg/kg/day; Maintenance 5–50 mg/kg/day.
    - **Severe (GFR ≤29 mL/min):** Initial 15–40 mg/kg/day; Maintenance 2–20 mg/kg/day.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dose adjustments are not provided, though monitoring is advised due to potential hepatotoxicity risks.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Structural analogue of N-acetylglutamate (NAG). It activates carbamoyl phosphate synthetase 1 (CPS 1), the initial enzyme of the urea cycle. In cases of NAGS deficiency, it replaces the missing natural activator to trigger the urea cycle.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Induces rapid normalisation of plasma ammonia (usually within 24 hours).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 30% absorbed. Peak plasma levels occur at ~3 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Biphasic elimination. Terminal half-life up to 28 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** A portion is metabolised, likely by intestinal flora; a possible end product is carbon dioxide (exhaled).
    - **Elimination:** 9% excreted unchanged in urine; up to 60% eliminated in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **NAGS Deficiency:** A retrospective study of 23 patients showed that carglumic acid induced rapid ammonia normalisation. Patients treated before permanent brain damage exhibited normal growth and development. Ammonia levels fell from a mean baseline of 271 µmol/L to 23 µmol/L during long-term therapy (mean 8 years).
- **Organic Acidaemias:** A retrospective study of 57 patients (IVA, MMA, PA) during acute decompensation showed a mean ammonia reduction from 350.7 µmol/L at baseline to 58.5 µmol/L at the primary endpoint. The mean time to achieve the endpoint was 2.4 days.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze). Protect from light [Australian].
- **After Opening:** Once the tablet container is opened, it must be used within 3 months. Store the opened container below 30°C (do not refrigerate). Keep the container tightly closed to protect from moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
