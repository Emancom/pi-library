# Editorial Product Information: cenegermin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **cenegermin**
- Chemical class: Recombinant human nerve growth factor (a neurotrophin); 118 amino acids produced by recombinant DNA technology
- Molecular mass: approximately 13,266 Daltons
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Topical ophthalmic

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2018
- Date of last revision/update of source prescribing information: February 2025
- Withdrawals, suspensions, restrictions: Not specified in available sources
- Other jurisdictions' first-registration or last-revision dates: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of neurotrophic keratitis. [US-approved]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Instill one drop in the affected eye(s) six times per day at approximately 2‑hour intervals for eight weeks.

### Dosing by indication
- Neurotrophic keratitis: one drop in affected eye(s) 6 times daily for 8 weeks.

### Administration instructions and preparation for use
- Administer topically to the ocular surface. Use the provided administration accessories (single-use pipettes/withdrawal devices and disinfectant wipes) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.
- Use a new sterile pipette for each dose and each eye; discard pipette immediately after use.
- Do not touch the eye with the tip of the pipette during instillation.
- If concurrent topical ophthalmic products are used, administer products at least 15 minutes apart to avoid dilution; instill cenegermin prior to application of ointments, gels, or other viscous ophthalmic preparations (allow ~15 minutes before these are applied).
- Remove contact lenses prior to instillation of cenegermin; lenses may be reinserted after an interval of approximately 15 minutes following dose instillation.
- If a dose is missed, continue the regimen at the next scheduled time (do not double doses).

### Titration
- Not applicable / Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- No contraindications reported in available sources (none specified).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed warnings: None reported.
- General warnings and precautions:
  - Contact lenses: Remove contact lenses before applying cenegermin; reinsert lenses approximately 15 minutes after instillation.
  - Ocular discomfort: Mild to moderate eye discomfort, including eye pain on instillation, may occur; eye pain can accompany corneal healing. Advise patients to report severe or persistent eye reactions.
  - Use with other topical ocular products: Administer products at least 15 minutes apart; give cenegermin prior to viscous ophthalmic formulations.
- Monitoring requirements:
  - Monitor the treated eye for signs of worsening ocular inflammation, new or worsening corneal findings (including deposits or neovascularization), blepharitis, or other adverse ocular events.
  - If a more serious eye reaction occurs, consider ophthalmic reassessment and management.
- Specific clinical precautions:
  - Adhere to single-use pipette and aseptic handling procedures to reduce risk of contamination.
  - Discard single-use delivery devices after each application.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Human data: No data from use in pregnant women to establish drug-associated risks.
- Animal data: In embryofetal development studies, subcutaneous administration to pregnant rats and rabbits during organogenesis produced:
  - Slight increase in post-implantation loss at doses ≥42 mcg/kg/day (reported as 267 times the maximum recommended human ophthalmic dose analogous exposure); a NOAEL for this finding was not established.
  - In rats at very high doses (267 mcg/kg/day) isolated findings included hydrocephaly and ureter anomalies in single fetuses.
  - In rabbits at high doses (83 mcg/kg/day) isolated cardiovascular malformations (ventricular/atrial septal defects, enlarged heart, aortic arch dilation) were observed in single fetuses.
  - No fetal malformations were observed in rats and rabbits at lower evaluated doses (rats at 133 mcg/kg/day; rabbits at 42 mcg/kg/day).
- Regulatory pregnancy category/classification: Not specified in available sources.
- Clinical recommendation: Use in pregnancy only if potential benefit justifies potential risk to fetus; discuss with patient given limited human data and animal findings at high exposures.

### Breastfeeding
- Human data: No information on presence in human milk, effects on breastfed infant, or effects on milk production.
- Recommendation: Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding along with the mother’s clinical need for treatment and potential risks; counsel patient accordingly.

### Fertility
- Animal fertility data: Daily subcutaneous administration to male and female rats before mating and through early post-coitum showed no effects on fertility parameters at doses up to 267 mcg/kg/day. General toxicology studies identified ovarian findings (persistent estrus, follicular cysts, reduced corpora lutea, changes in ovarian weight) in females at doses ≥19 mcg/kg/day (reported as 119 times maximum recommended human ophthalmic dose analog).

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Cenegermin** is a recombinant human nerve growth factor that acts via high-affinity (TrkA) and low-affinity (p75NTR) nerve growth factor receptors in the anterior segment of the eye to support corneal innervation and epithelial integrity.

### Pharmacodynamics
- No dedicated pharmacodynamic studies in humans available in the provided sources.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Systemic exposure assessment: Serum nerve growth factor concentrations were measured in healthy volunteers after single and multiple topical ocular administrations; there was no apparent relationship between topical cenegermin administration and systemic serum NGF concentrations in the small study reported.
- Absorption/distribution/metabolism/excretion: Detailed PK parameters (bioavailability, systemic clearance, metabolism, elimination) are not specified in available sources beyond the serum NGF study findings.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Two randomized, multi-center, double-masked, vehicle-controlled studies evaluated efficacy and safety in patients with neurotrophic keratitis. Treatment duration in trials was 8 weeks with dosing six times daily.
  - Study designs:
    - Study NGF0212: randomized to cenegermin 20 mcg/mL, cenegermin 10 mcg/mL, or vehicle; included only unilateral disease.
    - Study NGF0214: randomized to cenegermin 20 mcg/mL or vehicle; included bilateral disease treated bilaterally.
  - Patient numbers:
    - Across the two pivotal studies, a total of 151 patients were studied; 101 patients received the 20 mcg/mL dose regimen in the trials referenced.
  - Primary efficacy outcome:
    - Complete corneal healing at Week 8, defined as absence of staining of the corneal lesion and no persistent staining of the rest of the cornea.
  - Key results (complete corneal healing at Week 8):
    - NGF0214: 65.2% (15/23) with cenegermin vs 16.7% (4/24) with vehicle; treatment difference 48.6% (95% CI 24.0% to 73.1%); p < 0.01.
    - NGF0212: 72.0% (36/50) with cenegermin vs 33.3% (17/51) with vehicle; treatment difference 38.7% (95% CI 20.7% to 56.6%); p < 0.01.
  - Recurrence after healing:
    - In patients healed at Week 8, recurrences occurred in approximately 20% (NGF0212) and 14% (NGF0214) of patients.
  - Corneal sensitivity:
    - Mean changes from baseline in corneal sensitivity within the lesion at Week 8 were not consistently clinically significant across studies.
