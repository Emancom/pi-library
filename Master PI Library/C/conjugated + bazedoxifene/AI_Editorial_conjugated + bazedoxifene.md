# Editorial Product Information: conjugated + bazedoxifene

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **conjugated + bazedoxifene**
- Chemical class:
  - Conjugated estrogens: postmenopausal estrogen replacement
  - Bazedoxifene: selective estrogen receptor modulator (estrogen agonist/antagonist)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Last revision noted as 3/2024 in one source
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms (hot flushes) associated with menopause in women with a uterus.
- Prevention of postmenopausal osteoporosis in women with a uterus.
- Important limitation: Use for the shortest duration consistent with treatment goals and risks for the individual woman; re-evaluate periodically whether treatment is still necessary. When used solely for osteoporosis prevention, consider non-estrogen options and reserve for women at significant risk of osteoporosis.

(If any jurisdiction-specific indication limitations exist in other sources, they are not specified in the available sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dose: One oral tablet once daily containing conjugated estrogens 0.45 mg and bazedoxifene 20 mg.
### Dosing by indication
- Treatment of vasomotor symptoms: One tablet orally once daily.
- Prevention of postmenopausal osteoporosis: One tablet orally once daily; consider supplemental calcium and/or vitamin D if dietary intake is inadequate.
### Administration instructions
- Administer once daily, without regard to meals.
- Tablets should be swallowed whole.
### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.
### Missed dose instructions
- If a dose is missed, take it as soon as remembered unless it is almost time for the next scheduled dose. Do not take two doses at the same time.

## 5. Contraindications
(consolidated from available prescribing information; include all listed)
- Undiagnosed abnormal uterine bleeding.
- Known, suspected, or past history of breast cancer.
- Known or suspected estrogen-dependent neoplasia.
- Active deep venous thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE), or a history of these conditions.
- Active arterial thromboembolic disease (for example, stroke, myocardial infarction) or a history of these conditions.
- Known hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema) to estrogens, bazedoxifene, or any component of the product.
- Known hepatic impairment or active liver disease.
- Known deficiency of protein C, protein S, or antithrombin, or other known thrombophilic disorders.
- Pregnancy (may cause fetal harm).

- Additional prescribing instruction: Women taking conjugated + bazedoxifene should not take progestins, additional estrogens, or additional estrogen agonist/antagonists concurrently.

(If any jurisdiction-specific contraindications exist in other sources, they are not specified in the available sources.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- **ENDOMETRIAL CANCER, CARDIOVASCULAR DISORDERS, AND PROBABLE DEMENTIA** — see details below.
  - Note: The large Women's Health Initiative (WHI) findings referenced in the available sources come from studies of conjugated estrogens alone (oral conjugated estrogens 0.625 mg daily). The applicability of those specific WHI incidence data to conjugated + bazedoxifene and to other doses or routes is unknown; however, similar risks cannot be excluded.

### General warnings and precautions
- Endometrial cancer:
  - Unopposed estrogens increase endometrial cancer risk in women with a uterus. The bazedoxifene component is included to reduce endometrial hyperplasia risk; conjugated + bazedoxifene has been shown to reduce endometrial hyperplasia versus estrogen alone.
  - In postmenopausal women with undiagnosed persistent or recurring abnormal genital bleeding, adequate diagnostic measures (including directed or random endometrial sampling when indicated) should be undertaken to rule out malignancy.
- Cardiovascular disorders:
  - Estrogens and estrogen agonist/antagonists are associated with increased risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE). An increased risk of stroke, DVT and pulmonary embolism has been reported with estrogen-alone therapy in WHI studies; if VTE, PE, stroke, or retinal vascular thrombosis occur or are suspected, discontinue therapy immediately.
  - Manage risk factors for arterial vascular disease (e.g., hypertension, diabetes, tobacco use, hypercholesterolemia, obesity) and VTE (e.g., personal or family history of VTE, obesity, systemic lupus erythematosus) appropriately.
  - Consider discontinuing conjugated + bazedoxifene 4 to 6 weeks prior to surgery associated with increased thromboembolism risk, and during periods of prolonged immobilization.
- Probable dementia:
  - In women 65 years of age and older, estrogen-alone therapy in WHIMS was associated with increased risk of probable dementia. The relevance to conjugated + bazedoxifene or younger women is unknown.
- Gallbladder disease:
  - Estrogen therapy increases the risk of gallbladder disease requiring surgery.
- Visual abnormalities:
  - Retinal vascular thrombosis and other visual changes have been reported with estrogens; discontinue pending examination if sudden partial or complete loss of vision, proptosis, diplopia, or migraine occur. Permanently discontinue if papilledema or retinal vascular lesions are found.
- Elevated blood pressure:
  - Substantial increases in blood pressure have been attributed in rare cases to estrogens; monitor blood pressure as clinically indicated.
- Hypertriglyceridemia:
  - Estrogens may increase triglyceride levels; in patients with pre-existing severe hypertriglyceridemia, estrogen therapy has been associated with pancreatitis.
- Hepatic impairment and cholestatic jaundice:
  - Use is contraindicated in women with hepatic impairment; estrogens may be poorly metabolized in patients with impaired liver function. Bazedoxifene exposure was reported to be increased in women with hepatic impairment (about 4.3-fold overall exposure).
- Thyroid function:
  - Estrogen administration increases thyroid-binding globulin (TBG) and may necessitate increased doses of thyroid replacement therapy in women dependent on replacement; monitor thyroid function and adjust therapy as needed.
- Fluid retention:
  - Estrogens may cause fluid retention; use caution in patients with cardiac dysfunction or renal impairment.
- Hypocalcemia:
  - Estrogens may induce hypocalcemia in patients with hypoparathyroidism; use with caution.
- Hereditary angioedema:
  - Exogenous estrogens may exacerbate symptoms of hereditary angioedema.
- Exacerbation of other conditions:
  - Estrogens may exacerbate asthma, diabetes mellitus, epilepsy, migraine, porphyria, systemic lupus erythematosus, hepatic hemangiomas, and should be used with caution in women with these conditions.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic assessment as clinically indicated:
  - Evaluate for signs/symptoms of VTE, stroke, myocardial ischemia.
  - Monitor blood pressure.
  - Monitor liver function in patients with history of liver disease (use is contraindicated in hepatic impairment).
  - Monitor thyroid function in women taking thyroid replacement therapy.
  - Monitor lipid profile, including triglycerides, in patients with hypertriglyceridemia risk.
  - Evaluate for abnormal uterine bleeding and perform endometrial assessment if persistent or recurrent bleeding occurs.
  - Annual breast examinations and mammography scheduling per patient age, risk factors, and prior mammogram results.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Do not coadminister additional estrogens, progestins, or other estrogen agonist/antagonists with conjugated + bazedoxifene.
- Use in women older than 75 years: Use not recommended; conjugated + bazedoxifene was not studied in women over 75.
- Use in renal impairment: Not recommended; pharmacokinetics not evaluated in renal impairment.
- Discontinue therapy if pancreatitis, cholestatic jaundice, or severe hypertriglyceridemia occurs.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The safety profile is drawn from randomized clinical trials and post-marketing reports in available sources.
- In four prospective, randomized, placebo-controlled trials, the adverse reactions with incidence ≥5% included: muscle spasms, nausea, diarrhea, dyspepsia, upper abdominal pain, oropharyngeal pain, dizziness, and neck pain.

### Serious adverse reactions and notable events reported in available sources
- Venous thromboembolism (DVT, PE)
- Stroke
- Coronary heart disease events (no overall effect seen in WHI estrogen-alone substudy; see Clinical Trials)
- Endometrial cancer (risk increased with unopposed estrogens; bazedoxifene reduces endometrial hyperplasia risk)
- Breast cancer (effect of conjugated + bazedoxifene on breast cancer risk is unknown)
- Ovarian cancer (epidemiology data suggest increased risk with hormonal therapy overall)
- Probable dementia (increased risk in women ≥65 in WHIMS estrogen-alone ancillary study)
- Retinal vascular thrombosis and other visual abnormalities
- Gallbladder disease requiring surgery
- Elevated blood pressure
- Hypertriglyceridemia and pancreatitis (in patients with pre-existing hypertriglyceridemia)
- Hepatic impairment and cholestatic jaundice
- Hypocalcemia
- Hereditary angioedema exacerbation
- Fluid retention
- Exacerbation of asthma, diabetes, epilepsy, migraine, porphyria, systemic lupus erythematosus, hepatic hemangiomas
- Hypersensitivity reactions including angioedema and anaphylaxis

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Sections on cytochrome P450 (CYP) and uridine diphosphate glucuronosyltransferase (UGT) interactions are identified in available sources, but specific interaction details (e.g., inhibitors/inducers and clinical management) are **Not specified in available sources**.
- Bazedoxifene exposure is increased in hepatic impairment (overall exposure increased ~4.3-fold in women with hepatic impairment in available data).
- An interaction section referencing atorvastatin exists in available sources but specific clinical recommendations and data are **Not specified in available sources**.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant use with other estrogens, progestins, or estrogen agonist/antagonists: **Do not coadminister**; women taking conjugated + bazedoxifene should not take additional estrogens, progestins, or estrogen agonist/antagonists.
- Estrogens alter coagulation/hematologic parameters (increase in coagulation factors, fibrinogen, changes in antithrombin III and plasminogen) and may increase thrombotic risk; manage concomitant medications affecting coagulation accordingly.
- Estrogens increase thyroid-binding globulin (TBG) and affect total circulating thyroid hormone measurements; may necessitate adjustment of thyroid hormone replacement therapy.
- Estrogens can increase levels of corticosteroid-binding globulin (CBG) and sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG), altering total circulating corticosteroids and sex steroids.

### Clinically significant interactions and management notes
- If concomitant drugs that markedly affect coagulation or that predispose to thromboembolism are used, exercise caution and monitor patients closely.
- Specific dosage adjustments or contraindications with particular CYP or UGT modulators are **Not specified in available sources**.
- For interactions noted but lacking clinical guidance in available sources, use clinical judgment and monitor patient status and relevant laboratory parameters.

(Where jurisdiction-specific interaction information exists in other sources, it is not specified in the available sources.)

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Not indicated for premenopausal or paediatric use. The efficacy and safety in premenopausal women have not been established and use is not recommended. (No pediatric dosing or approvals provided.)
### Elderly use
- Conjugated + bazedoxifene was not studied in women over 75 years of age; use in women >75 years is not recommended.
- In women 65 years and older, WHIMS data with conjugated estrogens alone showed an increased risk of probable dementia; relevance to the combination product is unknown.
### Renal impairment
- The pharmacokinetics have not been evaluated in patients with renal impairment. Use in patients with renal impairment is not recommended.
### Hepatic impairment
- Use in patients with hepatic impairment is contraindicated. Bazedoxifene exposure is substantially increased in hepatic impairment (~4.3-fold increase in overall exposure reported). Estrogens may be poorly metabolized in women with impaired liver function.
### Body mass index (BMI)
- Specific dosing adjustments by BMI: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Conjugated estrogens: provide estrogenic activity to relieve menopausal symptoms and to help maintain bone density.
- Bazedoxifene: selective estrogen receptor modulator (estrogen agonist/antagonist) designed to antagonize estrogen action in endometrial tissue, reducing estrogen-induced endometrial hyperplasia while allowing beneficial estrogen effects on vasomotor symptoms and bone.
### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption, distribution, detailed metabolism, and elimination parameters for the combination are **Not specified in available sources** beyond the following:
  - Bazedoxifene exposure increases in hepatic impairment (approximate 4.3-fold increase in overall exposure).
  - Pharmacokinetics in renal impairment have not been evaluated.
- Pharmacodynamics:
  - Conjugated estrogens exert estrogenic effects on target tissues; bazedoxifene acts as an estrogen agonist/antagonist with tissue-selective activity resulting in reduction of endometrial hyperplasia associated with estrogen therapy.
- Additional PK/PD details: Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Pivotal and key studies (as described in available sources)
- Multiple randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trials:
  - Four prospective, randomized, placebo-controlled trials are referenced; in these trials the most common adverse reactions (incidence ≥5%) for conjugated + bazedoxifene included muscle spasms, nausea, diarrhea, dyspepsia, upper abdominal pain, oropharyngeal pain, dizziness, and neck pain.
- Endometrial outcomes:
  - Conjugated + bazedoxifene has been shown to reduce the risk of endometrial hyperplasia that may occur with conjugated estrogen therapy alone.
- Women's Health Initiative (WHI) and Women's Health Initiative Memory Study (WHIMS) data:
  - The WHI estrogen-alone substudy (oral conjugated estrogens 0.625 mg daily in hysterectomized women) reported increased risks of stroke and deep vein thrombosis compared with placebo over an average follow-up of 7.1 years (stroke: 45 vs 33 per 10,000 women-years; VTE: 30 vs 22 per 10,000 women-years; DVT: 23 vs 15 per 10,000 women-years). Only daily 0.625 mg conjugated estrogens was studied in WHI; the relevance of these findings to lower estrogen doses, other administration routes, or other estrogen-containing products (including conjugated + bazedoxifene) is unknown.
  - The WHIMS estrogen-alone ancillary study (women 65–79 years) found an increased incidence of probable dementia with conjugated estrogens alone versus placebo (absolute rates 37 vs 25 per 10,000 women-years; relative risk 1.49 [95% CI 0.83–2.66]). Applicability to conjugated + bazedoxifene or younger women is unknown.
