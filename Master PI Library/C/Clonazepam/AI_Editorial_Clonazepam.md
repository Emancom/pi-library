# Editorial Product Information: Clonazepam

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **clonazepam** product information for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information from the provided source.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Clonazepam (a benzodiazepine derivative)
- **Chemical Name:** 5-(2-chlorophenyl)-1,3-dihydro-7-nitro-2H-1,4-benzodiazepin-2-one
- **Brand Name(s):** **PAXAM** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Tablets: 0.5 mg and 2 mg [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd trading as Viatris [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Epilepsy (Children):** Most types of epilepsy, specifically absences (petit mal), myoclonic seizures, and tonic-clonic fits (whether primary generalised or secondary to partial epilepsy) [Australian].
- **Epilepsy (Adults):** All varieties of generalised epilepsy (including myoclonic, akinetic, tonic, and tonic-clonic seizures) and partial epilepsy (including psychomotor seizures) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
Dosage must be individualised based on clinical response, age, and tolerance. Treatment should commence with low doses, titrated progressively to a maintenance level [Australian].

### Adult Dosing
- **Initiation:** 1 mg/day (divided into morning and evening doses).
- **Maintenance:** Generally 4 mg to 8 mg daily. 
- **Maximum Dose:** 20 mg/day [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (Initiation)
- **Ages 2 to 5 years:** 0.5 mg/day (divided).
- **Ages 6 to 12 years:** 0.75 mg/day (divided; e.g., 0.25 mg morning, 0.5 mg evening) [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (Maintenance)
- **Ages 2 to 5 years:** 1.5 mg to 3 mg daily.
- **Ages 6 to 12 years:** 3 mg to 6 mg daily [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- The daily total should ideally be divided into three or four doses.
- Maintenance doses are typically achieved after 2 to 4 weeks of treatment.
- **Discontinuation:** Must not be stopped abruptly; a stepwise reduction is mandatory to prevent withdrawal and rebound phenomena [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **clonazepam**, other benzodiazepines, or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- Chronic obstructive airways disease accompanied by incipient respiratory failure [Australian].
- Clinical dependence on alcohol, CNS depressants, or drugs of abuse [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment (due to the risk of precipitating hepatic encephalopathy) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided Australian source.
- **Withdrawal and Tapering:** Abrupt cessation may lead to withdrawal symptoms (convulsions, psychosis, tremors) or a temporary increase in seizure frequency/severity.
- **Suicidality and Depression:** Patients with a history of depression or suicidal attempts require close supervision. Benzodiazepines may unmask suicidal tendencies or exacerbate depression [Australian].
- **CNS Depression:** Concomitant use with alcohol or other CNS depressants is strongly discouraged due to the risk of severe sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.
- **Respiratory Function:** Caution is required in patients with pre-existing respiratory disease (e.g., COPD) or sleep apnoea. 
- **Paradoxical Reactions:** Restlessness, agitation, and aggression may occur; treatment should be discontinued if these emerge.
- **Porphyria:** Use with caution due to potential porphyrogenic effects.
- **Tolerance:** Anticonvulsant tolerance may develop within 4 weeks to 6 months of treatment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects occur in approximately 50% of patients and are often dose-related and transitory.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Drowsiness/Somnolence (50%), Ataxia (30%), Behaviour problems (25%), Hypersalivation (10%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Fatigue (8%), Muscle weakness (5%), Vertigo (5%), Light-headedness, Dizziness, Confusion (1.9%), Agitation (1.5%), Irritability (1.5%), Bronchial hypersecretion (1.4%), Depression (1%) |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anaphylaxis, Cardiac arrest, Thrombocytopenia, Hepatomegaly, Hallucinations |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory depression, coma, cardiac failure, and blood dyscrasias (leucopenia, anaemia) [Australian].
- **Post-Marketing Experience:** Reversible vision disorders (diplopia), urinary incontinence, and erectile dysfunction have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, and valproate may increase **clonazepam** clearance, lowering plasma levels.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Fluconazole and other inhibitors may impair metabolism, leading to toxic concentrations.
- **Others:** Cimetidine and disulfiram may increase **clonazepam** levels.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with alcohol, neuroleptics, opioids, sedatives, and antidepressants.
- **Sodium Valproate:** Concurrent use has been associated with the development of petit mal status epilepticus.
- **Anticholinergics:** Potentiation of atropine-like effects [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Risk of fetal malformation is approximately three times higher in mothers on AEDs. 
    - Continuous use in late pregnancy may cause neonatal cardiac arrhythmias, hypotonia, and withdrawal symptoms.
    - Folic acid supplementation is recommended for women of childbearing potential [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Clonazepam** is excreted in breast milk. It must not be given to breastfeeding women; if therapy is essential, breastfeeding should be discontinued [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Supervision is required due to the risk of salivary and bronchial hypersecretion, which may compromise airway patency in infants and small children. Precocious puberty (reversible) has been reported rarely [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity to benzodiazepines. Use the lowest possible dose and titrate cautiously due to risks of confusion, ataxia, and falls/fractures [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is strictly required based on pharmacokinetics, but dosage review is advised due to potential metabolite accumulation [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. Use the lowest possible dose in mild-to-moderate cases [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Enhances polysynaptic inhibitory processes across all levels of the CNS. It is particularly effective in blocking the spread of electrical activity from seizure foci.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 82–98% bioavailable; peak serum levels reached in 2–3 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Rapid entry into cerebral tissues; 82–86% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism via hydroxylation and reduction (CYP3A4 involved).
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life is approximately 39 hours. 50–70% excreted in urine, primarily as metabolites [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Container:** HDPE bottle with child-resistant closure.
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be returned to a pharmacy for disposal [Australian].
