# Editorial Product Information: Carbamazepine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for carbamazepine, specifically designed for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and regulatory compliance. At the time of compilation, only the Australian PI was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Carbamazepine
- **Chemical Class:** Dibenzazepine derivative; structurally related to tricyclic antidepressants.
- **Brand Names:** Avloire [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N03AF01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Conventional Tablets: 100 mg and 200 mg [Australian].
    - Controlled Release (CR) Tablets: 200 mg and 400 mg [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Epilepsy:** 
    - Partial seizures (simple or complex), with or without secondary generalisation.
    - Generalised tonic-clonic seizures.
    - Mixed seizure patterns including the above.
- **Trigeminal Neuralgia:** 
    - Relief of pain in idiopathic trigeminal neuralgia or neuralgia associated with multiple sclerosis.
    - Idiopathic glossopharyngeal neuralgia.
- **Mania and Bipolar Affective Disorder:** 
    - Treatment of acute mania.
    - Maintenance treatment to prevent or attenuate recurrence [Australian].

*Note: Carbamazepine is generally ineffective for absence (petit mal), myoclonic, or atonic seizures and is not indicated for status epilepticus.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Epilepsy:** Initial dose of 100–200 mg once or twice daily. Gradually increase to an optimum response, typically 400 mg 2–3 times daily. Some patients may require up to 1600 mg or 2000 mg daily [Australian].
- **Trigeminal Neuralgia:** Initial dose of 200–400 mg daily in divided doses. Increase by 200 mg daily until pain relief (usually 800 mg daily). Maximum dose: 1200 mg daily.
- **Mania and Bipolar Maintenance:** Range is 400–1600 mg daily. For acute mania, titrate rapidly; for maintenance, increase in 100 mg weekly increments [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken during or after meals with liquid.
- **CR Tablets:** Must be swallowed unchewed. They can be halved if prescribed. 
- **Switching Formulations:** When moving from conventional to CR tablets, the CR dose may need to be increased. CR is typically dosed twice daily [Australian].

### Titration and Monitoring
- Doses should be adjusted to the minimum effective level once control is established.
- Therapeutic plasma range: 4 to 12 microgram/mL (17–50 micromol/L).

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to carbamazepine, structurally related drugs (e.g., tricyclic antidepressants), or any excipients.
- Atrioventricular (AV) block.
- History of bone marrow depression.
- History of hepatic porphyrias (e.g., acute intermittent porphyria, variegate porphyria, porphyria cutanea tarda).
- Concomitant use with Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs). MAOIs must be discontinued at least 2 weeks prior to starting carbamazepine.
- Hepatic failure [Australian].
- Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) [Australian].
- Neonates under 4 weeks of age [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Dermatological Reactions
- **SJS/TEN:** Life-threatening reactions (Stevens-Johnson Syndrome and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis) have been reported. Most occur within the first few months of treatment.
- **Pharmacogenomics:** 
    - **HLA-B\*1502:** High prevalence in Asian populations (Han Chinese, Thai, Filipino, Malaysian). Testing is recommended for patients of these ancestries prior to initiation. Avoid use if positive unless benefits clearly outweigh risks [Australian].
    - **HLA-A\*3101:** Risk factor for SJS/TEN, DRESS, and AGEP in Northern European and Japanese populations. Testing should be considered in high-risk groups (Japanese, Caucasian, Arabic, Hispanic, and Indigenous American populations).

### Haematological Effects
- Aplastic anaemia and agranulocytosis have occurred (rare but potentially fatal). 
- **Monitoring:** Baseline and periodic complete blood counts (CBC), including platelets, reticulocytes, and serum iron, are required. Discontinue if significant bone marrow depression appears.

### General Precautions
- **Suicidal Behaviour:** Increased risk of suicidal ideation and behaviour. Monitor patients for mood changes.
- **Hyponatremia:** Risk of low sodium, especially in the elderly or those on diuretics. Monitor serum sodium.
- **Hypothyroidism:** May reduce thyroid hormone levels; monitoring is suggested.
- **Anticholinergic Effects:** Use with caution in patients with increased intraocular pressure or urinary retention.
- **Withdrawal:** Abrupt cessation may precipitate seizures or status epilepticus; taper gradually.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Leucopenia, ataxia, dizziness, somnolence, fatigue, nausea, vomiting, urticaria, elevated gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Eosinophilia, thrombocytopenia, oedema, fluid retention, weight increase, hyponatremia, headache, diplopia, blurred vision, dry mouth, elevated alkaline phosphatase. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Abnormal involuntary movements (tremor, nystagmus), dermatitis exfoliative, diarrhoea, constipation, elevated transaminases. |
| **Rare (0.1–0.01%)** | Leucocytosis, lymphadenopathy, folic acid deficiency, hallucinations, depression, agitation, orofacial dyskinesia, cardiac conduction disorders, hypertension/hypotension, cholestatic hepatitis, SLE. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia, anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN, pancreatitis, hepatic failure, arrhythmia, AV-block, thromboembolism, renal failure, sexual dysfunction. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Induction:** Carbamazepine is a potent inducer of CYP3A4 and other enzyme systems. It may significantly decrease the plasma levels of:
    - Oral anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin, rivaroxaban, apixaban).
    - Antiepileptics (e.g., lamotrigine, valproic acid, phenytoin).
    - Hormonal contraceptives (may lead to contraceptive failure).
    - Immunosuppressants (e.g., cyclosporin).
    - Antipsychotics, antidepressants, and protease inhibitors.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Agents that increase carbamazepine levels include macrolide antibiotics (erythromycin), azole antifungals, diltiazem, verapamil, and grapefruit juice.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Lithium/Neuroleptics:** May increase the risk of neurotoxic adverse effects.
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated.
- **Alcohol:** May reduce alcohol tolerance.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** Evidence of human fetal risk. Associated with a 1% risk of spina bifida. Other reported anomalies include craniofacial defects and cardiovascular malformations. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use only if benefits outweigh risks. Use monotherapy at the lowest effective dose. Supplement with folic acid (e.g., 5 mg) before and during pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Carbamazepine passes into breast milk (25–60% of plasma levels). Breastfeeding is generally permissible provided the infant is monitored for sedation and hepatobiliary effects [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **6–12 years:** Start 100 mg/day in divided doses; increase by 100 mg weekly (max 1000 mg/day).
    - **Under 6 years:** Start 20–60 mg/day; increase by up to 60 mg every 3–7 days (max 600 mg/day) [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Choose doses with caution due to higher risk of confusion, agitation, and drug interactions.
- **Renal Impairment:** Baseline and periodic urinalysis and BUN recommended. No specific dosing adjustments provided in source, but monitor closely [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in hepatic failure. Baseline and periodic liver function tests required for patients with history of liver disease [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Stabilises hyperexcited nerve membranes by providing use- and voltage-dependent blockade of sodium channels. This inhibits repetitive neuronal firing and reduces synaptic propagation of excitatory impulses.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Slow but nearly complete (conventional); CR tablets are absorbed more slowly.
    - **Distribution:** 70–80% protein bound. Crosses the placenta and enters breast milk.
    - **Metabolism:** Hepatic via the epoxide-diol pathway. The 10,11-epoxide is an active metabolite. Carbamazepine induces its own metabolism (auto-induction).
    - **Elimination:** Single dose half-life ~36 hours; repeated dose half-life ~16–24 hours. Excreted primarily in urine (72%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Suicidal Ideation Analysis:** A pooled analysis of 199 placebo-controlled trials of 11 different antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) showed a relative risk of 1.8 for suicidal thinking or behaviour compared to placebo. For carbamazepine specifically, the odds ratio was 0.65 (95% CI: 0.08, 4.42) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- Protect from moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
