# Editorial Product Information: Carvedilol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, regulatory warnings, and pharmacological profiles into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for the most up-to-date legal requirements and prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Carvedilol
- **Chemical Class:** Non-selective beta-adrenoreceptor antagonist with alpha-1 selective adrenoreceptor antagonist activity.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Carvedilol [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C07AG02
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Film-Coated Tablets:** 3.125 mg, 6.25 mg, 12.5 mg, and 25 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Treatment of essential hypertension. It may be used alone or in combination with other agents, particularly thiazide diuretics [Australian].
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** Treatment of symptomatic mild to severe (NYHA Class II to IV) heart failure. Carvedilol is typically used as an adjunct to standard therapies, including ACE inhibitors, diuretics, digitalis, and vasodilators [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Administration Instructions
- **Food Effect:** It is recommended to take **carvedilol** with food to decrease the rate of absorption and minimize the risk of orthostatic hypotension [Australian].
- **Tablets:** Should be swallowed with sufficient fluid [Australian].

### 3.2 Hypertension
- **Adults:** The recommended starting dose is 12.5 mg once daily for the first two days. The usual maintenance dose is 25 mg once daily. If required, the dose may be increased at intervals of at least two weeks to a maximum of 50 mg daily (administered once daily or in divided doses) [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Initial dose is 12.5 mg once daily. This may be sufficient for some patients; if not, titration may proceed at two-week intervals toward the maximum daily dose [Australian].

### 3.3 Symptomatic Congestive Heart Failure
Dosage must be strictly individualised and titrated slowly under close physician supervision.
- **Initiation:** 3.125 mg twice daily for two weeks.
- **Titration:** If tolerated, increase to 6.25 mg twice daily for two weeks, followed by 12.5 mg twice daily, and then 25 mg twice daily. Doses should be increased to the highest level tolerated by the patient [Australian].
- **Maximum Doses:**
    - **Mild to Moderate CHF (< 85 kg):** 25 mg twice daily [Australian].
    - **Mild to Moderate CHF (> 85 kg):** 50 mg twice daily [Australian].
    - **Severe CHF:** 25 mg twice daily [Australian].

### 3.4 Missed Doses
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
**Carvedilol** must not be used in patients with:
- NYHA Class IV decompensated heart failure requiring intravenous inotropic support [Australian].
- Bronchial asthma or related bronchospastic conditions (including COPD with a bronchospastic component) [Australian].
- Allergic disorders such as allergic rhinitis that predispose to bronchospasm [Australian].
- Severe sinus bradycardia (< 45–50 bpm) or sick sinus syndrome (without a permanent pacemaker) [Australian].
- Second or third-degree atrioventricular (AV) block [Australian].
- Cardiogenic or hypovolaemic shock [Australian].
- Severe hypotension (systolic BP < 85 mmHg) [Australian].
- Clinically manifest liver dysfunction or hepatic impairment [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to **carvedilol** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### 5.1 Boxed Warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### 5.2 General Warnings
- **Worsening Heart Failure:** **Carvedilol** may cause deterioration of heart failure during the initiation and titration phase. If this occurs, diuretics may be increased, or the **carvedilol** dose reduced/interrupted [Australian].
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Cessation of therapy in patients with ischaemic heart disease or angina should be gradual (over approximately two weeks) to avoid exacerbation of angina, myocardial infarction, or ventricular arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Hypotension and Syncope:** Risk is highest during the first 30 days of therapy/titration. Patients should avoid hazardous tasks (e.g., driving) during this period until their response is stabilised [Australian].
- **Renal Function:** Reversible deterioration of renal function has been observed in CHF patients with low blood pressure (systolic < 100 mmHg), ischaemic heart disease, or underlying renal insufficiency. Monitor renal function during titration [Australian].

### 5.3 Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Diabetes:** May mask symptoms of acute hypoglycaemia (tachycardia) and worsen glycaemic control [Australian].
- **Peripheral Vascular Disease/Raynaud's:** May precipitate or aggravate symptoms of arterial insufficiency [Australian].
- **Thyrotoxicosis:** May mask symptoms such as tachycardia; abrupt withdrawal may precipitate a thyroid storm [Australian].
- **Major Surgery:** Synergistic negative inotropic effects with anaesthetics require careful monitoring [Australian].
- **Psoriasis:** Use only after careful risk-benefit consideration [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Very rare cases of SJS/TEN have been reported; discontinue permanently if these occur [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are most common at the start of treatment and are often dose-related.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Dizziness (32.4%), Fatigue (23.9%), Upper respiratory tract infection (18.3%), Hyperglycaemia (12.2%), Diarrhoea (11.8%) |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Bradycardia (8.8%), Hypotension (8.5%), Weight increase (9.7%), Headache (8.1%), Nausea (8.5%), Visual abnormalities (5.0%), Oedema (generalised, dependent, or legs) |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Sleep disorder, impaired concentration, depression, paraesthesia, peripheral ischaemia, tachycardia, constipation, vomiting, pruritus, erythematous rash |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Complete AV block, GI haemorrhage, bronchospasm, anaphylactoid reaction, pancytopenia, alopecia, SJS/TEN |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### 7.1 Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Digoxin:** **Carvedilol** increases digoxin exposure by up to 56%. Monitor digoxin levels when starting or adjusting **carvedilol** [Australian].
- **Ciclosporin/Tacrolimus:** Carvedilol increases oral absorption of these agents via P-gp inhibition. Dose reductions of 10–20% for ciclosporin may be required [Australian].
- **CYP2D6/CYP2C9 Inhibitors (e.g., Fluoxetine, Paroxetine, Amiodarone):** May increase plasma levels of **carvedilol** enantiomers, increasing the risk of dizziness or bradycardia [Australian].
- **Rifampicin:** Decreases **carvedilol** plasma concentrations by approximately 60% [Australian].
- **Grapefruit Juice:** May increase **carvedilol** AUC by ~16%; avoid concomitant intake until stable [Australian].

### 7.2 Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Calcium Channel Blockers (Verapamil, Diltiazem):** Increased risk of AV conduction disturbances. Monitor ECG and blood pressure [Australian].
- **Clonidine:** Potentiates blood pressure and heart rate lowering. If terminating combination therapy, discontinue **carvedilol** several days before gradually reducing clonidine [Australian].
- **Insulin/Oral Hypoglycaemics:** Enhanced blood-glucose-lowering effect; symptoms of hypoglycaemia may be masked [Australian].
- **NSAIDs:** Concurrent use may result in increased blood pressure and impaired BP control [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. Beta-blockers reduce placental perfusion, which may lead to fetal death or premature delivery. **Carvedilol** should not be used unless the potential benefit outweighs the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Carvedilol** is excreted in breast milk. Not recommended during lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies at toxic doses (55x MRHD) showed impaired fertility; no effects seen at 11x MRHD [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in those under 18 years have not been established. Use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No notable differences in efficacy compared to younger patients, though dizziness was reported more frequently (8.8% vs 6%) [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild-to-moderate impairment, but renal function must be monitored during titration in CHF [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in clinically manifest liver dysfunction. AUC is increased 6.8-fold in patients with cirrhosis [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Dual-action cardiovascular agent. It is a non-selective beta-adrenoreceptor blocker (Beta-1 and Beta-2) and a selective Alpha-1 adrenoreceptor blocker. It possesses no intrinsic sympathomimetic activity (ISA) and has membrane-stabilising properties. It also exhibits antioxidant activity [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; absolute bioavailability ~25% due to significant first-pass metabolism.
    - **Distribution:** >98% protein bound (primarily albumin); highly lipophilic.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver via CYP2D6, CYP2C9, CYP1A2, and CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is 6–10 hours. Primarily excreted via bile into the faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **US and Australia-New Zealand Trials:** Multicentre studies in NYHA II-III CHF patients showed that **carvedilol** reduced heart failure progression by 48% and decreased the risk of death and hospitalisation [Australian].
- **COPERNICUS Trial:** In 2,289 patients with severe CHF (ejection fraction < 20%), **carvedilol** reduced all-cause mortality by 35% compared to placebo. The trial was terminated early due to significant survival benefits [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Protect from light and moisture.
- Tablets should be kept in their original blister or bottle until immediately before use [Australian].
