# Editorial Product Information: Cevimeline

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesised from the following jurisdiction's official Product Information: United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of cevimeline for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cevimeline hydrochloride (a cholinergic agonist).
- **Chemical Class:** cis-2’-methylspiro {1-azabicyclo [2.2.2] octane-3, 5’ -[1,3] oxathiolane} hydrochloride, hydrate (2:1).
- **Brand Names:** Evoxac (original brand; source document refers to generic cevimeline hydrochloride by Asclemed USA Inc. [US]).
- **ATC Code:** N07AX03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Capsules:** 30 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Asclemed USA Inc. (Repackaged by Enovachem Pharmaceuticals) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Sjögren’s Syndrome:** Indicated for the treatment of symptoms of xerostomia (dry mouth) in patients with Sjögren’s Syndrome [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 30 mg taken three times daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - May be taken with or without food. However, administration with food decreases the rate of absorption (Tmax increases from 1.53 to 2.86 hours) and reduces peak concentration by approximately 17% [US].
    - Patients should be advised to increase fluid intake if excessive sweating occurs to prevent dehydration [US].
- **Dosing Limitations:** Doses exceeding 30 mg tid have not demonstrated additional efficacy and lack sufficient safety data [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Uncontrolled Asthma:** Cevimeline is contraindicated in patients with poorly controlled bronchial asthma [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to cevimeline or any component of the formulation [US].
- **Miosis Precautions:** Contraindicated where miosis (pupillary constriction) is undesirable, specifically:
    - Acute iritis [US].
    - Narrow-angle (angle-closure) glaucoma [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Cevimeline may alter heart rate and cardiac conduction. Caution and close medical supervision are required for patients with significant cardiovascular disease, including a history of angina pectoris or myocardial infarction [US].
- **Pulmonary Disease:** As a muscarinic agonist, cevimeline can increase bronchial secretions, airway resistance, and bronchial smooth muscle tone. Use with caution in patients with controlled asthma, COPD, or chronic bronchitis [US].
- **Ocular Effects:** May cause blurred vision and decreased visual acuity, particularly at night or in patients with central lens changes. Impairment of depth perception is possible. Caution is advised for night driving and hazardous activities in low light [US].
- **Gallbladder and Biliary Disease:** Use with caution in patients with cholelithiasis. Muscarinic stimulation may cause gallbladder contractions, potentially leading to cholecystitis, cholangitis, or biliary obstruction [US].
- **Renal/Ureteral Disease:** Use with caution in patients with nephrolithiasis. Increased ureteral smooth muscle tone may precipitate renal colic or ureteral reflux [US].
- **CYP2D6 Deficiency:** Patients known or suspected to be deficient in CYP2D6 activity should be treated with caution as they may face an increased risk of adverse events [US].

- **Respiratory:** Coughing (6.1%), Pharyngitis (5.2%), Bronchitis (4.1%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Vomiting (4.6%), Excessive salivation (2.2%).
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Dizziness (4.1%), Insomnia (2.4%), Anxiety (1.3%).

### Serious and Post-Marketing Events
- **Hepatobiliary:** Cholecystitis [US-Post-marketing].
- **General Cholinergic Toxicity:** Characterized by respiratory distress, GI spasm, AV block, tachycardia/bradycardia, hypotension/hypertension, shock, mental confusion, cardiac arrhythmia, and tremors [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Beta-adrenergic antagonists:** Increased risk of cardiac conduction disturbances [US].
    - **Parasympathomimetics:** Additive cholinergic effects [US].
    - **Antimuscarinics (Anticholinergics):** Cevimeline may antagonize the therapeutic effects of concurrently administered antimuscarinics [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 inhibitors:** Concurrent use may inhibit the metabolism of cevimeline, potentially increasing drug levels and toxicity [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C:** Animal studies (rats) showed a reduction in the number of implantations at doses approximately 5 times the maximum human dose. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if cevimeline is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the drug's importance to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on male rat fertility were noted at 5 times the maximum human dose. In female rats, a reduction in implantations was observed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects over 65 to determine if they respond differently. Caution is advised when initiating treatment due to the higher prevalence of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function in this population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The effects of renal impairment on cevimeline pharmacokinetics have not been investigated [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** The effects of hepatic impairment on cevimeline pharmacokinetics have not been investigated [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A cholinergic agonist that binds to muscarinic receptors. It increases secretion from exocrine glands (salivary and sweat) and increases smooth muscle tone in the gastrointestinal and urinary tracts [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; Tmax 1.5 to 2 hours (fasting).
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is ~6 L/kg; <20% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP2D6 and CYP3A3/4. Major metabolites include cis- and trans-sulfoxide, glucuronic acid conjugates, and N-oxide.
    - **Excretion:** Mean half-life is 5 ± 1 hours. 97% of the dose is recovered in urine within seven days [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **6-week Study (n=75):** Cevimeline 30 mg tid showed statistically significant global improvement in dry mouth symptoms (76%) compared to placebo (35%; p=0.0043). Doses of 60 mg tid did not provide superior results to 30 mg tid [US].
- **12-week Study 1 (n=197):** Cevimeline 30 mg tid showed significant global improvement (p=0.0004) and increased salivary flow compared to placebo. 15 mg tid did not reach statistical significance for global improvement [US].
- **12-week Study 2 (n=212):** While this study showed a higher placebo response and did not meet significant global evaluation differences, the 30 mg tid group showed a statistically significant increase in salivary flow (p=0.0017) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F).
- **Packaging:** Dispense in child-resistant containers [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx only). Repackaged under NDC 87063-180 [US].
