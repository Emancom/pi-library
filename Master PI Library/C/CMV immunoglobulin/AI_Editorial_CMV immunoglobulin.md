# Editorial Product Information: Human Cytomegalovirus Immunoglobulin

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise reference.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Human Cytomegalovirus Immunoglobulin (mainly IgG)
- **Brand Name(s):** CMV Immunoglobulin-VF [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** J06BB09 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for this class)
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile, preservative-free solution for intravenous infusion.
    - Contains 50–70 mg/mL human plasma protein (at least 98% IgG).
    - Cytomegalovirus (CMV) activity: 1.5 million units per vial (at least 20,000 units/mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** CSL Behring (Australia) Pty Ltd; distributed by Australian Red Cross Lifeblood [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prophylaxis of CMV Infection:** Prevention of CMV infection following bone marrow and renal transplantation. Specifically indicated when a seronegative recipient receives a graft from a CMV-positive donor [Australian].
- **Adjunctive Therapy:** Used as an adjunct to treatment in patients with established CMV infection, such as CMV pneumonitis [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Due to the absence of an international standard, dosing is empirical [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication [Australian]
- **Prophylaxis:** 25,000 units/kg administered on:
    - Day -4 and Day -2 relative to transplant.
    - The day of transplantation (intra-operatively).
    - Weekly thereafter for two months.
- **Therapy:** 50,000 units/kg initially, repeated after 4 to 5 days, then every 10 to 14 days until clinical improvement is observed.

### Weight-Based Vial Requirements [Australian]
For patients weighing >15 kg, if the calculated dose includes a fraction of a vial, the higher whole number of vials should be administered.
- **>15–30 kg:** 1 vial (Prophylaxis); 1 vial (Therapy)
- **31–60 kg:** 1 vial (Prophylaxis); 2 vials (Therapy)
- **61–90 kg:** 2 vials (Prophylaxis); 3 vials (Therapy)
- **91–120 kg:** 2 vials (Prophylaxis); 4 vials (Therapy)

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Allow the solution to reach room temperature. Inspect for turbidity or sediment; do not use if present or if the solution has been frozen.
- **Dilution:** May be infused undiluted or diluted with up to four times its volume of 0.9% saline or 5% glucose [Australian].
- **Infusion Rate:** Start at 1 mL/minute for the first 15 minutes. If tolerated, increase gradually to a maximum of 3 to 4 mL/minute over the next 15 minutes.
- **Monitoring:** Monitor vital signs and general status regularly, particularly during the first hour of infusion.

## 4. Contraindications
- Patients with a history of true anaphylactic reactions to the active substance or any excipients [Australian].
- Patients with IgA deficiency, unless they have been tested and shown not to have circulating anti-IgA antibodies, due to the risk of severe reactions to trace IgA [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rare precipitous falls in blood pressure and anaphylactic reactions may occur, even in patients who tolerated previous treatment. Adrenaline (epinephrine) and emergency support must be available.
- **Acute Renal Failure:** Occasional reports of renal dysfunction/failure have occurred with IVIg products. Risk factors include pre-existing renal insufficiency, diabetes, age >65, volume depletion, sepsis, monoclonal gammopathy, and concomitant nephrotoxic drugs. Ensure adequate hydration prior to infusion [Australian].
- **Thromboembolic Events:** Clinical evidence suggests a link between IVIg and thromboembolism (e.g., thrombophlebitis), likely related to increased blood viscosity. Use with caution in patients with cardiovascular risk factors, hypercoagulable states, or prolonged immobilisation [Australian].
- **Aseptic Meningitis Syndrome (AMS):** May occur within hours to two days post-treatment, especially with high doses (2 g/kg). Symptoms include severe headache, nuchal rigidity, and photophobia [Australian].
- **Haemolytic Anaemia:** IVIg may contain blood group antibodies acting as haemolysins. Monitor for clinical signs of haemolysis and positive Coombs’ test [Australian].
- **Pathogen Safety:** Manufactured from human plasma; while screening and inactivation steps (low pH, nanofiltration) are employed, the risk of transmitting infectious agents (including viruses and CJD) cannot be fully eliminated [Australian].
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** Passively transferred antibodies may result in misleading positive serological tests (e.g., Coombs' test).
- **Glucose Monitoring:** The maltose content may interfere with certain glucose dehydrogenase-based testing systems, leading to falsely elevated glucose readings and potential untreated hypoglycaemia [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Frequency data (e.g., very common/common) is not specified in available sources.*

- **Infusion-Related (Acute):** Most likely in the first hour. Includes abdominal pain, headache, chest tightness, facial flushing/pallor, dyspnoea, non-urticarial skin rash, itching, hypotension, nausea, and vomiting.
- **Delayed Reactions:** Occurring within 24 hours of completion; includes nausea, vomiting, chest pain, rigors, or aching legs.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
    - True anaphylactic reactions (urticaria, angioedema, bronchospasm).
    - Acute renal failure and renal dysfunction.
    - Thromboembolic events.
    - Aseptic meningitis syndrome.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Live Attenuated Vaccines:** Passively acquired antibodies may interfere with the immune response to live vaccines (e.g., measles, mumps, rubella, polio). Defer vaccination for at least 3 months post-infusion. For measles, efficacy may be impaired for up to one year; antibody status should be checked [Australian]. 
- **Compatibility:** Should be administered separately from other IV fluids or medications unless diluted with 0.9% saline or 5% glucose [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Safety in human pregnancy has not been established in controlled trials. Use only if clearly needed. Clinical experience with other immunoglobulins suggests no adverse effects on the foetus are to be expected [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Immunoglobulins are excreted in breast milk. While it is unknown if this applies to the passively administered CMV product, no harmful effects on the neonate are anticipated based on clinical experience with the class [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No reproductive toxicity studies have been conducted [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in the paediatric population has not been established in specific studies. For patients ≤15 kg, dosing must be calculated strictly by body weight (units/kg) [Australian]. 
- **Neonates:** Consideration should be given to the acid load and volume load if administered to neonates [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in specific studies. The elderly are at higher risk for fluid overload, hyperviscosity, and renal complications [Australian].
- **Renal/Cardiac Impairment:** Increased risk of fluid overload and acute renal failure. Administer at the minimum rate and dose possible [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Derived from plasma donations containing high levels of specific antibodies to Cytomegalovirus. It provides passive immunity by increasing the recipient’s titre of CMV-neutralising antibodies [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** The serum half-life is approximately three weeks, though it may be significantly shorter in immunosuppressed patients [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The protein is not chemically or enzymatically modified, preserving the biological activity of the IgG subclasses [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Clinical Data:** No specific clinical trial data provided in available sources [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Handling:** Protect from light. Use immediately once the vial is opened. Discard any unused portion.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
