# Editorial Product Information: Citalopram

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: Australian (TGA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of citalopram for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Citalopram (present as citalopram hydrobromide).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitor (SSRI).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Citalopram [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 20 mg and 40 mg strengths [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Treatment of major depression [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard starting dose is 20 mg once daily, administered in the morning or evening without regard to food. The dose may be increased in 10 mg increments to a maximum of 40 mg/day [Australian].
- **Dose Titration:** Clinical response is typically evaluated after 2–3 weeks. Dose increases should occur at intervals of 2–3 weeks [Australian].
- **Maintenance and Duration:** For major depression, treatment should generally continue for at least 6 months to prevent relapse [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Abrupt cessation must be avoided. Doses should be tapered gradually over 1–2 weeks to mitigate withdrawal reactions. If intolerable symptoms occur, resuming the previous dose before tapering at a more gradual rate is recommended [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to citalopram or any tablet excipients [Australian].
- Congenital long QT syndrome [Australian].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Use is contraindicated in combination with MAOIs (including selegiline >10 mg/day) or within 14 days of discontinuing an irreversible MAOI. At least 14 days must elapse after stopping citalopram before starting an MAOI [Australian].
- **Linezolid:** Contraindicated unless facilities for close observation and blood pressure monitoring are available [Australian].
- **Pimozide:** Concomitant administration is contraindicated due to the risk of QT interval prolongation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Prolongation and Torsade de Pointes:** Citalopram causes dose-dependent QT prolongation. It should not exceed 40 mg/day. Use with caution in patients with congestive heart failure, bradyarrhythmias, or predispositions to hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia. Electrolyte imbalances should be corrected prior to initiation [Australian].
- **Clinical Worsening and Suicide Risk:** Patients of all ages, particularly young adults (18–24 years), should be monitored for suicidal ideation and behaviour, especially during the first few months of therapy or following dose changes [Australian]. 
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** A potentially life-threatening condition characterised by neuromuscular excitation, altered mental status, and autonomic dysfunction. Discontinue citalopram immediately if suspected [Australian].
- **Haemorrhage:** SSRIs increase the risk of bleeding (e.g., ecchymosis, gastrointestinal bleeding, postpartum haemorrhage). Caution is advised in patients taking anticoagulants or antiplatelet agents (NSAIDs, aspirin) [Australian].
- **Seizures:** Use with caution in patients with a history of seizures; discontinue therapy if seizures develop or increase in frequency [Australian].
- **Hyponatraemia:** Reported rarely, likely due to SIADH; elderly patients are at higher risk [Australian].
- **Mania/Bipolar Disorder:** Citalopram may precipitate a manic episode in patients with undiagnosed bipolar disorder. Screen patients for psychiatric history before initiation [Australian].
- **Angle-closure Glaucoma:** Mydriasis may occur; use with caution in patients with raised intraocular pressure or at-risk anatomy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea, somnolence, dry mouth, increased sweating, insomnia, headache [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Tremor, diarrhoea, ejaculation disorder, agitation, anorexia, anxiety, impotence, fatigue, yawning, upper respiratory tract infection [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Photosensitivity, urticaria, hypotension, tachycardia, syncope, myalgias [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Seizures, Torsade de Pointes, hepatitis, hypokalaemia, anaphylactic reactions [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** QT prolongation, Serotonin Syndrome, suicidality, severe haemorrhage, and hyponatraemia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **QT-prolonging drugs:** Concomitant use increases the risk of arrhythmias (e.g., pimozide is contraindicated) [Australian].
    - **Serotonergic agents:** Increased risk of Serotonin Syndrome with triptans, opioids (tramadol), and St John’s Wort [Australian].
    - **CNS depressants/Alcohol:** Use with alcohol is not advisable [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP2C19 Inhibitors:** Cimetidine and omeprazole increase citalopram levels. Maximum citalopram dose is limited to 20 mg/day when co-administered [Australian].
    - **Metoprolol:** Citalopram increases metoprolol concentrations two-fold, though blood pressure effects may not be significantly altered [Australian].
    - **TCAs:** Citalopram may increase desipramine levels [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C [Australian]:** SSRIs have been associated with Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN) when used in late pregnancy.
    - Newborns should be monitored for withdrawal or toxic effects (e.g., respiratory distress, seizures, temperature instability) if citalopram is used until delivery [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Citalopram is excreted in human milk at very low concentrations. Caution is advised as the impact on the infant is not fully determined [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggest citalopram may affect sperm quality and reduce fertility indices, although female fertility in rats was unaffected [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Starting dose is 10 mg/day with a maximum recommended dose of 20 mg/day due to increased AUC and half-life in this population [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Recommended starting dose is 10 mg/day for the first two weeks. Maximum dose is 20 mg/day. Titrate with caution in severe impairment [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment required for mild-to-moderate impairment. No data are available for severe impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min) [Australian].
- **CYP2C19 Poor Metabolisers:** Starting dose of 10 mg/day is recommended, with a maximum dose of 20 mg/day [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective inhibitor of serotonin (5-HT) reuptake with minimal effect on noradrenaline, dopamine, or GABA uptake. It lacks significant affinity for most neurotransmitter receptors (histamine, muscarinic, adrenergic) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability ~80%; $T_{max}$ ~3.8 hours. Unaffected by food [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** $V_d$ ~12–17 L/kg; protein binding <80% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised to active demethylcitalopram (DCIT) and other metabolites via CYP2C19, CYP3A4, and CYP2D6. Citalopram is the predominant compound in plasma [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life ~1.5 days. Excreted primarily via the liver (85%) and kidneys (12–23% unchanged) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Depression:** Trials in over 1,000 patients demonstrated superiority over placebo. Minimal effective dose established at 20 mg/day. Severe or recurrent depression showed better response at 40 mg or 60 mg/day (note: current safety guidelines now limit max dose to 40 mg) [Australian].
- **Comparators:** Citalopram showed equivalent efficacy to tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs) and other SSRIs, including fluoxetine and fluvoxamine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C in the original container [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
