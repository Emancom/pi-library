# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory guidance on the clinical use of levocarnitine injection.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and not less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels (normal range 40 to 50 µmol/L) to prompt initiation or adjustment of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no known contraindications listed in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon signs of hypersensitivity [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Note):** While this document pertains to the injection, it is noted that safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine in renal failure has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In ESRD, while efficacy in increasing carnitine levels is proven, the direct effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) has not been determined [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Very Common Effects
Adverse events reported in ESRD clinical trials with a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), chest pain (12%), accidental injury (10%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), rhinitis (9%), dyspnea (7%) [US].
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%) [US].

### Other Adverse Effects
- **Transient Effects:** Nausea and vomiting have been observed. Less frequent reactions include body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Serious Effects:** Seizures and anaphylactic reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels regularly after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins or albumin, suggesting a low potential for protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a letter grade, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US]. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The benefits to the mother should be weighed against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake for the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for pediatric patients are included under the metabolic disorder and ESRD dosing guidelines [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in available sources, though caution is generally advised in patients with age-related renal decline [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. The injection is preferred over oral forms in this population to avoid toxic metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments by severity are not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Corrects carnitine deficiency (defined as plasma concentrations <20 µmol/L at one week post-term) and improves the ratio of acylcarnitine to levocarnitine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean trough plasma concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in dialysis patients demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **US:** Rx-only. Poison schedule not applicable.
