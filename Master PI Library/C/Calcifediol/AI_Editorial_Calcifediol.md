# Editorial Product Information: Calcifediol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of calcifediol for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the current approved PI in their specific region before prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Calcifediol monohydrate (25-hydroxycholecalciferol).
- **Chemical Class:** Vitamin D3 analogue/metabolite.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Vistella** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A11CC06.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Orange, soft gelatin capsules containing 255 micrograms of calcifediol [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Vitamin D Deficiency:** Treatment of vitamin D deficiency in adults and maintenance therapy as required [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard dose is one 255-microgram capsule administered once per month [Australian].
- **Dose Titration:** Higher doses may be initiated based on the severity of deficiency as verified by analytical testing; however, the frequency must not exceed one capsule every fortnight [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - **Vistella** must **NOT** be administered daily [Australian].
    - Potency is expressed in micrograms; these units are not interchangeable with International Units (IU) used for cholecalciferol (Vitamin D3) [Australian].
- **Monitoring and Duration:** Serum 25(OH)D levels should be monitored 3 to 4 months after initiating treatment. Therapy should be discontinued or the frequency reduced once plasma levels stabilise within the target range [Australian].
- **Dietary Considerations:** Clinicians should account for total dietary vitamin D intake and sunlight exposure when determining the dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to calcifediol or any excipients [Australian].
- Hypercalcemia (serum calcium > 2.620 mmol/L) [Australian].
- Hypercalciuria [Australian].
- Calcium lithiasis (kidney stones) [Australian].
- Hypervitaminosis D [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Hypercalcemia Monitoring:** To ensure clinical response and safety, clinicians must monitor 25(OH)D, serum calcium, phosphorus, and alkaline phosphatase. Additionally, 24-hour urinary calcium and phosphorus should be monitored [Australian]. A decline in alkaline phosphatase often precedes hypercalcemia.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution. In severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min), the pharmacological effect may be significantly reduced as transformation to calcitriol occurs in the kidney [Australian].
- **Cardiac Considerations:** Use with extreme caution in patients with heart failure or those taking digitalis glycosides; monitor serum calcium twice weekly during treatment initiation to prevent arrhythmias [Australian].
- **Specific Clinical Conditions:**
    - **Hypoparathyroidism:** Activity of calcifediol may be reduced due to low 1-alpha-hydroxylase activation [Australian].
    - **Granulomatous Diseases:** Use with caution in sarcoidosis or tuberculosis due to increased sensitivity to Vitamin D [Australian].
    - **Primary Hyperparathyroidism:** Increased risk of hypercalcemia/hypercalciuria [Australian].
- **Excipient Warnings:** Contains 1% ethanol (4.98 mg/cap), sorbitol (avoid in hereditary fructose intolerance), and sunset yellow (potential for allergic reactions/asthma) [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** Calcifediol may interfere with the Zlatkis-Zak method, causing false elevations in serum cholesterol [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally uncommon (≥ 0.1% to < 1%) but may be common (≥ 1% to < 10%) at higher doses. Most significant effects are related to excessive Vitamin D intake and resulting hypercalcemia.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Abdominal discomfort, Dyspepsia | Uncommon [Australian] |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, constipation, dry mouth, metallic taste | Hypercalcemia-related |
| **Nervous System** | Weakness, fatigue, drowsiness, headache, irritability | Moderate hypercalcemia |
| **Cardiac** | Arrhythmias | Hypercalcemia-related |
| **Renal** | Nephrocalcinosis, polyuria, polydipsia, proteinuria | Hypercalcemia-related |
| **Metabolism** | Hypercalcemia, hypercholesterolemia, elevated BUN | Hypercalcemia-related |
| **Eye Disorders** | Photophobia, conjunctivitis, corneal calcification | Rare (at high doses) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Phenytoin, phenobarbital, and primidone may reduce calcifediol plasma concentrations via CYP3A4 induction [Australian].
- **Cardiac Glycosides:** Calcifediol-induced hypercalcemia may potentiate digoxin toxicity [Australian].
- **Absorption Inhibitors:** Cholestyramine, colestipol, or orlistat can decrease calcifediol absorption. Space doses by at least 2 hours [Australian].
- **Thiazide Diuretics:** Risk of hypercalcemia when co-administered [Australian].
- **Paraffin/Mineral Oil:** May decrease absorption due to the liposolubility of calcifediol [Australian].
- **Antibiotics:** Penicillin, neomycin, and chloramphenicol may increase calcium absorption [Australian].
- **Corticosteroids:** Counteract the effects of Vitamin D analogues [Australian].
- **Additive Effects:** Avoid co-administration with other Vitamin D analogues or uncontrolled calcium supplements [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Do not use during pregnancy. Animal studies show fetal skeletal abnormalities at high doses (12–52 times clinical dose). Human data suggests hypercalcemia during pregnancy may lead to supravalvular aortic stenosis and intellectual disability in neonates [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Calcifediol is excreted in breast milk. Risk to the infant cannot be excluded; use is not recommended during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on male or female fertility was observed in rat studies at doses up to 42 times the clinical dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Efficacy and safety not established; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources, though general precautions regarding renal and cardiac function apply.
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires periodic monitoring of calcium and phosphorus. Reduced efficacy in severe impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Calcifediol (25-hydroxycholecalciferol) is the precursor to calcitriol (1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol), the most active form of Vitamin D3. It increases intestinal calcium/phosphorus absorption, enhances bone mineralisation via osteoblast stimulation, and inhibits PTH secretion [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well-absorbed in the intestine; unlike cholecalciferol, it is not dependent on bile acids or micelle formation. Tmax is approximately 5.5 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Bound to vitamin D-binding protein (DBP). Stored in adipose and muscle, but less lipophilic than Vitamin D3 [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Inactivated via hydroxylation to 24,25-OH2D by CYP24A1 [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is 12 to 21 days; primarily excreted in the bile [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Phase 3 Study:** A randomised, double-blind trial in 303 post-menopausal women with Vitamin D deficiency (< 50 nmol/L).
- **Outcomes:** 255 mcg/month calcifediol achieved target 25(OH)D levels (> 30 ng/mL) in 13.5% of patients at 1 month and 35% at 4 months. Mean increases in 25(OH)D were 9.7 ng/mL at 1 month and 14.9 ng/mL at 4 months. Levels remained stable through 12 months with no evidence of accumulation [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** PVC/PVDC-Al blisters [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
