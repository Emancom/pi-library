# Editorial Product Information: cetirizine hydrochloride

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **cetirizine hydrochloride**
- Chemical class: Selective peripheral histamine H1‑receptor antagonist (antihistamine)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous (IV) injection; oral preparations discussed in clinical data and safety experience but IV dosing recommendations are provided for the injectable product in the available sources

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 1995 [US]
- Date of last revision/update reported in the source PI: March 2026 [US]
- Withdrawal/suspension/restriction history: Not specified in available sources for any jurisdiction other than the recommendation below restricting use in certain pediatric impaired‑function populations
- Other jurisdictions' registration dates or last-revision dates: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of acute urticaria (hives) in adults and children 6 months of age and older.
- Limitations of use: Not recommended in pediatric patients less than 6 years of age with impaired renal or hepatic function. [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adults)
- Recommended IV dose for adults and adolescents ≥ 12 years: 10 mg administered by intravenous injection once every 24 hours, as needed for acute urticaria.

### Dosing by pediatric age group
- Children 6 to 11 years: 5 mg or 10 mg IV depending on symptom severity; administered once every 24 hours as needed.
- Children 6 months to 5 years: 2.5 mg IV administered once every 24 hours as needed.
- Not recommended in pediatric patients less than 6 years of age with impaired renal or hepatic function. [US]

### Administration instructions
- For intravenous administration only.
- Administer as an intravenous push over 1 to 2 minutes.
- Single‑use injectable product; use appropriate aseptic technique.
- If cetirizine hydrochloride IV is used prior to administration of an infusion product, follow the infusion product prescribing information for any additional instructions.

### Titration and missed dose
- Titration schedules: Not specified in available sources.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **cetirizine hydrochloride** or any ingredient of the formulation.
- Known hypersensitivity to levocetirizine.
- Known hypersensitivity to hydroxyzine.
- Any other contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Somnolence / Sedation**: Sedation and somnolence have been reported with cetirizine hydrochloride. Exercise caution when driving, operating machinery, or performing other tasks requiring alertness. Avoid concurrent use with alcohol or other CNS depressants because of the potential for additive impairment.
- Monitor for antihistaminic side effects (e.g., sedation) in patients with hepatic impairment and in patients with renal impairment; increased sensitivity may occur.
- Use in pediatric patients with impaired renal or hepatic function: Not recommended in children < 6 years with renal or hepatic impairment because of absence of pharmacokinetic and safety data. [US]
- Geriatric patients: No overall differences in safety observed in available trials, but greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out; monitor accordingly.
- QT interval: Clinical studies did not show clinically significant mean QTc prolongation at recommended doses, but cetirizine produced small mean QTc changes at higher doses and additive QTc effects were observed when combined with certain drugs that prolong QTc (see Section 8).
- Overdose management: No specific antidote; treatment should be symptomatic and supportive. Cetirizine is not effectively removed by dialysis (see Section 10).

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - Theophylline: No clinically significant interaction observed at a low theophylline dose; a small decrease in cetirizine clearance was observed with a 400 mg dose of theophylline. Larger theophylline doses could potentially have a greater effect on cetirizine clearance — monitor if coadministered.
  - Ketoconazole: No clinically significant PK interaction on AUC reported for oral cetirizine at therapeutic doses, but coadministration produced additive QTc increases (see Pharmacology). No clinically significant interaction on QTc was found beyond additive effects.
  - Erythromycin: No clinically significant interaction observed on QTc when coadministered with cetirizine in the studies described.
  - Azithromycin: No clinically significant interaction observed.
  - Pseudoephedrine: No clinically significant interactions reported.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Alcohol and other central nervous system depressants: Concomitant use may increase sedation and impair alertness; concurrent use is discouraged or should be approached with caution.
- Clinically significant interaction management:
  - Monitor for increased sedation if administered with other CNS depressants or alcohol.
  - When coadministering with higher doses of theophylline or strong CYP/PGP inhibitors, consider monitoring for increased cetirizine exposure or QTc effects; adjust monitoring as clinically indicated.
- Dialysis: Cetirizine is not effectively removed by dialysis; dialysis is unlikely to be helpful for overdose unless a dialyzable co‑ingestant is present.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Risk summary: No adequate and well‑controlled studies in pregnant women with cetirizine hydrochloride are available.
- Animal data:
  - Embryofetal development studies: No teratogenicity was observed in mice, rats and rabbits at oral doses up to approximately 45×, 220×, and 260× the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) on a mg/m² basis, respectively.
  - Prenatal and postnatal development: In mice, maternal doses ≈45× MRHD produced lower pup body weight gain during lactation, while doses ≈10× MRHD did not. In rats, no adverse pup or dam developmental effects were seen up to ≈30× MRHD; excessive maternal toxicity occurred at ≈180× MRHD.
- Background risk statements: The general population risks of major birth defects and miscarriage in clinically recognized pregnancies were cited in the available source (2–4% for major birth defects; 15–20% for miscarriage), but no clinical human safety signal or definitive risk category is assigned in the provided material.
- Pregnancy category/classification: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Cetirizine has been reported to be present in human breast milk.
- Animal data: Cetirizine detected in milk of mice; in beagle dogs approximately 3% of dose recovered in milk. No adverse developmental effects on pups were observed in animal studies at doses roughly 10× MRHD in dams.
- Clinical recommendation: Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding along with the mother’s clinical need for cetirizine and potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant.

### Fertility
- Data: Not specified in available sources beyond animal reproductive toxicity summaries referenced above.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Approved use: Safety and efficacy established in patients 6 months to 17 years of age for treatment of acute urticaria [US].
- Basis: Pediatric efficacy down to 6 months is based on extrapolation from adult efficacy and supported by pharmacokinetic and oral formulation data in children; exposure from labeled IV doses in pediatric patients 6 months–17 years is expected to be similar to adults.
- Dosing: See Section 4 for age‑specific IV dosing recommendations.
- Not recommended: Use in children < 6 years with impaired renal or hepatic function is not recommended due to lack of PK and safety data. Safety and efficacy in patients < 6 months have not been established.

### Elderly use
- Clinical trial exposure: A limited number of patients ≥ 65 years were included in trials; no overall differences in safety were observed compared with younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be excluded.
- Dose adjustment: Not specified; monitor for increased sensitivity (e.g., sedation).

### Renal impairment
- Dosage adjustment: No dosage adjustment is required in patients with moderate or severe renal impairment, including patients on dialysis, according to the available PI. Monitor for antihistaminic side effects in this population.

### Hepatic impairment
- Dosage adjustment: No dosage adjustment is required in patients with hepatic impairment according to the available PI. Monitor for antihistaminic side effects.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **cetirizine hydrochloride** is a selective antagonist of human peripheral histamine H1 receptors. It is a human metabolite of hydroxyzine. Antihistaminic effects are mediated primarily via inhibition of H1 receptors; in vitro receptor binding studies show minimal affinity for non‑H1 receptors.

### Pharmacodynamics
- In adult volunteers, single 5 mg and 10 mg oral doses strongly inhibited histamine‑induced wheal and flare. Onset after a single 10 mg oral dose: approximately 20 minutes in 50% of subjects and within 1 hour in 95% of subjects; effect persisted at least 24 hours.
- In pediatric volunteers (5–12 years) and in infants (7–25 months—oral dosing 0.25 mg/kg twice daily for 4–9 days), substantial inhibition of histamine‑induced wheal and flare was demonstrated (e.g., ~90% inhibition of wheal in infants).
- In mildly asthmatic subjects, cetirizine (5–20 mg oral) blocked histamine‑induced bronchoconstriction, with near‑total blockade at 20 mg.
- Cardiac electrophysiology: Multiple studies in healthy adults showed no clinically significant mean QTc prolongation at recommended doses. In one study cetirizine 20 mg increased QTc by a mean of 9.1 ms after 10 days; ketoconazole increased QTc by 8.3 ms; the combination produced an additive increase (17.4 ms), indicating additive effects when combined with agents that affect QTc.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Intravenous administration (single‑dose crossover in healthy volunteers):
  - 5 mg IV (1–1.5 min): mean Cmax ~495 ng/mL; Tmax ~0.06 hr (range 0.03–0.07 hr); AUC∞ ~1318 ng·hr/mL.
  - 10 mg IV (1–1.5 min): mean Cmax ~1344 ng/mL; Tmax ~0.03 hr (range 0.03–2.00 hr); AUC∞ ~2746 ng·hr/mL.
  - For comparison in the same study, 10 mg oral tablet AUC∞ ~2651 ng·hr/mL.
- Oral administration:
  - Tmax approximately 1 hour in adults.
  - Multiple oral doses (10 mg once daily × 10 days) produced mean Cmax ~311 ng/mL with no accumulation.
  - Pharmacokinetics linear across oral doses 5–60 mg.
  - Food: No effect on extent of exposure (AUC) but delayed Tmax by ~1.7 hours and reduced Cmax by ~23% when taken with food.
- Distribution, plasma protein binding, elimination half‑life, route of metabolism and complete clearance parameters: Not specified in available sources (text truncated in provided PI).
- Removal by dialysis: Cetirizine is not effectively removed by dialysis.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- IV cetirizine clinical experience:
  - A randomized, double‑blind, single‑dose, non‑inferiority trial compared IV cetirizine to IV diphenhydramine in 262 adults with acute urticaria. Safety findings: adverse reactions with IV cetirizine occurred at incidences <1% (dyspepsia, feeling hot, dysgeusia, headache, paresthesia, presyncope, hyperhidrosis). Subject‑rated sedation scores were lower with IV cetirizine than with diphenhydramine at assessed time points (baseline, 1 hr, 2 hr, readiness for discharge).
  - An additional randomized, double‑blind, single‑dose study in 33 adults showed similar safety results.
- Oral cetirizine clinical data (relevant to pharmacodynamics, pediatric safety, and adverse reaction profiling):
  - Adult volunteers: 5–10 mg oral doses strongly inhibited histamine‑induced wheal and flare; onset and duration as noted in Section 11.
  - Pediatric trials: Placebo‑controlled trials in children 6–11 years and 2–5 years identified adverse reactions with ≥2% incidence (headache, pharyngitis, abdominal pain, coughing, somnolence, diarrhea, epistaxis, bronchospasm, nausea, vomiting). Trials in 6–24 months showed overall adverse experience rates similar between cetirizine and placebo; one 6–11 month trial showed increased irritability/fussiness vs placebo; in an 18‑month trial in patients ≥12 months, insomnia was reported more frequently with cetirizine vs placebo.
- Overdose case reports:
  - Adult oral overdoses (70–800 mg): most commonly somnolence and fatigue; other effects included tachycardia, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting.
  - Pediatric oral overdoses (90–300 mg): reported somnolence, difficulty walking, agitation/irritability, slurred speech, tachycardia, vomiting, mydriasis, elevated creatine phosphokinase.
