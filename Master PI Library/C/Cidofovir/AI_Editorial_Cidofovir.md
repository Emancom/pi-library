# Editorial Product Information: Cidofovir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cidofovir (as the dihydrate)
- **Chemical Class:** Acyclic nucleoside phosphonate analogue
- **Brand Name(s):** Empovir [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** J05AF06
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated solution for injection; 375 mg anhydrous cidofovir in 5 mL (75 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Emcure Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd; Distributed by Luminarie Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **CMV Retinitis:** Indicated for the treatment of cytomegalovirus (CMV) retinitis in adult patients with acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Cidofovir must be administered by a healthcare professional experienced in the care of AIDS patients. Strict adherence to the infusion rate, probenecid co-administration, and hydration protocol is required to mitigate nephrotoxicity.

- **Induction Treatment:** 5 mg/kg body weight administered as an intravenous infusion at a constant rate over 1 hour, once weekly for two consecutive weeks [Australian].
- **Maintenance Treatment:** 5 mg/kg body weight administered as an intravenous infusion over 1 hour, once every two weeks, beginning two weeks after the completion of induction [Australian].

### Mandatory Concomitant Medications
To reduce the risk of nephrotoxicity, both probenecid and intravenous saline hydration must be administered with every dose.

- **Oral Probenecid:** 
    - 2 grams administered 3 hours prior to the cidofovir infusion.
    - 1 gram administered at 2 hours and 1 gram at 8 hours after the completion of the infusion (Total: 4 grams) [Australian].
    - *Note:* Patients should eat prior to probenecid doses to reduce nausea; anti-emetics or antihistamines may be required for tolerability.
- **Intravenous Hydration:**
    - 1 litre of 0.9% (normal) saline infused over 1 hour immediately before the cidofovir infusion.
    - An optional second litre may be infused over 1–3 hours starting simultaneously with or immediately after the cidofovir infusion [Australian].

### Dose Adjustments (Renal)
- **Reduction:** The dose must be reduced from 5 mg/kg to 3 mg/kg if serum creatinine increases by 0.027 to 0.043 mmol/L (0.3 to 0.4 mg/dL) above the patient's baseline [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Therapy must be stopped if serum creatinine increases by $\ge$ 0.044 mmol/L ($\ge$ 0.5 mg/dL) or if persistent proteinuria $\ge$ 2+ develops [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Renal Impairment:** Baseline serum creatinine > 0.133 mmol/L (> 1.5 mg/dL), creatinine clearance $\le$ 0.92 mL/s ($\le$ 55 mL/min), or proteinuria $\ge$ 2+ ($\ge$ 100 mg/dL) [Australian].
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Concomitant use with potentially nephrotoxic drugs (e.g., amphotericin B, foscarnet, IV pentamidine, vancomycin, aminoglycosides, NSAIDs). Such agents must be discontinued at least 7 days prior to starting cidofovir [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to cidofovir, probenecid, or other sulfur-containing medications [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Administration Route:** Direct intraocular injection is contraindicated due to risks of significant intraocular pressure decrease and vision impairment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Dose-dependent nephrotoxicity is the major dose-limiting toxicity. Proximal tubular cell injury may occur, potentially leading to acute renal failure and the need for dialysis.
- **Ocular Effects:** Iritis and uveitis have been reported. Regular ophthalmologic follow-up is required. Decreased intraocular pressure (ocular hypotony) may occur and is more common in patients with diabetes mellitus [Australian].
- **Hematologic:** Neutropenia has been observed; white blood cell counts with differentials must be monitored regularly [Australian].
- **Carcinogenicity:** Based on animal data showing mammary adenocarcinomas and Zymbal’s gland carcinomas, cidofovir should be considered a potential human carcinogen [Australian].
- **Male Fertility:** Animal studies show reduced testes weight and hypospermia. Men should be advised of the risk of infertility and must use barrier contraception during and for 3 months following treatment [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Renal Function:** Serum creatinine and urine protein levels must be reviewed within 48 hours prior to **each** dose [Australian].
- **Hematology:** Neutrophil counts should be monitored regularly [Australian].
- **Ocular:** Regular monitoring for signs of iritis, uveitis, and changes in intraocular pressure [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Zidovudine:** Probenecid reduces the clearance of zidovudine. Patients on zidovudine should either temporarily discontinue it or reduce the dose by 50% on days cidofovir/probenecid is administered [Australian].
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Synergistic risk of renal damage when used with aminoglycosides, amphotericin B, foscarnet, pentamidine, vancomycin, or NSAIDs [Australian].
- **Probenecid Interactions:** Probenecid interferes with the renal tubular secretion of many drugs, including paracetamol, acyclovir, ACE inhibitors, methotrexate, and various NSAIDs [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category D. Cidofovir is teratogenic, embryotoxic, and carcinogenic in animal studies. It must not be used during pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during and for one month after treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if cidofovir is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued if the mother is receiving cidofovir [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential for hypospermia and reduced testes weight [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients under 18 years; use is not recommended in this population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients over 60 years. Use with caution due to the higher prevalence of reduced glomerular function [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in moderate to severe impairment. See Section 3 for adjustment/discontinuation criteria for mild changes during therapy [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients with hepatic disease [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Cidofovir is an acyclic nucleoside phosphonate that is intracellularly phosphorylated to its active metabolite, cidofovir diphosphate. It selectively inhibits viral DNA polymerases (CMV, HSV-1, HSV-2), preventing viral DNA synthesis [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Demonstrates 8- to 600-fold higher affinity for viral DNA polymerases over human cellular DNA polymerases.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Low protein binding ($\le$10%).
    - **Metabolism:** Not dependent on or facilitated by viral infections.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily unchanged in urine (80-100%). Renal clearance exceeds creatinine clearance, indicating active tubular secretion. Probenecid significantly reduces renal clearance by blocking this secretion [Australian].
    - **Intracellular Half-life:** Cidofovir diphosphate persists in cells with a half-life of 17–65 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study GS-93-106:** Open-label, multicentre study of 48 untreated patients. Immediate treatment with cidofovir (5 mg/kg) resulted in a median time to progression of 120 days vs 22 days in the deferred group [Australian].
- **Study GS-93-107:** Study of 100 patients with relapsing CMV retinitis. Median time to progression was significantly longer in the 5 mg/kg maintenance group (115 days) compared to the 3 mg/kg group (49 days) [Australian].
- **Study GS-93-105:** Randomised controlled study comparing immediate vs. deferred treatment. Median time to progression was 64 days for the 3 mg/kg maintenance group vs. 21 days for the deferred group [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C. Do not refrigerate or freeze [Australian].
- **Admixture Stability:** Diluted infusion solutions may be stored in a refrigerator (2–8°C) for up to 24 hours if prepared aseptically. Allow the solution to reach room temperature before administration [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
