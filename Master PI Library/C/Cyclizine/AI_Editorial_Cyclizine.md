# Editorial Product Information: Cyclizine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- At the time of this editorial, documentation from other regulatory authorities (e.g., US FDA, European EMA, Health Canada) was not provided for synthesis. Consequently, this document reflects the harmonised position based on the available Australian regulatory data.
- **Purpose:** This document serves as a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cyclizine lactate, a histamine H1 receptor antagonist of the piperazine class.
- **Brand Name(s):** Cyclizine-AFT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R60AE03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Clear, colourless solution for injection; 50 mg cyclizine lactate per 1 mL ampoule [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Post-operative Nausea and Vomiting (PONV):** Indicated for the prevention of nausea and vomiting in the post-operative period [Australian].
- **Note:** There is currently no data supporting the use of cyclizine injection for the treatment of established PONV [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 50 mg intravenously up to three times daily.
- **Dosing for PONV Prevention:** The initial dose should be administered via slow intravenous injection 20 minutes before the anticipated end of surgery [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Must be injected slowly into the bloodstream.
    - Minimal withdrawal of blood into the syringe is recommended during administration.
    - Treatment should commence within 24 hours of surgery and must not exceed a 48-hour duration.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources (primarily intended for clinical/hospital administration).

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to cyclizine or any of the excipients (lactic acid, water for injection) [Australian].
- Severe heart failure or acute myocardial infarction; cyclizine may cause a decrease in cardiac output accompanied by increased heart rate, mean arterial pressure, and pulmonary wedge pressure [Australian].
- Acute alcohol intoxication; the anti-emetic properties may increase alcohol toxicity [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anticholinergic Effects:** Cyclizine possesses significant anticholinergic activity. It may exacerbate pre-existing conditions including:
    - Urinary retention or prostatic hypertrophy.
    - Glaucoma (angle-closure, untreated intraocular hypertension, or uncontrolled primary open-angle).
    - Hepatic disease.
    - Phaeochromocytoma and hypertension.
    - Epilepsy.
    - Obstructive gastrointestinal disorders.
- **Respiratory Precautions:** Anticholinergic effects may thicken bronchial secretions and reduce volume, potentially obstructing the respiratory tract. Use with caution in patients with asthma or COPD [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Disorders:** Rare cases of paralysis have been reported with IV use, particularly in patients with underlying neuromuscular disorders. Caution is advised for all patients during IV administration [Australian].
- **Abuse Potential:** Reports of abuse (oral and IV) exist due to euphoric or hallucinatory effects [Australian].
- **Porphyria:** Cyclizine should be avoided in patients with porphyria [Australian].
- **Driving and Machinery:** Drowsiness and impaired motor skills may occur. Patients should not drive or operate machinery until their individual response is established.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The frequency of adverse events has not been accurately determined from clinical trials [Australian]. 

- **Neurological:** Drowsiness, sedation, headache, coordination abnormalities, dystonia, dyskinesia, extrapyramidal motor disturbances, tremor, convulsions, dizziness, and paraesthesia. Transient speech disorders and rare paralysis of limbs have been reported.
- **Psychiatric:** Disorientation, restlessness, nervousness, euphoria, insomnia, and hallucinations (auditory/visual), especially in overdose.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Dry mouth, nose, and throat; constipation; duodenogastric reflux; abdominal pain; decreased appetite.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, palpitations, arrhythmias, hypertension, and hypotension.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Hepatic dysfunction, hypersensitivity hepatitis, and cholestatic jaundice.
- **Dermatological:** Urticaria, pruritus, drug rash, angioedema, fixed drug eruption, and photosensitivity.
- **Injection Site:** Vein tracking, erythema, pain, thrombophlebitis, and blisters.
- **Hematological:** Agranulocytosis, leukopenia, haemolytic anaemia, and thrombocytopenia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects occur with alcohol, hypnotics, tranquilisers, anaesthetics, antipsychotics, and barbiturates [Australian].
- **Opioids:** Enhances the soporific effect of pethidine. It may counteract the haemodynamic benefits of opioid analgesics [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** Additive antimuscarinic action with atropine, tricyclic antidepressants, and MAOIs. Atropine may reduce the anti-emetic efficacy of cyclizine in PONV [Australian].
- **Ototoxic Drugs:** May mask warning signs of damage from drugs such as aminoglycoside antibacterials [Australian].
- **Enzymatic Activity:** In vitro data suggests weak inhibition of CYP2D6 (likely clinically irrelevant) and moderate inhibition of estrone sulfotransferase [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3**. Animal studies (rats, mice, rabbits) have shown malformations, including cleft palate, at doses above 25–50 mg/kg/day. Use in human pregnancy is not recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion into human milk is unknown. Use in breastfeeding women is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adequate human or non-clinical data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children for PONV have not been established; use is not recommended as children may be more sensitive to anticholinergic effects [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific studies are lacking; however, clinical experience suggests that standard adult dosing is appropriate [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing adjustments not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to anticholinergic effects; specific dosing adjustments not specified [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Histamine H1 receptor antagonist. While it possesses anticholinergic and anti-emetic properties, the exact mechanism for suppressing nausea and vomiting is unknown [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Following a 25 mg IV bolus, $V_{ss}$ is approximately 13.43 L/kg [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised to norcyclizine (N-demethylated derivative), which has minimal antihistaminic activity.
    - **Elimination:** Plasma elimination half-life is approximately 13.5 hours for cyclizine and 14 hours for norcyclizine. Less than 1% of a 50 mg dose is excreted unchanged in the urine over 24 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Cochrane Review:** A systematic review of 737 studies (including 10 studies on cyclizine) identified cyclizine as one of only eight drugs (out of 60) effectively achieving benefits in nausea, vomiting, and rescue anti-emetic use compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Comparative Efficacy:**
    - Superior to placebo and dexamethasone at 3 and 6 hours for nausea severity in post-caesarean patients [Australian].
    - Demonstrated comparable efficacy to droperidol when included in PCA morphine infusions [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Light:** Protect from light; store in the original carton.
- **Handling:** Single-use ampoules; any residue must be discarded [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine – Schedule 4 [Australian].
