# Editorial Product Information: Ceftaroline Fosamil

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ceftaroline fosamil for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory information into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ceftaroline fosamil monoacetate monohydrate (a semi-synthetic prodrug).
- **Chemical Class:** Cephalosporin antibiotic (often referred to as a fifth-generation cephalosporin with anti-MRSA activity).
- **Brand Names:** **Zinforo** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01DI02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Powder for injection; 600 mg of ceftaroline fosamil (equivalent to 530 mg of active ceftaroline) per vial.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Complicated Skin and Soft Tissue Infections (cSSTI):** Treatment of infections proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible bacteria [Australian].
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP):** Treatment of infections proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible bacteria [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Normal Renal Function: CrCL > 50 mL/min)
*   **Adults and Adolescents (12 to <18 years, ≥33 kg):** 600 mg every 12 hours.
*   **Adolescents (12 to <18 years, <33 kg) and Children (2 to <12 years):** 12 mg/kg (max 400 mg) every 8 hours.
*   **Infants (2 months to <2 years):** 8 mg/kg every 8 hours.
*   **Neonates (Birth to <2 months):** 6 mg/kg every 8 hours.

### Dosing for S. aureus with Elevated MIC
For cSSTI confirmed or suspected to be caused by *S. aureus* with a ceftaroline MIC of 2 mg/L or 4 mg/L, a **High Dose** regimen is recommended:
*   **Adults:** 600 mg every 8 hours (administered over 120 minutes).
*   **Children (2 to <18 years):** 12 mg/kg (max 600 mg) every 8 hours.
*   **Infants (2 months to <2 years):** 10 mg/kg every 8 hours.

### Administration Instructions
*   **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with 20 mL of sterile water for injections.
*   **Dilution:** Must be further diluted (e.g., 0.9% NaCl, 5% Dextrose, Lactated Ringer’s). The final concentration must not exceed 12 mg/mL.
*   **Infusion Time:** Standard dosing is typically infused over 5–60 minutes. High-dose regimens for *S. aureus* (MIC > 1 mg/L) require a 120-minute infusion [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   Hypersensitivity to the active substance (ceftaroline fosamil) or the excipient L-arginine [Australian].
*   Hypersensitivity to any antibiotic within the cephalosporin class [Australian].
*   Immediate and severe hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis) to any other type of β-lactam antibiotic, such as penicillins or carbapenems [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal reactions are possible. Cross-reactivity may occur in patients allergic to other β-lactams.
*   **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhoea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents; severity ranges from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis.
*   **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Ceftaroline must be discontinued immediately if SCAR is suspected.
*   **Neurotoxicity:** Cephalosporin treatment is associated with risks of encephalopathy, seizures, and myoclonus. Risk is higher in the elderly and those with renal impairment.
*   **Direct Antiglobulin Test (Coombs Test) Seroconversion:** Positive Coombs tests occurred in up to 32.3% of patients in high-dose studies. While clinical haemolysis is rare, it remains a potential risk.
*   **Non-Susceptible Organisms:** As with other antibiotics, superinfections may occur. Ceftaroline is **not** active against *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* or ESBL-producing *Enterobacterales* [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Table (Adults)
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Coombs Direct Test Positive |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Diarrhoea, headache, nausea, rash, pruritus, increased transaminases, infusion site reactions (erythema, phlebitis, pain) |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Anaemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, anaphylaxis, urticaria, *C. difficile* colitis, prolonged prothrombin time (PT/INR), increased blood creatinine |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Agranulocytosis |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, *C. difficile* colitis, SCAR, and haemolytic anaemia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No clinical drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted. Ceftaroline is not a substrate, inhibitor, or inducer of major CYP450 enzymes.
*   **Transporters:** It is not a substrate of P-gp or BCRP. It does not inhibit OCT2, OAT1, or OAT3.
*   **Probenecid:** Interactions with drugs that inhibit active renal secretion (like probenecid) are not expected based on in vitro data [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Clinical data are lacking. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk. Animal studies did not indicate direct harmful effects on development, though skeletal variations (angulated hyoid alae) were noted in rabbits at sub-therapeutic exposures.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if ceftaroline is excreted in human milk. As many β-lactams are excreted, breastfeeding women should be treated only if clearly indicated; interruption of breastfeeding is recommended [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** No adverse effects on rat fertility at 4-fold human exposures.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Approved from birth. Dosing is weight-based and frequency is typically every 8 hours.
*   **Elderly Use:** No adjustment required if CrCL > 50 mL/min. Caution is advised due to the higher prevalence of renal impairment and risk of neurotoxicity.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustments are mandatory for CrCL ≤ 50 mL/min.
    *   **CrCL > 30 to ≤ 50 mL/min:** 400 mg q12h (Adults).
    *   **CrCL ≥ 15 to ≤ 30 mL/min:** 300 mg q12h (Adults).
    *   **ESRD (including haemodialysis):** 200 mg q12h (Adults). Ceftaroline is haemodialysable; administer after dialysis.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment recommended as ceftaroline does not undergo significant hepatic metabolism [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits cell wall synthesis by binding to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs). Uniquely, it has a high affinity for PBP2a in MRSA and altered PBPs in *S. pneumoniae*.
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** The time that the unbound plasma concentration exceeds the MIC (%fT > MIC) is the best predictor of efficacy.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is low (~20%).
    *   **Metabolism:** The prodrug is converted to active ceftaroline by plasma phosphatases. It is NOT metabolised by CYP450.
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily renal (88% in urine). Elimination half-life is approximately 2.5 hours in adults.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **cSSTI:** The CANVAS 1 and 2 trials demonstrated non-inferiority to vancomycin plus aztreonam. Clinical cure rates were ~91%. The COVERS study further established efficacy in cSSTI with systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS).
*   **CAP:** The FOCUS 1 and 2 trials demonstrated non-inferiority to ceftriaxone. Clinical cure rates were ~84%. High efficacy was noted against *S. pneumoniae* [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Vials:** Store below 25°C in the original package to protect from light [Australian].
*   **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:** To reduce microbial hazard, use as soon as practicable. In-use stability is demonstrated for 6 hours at room temperature and 24 hours under refrigeration (2–8°C) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
