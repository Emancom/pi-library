# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single, clinically applicable reference.
- **Note**: This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class**: Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**:
    - **Intravenous Injection**: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors)**: For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders**:
    - **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose**: Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours.
    - **Frequency**: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose**: The highest recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
    - **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration**: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustment**: Dosage should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Patients**: Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **ESRD Patients**: Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) levels. Normal range is 40–50 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Activity**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral formulation precaution)**: While the injection is indicated for ESRD, the safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are often confounded by underlying pathology, particularly in the ESRD population.

### Frequency ≥5% (Regardless of Causality) in ESRD Clinical Trials:
*   **Body as a Whole**: Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%), asthenia (8–12%) [US].
*   **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
*   **Digestive**: Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
*   **Metabolic/Nutritional**: Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%) [US].
*   **Nervous System**: Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].
*   **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].

### Serious and Post-marketing Effects:
*   **Neurologic**: Seizures (both new-onset and exacerbated).
*   **Hypersensitivity**: Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm.
*   **Miscellaneous**: Body odor, gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitor INR levels at therapy initiation and after any dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**: Levocarnitine is not protein-bound; therefore, displacement interactions are unlikely [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Category**: Not formally assigned a letter grade in the current labelling; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). Refer to Section 3 for specific instructions [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. The drug is easily removed by dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and subsequent energy production. In metabolic disorders, it clears toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitines, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination**: Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies**: In 15 healthy volunteers, IV bolus administration (20 mg/kg) showed a distribution half-life of 0.585 hours and total body clearance of 4.00 L/h.
- **ESRD Trials**: In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma carnitine from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While carnitine levels increase, clinical outcome improvements in ESRD have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Precautions**: Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Compatibility**: Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when diluted in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentration 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States**: Rx-only.
