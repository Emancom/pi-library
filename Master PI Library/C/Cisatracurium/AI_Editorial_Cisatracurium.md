# Editorial Product Information: Cisatracurium

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulations and the current official PI for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cisatracurium besilate, a stereoisomer of atracurium.
- **Chemical Class:** Intermediate-duration, non-depolarising benzylisoquinolinium skeletal muscle relaxant.
- **Brand Names:** Cisatracurium-AFT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Solution for injection/infusion [Australian].
    - 2 mg/mL (available in 5 mL, 10 mL, and 30 mL containers) [Australian].
    - 5 mg/mL (available in 2.5 mL containers) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) bolus injection or continuous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Used during surgical and other medical procedures as an adjunct to general anesthesia or sedation in the intensive care unit (ICU) [Australian].
- Facilitation of tracheal intubation [Australian].
- Relaxation of skeletal muscles to facilitate mechanical ventilation [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Tracheal Intubation:** The standard recommended dose is 0.15 mg/kg body weight, providing optimal conditions within 120 seconds [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** Neuromuscular blockade may be extended with bolus doses of 0.03 mg/kg, providing approximately 20 minutes of additional relaxation [Australian].
- **Continuous Infusion:** Initial rate of 3 µg/kg/min (0.18 mg/kg/hr) to restore 89–99% T1 suppression. A maintenance rate of 1 to 2 µg/kg/min is typically sufficient [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (1 month to 12 years)
- **Tracheal Intubation:** 0.15 mg/kg body weight administered over 5 to 10 seconds [Australian].
- **Maintenance (Infusion):** Same initial and maintenance rates as adults (3 µg/kg/min initially, then 1–2 µg/kg/min) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Compatibility:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride (0.9%), Glucose (5%), and combinations thereof [Australian].
- **Physical Incompatibilities:** Not chemically stable in Lactated Ringer’s Injection. Must not be mixed with alkaline solutions (e.g., thiopentone) or specific agents like ketorolac, trometamol, or propofol emulsion [Australian].
- **ICU Use:** Initial infusion rate of 3 µg/kg/min is recommended, though wide interpatient variation exists (range 0.5 to 10.2 µg/kg/min) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to cisatracurium besilate, atracurium, or benzenesulfonic acid [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neuromuscular Paralysis:** This agent paralyses respiratory and skeletal muscles without affecting consciousness or the pain threshold. It must only be administered by clinicians skilled in anesthesia and airway management; facilities for intubation and oxygenation must be immediately available [Australian].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Laudanosine, a metabolite, may accumulate during prolonged ICU administration. While linked to hypotension and seizures in animal models, its clinical significance in humans remains unclear, though seizures have been rarely reported in ICU patients [Australian].
- **Cross-Sensitivity:** Caution is advised in patients with hypersensitivity to other neuromuscular blocking agents, as cross-sensitivity rates exceed 50% [Australian].
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Patients with myasthenia gravis or other neuromuscular diseases exhibit significantly increased sensitivity. Initial doses should not exceed 0.02 mg/kg [Australian].
- **Burns:** Potential for resistance and shortened duration of action; increased dosing may be necessary [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Neuromuscular function monitoring is strongly recommended to tailor individual dosing [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorized by frequency based on surgical clinical trials [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>1%)** | None reported |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Bradycardia (0.4%), Hypotension (0.2%), Flushing (0.2%), Bronchospasm (0.2%), Rash (0.1%) |
| **Very Rare (<0.1%)** | Severe anaphylactic reactions |

- **ICU Patients:** One case of bronchospasm was reported in clinical trials. Rare reports of prolonged recovery (up to several hours) and muscle weakness/myopathy have occurred following long-term use, typically in patients receiving concurrent corticosteroids [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Enhancements (Increased Effect)
- **Volatile Anaesthetics:** Enflurane, isoflurane, and halothane can extend the duration of action by 15–20% [Australian].
- **Antibiotics:** Aminoglycosides, polymyxins, spectinomycin, tetracyclines, lincomycin, and clindamycin [Australian].
- **Anti-arrhythmics:** Propranolol, calcium channel blockers, lignocaine, procainamide, and quinidine [Australian].
- **Diuretics:** Furosemide, thiazides, mannitol, and acetazolamide [Australian].
- **Others:** Magnesium salts, lithium salts, and ketamine [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Reductions (Decreased Effect)
- **Anticonvulsants:** Chronic administration of phenytoin or carbamazepine [Australian].
- **Cholinesterase Inhibitors:** Drugs used for Alzheimer’s disease (e.g., donepezil) may shorten the duration of the block [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category C. Animal studies show no fetal toxicity, but no adequate human trials exist. Use only if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if cisatracurium or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Safety has not been established [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Neonates (< 1 month):** No dosage recommendations can be made as this population has not been studied [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No dose adjustments required. Pharmacokinetics are similar to younger adults, though the onset of action may be slightly slower [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose alterations required for patients with renal failure, though onset may be slightly slower [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose alterations required for end-stage liver disease; onset may be slightly faster [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive antagonist at cholinergic receptors on the motor end-plate. It blocks neuromuscular transmission, a process reversible by anticholinesterase agents like neostigmine [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The ED95 is approximately 0.05 mg/kg during opioid anesthesia. It does not possess significant vagolytic or ganglion blocking activity, meaning it has no major effect on heart rate [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via Hofmann elimination (organ-independent degradation at physiological pH and temperature) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Forms laudanosine and a monoquaternary acrylate metabolite. These metabolites do not possess neuromuscular blocking activity [Australian].
    - **Half-life:** 22 to 29 minutes in healthy adults [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- The clinical program included 23 trials involving 1,295 surgical and ICU patients. Studies demonstrated that recovery profiles are independent of the duration of infusion and that the drug is effective across various ASA classes (I-IV), including those with serious cardiovascular disease undergoing bypass surgery [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store between 2°C and 8°C (Refrigerate) [Australian].
- Do not freeze [Australian].
- Protect from light [Australian].
- **Stability after dilution:** Chemically and physically stable for 12 hours at room temperature (0.1 to 2.0 mg/mL); however, since it lacks preservatives, it should ideally be used immediately or stored at 2–8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
