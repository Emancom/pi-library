# Editorial Product Information: clindamycin + benzoyl

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **clindamycin + benzoyl**
- Chemical class:
  - Clindamycin: lincosamide antibiotic (clindamycin phosphate is a prodrug converted in vivo to active clindamycin)
  - Benzoyl: benzoyl peroxide — keratolytic/oxidising agent with bactericidal activity
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Topical (dermal)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of source PI(s): Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Topical treatment of comedo, papular and pustular acne vulgaris. [Australian]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult and adolescent)
- Recommended concentration in available product: clindamycin 1% (as phosphate) with benzoyl peroxide 5% (w/w). [Australian]
- Recommended frequency: apply a thin film once daily in the evening to affected areas after cleansing the skin. [Australian]
- Recommended maximum duration of a treatment course: 11 weeks. [Australian]

### Dosing by indication
- Indication: acne vulgaris (comedonal, papular, pustular) — use the regimen above. No separate doses provided for other indications. [Australian]

### Administration instructions
- For external dermatological use only.
- Apply only to intact, non‑mucosal skin; avoid contact with mouth, eyes, lips and other mucous membranes, and avoid application to broken or severely irritated skin. In case of accidental mucous‑membrane contact, rinse thoroughly with water.
- Patients should cleanse and gently pat the skin dry before application.
- Patients should be advised that excessive application does not increase efficacy and may increase irritation or other local adverse effects.
- Patients may require 4–6 weeks of therapy before full therapeutic effect becomes apparent. [Australian]

### Titration and temporary interruption
- If excessive dryness, peeling or irritation occurs, reduce application frequency or interrupt therapy temporarily. Efficacy has not been established with dosing frequencies less than once daily. [Australian]

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to lincomycin, clindamycin, benzoyl peroxide, or any component of the formulation. [Australian]
- Current or past history of regional enteritis, ulcerative colitis, or antibiotic-associated colitis (including pseudomembranous colitis). [Australian]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/major warnings
- Not specified in available sources as a boxed warning. Monitor for serious gastrointestinal events (see below). [Australian]

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity/contact sensitisation: discontinue immediately if allergic contact dermatitis or severe irritation occurs. Patch testing in exaggerated (semi‑occluded) conditions produced contact sensitivity; true clinical incidence expected to be lower. [Australian]
- Avoid application to mucous membranes, eyes, lips or areas of broken/irritated skin. Rinse promptly if accidental contact occurs. [Australian]
- Local irritation: redness, peeling, dryness and stinging/burning may occur, especially in the first weeks. If severe local irritancy develops, discontinue therapy. Avoid occlusion, which may increase irritation. [Australian]
- Photosensitivity: benzoyl peroxide may increase sensitivity to sunlight. Advise patients to avoid deliberate or prolonged sun exposure and sunlamps; when unavoidable, use sunscreen and protective clothing. Do not commence treatment until sunburn has resolved. [Australian]
- Cosmetic effects: benzoyl peroxide may bleach hair, colored fabrics, furniture and carpeting; avoid contact. [Australian]
- Time to response: patients should be counselled that 4–6 weeks may be required to see full benefit. [Australian]

### Serious systemic antibiotic‑related precaution
- Pseudomembranous colitis: systemic clindamycin is associated with severe diarrhoea and pseudomembranous colitis, with onset up to several weeks after therapy cessation. Although topical use makes this unlikely, prolonged or significant diarrhoea or abdominal cramps during or after treatment should prompt immediate discontinuation and investigation (including testing for Clostridium difficile and toxin and colonoscopy if indicated). Appropriate treatment for colitis should be initiated when diagnosed. [Australian]

### Resistance
- Prolonged topical use of clindamycin can select for resistant microorganisms and overgrowth. Reports exist of Propionibacterium acnes (Cutibacterium acnes) resistance to clindamycin. The combination with benzoyl peroxide reduces but does not eliminate the potential for development of clindamycin resistance. Limit duration of therapy (maximum recommended 11 weeks). Local resistance prevalence and antibiotic stewardship principles should guide use. Patients with recent systemic or topical clindamycin or erythromycin exposure may harbour resistant flora. Cross‑resistance exists between clindamycin and lincomycin; inducible resistance to erythromycin may coexist (see Interactions). Clindamycin and erythromycin should not be used together. [Australian]

### Use in severe acne
- The combination may be inadequate for severe nodulocystic acne; consider appropriate systemic therapy. [Australian]

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of severe local irritation and for any new or worsening abdominal symptoms (diarrhoea, abdominal pain) that could indicate antibiotic-associated colitis. Monitor clinical response of acne lesions. No laboratory monitoring requirements specified for routine therapy. [Australian]

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Topical co‑applications that increase local irritation:
  - Concomitant use with other topical antibiotics, medicated or abrasive soaps/cleansers, cosmetics with strong drying effect, or products with high concentrations of alcohol and/or astringents may lead to cumulative skin irritation. Use with caution. No formal clinical studies of topical interactions available. [Australian]
- Antibiotic antagonism:
  - Concomitant topical use with erythromycin‑containing products is not recommended due to in vitro antagonism between clindamycin and erythromycin. [Australian]
- Neuromuscular blocking agents:
  - Systemic clindamycin has neuromuscular blocking properties that can potentiate other neuromuscular blocking agents (more likely at higher systemic clindamycin doses). Exercise caution if the patient is receiving such agents. While topical use yields minimal systemic exposure, caution is advised in those receiving neuromuscular blockers. [Australian]
- Retinoids:
  - Concurrent topical use of tretinoin, isotretinoin or tazarotene may increase irritation and may reduce efficacy of one or both agents. If combination treatment is required, apply products at different times of day (for example, one in the morning and the other in the evening). [Australian]
- Colour changes:
  - Simultaneous use of benzoyl peroxide and topical sulfonamide-containing products can produce temporary yellow/orange staining of skin and facial hair. [Australian]

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy classification/system: Pregnancy category A stated in source for the combination product. [Australian]
- Clinical data: No well‑controlled studies of the combination in pregnant women. Limited clinical data exist for topical clindamycin or benzoyl peroxide alone in pregnancy. Systemic exposure from topical application is expected to be low. Animal data: benzoyl peroxide given orally at very high doses produced reduced pup bodyweights in rats at doses far exceeding topical exposure; reproductive studies of clindamycin in animals showed no evidence of fetal harm. Use in pregnancy only if expected benefit justifies potential risk to fetus. [Australian]

### Breastfeeding
- There are no formal studies of the combination during breastfeeding. Percutaneous absorption of both agents is low; it is not known whether topical clindamycin or benzoyl peroxide are excreted in human milk after topical application. Systemic administration of clindamycin has resulted in clindamycin appearing in breast milk. Use during lactation only if expected benefit justifies potential risk to the infant. To avoid accidental infant ingestion, do not apply to the breast area if breastfeeding. [Australian]

### Fertility
- Human data: Not available. Animal data: benzoyl peroxide did not impair fertility in rats at oral doses up to 500 mg/kg/day; clindamycin phosphate did not impair fertility in rats at dietary doses up to 300 mg/kg/day. [Australian]

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Adolescents aged 12 years and above: use as per adult dosing. [Australian]
- Prepubescent children (<12 years): safety and efficacy have not been established; acne rarely presents in this age group; use not recommended unless specifically supported by clinical judgement. [Australian]

### Elderly
- No data available on use in elderly patients. Use with usual clinical caution. [Australian]

### Renal impairment
- Not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Clindamycin: a lincosamide antibiotic that binds to the 23S subunit of the bacterial ribosome inhibiting protein synthesis; action is predominantly bacteriostatic though high concentrations may be slowly bactericidal for susceptible organisms. In acne, topical clindamycin reduces Propionibacterium acnes (Cutibacterium acnes) counts and decreases free fatty acids on skin. [Australian]
- Benzoyl peroxide: a keratolytic and oxidizing agent that is bactericidal against P. acnes and has sebostatic effects; active against comedonal and inflammatory lesions. The combination provides both keratolytic and antibacterial activity and reduces the potential for emergence of clindamycin resistance compared with clindamycin alone. [Australian]

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Clindamycin: In a maximized percutaneous absorption study, mean plasma clindamycin levels during a four‑week dosing period were negligible (reported as 0.043% of applied dose). Presence of benzoyl peroxide did not alter clindamycin percutaneous absorption in that study. [Australian]
  - Benzoyl peroxide: absorbed into skin and metabolised to benzoic acid which is conjugated (mostly to hippuric acid) and excreted in urine. In vivo animal radiolabel studies (rhesus monkeys) indicated about 1.9% of dose enters systemic circulation as benzoic acid. [Australian]
- Distribution, metabolism and elimination specifics for topical use are not further detailed in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Clindamycin exhibits activity against Gram‑positive aerobes and many anaerobes including P. acnes; reported MIC for P. acnes in source material was 0.4 mcg/mL. Benzoyl peroxide exerts bactericidal oxidizing activity and keratolytic effects. Combined therapy reduces selection pressure for clindamycin‑resistant organisms compared with topical clindamycin alone. [Australian]

### Preclinical safety data
- Genotoxicity: clindamycin phosphate was negative in assays for gene mutation and chromosomal damage. [Australian]
- Carcinogenicity: benzoyl peroxide has acted as a tumour promoter/progression agent in some animal models; lifetime dermal carcinogenicity studies in mice and rats showed no evidence of carcinogenicity at tested concentrations. A photocarcinogenicity study in hairless mice showed dermal application of the combination reduced time to skin tumour onset after simulated sunlight exposure; clinical significance is unclear. Long‑term carcinogenicity studies of the combination have not been performed. [Australian]

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Trial program summary:
  - Five randomised, double‑blind clinical studies enrolled 1,319 patients with facial acne (both inflammatory and non‑inflammatory lesions). Treatment was applied once daily for 11 weeks with lesion counts and evaluations at weeks 2, 5, 8 and 11. Across studies the combination product was more effective than clindamycin alone in reducing inflammatory lesions in four of five studies and superior to benzoyl peroxide alone in three of five studies for inflammatory lesions. Against non‑inflammatory lesions the combination was better than clindamycin in four of five studies and better than benzoyl peroxide in one of five studies. Overall physician‑assessed improvement was significantly better with the combination than with monotherapies in three of five studies. [Australian]
- Pivotal study (Study 158) — intention‑to‑treat population:
  - N per arm: combination 113; benzoyl peroxide 112; clindamycin 65; vehicle 68.
  - Least‑square mean % reduction in inflammatory lesions: combination 60% vs benzoyl peroxide 46% (p=0.005); vs clindamycin 37% (p<0.001); vs vehicle 36% (p<0.001).
  - Least‑square mean % reduction in non‑inflammatory lesions: combination 32% vs benzoyl peroxide 25% (p=0.521); vs clindamycin 15% (p=0.204); vehicle 10% (increase) (p=0.002).
  - Global improvement (good to excellent): combination 58% vs benzoyl peroxide 44% (p=0.059); vs clindamycin 30% (p<0.001); vs vehicle 26% (p<0.001).
- Adverse event incidence in trials:
  - Skin reactions (erythema, peeling, dryness, burning) were the most commonly reported adverse events across trials and were numerically more frequent with combination or benzoyl peroxide arms than with clindamycin alone or vehicle in some studies. Seven cases of diarrhoea were reported across three studies with varied attribution; contact sensitisation was observed in patch testing under semi‑occlusive conditions. [Australian]
