# Editorial Product Information: Cholic Acid

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of cholic acid for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical practice in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cholic acid (a primary bile acid).
- **Brand Names:** **Cholbam** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral Capsules: 50 mg (Swedish orange, imprinted "50mg" and "ASK001") [United States].
    - Oral Capsules: 250 mg (White, imprinted "250mg" and "ASK002") [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mirum Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (Manufactured by Patheon France S.A.S.) [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Bile Acid Synthesis Disorders:** Treatment of disorders caused by single enzyme defects (SEDs) [United States].
- **Peroxisomal Disorders (PDs):** Adjunctive treatment of PDs, including Zellweger spectrum disorders, in patients presenting with liver disease, steatorrhea, or complications related to the malabsorption of fat-soluble vitamins [United States].
- **Limitations of Use:** The efficacy of cholic acid for extrahepatic manifestations of SEDs or PDs has not been established [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Standard Dosage:** 10 mg/kg to 15 mg/kg per day, administered once daily or in two divided doses [United States].
- **Familial Hypertriglyceridemia:** In patients with concomitant familial hypertriglyceridemia, the recommended dosage is increased to 11 mg/kg to 17 mg/kg per day, with adjustments based on clinical response [United States].

### Administration Instructions
- **Food:** Administer with food [United States].
- **Oral Integrity:** Capsules should not be crushed or chewed [United States].
- **Alternative Administration (Inability to Swallow):** For infants or patients unable to swallow, capsules may be opened and the contents mixed with 15 mL to 30 mL of infant formula, expressed breast milk, or soft food (e.g., mashed potatoes, apple puree). The mixture should be stirred for 30 seconds and administered immediately. The granules do not dissolve [United States].
- **Separation from Antacids/Resins:** Administer cholic acid at least 1 hour before or 4 to 6 hours after any bile acid-binding resins or aluminum-based antacids [United States].

### Treatment Monitoring and Titration
- **Initiation:** Treatment must be initiated and monitored by an experienced hepatologist or paediatric gastroenterologist [United States].
- **Laboratory Schedule:** Monitor AST, ALT, GGT, alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin, and INR according to the following schedule [United States]:
    - Monthly for the first 3 months.
    - Every 3 months for the next 9 months.
    - Every 6 months for the subsequent 3 years.
    - Annually thereafter.
- **Special Circumstances:** Increased monitoring frequency is required during periods of rapid growth, pregnancy, or concomitant disease [United States].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- No contraindications are listed in the available source [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Exacerbation of Liver Impairment:** Cholic acid may worsen liver function. Monitor laboratory parameters closely. 
    - Discontinue treatment if liver function does not improve within 3 months of initiation [United States].
    - Discontinue immediately if complete biliary obstruction occurs or if there are persistent indicators of worsening cholestasis or liver function [United States].
    - If liver parameters return to baseline after discontinuation, restarting at a lower dose may be considered [United States].
- **Overdose Indicators:** Concurrent elevations in serum GGT and ALT may indicate an overdose [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Adverse Effects (1–10%):**
    - Diarrhea (2%) [United States].
    - Reflux esophagitis (1%) [United States].
    - Malaise (1%) [United States].
    - Jaundice (1%) [United States].
    - Skin lesion (1%) [United States].
    - Nausea (1%) [United States].
    - Abdominal pain (1%) [United States].
    - Intestinal polyp (1%) [United States].
    - Urinary tract infection (1%) [United States].
    - Peripheral neuropathy (1%) [United States].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Voluntary reports include gastrointestinal discomfort, distension, emesis, constipation, pyrexia/fever, and rash [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Bile Acid Binding Resins (e.g., cholestyramine, colestipol, colesevelam):** May adsorb cholic acid and reduce its absorption/efficacy [United States].
- **Aluminum-Based Antacids:** Shown to adsorb bile acids *in vitro*, potentially reducing cholic acid availability [United States].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Bile Salt Efflux Pump (BSEP) Inhibitors (e.g., cyclosporine):** Concomitant use should be avoided as it may exacerbate the accumulation of conjugated bile salts in the liver. If necessary, rigorous monitoring of transaminases and bilirubin is required [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not assigned a letter category; however, a pregnancy surveillance program is available to monitor outcomes [United States].
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Data from limited case reports suggest no clear drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. Increased monitoring of liver function and INR is recommended during pregnancy [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Endogenous cholic acid is present in human milk. There are no data on the effects of exogenous cholic acid on the breastfed infant or milk production. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug [United States].
- **Fertility:** No nonclinical fertility studies have been performed [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have been established in paediatric patients from 3 weeks of age [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include patients aged 65 and over. Response differences between elderly and younger patients are unknown [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Patients with baseline liver impairment are at higher risk for exacerbation. Severe hepatotoxicity has been observed in patients with cirrhosis [United States]. (See Section 5 for discontinuation criteria).
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Cholic acid is a primary bile acid. In patients with SEDs or PDs, it provides the physiologic feedback inhibition of bile acid synthesis, reducing the accumulation of toxic intermediate bile acids. It also facilitates fat digestion and the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Cholic acid and its conjugates are ligands for the farnesoid X receptor (FXR), which regulates enzymes and transporters involved in bile acid homeostasis [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absorbed via passive diffusion in the GI tract [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Conjugated in the liver with glycine or taurine [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Conjugated forms are secreted into bile via BSEP, undergo enterohepatic circulation, and are eventually deconjugated by colonic bacteria and excreted in the feces [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bile Acid Synthesis Disorders (SEDs):** In a non-randomized, open-label study (Trial 1) and an extension (Trial 2), 64% of patients (28/44) with SEDs were classified as responders based on laboratory (ALT, AST, bilirubin) and clinical (weight, survival) criteria. The highest response rates were seen in 3β-HSD (59%) and AKR1D1 (75%) deficiencies [United States].
- **Peroxisomal Disorders (PDs):** In the same trial program, 46% of patients (11/24) with PDs were responders. No evidence of improved survival compared to historical controls was demonstrated for PDs [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [United States].
- **Handling:** Store in the original package to protect from light [United States].
