# Editorial Product Information: Cyclopentolate

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of cyclopentolate hydrochloride product information for healthcare professionals. 
- Note: This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Cyclopentolate hydrochloride (Anticholinergic)
- **Brand Names**: Cyclogyl [Australian]
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: 
    - Sterile ophthalmic solution (eye drops): 10 mg/mL (1.0%) [Australian]. 
- **Route of Administration**: Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Alcon Laboratories (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Induction of mydriasis and cycloplegia for diagnostic procedures [Australian].
- Management of some pre- and post-operative states requiring mydriasis [Australian].
- Therapy for iridocyclitis, particularly when a shorter-acting mydriatic and cycloplegic is preferred [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- Instil one drop, followed by a second drop five minutes later [Australian].
- Complete recovery typically occurs within 24 hours [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- Instil one drop in each eye, followed by a second application five minutes later if necessary [Australian].
- **Note**: Cyclopentolate 1% should not be used in small infants; concentrations greater than 0.5% are not recommended for this population due to the risk of serious systemic toxicity [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- For ophthalmic use only; not for injection [Australian].
- To minimise systemic absorption, apply pressure to the tear duct (nasolacrimal occlusion) for two minutes immediately following administration [Australian].
- Instruct patients to avoid touching the dropper tip to any surface to prevent contamination [Australian].
- Remove soft contact lenses before instillation and wait at least 15 minutes before reinsertion [Australian].
- In infants, it is recommended to withhold feeding for four hours following the examination due to risks of feeding intolerance [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Narrow-angle glaucoma or the presence of anatomically narrow angles [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to cyclopentolate hydrochloride or any excipient (including benzalkonium chloride) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **CNS and Psychotic Reactions**: This preparation may induce psychotic reactions and behavioural disturbances (e.g., ataxia, incoherent speech, hallucinations). Susceptibility is highest in children but can occur at any age [Australian].
- **Intraocular Pressure (IOP)**: May cause a rise in IOP. Evaluate the depth of the anterior chamber and measure IOP prior to initiation, especially in elderly patients or those predisposed to glaucoma [Australian].
- **Systemic Toxicity in Infants**: Premature and small infants are highly prone to CNS, cardiopulmonary, and gastrointestinal side effects (including necrotising enterocolitis). Cyclopentolate should be avoided in premature/small infants and used with extreme caution in children with Down syndrome, spastic paralysis, or brain damage [Australian].
- **Hyperthermia**: Use with caution in patients (particularly children) who are febrile or exposed to high environmental temperatures [Australian].
- **Driving and Machinery**: Drowsiness and blurred vision may occur. Advise patients not to drive or engage in hazardous activities while pupils are dilated unless vision is clear.
- **Other Precautions**: Use with caution in patients with known epilepsy or those who have previously experienced severe systemic reactions to atropine [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are "Not Known" (cannot be estimated from available data) unless otherwise specified [Australian].

### Ocular Effects
- **Common/Expected**: Transient burning sensation upon instillation, blurred vision, photophobia.
- **Other**: Increased intraocular pressure, ocular hyperaemia, eye pain, irritation, conjunctivitis, blepharoconjunctivitis, punctate keratitis, synechiae, and prolonged mydriasis.

### Systemic Effects
- **Psychiatric/Neurological**: Hallucination, confusional state, disorientation, agitation, restlessness, ataxia, incoherent speech, hyperactivity, seizures, retrograde amnesia, dizziness, headache, and somnolence.
- **Anticholinergic Manifestations**: Tachycardia, hyperpyrexia, vasodilation (flushing), skin rash (urticarial in children), dry mouth, decreased secretion (salivary/sweat glands), vomiting, nausea, and urinary retention.
- **Serious Reactions**: Coma, medullary paralysis, death. Severe reactions may manifest as hypotension with rapid progressive respiratory depression [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Potentiation**: Effects may be enhanced by the concurrent use of other drugs with antimuscarinic properties, such as amantadine, tricyclic antidepressants, some antihistamines, and phenothiazine antipsychotics [Australian].
- **Antagonism of Anti-glaucoma Agents**: Cyclopentolate may interfere with the anti-glaucoma and miotic actions of carbachol, pilocarpine, or ophthalmic cholinesterase inhibitors [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: Australian Category B2. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if cyclopentolate causes foetal harm or affects reproductive capacity. Administer to pregnant women only if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if cyclopentolate is excreted in human milk. A risk to the suckling child cannot be excluded. Use only when considered essential [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No studies have been performed to evaluate the effects of topical ocular administration on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: High risk of systemic toxicity. Infants should be observed closely for at least 30 minutes post-instillation. Fair-skinned children with blue eyes may exhibit increased sensitivity [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: Increased risk for undiagnosed glaucoma and cyclopentolate-induced psychotic/behavioural disturbances [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: No formal studies have been conducted in these populations [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: An anticholinergic (antimuscarinic) agent that blocks the response of the iris sphincter muscle and the ciliary body muscle to cholinergic stimulation.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Produces rapid pupillary dilation (mydriasis) and paralysis of accommodation (cycloplegia). It has a shorter duration of action than atropine [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data were provided in the source documents [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store between 2°C and 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Shelf Life**: 18 months; discard 4 weeks after opening [Australian].
- **Container**: Multi-dose LDPE bottles (e.g., 15 mL Drop-Tainer™) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
