# Editorial Product Information: chromium + copper + iron + manganese + iodine + fluoride + molybdenum + selenium + zinc

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **chromium + copper + iron + manganese + iodine + fluoride + molybdenum + selenium + zinc**
- Chemical class: Combination of essential trace elements (electrolytes/trace element admixture) for parenteral nutrition
- ATC code: B05XA31
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous infusion (central vein preferred). Peripheral infusion may cause local intolerance due to low pH and high osmolarity.
- Excipients with known effect: xylitol (3.0 g per 10 mL in available source), hydrochloric acid for pH adjustment, water for injections to volume
- Physicochemical parameters (as reported): pH approx. 2.5; osmolality reported ~3100 mOsm/kg water

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended daily dosage for patients aged >12 years: one unit (10 mL) per day to meet basal to moderately increased trace element requirements.
- Dose adjustment: Reduce or adapt dose in patients with renal impairment, hepatic impairment, or mild cholestasis.

### Dosing by indication
- Single standard regimen for replacement/maintenance of trace elements in parenteral nutrition in adults: 10 mL daily (see cautions for special populations).

### Administration instructions
- **Do not administer undiluted.**
- Add only to parenteral nutrition admixtures or compatible aqueous diluents for which compatibility has been documented.
- When added to a total parenteral nutrition (TPN) admixture, administer over the same infusion period as the TPN bag (recommended infusion time 8–24 hours in line with the TPN bag infusion rate).
- When added to a compatible aqueous diluent and infused separate to a TPN bag, one unit (10 mL) may be infused over a minimum of 1 hour. The minimum infusion time of 1 hour is based on safety concerns related to xylitol content.
- For faster infusions (1-hour minimum), a minimum dilution ratio of 1:6 is recommended (i.e., at least 50 mL diluent per 10 mL of product). The same minimum diluent volume (50 mL per 10 mL) can be applied to both fast and slow infusion times.
- Route: Intravenous infusion into a central vein is recommended. Peripheral infusion may cause local intolerance due to low pH and high osmolarity.
- Additions/compatibility:
  - Only add to medicinal products or nutrition solutions where compatibility has been documented.
  - Do not add directly to a lipid emulsion because trace elements can destabilize lipid emulsions. Recommended sequence: mix macronutrients (amino acids and glucose ± lipid emulsion) first, then add trace elements and any further additions (e.g., vitamins, electrolytes) aseptically.
  - Compatibility data exist for addition of one 10 mL unit to specific parenteral nutrition brands and formulations (details not reproduced here). If compatibility data are not available for a particular admixture, do not add.
- Single-patient use: Use for one patient only; discard any residue.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to any of the active substances (chromium, copper, iron, manganese, iodine, fluoride, molybdenum, selenium, zinc) or to any excipient (including xylitol).
- Conditions with total biliary obstruction.
- Wilson’s disease.
- Hemochromatosis.
- Children less than 12 years of age (product not recommended).

(Where a contraindication is specified in the available source it is presented above. No additional region-specific contraindications were provided in the available sources.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not provided in the available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Parenteral iron and iodine preparations can cause hypersensitivity reactions on rare occasions, including serious and potentially fatal anaphylactic reactions. Patients should be observed clinically for signs/symptoms of hypersensitivity during infusion; if hypersensitivity occurs, stop the infusion immediately and institute appropriate measures.
- Risk of accumulation of trace elements in patients with hepatic dysfunction (including impaired biliary excretion) or renal impairment; use with caution and adjust dose as appropriate.
- Monitor trace element levels, with particular attention to manganese, to detect accumulation.
- Peripheral infusion may cause local intolerance due to low pH and osmolarity; central venous administration is preferred.
- Xylitol content:
  - Xylitol infusion is associated with a risk of oxalate accumulation/oxalosis; infusing one unit over at least 1 hour is recommended to minimise this risk.
  - Vitamin B6 deficiency may increase oxalate production from xylitol; ensure adequate vitamin B6 status.
- If oral iron is being taken concurrently, determine total iron intake to avoid iron accumulation.
- Copper-ascorbic acid interactions: copper may oxidatively degrade ascorbic acid in PN admixtures; oxygen-impermeable bags may limit this.
- Amino acids in PN may complex with zinc and copper and facilitate urinary loss of these elements; usually clinically small.
- Molybdenum may interact with copper to form complexes that increase urinary copper elimination.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor clinical signs of hypersensitivity during and after infusion.
- Periodic monitoring of trace element concentrations is recommended, especially in patients with hepatic or renal impairment or in long-term parenteral nutrition; monitor manganese specifically.
- Monitor iron status if there is potential for concurrent oral/enteral iron intake.
- Monitor for signs of zinc or iron overload/toxicity in prolonged use or in patients with impaired excretion.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use with caution in elderly patients because of increased likelihood of impaired renal or hepatic function and concomitant disorders; monitor closely.
- Not recommended for use in children under 12 years of age.
- Dose adjustment recommended in renal and hepatic impairment and in cholestasis (specific dosing schemes by eGFR or severity not provided).
- Do not add to lipid emulsions directly; follow recommended order of admixture to preserve emulsion stability.

- Notes on frequencies:
  - The very rare frequency (<1/10,000) applies to nausea, vomiting, chills, pyrexia and headache as reported post-marketing for related formulations and the product itself in post-marketing surveillance.
  - For several adverse effects (hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis; manganese-related neurological changes; oxalosis; zinc poisoning symptoms; iron overload/haemosiderosis) the available source indicates occurrence but does not provide numerical frequency; frequency cannot be estimated from available data.
- Serious adverse effects highlighted:
  - Anaphylaxis (parenteral iron/iodine) — can be life-threatening; stop infusion and treat immediately.
  - Manganese-related neurological toxicity (including MRI changes) — reported with some historical formulations and with manganese exposure; monitor levels and clinical status.
  - Oxalosis from xylitol metabolism in susceptible settings — reduce risk by slow infusion and maintaining vitamin B6 levels.
  - Zinc toxicity and iron overload — signs described above; monitor and manage as clinically indicated.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions (reported)
- Molybdenum — interacts with copper to form complexes that increase urinary elimination of copper; monitor copper status if molybdenum exposure is altered.
- Amino acids in total IV nutrition — can complex with zinc and copper; such complexes may be excreted in urine, potentially reducing retained amounts of zinc and copper. Usually the urinary loss is small.
- Copper and ascorbic acid — copper may oxidatively degrade ascorbic acid in parenteral nutrition mixtures; use of oxygen-impermeable bags may limit this oxidative loss.
- Concurrent oral iron — may increase total iron exposure; check cumulative iron intake to prevent iron accumulation.
- No additional region-specific interactions reported in available sources.

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- If molybdenum is increased or decreased, monitor copper levels and signs of copper deficiency or excess.
- When adding trace elements to PN admixtures containing ascorbic acid (vitamin C), be aware of possible vitamin degradation; consider bag material and storage conditions to minimize oxidation.
- When co-administering other sources of iron, calculate total iron intake and adjust to avoid iron overload.
- Specific dose adjustments for interacting drugs not provided in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- The active trace elements are excreted in human milk and effects have been observed in breastfed newborns/infants of treated women (reported in available source).
- Theoretical risk: high maternal zinc exposure could theoretically induce copper deficiency in the infant; however, because the product contains copper in addition to zinc, the risk may be mitigated. Clinician should weigh benefits versus risks before administration to breastfeeding women.

### Fertility

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Not recommended for use in children under 12 years of age (contraindicated/not recommended as per available source).
- Dosing for children ≥12 years and adults: standard adult dose (one 10 mL unit) for those over 12 years is reported; specific paediatric dosing by weight or age groups not provided.

### Elderly use
- Use with caution in elderly patients due to increased likelihood of impaired renal and/or hepatic function and comorbidities. Monitor renal and hepatic function and trace element status.

### Renal impairment
- Use with caution; excretion of some trace elements (selenium, zinc, chromium) is primarily renal and may be significantly decreased in renal impairment leading to accumulation. Dose adjustment recommended but specific adjustment strategies (by eGFR or stage) are not provided in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment / Cholestasis
- Use with caution; copper and manganese are normally excreted via bile — impaired biliary excretion (including cholestasis or total biliary obstruction) increases risk of accumulation. Dose should be adapted in hepatic impairment and cholestasis; total biliary obstruction is a contraindication.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Not specified in available sources in mechanistic detail. The combination provides essential trace elements required as cofactors and structural components for multiple enzymatic and metabolic processes; intended to maintain or replenish nutritional status and prevent/treat deficiencies.

### Pharmacodynamics
- The product supplies physiological amounts of chromium, copper, iron, manganese, iodine, fluoride, molybdenum, selenium and zinc to meet basal to moderately increased requirements during parenteral nutrition.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Not applicable for intravenous administration.
- Distribution/metabolism:
  - Individual trace elements are taken up by tissues according to tissue-specific requirements.
- Elimination/excretion:
  - Copper and manganese: predominantly excreted via bile.
  - Selenium, zinc, chromium (especially during IV nutrition): mainly excreted in urine.
  - Molybdenum: primarily excreted in urine; small amounts in bile.
  - Iron: eliminated only in small amounts by superficial loss and desquamation of gut cells; premenopausal women lose variable amounts with menses (reported 30–150 mg monthly in source).
- Special populations: impaired renal or biliary function may reduce excretion and increase accumulation risk.
