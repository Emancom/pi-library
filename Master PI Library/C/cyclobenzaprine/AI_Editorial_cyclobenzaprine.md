# Editorial Product Information: Cyclobenzaprine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized clinical editorial intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesizes information from the provided regulatory source into a single, coherent reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Cyclobenzaprine hydrochloride, a tricyclic amine salt.
*   **Chemical Class:** Skeletal muscle relaxant; structurally related to tricyclic antidepressants.
*   **Brand Names:** Generic [US]; Reference brand: Flexeril [US].
*   **ATC Code:** M03BX08.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    *   **Oral Tablets:** 5 mg, 7.5 mg, and 10 mg [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Jubilant Cadista Pharmaceuticals Inc.; Repackaged by Preferred Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Musculoskeletal Spasm:** Indicated as an adjunct to rest and physical therapy for the relief of muscle spasms associated with acute, painful musculoskeletal conditions [US].
*   **Limitations of Use:**
    *   Should only be used for short durations (up to 2 or 3 weeks) due to lack of evidence for long-term efficacy in acute conditions [US].
    *   Not effective for spasticity related to cerebral or spinal cord disease, or in pediatric patients with cerebral palsy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
*   **Standard Regimen:** 5 mg taken three times daily.
*   **Titration:** Based on individual patient response, the dose may be increased to 10 mg three times daily [US].
*   **Duration:** Therapy should not exceed 3 weeks [US].

### Special Population Dosing
*   **Elderly:** Initiate at 5 mg and titrate upward slowly [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment (Mild):** Initiate at 5 mg and titrate upward slowly [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   May be taken with or without food.
*   **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to any component of the formulation [US].
*   **MAO Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 14 days of discontinuation. Risk of hyperpyretic crisis, seizures, and death [US].
*   **Cardiac Disorders:**
    *   Acute recovery phase following myocardial infarction [US].
    *   Arrhythmias, heart block, or cardiac conduction disturbances [US].
    *   Congestive heart failure [US].
*   **Endocrine:** Hyperthyroidism [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening cases have been reported when used with other serotonergic drugs (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, tramadol, bupropion, meperidine, verapamil). Symptoms include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular abnormalities [US].
*   **Tricyclic Antidepressant-like Effects:** Due to structural similarities to TCAs, patients may experience similar serious adverse reactions at higher doses, including arrhythmias and myocardial infarction [US].
*   **CNS Depression:** May impair mental or physical abilities required for hazardous tasks (e.g., driving, operating machinery). Effects are enhanced by alcohol and other CNS depressants [US].
*   **Antimuscarinic Effects:** Use with caution in patients with a history of urinary retention, angle-closure glaucoma, or increased intraocular pressure [US].
*   **Hepatic Insufficiency:** Not recommended for use in patients with moderate to severe hepatic impairment [US].

| **Fatigue** | 6% | 6% | 3% |

### Less Common Adverse Effects (1–3%)
Abdominal pain, acid regurgitation, constipation, diarrhea, dizziness, nausea, irritability, decreased mental acuity, nervousness, upper respiratory infection, and pharyngitis [US].

### Rare/Post-marketing Adverse Effects (<1%)
*   **Body as a Whole:** Syncope, malaise, chest pain, edema.
*   **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, arrhythmia, palpitation, hypotension, hypertension, myocardial infarction.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Vomiting, anorexia, gastritis, flatulence, tongue edema, abnormal liver function, hepatitis, jaundice, cholestasis, paralytic ileus.
*   **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Seizures, ataxia, vertigo, tremors, insomnia, depression, psychosis, hallucinations, disorientation, serotonin syndrome.
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, pruritus, facial edema, urticaria, rash.
*   **Genitourinary:** Urinary frequency/retention, impaired urination, impotence, gynecomastia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **MAO Inhibitors:** Contraindicated; risk of fatal interaction/serotonin syndrome [US].
*   **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome when combined with SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, tramadol, etc [US].
*   **CNS Depressants:** Enhances the sedative effects of alcohol, barbiturates, and other depressants [US].
*   **Guanethidine:** TCAs may block the antihypertensive action of guanethidine and similar compounds [US].
*   **Tramadol:** TCAs may increase the risk of seizures in patients taking tramadol [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category B:** Animal studies (rats, mice, rabbits) at 20 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate human trials exist; use only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Because related TCAs are excreted, caution is advised during lactation [US].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats at 10 times the human dose showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 15 years of age have not been established [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Increased plasma levels (approx. 1.7-fold higher AUC) and higher risk of CNS effects (confusion, hallucinations) and cardiac events. Initiate at 5 mg and titrate slowly [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically addressed in available sources regarding dose adjustments; however, the drug is primarily excreted via the kidneys as glucuronides [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    *   **Mild:** Initiate at 5 mg; use with caution [US].
    *   **Moderate to Severe:** Use is not recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Cyclobenzaprine relieves skeletal muscle spasm of local origin without interfering with muscle function. It acts primarily within the central nervous system at the brain stem (rather than the spinal cord) to reduce tonic somatic motor activity [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Bioavailability is approximately 33% to 55%. Exhibits linear pharmacokinetics (2.5 mg to 10 mg) [US].
*   **Distribution:** Highly protein bound. Subject to enterohepatic circulation [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized via CYP3A4, 1A2, and 2D6 (N-demethylation) [US].
*   **Elimination:** Excreted primarily as glucuronides via the kidney. Mean effective half-life is 18 hours (range 8–37 hours); plasma clearance is 0.7 L/min [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Comparison to Placebo and Diazepam:** Eight double-blind studies in 642 patients showed cyclobenzaprine 10 mg was superior to placebo and sometimes superior to diazepam in improving muscle spasm and local pain [US].
*   **Dose-Response (5 mg vs 10 mg):** Two 7-day trials in 1,405 patients demonstrated that 5 mg TID was statistically superior to placebo for relief of backache and muscle spasm. Efficacy of the 5 mg dose was comparable to the 10 mg dose in these studies [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [US].
*   **Container:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Rx Only (Prescription Only). Poison schedule not applicable in the US context [US].
