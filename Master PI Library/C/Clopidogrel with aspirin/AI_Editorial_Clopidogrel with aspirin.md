# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, concise reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though therapy must be individualised. The highest recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations <40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments should be guided by trough levels and may be reduced (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a known history, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While not specific to the injection, chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels and monitor weekly/monthly. Monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Events reported at a frequency of $\ge$5% regardless of causality [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcaemia (9%), hypervolaemia (5%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).

### Postmarketing and Neurological Experience
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure disorders.
- **Body Odour:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and body odour have been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled human studies; use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after administration. The benefits of maternal treatment should be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is consistent with adult weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine is not established in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO); however, the *intravenous* form is specifically indicated for use post-dialysis in ESRD [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD/Dialysis Trials:** Two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic haemodialysis patients demonstrated that levocarnitine injection (10, 20, or 40 mg/kg) significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels typically reached steady state by 12 weeks. However, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentrations). Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **US:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local jurisdictions for S4/Schedule classifications).
