# Editorial Product Information: clopidogrel + aspirin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **clopidogrel + aspirin**
- Chemical class:
  - clopidogrel: thienopyridine antiplatelet (prodrug)
  - aspirin: salicylate (antiplatelet/antiplatelet NSAID class)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Secondary prevention in acute coronary syndromes (as continuation therapy after initiation with separate clopidogrel and aspirin products) to reduce atherothrombotic events (myocardial infarction, stroke, vascular death or refractory ischaemia), including:
  - Unstable angina or non‑ST elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI).
  - ST‑segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) to reduce death and the composite of death, reinfarction or stroke in medically treated patients eligible for thrombolytic therapy.
- Use is indicated whether or not patients undergo cardiac revascularisation (surgical or percutaneous coronary intervention, with or without stent).
- Use following percutaneous coronary intervention with stent insertion: continue clopidogrel + aspirin for the duration recommended in evidence‑based guidelines for the stent type and clinical situation, or as otherwise indicated taking into account the patient’s overall atherothrombotic risk profile.
- Note: The benefit of combined clopidogrel and aspirin beyond four weeks following STEMI has not been studied in the documented data available.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Maintenance: clopidogrel 75 mg + aspirin 100 mg once daily (one combined dose as described in available sources).
- Administration: take orally once daily with adequate water; to reduce gastric irritation take with or after food.

### Dosing by indication
- Acute coronary syndrome (unstable angina/NSTEMI)
  - Initiate with a single loading dose of clopidogrel 300 mg plus aspirin 75–325 mg, then continue long‑term with clopidogrel 75 mg + aspirin 100 mg once daily.
- ST‑segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI)
  - Treatment should be initiated as soon as possible after symptoms start with or without a 300 mg clopidogrel loading dose in combination with aspirin; thrombolytics may be used as clinically indicated.
  - Note: No data on use of a 300 mg clopidogrel loading dose in patients aged ≥75 years in the referenced studies (some trials excluded patients >75 or did not use a loading dose in older patients). Benefit beyond four weeks in this setting has not been studied.

### Special dosing notes
- In patients who are CYP2C19 poor metabolisers, a higher clopidogrel dose (example regimen reported: 600 mg loading dose followed by 150 mg once daily) increases antiplatelet response; however, an appropriate dose regimen for CYP2C19 poor metabolisers has not been established in clinical outcome trials. Consider alternative treatment strategies or specialist advice for CYP2C19 poor metabolisers.
- If daily aspirin maintenance doses greater than 100 mg are required, administer clopidogrel and aspirin as separate products rather than as the fixed‑dose combination.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
Due to combined presence of clopidogrel and aspirin, clopidogrel + aspirin is contraindicated in patients with any of the following:
- Hypersensitivity to clopidogrel, salicylates (aspirin) or any excipient of the product.
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- Active pathological bleeding (e.g., haemophilia, intracranial haemorrhage, gastrointestinal bleeding).
- Peptic ulcer disease or erosive gastritis.
- Breast‑feeding (contraindicated).
- Hypersensitivity to non‑steroidal anti‑inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), or patients with the triad of asthma, rhinitis and/or nasal polyps associated with aspirin sensitivity.
- Patients with pre‑existing mastocytosis (risk of severe hypersensitivity reactions including circulatory shock).
- Severe renal impairment.
- Third trimester of pregnancy.

(Each individual patient’s suitability should be assessed and contraindications confirmed prior to use.)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / Major warnings
- **Increased bleeding risk**: clopidogrel + aspirin prolongs bleeding time and increases bleeding risk. Use with caution in any patient at increased risk of bleeding; discontinue prior to elective surgery when antiplatelet effect is not desired (see below).

### General warnings and precautions
- Discontinue at least 7 days prior to elective surgery when an antiplatelet effect is not desired.
- Use with caution in patients with lesions at risk of ophthalmic bleeding (intraocular lesions).
- Use with caution in patients with lesions with propensity to bleed (particularly gastrointestinal and intraocular); avoid concomitant drugs that may induce such lesions (e.g., NSAIDs).
- Use with caution in patients with a history of peptic ulcer or gastroduodenal haemorrhage, or with minor upper gastrointestinal symptoms (which may indicate gastric ulceration).
- Triple antiplatelet therapy (clopidogrel + aspirin + dipyridamole) for stroke secondary prevention is not recommended in acute non‑cardioembolic ischaemic stroke or TIA due to increased bleeding risk.
- Counsel patients regarding signs of gastrointestinal side effects and bleeding. Inform all treating clinicians and dentists that the patient is taking clopidogrel + aspirin.
- Caution in patients with history of asthma or allergic disorders (increased risk of hypersensitivity reactions) and in patients with gout (low‑dose aspirin increases urate concentrations).
- Hypersensitivity reactions to aspirin may progress to Kounis syndrome (allergic coronary syndrome presenting with chest pain associated with allergic reaction).
- Concomitant nicorandil with NSAIDs including aspirin increases risk of severe gastrointestinal complications (ulceration, perforation, haemorrhage).
- Avoid in patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency or glucose‑galactose malabsorption due to excipient lactose.
- Administer under close medical supervision in patients with glucose‑6‑phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency because of risk of haemolysis.
- Co‑administration with levothyroxine and salicylates should be avoided.
- Tinnitus may be an early sign of salicylate toxicity (salicylism) but may be missed in patients with hearing loss.
- Clopidogrel + aspirin should not be used during the first two trimesters of pregnancy unless clinically required; contraindicated in the third trimester.
- Use with caution in moderate hepatic disease (may have bleeding diatheses); contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment.
- Use with caution in mild to moderate renal impairment; contraindicated in severe renal impairment.

### Pharmacogenetics
- Clopidogrel is a prodrug requiring metabolic activation to an active metabolite; CYP2C19 plays a major role. CYP2C19 poor metabolisers form less active metabolite and have diminished platelet inhibition; poor metabolisers with acute coronary syndrome or undergoing PCI treated with clopidogrel at recommended doses may exhibit higher cardiovascular event rates.
- Concomitant use of strong or moderate CYP2C19 inhibitors (e.g., omeprazole, esomeprazole) should be discouraged; if a proton pump inhibitor is required, consider one with less CYP2C19 inhibitory activity (e.g., pantoprazole).
- Strong CYP2C19 inducers may increase active metabolite levels and bleeding risk; as a precaution, concomitant use of strong CYP2C19 inducers should be discouraged.
- Tests to determine CYP2C19 genotype are available and may aid therapeutic decisions.

### Haematological precautions
- Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) has been reported very rarely following clopidogrel use; presents with thrombocytopenia and microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia with possible neurological findings, renal dysfunction or fever—requires prompt treatment (plasmapheresis).
- Thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, aplastic anaemia and pancytopenia have been reported very rarely.
- Acquired haemophilia has been reported; in cases of isolated aPTT prolongation with or without bleeding, consider acquired haemophilia and manage with specialist input; discontinue clopidogrel if confirmed.
- Monitor blood counts and evaluate promptly for bleeding or haematological abnormalities during treatment.

### Hypersensitivity and immune-mediated reactions
- Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) has been reported with NSAIDs such as aspirin; may be life‑threatening. Presentations include fever, rash, lymphadenopathy, facial swelling, hepatitis, nephritis, hematological abnormalities, myocarditis or myositis. Discontinue aspirin and evaluate immediately if DRESS is suspected.
- Cross‑reactivity among thienopyridines (e.g., ticlopidine, prasugrel) has been reported; evaluate history of hypersensitivity to thienopyridines prior to use.

### Other precautions
- In patients receiving chronic therapy, occult blood loss may lead to iron deficiency anaemia.
- Concurrent use of clopidogrel + aspirin with oral anticoagulants is not recommended because of increased bleeding intensity.
- Concomitant use with other drugs associated with bleeding risk (e.g., other antiplatelet agents, injectable anticoagulants, glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors, SSRIs) should be approached with caution.
- Safety of concomitant administration with thrombolytic agents has not been formally established—use with caution.
- Alcohol may increase gastrointestinal injury risk when combined with aspirin and chronic heavy alcohol use increases bleeding risk; the effect of alcohol on combined product safety/efficacy has not been investigated in clinical trials. Ethanol may alter clopidogrel metabolism in vitro; clinical relevance not established.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic clinical monitoring for signs of bleeding, including occult bleeding (stool occult blood), especially during the initial weeks of therapy and after invasive procedures.
- Prompt full blood count and other investigations if bleeding or signs of haematological disorder occur.
- Monitor for hypersensitivity reactions and DRESS; discontinue drug and investigate if suspected.
- Consider genotyping for CYP2C19 where clinically relevant.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Additive bleeding risk with agents that affect haemostasis. Use caution and consider risk/benefit:
  - NSAIDs (including other salicylates): increased gastrointestinal side effects and bleeding; avoid simultaneous administration with other salicylate‑containing preparations or multiple NSAIDs.
  - Oral anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin): concomitant use is not recommended because of increased bleeding intensity; if unavoidable, monitor closely.
  - Injectable anticoagulants (e.g., heparin): increased bleeding risk; use with caution.
  - Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors: additive antiplatelet effects; increased bleeding risk—use with caution.
  - Other antiplatelet agents (e.g., eptifibatide, ticlopidine, tirofiban): additive effects and increased bleeding risk.
  - Thrombolytics: safety with clopidogrel + aspirin not formally established; use with caution.
  - SSRIs: co‑administration may increase bleeding risk; use caution.
  - Dipyridamole: triple antiplatelet therapy (clopidogrel + aspirin + dipyridamole) for stroke prevention is not recommended in acute non‑cardioembolic ischaemic stroke or TIA due to increased major bleeding.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- CYP2C19 inhibitors (reduce formation of clopidogrel active metabolite; clinical relevance uncertain but concomitant use discouraged):
  - Examples: omeprazole, esomeprazole, fluvoxamine, fluoxetine, moclobemide, voriconazole, fluconazole, ticlopidine, ciprofloxacin, cimetidine, carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, chloramphenicol.
- CYP2C19 inducers (may increase formation of active metabolite and bleeding risk): concomitant use of strong CYP2C19 inducers should be discouraged.
- Methotrexate: aspirin can inhibit renal clearance of methotrexate and displace methotrexate from albumin — increased risk of methotrexate toxicity (bone marrow suppression); use with caution.
- Acetazolamide: co‑administration with salicylates may increase risk of metabolic acidosis; use caution.
- Ibuprofen: may inhibit the effect of low‑dose aspirin on platelet aggregation when dosed concomitantly and may limit cardiovascular benefit of aspirin.
- Interaction with alcohol: alcohol may increase gastrointestinal injury with aspirin; chronic heavy alcohol use increases bleeding risk. Ethanol in vitro alters clopidogrel hydrolysis; clinical significance not established.

### Clinically significant interactions — management notes
- Avoid concomitant use with other salicylate preparations and multiple NSAIDs.
- Avoid or use caution with oral anticoagulants; closely monitor INR and for bleeding if co‑administration is necessary.
- If PPI therapy is required, prefer agents with lower CYP2C19 inhibition (e.g., pantoprazole) where clinically appropriate.
- Screen medications for CYP2C19 inhibitors/inducers and consider alternative agents or monitoring strategies.
- Avoid nicorandil plus NSAIDs, including aspirin, when possible due to increased risk of GI ulceration/perforation/haemorrhage.
- Monitor methotrexate levels/toxicity if co‑administered with aspirin.
- Counsel patients regarding alcohol and bleeding risk.

(When regional product information lists additional or differing interaction precautions, follow those jurisdictional recommendations as applicable. Not specified in available sources: further jurisdictional differences.)

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- First and second trimesters: clopidogrel + aspirin should not be used during the first two trimesters unless the clinical condition of the woman requires treatment with clopidogrel + aspirin.
- Third trimester: contraindicated.
- Pregnancy categorisation (by jurisdictional system): Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Contraindicated during breastfeeding (use in lactation is contraindicated).

### Fertility
- Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and efficacy in patients below 18 years of age have not been established.
- Reye’s syndrome: there is a possible association between aspirin and Reye’s syndrome when aspirin is given to children; avoid use in children and adolescents where risk exists.

### Elderly use
- No routine dosage adjustment necessary for elderly patients in documented sources.
- Note: limited data for clopidogrel loading dose in patients aged ≥75 years in certain trials; clinical judgement required.

### Renal impairment
- Experience limited in mild to moderate renal impairment; use with caution and observe closely for signs of salicylism.
- Contraindicated in severe renal impairment.

### Hepatic impairment
- Experience limited in moderate hepatic disease; use with caution due to potential bleeding diatheses.
- Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- clopidogrel: a prodrug that requires metabolic conversion to an active metabolite which inhibits platelet aggregation (formation of active metabolite is partly dependent on CYP2C19).
- aspirin: salicylate that increases bleeding time and produces antiplatelet effects (specific enzymatic mechanism not detailed in available sources).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination: Not specified in available sources in detail.
- Pharmacogenetics: CYP2C19 genetic variants affect formation of clopidogrel active metabolite; poor metabolisers form less active metabolite and have reduced platelet inhibition.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Combined administration of clopidogrel and aspirin results in additive antiplatelet effects and prolongation of bleeding time.
- Increased antiplatelet response observed with higher clopidogrel dosing in CYP2C19 poor metabolisers (pharmacodynamic observation; clinical outcome benefit not established).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Unstable angina/NSTEMI: clopidogrel plus aspirin is indicated to prevent early and long‑term atherothrombotic events (myocardial infarction, stroke, vascular death or refractory ischaemia) — described as clinical benefit in indicated acute coronary syndrome populations.
- STEMI: combination therapy shown in documented study population to reduce rate of death from any cause and the composite endpoint of death, reinfarction or stroke in medically treated patients eligible for thrombolytic therapy.
- Loading dose data: typical initiation included a 300 mg clopidogrel loading dose in combination with aspirin 75–325 mg for unstable angina/NSTEMI; for STEMI, a 300 mg loading dose was used in some studies but not in others. Specific trials mentioned in available sources include studies where patients >75 years were either not included or a loading dose was not used in older cohorts; benefit beyond four weeks in the STEMI population has not been studied in the available data.
- Detailed trial design parameters, patient numbers, outcome statistics, and more comprehensive efficacy/safety data: Not specified in available sources.
