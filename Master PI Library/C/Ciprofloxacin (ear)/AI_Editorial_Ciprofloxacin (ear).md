## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/generic name).
- **Chemical Class:** A carrier molecule involved in mammalian energy metabolism; specifically, the (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for Levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Sterile solution for injection containing 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) [US].
    - Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administration: Slow 2- to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable and compatible for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

### Monitoring
- Establish baseline plasma carnitine levels before starting parenteral therapy [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None [US].

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred—often within minutes of administration. These are primarily reported in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a severe reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Caveat):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal failure. High-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (regardless of causality) include:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), accidental injury (10%), asthenia (9%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), injection site reaction (33%), and pain (30%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), nausea (8%), vomiting (15%) [US].
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%) [US].
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), pharyngitis (23%), rhinitis (9%) [US].

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when co-administered with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a specific letter category in provided text, but follows current labeling standards]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in nursing mothers. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Weigh the benefit to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models at 3.8 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to weight-based dosing (mg/kg) as described in Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing for ESRD patients is established (10–20 mg/kg post-dialysis). Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be required as early as the third week of therapy based on trough levels [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are quickly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations from subnormal levels to concentrations above or within the normal range [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times per week showed mean pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, clinical outcomes related to supplemental carnitine in this population have not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 20°C and 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; contains no preservative. Discard any unused portion [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not applicable for US jurisdiction (Prescription Drug).
