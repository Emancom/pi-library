# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinical reference.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the US clinical data was available for analysis; clinical guidance for other regions should be verified against local regulatory databases.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (trimethylammonium).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and no less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours [US].
    - Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations [US].
- Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are currently no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the provided source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred—often within minutes of administration. These are reported most frequently in ESRD patients. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure history, the frequency or severity of seizures may increase [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic high doses of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are potentially toxic and usually excreted renally. The safety of oral levocarnitine in renal insufficiency has not been established [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%) [US]
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), Pain (30–35%), Headache (3–37%), Flu syndrome (25–29%), Chest pain (12–15%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%).

### Common (1–10%) [US]
- **General:** Abdominal pain, Accidental injury, Fever, Infection, Asthenia.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, Palpitations, Arrhythmia, Atrial fibrillation.
- **Nervous:** Dizziness, Insomnia, Paresthesia, Depression.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Leg cramps.
- **Skin:** Pruritus, Rash.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis** and severe respiratory distress (bronchospasm) [US].
- **Seizures** [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned in modern labeling, but historically aligned with Category B]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the maternal benefits. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No animal studies have evaluated carcinogenic potential. Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, yeast) were negative [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg). See Section 3 for specific instructions [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Chronic high-dose **oral** therapy should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (excluding endogenous carnitine) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In clinical trials of ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis, trough plasma levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations, clinical outcomes regarding signs and symptoms of deficiency in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other regions.
