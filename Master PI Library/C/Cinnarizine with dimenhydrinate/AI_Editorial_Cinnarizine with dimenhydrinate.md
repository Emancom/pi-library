# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the provided Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a concise reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local regulatory guidelines for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Doses:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit assessment and close monitoring [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with reports of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy and monitor weekly/monthly thereafter. Target plasma free carnitine concentrations should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Very Common (>10%) [US - reported in ESRD trials]
- **General:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), Pain (21–35%), Chest pain (6–15%), Headache (3–37%), Fever (5–12%), Flu syndrome (15–29%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%).

### Common (1–10%) [US]
- **General:** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, infection.
- **Cardiovascular:** Arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation, palpitations, tachycardia.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes.
- **Neurological:** Paresthesia, insomnia, depression, anxiety.
- **Special Senses:** Taste perversion, amblyopia, eye disorder.
- **Other:** Body odor, gastritis, anemia, leg cramps, pruritus, rash, urinary tract infection.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category:** Not specified by letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US]. 
  - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk after administration, the risks to the child must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is consistent with adult weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. High doses of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities resulting from the accumulation of toxic acylCoA compounds.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma protein or albumin.
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
  - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Total body clearance is approximately 4.00 L/h [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly, mean pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine concentrations increased from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed a dose-dependent increase in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. While increased concentrations were proven, clinical outcomes regarding carnitine deficiency symptoms in this population were not definitively determined in these trials [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
