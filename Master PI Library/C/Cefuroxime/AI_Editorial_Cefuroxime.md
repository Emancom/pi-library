# Editorial Product Information: Cefuroxime Axetil

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial synthesis:
    - Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefuroxime axetil, a semisynthetic second-generation cephalosporin antibiotic. It is a prodrug of the active compound, cefuroxime.
- **Brand Names:** AL-CEFUROXIME [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 250 mg and 500 mg (as cefuroxime) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Cefuroxime is indicated for the treatment of mild to moderately severe infections in adults caused by susceptible organisms, specifically:
- **Upper Respiratory Tract Infections:** Acute otitis media, sinusitis, tonsillitis, and pharyngitis [Australian-approved].
- **Lower Respiratory Tract Infections:** Acute bronchitis and acute exacerbations of chronic bronchitis [Australian-approved].
- **Skin and Soft Tissue Infections:** Including furunculosis, impetigo, and pyoderma [Australian-approved].
- **Genitourinary Infections:** Acute uncomplicated gonococcal urethritis and cervicitis caused by non-penicillinase-producing strains of *Neisseria gonorrhoeae* [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Standard Course:** Usually 7 to 10 days; however, bronchitis may be treated for 5 to 7 days [Australian].
- **Administration Instruction:** For optimal absorption, tablets should be taken following a light meal [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Acute Bronchitis/Chronic Bronchitis Exacerbations:** 250 mg to 500 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Uncomplicated Gonococcal Urethritis/Cervicitis:** A single dose of 1 g [Australian].
- **Other Sensitive Infections:** 250 mg twice daily [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to cephalosporin-class antibiotics [Australian].
- History of major or severe allergic reactions to penicillins (e.g., anaphylaxis, urticaria, angioneurotic oedema) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and potentially fatal anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions have been reported. Inquiry into past penicillin or cephalosporin allergy is essential before initiation.
- **Kounis Syndrome:** There have been reports of hypersensitivity-induced acute allergic coronary arteriospasm (Kounis syndrome) which may lead to myocardial infarction [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Use must be discontinued immediately if symptoms of Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS), or acute generalised exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP) occur. Therapy must never be restarted in these patients [Australian].
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Disease:** Pseudomembranous colitis has been reported. Therapy should be discontinued if significant diarrhoea or abdominal cramps occur. Drugs that inhibit peristalsis (e.g., opiates) are contraindicated in these cases [Australian].
- **Neurotoxicity:** Cephalosporins may trigger seizures, encephalopathy, or myoclonus, particularly in elderly patients or those with renal impairment if dosages are not appropriately reduced [Australian].
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, such as *Candida*, *Enterococci*, or *Clostridioides difficile* [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** Ferricyanide tests for blood glucose may yield false negatives; glucose oxidase or hexokinase methods are recommended. It does not interfere with alkaline picrate assays for creatinine [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild and transient; clinical trial data indicates discontinuation occurred in approximately 2.1% of cases, primarily due to gastrointestinal distress [Australian].

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain/discomfort, flatulence, indigestion, mouth ulcers, dry mouth, pseudomembranous colitis | Reported |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Transient elevations of AST, ALT, and LDH; hepatitis; jaundice (primarily cholestatic) | Reported |
| **Nervous System** | Headache, dizziness, seizure; encephalopathy and myoclonus | Frequency unknown |
| **Haematological** | Eosinophilia, positive Coomb's test, prolonged coagulation time, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia (rarely profound), haemolytic anaemia | Reported |
| **Hypersensitivity** | Rash, pruritus, urticaria, drug fever, serum sickness-like reaction, anaphylaxis | Rare |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | SJS, TEN, DRESS, AGEP, Symmetrical drug-related intertriginous and flexural exanthema (SDRIFE/Baboon syndrome) | Reported |
| **Other** | Vaginitis, Candida overgrowth | Reported |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Gastric Acid Reducers:** Concurrent administration with drugs such as ranitidine reduces the bioavailability of cefuroxime axetil compared to the fasting state [Australian].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Cefuroxime may alter gut flora, potentially leading to decreased oestrogen reabsorption and reduced efficacy of combined oral contraceptives [Australian].
- **Probenecid:** Concurrent use inhibits renal tubular secretion, thereby prolonging the terminal half-life and increasing serum levels of cefuroxime [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. There is no evidence of teratogenic or embryopathic effects in experimental models; however, human clinical data is absent. Use only if considered essential by the prescriber [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Cefuroxime is excreted in human milk. Caution and monitoring are advised when administered to nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in humans [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available in the provided source for this oral formulation [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Plasma levels may be higher and half-life increased due to age-related decline in renal function. Dosage reduction is not required unless significant renal impairment is present, but monitoring for neurotoxicity is advised [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Cefuroxime is primarily renally excreted. In patients with marked impairment, the dose must be reduced. The daily dose should not exceed 500 mg, and dosing should be repeated following dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Cefuroxime is a bactericidal agent that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to essential target proteins. It exhibits stability against many bacterial beta-lactamases [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Active against Gram-positive aerobes (e.g., *S. aureus*, *S. pneumoniae*) and Gram-negative aerobes (e.g., *H. influenzae*, *E. coli*, *N. gonorrhoeae*). It is notably inactive against *Pseudomonas* spp., *Listeria monocytogenes*, and Methicillin-resistant *Staphylococci* [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 60% of an oral dose is absorbed; absorption is optimal when taken with food [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Peak serum levels (approx. 4.1 mg/L for a 250 mg dose) occur 2 to 3 hours post-dose. Protein binding is 33–50% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Cefuroxime does not undergo significant metabolism [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily by the kidneys via glomerular filtration and tubular secretion. The serum half-life is approximately 1.2 hours. Haemodialysis can reduce serum levels [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** The provided source notes that cefuroxime's activity has been demonstrated *in vitro* and in clinical studies against specific pathogens, but detailed pivotal trial summaries were not included [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 3 years [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
