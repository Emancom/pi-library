# Editorial Product Information: capsaicin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **capsaicin**
- Chemical class: Not specified in available sources
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Topical (topical high-concentration capsaicin-containing topical system)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: 07/2024 (date of revision noted in available source)
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of neuropathic pain associated with postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) in adults.
- Treatment of neuropathic pain associated with diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN) of the feet in adults.
- Use is described and labeled for adults only. Pediatric indications: Not indicated.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- High-concentration topical system containing 8% capsaicin (reported as 640 mcg per cm²; each topical system ≈ 14 cm × 20 cm [280 cm²] contains approximately 179 mg capsaicin).
- Maximum number of topical systems applied at one treatment session: up to four systems.

### Dosing by indication
- PHN: Single application for 60 minutes of up to four topical systems to the identified painful area.
- DPN (feet): Single application for 30 minutes of up to four topical systems applied to the feet.
- Repeat treatment: May be repeated every 3 months or as warranted by return of pain; do not treat more frequently than every 3 months.

### Administration instructions (healthcare professional–administered product)
- Intended to be applied and handled only by physicians or other qualified healthcare professionals. Do not dispense to patients for self‑administration or handling.
- Administer in a well‑ventilated treatment area.
- Prior to application:
  - Identify and mark the treatment area (including areas of hypersensitivity/allodynia) and, if needed, clip (do not shave) hair to facilitate adhesion.
  - For DPN of the feet: perform a careful examination of the feet prior to each application to detect skin lesions related to neuropathy or vascular insufficiency.
  - Optional: Pretreat the treatment area with a topical anesthetic to reduce procedural discomfort. Apply the local anesthetic to the entire treatment area and surrounding 1–2 cm; keep in place until anesthetized; remove the anesthetic with a dry wipe; wash and dry the area before application of capsaicin topical system.
- Handling precautions:
  - Wear nitrile gloves — do not use latex gloves — when handling the topical system, cleaning treated skin, and when disposing of treatment materials.
  - Use of a face mask and protective eyewear is advisable for healthcare professionals.
  - Keep the topical system in its sealed pouch until immediately before use.
  - Do not apply to broken or compromised skin.
  - Avoid unnecessary contact of treated surfaces with other objects (horizontal surfaces, bedding, furniture).
- Application technique:
  - The topical system can be cut to fit the treatment area before removing the release liner; ensure unused pieces are not left exposed and are disposed of appropriately.
  - Remove the release liner and apply adhesive side to the skin, smoothing to ensure contact; a dressing (e.g., rolled gauze) may be used to maintain contact.
  - For PHN leave in place 60 minutes; for DPN of the feet leave in place 30 minutes.
- Removal and post‑treatment care:
  - Put on nitrile gloves before removal.
  - Remove gently and slowly by rolling the adhesive inward to minimize aerosolization of capsaicin.
  - After removal, apply an appropriate cleansing gel to the treatment area and leave on for at least one minute; wipe off and then wash gently with mild soap and water; dry thoroughly.
  - Dispose of all treatment materials (topical system, gloves, cleansing materials, packaging) in accordance with applicable biomedical waste procedures.
  - Instruct patients that treated areas may remain sensitive to heat (hot baths/showers, direct sunlight, vigorous exercise) for several days.
- If the topical system is cut, ensure that unused pieces are disposed of properly and do not contact unintended surfaces or people.

### Titration schedules
- Not applicable to the labeled high‑concentration topical system. No stepwise titration schedule specified.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not applicable because product is administered by a healthcare professional as discrete treatment sessions.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Severe irritation with unintended exposure:
  - Capsaicin can cause severe irritation to eyes, mucous membranes, respiratory tract, and skin in patients, healthcare professionals, and others exposed inadvertently.
  - Avoid contact with face, eyes, mouth, nose, and scalp.
  - If eyes or mucous membranes become irritated, flush with cool water and remove the affected person from the exposure area.
  - If skin not intended for treatment is exposed, apply the recommended cleansing gel for one minute, wipe off with dry gauze, then wash with soap and water.
  - Aerosolization may occur with rapid removal; remove gently and roll inward.
- Application‑associated pain:
  - Patients may experience substantial procedural burning and pain during application and after removal even if a local anesthetic is used beforehand. Prepare to manage acute pain during and after the procedure with local cooling measures and/or appropriate analgesic medications.
- Increase in blood pressure:
  - Transient increases in blood pressure have been observed during or shortly after treatment; average changes were <10 mm Hg but some patients experienced larger increases lasting ~2 hours post‑removal.
  - Blood pressure changes appeared related to treatment‑related pain rather than baseline blood pressure.
  - Monitor blood pressure periodically during and following treatment. Use caution in patients with unstable or poorly controlled hypertension or recent cardiovascular/cerebrovascular events.
- Sensory function:
  - Reductions in sensory function (including to thermal and potentially harmful stimuli) have been reported; these are generally minor and temporary.
  - Assess patients with pre‑existing sensory deficits prior to each application; if sensory deterioration or significant loss is detected or pre‑existing deficits worsen, reconsider continued use.
- Severe application site burns:
  - Cases of deep partial‑thickness and full‑thickness burns (including cases requiring hospitalization and skin grafting) have been reported, sometimes associated with unapproved use (different indications/frequency) or application to skin with prior trauma.
  - Follow dosage and administration recommendations carefully to reduce risk.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for and manage acute procedural pain during and after application.
- Monitor blood pressure during and after treatment sessions.
- Clinically assess sensory function at baseline prior to each application.
- Observe for signs of inadvertent exposure (ocular, mucosal, respiratory) among staff and other patients.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Administer only in settings and by personnel prepared to manage acute pain and potential respiratory or ocular irritation.
- Remove affected individuals from the exposure area if airway irritation occurs; provide supportive care and do not re‑expose if symptoms persist or worsen.
- Ensure footwear/bed linens/room surfaces that may contact the patient after treatment are managed to avoid secondary exposure.

### Postmarketing adverse reactions
- Deep partial‑thickness (second‑degree) and full‑thickness (third‑degree) burns and scarring.
- Accidental exposure events: eye pain, cough, eye and throat irritation.
- Serious burns requiring hospitalization and skin grafting have been reported, sometimes associated with off‑label/incorrect use or treatment of previously traumatized skin.

### Serious adverse effects
- Severe application site burns (deep partial‑thickness, full‑thickness) that in rare cases required hospitalization and skin grafting.
- Respiratory tract irritation from aerosolized capsaicin exposure; may cause coughing, sneezing, and, rarely, more significant respiratory symptoms necessitating supportive care.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - No clinical drug–drug interaction studies have been reported in the available sources.
  - In vitro studies indicate capsaicin does not inhibit or induce cytochrome P450 enzymes at concentrations far exceeding those measured systemically after topical application; systemic interactions via CYP are considered unlikely.

  - Not specified in available sources.
- Clinically significant interactions & management:
  - No specific clinically significant drug interactions were reported; caution and clinical judgment advised when combining with agents that may affect blood pressure or nociception, given the potential for treatment‑related pain and transient blood pressure increases.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **capsaicin** is an agonist of the transient receptor potential vanilloid 1 (TRPV1) ion channel. Activation of TRPV1 on nociceptive nerve endings produces an initial burning/irritation sensation followed by defunctionalization of these nerve terminals, resulting in reductions in pain signaling.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Systemic absorption after topical application of the high‑concentration topical system is reported to be negligible; systemic exposure is very low.
- Distribution: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism: Not specified in available sources.
- Elimination: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Produces an initial activation of TRPV1 with associated nociceptive sensations (burning, pain) during and shortly after application; subsequently leads to reduced peripheral nociceptor function and reductions in sensory perception in the treated area which are generally minor and temporary.
