# Editorial Product Information: cantharidin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **cantharidin**
- Chemical class: Terpenoid vesicant (1,2-dimethyl-3,6-epoxyperhydrophthalic anhydride)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Topical
- Strength used in pivotal clinical data: 0.7% cantharidin topical solution

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of available source PI: Last revision noted as October 2024 in the available source
- Withdrawals, suspensions, restrictions: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Topical treatment of molluscum contagiosum in adult and pediatric patients 2 years of age and older.  
  - [US] Indication explicitly supported for patients ≥2 years.  
  - If other jurisdictions have differing approved indications, those differences are not specified in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult and pediatric ≥2 years)
- Apply a single topical application directly to each molluscum lesion every 3 weeks as needed.
- A single treatment session is a single application to all target lesions; repeat sessions may be given at approximately 21-day (3-week) intervals.
- In pivotal trials, treatment was administered up to 4 times (Days 1, 21, 42, 63) with the primary efficacy assessment at Day 84.
- Strength: 0.7% topical solution.

### Limits and removal
- Do not use more than two single-use applicators during a single treatment session.
- Remove cantharidin from treated lesions by washing with soap and water approximately 24 hours after application.
- If severe blistering, severe pain, or other severe local adverse reactions occur, remove the product earlier than 24 hours by washing with soap and water.

### Administration instructions (healthcare professional)
- Healthcare professionals should receive instruction and training prior to preparation and administration.
- Use personal protective equipment (PPE): nitrile or vinyl gloves and eye protection during preparation and administration.
- Apply a small droplet to each lesion and spread to cover the entire lesion; allow solution to dry completely (up to ~5 minutes) before contacting adjacent healthy skin to avoid transfer.
- Avoid application near the eyes, mucosal tissues, or to adjacent healthy skin. If the product contacts unintended surfaces or healthy skin, immediately remove by wiping and washing with soap and water.
- Do not cover treated lesions with bandages.
- Do not reuse single-use applicators. Do not attempt to use a clogged applicator.
- Avoid concurrent application of other topical products (e.g., creams, lotions, sunscreen) to treated areas until 24 hours after treatment or until washing, because other products may spread cantharidin and cause blistering of healthy skin.

### Titration / dose adjustment
- Not applicable; no titration schedules described.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- None specified in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed/major warnings
- **Fatal or life‑threatening toxicity can occur with oral ingestion.** Avoid any oral contact and instruct caregivers/patients to seek immediate medical attention if accidental ingestion occurs. [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- **Toxicities associated with inappropriate administration / oral ingestion:** Oral ingestion has been associated with renal failure, severe blistering and gastrointestinal tract injury, coagulopathy, seizures, and flaccid paralysis. Seek urgent medical care for ingestion and contact Poison Control/medical toxicology resources as appropriate. [US contact details provided in source]
- **Ocular toxicity:** Contact with the eyes can cause severe ocular injury (including corneal necrosis and ocular perforation). Do not apply near the eyes. If ocular exposure occurs, flush eyes with water for at least 15 minutes and seek immediate medical attention.
- **Local skin reactions:** Cantharidin is a vesicant. Local reactions (vesiculation/blistering, pain, pruritus, scabbing, erythema, discoloration, dryness, edema, erosion) are extremely common and expected. Avoid application to healthy skin or mucosa. If severe blistering, severe pain, or other severe local adverse reactions occur, remove product earlier than planned by washing with soap and water.
- **Flammability:** The topical solution is flammable, including after drying. Advise patients/caregivers to avoid fire, flame, or smoking near treated lesions during treatment and until the product is removed.
- **Product handling and training:** Healthcare professionals should be trained in safe handling; use PPE; dispose of single-use applicators appropriately (local disposal regulations).

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor treated sites for development and severity of local skin reactions (vesicles, pain, excessive blistering, signs of secondary infection).  
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of ocular exposure or accidental ingestion.  
- Specific laboratory monitoring instructions (e.g., renal function, coagulation) for routine topical use are not specified in available sources; however, in the event of ingestion, appropriate systemic evaluation and monitoring (including renal function) should be undertaken per toxicology advice.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid applying product to areas where ingestion by or ocular exposure of a breastfeeding child is possible.
- Do not apply to mucosal surfaces, and do not permit the patient/caregiver to touch treated lesions until product has dried and ideally until it is washed off per instructions.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- Local skin reactions at the application site were reported in the majority of treated subjects in clinical trials (97% of subjects experienced at least one local skin reaction).
- Discontinuation due to adverse reaction: 2.3% of subjects treated with cantharidin in controlled trials discontinued due to an adverse reaction (vs 0.5% for vehicle).
- No serious adverse reactions were reported in the two pivotal controlled trials.

### Serious adverse effects (including overdose/exposure consequences)
- Oral ingestion (overdose) reported to cause: renal failure, severe blistering and gastrointestinal tract damage, coagulopathy, seizures, and flaccid paralysis. These events can be life‑threatening or fatal. [US]
- Ocular exposure reported to cause: corneal necrosis, ocular perforation, and deep ocular injuries. Immediate irrigation and urgent ophthalmologic assessment required.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted with cantharidin; pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interaction profiles are not characterized in the available sources.
- Management: Because systemic absorption after topical administration is generally low and plasma levels were largely undetectable in a small PK assessment, routine interaction concerns are not described. However, interactions in the event of accidental systemic exposure (e.g., ingestion) are not characterized and toxicology consultation is recommended.
- If regional guidance or local formularies list interactions, those are not specified in the available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
- Human data: No available data in pregnant women to assess risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal or fetal outcomes.
- Animal data: Animal reproduction studies were not conducted.
- Systemic exposure: Systemic exposure following topical administration is generally low; maternal use is not expected to result in fetal exposure based on available pharmacokinetic data.
- Pregnancy category systems (regional categories): Not specified in the available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Human data: No data on presence of cantharidin in human milk, effects on breastfed infant, or effects on milk production.
- Recommendation: Avoid application to areas that increase the potential for ingestion or ocular exposure to the breastfeeding infant. Breastfeeding exposure is not expected with typical topical use because systemic absorption is low.
- Fertility: Effects on fertility have not been evaluated in available nonclinical studies.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- The precise mechanism by which cantharidin treats molluscum contagiosum is unknown.
- Pharmacologically, cantharidin is a vesicant, producing localized blistering at the application site.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Cantharidin causes localized epidermal blister formation (vesication). The relationship between vesicant activity and antiviral/lesion clearance mechanism is not defined in the available sources.

### Pharmacokinetics
- In a pharmacokinetic assessment of 16 subjects (≥2 years) with at least 21 lesions, subjects had a single dermal application to each lesion:
  - Average number of lesions treated in the assessed cohort: 47.4 (median 35).
  - Cantharidin plasma concentrations were below the limit of detection in 15 of 16 subjects.
  - One subject had a detectable plasma concentration of 3.391 ng/mL at 2 hours post-dose.
- Metabolism and elimination pathways after topical administration are not described in the available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Overview of pivotal trials
- Two randomized, double‑blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trials (Trial 1 and Trial 2) enrolled a total of 528 subjects aged 2 to 60 years with molluscum contagiosum. Subjects were randomized by household to cantharidin topical solution 0.7% or vehicle.
- Baseline lesion counts ranged widely (mean ~19–23 lesions); median age 6 years; 51% male; majority Caucasian in the trials described.

### Treatment regimen in trials
- Single application to each lesion left in place for approximately 24 hours; applications were repeated about every 21 days as needed for up to 4 treatments (Days 1, 21, 42, 63). Primary efficacy evaluated at Day 84.

### Primary and secondary endpoints
- Primary endpoint: proportion of patients achieving complete clearance of all treated molluscum contagiosum lesions by Day 84.
- Secondary endpoints: complete clearance at Days 63, 42, and 21.

### Key efficacy results
- Trial 1 (Intent-to-Treat):
  - Day 84: cantharidin 46% vs vehicle 18% — treatment difference ~29% (95% CI: 19%, 38%).
  - Day 63: cantharidin 32% vs vehicle 17% — difference 15% (95% CI: 4%, 25%).
  - Day 42: cantharidin 21% vs vehicle 9% — difference 10% (95% CI: 2%, 19%).
  - Day 21: cantharidin 11% vs vehicle 4% — difference 8% (95% CI: 2%, 14%).
- Trial 2 (Intent-to-Treat):
  - Day 84: cantharidin 54% vs vehicle 13% — treatment difference ~40% (95% CI: 30%, 51%).
  - Day 63: cantharidin 28% vs vehicle 5% — difference 23% (95% CI: 15%, 32%).
  - Day 42: cantharidin 13% vs vehicle 4% — difference 9% (95% CI: 3%, 16%).
  - Day 21: cantharidin 5% vs vehicle 2% — difference 3% (95% CI: -1%, 8%).

### Safety observations in trials
- Local skin reactions were very common (observed in 97% of treated subjects).
- Study drug was removed prior to the planned 24-hour exposure in some subjects due to treatment-emergent adverse events (35% of treated subjects in pooled data had early removal vs 21% for vehicle in the cited analysis of exposed subjects).
- No serious adverse reactions were reported in the two controlled trials.
