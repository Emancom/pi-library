# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following information has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) source documents provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing decisions in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (quaternary ammonium compound).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by intravenous infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). It should be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable and compatible for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in PVC bags with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
- **Monitoring:** Pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels should be used to guide dose adjustments. Levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

### Titration and Missed Doses
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours [US].
- **ESRD Adjustments:** Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].
- **Missed Doses:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** While the official PI states "None known" in the dedicated section, the Warnings section advises against use in patients with a history of severe hypersensitivity to the product [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, there may be an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Precaution):** While this document focuses on the injection, healthcare providers should note that high-dose *oral* levocarnitine is not recommended in severe renal impairment due to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting [US].
- **Less Frequent/Rare:** Gastritis and body odor (a "fishy" smell) [US].
- **Dialysis-Specific Adverse Events:** In clinical trials of ESRD patients, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (though causality was not established):
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), abdominal pain (9%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough (9%), dyspnea (7%).
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a letter category in this PI, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, S. cerevisiae) were negative. No long-term animal studies for carcinogenic potential have been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), which applies to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The injection is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Dose adjustments should be guided by plasma trough concentrations [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria for beta-oxidation. It also aids in the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations and may alleviate metabolic abnormalities associated with the accumulation of toxic acyl-CoA compounds [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis showed that trough levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies evaluating 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, effects on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population were not determined in these studies [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
