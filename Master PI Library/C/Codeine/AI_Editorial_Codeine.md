# Editorial Product Information: Codeine Phosphate Hemihydrate

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of codeine phosphate hemihydrate, specifically in oral liquid formulation, for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, safety warnings, and regulatory requirements.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian (TGA) source was provided for this specific formulation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Codeine phosphate hemihydrate.
- **Chemical Class:** Opioid analgesic and antitussive.
- **Brand Name(s):** Actacode [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R05DA04 (Codeine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral liquid: 5 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Antitussive Therapy:** Relief of unproductive, dry, and intractable coughs associated with the common cold and influenza [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 5 mL (25 mg) administered every four to six hours [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Administered orally.
- **Ceasing Therapy:** In patients who may have developed physical dependence, the medication should not be discontinued abruptly. Doses should be tapered gradually (e.g., 10% to 25% reduction every 2 to 4 weeks) to mitigate withdrawal symptoms [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
**Codeine** is contraindicated in the following scenarios [Australian]:
- **Age Restrictions:** Children younger than 12 years.
- **Compromised Respiratory Function:** Children aged 12–18 years following tonsillectomy or adenoidectomy for obstructive sleep apnoea, or those with otherwise compromised respiratory function.
- **Metabolism:** Known CYP2D6 ultra-rapid metabolisers.
- **Respiratory Disease:** Severe or acute respiratory disease, respiratory depression (including cyanosis or excessive bronchial secretions), and heart failure secondary to chronic lung disease.
- **Neurological/Acute Conditions:** Acute alcohol intoxication, head injuries, or conditions involving elevated intracranial pressure.
- **Gastrointestinal/Biliary:** Following biliary tract surgery (risk of biliary contraction); diarrhoea caused by poisoning or pseudomembranous colitis (risk of delayed toxin elimination).
- **Drug Interactions:** Concurrent use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 10 days of discontinuing MAOI treatment.
- **Lactation:** Breastfeeding.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to codeine or structural analogues (e.g., morphine, oxycodone).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [Australian]
- **Hazardous and Harmful Use:** Codeine poses significant risks of misuse, addiction, and overdose. Healthcare providers should assess patient risk factors (e.g., history of substance abuse or mental illness) and monitor for signs of addiction.
- **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Fatal respiratory depression may occur. Monitoring is essential during treatment initiation and following dose escalations.
- **CNS Depressant Interaction:** Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, antihistamines, or alcohol may result in profound sedation, coma, and death.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Tolerance and Physical Dependence:** Prolonged use can lead to neuroadaptation and the need for higher doses to achieve clinical effects.
- **Sleep-Related Breathing Disorders:** Opioids can cause dose-dependent central sleep apnoea and sleep-related hypoxemia [Australian].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported primarily with long-term use; symptoms include nausea, dizziness, and hypotension [Australian].
- **Endocrine Effects:** Potential for androgen deficiency (libido loss, erectile dysfunction) and increased serum prolactin [Australian].
- **Hepatobiliary Effects:** May cause Sphincter of Oddi dysfunction, increasing intrabiliary pressure and the risk of pancreatitis.
- **Gastrointestinal Toxicity:** Long-term use is associated with oesophageal dysfunction (dysphagia, regurgitation) [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Regular monitoring for signs of respiratory depression, sedation, and opioid use disorder is required for all patients.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Enhanced risk of respiratory depression and death with alcohol, other opioids, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, sedatives, and tricyclic antidepressants [Australian].
- **Antihistamines:** Potentiation of CNS depression; increased risk of severe constipation or urinary retention with anticholinergic antihistamines [Australian].
- **MAOIs:** Serious/fatal reactions; avoid codeine within 10 days of non-selective MAOI use [Australian].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors (e.g., Quinidine):** Interference with the conversion of codeine to its active metabolite (morphine), reducing efficacy [Australian].
- **Cimetidine:** May inhibit codeine metabolism, increasing the risk of toxicity [Australian].
- **Laboratory Effects:** May elevate plasma amylase and lipase activity; invalidates gastric emptying studies [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Australian Category A:** Opioids cross the placenta. Regular use may cause physical dependence in the foetus and neonatal withdrawal syndrome (tremor, hypertonia, apnoea).
- **Breastfeeding:** **Contraindicated.** Ultra-rapid metabolisers convert codeine to morphine at high levels, leading to potential infant opioid toxicity and death. If codeine is required, breastfeeding must be suspended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No significant clinical effects reported [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in all children under 12. Contraindicated in children 12–18 post-adenotonsillectomy or with respiratory compromise due to the risk of fatal respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased susceptibility to respiratory depression and age-related renal impairment. Dose reductions may be necessary [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires caution and potential dose reduction due to decreased clearance of metabolites [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Requires caution and reduced dosing [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Direct suppression of the medullary cough centre. It increases the viscosity of bronchial secretions and has mild analgesic properties.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Well absorbed orally.
  - **Metabolism:** Metabolised in the liver via CYP2D6 to morphine and norcodeine.
  - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in urine (up to 86% within 24 hours). Approximately 70% is excreted as free or conjugated codeine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific clinical trial data for the antitussive liquid formulation provided in the source documents [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Light:** Protect from light.
- **Container:** Glass bottle with child-resistant closure [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 8 (Controlled Drug) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine.
