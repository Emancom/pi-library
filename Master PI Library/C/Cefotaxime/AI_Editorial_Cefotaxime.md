# Editorial Product Information: Cefotaxime Sodium

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Cefotaxime Sodium for healthcare professionals. It synthesises the available clinical and regulatory data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian (TGA) source documentation was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefotaxime sodium (a semi-synthetic third-generation cephalosporin).
- **Brand Names:** DBL Cefotaxime Sodium [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01DD01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Powder for injection (vials containing 1 g cefotaxime sodium).
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (injection or infusion) or intramuscular injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
[Australian-approved indications]:
- **Respiratory Tract Infections:** Upper and lower.
- **Urinary Tract Infections (UTI).**
- **Septicaemia:** May be initiated with an aminoglycoside before culture results.
- **Intra-abdominal Infections.**
- **Gonorrhoea:** Including strains produced by beta-lactamase.
- **Ear, Nose, and Throat (ENT) Infections.**
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections.**
- **Bone and Joint Infections.**
- **Meningitis:** Specific recommendations for combination therapy in children (excluding neonates) pending cultures.
- **Surgical Prophylaxis:** Prevention of post-operative infection in obstetrical surgery (caesarean section), vaginal/abdominal hysterectomy, and biliary surgery.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **Urinary Tract Infections:** 2 g daily in 2 divided doses.
- **General Infections:** Minimum 2 g daily in divided doses. Severity may warrant increases to 3 g, 4 g, or 6 g daily.
- **Uncomplicated Gonorrhoea:** 
    - Non-beta-lactamase producing: Single 1 g IM dose.
    - Beta-lactamase producing: 0.5 g IM dose plus 1 g oral probenecid (given 1 hour prior).

### Surgical Prophylaxis
- **Hysterectomy:** 1 g IM 30–60 minutes before incision, repeated on completion and then at 8-hour intervals for 24 hours.
- **Caesarean Section:** 1 g IV after the umbilical cord is clamped, followed by doses at 6 and 12 hours.
- **Biliary Surgery:** 1 g IV at induction.

### Administration Instructions
- **Intravenous Injection:** Dissolve 1 g in at least 4 mL Water for Injections; inject over 3 to 5 minutes.
- **Intravenous Infusion:** 
    - Short infusion: 2 g in 40 mL Water for Injections/infusion fluid over 20–30 minutes.
    - Continuous infusion: 2 g in 100 mL isotonic saline/glucose over 4 hours.
- **Intramuscular Injection:** Deep injection into the gluteus muscle. Maximum 4 mL per site. May be dissolved in 0.5% lignocaine to reduce pain (see Contraindications).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to cefotaxime or cephalosporins. 
- **Penicillin Allergy:** History of a previous major allergic response to penicillin (due to potential cross-sensitivity).
- **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Specifically contraindicated in these populations [Australian].
- **Lignocaine-Specific (when used as a diluent for IM):** 
    - Known hypersensitivity to amide-type local anaesthetics.
    - Non-paced heart block.
    - Severe heart failure.
    - Infants under 30 months of age.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis:** Serious and occasionally fatal reactions have occurred. Immediate discontinuation and emergency treatment are required if a reaction occurs.
- **Neurotoxicity:** High doses, especially in patients with renal impairment, can cause encephalopathy (loss of consciousness, abnormal movements, convulsions), seizures, or myoclonus.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue immediately if suspected.
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhoea (CDAD):** May range from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis. Avoid drugs that inhibit peristalsis.
- **Haematological Monitoring:** For treatments exceeding 7–10 days, white blood cell counts should be monitored; discontinue if neutropenia develops.
- **Arrhythmias:** Rapid IV administration through a central venous catheter has been associated with life-threatening arrhythmias. Infuse over at least 3–5 minutes.
- **Sodium Intake:** Each gram contains approximately 48 mg (2.09 mmol) of sodium.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** Pain at the injection site (approx. 4.8%), phlebitis, tenderness.
- **Uncommon/Rare/Frequency Not Specified:**
    - **GI:** Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, pseudomembranous colitis.
    - **Hepatobiliary:** Transient rises in AST, ALT, LDH, and bilirubin; hepatitis (potentially with jaundice).
    - **Skin:** Rash, pruritus, urticaria, SJS/TEN (rare), AGEP.
    - **Haematological:** Leucopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, eosinophilia, haemolytic anaemia.
    - **Nervous System:** Headache, dizziness, encephalopathy (at high doses).
    - **Immune:** Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction (during treatment for borreliosis), angioedema, bronchospasm, Kounis syndrome.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Uricosuric Agents (Probenecid):** Decreases renal clearance and increases total body concentrations of cefotaxime.
- **Bacteriostatic Antibiotics (e.g., Tetracycline, Erythromycin, Chloramphenicol):** May exert an antagonistic effect *in vitro*.
- **Aminoglycosides/Loop Diuretics:** May potentiate nephrotoxicity. Renal function must be monitored.
- **Gentamicin:** Exhibits additive microbiological effects but is physically incompatible (do not mix in the same syringe/bag).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1 [Australian].** Safety has not been established. Crosses the placenta. The Australian PI lists pregnancy as a contraindication [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in breast milk. The Australian PI recommends stopping breastfeeding during administration [Australian]. Potential effects on the infant include diarrhoea and yeast-like fungal colonisation.
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Neonates (0–1 week): 50 mg/kg IV every 12 hours.
    - Neonates (1–4 weeks): 50 mg/kg IV every 8 hours.
    - Children: 100–150 mg/kg/day in 3–4 divided doses (up to 200 mg/kg/day in severe cases).
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data provided, but monitoring of renal function is advised due to risk of neurotoxicity.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Creatinine Clearance (CrCl) ≤ 20 mL/min: Reduce dose by half and administer every 12 hours.
    - CrCl ≤ 5 mL/min: Recommended dose is 500 mg every 12 hours.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Careful monitoring is advised; avoid lignocaine as a diluent in severe liver disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis.
- **Microbiology:** Broad-spectrum against many Gram-positive and Gram-negative pathogens. Notably resistant to many beta-lactamases. *Streptococcus faecalis* and methicillin-resistant staphylococci are typically resistant.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Not absorbed orally; peak plasma levels are reached 30 mins after IM injection (approx. 20.5 mg/L for 1 g dose).
    - **Distribution:** 32–44% protein bound. Diffuses into CSF effectively in the presence of inflamed meninges.
    - **Metabolism:** Partially metabolised to desacetylcefotaxime, which possesses antibacterial activity.
    - **Elimination:** Approx. 85% recovered in urine (20% as unchanged drug, 15% as desacetyl metabolite). Half-life is approx. 1–1.5 hours in adults with normal renal function.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data was detailed in the available source document.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light.
- **Reconstitution:** Use immediately to reduce microbiological hazard. Discard any residue.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible with Water for Injections, Sodium Chloride, 5% Glucose, and Ringer-Lactate. Do **not** mix with sodium bicarbonate.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
