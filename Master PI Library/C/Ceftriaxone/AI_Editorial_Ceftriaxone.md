# Editorial Product Information: Ceftriaxone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local guidelines and the current approved PI for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ceftriaxone (as sodium).
- **Chemical Class:** Third-generation semi-synthetic cephalosporin antibiotic.
- **Brand Names:** Ceftriaxone-AFT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01DD04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Powder for injection in 500 mg, 1 g, and 2 g vials [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) or Intravenous (IV) injection/infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Ceftriaxone** is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by susceptible organisms in the following categories [Australian-approved]:
- **Lower Respiratory Tract Infections:** Including pneumonia caused by *S. pneumoniae*, *Streptococcus spp.*, *S. aureus*, *H. influenzae*, and various Enterobacteriaceae.
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** Including those caused by *S. aureus*, *S. epidermidis*, *S. pyogenes*, and *E. coli*.
- **Urinary Tract Infections (Complicated and Uncomplicated):** Caused by *E. coli*, *Proteus*, and *Klebsiella*.
- **Uncomplicated Gonorrhoea:** Cervical, urethral, and rectal infections.
- **Bacterial Septicaemia:** Caused by *S. pneumoniae*, *E. coli*, and *H. influenzae*.
- **Bone and Joint Infections:** Caused by *S. aureus*, *S. pneumoniae*, *Streptococcus spp.*, and *Enterobacter spp*.
- **Meningitis:** Initial treatment as a single agent for infections caused by *H. influenzae* type b, *N. meningitidis*, *S. pneumoniae*, or Enterobacteriaceae.
- **Surgical Prophylaxis:** Pre-operative administration (vaginal/abdominal hysterectomy, cholecystectomy, or coronary artery bypass surgery).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 1–2 g once daily or in equally divided doses twice daily, depending on severity [Australian].
- **Uncomplicated Gonorrhoea:** A single IM dose of 250 mg [Australian].
- **Surgical Prophylaxis:** A single 1 g dose administered 0.5–2 hours prior to surgery [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Serious Infections:** 50–75 mg/kg (maximum 2 g) daily, given once daily or in divided doses every 12 hours [Australian].
- **Meningitis:** The total daily dose should be divided and administered every 12 hours [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **IM Injection:** Dissolve 500 mg in 2 mL or 1 g in 3.5 mL of 1% lignocaine. Inject deeply into a large muscle mass. **Warning:** Lignocaine must never be given intravenously [Australian].
- **IV Injection:** Dissolve 500 mg in 5 mL or 1 g in 10 mL sterile water; administer over 2–4 minutes [Australian].
- **IV Infusion:** Dissolve 2 g in 40 mL of compatible fluid (e.g., 0.9% NaCl, 5% Glucose); infuse over at least 30 minutes [Australian].
- **Compatibility:** Do NOT use diluents containing calcium (e.g., Ringer’s or Hartmann’s) as precipitates may form [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Allergy:** Known hypersensitivity to cephalosporins or major allergy (anaphylaxis) to penicillins [Australian].
- **Lignocaine:** Contraindicated as a diluent for IM use in patients with lignocaine hypersensitivity [Australian].
- **Neonates (<28 days):**
    - Hyperbilirubinaemic neonates and preterm neonates (risk of bilirubin encephalopathy) [Australian].
    - Neonates requiring (or expected to require) calcium-containing IV solutions, including parenteral nutrition, due to the risk of fatal ceftriaxone-calcium precipitation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylactic reactions may be fatal. **Kounis Syndrome** (acute allergic coronary arteriospasm leading to myocardial infarction) has been reported [Australian].
- **Calcium Precipitation:** Ceftriaxone must not be mixed or administered simultaneously with calcium-containing solutions in any patient. In non-neonates, they may be given sequentially if lines are thoroughly flushed [Australian].
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhoea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterials; ranges from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis [Australian].
- **Biliary Precipitation:** Ceftriaxone-calcium precipitates in the gallbladder (sludge) may appear as shadows on sonograms; usually resolves upon discontinuation [Australian].
- **Immune-Mediated Haemolytic Anaemia:** Severe cases, including fatalities, have occurred in adults and children [Australian].
- **Neurological Effects:** Encephalopathy and neurotoxicity (seizures, myoclonus) may occur, particularly in the elderly or those with renal impairment [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** Includes SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP. Discontinue immediately if suspected [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies are based on Australian PI terminology.*

| System Organ Class | Common/Occasional | Infrequent/Rare |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea | Nausea, vomiting, stomatitis, pseudomembranous colitis (rare) |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Elevated AST/ALT | Biliary sludge, hepatitis, cholestatic jaundice |
| **Haematological** | Eosinophilia, thrombocytosis, leucopenia | Haemolytic anaemia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis (very rare) |
| **Dermatological** | Rash | Pruritus, urticaria, SCAR (SJS/TEN) |
| **Renal** | — | Elevated BUN/Creatinine, crystalluria, oliguria |
| **General/Local** | Local pain (IM), phlebitis (IV) | Fever, chills, headache, dizziness |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, Kounis syndrome, fatal calcium-ceftriaxone precipitates (neonates), and severe haemolytic anaemia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Calcium-containing IV solutions:** Risk of precipitation; contraindicated in neonates and requires specific flushing protocols in older patients [Australian].
- **Chloramphenicol:** Antagonistic effects observed *in vitro* [Australian].
- **Probenecid:** Does not significantly affect ceftriaxone elimination [Australian].
- **Physical Incompatibilities:** Incompatible with amsacrine, vancomycin, fluconazole, and aminoglycosides [Australian].
- **Laboratory Tests:** May cause false-positive Coombs’ test, false-positive galactosemia test, and false-positive non-enzymatic urine glucose tests [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Animal studies show no embryotoxicity or teratogenicity, but well-controlled human studies are lacking. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Ceftriaxone is excreted in human milk in low concentrations; exercise caution [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility observed in rats at doses up to 586 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Established for specific indications (see Section 3). Contraindicated in hyperbilirubinaemic or calcium-requiring neonates [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Higher risk of renal adverse effects and neurotoxicity; monitoring recommended [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment needed for mild-to-moderate impairment if hepatic function is normal. In severe renal impairment (e.g., dialysis), monitor serum levels (target <280 µg/mL) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment generally required; however, monitor levels if concurrent severe renal impairment exists [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis. Stable against many beta-lactamases (penicillinases and cephalosporinases).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed from the GI tract; 100% bioavailable after IM injection.
    - **Distribution:** 85–95% protein bound (reversible). Crosses the placenta and enters CSF in meningitis [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Not significantly metabolised; converted to inactive metabolites by gut flora.
    - **Elimination:** 33–67% excreted unchanged in urine; the remainder in bile. Plasma half-life is 5.8–8.7 hours in healthy adults [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Surgical Prophylaxis:** Ceftriaxone demonstrated efficacy comparable to cefazolin in preventing infection following coronary artery bypass surgery, though no placebo-controlled trials were conducted for this specific indication [Australian].
- **General Data:** No specific pivotal trial summaries were provided in the source PI [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Protection:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life (Reconstituted):** Stable for 6 hours at room temperature or 24 hours at 2–8°C. Do not freeze [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Approval Date:** First approved 2 February 2012 [Australian].
