# Editorial Product Information: Cetrorelix

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cetrorelix (as acetate).
- **Chemical Class:** Luteinising hormone-releasing hormone (LHRH) antagonist; synthetic decapeptide.
- **Brand Name:** **Cetrotide** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H01CC02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for injection (250 micrograms per vial). Following reconstitution with the provided 1 mL solvent, the concentration is 250 micrograms/mL. 
    - *Editorial Note:* Due to losses during reconstitution and administration, the actual deliverable dose to the patient is approximately 210 micrograms [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian]; Healthcare Logistics [New Zealand].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prevention of premature ovulation:** Indicated for the prevention of premature luteinisation and ovulation in patients undergoing controlled ovarian stimulation (COS), followed by oocyte pick-up and assisted reproductive techniques (ART) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Cetrotide** should only be prescribed by or under the supervision of a specialist experienced in fertility treatment [Australian].
- **Dose:** One vial (250 micrograms) administered once daily at 24-hour intervals.

### Administration Schedules
Treatment may be initiated using either a morning or evening regimen:
- **Morning Administration:** Treatment begins on day 5 or 6 of ovarian stimulation (approx. 96 to 120 hours after starting gonadotrophins) and continues throughout the gonadotrophin treatment period, including the day of ovulation induction [Australian].
- **Evening Administration:** Treatment begins on day 5 of ovarian stimulation (approx. 96 to 108 hours after starting gonadotrophins) and continues until the evening prior to the day of ovulation induction [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Site:** Subcutaneous injection into the lower abdominal wall. Injection sites should be rotated daily [Australian].
- **First Dose:** Must be performed under physician supervision. The patient should be monitored for 30 minutes post-injection to manage any potential allergic or pseudo-allergic reactions.
- **Self-Administration:** Subsequent doses may be self-administered once the patient is trained on recognizing hypersensitivity symptoms and the need for immediate intervention [Australian].
- **Reconstitution:** Dissolve only using the provided water for injections. Mix with a gentle swirling motion; avoid vigorous shaking and bubble formation. The solution must be clear and particle-free [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to cetrorelix acetate, structural analogues of GnRH, extrinsic peptide hormones, or mannitol [Australian].
- Pregnancy and lactation [Australian].
- Post-menopausal women [Australian].
- Patients with moderate to severe renal impairment [Australian].
- Patients with moderate to severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ovarian Hyperstimulation Syndrome (OHSS):** OHSS is an intrinsic risk of ovarian stimulation with gonadotrophins. Cases should be managed by specialists experienced in the field [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Caution is required in women with active allergic conditions or a history of allergic predisposition. Use is not advised in women with severe allergic conditions [Australian].
- **Repeated Cycles:** Clinical experience with cetrorelix in repeated ovarian stimulation cycles is limited; use should only proceed after a careful risk/benefit assessment [Australian].
- **Congenital Anomalies:** While ART is associated with a slightly higher prevalence of anomalies compared to spontaneous conception, it is unclear if this is related to the procedures or the underlying infertility of the couple [Australian].
- **Luteal Phase Support:** Should be provided according to the standard practice of the reproductive medical centre [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Systemic Adverse Events (Incidence ≥ 1%)
Based on clinical studies of doses ranging from 0.1 mg to 5 mg:
- **Genitourinary:** Ovarian disorder/OHSS (2.1% – 4.4%) [Australian].
- **Neurological:** Headache (1.3%) [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (1.1% – 1.3%) [Australian].

### Adverse Effect Frequency Terminology
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Mild to moderate OHSS (WHO Grade I or II); Local injection site reactions (pain, erythema, haematoma, swelling, irritation) |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Systemic allergic/pseudo-allergic reactions (including anaphylaxis); Severe OHSS (WHO Grade III); Headache; Nausea |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Generalised reactions |

*Note: A single case of hot flushes and one severe hypersensitivity reaction (cough, rash, hypotension) have been reported in clinical contexts [Australian].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** *In vitro* data suggest interactions are unlikely with drugs metabolised by Cytochrome P450, or those undergoing glucuronidation or conjugation [Australian].
- **General Caution:** The possibility of interactions with other commonly used medicinal products cannot be entirely excluded [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Cetrorelix is not intended for use during pregnancy and is contraindicated. Animal studies show increased foetal resorptions and a theoretical risk of abortion exists if GnRH antagonists are used during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if cetrorelix is excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** In animal models (rats), cetrorelix caused reversible cessation of mating in females and decreased fertility in males, consistent with its pharmacological class [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not specified in available sources (indication is for adult fertility).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified; drug is contraindicated in post-menopausal women [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with moderate or severe renal impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Cetrorelix is a LHRH antagonist that competes with endogenous LHRH for membrane receptors on pituitary cells. It dose-dependently inhibits the secretion of LH and FSH [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of suppression is immediate with no initial stimulatory ("flare") effect. In women, it delays the LH surge and subsequent ovulation. Suppression of LH is approximately 70% four days after a single 3 mg dose [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability after subcutaneous injection is approx. 85%.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution ($V_{d,area}$) is 1.1 L/kg.
    - **Elimination:** Total plasma clearance is 1.2 mL/min/kg; renal clearance is 0.1 mL/min/kg.
    - **Half-life:** Mean terminal half-life is approx. 12 hours (IV) and 30 hours (SC), the latter reflecting absorption processes at the injection site [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Efficacy was established in 9 clinical studies (6 Phase II, 3 Phase III) involving 884 patients.
- **Pivotal Phase III Trials:**
    - **Trial 1:** Open randomised controlled trial (n=188) vs. buserelin (LHRH agonist).
    - **Trial 2:** Open uncontrolled study (n=346) using 250 mcg daily.
    - **Trial 3:** Open randomised controlled trial (n=115) using a 3 mg single dose vs. triptorelin.
- **Outcomes:** Cetrorelix effectively prevented premature ovulation and luteinisation, showing comparable efficacy to LHRH agonists across parameters including HMG dose, egg numbers, and pregnancy rates [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Standard Storage:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Patient Convenience:** If refrigeration is unavailable, the product may be stored below 30°C for a single period of up to 3 months, after which it must be discarded [Australian].
- **Light Protection:** Store in the original package [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
