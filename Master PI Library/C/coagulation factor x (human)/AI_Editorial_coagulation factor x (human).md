## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General principles
- Administer only by intravenous infusion after reconstitution. Use the labeled potency (IU per vial) when calculating dose.
- Base dose and duration on severity of deficiency, bleeding location/extent, patient age (<12 years vs ≥12 years), clinical condition and individual response.
- Do not administer more than 60 IU/kg in a single day.
- Do not exceed a peak plasma Factor X level of 120 IU/dL.

### Prophylaxis
- Children (<12 years): initial dose 40 IU/kg twice weekly. Monitor trough Factor X activity and adjust to clinical response and trough levels; target trough ≥5 IU/dL.
- Adults and adolescents (≥12 years): initial dose 25 IU/kg twice weekly. Monitor trough Factor X activity and adjust to clinical response and trough levels; target trough ≥5 IU/dL.
- Monitoring: due to inter‑ and intra‑patient variability, measure trough levels at intervals, particularly in the first weeks of therapy and after dose changes.

### On‑demand treatment of bleeding episodes
- Children (<12 years): initial dose 30 IU/kg. Infuse at first sign of bleeding; repeat every 24 hours until bleeding stops.
- Adults and adolescents (≥12 years): initial dose 25 IU/kg. Repeat dosing and frequency guided by clinical response and laboratory monitoring.

### Perioperative management
- Goal pre‑surgery: raise plasma Factor X levels to 70–90 IU/dL.
- Goal post‑surgery: maintain plasma Factor X levels ≥50 IU/dL until no longer at risk of surgical bleeding.
- Dose calculation (general): Dose (IU) = body weight (kg) × desired Factor X rise (IU/dL or % of normal) × factor
  - Children (<12 years): use factor 0.6
  - Adults and adolescents (≥12 years): use factor 0.5
- For pediatric patients (<12 years) an alternative description is provided using incremental recovery (see Pharmacology/Pharmacokinetics).

### Dose calculation using incremental recovery
- Estimated increment of Factor X (IU/dL per IU/kg):
  - Children (<12 years): approximately 1.7 IU/dL per IU/kg.
    - Therefore: Estimated increment (IU/dL) = [Total dose (IU) / body weight (kg)] × 1.7
    - Equivalent dose formula: Dose (IU) = body weight (kg) × desired Factor X rise (IU/dL) × 0.6
  - Adolescents and adults (≥12 years): approximately 2.0 IU/dL per IU/kg.
    - Therefore: Estimated increment (IU/dL) = [Total dose (IU) / body weight (kg)] × 2.0
    - Equivalent dose formula: Dose (IU) = body weight (kg) × desired Factor X rise (IU/dL) × 0.5

### Reconstitution and administration (clinical summary)
- Reconstitute using the Sterile Water for Injection and transfer device supplied with the product kit.
- Bring product and diluent to room temperature prior to reconstitution. Use aseptic technique.
- Do not shake; gently swirl to dissolve. Use immediately after reconstitution. Do not store reconstituted solution.
- If more than one vial is required, reconstitute each vial separately and combine into a single syringe prior to administration.
- Infusion rate: administer intravenously at approximately 10 mL/min, but not exceeding 20 mL/min.
- Visual inspection: do not use if particulate matter or discoloration is observed.
- Specific step‑by‑step reconstitution technique (device use, syringe handling) is described in the available product instructions and should be followed exactly in practice.

### Titration / monitoring to guide dosing
- Titrate dose and frequency to clinical response and measured plasma Factor X activity (see Section 6 Monitoring and Section 11 Pharmacology).
- Use validated assays for Factor X activity. For inadequate clinical response or unexpected trough/plasma levels, assess for inhibitors (see Section 6).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed warnings: None specified in available sources.

- Hypersensitivity reactions (including anaphylaxis): **coagulation factor x (human)** can cause allergic reactions; early signs include angioedema, infusion site inflammation (burning, stinging, erythema), chills, cough, dizziness, fever, flushing, generalized urticaria/hives, headache, hypotension, lethargy, musculoskeletal pain, nausea, pruritus, rash, restlessness, tachycardia, chest tightness, tingling, vomiting, wheezing. If hypersensitivity occurs, discontinue the product and institute appropriate emergency treatment.

- Neutralizing antibodies (inhibitors): Development of anti‑Factor X neutralizing antibodies is possible. Monitor patients clinically and by laboratory testing. If expected plasma Factor X activity levels are not attained, or bleeding is not controlled with an appropriate dose, perform an assay to measure Factor X inhibitor concentration (Nijmegen‑Bethesda assay recommended). In the available clinical studies, inhibitor tests were negative for subjects tested.

- Transmission of infectious agents: This product is plasma‑derived. Although donor screening and virus testing/inactivation/removal steps reduce risk, transmission of infectious agents (including viruses, variant Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease agent and theoretically Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease agent) cannot be completely excluded. Inform patients of this potential risk and report suspected transmission events to the manufacturer/appropriate authority.

- Monitoring and laboratory tests:
  - Measure plasma Factor X activity using validated assays (e.g., one‑stage clotting assay or other validated method) to ensure therapeutic levels are reached and maintained.
  - For prophylaxis: monitor trough levels (target ≥5 IU/dL) and adjust regimen accordingly.
  - Perioperative: measure pre‑ and post‑infusion levels to confirm hemostatic levels and maintenance.
  - If inadequate response, perform inhibitor testing (Nijmegen‑Bethesda assay) and report results in Nijmegen‑Bethesda Units (BU).

- Specific clinical precautions:
  - Use caution in patients receiving other plasma products that may contain Factor X (e.g., fresh frozen plasma, prothrombin complex concentrates).
  - Based on mechanism of action, the activity of **coagulation factor x (human)** is likely to be counteracted by direct and indirect Factor Xa inhibitors.
  - Dose selection in elderly patients should be cautious; start at the low end of dosing range given potential for comorbidities and concomitant medications.
  - Observe infusion site and systemic status during and after administration for early detection of adverse reactions.

- Serious adverse effects: severe hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis and transmission of infectious agents are potential serious risks. Thrombotic events were not reported as attributable adverse events in the provided clinical summaries; Factor Xa/IIa activity was not detectable in the product by NaPTT/FCT and sensitive analytical methods showed very low prothrombinase contamination (<10 ppm), but thrombogenic risk should be considered clinically.

- Immunogenicity: In the clinical development program summarized in available sources, inhibitor assays (screen and Nijmegen‑Bethesda) were negative at baseline and during follow‑up for subjects tested. However, the formation of inhibitors remains a potential risk and patients should be monitored.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: No formal drug interaction studies reported in the available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Use with other plasma products that may contain Factor X (e.g., fresh frozen plasma, prothrombin complex concentrates) should be approached with caution because of potential additive effects.
  - Based on mechanism of action, direct and indirect Factor Xa inhibitors are expected to antagonize the haemostatic effect of **coagulation factor x (human)** — avoid concomitant use where possible and consult specialist guidance if coadministration is considered necessary.
- Clinically significant interactions and management:
  - Monitor coagulation status and Factor X activity when products that alter haemostasis are coadministered.
  - Adjust dosing based on clinical response and laboratory monitoring.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Clinical data:
  - A prospective, open‑label, multicenter pediatric study enrolled nine pediatric patients (ages 0–5 years, n=4; 6–11 years, n=5) with severe (n=8) or moderate (n=1) hereditary Factor X deficiency. These patients were dosed to achieve post‑treatment Factor X levels ≥5 IU/dL. After ≥26 weeks and ≥50 exposure days, investigators rated prophylactic efficacy as excellent for all subjects. Ten bleeds occurred in 3 subjects (6 major, 3 minor, 1 unassessed). Trough FX:C levels remained >5 IU/dL after steady state was achieved. No adverse drug reactions were reported in this study; no inhibitor development was observed.
  - Six pediatric subjects aged 12–17 years were included in other study cohorts for treatment of spontaneous, traumatic and menorrhagic bleeding; outcomes were comparable to younger pediatric groups.
- Approved pediatric dosing (as per Section 4): prophylaxis and on‑demand dosing regimens are specified by age (<12 years vs ≥12 years).
- Safety and monitoring in pediatrics: monitor trough levels and clinical response; use age‑appropriate assays and dosing calculations (see Section 4).

### Elderly use
- Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers (≥65 years) to determine whether response differs from younger subjects. Use caution and consider starting at lower end of dose range, taking comorbidities and concomitant medicines into account.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **coagulation factor x (human)** is a plasma‑derived concentrate of human coagulation Factor X that replaces missing Factor X activity in patients with hereditary Factor X deficiency, restoring coagulation cascade function.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Therapeutic effect is achieved by increasing plasma Factor X activity; prophylaxis aims for trough activity ≥5 IU/dL; perioperative targets include raising levels to 70–90 IU/dL pre‑surgery and maintaining ≥50 IU/dL post‑surgery until bleeding risk has resolved.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Incremental recovery:
  - Children (<12 years): approximately 1.7 IU/dL increase in plasma Factor X per IU/kg administered.
  - Adolescents and adults (≥12 years): approximately 2.0 IU/dL increase in plasma Factor X per IU/kg administered.
- Product potency and composition:
  - Reconstituted concentration (approximate) reported as ~100 IU/mL.
  - Specific activity typically 80–137 IU per mg protein (as reported).
  - Content of Factor II and Factor IX in the reconstituted product reported ≤1 IU/mL.
  - Factor Xa and Factor IIa were not detectable by NaPTT/FCT and were measured as <10 parts per million by weight with sensitive analytical methods.
- Absorption/distribution/metabolism/elimination: Not specified in available sources beyond above recovery and PK assessment notes.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Overview of clinical development:
  - Three multicenter, open‑label, non‑randomized studies contributed to clinical experience summarized in available sources.
  - Total subjects exposed: 27 individuals with hereditary Factor X deficiency received at least one dose in the development program.
- Key trial findings:
  - Sixteen subjects aged 12–58 years with moderate to severe hereditary Factor X deficiency (basal FX:C <5 IU/dL) received COAGADEX for pharmacokinetic evaluation, on‑demand treatment of bleeding episodes, and/or perioperative management. A total of 468 infusions were administered in this cohort, including 242 for on‑demand treatment and control of bleeding episodes, 6 for perioperative management, and 31 for PK assessments. On‑demand dosing with 25 IU/kg was used to treat spontaneous, traumatic and menorrhagic bleeding over up to 2 years.
  - Two subjects (ages 55 and 59) with mild hereditary Factor X deficiency (basal FX:C 6 and 8 IU/dL) received product for perioperative management of four major surgical procedures (40 exposure days).
  - Pediatric study: nine children (ages 2–11 years; four <6 years) received a total of 537 doses (mean 59.7) as routine prophylaxis for at least 6 months; 22 infusions were used to treat bleeds (approx. 2.1 bleeds per subject per year). Investigators reported prophylactic efficacy as excellent; no adverse drug reactions were reported in this pediatric cohort.
- Immunogenicity in trials:
  - All subjects underwent inhibitor screening and Nijmegen‑Bethesda assays at baseline and during follow‑up; all tests were negative in the studies reported.
  - Repeat PK assessments did not suggest development of inhibitors.
