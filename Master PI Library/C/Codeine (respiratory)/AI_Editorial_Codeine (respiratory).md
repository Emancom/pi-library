# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local regulatory guidelines for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from genetic metabolic disorders [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing chronic hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration should be via slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses administered every 3 or 4 hours (and never less frequently than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirements [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments: May be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Target Levels:** In metabolic disorders, plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **ESRD Threshold:** Therapy is typically prompted when trough plasma levels fall below the normal range of 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These occurred primarily in ESRD patients, sometimes within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before any attempt at re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine administration (both oral and intravenous) has been associated with reports of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation):** While this document focuses on the injection, it is noted that safety/efficacy of high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD has not been evaluated, as it may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General Reactions:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Body odor and gastritis are reported less frequently.
- **Serious Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].
- **Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients):** Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) in patients on chronic hemodialysis include:
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%).
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), abdominal pain (5–21%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
    - **Neurological:** Headache (3–37%), dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
    - **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), fever (5–12%), asthenia (8–12%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter category in available sources.
    - **Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Recommendation:** Due to lack of adequate human controlled studies, use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations of carnitine following administration. The benefits to the mother must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as described in the Dosage and Administration section. Efficacy for metabolic disorders is established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific geriatric data was not provided in the source document [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis. Dosing is adjusted based on pre-dialysis trough levels (see Section 3) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into the cellular mitochondria. This provides the substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, intravenous administration of 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis successfully increased mean plasma carnitine concentrations from 20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) has not been definitively determined in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC plastic bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not applicable in the US jurisdiction; refer to local schedules (e.g., Australian S4) in other regions where applicable.
