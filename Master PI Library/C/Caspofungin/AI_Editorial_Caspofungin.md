# Editorial Product Information: Caspofungin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Caspofungin (as acetate)
- **Chemical Class:** Echinocandin; semi-synthetic lipopeptide (synthesised from *Glarea lozoyensis* fermentation).
- **Brand Names:** **CASPOFUNGIN AN** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J02AX04
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** White to off-white lyophilised compact powder for infusion in single-use glass vials containing 50 mg or 70 mg of caspofungin.
- **Route of Administration:** Slow intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Juno Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Empirical Therapy:** For presumed fungal infections in febrile, neutropenic patients who have not responded to broad-spectrum antibiotics.
- **Invasive Candidiasis:** Including candidaemia.
- **Oesophageal Candidiasis.**
- **Invasive Aspergillosis:** Specifically for patients who are refractory to or intolerant of other therapies (e.g., amphotericin B, itraconazole, or voriconazole).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Loading Dose:** A single 70 mg dose on Day 1.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 50 mg daily thereafter.
- **Oesophageal Candidiasis Exception:** 50 mg daily; a 70 mg loading dose is not required.

### Administration Instructions
- **Infusion Rate:** Administer by slow IV infusion over approximately 1 hour.
- **Diluent Restrictions:** **DO NOT USE GLUCOSE-CONTAINING DILUENTS.** Caspofungin is not stable in glucose.
- **Compatibility:** Do not mix or co-infuse with other medications; no compatibility data is available for other substances or additives.
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with 10.5 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Water for Injections. Further dilute the volume (e.g., 250 mL) in 0.9%, 0.45%, or 0.225% Sodium Chloride, or Lactated Ringer’s Injection.

### Indication-Specific Duration
- **Empirical Therapy:** Continue until resolution of neutropenia (typically within 28 days). If fungal infection is confirmed, treat for at least 14 days and at least 7 days after neutropenia and clinical symptoms resolve.
- **Invasive Candidiasis:** Continue for at least 14 days after the last positive culture.
- **Invasive Aspergillosis:** Duration depends on the severity of the underlying disease and clinical response.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to caspofungin acetate or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis:** Reported during administration. Discontinue immediately and initiate appropriate treatment if anaphylaxis occurs.
- **Histamine-Mediated Reactions:** Rash, facial swelling, angioedema, pruritus, sensation of warmth, or bronchospasm may occur.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Post-marketing cases of **Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS)** and **toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN)** have been reported. Use caution in patients with a history of allergic skin reactions.
- **Hepatic Effects:** Laboratory abnormalities in liver function tests (LFTs) have been observed. Isolated cases of significant hepatic dysfunction, hepatitis, and hepatic failure have been reported. Monitor LFTs in patients who develop abnormalities and evaluate the risk/benefit of continuing therapy.
- **Infusion Duration:** Must be administered over 1 hour to reduce the risk of infusion-related reactions.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Tiers
*   **Very Common (>10%):** Pyrexia (pediatric), chills (adult/pediatric), headache (pediatric), elevated liver enzymes (ALT/AST).
*   **Common (1–10%):**
    *   **Blood:** Decreased haemoglobin/haematocrit, decreased WBC.
    *   **Metabolism:** Hypokalaemia.
    *   **Nervous System:** Headache.
    *   **Vascular:** Phlebitis.
    *   **Respiratory:** Dyspnoea.
    *   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea.
    *   **Skin:** Rash, pruritus, erythema, hyperhidrosis.
    *   **General:** Pyrexia, chills, infusion-site pruritus.
*   **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Anaemia, thrombocytopaenia, tachycardia, flushing, hypotension, abdominal pain, cholestasis, renal failure/acute renal failure, and various infusion-site reactions (swelling, pain, erythema).
*   **Not Known:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, pancreatitis [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
Anaphylaxis, hepatic failure, and severe bullous skin reactions (SJS/TEN).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Ciclosporin A:** Co-administration leads to a ~35% increase in caspofungin AUC. Transient elevations in ALT and AST have been observed. Close monitoring of liver enzymes is required if used concomitantly.
- **Tacrolimus:** Caspofungin reduces the AUC of tacrolimus by approximately 20% and the 12-hour concentration ($C_{12hr}$) by 26%. Standard monitoring of tacrolimus blood levels is recommended.
- **Inducers of Drug Clearance:** Rifampicin, efavirenz, nevirapine, phenytoin, dexamethasone, and carbamazepine may reduce caspofungin trough concentrations.
    *   **Management:** Consider increasing the daily dose to 70 mg in adults or 70 mg/m² (max 70 mg) in pediatric patients [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Amphotericin B, Itraconazole, Mycophenolate, Nelfinavir:** No clinically significant pharmacokinetic interactions observed.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Animal studies showed weak embryotoxicity (incomplete ossification, increased resorptions/pre-implantation loss) at exposures similar to clinical doses. Caspofungin crosses the placenta. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Caspofungin is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. It is not known if it is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during therapy.
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility or reproductive performance observed in rat studies at doses up to 5 mg/kg/day.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for ages 3 months to 17 years.
    *   **Dosing:** Based on Body Surface Area (BSA) using the Mosteller formula.
    *   **Standard Dose:** 70 mg/m² loading dose on Day 1, then 50 mg/m² daily (max 70 mg/day).
    *   **Infants <3 months:** Limited data; 25 mg/m² daily may be considered.
- **Elderly Use:** AUC is increased by approximately 28% in healthy elderly subjects, but no dosage adjustment is required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is necessary. Caspofungin is not dialysable.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    *   **Mild (Child-Pugh 5–6):** No adjustment required.
    *   **Moderate (Child-Pugh 7–9):** Maintenance dose should be reduced to 35 mg daily (after a 70 mg loading dose where indicated).
    *   **Severe (Child-Pugh >9):** No clinical experience; use with extreme caution.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the synthesis of $\beta$ (1,3)-D-glucan, an essential component of the cell wall in many filamentous fungi (e.g., *Aspergillus*) and yeast (e.g., *Candida*). Mammalian cells do not contain $\beta$ (1,3)-D-glucan.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound to albumin (~97%). Polyphasic decline with a $\beta$-phase half-life of 9–11 hours and a $\gamma$-phase (terminal) half-life of 40–50 hours.
    *   **Metabolism:** Slow metabolism via hydrolysis and N-acetylation; also undergoes spontaneous chemical degradation.
    *   **Elimination:** Approximately 41% excreted in urine and 34% in faeces. Only 1.4% of the dose is excreted unchanged in urine.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Concentration-dependent activity against *Candida* species.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Empirical Therapy:** A multicentre, double-blind study (N=1111) showed caspofungin (50 mg/day) was as effective as liposomal amphotericin B (3 mg/kg/day) in febrile neutropenic patients, with significantly lower rates of nephrotoxicity (2.6% vs 11.5%) and infusion-related events.
- **Invasive Candidiasis:** In a Phase III study (N=239), caspofungin was comparable to amphotericin B deoxycholate, with a favourable response rate of 73.4% vs 61.7%.
- **Invasive Aspergillosis:** An open-label study (N=69) in refractory or intolerant patients showed a 41% favourable response rate compared to 17% in a historical control group.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Lyophilised Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Stable for up to 1 hour at or below 25°C.
- **Diluted Infusion Solution:** Chemically and physically stable for 24 hours at or below 25°C, or 24 hours if refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C.
