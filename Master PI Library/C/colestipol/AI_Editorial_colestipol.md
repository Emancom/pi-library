# Editorial Product Information: Colestipol Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of colestipol hydrochloride for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for the most current and specific prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Colestipol hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** High molecular weight basic anion-exchange copolymer (bile acid sequestrant).
- **Brand Names:** Available as various generic formulations; notably manufactured by Amneal Pharmaceuticals [US].
- **ATC Code:** C10AC02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 1 gram [US].
    - Also referenced as granules in clinical trial data [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amneal Pharmaceuticals of New York LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Primary Hypercholesterolemia:** Indicated as adjunctive therapy to dietary management for the reduction of elevated serum total and LDL-cholesterol (LDL-C) in patients who do not respond adequately to diet alone [US].
- **Note on Triglycerides:** Colestipol generally has no clinically significant effect on serum triglycerides, though levels may be elevated in some patients during treatment [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Maintenance Range:** 2 grams to 16 grams per day [US].
- **Frequency:** May be administered once daily or in divided doses [US].

### Titration Schedule
- **Initial Dose:** 2 grams once or twice daily [US].
- **Increments:** Increase dose by 2 grams (once or twice daily) at 1- or 2-month intervals, based on lipid profile response (LDL-C and triglycerides) [US].
- **Note:** If a satisfactory response is not achieved at 2 to 16 grams/day with good compliance, combination or alternative therapy should be considered [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Tablets:** Must be swallowed whole. Do not cut, crush, or chew [US]. 
- **Hydration:** Take one tablet at a time with plenty of water or other liquid to facilitate swallowing and prevent esophageal obstruction [US].
- **Timing with Other Meds:** Administer other medications at least one hour before or four hours after colestipol to prevent binding and reduced absorption [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to colestipol hydrochloride or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal Effects:** May produce or severely aggravate pre-existing constipation. Dosage should be increased gradually to minimize risk of fecal impaction. Particular caution is required in patients with symptomatic coronary artery disease (to avoid straining) and those with hemorrhoids [US].
- **Malabsorption of Nutrients:** As a bile acid sequestrant, colestipol may interfere with the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) and folic acid. Chronic use may lead to Vitamin K deficiency and increased bleeding tendency [US].
- **Electrolyte Imbalance:** As an anion exchange resin in the chloride form, prolonged use may theoretically lead to hyperchloremic acidosis [US].
- **Secondary Causes:** Before initiation, exclude secondary causes of hypercholesterolemia (e.g., hypothyroidism, diabetes mellitus, nephrotic syndrome, obstructive liver disease) [US].
- **Swallowing Risk:** Large tablet size may pose a choking hazard or esophageal obstruction, especially in patients with a history of swallowing difficulties [US].
- **Hypothyroidism:** While not reported in patients with normal thyroid function, a theoretical risk exists for those with limited thyroid reserve [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Gastrointestinal (Very Common)
- **Constipation:** The most frequent complaint; may be severe and lead to fecal impaction or aggravation of hemorrhoids [US].

### Gastrointestinal (Common/Infrequent)
- Abdominal pain and cramping, bloating, flatulence, indigestion, heartburn, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea [US].

### Other Reported Effects (Frequency Not Defined)
- **Cardiovascular:** Chest pain, angina, and tachycardia [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rash (infrequent); urticaria and dermatitis (rarely noted with granules) [US].
- **Musculoskeletal:** Aches and pains in extremities, joint pain, arthritis, and backache [US].
- **Neurologic:** Headache, migraine, dizziness, light-headedness, and insomnia [US].
- **Laboratory:** Transient and modest elevations in AST, ALT, and alkaline phosphatase [US].
- **Miscellaneous:** Anorexia, fatigue, shortness of breath, and peripheral edema [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
Colestipol is an anion exchange resin and binds various drugs in the GI tract, reducing their absorption.
- **Pharmacokinetic (Reduced Absorption):**
    - Propranolol (delayed/reduced rate of absorption) [US].
    - Chlorothiazide, hydrochlorothiazide [US].
    - Furosemide [US].
    - Tetracycline and Penicillin G [US].
    - Gemfibrozil [US].
    - Digitalis preparations (digoxin/digitoxin): Conflicting results, but binding potential exists [US].
    - Mycophenolate mofetil: May reduce exposure and efficacy [US].
    - Oral phosphate supplements and hydrocortisone [US].
- **Management:** Most drugs should be taken 1 hour before or 4 hours after colestipol [US]. If a drug with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., digitalis) is titrated while the patient is on colestipol, discontinuation of the resin poses a risk of toxicity [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter in this PI, but noted as essentially non-absorbed systemically (<0.17%). 
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Potential for maternal vitamin malabsorption may be detrimental even with supplementation. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Caution should be exercised. Malabsorption of vitamins in the mother may impact the nutrient content of breast milk [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources (refer to general adult dosing) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Colestipol binds bile acids in the intestine, forming an insoluble complex excreted in feces. This interrupts the enterohepatic circulation of bile acids, forcing the liver to convert more cholesterol into bile acids. This results in increased LDL receptor expression and increased hepatic uptake of LDL from the plasma [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Not absorbed systemically. Less than 0.17% of a dose is recovered in urine [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Not hydrolyzed by digestive enzymes [US].
    - **Excretion:** Primarily via feces as a resin-bile acid complex [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Cholesterol reduction is usually evident within one month. If discontinued, levels return to baseline within one month [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **LRC-CPPT Study:** While using cholestyramine (a similar agent), this 7-year study showed a 19% reduction in coronary heart disease death and nonfatal myocardial infarction [US].
- **Combination Therapy:** Clinical trials demonstrate additive effects when colestipol is combined with niacin or lovastatin. Intensive combination therapy has been shown to reduce the progression and increase the regression of coronary atherosclerotic lesions [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from moisture [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
