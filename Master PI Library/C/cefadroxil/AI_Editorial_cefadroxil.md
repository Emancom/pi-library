# Editorial Product Information: Cefadroxil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **cefadroxil** for healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory data into a single, clinically applicable reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefadroxil monohydrate (a semisynthetic cephalosporin antibiotic).
- **Brand Names:** Available under various generic labels; previously branded as Duricef [United States].
- **ATC Code:** J01DB05 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard classification).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral Capsule: 500 mg [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lupin Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (distributed/relabeled by Proficient Rx LP) [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Cefadroxil** is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by susceptible strains of designated microorganisms in the following conditions [United States]:
- **Urinary Tract Infections:** Caused by *E. coli*, *P. mirabilis*, and *Klebsiella* species.
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** Caused by staphylococci and/or streptococci.
- **Pharyngitis and/or Tonsillitis:** Caused by *Streptococcus pyogenes* (Group A beta-hemolytic streptococci).

*Note: Cefadroxil is effective in eradicating streptococci from the oropharynx; however, data establishing its efficacy in the prophylaxis of subsequent rheumatic fever are not available [United States].*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Urinary Tract Infections:**
    - Uncomplicated lower UTI (e.g., cystitis): 1 g or 2 g per day as a single dose or in two divided doses.
    - Other UTIs: 2 g per day in divided doses.
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** 1 g per day as a single dose or in two divided doses.
- **Pharyngitis and Tonsillitis:** 1 g per day as a single dose or in two divided doses for 10 days.

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Urinary Tract Infections:** 30 mg/kg/day in divided doses every 12 hours.
- **Pharyngitis, Tonsillitis, and Impetigo:** 30 mg/kg/day as a single dose or in two divided doses every 12 hours. For beta-hemolytic streptococcal infections, treatment must continue for at least 10 days.
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** 30 mg/kg/day in equally divided doses every 12 hours.

### Administration Instructions
- May be administered without regard to meals. 
- Administration with food may assist in diminishing potential gastrointestinal complaints sometimes associated with oral cephalosporin therapy.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known allergy to the cephalosporin group of antibiotics [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity and Cross-Reactivity:** Before initiation, evaluate for history of hypersensitivity to cefadroxil, cephalosporins, penicillins, or other drugs. Cross-sensitivity among beta-lactam antibiotics occurs in up to 10% of patients with a history of penicillin allergy. If an allergic reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately.
- **Clostridium difficile-associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents, ranging in severity from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis. Consider CDAD in all patients who present with diarrhea following antibiotic use.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution in patients with markedly impaired renal function (CrCl < 50 mL/min/1.73 m²). Clinical observation and laboratory studies are required.
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms.
- **Seizure Risk:** Several cephalosporins have been implicated in triggering seizures, particularly in patients with renal impairment when dosage is not reduced.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by system organ class; specific frequencies (e.g., % rates) were not detailed in the source, though most are described as rare.

- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, and diarrhea. Symptoms of pseudomembranous colitis may occur during or after treatment.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rash, urticaria, angioedema, pruritus, and anaphylaxis.
- **Hepatic:** Hepatic dysfunction including cholestasis, elevations in serum transaminase, and idiosyncratic hepatic failure.
- **Haematologic:** Moderate transient neutropenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, and pancytopenia. 
- **Dermatologic:** Erythema multiforme, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, and toxic epidermal necrolysis.
- **Other:** Genital pruritus, genital moniliasis, vaginitis, fever, arthralgia, and serum sickness.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Drug/Laboratory Test Interactions:**
    - **Positive Direct Coombs' Test:** Reported during treatment with cephalosporin antibiotics. This may also occur in newborns whose mothers received cephalosporins before parturition.
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category B.** Reproduction studies in mice and rats (up to 11 times the human dose) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Cefadroxil is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to a nursing mother.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 for weight-based dosing.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical experience has not identified differences in responses between elderly and younger patients. However, because cefadroxil is substantially excreted by the kidney, dose selection should be cautious, and monitoring of renal function is recommended in the elderly [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Adjust dosage for patients with CrCl < 50 mL/min/1.73 m². In adults, the initial dose is 1000 mg, followed by a 500 mg maintenance dose at the following intervals:
    - **CrCl 25–50 mL/min:** Every 12 hours.
    - **CrCl 10–25 mL/min:** Every 24 hours.
    - **CrCl 0–10 mL/min:** Every 36 hours.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; acts by inhibiting bacterial cell-wall synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed after oral administration; acid-stable. Peak serum concentrations occur at approximately 16 mcg/mL (500 mg dose) and 28 mcg/mL (1000 mg dose).
    - **Distribution:** Measurable levels present 12 hours after administration.
    - **Excretion:** Over 90% is excreted unchanged in the urine within 24 hours. 
- **Microbiology:** Active against Beta-hemolytic streptococci, Staphylococci (including penicillinase-producing strains), *S. pneumoniae*, *E. coli*, *P. mirabilis*, *Klebsiella* species, and *M. catarrhalis*.
- **Resistance:** Most strains of *Enterococcus faecalis* and *Enterococcus faecium* are resistant. Not active against most *Enterobacter* species, *Morganella morganii*, *P. vulgaris*, *Pseudomonas* species, and *Acinetobacter calcoaceticus*.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Geriatric Data:** Summarised from three UTI trials (n=650, 28% ≥ 60 years) and 14 skin/skin structure infection trials (n=1000, 12% ≥ 60 years). No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between elderly and younger patients.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F) [United States].
- Keep medication out of the reach of children.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [United States].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
