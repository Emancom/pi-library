# Editorial Product Information: Cefotetan

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States** (Food and Drug Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically relevant format. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefotetan (as cefotetan disodium)
- **Chemical Class:** Semisynthetic, broad-spectrum, beta-lactamase resistant cephamycin (cephalosporin) antibacterial.
- **Brand Names:** Cefotetan for Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01DC05
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Powder for injection: 1 gram and 2 grams per vial [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Intramuscular (IM).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Fresenius Kabi USA, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Cefotetan is indicated for the treatment of the following infections when caused by susceptible strains of designated microorganisms [US]:
- **Urinary Tract Infections (UTI):** Caused by *E. coli, Klebsiella* spp. (including *K. pneumoniae*), *Proteus mirabilis, Proteus vulgaris, Providencia rettgeri,* and *Morganella morganii*.
- **Lower Respiratory Tract Infections:** Caused by *S. pneumoniae, S. aureus* (methicillin-susceptible), *H. influenzae, Klebsiella* species, *E. coli, P. mirabilis,* and *Serratia marcescens*.
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** Caused by *S. aureus, S. epidermidis* (methicillin-susceptible), *S. pyogenes, Streptococcus* species, *E. coli, K. pneumoniae, Peptococcus niger,* and *Peptostreptococcus* species.
- **Gynecologic Infections:** Caused by *S. aureus, S. epidermidis, Streptococcus* species, *S. agalactiae, E. coli, P. mirabilis, N. gonorrhoeae, B. fragilis, Prevotella melaninogenica, B. vulgatus, Fusobacterium* species, and gram-positive anaerobic cocci.
- **Intra-abdominal Infections:** Caused by *E. coli, Klebsiella* species, *Streptococcus* species, *B. fragilis, P. melaninogenica, B. vulgatus,* and *Clostridium* species (excluding *C. difficile*).
- **Bone and Joint Infections:** Caused by *S. aureus* (methicillin-susceptible).
- **Surgical Prophylaxis:** To reduce the incidence of postoperative infections in clean-contaminated or potentially contaminated procedures (e.g., cesarean section, hysterectomy, biliary tract surgery, GI surgery) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The standard adult dosage is 1 g to 2 g administered IV or IM every 12 to 24 hours. The total daily dose should not exceed 6 g [US].

| Type of Infection | Daily Dose | Frequency and Route |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Urinary Tract** | 1–4 g | 500 mg q12h (IV/IM); 1–2 g q24h (IV/IM); or 1–2 g q12h (IV/IM) |
| **Skin/Structure (Mild-Mod)** | 2 g | 2 g q24h (IV) or 1 g q12h (IV/IM) |
| **Skin/Structure (Severe)** | 4 g | 2 g q12h (IV) |
| **Other Sites** | 2–4 g | 1–2 g q12h (IV/IM) |
| **Severe Infections** | 4 g | 2 g q12h (IV) |
| **Life-Threatening** | 6 g | 3 g q12h (IV) |

### Surgical Prophylaxis
- **General Surgery:** A single 1 g or 2 g dose IV, administered 30 to 60 minutes prior to the start of surgery.
- **Cesarean Section:** A single 1 g or 2 g dose administered IV as soon as the umbilical cord is clamped [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Intravenous:** Preferred for life-threatening infections or patients at poor risk. Bolus injection should occur over 3 to 5 minutes. Continuous or intermittent infusion is also permissible.
- **Intramuscular:** Inject deeply into a large muscle mass (e.g., gluteus maximus).
- **Reconstitution:** Vials should be reconstituted with Sterile Water for Injection, Bacteriostatic Water for Injection, or 0.9% Sodium Chloride. For IM use, 0.5% or 1% Lidocaine HCl may be used.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to the cephalosporin class of antibiotics [US].
- History of cephalosporin-associated hemolytic anemia [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reactions have been reported. Cross-reactivity with penicillins may occur in up to 10% of patients with a history of penicillin allergy [US].
- **Hemolytic Anemia:** Immune-mediated hemolytic anemia, including fatalities, has been reported. There is a 3-fold increased risk of developing hemolytic anemia with **cefotetan** compared to other cephalosporins. Monitor hematological parameters during treatment [US].
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** May range in severity from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis. Evaluate if diarrhea occurs during or after treatment [US].
- **Coagulopathy:** Like other broad-spectrum antibiotics, **cefotetan** may cause a fall in prothrombin activity. Patients with renal/hepatic impairment, poor nutritional status, or cancer are at higher risk. Monitor prothrombin time and administer exogenous Vitamin K if indicated [US].
- **Disulfiram-like Reaction:** Ingestion of alcohol within 72 hours of administration may result in flushing, sweating, headache, and tachycardia [US].
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported in clinical trials and post-marketing experience include:

| System Organ Class | Frequency/Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Common (1.5%): Diarrhea (1:80), nausea (1:700). Rare: Pseudomembranous colitis. |
| **Hematologic** | Common (1.4%): Eosinophilia (1:200), positive direct Coombs test (1:250), thrombocytosis (1:300). Rare: Agranulocytosis, hemolytic anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, prolonged prothrombin time. |
| **Hepatic** | Common (1.2%): ALT/SGPT rise (1:150), AST/SGOT rise (1:300), Alk Phos rise (1:700), LDH rise (1:700). |
| **Hypersensitivity** | Common (1.2%): Rash (1:150), itching (1:700). Rare: Anaphylaxis, urticaria, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis. |
| **Local** | Uncommon (<1%): Phlebitis at injection site (1:300), discomfort (1:500). |
| **Renal** | Rare: Elevations in BUN and serum creatinine, nephrotoxicity. |
| **Neurologic** | Rare: Seizures (particularly in patients with renal impairment receiving unadjusted doses). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Aminoglycosides:** Concomitant use may increase the risk of nephrotoxicity. Monitor renal function closely [US].
- **Alcohol:** Disulfiram-like reactions occur if alcohol is consumed during or within 72 hours after therapy [US].
- **Laboratory Test Interactions:**
    - **Glucose:** May cause false-positive reactions for glucose in the urine using copper reduction tests (Clinitest, Benedict’s, or Fehling’s solution). Use enzymatic glucose oxidase tests instead [US].
    - **Creatinine:** High concentrations may interfere with the Jaffé reaction, causing false increases in reported creatinine levels [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **US Category B**. Animal studies (rats/monkeys) at 2-3 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use in pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Cefotetan is excreted in human milk in very low concentrations. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in prepubertal rats showed testicular degeneration at 500 mg/kg/day. The clinical relevance to humans is unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety/effectiveness were observed between elderly and younger subjects, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out. Dose selection should be cautious due to a higher prevalence of decreased renal function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage must be adjusted based on Creatinine Clearance (CrCl) [US]:
    - **CrCl >30 mL/min:** Usual dose every 12 hours.
    - **CrCl 10–30 mL/min:** Usual dose every 24 hours OR half the usual dose every 12 hours.
    - **CrCl <10 mL/min:** Usual dose every 48 hours OR one-quarter the usual dose every 12 hours.
    - **Haemodialysis:** Administer one-quarter of the usual dose every 24 hours on days between dialysis, and one-half the usual dose on the day of dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, but caution is advised regarding potential coagulopathy [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal agent that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis. It is resistant to many beta-lactamases [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** 88% plasma protein bound. Achieves therapeutic concentrations in various tissues (skin, muscle, bile, peritoneal fluid, etc.) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** No active metabolites; <7% converted to a less active tautomer [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (51% to 81% excreted unchanged in urine). The plasma half-life is 3 to 4.6 hours in patients with normal renal function [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Specific pivotal trial data is not detailed in the provided source; however, efficacy was established for the listed indications. Note that for certain organisms (e.g., *Peptococcus niger, Fusobacterium* species), efficacy was studied in fewer than ten infections for specific organ systems [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Dry Powder:** Store vials at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from light [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) and 96 hours under refrigeration (5°C).
- **Frozen Solution:** Stable for at least 1 week at -20°C. Thaw at room temperature and do not refreeze [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only [US].
- **Schedule:** Not a scheduled controlled substance, but regulated as a prescription-only antibacterial [US].
