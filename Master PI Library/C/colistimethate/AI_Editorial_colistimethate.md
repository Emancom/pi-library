# Editorial Product Information: Colistimethate Sodium

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from the official regulatory filings of the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It combines clinical data and safety guidelines from both agencies to provide a comprehensive reference for healthcare professionals. Where specific recommendations or data points differ between the two regions, the source agency is noted in brackets.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Colistimethate Sodium for Injection  
**Active Ingredient:** Colistimethate sodium (also known as pentasodium colistinmethanesulfonate).

Each vial contains colistimethate sodium equivalent to 150 mg of colistin base activity. In some jurisdictions, this is equivalent to approximately 4,500,000 International Units (IU) [TGA].

## 2. Approved Indications
Colistimethate sodium is a targeted parenteral antibiotic indicated for the treatment of acute or chronic infections caused by susceptible strains of specific aerobic Gram-negative bacilli. It is particularly indicated for infections caused by *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*.

Clinical efficacy has been established for infections involving:
*   *Enterobacter aerogenes*
*   *Escherichia coli*
*   *Klebsiella pneumoniae*
*   *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*

**Clinical Usage Note:** This agent is not effective against *Proteus* or *Neisseria* species. Therapy may be initiated based on clinical suspicion of Gram-negative infection while awaiting formal culture and susceptibility results. To maintain antimicrobial stewardship and prevent the emergence of drug-resistant bacteria, this drug should only be used for infections proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible organisms [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to colistimethate sodium, colistin, or any component of the formulation.
*   **Myasthenia Gravis:** Use is contraindicated in patients with myasthenia gravis, as the drug reduces the release of acetylcholine at the neuromuscular junction, which may exacerbate muscle weakness [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Maximum Dosage:** The total daily dose should not exceed 5 mg/kg/day in patients with normal renal function.
*   **Nephrotoxicity:** Dose-dependent renal impairment may occur. This is typically reversible upon discontinuation. Monitor renal function (BUN, serum creatinine, and creatinine clearance) closely, especially in the elderly and those with pre-existing renal disease.
*   **Neurological Disturbances:** Patients may experience transient neurological effects, including circumoral paresthesia, numbness, tingling in the extremities, vertigo, dizziness, and slurred speech. Patients should be cautioned against driving or operating hazardous machinery during therapy [TGA].
*   **Respiratory Paralysis:** High serum levels resulting from overdosage or renal failure can lead to neuromuscular blockade, muscle weakness, and apnea.
*   **Electrolyte Abnormalities:** Post-marketing reports have identified cases of renal tubulopathy (Pseudo-Bartter syndrome) associated with IV use. Monitor for hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypocalcemia, and metabolic alkalosis [FDA].
*   **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Like most antibiotics, colistimethate may predispose patients to CDAD, ranging in severity from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis [FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
For adults and pediatric patients with normal renal function, the recommended dose is **2.5 to 5 mg/kg per day** of colistin base activity, administered in 2 to 4 divided doses. In obese patients, dosing should be calculated based on ideal body weight [FDA].

### Renal Impairment
Dosage must be reduced in the presence of renal impairment. The following table provides a suggested modification based on creatinine clearance (CrCl):

| Degree of Impairment | CrCl (mL/min) | Suggested Dosage Schedule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal** | ≥80 | 2.5–5 mg/kg/day, divided into 2–4 doses |
| **Mild** | 50–79 | 2.5–3.8 mg/kg/day, divided into 2 doses |
| **Moderate** | 30–49 | 2.5 mg/kg/day (once daily or in 2 divided doses) |
| **Severe** | 10–29 | 1.5 mg/kg every 36 hours |

### Reconstitution and Administration
*   **Reconstitution:** Dissolve the 150 mg vial with 2.0 mL of Sterile Water for Injection. Swirl gently to avoid frothing. The resulting concentration is 75 mg/mL.
*   **Intravenous (IV) Administration:** May be given as a direct intermittent injection (half the daily dose injected slowly over 3–5 minutes every 12 hours) or as a continuous infusion (half the dose injected slowly, with the remainder added to compatible infusion solutions like 0.9% NaCl or 5% Dextrose and infused over 22–23 hours).
*   **Intramuscular (IM) Administration:** Administer by deep injection into a large muscle mass.
*   **Stability:** Infusion solutions should be used within 24 hours of preparation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Renal:** Nephrotoxicity, decreased urine output, increased BUN/creatinine, and renal tubulopathy.
*   **Neurological:** Paresthesia (tongue and extremities), dizziness, vertigo, slurred speech, seizures, and ataxia.
*   **Respiratory:** Respiratory distress, apnea, and respiratory arrest (particularly following IM injection or in renal failure).
*   **Dermatological/Allergic:** Generalized itching, urticaria, rash, drug fever, and anaphylaxis.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Gastrointestinal upset.
*   **Electrolytes:** Hypokalemia and metabolic alkalosis [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Neuromuscular Blockers:** Extreme caution is required when used concomitantly with curariform muscle relaxants (e.g., tubocurarine, succinylcholine) or other drugs that interfere with nerve transmission (e.g., ether, sodium citrate), as they may potentiate respiratory paralysis.
*   **Other Antibiotics:** Concomitant use with other nephrotoxic or neurotoxic drugs (e.g., aminoglycosides like kanamycin or streptomycin, or polymyxin B) should be avoided if possible. Sodium cephalothin may increase the risk of nephrotoxicity and should be avoided [FDA].
*   **MG Contraindicated Interactions:** Macrolides (azithromycin, clarithromycin) and fluoroquinolones (norfloxacin, ciprofloxacin) should not be used in patients with myasthenia gravis who are receiving colistimethate [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** (TGA Category B2). Animal studies in rabbits have shown evidence of embryotoxicity and teratogenic effects (e.g., talipes varus) at human-equivalent doses [FDA]. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Colistimethate crosses the placental barrier. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if colistimethate is excreted in human milk; however, colistin sulfate is known to be excreted. Caution and clinical monitoring are advised when administering to nursing women.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Peak serum levels are achieved within 10 minutes after IV administration and roughly 2 hours after IM injection.
*   **Distribution:** The drug crosses the placental barrier. It has a serum half-life of approximately 2 to 3 hours in both adults and children, including premature infants.
*   **Elimination:** Primarily eliminated via renal excretion. In the first 8 hours following IV administration, approximately 60% to 80% of the dose is recovered in the urine.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Colistimethate sodium is a surface-active polypeptide antibiotic. It acts as a detergent on the bacterial cytoplasmic membrane, disrupting its integrity. This leads to the leakage of intracellular components and results in rapid bactericidal activity against susceptible Gram-negative organisms.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Unreconstituted Vials:** Store at room temperature (below 25°C [TGA] or 20°C–25°C [FDA]).
*   **Reconstituted Solution:** The reconstituted solution is stable for up to 7 days when stored under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C) or at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) [FDA]. However, any infusion solution intended for IV drip should be freshly prepared and used within 24 hours [TGA/FDA].
