# Editorial Product Information: cabotegravir + rilpivirine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **cabotegravir + rilpivirine**
- Chemical class: Not specified in available sources
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular (gluteal) injection only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of Human Immunodeficiency Virus type 1 (HIV-1) infection in adults who are virologically suppressed (HIV‑1 RNA <50 copies/mL) and who have no known or suspected resistance to either cabotegravir or rilpivirine.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adults)
- Dosing comprises three phases: optional oral lead‑in, initiation injections, and continuation injections.
- Optional oral lead-in (to assess tolerability): cabotegravir 30 mg once daily plus rilpivirine 25 mg once daily for approximately 1 month (at least 28 days). When cabotegravir tablets are given with rilpivirine tablets, both should be taken with a meal.
- Initiation injections (on final day of prior antiretroviral therapy or on last day of oral lead‑in if used):
  - Single 3 mL intramuscular (IM) injection of cabotegravir (600 mg)
  - Single 3 mL IM injection of rilpivirine (900 mg)
- Continuation injection options:
  - Monthly schedule:
    - One month after initiation: single 2 mL IM injection cabotegravir (400 mg) and single 2 mL IM rilpivirine (600 mg), then every month thereafter.
    - Patients may receive continuation injections up to 7 days before or after scheduled monthly dosing.
  - Every‑2‑month schedule:
    - Second initiation injection (one month after first initiation): repeat single 3 mL IM cabotegravir (600 mg) and single 3 mL IM rilpivirine (900 mg).
    - Thereafter, single 3 mL IM cabotegravir (600 mg) and single 3 mL IM rilpivirine (900 mg) every 2 months.
    - Patients may receive injections up to 7 days before or after scheduled every‑2‑month dosing.
- Switching regimens:
  - Monthly → every‑2‑month: one single 3 mL IM cabotegravir (600 mg) and one single 3 mL IM rilpivirine (900 mg) administered one month after the last monthly 2 mL continuation injection; then every‑2‑month 3 mL dosing thereafter.
  - Every‑2‑month → monthly: one single 2 mL IM cabotegravir (400 mg) and one single 2 mL IM rilpivirine (600 mg) given two months after the last 3 mL continuation injection, then monthly 2 mL dosing thereafter.

### Missed dose instructions
- Adherence to scheduled injections is strongly recommended to reduce risk of loss of virologic suppression and resistance.
- If a deviation of more than 7 days from a scheduled visit cannot be avoided:
  - Monthly regimen:
    - Oral therapy (cabotegravir 30 mg tablet + rilpivirine 25 mg tablet once daily) may be used to substitute up to two consecutive monthly injection visits. First oral dose should be taken approximately one month (±7 days) after the last injection. Resume injection dosing on the day oral dosing completes.
    - If time since last injection is ≤2 months: resume monthly 2 mL injection dosing as soon as possible.
    - If time since last injection is >2 months: re‑initiate with 3 mL initiation injections (cabotegravir 600 mg and rilpivirine 900 mg IM), then continue monthly 2 mL injections.
  - Every‑2‑month regimen:
    - Oral therapy (cabotegravir 30 mg + rilpivirine 25 mg once daily) may replace one two‑monthly injection visit. First oral dose should be taken approximately two months (±7 days) after the last injection. Resume injections on the day oral dosing completes.
    - Specific re‑initiation guidance for missed every‑2‑month injections:
      - Injection 2 (the second initiation dose): if ≤2 months since last injection → resume with 3 mL injections as soon as possible and continue 2‑month schedule; if >2 months → re‑initiate with a 3 mL injection followed by a second 3 mL initiation injection one month later, then continue every‑2‑month dosing.
      - Injection 3 or later: if ≤3 months since last injection → resume with 3 mL injections and continue 2‑month schedule; if >3 months → re‑initiate with two 3 mL initiation injections one month apart, then continue every‑2‑month dosing.

### Administration instructions
- For gluteal IM injection only. Do not inject intravenously.
- Two separate injections are required at each dosing visit: one cabotegravir injection and one rilpivirine injection; use separate gluteal injection sites during the same visit.
- Vials should be brought to room temperature prior to administration (not exceeding 25°C).
- Inject slowly and take care to avoid accidental intravenous administration.
- Observe patients briefly (approximately 10 minutes) after injection for post‑injection reactions.
- Administer only by a healthcare professional.
- Consider patient BMI when selecting needle length; the 1.5‑inch needle provided may be insufficient for patients with higher BMI (example: >30 kg/m2); longer needles may be required.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to cabotegravir or rilpivirine or to any excipient in the product.
- Concomitant use with the following (these combinations are contraindicated):
  - Anticonvulsants that are strong inducers (phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine)
  - Antimycobacterials that are strong inducers (rifabutin, rifampicin, rifapentine)
  - Systemic dexamethasone (except single‑dose use)
  - St John’s wort (Hypericum perforatum)
- Because the combination constitutes a complete antiretroviral regimen, coadministration with other antiretroviral medications for HIV‑1 treatment is not recommended.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed / Emphasised warnings
- **Risk of resistance following treatment discontinuation:** Due to prolonged subtherapeutic plasma concentrations after final injection, it is essential to start an alternative fully suppressive antiretroviral regimen no later than one month after the final monthly injection or no later than two months after the final every‑2‑month injection to minimize risk of resistance.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity reactions:
  - Hypersensitivity reactions have been reported in association with integrase inhibitors; manifesting as rash, constitutional symptoms and sometimes organ dysfunction, including liver injury. If hypersensitivity is suspected (severe rash or rash with systemic features such as fever, malaise, muscle/joint aches, blisters, oral lesions, conjunctivitis, facial oedema, hepatitis, eosinophilia, angioedema), discontinue **cabotegravir + rilpivirine** and other suspected agents immediately. Monitor clinical status and liver aminotransferases; initiate appropriate therapy.
- Post‑injection reactions:
  - Serious post‑injection reactions have been reported within minutes of injection (including dyspnoea, bronchospasm, agitation, abdominal cramping, rash/urticaria, dizziness, flushing, sweating, oral numbness, changes in blood pressure, and pain). These events were uncommon in clinical trials and may have been associated with accidental intravenous administration. Ensure correct IM technique and monitor patients after injection.
- Hepatotoxicity:
  - Hepatotoxicity, including transaminase elevations and liver injury, has been reported. Monitor liver chemistries; discontinue if hepatotoxicity is suspected.
- Prolonged‑release properties:
  - Residual subtherapeutic concentrations of cabotegravir and rilpivirine may persist for prolonged periods (up to 12 months or longer) after treatment discontinuation. This has implications for selection and timing of subsequent antiretroviral therapy and for management of adverse events that may be prolonged.
- Interactions:
  - Caution when co‑prescribing medicines known to induce UGT1A1/UGT1A9 (cabotegravir) or CYP3A (rilpivirine) due to potential loss of efficacy or increased concentrations. See Section 8 for full interaction details.
- Opportunistic infections:
  - Patients remain at risk of opportunistic infections and should be clinically monitored by physicians experienced in management of HIV.
- Transmission of infection:
  - Effective viral suppression reduces but does not eliminate risk of sexual transmission; follow national prevention guidelines.
- Effects on laboratory tests:
  - Small, non‑progressive increases in total bilirubin (without clinical jaundice) have been observed.
  - Elevated transaminases primarily associated with acute viral hepatitis in trials; a few subjects had suspected drug‑related hepatotoxicity.
  - Elevated lipases and asymptomatic creatine phosphokinase (CPK) elevations have been observed in clinical trials.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic monitoring of liver function tests (ALT/AST, bilirubin) is recommended.
- Monitor for signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity and hepatotoxicity.
- Clinical reassessment if injections are missed to determine appropriateness of resumption and need for oral bridging therapy.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### General notes
- No drug interaction studies have been performed with the intramuscular injection formulation; interaction data is based on oral cabotegravir and oral rilpivirine studies. Exercise caution when extrapolating interactions to the long‑acting IM formulation.
- Because cabotegravir + rilpivirine is a complete antiretroviral regimen, coadministration with other antiretroviral medications for HIV‑1 treatment is not recommended.

### Clinically significant contraindicated interactions (do not coadminister)
- Strong inducers of UGT1A1/UGT1A9 (cabotegravir is primarily metabolised by UGT1A1 with some contribution from UGT1A9): anticonvulsants — phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine. Concomitant use is contraindicated.
- Strong inducers of drug metabolism that reduce rilpivirine levels (CYP3A inducers): rifampicin, rifapentine, rifabutin — concomitant use is contraindicated.
- Systemic dexamethasone (multiple doses) — contraindicated (single‑dose systemic dexamethasone is an exception).
- St John’s wort (Hypericum perforatum) — contraindicated.

### Pharmacokinetic interaction details
- Cabotegravir:
  - Metabolism: primarily UGT1A1, some UGT1A9.
  - Strong inducers of UGT are expected to decrease cabotegravir plasma concentrations and may lead to loss of efficacy.
  - Cabotegravir is a substrate of P‑glycoprotein (P‑gp) and breast cancer resistance protein (BCRP), but due to high permeability no clinically meaningful absorption changes are expected with P‑gp or BCRP inhibitors.
  - In vitro, cabotegravir inhibited OAT1 and OAT3; PBPK modelling suggests no clinically relevant interaction expected at therapeutic concentrations.
  - Cabotegravir did not affect midazolam (CYP3A4 probe) in vivo and is not expected to inhibit or induce a range of CYPs and UGTs clinically.
- Rilpivirine:
  - Metabolism: primarily CYP3A. Inducers of CYP3A may decrease rilpivirine exposure; inhibitors may increase rilpivirine exposure.

### Management notes for co‑medications
- Avoid coadministration with known strong inducers of UGT or CYP3A (see contraindications above).
- Exercise caution when prescribing concomitant medications that may induce or inhibit the relevant metabolic pathways; consider alternative therapies that do not interact.
- Given the long‑acting nature with prolonged subtherapeutic exposure after discontinuation, consider potential interactions with subsequent therapies initiated after stopping injections.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents under 18 years have not been established. No data available.

### Elderly
- No dose adjustment required in elderly patients. Limited data available in patients aged ≥65 years.

### Renal impairment
- No dosage adjustment required for patients with mild (CrCl ≥60 to <90 mL/min) or moderate (CrCl ≥30 to <60 mL/min) renal impairment and not on dialysis.
- Not studied in patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl ≥15 to <30 mL/min) or in patients on dialysis; increased monitoring for adverse events is recommended.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dosage adjustment required for patients with mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh A or B).
- Not studied in severe hepatic impairment (Child‑Pugh C); use in severe hepatic impairment is not established.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacokinetics (selected information available)
- Cabotegravir:
  - Primarily metabolised by UGT1A1 with some contribution from UGT1A9.
  - Substrate of P‑gp and BCRP; not a substrate of OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OATP2B1, or OCT1 in vitro.
  - In vitro inhibition of OAT1 and OAT3 observed; PBPK modelling indicates no clinically relevant interactions expected at therapeutic concentrations.
- Rilpivirine:
  - Primarily metabolised by CYP3A.
- Formulation and release:
  - The intramuscular formulations have prolonged‑release characteristics. Residual subtherapeutic concentrations of both agents can persist in systemic circulation for extended periods (up to 12 months or longer) following discontinuation, which may influence subsequent antiretroviral selection and the duration of adverse effects.

## 12. Clinical Trials

### Key trial observations reported in the available source
- ATLAS‑2M trial (referenced in the available source):
  - A higher rate of confirmed virological failure (CVF: two consecutive plasma HIV‑1 RNA ≥200 copies/mL) was observed with the every‑2‑month injection schedule compared with the monthly schedule.
  - CVF rates reported: 8/522 (1.5%) in the every‑2‑month arm vs 2/523 (0.4%) in the monthly arm.
- Clinical trial safety observations:
  - Serious post‑injection reactions were reported within minutes after injection of rilpivirine in clinical trials; events included dyspnoea, bronchospasm, agitation, abdominal cramping, rash/urticaria, dizziness, flushing, sweating, oral numbness, blood pressure changes, and pain. Events were uncommon and some patients required supportive care.
  - Elevated lipase values (Grade 3 and 4) occurred at a higher incidence with cabotegravir + rilpivirine compared with the comparator antiretroviral regimen group in clinical studies; these were generally asymptomatic and did not lead to discontinuation.
  - Elevated transaminases were observed; most were attributed to acute viral hepatitis, with a few assessed as suspected drug‑related hepatotoxicity.
  - Asymptomatic CPK elevations mainly associated with exercise were observed.
