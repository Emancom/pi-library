# Editorial Product Information: Cefiderocol

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of cefiderocol for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information in any specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should consult local regulatory documents for specific jurisdictional requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefiderocol (as cefiderocol sulfate tosylate).
- **Chemical Class:** Siderophore cephalosporin antibacterial.
- **Brand Name(s):** **FETROJA** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 1 gram of cefiderocol (lyophilized powder for reconstitution) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Shionogi Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Complicated Urinary Tract Infections (cUTI):** Treatment of patients 18 years of age or older with cUTI, including pyelonephritis, caused by susceptible Gram-negative microorganisms: *Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Proteus mirabilis*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, and *Enterobacter cloacae* complex [US].
- **Hospital-acquired and Ventilator-associated Bacterial Pneumonia (HABP/VABP):** Treatment of patients 18 years of age or older with HABP/VABP caused by susceptible Gram-negative microorganisms: *Acinetobacter baumannii* complex, *Escherichia coli*, *Enterobacter cloacae* complex, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, and *Serratia marcescens* [US].
- **Usage Note:** To minimize drug-resistant bacteria, cefiderocol should only be used for infections proven or strongly suspected to be caused by susceptible bacteria [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
The standard recommended regimen for patients with a creatinine clearance (CLcr) of 60 to 119 mL/min is **2 grams administered every 8 hours**.

### Administration Instructions
- **Infusion Time:** Must be administered over **3 hours** [US].
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute each 1-gram vial with 10 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection or 5% Dextrose Injection. Shake gently and let stand until foam disappears (approx. 2 minutes). 
- **Dilution:** The reconstituted solution must be further diluted into a 100 mL infusion bag of 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Dextrose prior to administration [US].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Generally 7 to 14 days, guided by clinical status [US].

### Dosing in Renal Impairment and Augmented Clearance
Dose adjustments are required for renal function outside the 60–119 mL/min range [US]:

| Estimated CLcr (mL/min)* | Dose | Frequency | Infusion Time |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **≥ 120 (Augmented)** | 2 grams | Every 6 hours | 3 hours |
| **30 to 59** | 1.5 grams | Every 8 hours | 3 hours |
| **15 to 29** | 1 gram | Every 8 hours | 3 hours |
| **< 15 (without HD)** | 0.75 grams | Every 12 hours | 3 hours |
| **Intermittent Hemodialysis (HD)** | 0.75 grams | Every 12 hours | 3 hours |

*\*Estimated by Cockcroft-Gault equation.*
- **HD Note:** Complete HD at the latest possible time before starting a dose. If dosing coincides with HD, administer the dose immediately after HD completion [US].
- **Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT):** Dosing should be based on effluent flow rate (ranging from 0.75g–2g at intervals of 8–12 hours). Clinicians should monitor residual renal function as adjustments may be needed [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known history of severe hypersensitivity to **cefiderocol**, other beta-lactam antibacterial drugs, or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Increase in All-Cause Mortality:** An increase in all-cause mortality was observed in a trial of critically ill patients with carbapenem-resistant Gram-negative infections treated with cefiderocol compared to best available therapy. The cause is not established. Clinical response should be closely monitored in patients with cUTI and HABP/VABP [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal anaphylactic reactions and serious skin reactions have been reported with beta-lactams. Cross-hypersensitivity may occur in patients with penicillin allergy. Discontinue immediately if an allergic reaction occurs [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all systemic antibacterials. Severity can range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis [US].
- **Seizures and CNS Adverse Reactions:** Cephalosporins have been implicated in triggering seizures, nonconvulsive status epilepticus, and encephalopathy, particularly in patients with renal impairment or known seizure disorders. Adjust dose for renal function and consider discontinuation if CNS reactions occur [US].
- **Development of Drug-Resistant Bacteria:** Use in the absence of a proven infection increases the risk of resistance [US].

### Other Significant Effects
- **Uncommon (< 2%):** Thrombocytosis, congestive heart failure, bradycardia, stomatitis, pyrexia, peripheral edema, cholelithiasis, seizure, dysgeusia, and pleural effusion [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Neutropenia, eosinophilia, and chromaturia (abnormal urine color) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Cefiderocol is not an inhibitor or inducer of major CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, 3A4). It is not an inhibitor of OATP1B1, MATE1, P-gp, BCRP, or BSEP. It is not a substrate of OAT1, OAT3, OCT2, or MATE transporters [US].
- **Drug/Laboratory Test Interactions:** Cefiderocol may result in false-positive results in urine dipstick tests (protein, ketones, or occult blood). Use alternate laboratory methods to confirm positive results [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No specific category]. There are no available data on use in pregnant women. Animal studies in rats and mice showed no evidence of embryo-fetal toxicity at exposures up to 1.3 times the maximum human recommended dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if cefiderocol is excreted in human milk. Cefiderocol was detected in rat milk (6% of peak plasma levels). The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the mother and potential adverse effects on the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility were observed in adult rats at intravenous doses up to 1000 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients ≥ 65 years and younger patients. Because the drug is substantially excreted by the kidney, dose selection should be cautious and renal function monitored [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Significant adjustments are required for CLcr < 60 mL/min and CLcr ≥ 120 mL/min (see Section 3).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Hepatic metabolism is a minor pathway. No dosage adjustments are necessary in patients with hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Cefiderocol is a siderophore cephalosporin. It binds to extracellular free (ferric) iron and is actively transported across the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria via siderophore iron uptake mechanisms. It exerts bactericidal action by inhibiting cell wall biosynthesis through binding to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is 40% to 60%. Volume of distribution is approximately 18 L [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Minimally metabolized (< 10%) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted by the kidneys (98.6% in urine, 90.6% as unchanged drug). The terminal elimination half-life is 2 to 3 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Activity correlates with the percentage of time that unbound plasma concentrations exceed the minimum inhibitory concentration (%T > MIC) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Trial 1 (APEKS-cUTI):** A double-blind trial in 448 adults with cUTI. Cefiderocol demonstrated a higher composite response (microbiological eradication and clinical cure) of 72.6% compared to 54.6% for imipenem/cilastatin [US].
- **Trial 2 (PROMETHEUS):** A noninferiority trial in 292 adults with HABP/VABP. Cefiderocol was noninferior to meropenem regarding Day 14 all-cause mortality (12.4% vs 12.2%, respectively) [US].
- **Trial 3 (CREDIBLE-CR):** An open-label trial in patients with carbapenem-resistant infections, which noted the increase in all-cause mortality mentioned in Warnings (Section 5) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Protect from light [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Should be transferred to the infusion bag immediately; stable for up to 1 hour at room temperature [US].
- **Diluted Infusion Solution:** Stable for 6 hours at room temperature. If refrigerated (2°C to 8°C), it is stable for 24 hours (protected from light), after which the infusion must be completed within 6 hours at room temperature [US].
