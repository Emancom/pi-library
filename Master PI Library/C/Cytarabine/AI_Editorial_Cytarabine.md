# Editorial Product Information: Cytarabine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of cytarabine, synthesized from official product information for use by healthcare professionals.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Cytarabine
*   **Chemical Class:** Synthetic nucleoside (pyrimidine analogue)
*   **Brand Names:** Cytarabine ACC, Cytarabine Accord [Australian]
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    *   **Solution for Injection (20 mg/mL):** 40 mg/2 mL and 100 mg/5 mL vials [Australian].
    *   **Solution for Injection (100 mg/mL):** 100 mg/1 mL, 500 mg/5 mL, 1000 mg/10 mL, 2000 mg/20 mL, 4000 mg/40 mL, and 5000 mg/50 mL vials [Australian].
*   **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous injection or infusion, subcutaneous, and intrathecal. Note: The 100 mg/mL concentration is unsuitable for intrathecal use [Australian].
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Accord Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Acute Myelocytic Leukaemia (AML):** Induction and maintenance of remission in both adults and children [Australian].
*   **Acute Lymphocytic Leukaemia (ALL):** Treatment and remission induction/maintenance [Australian].
*   **Chronic Myelocytic Leukaemia (CML):** Treatment of the blast phase [Australian].
*   **Non-Hodgkin’s Lymphoma:** In children as part of a combination drug program (e.g., LSA2L2) [Australian].
*   **Meningeal Leukaemia:** Intrathecal administration for prophylaxis or treatment [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Single Agent Therapy:** Doses up to 200 mg/m² daily as a continuous IV infusion for five days (120 hours), repeated at approximately two-weekly intervals.
*   **Maintenance:** Generally involves modified induction schedules, typically with greater intervals between courses.

### Indication-Specific Dosing
*   **Intrathecal Use (Meningeal Leukaemia):** Doses range from 5 mg/m² to 75 mg/m². The most frequent regimen is 30 mg/m² every four days until cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) findings are normal, followed by one additional treatment [Australian].
*   **Combination Therapy:** Dosages must be adjusted based on the specific regimen and the myelosuppressive potential of concurrent agents.

### Administration Instructions
*   **Preparation:** Can be diluted in 5% Glucose or 0.9% Sodium Chloride to concentrations as low as 0.1 mg/mL. Diluted solutions should be used as soon as possible or stored at 2°C – 8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].
*   **Handling:** Cytotoxic agent; require trained personnel, designated preparation areas (laminar flow cabinet), and protective apparel. Pregnant personnel should avoid handling [Australian].
*   **Pharmacy Bulk Packs:** Restricted to hospital pharmacies/compounding centers; should be spiked once and discarded within 24 hours [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to **cytarabine** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
*   Not specified in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
*   **Myelosuppression:** Potent bone marrow suppressant. Therapy should be withheld if the polymorphonuclear granulocyte count falls below 1 x 10⁹/L or the platelet count falls below 50 x 10⁹/L [Australian].
*   **Neurological Toxicity:** Severe reactions including headache, paralysis, coma, and stroke-like episodes have been reported, primarily in adolescents when IV cytarabine is combined with intrathecal methotrexate [Australian].
*   **Experimental High-Dose Toxicity:** High-dose regimens are associated with severe CNS, GI, and pulmonary toxicity (e.g., adult respiratory distress syndrome, pulmonary oedema). Corneal toxicity and haemorrhagic conjunctivitis may occur but can be prevented with prophylactic corticosteroid eye drops [Australian].
*   **Anaphylaxis:** Acute cardiopulmonary arrest has been reported following IV administration [Australian].
*   **Hyperuricaemia:** Rapid lysis of neoplastic cells may induce high uric acid levels; monitor and treat as necessary [Australian].
*   **Infections:** Increased susceptibility to viral, bacterial, and fungal infections due to immunosuppression. Avoid live or live-attenuated vaccines [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
*   Daily assessment of leukocyte and platelet levels.
*   Frequent bone marrow examinations after blasts disappear from peripheral blood.
*   Periodic checks of liver and kidney function [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Conventional Dose Schedules
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Sepsis, pneumonia, bone marrow failure, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, leukopenia, stomatitis, mouth/anal ulceration, diarrhoea, vomiting, nausea, abdominal pain, abnormal hepatic function, alopecia, rash, pyrexia, Cytarabine Syndrome |
| **Common (1-10%)** | Anorexia, bleeding (all sites), thrombophlebitis, skin ulceration |
| **Uncommon (0.1-1%)** | Injection site cellulitis, anaphylactic reaction, allergic oedema, neurotoxicity, neuritis, dizziness, headache, conjunctivitis, sore throat, shortness of breath, oesophagitis, oesophageal ulceration, bowel necrosis, jaundice, urticaria, pruritus, renal dysfunction, urinary retention, chest pain |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Pericarditis, sinus bradycardia, pancreatitis, toxic erythema of chemotherapy, injection site reaction |

### Experimental/High-Dose Therapy
*   **Very Common:** Cerebral/cerebellar disorder, somnolence, haemorrhagic conjunctivitis, corneal disorder, ARDS, pulmonary oedema.
*   **Frequency Not Known:** Liver abscess, personality changes, coma, convulsion, peripheral neuropathy, cardiomyopathy, bowel necrosis, necrotising colitis, liver injury [Australian].

### Specific Syndromes
*   **Cytarabine Syndrome:** Characterized by fever, myalgia, bone pain, chest pain, maculopapular rash, conjunctivitis, and malaise (usually 6-12 hours post-administration). Responds well to corticosteroids [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Methotrexate:** Intravenous **cytarabine** may inhibit cellular uptake of methotrexate. Conversely, methotrexate may reduce the activity of **cytarabine**. Concomitant use increases neurotoxicity risk [Australian].
*   **Digoxin:** Reversible decrease in steady-state plasma digoxin levels and renal excretion. Monitor digoxin levels or consider digitoxin [Australian].
*   **Gentamicin:** Potential antagonism of gentamicin's antibacterial activity against *K. pneumoniae* [Australian].
*   **Fluorocytosine:** **Cytarabine** may inhibit the efficacy of fluorocytosine via competitive inhibition of uptake [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D**. Known teratogen in animals. Not recommended for use in pregnant women, especially during the first trimester. If used, the patient must be apprised of the risk to the foetus [Australian].
*   **Contraception:** Females should use highly effective contraception during and for 6 months post-treatment. Males should use effective contraception during and for 3 months post-treatment [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if **cytarabine** is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during and for at least one week after the last dose [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Indicated for childhood AML and NHL. Dosing is typically adjusted based on age, body weight, or surface area [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; higher likelihood of CNS toxicity after high-dose treatment [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** The liver is the primary site of inactivation; use with caution in patients with liver dysfunction [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Cell cycle phase-specific (S-phase). Acts by inhibiting DNA synthesis. It is converted intracellularly to cytarabine triphosphate (active metabolite) by deoxycytidine kinase [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Orally ineffective (<20% absorption). SC/IM administration produces peak plasma levels in 20-60 minutes [Australian].
    *   **Distribution:** Widely distributed; crosses the blood-brain barrier to a limited extent. CSF levels are approximately 40% of steady-state plasma levels after continuous IV infusion [Australian].
    *   **Metabolism:** Rapidly metabolized in the liver (and kidneys/GI mucosa) by cytidine deaminase to the inactive metabolite uracil arabinoside (ara-U) [Australian].
    *   **Elimination:** Biphasic elimination (half-lives ~10 min and 1-3 hours). 70-80% excreted in urine within 24 hours (90% as ara-U) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 25°C [Australian].
*   **Shelf Life:** Consult the packaging for expiry date; in Australia, refer to the ARTG public summary [Australian].
*   **Disposal:** Dispose of cytotoxic waste in double-sealed polythene bags and incinerate at 1100°C [Australian].
