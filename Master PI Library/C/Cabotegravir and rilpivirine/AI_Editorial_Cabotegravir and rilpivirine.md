# Editorial Product Information: Cabotegravir and Rilpivirine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local prescribing guidelines required for clinical practice in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local regulatory documentation before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Cabotegravir (integrase strand transfer inhibitor) and Rilpivirine (non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **CABENUVA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Cabotegravir prolonged-release suspension for injection:** 200 mg/mL (available as 400 mg/2 mL or 600 mg/3 mL vials).
    - **Rilpivirine prolonged-release suspension for injection:** 300 mg/mL (available as 600 mg/2 mL or 900 mg/3 mL vials).
- **Route of Administration:** Gluteal intramuscular (IM) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ViiV Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated for the treatment of Human Immunodeficiency Virus type 1 (HIV-1) in adults who are virologically suppressed (HIV-1 RNA <50 copies/mL). 
- **Requirements:** Patients must have no known or suspected resistance to either cabotegravir or rilpivirine [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment consists of three phases: an optional oral lead-in, initiation injections, and continuation injections. Therapy should be managed by a physician experienced in HIV management.

### Optional Oral Lead-in
To assess tolerability prior to starting injections, patients may take oral **VOCABRIA** (cabotegravir 30 mg) and oral **EDURANT** (rilpivirine 25 mg) once daily for approximately one month (at least 28 days).
- **Administration:** Cabotegravir may be taken with or without food; however, when co-administered with rilpivirine, both must be taken with a meal [Australian].

### Injection Schedules
Two dosing schedules are available:

**1. Monthly Dosing Schedule:**
- **Initiation (Month 1):** Single 3 mL IM injection of cabotegravir (600 mg) and single 3 mL IM injection of rilpivirine (900 mg).
- **Continuation (Month 2 onwards):** Monthly 2 mL IM injection of cabotegravir (400 mg) and 2 mL IM injection of rilpivirine (600 mg).
- **Window:** Injections may be given up to 7 days before or after the scheduled date.

**2. Every 2-Month (Q8W) Dosing Schedule:**
- **Initiation (Months 1 and 2):** Single 3 mL IM injection of cabotegravir (600 mg) and 3 mL IM injection of rilpivirine (900 mg) at Month 1 AND Month 2.
- **Continuation (Month 4 onwards):** 3 mL IM injection of cabotegravir (600 mg) and 3 mL IM injection of rilpivirine (900 mg) administered every 2 months.

### Administration Instructions
- **Gluteal IM use only.** Do not inject intravenously.
- Injections must be administered at separate gluteal sites during the same visit.
- Vials should reach room temperature (not exceeding 25°C) before administration.
- **BMI Considerations:** For patients with a high Body Mass Index (>30 kg/m²), the provided 1.5-inch needle may be insufficient; longer needles may be required to ensure reach to the gluteus muscle [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
Adherence is critical to prevent viral rebound and resistance.
- **Monthly Dosing:** If a dose is missed by >7 days, oral therapy (30 mg CAB/25 mg RPV) can be used for up to 2 months. If >2 months have passed since the last injection, re-initiate with the 3 mL initiation dose.
- **Every 2-Month Dosing:** Specific re-initiation protocols apply depending on whether the missed dose occurs at Injection 2 or Injection 3+ and the length of the delay [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to cabotegravir, rilpivirine, or any excipients.
- **Drug Interactions:** Co-administration with the following is contraindicated due to significant induction of UGT1A1 or CYP3A4, which may lead to loss of virologic response:
    - **Anticonvulsants:** Phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine.
    - **Antimycobacterials:** Rifabutin, rifampicin, rifapentine.
    - **Glucocorticoids:** Systemic dexamethasone (except as a single-dose treatment).
    - **Herbal Products:** St John's wort (*Hypericum perforatum*).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources [Australian].
- **Risk of Resistance Following Discontinuation:** To avoid resistance due to the "pharmacokinetic tail" (residual drug concentrations for up to 12 months), it is essential to start an alternative suppressive regimen within one month of the last monthly injection or two months of the last Q2M injection.
- **Post-Injection Reactions:** Serious reactions (e.g., dyspnea, agitation, flushing, dizziness, BP changes) have been reported within minutes of rilpivirine injection, likely due to accidental IV administration. Observe patients for 10 minutes post-injection.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Reported in patients with and without pre-existing liver disease. Monitoring of liver chemistries is recommended; discontinue if hepatotoxicity is suspected.
- **Long-Acting Properties:** Residual concentrations may persist in the systemic circulation for 12 months or longer.
- **Depressive Disorders:** Suicidal ideation and attempts have been reported [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Injection Site Reactions (ISRs)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Pain (up to 84%), nodules, induration, and pyrexia (10%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Swelling, erythema, pruritus, bruising, and warmth.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Cellulitis, abscess, and anesthesia.
- **Note:** ISRs are generally mild to moderate (Grade 1–2) and decrease in frequency over time.

### Systemic Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (up to 12%), nasopharyngitis, and upper respiratory tract infection.
- **Common (1–10%):** Depression, anxiety, insomnia, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, flatulence, rash, myalgia, fatigue, asthenia, and weight increase.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Hepatotoxicity, somnolence, vasovagal reactions, and increased transaminases.
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cabotegravir:** Primarily metabolised by UGT1A1. Strong inducers decrease plasma levels significantly.
- **Rilpivirine:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4. Inducers decrease exposure; inhibitors (e.g., azole antifungals) increase exposure.
- **QT Prolonging Drugs:** Use rilpivirine with caution when co-administered with drugs known to cause Torsade de Pointes.
- **Specific Interaction Notes:**
    - **Methadone:** R-methadone AUC decreased by 16%; clinical monitoring recommended as maintenance therapy may require adjustment.
    - **Oral Contraceptives:** No clinically relevant effect on ethinyl estradiol or levonorgestrel levels [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Human data are absent. Use only if the benefit justifies the risk. Lower exposures of oral rilpivirine have been observed during pregnancy; monitor viral load closely.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended due to the potential for HIV transmission and unknown risks of drug exposure to the infant. Cabotegravir and rilpivirine may persist in human milk for 12 months or longer after the last injection.
- **Fertility:** No human data. Animal studies showed no effect on male or female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children/adolescents <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required. Data are limited in patients ≥65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. Not studied in severe impairment or end-stage renal disease (CrCl <15 mL/min); increased monitoring is recommended.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild to moderate impairment (Child-Pugh A or B). Not studied in severe impairment (Child-Pugh C).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Cabotegravir:** Inhibits HIV integrase, preventing the strand transfer step of viral DNA integration.
    - **Rilpivirine:** Diarylpyrimidine NNRTI; non-competitively inhibits HIV-1 reverse transcriptase.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Both drugs exhibit absorption-limited ("flip-flop") kinetics.
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is 7 days for cabotegravir and 3–4 days for rilpivirine.
    - **Distribution:** Both are highly protein-bound (>99%). Both are present in the genital tract and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF).
    - **Metabolism:** Cabotegravir (UGT1A1); Rilpivirine (CYP3A4).
    - **Elimination:** Cabotegravir half-life 5.6–11.5 weeks; Rilpivirine half-life 13–28 weeks.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **FLAIR and ATLAS (Monthly Dosing):** Pooled Week 48 analysis showed **CABENUVA** was non-inferior to current antiretroviral therapy (CAR). Virologic failure (HIV-1 RNA ≥50 c/mL) occurred in 1.9% of the **CABENUVA** group vs 1.7% in CAR.
- **ATLAS-2M (Every 2-Month Dosing):** Week 48 analysis confirmed that every 2-month dosing (Q8W) was non-inferior to monthly dosing. Virologic failure occurred in 1.7% (Q8W) vs 1.0% (Monthly).
- **Resistance Factors:** Increased risk of failure was associated with a combination of: rilpivirine resistance mutations at baseline, HIV-1 subtype A6/A1, or BMI ≥30 kg/m² [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (refrigerate).
- **Special Handling:** Do not freeze. Vials may remain at room temperature (up to 25°C) for 6 hours before use. Once drawn into syringes, injections should be administered as soon as possible (discard after 2 hours).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
