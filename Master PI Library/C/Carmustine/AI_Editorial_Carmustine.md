# Editorial Product Information: Carmustine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial is derived from the official Product Information provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Note on Jurisdictional Scope:** While this document follows a harmonised structure intended for multiple sources, only the Australian Product Information was provided for this synthesis. Therefore, regional variations for the US, Europe, Canada, or Japan are not reflected unless they were explicitly mentioned within the Australian source.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis designed to provide healthcare professionals with a coherent overview of the clinical and regulatory profile of carmustine.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult local regulations and current official labeling before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Carmustine (a nitrosourea derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** 1, 3-bis(2-chloroethyl)-1-nitrosourea.
- **Brand Name(s):** **BiCNU** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01AD01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 100 mg powder for injection, supplied with a 3 mL dehydrated alcohol diluent vial.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Emcure Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd; Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**BiCNU** is indicated as palliative therapy, either as monotherapy or in established combination regimens, for the following:
- **Malignant Glioma** [Australian].
- **Multiple Myeloma:** In combination with prednisone [Australian].
- **Hodgkin’s Disease:** As secondary therapy in patients who have relapsed during primary therapy or failed to respond to primary therapy [Australian].
- **Non-Hodgkin’s Lymphomas:** As secondary therapy in patients who have relapsed during primary therapy or failed to respond to primary therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Single Agent (Untreated Patients):** 200 mg/m² intravenously every 6 weeks.
- **Administration Schedule:** May be administered as a single dose or divided into daily injections (e.g., 100 mg/m² on two successive days).
- **Repeat Courses:** Should not be administered until circulating blood elements return to acceptable levels (Platelets >100,000/mm³; Leucocytes >4,000/mm³). This recovery typically occurs within 6 weeks.

### Dosage Adjustments
Subsequent doses must be adjusted based on the haematologic response (nadir) to the preceding dose:

| Leucocytes/mm³ (Nadir) | Platelets/mm³ (Nadir) | Percentage of Prior Dose to be Given |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| >4,000 | >100,000 | 100% |
| 3,000–3,999 | 75,000–99,999 | 100% |
| 2,000–2,999 | 25,000–74,999 | 70% |
| <2,000 | <25,000 | 50% |

- **Cumulative Toxicity:** The total cumulative dose should not exceed 1500 mg/m² due to the high risk of pulmonary toxicity [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Allow the vial and diluent to reach room temperature (15°C to 30°C). Dissolve the 100 mg powder with 3 mL of the supplied absolute ethanol diluent, then aseptically add 27 mL of Sterile Water for Injection. The resulting solution contains 3.3 mg/mL of carmustine in 10% ethanol.
- **Further Dilution:** May be further diluted with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Dextrose for injection.
- **Infusion Rate:** Administer by IV drip over 1 to 2 hours. **BiCNU SHOULD NOT BE GIVEN BY RAPID INTRAVENOUS INJECTION** to avoid intense pain and burning at the site.
- **Equipment:** Use only glass containers for preparation and administration.

## 4. Contraindications
- Previous hypersensitivity to **BiCNU** (carmustine) [Australian].
- Patients with decreased circulating platelets, leucocytes, or erythrocytes due to previous chemotherapy or other causes [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- Not specified in provided Australian source.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** The most common and severe toxic effect. Thrombocytopenia and leucopenia are typically delayed (4–6 weeks post-dose) and cumulative. Complete blood counts must be monitored frequently for at least 6 weeks after each dose.
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** Dose-related risk; significantly higher in patients receiving a cumulative dose >1400 mg/m². Delayed toxicity may occur years after treatment and can be fatal. Baseline and frequent pulmonary function tests (FVC, DLco) are required.
- **Hepatotoxicity & Nephrotoxicity:** It is recommended that liver and renal function tests be monitored periodically.
- **Extravasation:** Monitor the infusion site closely. While a specific treatment for extravasation is unknown, infiltration can cause soft tissue toxicity, swelling, pain, and skin necrosis.
- **Ocular Toxicity:** Associated with investigational administration directly into the carotid artery; this route is not recommended.
- **Handling:** Accidental skin contact may cause transient hyperpigmentation. Wash skin or mucosa immediately with soap and water if contact occurs.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are grouped by frequency: Very common (≥10%), Common (1–10%), Uncommon (0.1–1%), Rare (<0.1%).

### Haematological
- **Very common:** Delayed myelosuppression (Platelet nadir at 4–5 weeks; Leucocyte nadir at 5–6 weeks); Anaemia.
- **Common:** Cumulative myelosuppression.
- **Rare:** Acute leukaemia and bone marrow dysplasias following long-term therapy; Thrombosis.

### Gastrointestinal
- **Very common:** Nausea and vomiting (dose-dependent, starting within 2 hours and lasting 4–6 hours).

### Pulmonary
- **Common:** Pulmonary infiltrates and/or fibrosis (at high cumulative doses >1400 mg/m²).
- **Uncommon:** Pulmonary fibrosis (at low cumulative doses).
- **Frequency Not Specified:** Pneumonitis and interstitial lung disease (post-marketing).

### Hepatic and Renal
- **Uncommon:** Elevated transaminases, alkaline phosphatase, and bilirubin; Decrease in kidney size; Progressive azotaemia; Renal failure.
- **Rare:** Fatal hepatic toxicity (associated with cumulative doses >1200–1500 mg/m²).

### Other Effects
- **Uncommon:** Hypotension, tachycardia, chest pain, allergic reactions, headache, facial flushing, and injection site burning.
- **Rare:** Neuroretinitis; Suffusion of conjunctiva (associated with rapid IV infusion).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Combination Chemotherapy:** Carmustine may require cautious use when combined with other myelosuppressive agents due to additive toxicity [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Thoracic irradiation or other drugs affecting pulmonary function may increase the risk of carmustine-associated pulmonary toxicity [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Carmustine is embryotoxic and teratogenic in animals. Safe use in human pregnancy is not established. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the mother and fetus. Patients should use adequate contraception during treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if carmustine is excreted in human milk. Due to potential harm to the newborn, breastfeeding is not recommended.
- **Fertility:** Carmustine has been shown to affect fertility in male rats at doses higher than the human dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established. A long-term study indicated that children are at high risk for very delayed pulmonary toxicity (up to 15 years post-treatment), with a ~50% mortality rate in reported cases. Children treated under age 5 appear at highest risk [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in the provided source.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Monitoring of function is recommended, but specific dose adjustment scales for eGFR or Child-Pugh scores are not specified in the source.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Carmustine alkylates DNA and RNA. It also inhibits several enzymes via carbamoylation of amino acids in proteins. It is not cross-resistant with other alkylating agents.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly degraded following IV administration; no intact drug is detectable after 15 minutes. It is highly lipid-soluble and effectively crosses the blood-brain barrier (CSF levels are ≥50% of plasma levels).
    - **Metabolism:** Likely mediated through active metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 60–70% of a dose is excreted in the urine within 96 hours; ~10% is excreted as respiratory CO₂.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific pivotal trial data was provided in the source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store under refrigeration between 2°C and 8°C. Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Decomposition Indicator:** Carmustine has a low melting point (30.5°C to 32.0°C). If the drug liquifies or appears as an oil film at the bottom of the vial, it has decomposed and must be discarded.
- **Reconstituted Stability:** Use immediately after preparation.
- **Handling:** Follow guidelines for the safe handling and disposal of cytotoxic agents.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
