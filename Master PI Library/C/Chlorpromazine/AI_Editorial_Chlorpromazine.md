# Editorial Product Information: Chlorpromazine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of chlorpromazine for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, regulatory warnings, and administration guidelines into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. It does not replace the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Chlorpromazine hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Dimethylamine derivative of phenothiazine; neuroleptic/antipsychotic.
- **Brand Names:** **Largactil** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths [Australian]:** 
    - **Oral Tablets:** 25 mg and 100 mg (film-coated).
    - **Oral Syrup:** 5 mg/mL.
    - **Injection Solution:** 50 mg/2 mL (25 mg/mL).
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral, Deep Intramuscular (IM) injection, Intravenous (IV) infusion (after dilution).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Clinect Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Psychiatric Disorders:** Management of acute functional psychoses (including schizophrenia, mania, and psychotic depression) and long-term maintenance in schizophrenia.
- **Mood and Agitation:** Short-term relief of severe depression and psychomotor agitation.
- **Paediatric Behavioural Disturbances:** Treatment of severe behavioral issues in children (e.g., those with autism or intellectual disabilities), specifically targeting aggression, overactivity, or self-injurious behaviors [Australian].
- **Nausea and Vomiting:** Control of emesis, particularly in the context of terminal illness.
- **Adjuvant Therapy:** Enhancement of analgesic effects in terminal care.
- **Hiccough:** Management of intractable cases.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Oral:** Initial therapy for ambulatory patients typically starts at 25 mg three times daily. This may be titrated upwards by 25 mg increments to a total of 600–800 mg daily if required. Maintenance doses generally range from 25–100 mg three times daily.
- **Parenteral:** 25–50 mg via deep IM injection. This dose may be repeated every 6–8 hours (3 to 4 times in 24 hours). 

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Children >5 years:** One-third to one-half of the adult dose.
- **Children <5 years (Oral):** Calculated at 0.5 mg/kg body weight. Typical doses include 5 mg at 1 year, 7.5 mg at 3 years, and 10 mg at 6 years, administered 3 to 4 times daily as required.
- **Note:** Children require close monitoring for hypotension and hypothermia.

### Administration Instructions
- **IM Injection:** Must be administered into the upper outer quadrant of the buttock or the deltoid. Sites should be rotated to avoid local irritation or abscess formation.
- **IV Administration:** Must be diluted with normal saline and administered via infusion.
- **Skin Contact:** Healthcare providers and caregivers should avoid crushing tablets or direct contact with the syrup/injection solution due to the risk of contact dermatitis.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Circulatory Status:** Circulatory collapse.
- **CNS Status:** Central nervous system depression (e.g., coma or acute drug intoxication).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to chlorpromazine, other phenothiazines, or any excipients (e.g., sulfites in syrup/injection).
- **Haematological:** Bone marrow depression.
- **Endocrine:** Phaeochromocytoma.
- **Hepatic:** Active hepatic disease or hepatic failure.
- **Paediatric:** Infants under 1 year of age (associated with Sudden Infant Death Syndrome) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** A potentially fatal condition characterised by muscle rigidity, hyperthermia, autonomic instability, and altered consciousness. Immediate discontinuation is mandatory upon suspicion.
- **Tardive Dyskinesia (TD):** Risk of irreversible, involuntary movements increases with treatment duration and cumulative dose. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest period.
- **QT Prolongation:** Chlorpromazine may increase the QT interval, raising the risk of torsade de pointes. Extreme caution is required in patients with bradycardia or electrolyte imbalances (hypokalaemia).
- **Seizure Threshold:** May lower the threshold for seizures; use with caution in patients with epilepsy.
- **Ocular Effects:** Long-term therapy requires periodic ophthalmological exams to monitor for lens opacities and retinopathy.
- **Photosensitivity:** Patients must avoid direct sunlight and use SPF 15+ protection, as skin reactions are common.
- **Agranulocytosis:** Though rare, periodic blood counts are recommended. Patients should report any signs of infection (fever, sore throat) immediately.
- **Postural Hypotension:** Common during initial parenteral dosing. Monitor vital signs and keep patients supine for the first few days of treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Sedation, somnolence, akathisia.
- **Common (1–10%):** Parkinsonism, dry mouth, constipation, weight gain, blurred vision, nasal congestion.
- **Autonomic/Endocrine:** Hypotension, urinary retention, hyperprolactinaemia (potentially leading to galactorrhoea, amenorrhoea, or gynaecomastia), impaired glucose tolerance/hyperglycaemia.
- **Skin:** Contact dermatitis, photosensitivity, abnormal skin pigmentation (blue-grey hue with chronic high doses).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome.
    - Tardive Dyskinesia.
    - Venous Thromboembolism (including pulmonary embolism).
    - Cholestatic jaundice and severe liver toxicity.
    - Agranulocytosis and hemolytic anaemia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **CNS Depressants:** Enhanced effects of alcohol, barbiturates, opioids, and sedatives.
    - **Dopaminergics:** Antagonises the effects of levodopa and other dopamine agonists.
    - **Antihypertensives:** May potentiate the effects of hypotensive agents but antagonises the effects of guanethidine and clonidine.
    - **QT Prolonging Drugs:** Increased risk of arrhythmias with Class Ia/III antiarrhythmics and certain antidepressants.
    - **Adrenaline:** Paradoxical fall in blood pressure may occur if adrenaline is used to treat phenothiazine-induced hypotension.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP1A2 Inhibitors (e.g., Ciprofloxacin, Fluvoxamine):** May increase chlorpromazine plasma levels.
    - **Antacids:** May reduce oral absorption (stagger doses by 2 hours).
    - **Lithium:** Concurrent use may increase the risk of neurotoxicity.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category D [Australian]**. Use is not recommended unless the clinical benefit outweighs the potential risk.
- **Neonatal Risks:** Exposure in the third trimester may cause extrapyramidal symptoms or withdrawal in the neonate (agitation, tremor, respiratory distress).
- **Labour:** May prolong labour; withhold until the cervix is dilated 3–4 cm.
- **Breastfeeding:** Chlorpromazine is excreted in breast milk. Not recommended for nursing mothers due to potential risks to the infant.
- **Fertility:** May cause hyperprolactinaemia, leading to impaired fertility in females. Animal studies show decreased fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in children under 6 years should be limited to exceptional circumstances in specialised environments. Annual clinical assessment of learning capacity is recommended.
- **Elderly Use:** More susceptible to orthostatic hypotension and sedation. Initial doses should be approximately 50% of the standard adult dose with gradual titration. Increased mortality risk is noted in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Extensive hepatic metabolism necessitates dose reduction and close monitoring for toxicity.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; dose reductions may be necessary.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primarily acts as a dopamine (D2) receptor antagonist in the brain. It also possesses significant alpha-adrenergic blocking activity and strong anticholinergic/antimuscarinic properties.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly but erratically absorbed from the GI tract (10–80% bioavailability due to high first-pass metabolism). Peak plasma levels: 1–4 hours (Oral); 15–30 minutes (IM).
    - **Distribution:** Highly lipophilic and protein-bound (90–99%). Crosses the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Extremely complex; over 150 metabolites postulated. Primary pathways include hydroxylation and N-oxidation.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily in the urine as metabolites; less than 1% is excreted unchanged. Half-life is multiphasic, with a terminal phase up to 60 days.
