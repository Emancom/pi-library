# Editorial Product Information: Sodium Cromoglycate

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Sodium cromoglycate (Chemical class: Mast cell stabiliser)
- **Brand Names:** Intal CFC-Free, Intal Forte CFC-Free [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** R03BC01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Pressurised oral inhalation (Metered Dose Inhaler)
    - **Intal CFC-Free:** 1.0 mg sodium cromoglycate per actuation [Australian]
    - **Intal Forte CFC-Free:** 5.0 mg sodium cromoglycate per actuation [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral inhalation
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prophylactic treatment of bronchial asthma:** Indicated for long-term management and prevention of symptoms [Australian].
- **Prevention of exercise-induced bronchospasm (EIB):** Indicated for use shortly before physical exertion [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children)
Patients exhibit variable responses to sodium cromoglycate; dosages should be individualised based on clinical response [Australian].

### Dosing by Strength [Australian]
- **Intal (1 mg) CFC-Free:** 
    - Two inhalations (2 mg) administered four times daily.
    - Typical regimen: Two inhalations on waking, at 4 to 6-hour intervals, and upon retiring at night.
- **Intal Forte (5 mg) CFC-Free:** 
    - **Initial dose:** Two inhalations (10 mg) twice daily.
    - **Severe cases/high challenge:** May be increased to four inhalations four times daily for optimal control.
- **Exercise-Induced Bronchospasm Prevention:**
    - **Intal (1 mg):** Additional doses may be taken before exercise.
    - **Intal Forte (5 mg):** Two to four inhalations (10–20 mg) taken 5–10 minutes prior to exercise.

### Maintenance and Titration
Sodium cromoglycate is essentially prophylactic. Therapy must be continued regularly in responding patients. If withdrawal is necessary, the dose should be reduced progressively over one week to prevent the recurrence of asthma symptoms [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Priming:** 
    - **Intal (1 mg):** Prime with four actuations before first use; re-prime with four actuations if unused for >2 days.
    - **Intal Forte (5 mg):** Prime with four actuations before first use. Re-priming (2 actuations) is required after 3–7 days of non-use; beyond 7 days, four actuations are required.
- **Spacer Use:** Consistent use of a spacer device is recommended, particularly for patients with poor inhaler technique. This reduces drug deposition in the oropharynx and limits local side effects like hoarseness or irritation.
- **Maintenance:** The mouthpiece must be washed daily to prevent blockage due to the HFA-227/apaflurane propellant.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to sodium cromoglycate or any excipients (povidone, macrogol 600, apaflurane) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Asthma Attacks:** Sodium cromoglycate is not intended for the relief of acute bronchospasm; it is a prophylactic agent [Australian].
- **Paradoxical Bronchospasm:** As with other inhaled therapies, sodium cromoglycate may cause immediate wheezing or coughing. If paradoxical bronchospasm occurs, the drug must be discontinued and alternative therapy initiated [Australian].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Severe anaphylactic reactions have been reported extremely rarely [Australian].
- **Steroid Reduction:** If reducing concomitant corticosteroid treatment, patients must be closely supervised. Stepwise reduction is required, ideally with peak flow monitoring [Australian].
- **Eosinophilic Pneumonia:** Treatment must be discontinued if this condition is diagnosed [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (1–10%)
Based on clinical trial data for Intal Forte [Australian]:
- **Respiratory:** Increased cough (2.1–4.8%), pharyngitis (1.3–4.8%), transient bronchospasm (3.3% in paediatric patients).
- **Other:** Bad taste (0.3–6.5%).

### Frequency Not Specified/Rare
- **General:** Fever, nausea, emotional lability, abdominal pain (primarily reported in paediatric populations).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Angioedema, hypotension, and collapse (extremely rare).
- **Post-marketing (causal association not established):** Headache, diarrhoea, arthralgia, myalgia, urticaria, and skin rashes.
- **Serious:** Eosinophilic pneumonia (very rare).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No specific drug-drug interaction studies have been performed for CFC-free formulations. However, no evidence of interaction with other medications has been observed in clinical practice [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B1. Large-scale human data have not shown an increased frequency of malformations or harmful effects on the foetus. Use is recommended only if the maternal benefit outweighs the potential risk to the foetus, particularly during the first trimester [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Sodium cromoglycate is excreted into human breast milk. Safety in infants has not been established; use is not recommended for nursing mothers unless the expected benefit outweighs potential risks [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (subcutaneous administration in rats) showed no evidence of impaired fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for use in children; dosing follows adult recommendations for Intal (1 mg) and is individualised for Intal Forte (5 mg). Paediatric patients showed higher incidences of bad taste and cough in clinical trials [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data regarding use in the elderly are not specified in available sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data available in the provided source document.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Sodium cromoglycate inhibits the release of inflammatory mediators (e.g., histamine) from sensitised mast cells. It prevents both immediate and late-phase asthmatic responses to immunological or environmental stimuli. It lacks intrinsic bronchodilator or antihistamine activity [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 10% of an inhaled dose is absorbed via the lungs; approximately 1% is absorbed via the gastrointestinal tract.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is approximately 65%.
    - **Metabolism:** The drug is not metabolised in humans.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted unchanged in the urine and bile in roughly equal proportions. Elimination half-life is 1.5 to 2 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Exercise Challenge Study:** A study of 16 paediatric and adult patients demonstrated that Intal Forte CFC-Free provided statistically significant protection against exercise-induced bronchospasm compared to placebo, showing equivalence to older CFC-containing formulations [Australian].
- **Safety Transition Study:** A 4-week study of 184 asthmatic children found that switching from CFC-containing formulations to CFC-free formulations maintained stable asthma control with no new safety concerns [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C in a dry place [Australian].
- Protect from direct sunlight [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Consult the expiry date on packaging; specific duration is not specified in the primary PI document.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
