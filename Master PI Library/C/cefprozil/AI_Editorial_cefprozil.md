# Editorial Product Information: Cefprozil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised clinical overview of cefprozil for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines mandated in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States PI was available for review; additional regional nuances (e.g., Australian, European, or Canadian) should be verified through local pharmacopoeias.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefprozil (as monohydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Semi-synthetic, broad-spectrum, second-generation cephalosporin antibiotic.
- **Brand Names:** Cefprozil [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01DC10.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Powder for Oral Suspension [US]:** 125 mg/5 mL and 250 mg/5 mL (when reconstituted).
    - **Note:** The US source indicates the powder is light pink and granular with a bubble-gum flavour.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** NorthStar Rx LLC [US]; Aurobindo Pharma Limited (Manufacturer) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Cefprozil is indicated for the treatment of mild to moderate infections caused by susceptible strains of designated microorganisms in the following conditions:

*   **Upper Respiratory Tract Infections:**
    - Pharyngitis and tonsillitis (*Streptococcus pyogenes*).
    - Otitis Media (*Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, Moraxella catarrhalis*).
    - Acute Sinusitis (*S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis*).
*   **Lower Respiratory Tract Infections:**
    - Acute bacterial exacerbation of chronic bronchitis (*S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis*).
*   **Skin and Skin Structure Infections:**
    - Uncomplicated infections of the skin and skin structures (*Staphylococcus aureus, S. pyogenes*). Note: Abscesses usually require surgical drainage.

## 3. Dosage and Administration

### General Dosing (Adults and Children >13 years) [US]
| Indication | Dosage | Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pharyngitis / Tonsillitis** | 500 mg every 24 hours | 10 days |
| **Acute Sinusitis** | 250 mg or 500 mg every 12 hours | 10 days |
| **Acute Exacerbation of Chronic Bronchitis** | 500 mg every 12 hours | 10 days |
| **Uncomplicated Skin/Skin Structure** | 250 mg q12h OR 500 mg q24h/q12h | 10 days |

### Paediatric Dosing (6 months to 12 years) [US]
| Indication | Dosage | Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Otitis Media** | 15 mg/kg every 12 hours | 10 days |
| **Pharyngitis / Tonsillitis (2–12 years)** | 7.5 mg/kg every 12 hours | 10 days |
| **Acute Sinusitis** | 7.5 mg/kg or 15 mg/kg every 12 hours | 10 days |
| **Uncomplicated Skin/Skin Structure (2–12 years)** | 20 mg/kg every 24 hours | 10 days |

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Add water in two portions and shake well after each. 
- **Food Effect:** May be administered with or without food. Food does not affect the extent of absorption (AUC) but may delay the time to maximum plasma concentration (Tmax) by 0.25 to 0.75 hours [US].
- **S. pyogenes Infections:** Treatment must continue for at least 10 days.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to cefprozil or any member of the cephalosporin class of antibiotics.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Before initiation, investigate history of reactions to cephalosporins, penicillins, or other drugs. Cross-sensitivity among $\beta$-lactam antibiotics occurs in up to 10% of penicillin-allergic patients. If an allergic reaction occurs, discontinue immediately.
- **Clostridioides difficile-associated Diarrhoea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents, ranging from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution in patients with known or suspected renal impairment. Dosage reduction is required for CrCl <30 mL/min.
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms (e.g., fungi).
- **History of GI Disease:** Prescribe with caution in patients with a history of colitis.
- **Phenylketonurics:** The oral suspension contains phenylalanine (8.4 mg per 5 mL) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Cefprozil is generally well-tolerated; approximately 2% of patients discontinue therapy due to adverse events.

| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Common (1–10%) | Diarrhoea (2.9%), Nausea (3.5%), Vomiting (1%), Abdominal pain (1%) |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Common (1–10%) | Elevated AST/ALT (2%) |
| | Rare (<0.1%) | Cholestatic jaundice |
| **Hypersensitivity** | Uncommon (0.1–1%) | Rash (0.9%), Urticaria (0.1%); higher frequency in children |
| **CNS** | Common (1%) | Dizziness |
| | Rare (<1%) | Hyperactivity, headache, nervousness, insomnia, confusion |
| **Hematopoietic** | Common (1–10%) | Eosinophilia (2.3%) |
| | Uncommon (0.1–1%) | Decreased leukocyte count (0.2%) |
| **Renal** | Uncommon (0.1–1%) | Elevated BUN (0.1%), serum creatinine (0.1%) |
| **Other** | Common (1–10%) | Diaper rash/superinfection (1.5%), Genital pruritus/vaginitis (1.6%) |

**Serious Post-marketing Events:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, erythema multiforme, serum-sickness-like reactions, and thrombocytopenia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** Concomitant administration doubles the AUC of cefprozil.
- **Aminoglycosides:** Increased risk of nephrotoxicity when administered with cephalosporins.
- **Diuretics:** Potent diuretics may adversely affect renal function, potentially increasing cefprozil levels.
- **Laboratory Test Interactions:**
    - **Coombs’ Test:** Positive direct Coombs’ tests have been reported.
    - **Urinary Glucose:** May produce false-positive reactions using copper reduction tests (e.g., Benedict’s, Fehling’s, Clinitest); enzyme-based tests (e.g., Clinistix) are unaffected.
    - **Blood Glucose:** False-negative reactions may occur in the ferricyanide test.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category B.** Reproduction studies in animals (up to 18.5 times human dose) showed no harm to the fetus. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women; use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Cefprozil is excreted in human milk in small amounts (<0.3% of a 1 g dose). Exercise caution when administering to nursing women.
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in rat studies at doses up to 18.5 times the maximum human dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety/efficacy established for children 6 months to 12 years (Otitis Media/Sinusitis) and 2 to 12 years (Pharyngitis/Skin infections). Accumulation of other cephalosporins has been reported in neonates due to prolonged half-life.
- **Elderly Use:** Geriatric patients (≥65 years) show higher AUC (35–60%) and lower renal clearance. While efficacy and safety remain comparable to younger adults, caution is advised due to the higher prevalence of renal impairment in this population.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **CrCl 30–120 mL/min:** Standard dose.
    - **CrCl 0–29 mL/min:** 50% of the standard dose [US].
    - **Hemodialysis:** Cefprozil is partially removed by dialysis; dose should be administered *after* completion of the session.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** The half-life increases to approximately 2 hours, but this does not necessitate dosage adjustment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell-wall synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 95% bioavailable. Peak plasma levels achieved within 1.5 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is approximately 36%. Minimal CSF penetration.
    - **Metabolism:** Not significantly metabolised.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (60% recovered unchanged in urine). Average plasma half-life is 1.3 hours in healthy adults.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Acute Otitis Media (US Study):** Cefprozil was compared to a $\beta$-lactamase inhibitor-containing agent. Cefprozil showed a 5% higher success rate against *S. pneumoniae* but was 17% less effective against *H. influenzae* and 12% less effective against *M. catarrhalis* compared to the control drug.
- **Acute Otitis Media (European Study):** In a population with lower rates of $\beta$-lactamase-producing organisms, cefprozil efficacy was equivalent to the control agent for all major pathogens (*S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis*).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Dry Powder:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Reconstituted Suspension:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). 
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the reconstituted suspension after 14 days.
