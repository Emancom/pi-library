It encompasses information for both the prescription biological medicine (Ilaris) and registered homeopathic formulations containing canakinumab.

# Product Information: Canakinumab

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
*   **Active Ingredient:** Canakinumab
*   **Formulations:** 
    *   **Prescription Biological:** A high-affinity human monoclonal anti-human-IL-1β antibody of the IgG1/κ isotype. Available as a 150 mg/mL solution for injection or a 150 mg lyophilized powder for reconstitution [TGA].
    *   **Homeopathic:** A 4C dilution (homeopathic) presented in an oral solution/drops [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
Canakinumab is indicated for the treatment of various inflammatory conditions based on formulation and regulatory approval:

**Autoinflammatory Periodic Fever Syndromes (Adults and Children ≥2 years):**
*   **Cryopyrin-Associated Periodic Syndromes (CAPS):** Including Familial Cold Autoinflammatory Syndrome (FCAS), Muckle-Wells Syndrome (MWS), and Neonatal-Onset Multisystem Inflammatory Disease (NOMID).
*   **Tumor Necrosis Factor Receptor Associated Periodic Syndrome (TRAPS).**
*   **Hyperimmunoglobulin D Syndrome (HIDS) / Mevalonate Kinase Deficiency (MKD).**
*   **Familial Mediterranean Fever (FMF):** To be used in conjunction with colchicine where appropriate [TGA].

**Systemic Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (sJIA):**
*   Treatment of active sJIA in patients aged 2 years and older [TGA].

**Sleep and Relaxation (Homeopathic Formulation):**
*   Temporary relief of difficulty falling asleep [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   Confirmed hypersensitivity to the active substance (canakinumab) or any of the listed excipients (e.g., sucrose, L-histidine, polysorbate 80, or mannitol) [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
**Serious Infections:** Canakinumab is associated with an increased risk of serious infections. Treatment should not be initiated or continued in patients with an active infection requiring medical intervention. Exercise extreme caution in patients with a history of recurring infections or underlying conditions predisposing them to infection [TGA].

**Tuberculosis (TB):** Reactivation of TB has been reported with IL-1 inhibitors. Patients must be screened for latent and active TB infection prior to starting therapy and monitored closely throughout treatment [TGA].

**Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS):** Rarely reported, primarily in sJIA patients. DRESS can be fatal; if symptoms appear and no other cause is identified, canakinumab should be discontinued permanently [TGA].

**Macrophage Activation Syndrome (MAS):** A life-threatening disorder known to develop in sJIA patients. While canakinumab does not appear to increase the incidence, physicians should remain vigilant for triggers such as infection or worsening of the underlying condition [TGA].

**Malignancies:** The risk of developing malignancies with anti-IL-1 therapy is currently unknown; however, a potential risk cannot be excluded [TGA].

**Neutropenia and Leukopenia:** Neutrophil counts should be assessed prior to treatment and monitored periodically. Treatment should not be initiated in patients with existing neutropenia or leukopenia [TGA].

**General (OTC/Homeopathic):** If sleep symptoms persist for more than 5 days, patients should consult a physician [FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Treatment with the prescription biological should be initiated and supervised by a specialist physician.

**Prescription Biological (Subcutaneous Injection):**
*   **CAPS (Adults and Children ≥4 years):** 150 mg for patients >40 kg; 2 mg/kg for patients 15–40 kg; 4 mg/kg for patients 7.5–15 kg. Administered every 8 weeks.
*   **TRAPS, HIDS/MKD, FMF:** 150 mg for patients >40 kg; 2 mg/kg for patients ≥7.5 kg and ≤40 kg. Administered every 4 weeks. Dose may be up-titrated if response is inadequate [TGA].
*   **sJIA:** 4 mg/kg (max 300 mg) every 4 weeks for patients ≥7.5 kg [TGA].

**Homeopathic (Oral Drops):**
*   **Adults and Children ≥12 years:** 20 drops in water, twice daily.
*   **Children 6–12 years:** 10 drops in water, twice daily.
*   **Children under 6 years:** Consult a physician. Administer 15 minutes before meals [FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
**Prescription Formulation:**
*   **Very Common (≥10%):** Nasopharyngitis, upper respiratory tract infections, urinary tract infections, injection site reactions, and abdominal pain (sJIA) [TGA].
*   **Common (1%–10%):** Dizziness, vertigo, and decreased neutrophil counts [TGA].
*   **Uncommon (<1%):** Decreased platelet counts without associated bleeding [TGA].

**Homeopathic Formulation:**
*   Contains 30% ethyl alcohol as an inactive ingredient [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **TNF Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with TNF inhibitors is not recommended due to a significantly increased risk of serious infections [TGA].
*   **CYP450 Substrates:** IL-1 inhibition may normalize CYP450 enzyme expression. Monitor patients taking drugs with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., warfarin) when starting canakinumab, as dose adjustments may be necessary [TGA].
*   **Vaccinations:** Live vaccines should **not** be administered concurrently with canakinumab. It is recommended that all immunizations be brought up to date before starting therapy [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
**Pregnancy:**
*   **Category B3:** There are no adequate or well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies (marmosets) showed some evidence of skeletal variations, but no teratogenicity. Canakinumab should only be used in pregnancy if the potential benefit outweighs the risk [TGA].
*   **Infant Exposure:** Infants exposed to canakinumab *in utero* should not receive live vaccines for 16 weeks following the mother’s last dose before childbirth [TGA].

**Breastfeeding:**
*   It is unknown if canakinumab is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during therapy [TGA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Peak serum concentration (Cmax) occurs approximately 7 days after subcutaneous injection. Absolute bioavailability is estimated at 66% [TGA].
*   **Distribution:** Canakinumab binds to serum IL-1β. Volume of distribution varies by body weight (e.g., 6.2 L for a 70 kg adult) [TGA].
*   **Elimination:** The mean terminal half-life is approximately 26 days. Clearance is weight-dependent, with no significant differences observed between genders or age groups after correcting for weight [TGA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Canakinumab is a human monoclonal antibody that binds specifically and with high affinity to Interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β). By blocking the interaction of IL-1β with its receptors, canakinumab neutralizes its biological activity, thereby preventing the activation of inflammatory genes and the production of mediators like IL-6. This mechanism is essential for treating diseases driven by the overproduction of IL-1β [TGA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Injection Formulations:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Keep the vial in the original carton to protect from light [TGA].
*   **Homeopathic Drops:** Keep out of reach of children. Contains 30% ethyl alcohol [FDA].
