# Editorial Product Information: ceftolozane + tazobactam

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **ceftolozane + tazobactam**
- Chemical class: Not specified in available sources
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous infusion only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of complicated intra‑abdominal infections (cIAI) in adults and paediatric patients (birth to <18 years) when caused by designated susceptible microorganisms — to be used in conjunction with metronidazole.
- Treatment of complicated urinary tract infections (cUTI), including pyelonephritis, in adults and paediatric patients (birth to <18 years) when caused by designated susceptible microorganisms.
- Treatment of nosocomial pneumonia, including ventilator‑associated pneumonia (VAP), in adults (≥18 years) when caused by designated susceptible microorganisms.
- Paediatric use for nosocomial pneumonia (including VAP): safety and efficacy not established / insufficient information — not recommended.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General
- Intended for intravenous infusion only. Recommended infusion time is 1 hour for standard adult doses.
- Each vial is single‑use; the product contains no bacteriostatic preservative — aseptic technique required.
- Reconstituted solution is not for direct intravenous injection and must be diluted prior to administration.
- Do not mix with other medicinal products except where explicitly permitted (see Preparation). Do not infuse simultaneously with other medications via the same IV line.

### Preparation and reconstitution
- Reconstitute each vial with 10 mL sterile water for injection or 0.9% sodium chloride; final volume ≈ 11.4 mL (approx. 132 mg/mL).
- Withdraw required volume from reconstituted vial(s) and add to an infusion bag containing 100 mL 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose for infusion.
- Visual inspection: solutions range from clear, colourless to clear and slightly yellow. Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before use.

### Common prepared dose volumes (examples)
- 1000 mg ceftolozane / 500 mg tazobactam: use entire contents of one reconstituted vial (≈11.4 mL) then dilute to infusion volume.
- 2000 mg / 1000 mg: use two vials (entire contents of two vials).
- Smaller doses: withdraw proportionate volumes from vial(s) as required (refer to product reconstitution instructions).

### Adult dosing (≥18 years) with estimated CrCL >50 mL/min
- Complicated intra‑abdominal infections (with metronidazole 500 mg IV q8h): 1000 mg / 500 mg every 8 hours, infusion over 1 hour. Duration: 4–14 days.
- Complicated urinary tract infections, including pyelonephritis: 1000 mg / 500 mg every 8 hours, infusion over 1 hour. Duration: 7 days.
- Nosocomial pneumonia, including VAP: 2000 mg / 1000 mg every 8 hours, infusion over 1 hour. Duration: 8–14 days.

### Paediatric dosing (birth to <18 years) for cIAI and cUTI
- Dose: 30 mg/kg (ceftolozane + tazobactam combined) up to a maximum of 1.5 g per dose, administered every 8 hours by 1‑hour IV infusion.
- Duration: cIAI — 5–14 days; cUTI — 7–14 days.
- Age/weight notes:
  - Paediatric patients weighing >50 kg should not exceed the 1.5 g dose.
  - Defined eligible neonates as >32 weeks gestational age and ≥7 days postnatal.
  - Use only in paediatric patients with estimated eGFR >50 mL/min/1.73 m2 (age‑appropriate eGFR equation). Not recommended in paediatric patients with eGFR ≤50 mL/min/1.73 m2.
- Nosocomial pneumonia including VAP in paediatric patients: insufficient information; safety and efficacy not established.

### Renal impairment — adult dosage adjustments (CrCL estimated by Cockcroft‑Gault)
- CrCL >50 mL/min: no adjustment.
- CrCL 30–50 mL/min:
  - cIAI and cUTI: 500 mg / 250 mg IV every 8 hours.
  - Nosocomial pneumonia (including VAP): 1000 mg / 500 mg IV every 8 hours.
- CrCL 15–29 mL/min:
  - cIAI and cUTI: 250 mg / 125 mg IV every 8 hours.
  - Nosocomial pneumonia (including VAP): 500 mg / 250 mg IV every 8 hours.
- End‑stage renal disease (ESRD) on haemodialysis:
  - cIAI and cUTI: single loading dose 500 mg / 250 mg, followed 8 hours later by maintenance 100 mg / 50 mg every 8 hours for remainder of treatment. On haemodialysis days administer at earliest possible time after completion of dialysis.
  - Nosocomial pneumonia (including VAP): single loading dose 1500 mg / 750 mg, followed 8 hours later by maintenance 300 mg / 150 mg every 8 hours for remainder of treatment. On haemodialysis days administer at earliest possible time after completion of dialysis.
  - Note: ESRD dosing for nosocomial pneumonia/VAP is based on population PK modelling.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dose adjustment necessary.

### Elderly
- No dose adjustment based on age alone; however, elderly patients are more likely to have decreased renal function — assess and adjust dosing based on renal function.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **ceftolozane + tazobactam** (active substances) or to any excipient listed in the full product information.
- Known serious hypersensitivity to cephalosporins or other beta‑lactam antibacterial agents.
- History of serious hypersensitivity to piperacillin/tazobactam or members of the cephalosporin class — contraindicated in such patients.
- Any other contraindication: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
- No boxed/black‑box warnings specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis: Serious and sometimes fatal hypersensitivity reactions (including anaphylaxis) have been reported. Patients with prior hypersensitivity to beta‑lactams (penicillins, cephalosporins) may be at increased risk. Discontinue therapy immediately if a severe allergic reaction occurs and institute appropriate therapy.
- Neurotoxicity: Cephalosporin‑class neurotoxicity has been reported (encephalopathy, seizures, myoclonus). Risk factors include advanced age, renal impairment, pre‑existing CNS disorders, and IV administration. Consider withdrawal if neurotoxicity occurs.
- Renal function effects: Declines in renal function have been observed. Because the drugs (and tazobactam metabolite M1) are renally eliminated, adjust dosing for renal impairment and monitor renal function frequently; modify dose as renal function changes.
- Haemodialysis removal: In subjects on haemodialysis approximately two‑thirds of the administered dose is removed by dialysis; dose timing recommendations provided above.
- Clostridioides difficile–associated diarrhoea: Antibacterial‑associated colitis including pseudomembranous colitis has been reported. Consider this diagnosis in patients who develop diarrhoea during or after therapy; discontinue therapy if severe C. difficile infection is suspected and manage appropriately.
- Severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCAR): SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported with beta‑lactam antibiotics. If SCAR is suspected, discontinue therapy immediately.
- Immunocompromised patients: Limited clinical experience in severely immunocompromised patients, those receiving immunosuppressive therapy, patients with severe neutropenia, and patients with ESRD on haemodialysis.
- Direct Coombs test: Development of positive direct Coombs tests has been observed. There was no evidence of haemolysis in patients who seroconverted in clinical studies, but monitor if clinically indicated.
- Paediatric considerations: Not recommended for paediatric patients with eGFR ≤50 mL/min/1.73 m2. Safety and efficacy for nosocomial pneumonia/VAP in paediatric patients have not been established.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic assessment of renal function (serum creatinine, urine output) during therapy.
- Observe for signs of hypersensitivity and severe cutaneous reactions; if suspected discontinue immediately.
- Monitor for neurotoxic signs (confusion, myoclonus, seizures).
- Monitor for diarrhoea and evaluate for C. difficile if clinically indicated.
- Consider periodic haematologic testing if clinically warranted (direct Coombs test seroconversion reported).

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Cytochrome P450 interactions: No significant interactions anticipated with substrates, inhibitors, or inducers of CYP enzymes based on available in vitro and in vivo data.
- Renal transporter interactions:
  - Co‑administration with OAT1/OAT3 substrate furosemide did not increase furosemide plasma concentrations in studies.
  - Drugs that inhibit OAT1 or OAT3 (examples given: probenecid, diclofenac, cimetidine) may increase tazobactam plasma concentrations.
- Other transporter interactions: No other clinically significant transporter interactions anticipated based on available data.
- Compatibility:
  - Do not mix with other medicinal products for infusion except those explicitly permitted in preparation instructions.
  - Avoid simultaneous infusion via the same IV line with other medications.
- Clinically significant interactions with management notes:
  - If co‑administration with OAT1/OAT3 inhibitors is necessary, monitor for potential increased tazobactam exposure and associated effects (specific management not provided in available sources).
- Region‑specific interactions: Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category: [Australian] Category B1.
- Human data: No adequate or well‑controlled studies in pregnant women available.
- Animal data:
  - Ceftolozane: embryo‑fetal development studies in mice and rats up to high doses showed no evidence of fetal harm; a pre‑ and postnatal rat study reported decreased auditory startle response in offspring at maternal doses ≥300 mg/kg/day. Ceftolozane had no adverse effect on fertility in rats at IV doses up to 1000 mg/kg/day.
  - Tazobactam: in rats, high‑dose IV administration produced maternal toxicity but no fetal toxicity up to doses studied; tazobactam crosses the placenta with fetal concentrations ≤10% of maternal plasma. Pre‑ and postnatal rat studies reported decreased maternal food consumption, reduced pup body weights at high doses, and increased stillbirths at very high doses in one study.
- Use in pregnancy: should be used during pregnancy only if potential benefit justifies potential risk to the fetus.

### Breastfeeding
- Specific data on excretion into human milk and effects on the breastfed infant: Not specified in available sources.
- Animal lactation data: pre‑ and postnatal studies described above for tazobactam/ceftolozane show effects at high doses in rats.
- Recommendation: Not specified in available sources.

### Fertility
- Ceftolozane: no adverse effect on fertility in male or female rats at studied doses.
- Tazobactam: in rat fertility studies, no effect on fertility parameters at doses ≤640 mg/kg/day (approx. 2× highest human dose by body surface area comparison).
- Human fertility data: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- cIAI and cUTI (including pyelonephritis): Approved for paediatric patients from birth to <18 years (defined as neonates >32 weeks gestational age and ≥7 days postnatal) with eGFR >50 mL/min/1.73 m2; dosing 30 mg/kg (up to 1.5 g) q8h by 1‑hour infusion. Duration: cIAI 5–14 days; cUTI 7–14 days.
- Paediatric patients with eGFR ≤50 mL/min/1.73 m2: dosing not established; use not recommended.
- Nosocomial pneumonia (including VAP) in paediatric patients: safety and efficacy not established.

### Elderly
- No dose adjustment based on age alone. Monitor renal function because of increased prevalence of renal impairment in elderly patients and renal elimination of the active substances.

### Renal impairment
- Dose adjustments required as specified in Section 4 (Dosage and Administration). Monitor renal function and adjust dosing based on CrCL (Cockcroft‑Gault for adults) or age‑appropriate eGFR for paediatrics.

### Hepatic impairment
- No dose adjustment required.

### Other (gender, ethnicity)
- No dose adjustments recommended based on gender or ethnicity.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Trial populations and key observations:
  - Adult cIAI and cUTI clinical trials: efficacy of ceftolozane + tazobactam was lower in patients with moderate renal impairment at baseline compared with patients with normal or mildly impaired renal function.
  - Nosocomial pneumonia trial: a markedly higher incidence of seroconversion to a positive direct Coombs test was observed in ceftolozane + tazobactam recipients (31.2%) versus comparator (3.6%) though no haemolysis was evident.
  - Clinical trial exclusions: immunocompromised patients, patients with severe neutropenia, and patients with ESRD on haemodialysis were generally excluded from Phase 3 trials.
