# Editorial Product Information: Cariprazine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of cariprazine Product Information (PI) for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and dosing strategies from the provided source.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cariprazine (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Atypical antipsychotic; dopamine $D_3/D_2$ receptor partial agonist.
- **Brand Names:** **REAGILA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05AX15.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Hard capsules: 1.5 mg, 3 mg, 4.5 mg, and 6 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Atnahs Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia:** Treatment of schizophrenia in adult patients [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended starting dose is 1.5 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** Dosage may be increased in 1.5 mg increments to a maximum recommended dose of 6 mg/day based on clinical efficacy and tolerability [Australian].
- **Administration:** Capsules should be taken at the same time each day, with or without food. 
- **Monitoring Note:** Due to the exceptionally long half-life of cariprazine and its active metabolites, changes in plasma concentration following dose adjustments will not be fully evident for several weeks. Patients must be monitored for delayed adverse reactions and treatment response following any dosage change.
- **Switching Regimens:**
    - **To Cariprazine:** Consider gradual cross-titration from the previous antipsychotic.
    - **From Cariprazine:** No gradual cross-titration is required when switching to another agent; initiate the new antipsychotic at its lowest dose while discontinuing cariprazine [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Take as soon as possible, unless it is nearly time for the next scheduled dose. Do not double the dose.
- **Discontinuation:** Treatment can be stopped immediately, though the drug remains in the body for 3 to 4 weeks after the final dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to cariprazine or any excipients [Australian].
- **Drug Interactions:** Concomitant administration of strong or moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin) or strong or moderate CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine) is strictly contraindicated [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available source documents.
- **Suicidality:** Close supervision of high-risk patients is required, particularly early in treatment, as the risk of suicidal ideation and behaviour is inherent in psychotic illnesses.
- **Akathisia and Restlessness:** Very common early in treatment. Management includes slow up-titration, dose reduction, or use of anti-extrapyramidal symptom (EPS) medication [Australian].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Discontinuation should be considered if signs of potentially irreversible involuntary movements appear.
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal; requires immediate discontinuation and intensive symptomatic treatment.
- **Elderly Patients with Dementia:** Not recommended for use in this population due to an increased risk of cerebrovascular accidents (CVA) and overall mortality [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Disorders:** 
    - Use with caution in patients with known cardiovascular disease. 
    - Monitor blood pressure for orthostatic hypotension or hypertension.
    - Monitor for QT prolongation, especially in patients with pre-existing cardiac conditions or those taking other QT-prolonging medications.
- **Ocular Effects:** Lens opacity and cataracts were observed in preclinical dog studies. While a human causal relationship is not established, patients should seek ophthalmological examination if visual symptoms develop [Australian].
- **Metabolic Changes:** Monitor weight, fasting blood glucose (for hyperglycaemia/diabetes), and lipid profiles regularly.
- **Seizures:** Use cautiously in patients with a history of seizures or conditions lowering the seizure threshold.
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Identify risk factors before and during treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often delayed due to the long half-life of cariprazine.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Akathisia (19%), Parkinsonism (17.5%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dyslipidaemia, weight increase, sleep disorders, anxiety, sedation, dizziness, dystonia, vision blurred, tachyarrhythmia, hypertension, nausea, constipation, vomiting, increased liver enzymes, fatigue. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anaemia, hypothyroidism, blood sodium abnormal, suicidal behaviour, tardive dyskinesia, lethargy, intraocular pressure increased, cardiac conduction disorders, hypotension, hiccups, gastrooesophageal reflux disease, pruritus, rash, dysuria. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Neutropenia, hypersensitivity, cataract, seizures, rhabdomyolysis, dysphagia. |
| **Not Known** | Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic hepatitis, neonatal drug withdrawal syndrome [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** NMS, Tardive Dyskinesia, Rhabdomyolysis, and Stevens-Johnson Syndrome.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Metabolism:** This is the primary metabolic pathway. 
    - **Inhibitors:** Strong/moderate inhibitors are contraindicated as they significantly increase cariprazine exposure. Avoid grapefruit juice [Australian].
    - **Inducers:** Strong/moderate inducers are contraindicated as they significantly decrease exposure.
- **CYP2D6:** Plays a minor role; inhibitors are unlikely to have a clinically relevant effect.
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** Cariprazine may inhibit P-gp in the intestine. Monitor P-gp substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran).
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Use caution with other centrally acting medicinal products and alcohol.
- **Contraceptives:** Cariprazine (6 mg) does not appear to affect the pharmacokinetics of oral ethinylestradiol and levonorgestrel [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Cariprazine is not recommended during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception. 
    - **Contraception:** Must be maintained for at least 10 weeks after the last dose due to the slow elimination of active moieties [Australian].
    - **Neonatal Risk:** Exposure in the third trimester carries a risk of extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms.
- **Breastfeeding:** Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment. It is unknown if cariprazine is excreted in human milk, but a risk to the infant cannot be excluded.
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed reduced fertility in female rats; human effects are not evaluated [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents (<18 years) have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Data for patients $\ge$ 65 years are limited; use caution and consider more conservative dosing. Not recommended for patients with dementia.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate (CrCl $\ge$ 30 mL/min):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (CrCl < 30 mL/min):** Not recommended due to lack of data [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended due to lack of data [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Partial agonist at $D_3$ and $D_2$ receptors (with 2-8 fold higher affinity for $D_3$) and 5-$HT_{1A}$ receptors. Antagonist at 5-$HT_{2B}$, 5-$HT_{2A}$, and $H_1$ receptors.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed; peak plasma concentrations occur within 3–8 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Highly plasma protein bound (92–97%). Extensive distribution (V/F ~916 L).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 to active metabolites: desmethyl cariprazine (DCAR) and didesmethyl cariprazine (DDCAR). Both metabolites have pharmacological activity similar to the parent drug.
    - **Elimination:** Effective half-life is ~2 days for cariprazine and DCAR, and ~8 days for DDCAR. Total cariprazine concentrations decline by 50% one week after discontinuation [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Primarily via hepatic metabolism; ~21% excreted in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Acute Schizophrenia:** Three 6-week studies demonstrated statistically significant improvements in PANSS total scores compared to placebo across doses of 1.5–9 mg/day.
- **Maintenance of Effect:** A long-term withdrawal study showed significantly longer time to relapse for cariprazine (3–9 mg) compared to placebo (Hazard Ratio 0.45) [Australian].
- **Negative Symptoms:** A 26-week study in patients with predominant negative symptoms found cariprazine (3–6 mg) significantly more effective than risperidone in improving the PANSS-FSNS score and social performance [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Conditions:** Keep in the original blister and outer carton to protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** 
    - **Australian:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **Legal Status:** Restricted to prescription use only.
