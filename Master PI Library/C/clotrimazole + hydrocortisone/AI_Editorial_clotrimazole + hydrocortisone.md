# Editorial Product Information: clotrimazole + hydrocortisone

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **clotrimazole + hydrocortisone**
- Chemical class:
  - Clotrimazole: imidazole antifungal
  - Hydrocortisone: topical corticosteroid (glucocorticoid)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Topical (dermal)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval: 04 June 2015 [Australian]
- Date of last revision/update of source PI: 26 October 2021 [Australian]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Additional regulatory dates for other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated for dermatophyte and yeast infections of the skin when inflammation is prominent. Examples include:
  - Fungal-infected dermatitis
  - Intertrigo
  - Candida nappy (diaper) rash
- Indication limitations or regional differences: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Apply a small amount to the affected area(s) twice daily.

### Administration instructions
- Apply thinly and evenly with gentle rubbing to the affected area(s).
- Use only until inflammation, itching and redness have subsided and for no longer than 7 days unless directed by a physician.
- After resolution of inflammatory signs, continue an antifungal-only topical preparation (for example, clotrimazole alone) for an additional period (source guidance: 14 days after symptoms disappear) to reduce the risk of recurrence.

### Dosing by indication
- No separate dosing schedules by specific indication are provided beyond the general instructions above.

### Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to clotrimazole, hydrocortisone, or any excipient (note: cetostearyl alcohol and chlorocresol are present as excipients; cetostearyl alcohol may cause local skin reactions).
- Application to broken skin.
- Presence of certain skin diseases (examples listed in source): acne, rosacea, perioral dermatitis, lues (syphilis), cutaneous tuberculosis.
- Application in or near the eyes.
- Viral skin infections (e.g. herpes simplex, varicella/zoster (chicken pox)).
- Dermal vaccination reactions.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- For external use only. Avoid contact with eyes. Do not swallow.
- Keep out of reach of children.
- Because of the corticosteroid component:
  - Do not apply to large areas (>10% of total body surface area) and avoid use under occlusive dressings (e.g. nappies, bandages) because occlusion and large surface area increase systemic absorption.
  - Long-term corticosteroid use may increase the risk of hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, particularly when used under occlusion.
  - Use for longer than 4 weeks can cause skin atrophy and striae.
  - Prolonged use on flexures and in intertriginous areas is undesirable.
- Cetostearyl alcohol (excipient) may cause local skin reactions (e.g. contact dermatitis).
- Latex interaction: topical application to the genital area may reduce the effectiveness and safety of latex-containing contraceptive devices (condoms, diaphragms). The effect is temporary and typically limited to the period of treatment. Discontinue use if associated infection does not respond to therapy until infection is controlled.
- If an associated infection develops during use that does not respond, discontinue treatment and reassess.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for local skin adverse effects (atrophy, striae, telangiectasia, skin discolouration, secondary infection).
- Monitor for signs of systemic corticosteroid effects (evidence of HPA suppression) with extensive, prolonged, or occluded use, particularly in children.
- No specific laboratory monitoring requirements are provided in the source.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Paediatric patients have increased risk of systemic absorption and systemic corticosteroid effects due to higher skin permeability and higher skin surface area-to-body weight ratio.
  - Do not use in children under 2 years of age except on medical advice.
- Use during pregnancy: exercise caution (see Section 9).
- Use during lactation: apply only if maternal benefit outweighs infant risk and avoid application to breasts to prevent accidental ingestion by the infant.
- Use in the elderly: no specific data available; exercise usual clinical caution.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Topical formulation may reduce effectiveness of latex contraceptive devices (condoms, diaphragms) when applied to the genital area; effect is temporary and may occur only during treatment. Avoid relying on latex barrier methods during treatment of the genital area, and counsel patients accordingly. [Australian]

- If new reactions or lack of response to therapy occur, discontinue and reassess.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category: Category A [Australian].
- Use in pregnancy: There are limited human data for topical clotrimazole and hydrocortisone in pregnancy. Animal studies with clotrimazole do not indicate reproductive toxicity. Systemic corticosteroids are associated with reproductive effects at high systemic doses in animals; there are no animal data specific to topical hydrocortisone. As a precaution, avoid prolonged use during pregnancy, particularly during the first trimester, and it is preferable to avoid use in the first trimester if possible. [Australian]

### Breastfeeding
- Clotrimazole: Animal data indicate excretion of clotrimazole and/or metabolites in milk after intravenous administration.
- Hydrocortisone: No specific data available for topical hydrocortisone excretion in human milk; topically applied hydrocortisone is unlikely to produce significant systemic effects in the breastfed infant under normal use, but cutaneous absorption can be increased under occlusion or with large treated areas or damaged skin.
- Recommendation: Use during lactation only if the expected maternal benefit justifies potential risk to the infant. If used, avoid application to the breasts to prevent accidental ingestion by the infant.

### Fertility
- Clotrimazole: No human fertility studies; animal studies did not demonstrate effects on fertility.
- Hydrocortisone: No data available for topically applied hydrocortisone effects on fertility.
- Overall fertility impact: Not specified in available sources beyond the above.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Clotrimazole: Synthetic imidazole antifungal active against dermatophytes and yeasts; antifungal effect is primarily via interference with ergosterol synthesis leading to altered fungal cell membrane permeability.
- Hydrocortisone: Topical corticosteroid that reduces inflammation by multiple mechanisms including stabilization of leukocyte lysosomal membranes, inhibition of macrophage accumulation, reduction of capillary permeability and edema, antagonism of histamine activity, reduction of complement and kinin-mediated effects, and inhibition of fibroblast proliferation and collagen deposition.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Clotrimazole:
  - Dermal absorption: Studies of urinary excretion indicate less than 0.5% of dermally applied clotrimazole appears in urine over five days of observation.
  - Faecal excretion (likely primary elimination route for absorbed drug) not studied in humans in the available sources.
- Hydrocortisone:
  - Absorption through skin varies between patients and is markedly increased when the stratum corneum is disrupted (e.g. inflamed, damaged skin) or when occlusion is used.
  - Regional variation in percutaneous absorption: greater from ear region (around and behind ear), scrotum, axilla, eyelid, face and scalp than from forearm, knee, elbow, palm and sole.
  - Prolonged absorption may persist after washing because the drug can be retained in the stratum corneum and dermoepidermal layers.
  - Metabolism: rapid hepatic and tissue metabolism to hydrogenated and degraded metabolites (e.g. tetrahydrocortisone, tetrahydrocortisol).
  - Elimination: metabolites excreted primarily in urine as conjugates; biological half-life approximately 100 minutes.

Overdosage and additional practical notes:
- Overdose: No reports of acute overdose with topical use. Acute systemic toxicity is unlikely after a single dermal application or inadvertent oral ingestion. Chronic excessive exposure can lead to systemic and dermal corticosteroid adverse effects; if suspected, discontinue topical corticosteroid and consider tapering. For clinical advice on overdose, contact local poison information services (source provided Australian Poison Information Centre contact: 13 11 26) [Australian].
