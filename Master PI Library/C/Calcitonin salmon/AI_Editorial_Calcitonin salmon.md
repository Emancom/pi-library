# Editorial Product Information: Calcitonin salmon

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Calcitonin salmon (as polyacetate polyhydrate). 
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic polypeptide (identical to the hormone produced by the ultimobranchial gland of salmon).
- **Brand Name:** **Miacalcic** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H05BA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection (1 mL ampoules) containing 50 I.U. or 100 I.U. per mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (s.c.), intramuscular (i.m.), or intravenous (i.v.).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Chiesi Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Paget’s Disease (Active):** Indicated for patients who are non-responsive to or unsuitable for alternative therapies [Australian].
- **Hypercalcaemia:** Treatment of elevated serum calcium levels [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
To mitigate the potential risk of malignancy associated with long-term use, treatment should be limited to the shortest duration possible using the minimum effective dose [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Hypercalcaemia:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 5–10 I.U. per kg body weight daily.
    - **IV Route:** Administered via slow i.v. infusion (in 500 mL 0.9% saline) over at least six hours, or by slow i.v. injection in 2 to 4 divided doses throughout the day.
    - **SC/IM Route:** The daily dose may be given in one or more injections. If the volume exceeds 2 mL, i.m. injection is preferred using multiple sites [Australian].
- **Paget's Disease:**
    - **Initial Dose:** 80–100 I.U. daily via s.c. or i.m. injection.
    - **Maintenance:** Depending on clinical and biochemical response, the frequency may be reduced to 80–100 I.U. every second day. In some patients, a daily dose of 50 I.U. may suffice once symptoms improve [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Infusion:** Considered the most effective method for emergency or severe hypercalcaemia.
- **Self-Administration:** Patients must receive thorough instruction from a healthcare professional if performing s.c. injections at home [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to calcitonin salmon or any excipients [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Use is contraindicated in these populations [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Malignancy Risk:** Meta-analyses of trials involving oral and nasal formulations have shown a small but statistically significant increase in the incidence of malignancies (absolute risk increase between 0.7% and 2.36%) with long-term therapy. Although data for parenteral administration are limited, an increased risk cannot be excluded [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** As a polypeptide, calcitonin may cause local or generalised allergic reactions. Rare cases of anaphylactic shock have been reported. **Sensitivity testing** (scratch or intradermal skin test using a 1:100 dilution) is advisable prior to the first dose [Australian].
- **Escape Phenomenon:** Long-term therapy may lead to a loss of therapeutic response due to binding site saturation. Response is typically restored after a treatment interruption [Australian].
- **Driving and Operating Machinery:** Patients should be cautioned that dizziness, fatigue, and visual disturbances may occur, potentially impairing reaction times [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are listed by frequency based on systemic administration and post-marketing data.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, dizziness, headache, dysgeusia, flushing (with heat sensation), fatigue, arthralgia. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Vomiting, hypertension, paraesthesia, visual impairment, injection site reactions, influenza-like symptoms (chills/fever), oedema (facial/peripheral). |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Generalised rash, pruritus, polyuria, hypersensitivity (rash, oedema, hypertension). |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, anaphylactic shock (leading to tachycardia, hypotension, and collapse). |
| **Not Known** | Tremor, hypocalcaemia, urticaria (post-marketing reports). |

- **Note:** Gastrointestinal side effects are dose-dependent and occur more frequently with i.v. administration than with i.m. or s.c. routes. These may be managed by anti-emetics or dose subdivision [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Lithium:** Concomitant use may result in decreased plasma lithium concentrations, necessitating dose adjustments of lithium [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. There is no human data on use during pregnancy; it should not be used unless benefits clearly outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Animal data suggest calcitonin may suppress lactation. Treatment during breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended except in exceptional cases (e.g., familial hyperphosphatasaemia). Long-term use should be avoided as it may interfere with bone growth. Dosing should be cautious and weight-based [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Use standard adult dosages with care, noting that Paget's disease is more prevalent in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Calcitonin salmon is primarily metabolised and excreted by the kidneys; a dose reduction may be required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific information provided [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Calcitonin salmon binds to specific receptors (involving cyclic AMP) to inhibit osteoclastic bone resorption. It reduces high bone turnover by decreasing both the number and activity of osteoclasts.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** It regulates calcium homeostasis (ATC H05BA01). It increases the renal excretion of calcium, phosphate, and sodium by decreasing tubular reabsorption. It may also have analgesic properties via binding sites in the central nervous system [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Inactivated by gastric proteases (not given orally). Bioavailability is ~70% for s.c. and i.m. routes. Peak plasma levels are reached in ~23 minutes (s.c.) or ~1 hour (i.m.).
    - **Distribution:** 30–40% protein bound. Volume of distribution: 0.15–0.30 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly converted to inactive metabolites primarily in the kidneys.
    - **Elimination:** 95% excreted via kidneys (less than 2% as unchanged drug). Elimination half-life is ~60–90 minutes [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Malignancy Meta-analysis:** Long-term use was associated with increased malignancy rates in trials for osteoporosis and osteoarthritis, with one 5-year trial showing a risk difference of 3.4% [Australian].
- No specific pivotal trial data for Paget's or Hypercalcaemia were detailed in the available source [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 2°C and 8°C (Refrigerate). 
- **Handling:** Do not freeze [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
