# Editorial Product Information: Colistimethate Sodium (Colistin)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration). 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Important:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Colistimethate sodium (a polypeptide antibiotic).
- **Brand Names:** Colistin Link [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Powder for injection; 150 mg of colistin (as colistimethate sodium) per vial. When reconstituted with 2 mL of water for injections, the solution provides 150 mg/2 mL, equivalent to 4,500,000 IU of antibiotic activity [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Intramuscular (IM).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Colistimethate sodium is indicated for the treatment of acute or chronic infections caused by susceptible strains of certain Gram-negative bacilli [Australian].
- **Key Susceptible Organisms:** *Enterobacter aerogenes*, *Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, and particularly *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* [Australian].
- **Initial Therapy:** May be used to initiate treatment for suspected serious Gram-negative infections while awaiting culture results [Australian].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not indicated for infections caused by *Proteus* or *Neisseria* species [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children)
- **Normal Renal Function:** 2.5 to 5 mg/kg per day, administered in 2 to 4 divided doses. The specific dose should be determined by the severity of the infection [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** Should not exceed 5 mg/kg/day in patients with normal renal function [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Gently swirl during reconstitution with 2.0 mL water for injections to avoid frothing [Australian].
- **Intramuscular:** Administer by deep IM injection.
- **Intravenous (Intermittent):** Slowly inject half of the total daily dose over 3 to 5 minutes every 12 hours [Australian].
- **Intravenous (Continuous Infusion):** Slowly inject half of the total daily dose over 3 to 5 minutes, then add the remaining half to a compatible infusion solution (e.g., 0.9% NaCl, 5% Glucose). Infuse at a rate of 5–6 mg/hr in patients with normal renal function, starting 1 to 2 hours after the initial dose [Australian].
- **Stability:** Infusion solutions should be freshly prepared and used within 24 hours [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to colistimethate sodium or any component of the formulation [Australian].
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Contraindicated as the drug reduces acetylcholine release at the pre-synaptic neuromuscular junction [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neurological Disturbances:** Potential for transient effects including circumoral paresthesia, numbness, tingling of extremities, vertigo, dizziness, and slurring of speech. Patients should be cautioned against driving or operating machinery [Australian].
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Dose-dependent renal impairment may occur, marked by decreased urine output or rising BUN and serum creatinine. These effects are typically reversible upon discontinuation [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blockade:** High serum levels may lead to respiratory arrest, particularly in patients with renal impairment or those receiving concomitant neuromuscular blockers [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Close monitoring of renal function is required, especially in the elderly and those with pre-existing renal impairment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Renal:** Nephrotoxicity (rising BUN/creatinine, decreased urine output).
- **Neurological:** Paresthesia (circumoral and extremities), tingling of the tongue, vertigo, slurring of speech, and dizziness [Australian].
- **Respiratory:** Respiratory arrest and apnoea, particularly following IM administration or in the context of renal failure [Australian].
- **Dermatological:** Generalised pruritus, urticaria [Australian].
- **General:** Drug fever and gastrointestinal upset [Australian].
- **Frequency:** Frequency data (e.g., % rates) is not specified in the available source.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Neuromuscular Blocking Agents:** Curariform muscle relaxants (e.g., tubocurarine, succinylcholine, decamethonium) and sodium citrate may have their effects potentiated; use with extreme caution [Australian].
- **Neurotoxic/Nephrotoxic Antibiotics:** Other antibiotics that interfere with neuromuscular transmission (e.g., kanamycin, streptomycin, polymyxin, neomycin) should only be used concomitantly with the greatest caution [Australian].
- **Macrolides and Fluoroquinolones:** Co-treatment with azithromycin, clarithromycin, norfloxacin, or ciprofloxacin should be avoided in patients with myasthenia gravis [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B2. Safety in human pregnancy has not been established. It has been used in the third trimester to treat bacteriuria, but animal data (rabbits) suggests potential embryotoxic and teratogenic effects. Use with caution in women of child-bearing potential [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specified in available sources (No data available) [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources (No data available) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is the same as adults (2.5–5 mg/kg/day). Note that subjective neurological symptoms may not be easily reported by infants or young children, requiring strict monitoring of renal function [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Decline in renal function with age should be considered when determining dosage [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires significant dose and interval adjustments.
    - *Mild impairment (Cr 1.3–1.5 mg/100mL):* ~2.5–3.8 mg/kg/day [Australian].
    - *Moderate impairment (Cr 1.6–2.5 mg/100mL):* ~2.5 mg/kg/day [Australian].
    - *Considerable impairment (Cr 2.6–4.0 mg/100mL):* ~1.5 mg/kg every 36 hours [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal polypeptide antibiotic [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Effective against specific Gram-negative bacilli; works by disrupting bacterial cell membranes [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Crosses the placental barrier. 
    - **Elimination:** Primarily eliminated via renal excretion. 
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 2 to 3 hours in both adults and children (including premature infants) following IV or IM administration [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Specific pivotal trial data is not detailed in the source, though the drug is noted for therapeutic value in urinary tract infections caused by sensitive *P. aeruginosa* and other Gram-negative bacilli resistant to broad-spectrum antibiotics [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Store between 2°C and 8°C (Refrigerate; do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 – Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
