# Editorial Product Information: Calcitriol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to their local regulatory approved labelling for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Calcitriol (INN); (5Z,7E)-9,10-secocholesta-5,7,10(19)-triene-1α,3β,25-triol.
- **Chemical Class:** Vitamin D3 metabolite (active form).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Calciprox** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A11CC04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Soft gelatin capsules containing 0.25 micrograms of calcitriol [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ERIS Pharmaceuticals (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Calcitriol is indicated for the following [Australian-approved]:
- Treatment of established osteoporosis (diagnosed by densitometry or radiographic evidence of atraumatic fracture).
- Prevention of corticosteroid-induced osteoporosis in patients starting oral steroid therapy expected to cause significant bone loss.
- Treatment of hypocalcaemia in patients with:
    - Uraemic osteodystrophy.
    - Hypoparathyroidism (postoperative, idiopathic, or pseudohypoparathyroidism).
    - Hypophosphataemic rickets.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
Dosage must be individualised based on serum calcium levels and specific indication.

### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Established Osteoporosis:** 0.25 mcg twice daily. If response is inadequate, may increase to a maximum of 0.5 mcg twice daily (rarely required) [Australian].
- **Corticosteroid-Induced Osteoporosis:** 0.25 mcg twice daily for steroid doses equivalent to <10 mg/day prednisone; increase to 0.75 mcg/day if steroid doses exceed 10 mg/day prednisone [Australian].
- **Uraemic Osteodystrophy:** Initial dose of 0.25 mcg/day. May increase by 0.25 mcg/day at intervals of 2 to 4 weeks. Patients with normal/slightly reduced serum calcium may respond to 0.25 mcg every other day. Dialysis patients typically require 0.5 to 1.0 mcg/day [Australian].
- **Hypoparathyroidism and Rickets:** Initial dose of 0.25 mcg/day given in the morning. May increase at 2 to 4-week intervals if clinical/biochemical response is inadequate [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Dietary calcium intake should be monitored; in osteoporosis, daily intake should not exceed 1000 mg [Australian].
- Patients with malabsorption may require higher doses [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypercalcaemia [Australian].
- Vitamin D toxicity [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to calcitriol or any capsule excipients (e.g., butylated hydroxyanisole, butylated hydroxytoluene) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypercalcaemia:** There is a strong relationship between calcitriol and the development of hypercalcaemia. In some uraemic osteodystrophy trials, up to 40% of patients developed hypercalcaemia [Australian].
- **Ectopic Calcification:** Calcitriol increases serum phosphate. Chronic hypercalcaemia may lead to vascular, soft-tissue, and renal calcification (nephrocalcinosis). This is a particular risk in patients with renal failure; serum phosphate should be managed with phosphate-binding agents [Australian].
- **Concomitant Vitamin D:** All other vitamin D compounds should be withheld to avoid additive effects and hypervitaminosis D [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Serum Calcium:** Must be monitored regularly. If levels exceed 0.25 mmol/L (1 mg/dL) above the normal range, treatment should be suspended immediately until normocalcaemia is restored [Australian].
- **Osteoporosis Monitoring:** At commencement, then at 2–4 weeks, and 2–3 monthly thereafter [Australian].
- **Renal/Hypoparathyroidism Monitoring:** Twice weekly during the initial stabilisation phase, then 2–4 weeks, then 2–3 monthly [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally associated with excessive vitamin D intake and resultant hypercalcaemia.

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **More Common (>10%)** | Metabolic | Hypercalcaemia |
| | CNS | Drowsiness, weakness |
| | Gastrointestinal | Nausea |
| **Less Common (1-10%)** | Dermatological | Pruritus (associated with hypercalcaemia) |
| | Gastrointestinal | Diarrhoea, constipation |
| | Genitourinary | Impairment of renal function |
| | Musculoskeletal | Metastatic or ectopic calcification |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Acute Hypercalcaemia:** May lead to cardiac arrhythmia or cardiac arrest.
- **Chronic Toxicity:** May cause dystrophy, fever, polyuria, dehydration, pancreatitis, and rarely, overt psychosis [Australian].
- **Life-threatening:** Severe dehydration [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Cholestyramine:** Reduces intestinal absorption of calcitriol [Australian].
    - **Enzyme Inducers (Phenytoin/Phenobarbital):** May increase metabolism of calcitriol, necessitating higher doses [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Thiazide Diuretics:** Increased risk of hypercalcaemia [Australian].
    - **Digitalis:** Hypercalcaemia may precipitate cardiac arrhythmias in digitalised patients [Australian].
    - **Magnesium Antacids:** Risk of hypermagnesaemia; avoid concomitant use [Australian].
    - **Corticosteroids:** Functional antagonism regarding calcium absorption [Australian].
    - **Phosphate-binders:** Dosage must be adjusted based on serum phosphate levels [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Calcitriol has shown teratogenicity in rabbits at high doses (external and skeletal abnormalities). In rats, no teratogenic potential was seen at twice the human dose. Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Calcitriol is assumed to pass into breast milk. Due to potential adverse effects on the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended during therapy [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No significant effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at doses up to 0.3 mcg/kg [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Used in children with hypoparathyroidism. Patients aged 1–5 years typically receive 0.25 to 0.75 mcg/day. Long-term use carries a risk of nephrocalcinosis; younger age and higher doses increase this risk [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is necessary. However, caution is advised in patients with ischaemic heart disease. Compliance and polypharmacy should be considered [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Special care and frequent monitoring are required. Uraemic patients may be vitamin D resistant due to the kidney's inability to convert precursors to active calcitriol [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Calcitriol is the active form of Vitamin D3 (1,25-(OH)2D3). It binds to intracellular receptors in osteoblasts, intestinal mucosa, and parathyroid cells to regulate calcium and phosphorus homeostasis. It stimulates intestinal calcium transport and osteoblastic activity [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak serum concentrations reached in 3–6 hours (single dose) or 2–6 hours (steady state) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 99.9% protein-bound in the blood [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolite is 1α,25R-dihydroxycholecalciferol-26,23S-lactone [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Serum half-life is 3–6 hours, but pharmacological effect lasts 3–5 days. Primarily excreted in faeces (approx. 49% after 6 days) and urine (approx. 16%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Post-menopausal Osteoporosis:** A 3-year randomised trial (n=432) compared calcitriol (0.25 mcg twice daily) to calcium (1000 mg/day). Calcitriol resulted in a three-fold reduction in new vertebral fractures by the second year of treatment [Australian].
- **Corticosteroid-Induced Osteoporosis:** A 2-year trial (n=103) in patients starting long-term steroids showed that calcitriol (0.5–1.0 mcg/day) significantly reduced bone loss at the lumbar spine in the first year compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.
