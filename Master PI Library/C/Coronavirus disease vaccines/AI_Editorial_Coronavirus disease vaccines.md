# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **Levocarnitine** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a concise reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should refer to the current local PI for legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Levocarnitine Injection** [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
  - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg via slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection or infusion. 
  - A loading dose may be required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over 24 hours. 
  - Dosing frequency: Every 3 to 4 hours (minimum every 6 hours). 
  - Maximum reported dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
  - Starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight, administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
  - Dose adjustments: Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
  - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations. 
  - Perform weekly and monthly monitoring including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Administration Note:** Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use. Discard unused portions as the product contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if severe reactions occur [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** In patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD, chronic high-dose oral administration (not specifically injection) may lead to accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Plasma carnitine levels should be monitored to guide therapy and avoid excessive concentrations [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

| Frequency | Adverse Event [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), Pain (30–35%), Headache (22%), Hypertension (20%), Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8–12%), Dizziness (10–18%), Pharyngitis (23%), Fever (5–12%), Chest pain (6–15%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, Asthenia, Back pain, Arrhythmia, Atrial fibrillation, Tachycardia, Anorexia, Constipation, Anemia, Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Peripheral edema, Depression, Insomnia, Paresthesia, Rhinitis, Pruritus, Rash. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].
- **Other:** Transient "fishy" body odor may occur with carnitine therapy [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category C equivalent:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate/well-controlled human studies exist. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
  - Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. 
  - Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentration in milk after exogenous administration. 
  - Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant; consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg body weight (see Section 3). The safety and efficacy in neonates/infants are supported by data showing levocarnitine as an essential factor in energy metabolism [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
  - Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. 
  - Oral levocarnitine safety/efficacy has not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors or ESRD, it clears toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. 
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis, trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Metabolic Outcome Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiac arrhythmias) has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s; stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
  - US: Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other regions.
