# Editorial Product Information: Clevidipine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **clevidipine** for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for specific legal and regulatory requirements. At the time of this writing, only the Australian source document was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Clevidipine (as an injectable emulsion)
- **Chemical Class:** Dihydropyridine L-type calcium channel blocker
- **Brand Name(s):** **Cleviprex** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C08CA16
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
  - Injectable emulsion (milky white, opaque)
  - 25 mg/50 mL (0.5 mg/mL) [Australian]
  - 50 mg/100 mL (0.5 mg/mL) [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Chiesi Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Short-term treatment of hypertension:** For use when oral therapy is not feasible or desirable [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial Dose:** 1–2 mg/hour.
- **Titration:** The dose may be doubled every 90 seconds initially. As blood pressure approaches the target goal, increments should be less than doubling, and the interval between adjustments should be extended to every 5–10 minutes. 
- **Maintenance Dose:** Most patients achieve the desired response at 4–6 mg/hour. 
- **Maximum Dose:** 32 mg/hour. Due to lipid load restrictions, the maximum recommended infusion volume is 1000 mL per 24-hour period (averaging 21 mg/hour) [Australian]. 
- **Transition to Oral Therapy:** Discontinue clevidipine or titrate downward while initiating oral therapy, accounting for the lag time of the oral agent’s onset.

### Administration Instructions
- **Aseptic Technique:** Clevidipine contains phospholipids and can support microbial growth. Infusion must begin immediately once the vial is spiked.
- **Handling:** Gently invert the vial before use to ensure emulsion uniformity. Do not use if discoloured or if particulate matter is present.
- **Restrictions:** Do not dilute. Do not administer in the same line as other medications.
- **Compatibility:** If using separate sites, clevidipine may be co-administered with Water for Injection, 0.9% Sodium Chloride, 5% Glucose, 5% Glucose in Ringers Lactate, or 10% amino acids [Australian].
- **Discard Policy:** Use within 12 hours of spiking and discard any unused portion.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Allergy to clevidipine, soybeans, soy products, eggs, or egg products [Australian].
- **Defective Lipid Metabolism:** Including pathologic hyperlipemia, lipoid nephrosis, or acute pancreatitis if accompanied by hyperlipidemia [Australian].
- **Severe Aortic Stenosis:** Excessive reduction in afterload may reduce myocardial oxygen delivery [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypotension and Reflex Tachycardia:** Rapid blood pressure reduction may cause systemic hypotension and reflex tachycardia. If these occur, the dose should be decreased. Use of beta-blockers to treat clevidipine-induced tachycardia is not recommended [Australian].
- **Negative Inotropy:** Dihydropyridines may exacerbate heart failure. Monitor patients with heart failure closely [Australian].
- **Beta-blocker Withdrawal:** Clevidipine does not protect against the effects of abrupt beta-blocker withdrawal; beta-blockers should be tapered gradually.
- **Rebound Hypertension:** Monitor patients for at least 8 hours after stopping prolonged infusions if they are not transitioned to other therapies [Australian].
- **Lipid Load:** The formulation contains 0.2 g of lipid per mL (2.0 kcal). Monitor lipid intake in patients with restricted lipid requirements.
- **Pheochromocytoma:** Data regarding use in hypertension associated with pheochromocytoma is unavailable [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Perioperative Hypertension Settings
- **Common (1–10%):** Oedema [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Atrial flutter, tachycardia, dizziness, anxiety, confusional state, insomnia, pain, chest pain, peripheral oedema, pyrexia, increased blood creatinine, increased AST, and incision site complications [Australian].

### Essential Hypertension Settings
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Dizziness, flushing, nausea, polyuria, feeling hot, and increased ALT [Australian].

### Post-Marketing and Serious Effects
- **Rare (>0.01% to <0.1%):** Hypersensitivity, arrhythmia, ileus, respiratory gas exchange disorder, oxygen saturation decreased, and death [Australian].
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** Cerebrovascular accident, atrial fibrillation, congestive heart failure, rash, urticaria, chills, decreased heart rate, and absent pulse [Australian].
- **Other:** Increased blood triglycerides [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Formal studies have not been conducted. However, interactions are unlikely as clevidipine is rapidly metabolised by non-CYP450 esterases [Australian].
- **CYP Enzymes:** Clevidipine and its primary metabolite have limited potential to inhibit or induce CYP enzymes [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies show increased maternal/fetal mortality and prolonged gestation. Calcium channel blockers may suppress uterine contractions and carry a risk of fetal hypoxia due to maternal hypotension. Use only if benefits justify risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if clevidipine is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution and consider the benefits of breastfeeding versus the maternal need for therapy [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, female rats showed changes in estrus cycles, but no adverse effects on mating performance or fertility were observed at doses up to the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established; use is not recommended in children or adolescents [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed compared to younger patients. However, doses should be titrated cautiously, typically starting at the lower end of the range [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A dihydropyridine L-type calcium channel blocker that inhibits calcium influx during depolarisation in arterial smooth muscle. It reduces mean arterial blood pressure by decreasing systemic vascular resistance without affecting venous capacitance vessels (pre-load) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (>99.5%). Volume of distribution is 0.17 L/kg in arterial blood [Australian].
  - **Metabolism:** Rapidly hydrolysed by esterases in the blood and extravascular tissues to an inactive carboxylic acid metabolite and formaldehyde.
  - **Elimination:** Multiphasic decline. Initial phase half-life is ~1 minute (85–90% of elimination); terminal half-life is ~15 minutes. 83% excreted in urine and faeces [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces a 4–5% reduction in systolic blood pressure within 2–4 minutes of initiation. Blood pressure typically recovers within 5–15 minutes after stopping the infusion [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESCAPE-1 & ESCAPE-2:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in cardiac surgery patients. Clevidipine demonstrated significant blood pressure reduction within 2–4 minutes.
- **ECLIPSE:** Three Phase 3 trials comparing clevidipine to nitroglycerine, sodium nitroprusside, and nicardipine. Blood pressure control was similar across groups, with comparable 30-day safety outcomes (death, MI, stroke, renal dysfunction) [Australian].
- **VELOCITY:** Open-label trial in severe hypertension (SBP >180 mmHg or DBP >115 mmHg). Target BP was achieved in 91% of patients within 30 minutes, and 91% were successfully transitioned to oral therapy within 6 hours of discontinuing clevidipine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging. Once spiked, the product must be used within 12 hours [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
