# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions included:** US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and administration guidelines.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the US (FDA) source documentation was provided for this specific formulation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) or by infusion. In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is typically followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours). Daily doses should generally be in the range of 50 mg/kg, though up to 300 mg/kg has been administered [US].
- **ESRD on Haemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). It should be administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is advised [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** In ESRD, downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) levocarnitine levels [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No specific contraindications are listed in the provided source, though hypersensitivity to the active ingredient is a standard clinical precaution [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without a strict risk-benefit analysis [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic high-dose oral administration (though less applicable to the IV route) in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events in ESRD patients are often confounded by underlying pathology. 

### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% Frequency) [US]
| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcaemia, hypervolaemia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnoea, rhinitis |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness, paresthesia |

### Serious/Specific Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (reported post-marketing) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and transient nausea/vomiting [US].
- **Other:** Body odour has been reported with carnitine supplementation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate that levocarnitine may increase the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concurrently with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specifically letter-graded in source text]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the maternal benefits of treatment [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3). Specific age-range safety studies were not detailed beyond standard metabolic disorder dosing [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; however, IV levocarnitine is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into the cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters that are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, the distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV levocarnitine three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
