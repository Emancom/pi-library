# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a clinically relevant format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for reference by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of publication, only the United States PI was available for review; information for other regions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) is marked as "Not specified in available sources."

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** 
    - **US:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **US:** Sterile injection for intravenous use, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials.
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - **US:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administration should occur as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Metabolic Crisis:** In acute cases, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance (Metabolic):** Subsequent daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized as clinically required [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Levocarnitine injection is compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **ESRD:** Dose adjustments may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) are appropriate if levels are high [US].
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **US:** None known.
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Warning:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
In clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred frequently (regardless of causality):

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, fever, flu syndrome |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness, paresthesia |

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis [US].
- **Metabolic:** Distinctive body odor [US].
- **Serious Effects:** Anaphylaxis, seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound, suggesting a low potential for displacement-based interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US:** Not formally assigned a letter category in this document; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA-adjusted) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The benefits of maternal therapy should be weighed against potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for children with inborn errors of metabolism is consistent with adult weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** This is a primary target population for the ESRD indication; however, oral formulations should be used with caution due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study (ESRD):** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg after dialysis showed that trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in dialysis patients demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
