# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport; amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer exclusively via the intravenous route [US].
- Parenteral products should be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Method:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses over the subsequent 24 hours. Frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Highest Reported Dose:** 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Method:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations before starting therapy [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- There are no known contraindications listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration.
- **Management:** Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs. Re-administration requires a careful risk-benefit analysis and close monitoring for symptom recurrence [US].

### Seizures
- Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Note)
- While this document focuses on the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Warfarin Therapy:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly. Monitor INR levels after treatment initiation or dose adjustments [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Serious Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are frequently observed. Gastritis is less common [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Section 5) [US].
- **Systemic:** Body odor [US].

### Clinical Trial Data (ESRD Patients)
In placebo-controlled trials of patients on chronic hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
- **General:** Hypertension (20%), pharyngitis (23%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Chest pain (12%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
- **Respiratory:** Cough (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Injection Site:** Injection site reaction (33%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Concomitant use may result in an increased INR. Enhanced monitoring of anticoagulation status is required during levocarnitine therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not lettered]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefits. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See dosage and administration. Safety and efficacy are established for the treatment of metabolic carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed; however, dosing in ESRD should follow established renal protocols [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety of *oral* levocarnitine is not established in renal insufficiency due to metabolite accumulation; however, the *intravenous* form is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis as it is readily removed by the dialysis procedure [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrates for oxidation and subsequent energy production in tissues (excluding the brain) [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose, approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. In ESRD patients, dialysis effectively removes levocarnitine from the plasma [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) plasma carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L by week 8 [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 12 and 24 weeks demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in trough concentrations. While efficacy in increasing carnitine levels is proven, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in ESRD remains to be determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Vials:** Single-dose; discard any unused portion as the formulation is preservative-free [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx only).
