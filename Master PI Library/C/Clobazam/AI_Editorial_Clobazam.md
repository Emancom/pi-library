# Editorial Product Information: Clobazam

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information documents from the following jurisdictions: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **clobazam** for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Clobazam
- **Chemical Class:** 1,5-benzodiazepine derivative
- **Brand Names:** Clobium [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** N05BA09
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 10 mg oral tablets (oval, white to off-white, functional score on one side) [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anxiety and Sleep Disorders (Adults):** Short-term symptomatic management (up to one month) of acute anxiety and associated sleep disturbances [Australian].
- **Epilepsy (Paediatric - 4 years and over):** Adjunctive therapy for patients with partial refractory epilepsy and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome who are not sufficiently stabilised by current antiepileptic therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Anxiety:** The typical dose range is 10 mg to 30 mg daily, administered as a single dose or in divided doses. For severe hospitalised cases, doses of 60 mg to 80 mg daily have been utilised [Australian].
- **Principle of Use:** Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest possible duration. Treatment for anxiety should generally not exceed four weeks [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (4 years and over)
- **Adjunctive Epilepsy:** Initiate treatment at 5 mg daily. A maintenance dose of 0.3 to 1.0 mg/kg body weight daily is typically sufficient. If the daily dose is divided, the larger portion should be administered at night. Single night-time doses up to 30 mg are acceptable [Australian].

### Administration and Titration
- Tablets are divisible into 5 mg halves [Australian].
- **Titration:** Dose increments should be gradual, especially in sensitive populations (elderly, children, renal/hepatic impairment), under close clinical observation [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Sudden withdrawal is strongly discouraged. A gradual tapering schedule is required to prevent withdrawal symptoms and increased seizure frequency. A minimum taper period of four weeks is recommended [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to clobazam, other benzodiazepines, or any tablet excipients [Australian].
- History of drug or alcohol dependence [Australian].
- Myasthenia gravis [Australian].
- Severe respiratory insufficiency or chronic obstructive airways disease (COAD) with incipient respiratory failure [Australian].
- Sleep apnoea syndrome [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- Pregnancy and Lactation [Australian] (See Section 8).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **CNS Depressant Effects:** Clobazam potentiates the effects of alcohol and other CNS depressants (e.g., opioids, neuroleptics). Concomitant use with opioids may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death; reserve such combinations for cases where alternative treatments are inadequate [Australian].
- **Dependence and Tolerance:** Physical and psychological dependence may occur, even at therapeutic doses. Risk increases with dose and duration. Tolerance to anti-epileptic effects may develop over time [Australian].
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) have been reported, sometimes with fatal outcomes. Monitor closely during the first 8 weeks of therapy; discontinue immediately if SJS/TEN is suspected [Australian].
- **Suicidality:** As an antiepileptic drug (AED), clobazam may increase the risk of suicidal ideation or behavior. Monitor patients for mood changes or worsening depression [Australian].
- **CYP2C19 Poor Metabolizers:** Patients with low CYP2C19 activity will experience increased exposure to the active metabolite N-desmethylclobazam. Lower starting doses and careful titration are necessary [Australian].
- **Amnesia:** Anterograde amnesia may occur, particularly at higher doses [Australian].
- **Paradoxical Reactions:** Rage, excitement, or stimulation may occur rarely and require discontinuation [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency as observed in clinical trials and post-marketing surveillance [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Sedation, tiredness, drowsiness (approx. 32%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, ataxia, headache, insomnia, confusion, slurred speech, tremor, depression, irritability, dry mouth, constipation, nausea, diarrhoea, rash |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypotension (1.2%), weight gain, sweating, anorexia |
| **Rare (<0.1%) / Unknown** | SJS/TEN, urticaria, pruritus, photosensitivity, amnesia, hallucinations, visual disturbances (blurred vision, diplopia), respiratory depression, blood dyscrasias |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Respiratory failure (especially in compromised patients).
- Fatal skin reactions (SJS/TEN).
- Potential for falls and fractures, particularly in the elderly.
- Withdrawal-induced seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic:** Potentiation of alcohol, opioids, barbiturates, antidepressants, and muscle relaxants. Alcohol can increase clobazam bioavailability by 50% [Australian].
- **CYP Interactions:**
    - **CYP3A4 and 2C19:** Clobazam is metabolised by these enzymes. Inhibitors (e.g., cannabidiol, fluconazole, omeprazole) can increase levels of the active metabolite, requiring dose adjustment [Australian].
    - **CYP2D6:** Clobazam is a weak inhibitor. Dose adjustment may be needed for drugs like paroxetine or pimozide [Australian].
- **Anticonvulsants:**
    - **Valproic acid/Phenytoin:** Levels may rise when combined with clobazam.
    - **Stiripentol:** Increases plasma levels of clobazam and its metabolite.
    - **Carbamazepine/Phenytoin:** May increase the metabolic conversion of clobazam to its active metabolite [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Classified as **Category C** [Australian]. Benzodiazepines cross the placenta. Use is contraindicated in the first trimester and generally not recommended throughout pregnancy unless the benefit clearly outweighs the risk. Risks include "floppy infant syndrome" (hypotonia, hypothermia, feeding difficulties) and neonatal withdrawal symptoms [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Clobazam is excreted in breast milk and may cause drowsiness or toxic effects in the neonate [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show no effect on fertility, but human data are lacking [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for anxiety/sleep disorders. Approved for adjunctive epilepsy therapy in children aged 4 years and older [Australian].
- **Elderly/Debilitated:** Increased sensitivity to CNS effects. Dosing should start at 10 mg once or twice daily, with minimal effective amounts used to prevent falls and ataxia [Australian].
- **Hepatic/Renal Impairment:** Increased susceptibility to adverse effects. Require low initial doses and gradual titration. Renal/hepatic function should be monitored regularly during long-term use [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Clobazam facilitates GABA-ergic neurotransmission by binding to specific benzodiazepine receptors in the CNS, primarily in the limbic system and cerebral cortex. It provides anxiolytic and anticonvulsant effects with less muscular relaxation than 1,4-benzodiazepines (e.g., diazepam) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak levels in 1-4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 85% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Demethylated by CYP3A4 (and 2C19/2B6) to the active metabolite **N-desmethylclobazam**.
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic; half-life varies by age/sex (e.g., 17-31 hours in young adults; up to 49 hours in the elderly). Approximately 90% is recovered in urine [Australian].
