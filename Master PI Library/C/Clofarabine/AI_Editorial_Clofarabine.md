# Editorial Product Information: Clofarabine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for full prescribing details. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian) was not specified in the available source.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Clofarabine (purine nucleoside anti-metabolite).
- **Brand Name(s):** Evoltra [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01BB06.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Concentrate for intravenous infusion; 20 mg of clofarabine in 20 mL (1 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Lymphocytic Leukaemia (ALL):** Indicated for the treatment of paediatric patients with relapsed or refractory ALL after receiving at least two prior regimens. This indication is based on the induction of complete responses [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Paediatric):** 52 mg/m² administered via intravenous infusion over 2 hours daily for 5 consecutive days.
- **Cycle Frequency:** Treatment cycles should be repeated approximately every 2 to 6 weeks, following recovery or return to baseline organ function.
- **Duration of Therapy:** Most responding patients show results within 1 to 2 cycles. The benefit-risk ratio of continuing therapy beyond 2 cycles in non-responders should be carefully assessed.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Must be diluted with sodium chloride 9 mg/mL (0.9%) prior to infusion.
    - Filtering through a sterile 0.2 micrometre syringe filter is required during preparation (or a 0.22 micrometre in-line filter if a syringe filter is not feasible).
    - Provide continuous IV fluids during the 5 days of administration to mitigate tumour lysis syndrome.
    - Consider prophylactic anti-emetics and steroids (e.g., 100 mg/m² hydrocortisone on days 1–3) to prevent cytokine release or Capillary Leak Syndrome.
- **Specific Populations:**
    - **Children < 20 kg:** Consider an infusion time > 2 hours to reduce anxiety, irritability, and peak plasma concentrations.
- **Dose Adjustments for Toxicity:**
    - **Haematological:** If Grade 4 neutropenia (ANC < 0.5 x 10⁹/L) lasts ≥ 4 weeks, reduce the subsequent cycle dose by 25%.
    - **Infection:** Withhold therapy until controlled; reinitiate at full dose. If a second significant infection occurs, reinitiate at a 25% dose reduction.
    - **Non-haematological:** Withhold for Grade 3 toxicities (excluding transient transaminase/bilirubin elevations or controlled nausea). Reinitiate at a 25% dose reduction upon resolution. For a second occurrence of severe toxicity, delay treatment; if continued, reduce dose by a further 25%.
    - **Hypotension:** Stop infusion immediately. If transient and resolves without intervention, reinitiate at a 25% dose reduction.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to clofarabine or any excipients.
- Severe renal insufficiency [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- Breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haematologic Toxicity:** Severe bone marrow suppression (neutropenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia) is expected and may be prolonged. Monitor CBC and platelets regularly.
- **Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (SIRS) / Capillary Leak Syndrome:** Administration can lead to a rapid reduction in leukaemia cells, potentially triggering cytokine release, SIRS, or capillary leak (hypotension, tachycardia, tachypnoea, pulmonary oedema). Discontinue immediately if symptoms occur.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Severe hepatotoxic events, including veno-occlusive disease (VOD), hepatitis, and fatal hepatic failure, have been reported. Patients post-haematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT) are at higher risk for VOD. Monitor liver function closely; discontinue if substantial increases in bilirubin or liver enzymes occur.
- **Renal Toxicity:** Elevated creatinine and acute renal failure have been observed, often secondary to tumour lysis syndrome or infection. Monitor renal function and avoid concomitant nephrotoxic drugs.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome:** Rapid cytoreduction may cause hyperuricaemia. Prophylactic allopurinol and adequate hydration are recommended.
- **Skin Toxicity:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), including fatal cases, have been reported. Discontinue for exfoliative or bullous rashes.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Enterocolitis (including neutropenic colitis and *C. difficile*) has been reported and can be fatal.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea (61%), vomiting (59%), febrile neutropenia (35%), headache (24%), rash (21%), diarrhoea (20%), pruritus (20%), pyrexia (19%), palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia syndrome (15%), fatigue (14%), anxiety (12%), flushing (11%), and mucosal inflammation (11%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Neutropenia, pericardial effusion, tachycardia, hypoacusis, abdominal pain, jaundice, VOD, hepatomegaly, hypersensitivity, septic shock, sepsis, and musculoskeletal pain.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Pancreatitis, elevated serum amylase/lipase.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Capillary leak syndrome, SIRS, bone marrow failure, gastrointestinal haemorrhage, and fatal infections/sepsis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Clofarabine is unlikely to be affected by CYP450 inhibitors/inducers. It does not significantly inhibit CYP3A4, 2D6, 2C9, 2C19, or 1A2 at clinical concentrations.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Nephrotoxic/Hepatotoxic agents:** Avoid concomitant use of drugs known to cause renal or hepatic toxicity.
    - **Renal tubular secretion:** Avoid drugs eliminated via tubular secretion during the 5-day treatment period.
    - **Vascular/Cardiac agents:** Closely monitor patients taking medications that affect blood pressure or cardiac function.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Clofarabine may cause foetal harm and was teratogenic in animal studies.
    - **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after. Men must use effective contraception and avoid fathering a child for 3 months post-treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if clofarabine is excreted in milk. Nursing should be avoided during treatment and for at least 2 weeks after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show dose-related atrophy and degeneration of reproductive organs. Effects on human fertility are unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for children aged > 1 to ≤ 21 years. No data available for infants < 1 year old.
- **Elderly Use:** Insufficient data to establish safety/efficacy in patients ≥ 65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. Use with extreme caution in mild to moderate dysfunction; monitor renal function closely.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. Use with extreme caution in mild to moderate dysfunction; monitor liver function closely.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A purine nucleoside analogue that acts via three mechanisms:
    1. Inhibition of DNA polymerase $\alpha$, terminating DNA chain elongation.
    2. Inhibition of ribonucleotide reductase, depleting dNTP pools.
    3. Disruption of mitochondrial integrity, releasing cytochrome C and triggering apoptosis even in non-dividing cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** 47% protein bound (predominantly to albumin). Volume of distribution at steady state: 172 L/m².
    - **Metabolism:** Very limited hepatic metabolism (0.2%).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal; 49–60% excreted unchanged in urine. Terminal half-life is approximately 5.2 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Phase II Study (Paediatric ALL):** An open-label, single-arm study of 61 patients (median age 12) who were refractory to at least two prior regimens.
    - **Efficacy Results:** The overall remission (OR) rate (Complete Remission [CR] + CR in the absence of platelet recovery [CRp]) was 19.7%.
    - **Survival:** Median overall survival for all patients was 12.9 weeks. For those achieving CR, median survival was 72.4 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Concentrate:** Store below 25°C. Do not freeze.
- **Diluted Solution:** Chemically and physically stable for 3 days at 2–8°C and at room temperature. From a microbiological standpoint, it should be used immediately (or within 24 hours if stored at 2–8°C).

- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Not specified for other regions in available sources.
