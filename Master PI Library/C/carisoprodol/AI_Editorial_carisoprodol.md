# Editorial Product Information: Carisoprodol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Carisoprodol (Chemical class: Carbamate).
- **Brand Name(s):** Carisoprodol Tablets, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** M03BA02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Oral Tablet: 250 mg, 350 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ScieGen Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (repackaged by St. Mary's Medical Park Pharmacy) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Musculoskeletal Pain:** Indicated for the symptomatic relief of discomfort associated with acute, painful musculoskeletal conditions in adults [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Therapy should be restricted to short-term periods (up to two or three weeks), as acute musculoskeletal conditions are typically of short duration and long-term efficacy has not been established [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 250 mg to 350 mg three times a day and at bedtime.
- **Duration of Therapy:** Maximum recommended duration is two to three weeks [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** May be administered with or without food [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Porphyria:** History of acute intermittent porphyria [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to carisoprodol or carbamates such as meprobamate [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Sedation:** Carisoprodol possesses significant sedative properties and may impair the mental and physical abilities required for hazardous tasks, such as operating motor vehicles or machinery. Sedative effects are additive when combined with other CNS depressants [US].
- **Abuse, Dependence, and Withdrawal:** As a precursor to meprobamate, carisoprodol carries a risk of misuse, abuse, and criminal diversion. Chronic use may lead to physical dependence and withdrawal symptoms (e.g., insomnia, vomiting, tremors, hallucinations, psychosis) upon abrupt cessation. Risk assessment for abuse is required prior to prescribing [US].
- **Seizures:** Post-marketing reports have identified seizures in patients treated with carisoprodol, most frequently in the context of multi-drug overdose [US].
- **CYP2C19 Activity:** Patients with reduced CYP2C19 activity (poor metabolizers) experience significantly higher exposure to carisoprodol and should be treated with caution [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (Common Reactions >2%)
The following frequencies were observed in controlled studies for acute low back pain:

| Adverse Reaction | Placebo (n=560) | Carisoprodol 250 mg (n=548) | Carisoprodol 350 mg (n=279) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Drowsiness** | 6% | 13% | 17% |
| **Dizziness** | 2% | 8% | 7% |
| **Headache** | 2% | 5% | 3% |

### Post-marketing Experience (Frequency Not Specified)
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, postural hypotension, facial flushing.
- **Central Nervous System:** Vertigo, ataxia, tremor, agitation, irritability, depressive reactions, syncope, insomnia, seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, epigastric discomfort.
- **Hematologic:** Leukopenia, pancytopenia.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Serotonin syndrome (noted in intoxication/overdose), severe respiratory depression, and death [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive sedative effects occur with alcohol, benzodiazepines, opioids, and tricyclic antidepressants. Concomitant use with meprobamate is not recommended [US].
- **CYP2C19 Inhibitors (e.g., Omeprazole, Fluvoxamine):** May increase carisoprodol exposure and decrease meprobamate exposure [US].
- **CYP2C19 Inducers (e.g., Rifampin, St. John’s Wort):** May decrease carisoprodol exposure and increase meprobamate exposure. Low-dose aspirin may also induce CYP2C19 [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category C [US]:** No adequate human data. Animal studies indicate carisoprodol crosses the placenta and may reduce fetal weight and survival at 1–1.5 times the human dose. Use only if potential benefits justify potential risks to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Carisoprodol is excreted in breast milk at concentrations 2–4 times those of maternal plasma. It may cause sedation in the infant or reduced milk production. Exercise caution and monitor the infant if used [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data show reduced sperm motility and testicular weight at high doses (1200 mg/kg/day); however, human significance is unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 16 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients over 65 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically evaluated; however, carisoprodol is renally excreted and dialyzable. Exercise caution in patients with impaired renal function [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically evaluated; however, carisoprodol is hepatically metabolized. Exercise caution in patients with impaired hepatic function [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The exact mechanism is not fully identified. In animals, it is associated with altered interneuronal activity in the spinal cord and descending reticular formation, leading to muscle relaxation.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Carisoprodol is a centrally acting relaxant; it does not directly relax skeletal muscle. Its metabolite, meprobamate, has independent anxiolytic and sedative properties [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ occurs at 1.5–2 hours.
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized in the liver via CYP2C19 to meprobamate.
  - **Elimination:** Eliminated via renal and non-renal routes. Terminal half-life is approximately 2 hours for carisoprodol and 10 hours for meprobamate [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Low Back Pain Studies:** Two 7-day, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials evaluated patients (n=1387) with acute mechanical low back pain. Patients receiving carisoprodol 250 mg or 350 mg TID and HS showed statistically significant improvement in "Relief from Starting Backache" and "Global Impression of Change" on Day 3 compared to placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
  - **United States:** Prescription-only (Rx-only); Schedule IV controlled substance (CIV) under the Controlled Substances Act [US].
