# Editorial Product Information: Chloral Hydrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of chloral hydrate for use by healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved documentation for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Chloral hydrate (a halogenated derivative of aldehyde).
- **Brand Names:** Noted generically as Chloral Hydrate Mixture [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05CC01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Oral liquid, solution: 1 g/10 mL [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ORION Laboratories Pty Ltd T/A Evaris Pharma [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pre-operative Sedation:** To induce sedation prior to surgical procedures [Australian].
- **Short-term Hypnotic:** Management of sleep disorders for a duration not exceeding 2 weeks [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Dilution:** To minimise gastric irritation, the mixture must be well diluted with water or other liquids (e.g., ginger ale, fruit juice) prior to administration [Australian].
- **Adults:** 
    - **Hypnotic Use:** 500 mg to 1 g, administered 15–30 minutes before bedtime [Australian].
    - **Pre-operative Use:** 500 mg to 1 g, administered 30 minutes before surgery [Australian].
    - **Maximum Daily Dose:** 2 g [Australian].
- **Children:**
    - Pre-operative sedation must only be administered in a healthcare facility where monitoring of consciousness levels can be maintained until discharge criteria are met [Australian].
    - **Hypnotic Dose:** 30–50 mg/kg or 1.5 g/m² (Maximum single dose: 1 g) [Australian].
    - **Premedication Dose:** 20–50 mg/kg (Maximum single dose: 1 g) [Australian].

### Special Considerations
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Dose reduction may be necessary [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Dose reduction may be required due to age-related declines in organ function [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Chloral hydrate is contraindicated in patients with:
- Marked hepatic or renal impairment [Australian].
- Severe cardiac disease [Australian].
- Active gastritis, oesophagitis, or gastric/duodenal ulcers [Australian].
- Porphyrias [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to chloral hydrate or its excipients [Australian].
- **Paediatrics:** Children with obstructive sleep apnoea (risk of life-threatening respiratory obstruction) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Alertness and Impairment:** May cause significant drowsiness persisting the following day; patients should avoid driving or operating machinery [Australian].
- **Dependence and Tolerance:** Tolerance and psychological dependence may develop by the second week of therapy. Use with caution in patients with histories of substance abuse, depression, or suicidal tendencies [Australian].
- **Withdrawal:** Abrupt cessation in dependent patients may precipitate symptoms similar to barbiturate withdrawal (e.g., delirium tremens, hallucinations). Withdrawal should occur under hospitalisation and proceed slowly [Australian].
- **Chronic Toxicity:** Long-term administration may lead to gastritis, skin eruptions, or renal parenchymal damage [Australian].
- **Respiratory Disease:** Use with caution in patients with respiratory insufficiency [Australian].
- **Carcinogenicity/Mutagenicity:** Data regarding long-term safety are incomplete; long-term use is not recommended [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are not strictly categorised by percentage in the source documents but are reported as follows:

| System Organ Class | Common/Frequent | Less Frequent/Rare |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Gastric irritation, nausea, vomiting | Diarrhoea, flatulence, abdominal distension, unpleasant taste |
| **Central Nervous System** | Drowsiness, residual "hangover" effect | Somnambulism, disorientation, hallucinations, delirium, ataxia, vertigo, light-headedness, paradoxical excitement |
| **Dermatological** | Not common | Erythematous rash, urticaria, angioedema, purpura, eczema, bullous lesions, erythema multiforme |
| **Haematological** | - | Leukopenia, eosinophilia, precipitation of acute intermittent porphyria |
| **Metabolic** | - | Ketonuria |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Coma, hypotension, hypothermia, respiratory depression, and cardiac arrhythmias may occur with high doses or overdose [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with alcohol, barbiturates, antihistamines, opiates, and antipsychotics. A vasodilation reaction (tachycardia, flushing, palpitations) may occur with alcohol [Australian].
- **Coumarin Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** A metabolite (trichloroacetic acid) may displace warfarin from protein binding sites, causing a transient increase in hypoprothrombinemic response. Monitor closely during initial therapy [Australian].
- **Intravenous Frusemide:** Administration following chloral hydrate may cause a hypermetabolic state (sweating, variable blood pressure, unease) due to thyroid hormone displacement [Australian].
- **Psychotropic/Cholinergic Drugs:** Concurrent use in the elderly may increase delirium risk [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category A:** Used by a large number of pregnant women without proven increase in malformations or harm. Use only when clearly needed [Australian].
    - **Risks:** Crosses the placenta; chronic use may lead to neonatal withdrawal [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts of the active metabolite are excreted in milk. May cause sedation in breast-fed infants [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Long-term safety data are incomplete; long-term use is not recommended [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use extreme caution. Neonates have a prolonged half-life (1–2 days) for the active metabolite trichloroethanol. Repeated dosing in infants can lead to metabolite accumulation, metabolic acidosis, and hyperbilirubinaemia [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of delirium and adverse effects. Dose reduction is recommended [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Dose reduction required due to decreased clearance and risk of toxicity [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Not fully elucidated, but CNS depressant effects are primarily attributed to the active metabolite, **trichloroethanol** [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed from the GI tract; onset within 30 minutes [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed; 70–80% protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Converted by alcohol dehydrogenase (liver and erythrocytes) to trichloroethanol. Further oxidised to trichloroacetic acid or conjugated to trichloroethanol glucuronide [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Metabolites are slowly excreted in urine and bile. Half-life of trichloroethanol is 4–12 hours in adults but extends to 24–48 hours in neonates [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Protect from light. Store in airtight containers as the substance slowly volatilises when exposed to air [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
