# Editorial Product Information: calcium + glucose + magnesium + potassium + sodium + sodium

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **calcium + glucose + magnesium + potassium + sodium + sodium**
- Chemical class: Electrolyte and bicarbonate-containing intravenous replacement fluid for renal replacement therapy  
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources  
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous infusion as replacement fluid in continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT)

## 3. Approved Indications
- Continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) replacement fluid used in intensive care units for the treatment of acute kidney injury.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult)
- Effluent volume guidance for CRRT (surrogate for delivered dose):
  - Effluent volume prescribed: 20–35 mL/kg/h (target delivered dose approximately 20–25 mL/kg/h).  
  - Maximum effluent volume: 75 L/day.
- Selection of potassium strength:
  - Product is provided in variants with potassium concentrations corresponding to approximately 0, 2, or 4 mmol/L in the final mixed solution. Choose the potassium strength according to the patient’s serum potassium status and ongoing clinical needs.

### Composition (after mixing) — per 1000 mL ready-to-use solution
- Glucose: 0 or 5.55 mmol (0 or ~1.0 g) depending on potassium variant  
- Calcium (Ca2+): 1.75 mmol  
- Magnesium (Mg2+): 0.50 mmol  
- Sodium (Na+): 140 mmol  
- Potassium (K+): 0 mmol (K0 variant), 2.0 mmol (K2 variant), or 4.0 mmol (K4 variant)  
- Bicarbonate (HCO3−): 35 mmol  
- Chloride (Cl−): approximately 109.3 mmol (K0), 111.3 mmol (K2), 113.3 mmol (K4)  
- Theoretical osmolarity (approx): 287 mOsm/L (K0), 296 mOsm/L (K2), 300 mOsm/L (K4)  
- pH of final solution: approximately 7.0–7.5  
- Note: The numeral "35" denotes bicarbonate concentration of 35 mmol/L in the final solution.

### Administration instructions
- Intended use: Infusion into the extracorporeal CRRT circuit as pre‑ and/or post‑dilutional replacement fluid according to physician prescription and CRRT modality.
- Use only under supervision of clinicians experienced in CRRT and only in settings with appropriate monitoring and trained personnel.
- The ready-to-use mixed solution must be prepared immediately prior to use by combining buffered and electrolyte components as per manufacturer instructions (mixing of separate components is required to obtain the bicarbonate-buffered final fluid).
- Additives:
  - If additions are required, add via the designated medication port located on the electrolyte chamber. Compatibility of any additive must be confirmed prior to admixture.
  - Solutions containing any added medications should be used immediately after addition and mixing.
  - Solutions with no additives should be used shortly after mixing and within a maximum of 24 hours.
- Warming: Replacement fluids should be warmed to approximate body temperature (target 36.5°C–38.0°C) immediately before infusion; do not infuse below room temperature.
- Single patient, single use: Do not reconnect partially used containers; discard unused residue. Maximum intended period of use for a single CRRT line: 72 hours.
- Inspection: Visually inspect final mixed solution for clarity, colour, particulate matter, and for integrity of seals/ports prior to connection. If any precipitate, cloudiness, particles, or leakage is noted, do not use and replace solution and CRRT tubing.
- Exact prescription (volume and strength) should be individualized according to patient weight, clinical status, ongoing fluid losses, and laboratory monitoring.

## 5. Contraindications
- Metabolic alkalosis (all strengths).  
- For potassium-containing variants:
  - K0 and K2 variants: Hypokalaemia if no appropriate potassium supplementation is provided.  
  - K4 variant: Hyperkalaemia if no appropriate potassium management is provided.  
- Technique- and patient-related contraindications for CRRT use:
  - Renal failure with pronounced protein catabolism in which uraemic symptoms cannot be corrected by CRRT.  
  - Inadequate blood flow or arterial pressure from vascular access that prevents effective CRRT.  
  - Any conditions associated with a high risk of haemorrhage such as when systemic anticoagulation is present (CRRT dependent contraindication).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed/Highlighted warnings
- **Do not administer bicarbonate-buffered chamber alone.** Failure to mix the buffered (bicarbonate) and electrolyte components before infusion can cause administration of concentrated bicarbonate and lead to metabolic alkalosis and associated clinical sequelae (nausea, lethargy, headache, arrhythmia, respiratory depression).
- **Inspect for precipitate.** Precipitation (e.g., calcium carbonate) may rarely occur several hours after starting therapy; if precipitate or particles are observed in the solution or tubing, stop and replace replacement fluid and CRRT circuit immediately.

### General warnings and precautions
- Use only by personnel experienced in CRRT in appropriately equipped units.  
- Strict aseptic technique during preparation and connection is required.  
- Do not infuse replacement fluid below room temperature; warm to approximately body temperature before infusion.  
- Monitor fluid balance meticulously (input/output, weights) to detect and manage hyper- or hypovolaemia.  
- Monitor acid–base status and electrolytes (natremia, chloraemia, calcaemia, magnesaemia, phosphataemia, potassium) regularly and adjust replacement fluid selection, supplementation, filtration rate, or other therapies accordingly.  
- Potassium management:
  - Measure serum potassium before and during CRRT.  
  - If hypokalaemia occurs or develops, consider potassium supplementation and/or use of higher potassium concentration replacement fluid (K2 or K4).  
  - If hyperkalaemia occurs, consider increased filtration rate and/or use of lower potassium strength replacement fluid (K2 or K0).  
- Glucose: Potassium-free variant may not contain glucose; episodes of hypoglycaemia have been reported. Monitor blood glucose, particularly in diabetic or at-risk patients.  
- Drug removal during CRRT: CRRT may reduce blood concentrations of certain drugs (particularly low protein binding, small distribution volume, molecular weight below hemofilter cut-off, or filter-adsorbing agents); dose adjustment may be required.  
- Concomitant therapies and nutrition:
  - Account for electrolytes and bicarbonate supplied by other infusions (parenteral nutrition, intravenous electrolytes, bicarbonate-containing fluids) to avoid cumulative electrolyte/acid–base disturbances.  
- Use in pregnancy/lactation: No clinical or animal data available; use only after individualized risk–benefit assessment.

### Monitoring requirements
- Vital signs (blood pressure, heart rate) and haemodynamic status.  
- Fluid balance, body weight.  
- Serum electrolytes: Na+, K+, Ca2+, Mg2+, Cl−, phosphate.  
- Blood glucose.  
- Acid–base status (arterial blood gas / bicarbonate).  
- Inspection of replacement fluid and CRRT tubing for precipitate during therapy.

### Overdose (clinical consequences and management)
- Over-infusion or incorrect administration may result in fluid overload (hypervolaemia) or under-replacement (hypovolaemia) with associated haemodynamic instability.  
- Excess bicarbonate administration (e.g., if bicarbonate chamber infused alone or excessively) may produce metabolic alkalosis, decreased ionized calcium, tetany, and related signs.  
- Management: Stop infusion, correct fluid balance and electrolytes, increase or decrease ultrafiltration as appropriate, and provide supportive intensive care measures. Contact local poison control resources as needed.

## 8. Drug Interactions

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- CRRT may remove drugs from the circulation, particularly those with:
  - Low protein binding,  
  - Small volume of distribution,  
  - Molecular weight below the hemofilter cut-off, or  
  - Tendency to adsorb to the hemofilter.  
- Dose adjustments of concomitant medications may be necessary during CRRT.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant use of cardiac glycosides: Monitor plasma potassium because hypokalaemia increases the risk of cardiac glycoside–related arrhythmias.
- Vitamin D, vitamin D analogues, and other calcium-containing medications: May increase risk of hypercalcaemia when given concomitantly.
- Additional sodium bicarbonate intake (from other infusions or fluids): May increase the risk of metabolic alkalosis when cumulative bicarbonate load is high.

### Management notes
- Review all concurrent infusions (parenteral nutrition, electrolytes, bicarbonate-containing solutions) to avoid cumulative electrolyte and bicarbonate excess or deficiency.  
- Monitor serum drug levels where applicable and adjust dosing of renally cleared or CRRT-affected drugs per pharmacology consult or institutional CRRT dosing guidelines.

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- The solution supplies physiologic electrolytes (Na+, K+, Ca2+, Mg2+, Cl−) and bicarbonate to replace ultrafiltrate during CRRT, thereby assisting correction of fluid, electrolyte, and acid–base imbalances seen in acute renal failure. Bicarbonate increases circulating buffer capacity and helps correct metabolic acidosis.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: 100% bioavailability when administered intravenously.  
- Distribution: Distribution of water and electrolytes is governed by osmotic and intra/extracellular concentration gradients; glucose distributes in extracellular fluid and is taken into cells via insulin‑mediated mechanisms.  
- Metabolism: Electrolyte components are not metabolized; glucose is metabolized by normal pathways.  
- Elimination: Elimination of water and electrolytes depends on residual renal function, cellular requirements, and other routes of loss (gastrointestinal, pulmonary, cutaneous).  
- No specific pharmacokinetic interactions between the individual electrolyte/buffer components are described in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Restoration and maintenance of electrolyte balance, correction of metabolic acidosis by bicarbonate delivery, and support of intravascular volume in the context of CRRT.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Limited controlled clinical trial data are described in the available sources. One small clinical trial (n=16) of high‑volume CRRT in severe acute renal injury reported hypoglycaemia as an adverse event attributed to replacement fluid use (frequency classified as rare).  
- The clinical role of bicarbonate-buffered replacement fluids in CRRT is established in ICU practice guidelines referenced in the available sources; however, detailed pivotal trial summaries, primary endpoints, and larger randomized trial data are not presented in the provided information.
