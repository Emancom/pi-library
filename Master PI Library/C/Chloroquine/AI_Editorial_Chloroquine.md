# Editorial Product Information: Chloroquine Phosphate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of chloroquine phosphate for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Chloroquine phosphate (a 4-aminoquinoline compound).
- **Brand Names:** Not specified as a proprietary brand name in the provided source [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets:** 500 mg chloroquine phosphate USP (equivalent to 300 mg chloroquine base) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured by Natco Pharma Limited; Distributed by Rising Pharma Holdings, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Malaria Prophylaxis:** For use in geographic regions where chloroquine resistance is not present [US].
- **Acute Malaria Treatment:** Treatment of uncomplicated malaria caused by susceptible strains of *Plasmodium falciparum*, *P. malariae*, *P. ovale*, and *P. vivax* [US].
- **Extraintestinal Amebiasis:** Treatment of amebic infections outside the intestinal tract [US].
- **Note on Limitations:** Chloroquine does not prevent relapses in *P. vivax* or *P. ovale* as it is ineffective against exoerythrocytic (liver stage) forms. It is not indicated for complicated/severe malaria [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
Dosage is frequently expressed in terms of chloroquine base. One 500 mg tablet of chloroquine phosphate contains 300 mg of chloroquine base.

### 3.1 Malaria Prophylaxis
- **Adults:** 500 mg (300 mg base) once weekly on the same day each week. Initiate 2 weeks prior to exposure if possible. If not, a loading dose of 1 g (600 mg base) may be taken in adults [US].
- **Pediatrics:** 5 mg base/kg body weight once weekly (max adult dose). If initiated at the time of exposure, 10 mg base/kg may be given in two divided doses 6 hours apart [US].
- **Duration:** Continue for 8 weeks after leaving the endemic area [US].

### 3.2 Treatment of Uncomplicated Malaria
- **Adults:** 
    - Initial dose: 1 g (600 mg base).
    - Second dose: 500 mg (300 mg base) after 6 to 8 hours.
    - Third and Fourth doses: 500 mg (300 mg base) on each of the two subsequent days.
    - Total dose: 2.5 g (1.5 g base) over three days [US].
- **Infants and Children:** Total dose of 25 mg base/kg over three days.
    - First dose: 10 mg base/kg (max 600 mg base).
    - Second dose: 5 mg base/kg (max 300 mg base) 6 hours after the first.
    - Third dose: 5 mg base/kg 24 hours after the first.
    - Fourth dose: 5 mg base/kg 36 hours after the first [US].

### 3.3 Extraintestinal Amebiasis
- **Adults:** 1 g (600 mg base) daily for 2 days, followed by 500 mg (300 mg base) daily for 2 to 3 weeks. Usually combined with an intestinal amebicide [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Retinal/Visual Changes:** Contraindicated in the presence of retinal or visual field changes of any etiology, except when used for the treatment of acute malaria [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to 4-aminoquinoline compounds [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Chloroquine-Resistant Malaria:** Resistance is widespread in *P. falciparum* and reported in *P. vivax*. Verify local resistance patterns via resources such as the CDC prior to prescribing [US].
- **Cardiotoxicity:** Risk of fatal cardiomyopathy and arrhythmias. Phospholipidosis may be seen on endomyocardial biopsy. ECG findings may include AV block or bundle branch blocks. QT interval prolongation has been reported; caution is required in patients with cardiac disease, electrolyte imbalances (hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia), or bradycardia [US].
- **Retinopathy:** Irreversible retinal damage can occur. Risks increase with daily doses >2.3 mg/kg (base), duration >5 years, and renal impairment. A baseline ophthalmic exam (BCVA, visual field, SD-OCT) is required within the first year of therapy [US].
- **Hypoglycemia:** Risk of severe, life-threatening hypoglycemia, even in patients not taking antidiabetic medications [US].
- **Neuromuscular Effects:** Skeletal muscle myopathy or neuropathy leading to progressive weakness may occur. Monitor deep tendon reflexes periodically [US].
- **Pediatric Toxicity:** Children are highly sensitive to 4-aminoquinolines. Accidental ingestion of small doses (e.g., 0.75 g) has resulted in fatalities [US].
- **Psoriasis and Porphyria:** May precipitate severe attacks or exacerbations; use only if the benefit outweighs the risk [US].
- **Central Nervous System:** May lower the seizure threshold in patients with epilepsy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are not specifically defined in the available source as reactions are reported voluntarily from a population of uncertain size [US].

- **Ocular:** Irreversible retinopathy (bull's eye appearance), maculopathy, visual field defects, blurred vision, reversible corneal opacities [US].
- **Cardiac:** Hypotension, QRS widening, T-wave inversion, cardiomyopathy, cardiac failure, arrhythmias (Torsades de pointes, ventricular fibrillation) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal cramps, hepatitis, increased liver enzymes [US].
- **Dermatologic:** Pruritus, pigmentary changes (skin/hair), photosensitivity, exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, TEN, DRESS syndrome [US].
- **Neurological:** Headache, convulsions, extrapyramidal disorders (dystonia, dyskinesia), polyneuropathy [US].
- **Psychiatric:** Psychosis, delirium, anxiety, agitation, hallucinations, suicidal behavior [US].
- **Hematologic:** Pancytopenia, aplastic anemia, reversible agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, hemolysis in G6PD deficiency [US].
- **Other:** Tinnitus, nerve-type deafness, hypoglycemia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antacids/Kaolin:** Reduced absorption of chloroquine; separate administration by at least 4 hours [US].
- **Cimetidine:** Inhibits metabolism of chloroquine, increasing plasma levels; avoid concomitant use [US].
- **Antidiabetics/Insulin:** Chloroquine enhances hypoglycemic effects; dose reduction of antidiabetics may be required [US].
- **Arrhythmogenic Drugs:** Increased risk of ventricular arrhythmias with drugs like amiodarone or moxifloxacin [US].
- **Ampicillin:** Chloroquine reduces ampicillin bioavailability; separate by at least 2 hours [US].
- **Cyclosporine:** Potential for sudden increase in serum cyclosporine levels; monitoring and possible discontinuation of chloroquine recommended [US].
- **Mefloquine:** Increased risk of convulsions [US].
- **Praziquantel:** Reduced bioavailability of praziquantel [US].
- **Tamoxifen:** Increased risk of retinal toxicity [US].
- **Vaccines:** May reduce the antibody response to intradermal human diploid-cell rabies vaccine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Pregnancy category not specified by letter]. Observational studies and meta-analyses show no increase in birth defects or spontaneous abortions at recommended doses for malaria. However, animal studies show embryo-fetal toxicity at high doses (3–16x therapeutic dose). Benefit-risk should be assessed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Chloroquine and its metabolite are excreted in breast milk (approx. 0.7% of maternal dose). Safety concerns for the infant exist; a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggest potential decreases in testosterone and male fertility parameters [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use with extreme caution due to risk of fatal accidental ingestion. Dose calculation must be based on body weight [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects ≥65 years. Because the drug is renally excreted, caution in dose selection and renal monitoring is advised [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Increased risk of toxicity; consider dose adjustment and monitor for retinopathy [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Chloroquine concentrates in the liver; use with caution in patients with hepatic disease or alcoholism [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Antiprotozoal agent. May concentrate in parasite acid vesicles, inhibiting heme polymerisation. Also interacts with DNA [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and nearly complete from the GI tract [US].
    - **Distribution:** 55% plasma protein bound. Highly concentrated in tissues (liver, spleen, kidney, lungs) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Partially metabolised to desethylchloroquine (active) and bisdesethylchloroquine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal excretion (slow). Acidification of urine increases excretion [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Active against erythrocytic forms of susceptible *Plasmodium* strains; inactive against exoerythrocytic forms and gametocytes [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Cardiac Electrophysiology Trial:** A randomized, placebo-controlled trial (n=116 healthy subjects) demonstrated that chloroquine (1000 mg) combined with azithromycin increased the QTc interval in a dose-dependent manner. Max mean increase in QTcF was 9 ms (95% CI 14 ms) at the highest azithromycin dose [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Container:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container as defined in USP/NF [US].

- **Classification:** Antimalarial and amebicidal drug [US].
