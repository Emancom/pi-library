# Editorial Product Information: Crisaborole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data, regulatory requirements, and safety information.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for definitive prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Crisaborole
- **Chemical Class:** Phosphodiesterase-4 (PDE-4) inhibitor; benzoxaborole derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** **STAQUIS** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** D11AH06 (Not explicitly stated in provided source).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 2% (w/w) ointment, containing 20 mg of crisaborole per gram [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Atopic Dermatitis:** Indicated for the topical treatment of mild to moderate atopic dermatitis in patients aged 2 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Paediatrics $\ge$ 2 years):** Apply a thin layer twice daily to the affected skin areas. 
- **Treatment Duration:** May be used continuously for up to 28 days per treatment course. Continuous use beyond 28 days has not been evaluated in controlled trials, though long-term safety (up to 48 weeks) has been assessed in open-label settings [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Suitable for use on all skin areas, including the head, face, neck, and intertriginous areas.
    - Application to the scalp has not been studied [Australian].
    - Do not co-apply with other topical medicines on the same lesion.
    - For topical use only; not for oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use. 
    - Wash hands after application unless the hands are the area being treated.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to crisaborole or any excipient in the formulation (e.g., white soft paraffin, propylene glycol, glyceryl monostearate, synthetic paraffin, butylated hydroxytoluene, sodium calcium edetate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Reactions, including contact urticaria, have occurred. Discontinue use immediately if signs of hypersensitivity (e.g., severe pruritus, swelling, erythema at the application or distant site) occur [Australian].
- **Oral PDE-4 Inhibitor Class Effects:** Risks associated with oral PDE-4 inhibitors (insomnia, anxiety, depression, suicidal ideation, weight loss) have not been observed with topical crisaborole in clinical or post-marketing experience [Australian].
- **General Precautions:** If accidental contact with eyes, mouth, or vagina occurs, the ointment should be wiped off and the area rinsed thoroughly with water.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common treatment-related adverse event is application site pain, typically occurring early in treatment and resolving spontaneously.

| Adverse Effect | Frequency | Jurisdiction/Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Application site pain** (burning, stinging) | Common (4.4%) | [Australian] |
| **Upper respiratory tract infection** | Common (3.0%) | [Australian] |
| **Nasopharyngitis** | Common (1.8%) | [Australian] |
| **Contact urticaria** | Uncommon (<1%) | [Australian] |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** No specific serious treatment-related adverse effects were highlighted as frequent in the provided source. Post-marketing safety is reported to be consistent with clinical trial data [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** 
    - *In vitro* data suggests that crisaborole and its main metabolite (Metabolite 1) do not inhibit CYP1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, or 3A4 at clinical concentrations.
    - Metabolite 2 is a weak/moderate inhibitor of CYP2C8 and 2C9; however, a clinical trial using warfarin (a CYP2C9 substrate) showed no drug interaction potential [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** 
    - Concurrent use with other topical atopic dermatitis medications on the same lesions has not been studied and is not recommended.
    - Emollients may be used on areas of skin not concurrently treated with crisaborole [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** No adequate data in pregnant women. Animal studies did not show direct or indirect harmful effects at maternally non-toxic doses. Clinical benefit to the mother should be weighed against potential fetal risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Crisaborole is systemically absorbed after topical application. The risk to the breastfed infant is unknown. To prevent unintentional ingestion, crisaborole should not be applied to the breast [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no effects on male or female fertility at doses up to 13 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for ages 2 years and older. Safety and efficacy in children <2 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include enough subjects aged 65 and older to determine if they respond differently than younger patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific clinical studies have not been conducted [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Specific clinical studies have not been conducted [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A competitive inhibitor of cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) at the PDE-4 catalytic site. This increases intracellular cAMP levels, suppressing the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-$\alpha$ from peripheral blood mononuclear cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 25% of the dose is absorbed systemically after topical application. Steady state is reached by Day 8 [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 97% bound to human plasma proteins [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly metabolised into inactive metabolites (Metabolite 1 via hydrolysis; Metabolite 2 via oxidation) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via renal excretion of metabolites [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies (301 and 302):** Two identical 28-day, multicentre, randomised, double-blind trials in 1,522 subjects ($\ge$2 years) with mild to moderate atopic dermatitis. 
- **Primary Outcome:** "Success in ISGA" (Investigator’s Static Global Assessment) at Day 29, defined as a score of 0 (Clear) or 1 (Almost Clear) with a $\ge$2-grade improvement from baseline.
- **Results:** Success rates were significantly higher for crisaborole (32.8% and 31.4%) compared to vehicle (25.4% and 18.0%). Time to success was significantly earlier in the crisaborole groups [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Handling:** Unused material should be disposed of according to local requirements.
