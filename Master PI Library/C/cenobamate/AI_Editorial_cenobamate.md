# Editorial Product Information: Cenobamate

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdiction(s) Contributing to this Editorial:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of cenobamate product information for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises regulatory data into a clinically focused format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Only one jurisdiction (United States) was provided for this synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cenobamate (chemical class: carbamate)
- **Brand Name(s):** **XCOPRI** [US]
- **ATC Code:** N03AX25 (Not specified in provided source)
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Tablets:** 12.5 mg (uncoated), 25 mg, 50 mg, 100 mg, 150 mg, and 200 mg (film-coated) [US]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** SK Life Science, Inc. [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Partial-onset Seizures:** Indicated for the treatment of partial-onset seizures in adult patients [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial Dosage:** 12.5 mg once daily.
- **Titration:** The dosage should be increased every two weeks according to the following schedule:
    - **Weeks 1 & 2:** 12.5 mg once daily
    - **Weeks 3 & 4:** 25 mg once daily
    - **Weeks 5 & 6:** 50 mg once daily
    - **Weeks 7 & 8:** 100 mg once daily
    - **Weeks 9 & 10:** 150 mg once daily
- **Maintenance Dosage:** 200 mg once daily.
- **Maximum Dosage:** 400 mg once daily. If needed, the dose may be increased from 200 mg to 400 mg by increments of 50 mg once daily every two weeks [US].

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken at any time of day, with or without food [US].
- **Crushed Tablet Administration:** Tablets may be taken whole or crushed. Crushed tablets can be mixed with 25 mL of water and administered by mouth as a suspension or via a nasogastric (NG) tube [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in provided source; however, patients are advised to talk to their healthcare provider if they miss a dose [US].

### Discontinuation
- Unless safety concerns (e.g., DRESS) require abrupt withdrawal, cenobamate should be gradually discontinued over a period of at least 2 weeks [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to cenobamate or any of its inactive ingredients [US].
- **Cardiac:** Familial Short QT syndrome [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **DRESS/Multi-organ Hypersensitivity:** Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) has been reported, including one fatality during rapid titration. Cenobamate must be initiated at 12.5 mg daily and titrated slowly (every 2 weeks). Discontinue immediately if DRESS is suspected [US].
- **QT Shortening:** Cenobamate causes dose-dependent shortening of the QT interval. Caution is required when using other drugs that shorten the QT interval [US].
- **Suicidal Behavior and Ideation:** Like all antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), cenobamate increases the risk of suicidal thoughts or behavior. Monitor patients for new or worsening depression and suicidal tendencies [US].
- **Liver Injury:** Clinically significant liver injury, including acute liver failure requiring transplantation, has occurred in the postmarketing setting. 
    - **Monitoring:** Obtain serum transaminases (ALT, AST) and total bilirubin before initiation and during treatment if clinically indicated [US].
- **Neurological Adverse Reactions:** Significant dose-dependent somnolence, fatigue, dizziness, and gait disturbance (ataxia, vertigo) may occur. Cognitive dysfunction (memory impairment, confusional states) and visual changes (diplopia, blurred vision) are also reported [US].
- **Withdrawal of AEDs:** Sudden withdrawal may increase seizure frequency and the risk of status epilepticus [US].

- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Dose-related elevations in serum potassium (>5 meq/L) and transaminases (>3x ULN) have been observed [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Psychosis, hallucinations, hostility, and aggression [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions [US]
- **Phenytoin:** Cenobamate increases phenytoin levels. Gradually decrease phenytoin dosage by up to 50% during cenobamate titration.
- **Phenobarbital & Clobazam:** Cenobamate increases levels of these drugs. Reduce dosage as clinically appropriate.
- **Lamotrigine & Carbamazepine:** Cenobamate decreases levels of these drugs. Dosage increases may be required.
- **CYP2B6 & CYP3A Substrates (e.g., Midazolam, Bupropion):** Cenobamate induces these enzymes, decreasing substrate levels. Dosage increases may be necessary.
- **CYP2C19 Substrates (e.g., Omeprazole):** Cenobamate inhibits this enzyme, increasing substrate levels. Dosage reduction may be necessary.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Cenobamate reduces the effectiveness of hormonal contraceptives. Women should use additional or alternative non-hormonal birth control.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **CNS Depressants & Alcohol:** Concomitant use may result in additive effects on somnolence, sedation, and cognitive impairment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter category in source [US].
    - **Risk Summary:** Animal data show embryofetal mortality and neurobehavioral impairment at clinically relevant doses. There is a pregnancy exposure registry (NAAED) available [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of cenobamate in human milk. The benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were seen in animal studies, though higher doses showed decreased corpora lutea and live fetuses [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of patients over 65. Dose selection should be cautious, typically starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Severe (CLcr <90 mL/min):** Use with caution; dosage reduction may be considered.
    - **End-stage Renal Disease/Dialysis:** Not recommended [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A to B):** Use with caution. The maximum recommended dose is 200 mg once daily.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The precise mechanism is unknown. It is demonstrated to reduce repetitive neuronal firing by inhibiting voltage-gated sodium currents and acting as a positive allosteric modulator of the $\text{GABA}_A$ ion channel [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics [US]:**
    - **Absorption:** At least 88% absorbed; $T_{max}$ 1–4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 60% protein bound, primarily to albumin. $V_d$ is 40–50 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized via glucuronidation (UGT2B7, UGT2B4) and oxidation (CYP2E1, 2A6, 2B6, 2C19, 3A4/5).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 50–60 hours. 87.8% excreted in urine (mostly as metabolites).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1 & 2:** Multicenter, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in adults with partial-onset seizures.
- **Outcomes:** Cenobamate (100 mg, 200 mg, and 400 mg) significantly reduced median seizure frequency per 28 days compared to placebo.
    - **Study 1 (200 mg):** -55.6% vs -21.5% (placebo).
    - **Study 2 (400 mg):** -55.3% vs -24.3% (placebo).
- **Seizure Freedom:** In Study 2, 21% of patients on 400 mg/day achieved seizure freedom during the maintenance phase compared to 1% for placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only; Schedule V (CV) controlled substance due to potential for abuse and dependence [US].
