# Editorial Product Information: chlorphenamine + pseudoephedrine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **chlorphenamine + pseudoephedrine**
- Chemical class:
  - Chlorphenamine: first‑generation antihistamine (histamine H1 antagonist)
  - Pseudoephedrine: sympathomimetic decongestant (alpha‑adrenergic agonist properties)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval:
  - 4 February 2008 [Australian]
- Date(s) of last revision/update of source PI:
  - 15 December 2025 [Australian]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- If registration dates for other jurisdictions are required: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Relief of upper respiratory mucosal congestion and hypersecretion associated with:
  - common cold
  - nasal allergy
  - hayfever
  - sinusitis

(Indications above are those stated in available source material. Indication status in other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.)

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Each approved tablet contains (example strength from available source): chlorphenamine 4 mg and pseudoephedrine 60 mg.
- Recommended dose for adults and adolescents 12 years and over: one tablet every 6 hours when necessary.

### Dosing by indication
- No separate dosing regimens by specific indication are specified in available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Oral administration only.
- Patients should be warned that chlorphenamine may cause drowsiness, which can persist into the following day; alcohol should be avoided while taking the medicine.
- Pseudoephedrine may cause insomnia if taken several hours before bedtime.
- Patients should not drive or operate machinery until they know how the medicine affects them.

### Missed dose instructions

## 5. Contraindications
List of all contraindications provided in available sources (do not use if any apply):
- Hypersensitivity to chlorphenamine, pseudoephedrine, chemically related drugs, or any excipient in the product.
- Severe or uncontrolled hypertension.
- Severe coronary artery disease.
- Severe acute or chronic kidney disease / renal failure.
- Concurrent use of monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) or use of MAOIs within the previous 14 days.
- Traditional antihistamines (such as chlorphenamine) are contraindicated in patients taking an antihypertensive agent.
- Narrow‑angle glaucoma.
- Stenosing peptic ulcer.
- Symptomatic prostatic hypertrophy.
- Bladder neck obstruction.
- Pyloroduodenal obstruction.
- Use in children under 12 years of age (administration is not recommended / product should not be used).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed/black‑box warnings specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Use with caution in patients with:
  - Hypertension
  - Hyperthyroidism
  - Diabetes mellitus
  - Coronary artery disease
  - Ischaemic heart disease
  - Glaucoma
  - Prostatic hypertrophy
  - Epilepsy
- Chlorphenamine may cause drowsiness, potentiate the effects of alcohol and other CNS depressants, and may impair performance (including driving). Drowsiness can persist into the next day.
- Pseudoephedrine may cause central nervous system stimulation manifesting as restlessness and insomnia; avoid taking several hours before bedtime.
- Cases of ischaemic colitis have been reported with pseudoephedrine. Discontinue the medicine and seek medical advice if sudden abdominal pain, rectal bleeding or other suggestive symptoms occur.
- Posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES) and reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome (RCVS) have been reported with pseudoephedrine. These events are more likely in patients with severe or uncontrolled hypertension or severe renal impairment. Discontinue pseudoephedrine and seek immediate medical attention if sudden severe or “thunderclap” headache, nausea, vomiting, confusion, seizures and/or visual disturbance occur.
- Ischaemic optic neuropathy has been reported; discontinue the product if sudden loss of vision, decreased visual acuity, or scotoma occurs.
- Use with caution in patients with impaired hepatic function.
- Use with caution in patients with impaired renal function.
- Elderly patients (approximately 60 years and older) are more likely to experience dizziness, sedation and hypotension with chlorphenamine‑containing products.
- Chlorphenamine may cause excitation in children; antihistamines should not be given to newborn or premature infants.
- Effects on laboratory tests: No data available.

### Monitoring requirements
- Specific monitoring schedules are not provided in available sources.
- Clinicians should remain vigilant for signs and symptoms described under adverse effects and warnings (e.g., blood pressure elevation, arrhythmia, neurovascular symptoms, visual changes, gastrointestinal bleeding) and act accordingly.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The following adverse effects and frequency categories are those reported in the available source material. Frequency definitions used: Very common (≥1/10), Common (≥1/100 and <1/10), Uncommon (≥1/1,000 and <1/100), Rare (≥1/10,000 and <1/1,000), Very rare (<1/10,000), Unknown (frequency not determined from available data).

- Additional adverse effects described in narrative form in the source material (without a frequency category) are also listed.

### Narrative adverse effects (reported without formal frequency)
- Drowsiness, dizziness, ataxia, nausea.
- Pseudoephedrine‑associated effects: cardiovascular stimulation (elevated blood pressure, tachycardia, arrhythmias), CNS stimulation (restlessness, insomnia, anxiety, tremors, rarely hallucinations), skin rashes, urinary retention, ischaemic colitis, weakness, mydriasis.
- Chlorphenamine‑associated effects:
  - CNS stimulatory: anxiety, hallucinations, appetite stimulation, muscle dyskinesias, activation of epileptogenic foci.
  - CNS depressive: sedation and impaired performance (impaired driving performance, poor work performance, incoordination, reduced motor skills, impaired information processing); impairment may occur without overt sedation and may persist into the morning after a night dose.
  - Anticholinergic effects: dry eyes/mouth/nose, blurred vision, urinary hesitancy and retention, constipation, tachycardia.
  - High doses may cause nervousness, tremor, insomnia, agitation, and irritability.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant use of antidepressant medications (e.g., tricyclic antidepressants and monoamine oxidase inhibitors [MAOIs]) with pseudoephedrine may cause a serious increase in blood pressure or hypertensive crisis.
- Other sympathomimetic agents (other decongestants, appetite suppressants, amphetamine‑like psychostimulants) may have additive hypertensive and sympathomimetic effects with pseudoephedrine.
- Pseudoephedrine may antagonise the effect of certain antihypertensive agents (e.g., methyldopa, beta‑blockers) and cause increased blood pressure.
- Chlorphenamine plus central nervous system depressants (alcohol, sedatives, opioid analgesics, hypnotics, barbiturates) may result in increased sedation and impairment.
- MAOIs and tricyclic antidepressants may prolong and intensify the anticholinergic and CNS depressive effects of chlorphenamine.
- Chlorphenamine (antihistamine) is contraindicated with antihypertensive agents per the contraindication list in available sources.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Concomitant phenytoin may decrease phenytoin elimination when taken with chlorphenamine (phenytoin interaction noted).
- Urinary acidifiers enhance elimination of pseudoephedrine.
- Urinary alkalinisers decrease elimination of pseudoephedrine.

### Clinically significant interactions with management notes
- MAOI use within the previous 14 days is a contraindication; concomitant or recent MAOI use increases the risk of severe hypertensive reactions — do not administer.
- Avoid concurrent use with other sympathomimetics and appetite suppressants.
- Use caution or consider alternative therapy in patients taking antihypertensives; monitor blood pressure if combination is unavoidable.
- Avoid or use caution with CNS depressants; warn patients about sedation and avoid alcohol.
- Monitor phenytoin levels if co‑administered with chlorphenamine due to possible decreased elimination of phenytoin.
- Adjust clinical decisions if urinary pH altering agents are used, as they affect pseudoephedrine elimination.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Fertility
- Effects on fertility: Not specified in available sources.

### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy categorisation: Pregnancy Category B2 [Australian].
- Safety in pregnancy: Safe use during pregnancy has not been established. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
- Chlorphenamine should not be used in the third trimester of pregnancy because newborn and premature infants may have severe reactions to antihistamines.

### Breastfeeding
- Pseudoephedrine: approximately 0.5% to 0.7% of a maternal single dose is estimated to be excreted into breast milk over 24 hours.
- Chlorphenamine is excreted in breast milk.
- Based on excretion data, the product is not recommended for breastfeeding mothers.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Not recommended for children under 12 years of age. Chlorphenamine may cause excitation in children. Antihistamines should not be given to newborn or premature infants.

### Elderly use
- Elderly patients (approximately 60 years and older) are more likely to experience dizziness, sedation and hypotension with chlorphenamine‑containing medicines; use with caution.

### Renal impairment
- Use with caution in patients with impaired renal function. Specific dosing adjustments by eGFR/stage: Not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Use with caution in patients with impaired hepatic function. Specific dosing adjustments by severity: Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Combination therapy: an antihistamine (chlorphenamine) to treat allergic symptoms and a sympathomimetic decongestant (pseudoephedrine) to relieve nasal and sinus congestion. Chlorphenamine blocks histamine H1 receptors; pseudoephedrine produces vasoconstriction of nasal mucosal blood vessels via sympathomimetic activity.
