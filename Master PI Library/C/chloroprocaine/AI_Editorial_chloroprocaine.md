# Editorial Product Information: Chloroprocaine Hydrochloride (Ophthalmic)

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the **United States (FDA)**.
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Chloroprocaine hydrochloride (an ester-type local anesthetic).
*   **Brand Name(s):** **IHEEZO** [United States].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Ophthalmic gel, 3% (containing 24 mg chloroprocaine hydrochloride per 800 mg of gel) [United States].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic use.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Harrow Eye, LLC [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Ocular Surface Anesthesia:** Indicated for inducing local anesthesia on the surface of the eye during ophthalmic procedures [United States-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*   **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended dose is 3 drops applied topically to the ocular surface in the area of the planned procedure [United States].
*   **Maintenance:** The gel may be reapplied as necessary to maintain the anesthetic effect throughout the procedure [United States].
*   **Administration Instructions:**
    *   For healthcare provider administration only; not intended for patient self-administration.
    *   The outer surface of the vial is not sterile.
    *   Strictly for topical application; not for injection or intraocular administration.
*   **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not applicable (administered by a healthcare professional at the time of procedure).

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a known history of hypersensitivity to chloroprocaine or any other component of the formulation [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None [United States].
*   **Route of Administration:** Strictly for topical ophthalmic use. Must not be injected or administered intraocularly [United States].
*   **Corneal Injury due to Insensitivity:** Patients should be advised not to touch the treated eye for at least 10 to 20 minutes post-application. Accidental trauma may occur while the eye is insensitive [United States].
*   **Corneal Opacification:** Prolonged use of topical ocular anesthetics is associated with the risk of permanent corneal opacification, ulceration, and subsequent visual loss [United States].
*   **Risk of Contamination:** To prevent contamination of the sterile gel, avoid touching the dropper tip to any surface [United States].
*   **Administration Setting:** Must be administered under the direct supervision of a healthcare professional [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reaction reported is mydriasis.

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (N=151) | Placebo (N=50) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mydriasis** | 26% | 2% |
| **Conjunctival hyperemia** | 11% | 12% |
| **Eye irritation** | 6% | 4% |

*Note: Frequencies are based on controlled clinical trials [United States].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Sulfonamides:** A metabolite of chloroprocaine, 2-chloro-4-aminobenzoic acid (ACBA), inhibits the action of sulfonamides. Co-administration should be approached with caution in contexts where sulfonamide activity is required [United States].
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not studied for topical ocular administration [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category:** Not assigned under current labeling rules. 
    *   There are no adequate or well-controlled studies of chloroprocaine use in pregnant women. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted [United States].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Data regarding the presence of chloroprocaine in human milk, its effects on the breastfed infant, or its impact on milk production are unavailable. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug [United States].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies evaluating the impairment of fertility have not been conducted [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in paediatric patients [United States].
*   **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between geriatric and younger adult patients [United States].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, the kidney is the primary excretory organ for local anesthetic metabolites [United States].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Chloroprocaine blocks the generation and conduction of nerve impulses by increasing the threshold for electrical excitation, slowing impulse propagation, and reducing the rate of rise of the action potential. The sequence of functional loss is: (1) pain, (2) temperature, (3) touch, (4) proprioception, and (5) skeletal muscle tone [United States].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Systemic exposure following topical ocular administration has not been studied.
    *   **Metabolism:** Rapidly hydrolyzed in plasma by pseudocholinesterases and by nonspecific esterases in ocular tissues.
    *   **Elimination:** The *in vitro* plasma half-life is approximately 25 seconds in adults and 43 seconds in neonates [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Study 1 & 2 (Healthy Volunteers):** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (N=145). Successful anesthesia (via conjunctival pinching) was achieved in 90% and 95% of the chloroprocaine groups vs. 12% and 20% in placebo. Median time to onset was 0.67 minutes; median duration ranged from 14.3 to 19.3 minutes [United States].
*   **Study 3 (Cataract Surgery):** Randomized, active-controlled, observer-masked study vs. tetracaine ophthalmic solution 0.5% (N=338). Chloroprocaine achieved successful anesthesia without supplementation in all patients, with procedures lasting an average of 22 minutes [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 15°C to 25°C (59°F to 77°F) [United States].
*   **Handling:** Sterile, single-patient-use vial. Discard any unused portion after the procedure [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Classified as **Rx Only** (Prescription Only).
