# Editorial Product Information: Cyclophosphamide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of cyclophosphamide for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian (TGA) source was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cyclophosphamide (as monohydrate)
- **Chemical Class:** Alkylating antineoplastic agent (nitrogen mustard group)
- **Brand Names:** **Cyclonex** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01AA01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 50 mg film-coated tablets [Australian]
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral, Intravenous (IV)
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amdipharm Mercury (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
Cyclophosphamide is indicated for the treatment of various neoplastic and autoimmune conditions. 

**Antineoplastic Indications [Australian]:**
- **Myeloproliferative and lymphoproliferative disorders:** Malignant lymphomas (Stage III and IV), multiple myeloma, leukaemias, and advanced mycosis fungoides.
- **Solid malignancies (frequently responsive):** Disseminated neuroblastoma, ovarian adenocarcinoma, and retinoblastoma.
- **Solid malignancies (infrequently responsive):** Carcinoma of the breast and malignant lung neoplasms.

**Immunosuppressive Indications [Australian]:**
- **Autoimmune diseases/Immunopathies:** For use in conditions such as Wegener’s granulomatosis when resistant to standard first- and second-line therapies.
- **Transplant Rejection:** Prevention of organ or tissue rejection.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Therapy should be managed by physicians experienced in cytotoxic or immunosuppressive treatment. Dosing is highly individualised based on clinical response, haematological tolerance, and the patient's general status.

### Antineoplastic Dosing (Adults) [Australian]
- **Induction (IV):** 40–50 mg/kg, typically administered in divided doses over 2 to 5 days. 
- **Induction (Oral):** 1–5 mg/kg daily, adjusted based on patient tolerance.
- **Maintenance Schedules:** 
    1. 1–5 mg/kg orally daily.
    2. 10–15 mg/kg IV every 7 to 10 days.
    3. 3–5 mg/kg IV twice weekly.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Reduce the initial loading dose by one-third to one-half in patients with prior marrow-compromising treatments (radiotherapy, other cytotoxics) or bone marrow infiltration.

### Immunosuppressive Dosing [Australian]
- **Oral:** 1–3 mg/kg daily, depending on clinical response and toxicity.

### Administration Instructions
- **Hydration:** To prevent haemorrhagic cystitis, patients must increase fluid intake for 24 hours before, during, and at least 24 hours after administration. Frequent voiding is essential.
- **Oral Administration:** Tablets should generally be taken in the morning.

## 4. Contraindications
Cyclophosphamide is contraindicated in the following scenarios [Australian]:
- Known hypersensitivity to the drug.
- Presence of active infections (due to risk of fatal complications from immunosuppression).
- Existing cystitis, urinary outflow obstruction, or acute systemic/urinary infection.
- History of drug- or radiation-induced haemorrhagic cystitis.
- Severely depressed bone marrow function.
- First trimester of pregnancy.
- Within 4 to 8 days following major surgical procedures.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified as a formal "Boxed Warning" in the source, but the drug is noted as a highly toxic substance requiring specialized handling [Australian].
- **Haematological Toxicity:** Severe leucopoenia and neutropenia are dose-limiting and expected. Monitor WBC counts weekly; if the count falls below 3,000/mm³, daily or alternate-day monitoring is required.
- **Haemorrhagic Cystitis:** Sterile cystitis is a severe, potentially fatal risk. Monitor urinary sediment regularly for erythrocytes. Discontinue therapy if haemorrhagic cystitis occurs.
- **Secondary Malignancies:** Increased risk of bladder carcinoma, myeloproliferative, and lymphoproliferative malignancies, sometimes appearing years after treatment cessation.
- **Cardiotoxicity:** High doses (120–270 mg/kg) are associated with arrhythmias, congestive heart failure, and haemorrhagic myocarditis. Caution is required in patients with pre-existing heart disease.
- **Handling Precautions:** Personnel must be trained in cytotoxic handling. Pregnant staff must be excluded from working with the drug. Accidental contact requires immediate lavage with water or sodium bicarbonate.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Categorisation [Australian]
- **Very Common (>10%):** Alopecia (~30% with normal doses; near-universal with high doses), leucopoenia, neutropenia, nausea, and vomiting.
- **Common (1–10%):** Amenorrhoea, azoospermia, oligospermia.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Anaphylactic reactions, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, acute pancreatitis, and hepatic dysfunction.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** Maximum depression typically occurs 7–14 days after a large single dose.
- **Veno-occlusive Disease (VOD):** Observed in 15–50% of patients receiving high doses with busulfan or whole-body irradiation.
- **SIADH:** Syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion and associated hyponatraemia.
- **Secondary Neoplasia:** Particularly bladder cancer and leukaemias.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzyme Inducers (e.g., Barbiturates, Phenytoin):** May increase the conversion of cyclophosphamide to active/toxic metabolites, increasing toxicity [Australian].
- **Enzyme Inhibitors (e.g., Chloramphenicol, Imipramine):** May decrease the therapeutic effect by slowing activation [Australian].
- **Allopurinol / Thiazide Diuretics:** Increased risk of bone marrow depression [Australian].
- **Suxamethonium / Succinylcholine:** Cyclophosphamide inhibits cholinesterase activity, potentially leading to prolonged apnoea. Notify anaesthetists if used within 10 days of surgery [Australian].
- **Anthracyclines:** Potentiation of cardiotoxicity [Australian].
- **Grapefruit Juice:** May impair drug activation and reduce efficacy [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Cyclophosphamide is teratogenic and crosses the placenta. It should not be used in pregnancy (especially the first trimester) unless the benefit outweighs the high risk of foetal malformation (e.g., limb defects, cardiac defects).
- **Contraception:** Both men and women should use effective contraception during and for at least 3 months after stopping treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Cyclophosphamide is excreted in breast milk; breastfeeding must be terminated before starting therapy.
- **Fertility:** Often causes permanent sterility; interferes with oogenesis and spermatogenesis.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available in source document [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Use with caution; monitor closely for infection and haematological toxicity.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustment may be necessary as cyclophosphamide and its metabolites are excreted in the urine.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. Pre-existing liver dysfunction increases the risk of VOD.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A prodrug that requires activation by hepatic microsomal enzymes (mixed-function oxidases) to form active alkylating metabolites. These metabolites cross-link DNA, interfering with neoplastic growth and normal tissue regeneration.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well-absorbed from the GI tract.
    - **Distribution:** Both parent drug and metabolites cross the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic. Peak levels of active metabolites are reached 2–3 hours after administration.
    - **Excretion:** Serum half-life is approximately 4 hours; 3–30% is excreted unchanged in the urine.
