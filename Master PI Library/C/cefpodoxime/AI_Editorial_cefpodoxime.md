# Editorial Product Information: Cefpodoxime Proxetil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official PI and should not be used as the sole basis for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefpodoxime proxetil (a prodrug of the active metabolite, cefpodoxime).
- **Chemical Class:** Semi-synthetic, extended-spectrum third-generation cephalosporin antibiotic.
- **Brand Names:** Information provided pertains to **Cefpodoxime Proxetil Tablets, USP** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01DD13 (not explicitly in source but standard for the class).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 100 mg and 200 mg (expressed as active cefpodoxime activity) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Repackaged by RemedyRepack Inc. (Original manufacturer: Ascend Labs, LLC) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Cefpodoxime proxetil** is indicated for the treatment of mild to moderate infections caused by susceptible strains of microorganisms in the following conditions [US]:
- **Acute Otitis Media:** Caused by *S. pneumoniae*, *S. pyogenes*, *H. influenzae*, or *M. catarrhalis*.
- **Pharyngitis and/or Tonsillitis:** Caused by *S. pyogenes*.
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP):** Caused by *S. pneumoniae* or *H. influenzae*.
- **Acute Bacterial Exacerbation of Chronic Bronchitis:** Caused by *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae* (non-beta-lactamase-producing strains), or *M. catarrhalis*.
- **Uncomplicated Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** Caused by *S. aureus* or *S. pyogenes*.
- **Acute Maxillary Sinusitis:** Caused by *H. influenzae*, *S. pneumoniae*, and *M. catarrhalis*.
- **Uncomplicated Urinary Tract Infections (Cystitis):** Caused by *E. coli*, *K. pneumoniae*, *P. mirabilis*, or *S. saprophyticus*.
- **Uncomplicated Gonorrhea (Cervical, Urethral, and Anorectal):** Caused by *N. gonorrhoeae*. (Note: Anorectal indication currently approved for women only [US]).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents ≥12 years)
All doses are administered every 12 hours (Q12h) unless otherwise noted.
- **Pharyngitis/Tonsillitis:** 100 mg Q12h for 5 to 10 days.
- **Acute Maxillary Sinusitis:** 200 mg Q12h for 10 days.
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia:** 200 mg Q12h for 14 days.
- **Acute Bacterial Exacerbation of Chronic Bronchitis:** 200 mg Q12h for 10 days.
- **Uncomplicated Gonorrhea:** A single 200 mg dose.
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** 400 mg Q12h for 7 to 14 days.
- **Uncomplicated Urinary Tract Infection:** 100 mg Q12h for 7 days.

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Effect:** Tablets must be administered **with food** to enhance systemic absorption [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources, though clinical standard suggests taking as soon as remembered unless nearly time for the next dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Cefpodoxime proxetil** is contraindicated in patients with a documented hypersensitivity to **cefpodoxime** or any other member of the **cephalosporin** class of antibiotics [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reactions have been reported. Before initiation, inquiry should be made regarding prior reactions to cephalosporins, penicillins, or other allergens. There is documented cross-sensitivity (up to 10%) between beta-lactam antibiotics [US].
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents; severity may range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis. Discontinuation of therapy and appropriate management (e.g., fluid/electrolyte/protein supplementation, *C. difficile* specific antibiotics) may be required [US].
- **Antimicrobial Resistance:** Prescribing in the absence of a proven or strongly suspected bacterial infection increases the risk of drug-resistant bacteria development [US].
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi.
- **Seizure Potential:** Several cephalosporins have been implicated in triggering seizures, particularly in patients with renal impairment if the dose was not adjusted [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data (Multiple-Dose Trials, N=4696)
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhea (7%), Nausea (3.3%), Vulvovaginal infections (1.3%), Abdominal pain (1.2%), Headache (1.0%), Vaginal fungal infections (1.0%) |
| **Uncommon/Rare (<1%)** | Vomiting, dyspepsia, dizziness, insomnia, anxiety, rash, pruritus, myalgia, cough, epistaxis |

### Serious/Other Notable Effects
- **Dermatologic:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, erythema multiforme [US].
- **Hematologic:** Eosinophilia, leukopenia, neutropenia, positive Coombs’ test, prolonged PT/PTT [US].
- **Hepatic:** Transient increases in AST, ALT, GGT, and bilirubin; rare reports of acute liver injury [US].
- **Renal:** Increases in BUN and creatinine; rare reports of toxic nephropathy [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antacids/H2 Blockers:** High doses of antacids or H2 blockers (e.g., ranitidine) reduce peak plasma levels by 24% to 42% and the extent of absorption by 27% to 32% [US].
- **Probenecid:** Inhibits renal excretion, resulting in an approximately 31% increase in AUC and 20% increase in peak plasma levels [US].
- **Nephrotoxic Drugs:** While not noted with cefpodoxime alone, close monitoring of renal function is advised during co-administration with drugs of known nephrotoxic potential [US].
- **Laboratory Test Interactions:** May induce a positive direct Coombs’ test [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category B.**
    - No evidence of teratogenicity or embryocidal effects in animal studies (rats and rabbits). 
    - Because there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women, use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Cefpodoxime is excreted in human milk. In a study of three lactating women, milk levels reached up to 16% of serum levels 6 hours post-dose. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the mother's clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** No untoward effects on fertility were noted in rats at doses up to 2 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in infants **less than 2 months of age** have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required for geriatric patients with normal renal function. Pharmacokinetic parameters (except for a slight increase in half-life) remain similar to younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - In patients with severe impairment (**Creatinine Clearance <30 mL/min**), the dosing interval should be increased to **every 24 hours** [US].
    - Patients on hemodialysis should receive the dose **3 times per week** following dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is recommended for patients with cirrhosis (with or without ascites) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs). It is stable in the presence of many beta-lactamase enzymes [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is approximately 50% in the fasted state; increased by food.
    - **Distribution:** Protein binding ranges from 22% to 33%. Penetrates well into lung tissue, tonsil tissue, and skin blister fluid [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal in vivo metabolism; the proxetil ester is de-esterified in the GI tract to the active cefpodoxime.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily unchanged in the urine (approx. 29–33% of a dose within 12 hours) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Cystitis:** In two double-blind, randomized trials in the US, cefpodoxime demonstrated a total bacterial eradication rate of 82% (compared to 81% for other beta-lactam comparators). However, eradication rates were lower than those observed with some other classes of approved agents (e.g., fluoroquinolones) [US].
- **Acute Otitis Media:** In US studies, a 5-day regimen (5 mg/kg Q12h) showed a clinical success rate of 67%, comparable to cefixime (64%) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Keep the container tightly closed and replace the cap securely after each opening [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only).
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified in available sources (standard is Rx/Schedule 4 equivalent).
