## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed warnings: Not specified in available sources
- General warnings and precautions:
  - **Folic acid monotherapy is not appropriate for treating pernicious anemia or other megaloblastic anemias in which vitamin B12 deficiency is the underlying cause.** Supplementation with folic acid at doses exceeding 1.0 mg per day may mask vitamin B12 deficiency by correcting hematologic abnormalities while neurological manifestations continue to worsen.
  - **Biotin supplementation may interfere with certain laboratory assays**, including some cardiovascular diagnostic tests (such as troponin assays) and certain hormone assays, potentially yielding erroneous results. Patients should be advised to inform their healthcare provider of all prescription and over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, and dietary supplements they are taking, including biotin.
  - When prescribing this supplement to women who are pregnant, breastfeeding, or planning conception, the patient's overall medical condition and concomitant use of other medications, herbal products, and supplements should be taken into account.
  - This product may contain soy.
  - Keep this and all medications out of the reach of children.
- Monitoring requirements:
  - Be aware of the potential for folic acid supplementation to mask the hematologic signs of vitamin B12 deficiency; monitor for neurological signs and symptoms where clinically appropriate.
  - Be alert to the possibility of biotin interference when laboratory results appear inconsistent with the clinical presentation.
- Specific clinical precautions:
  - Not specified in available sources beyond those described above.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- No dedicated drug interactions section is provided in the available source documents.
- The following clinically relevant interactions are noted under Precautions in the source labeling:
  - **Folic acid and vitamin B12 deficiency:** Folic acid given alone is not appropriate therapy for megaloblastic anemias due to vitamin B12 deficiency, as it may correct hematologic parameters without addressing the underlying deficiency, allowing neurological damage to progress.
  - **Biotin and laboratory assays:** Biotin supplementation can interfere with certain laboratory immunoassays, including some cardiac troponin tests and hormone assays, potentially producing inaccurate results. Patients should inform all healthcare providers and laboratory personnel about their biotin intake.
