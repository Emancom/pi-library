# Editorial Product Information: clindamycin + tretinoin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **clindamycin + tretinoin**
- Chemical class:
  - Clindamycin: lincosamide antibiotic (semisynthetic derivative of lincomycin)
  - Tretinoin: topical retinoid (all-trans retinoic acid)
- ATC code: D10AF51 (anti‑acne preparations; clindamycin combinations) — as stated in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: topical dermatological use only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of the source PI(s): Not specified in available sources.
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Topical treatment of acne vulgaris when comedones, papules and pustules are present in patients 12 years of age and older.
- Consideration should be given to official guidance on appropriate use of antibacterial agents and acne management.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult and adolescent)
- Once daily, applied at night to the affected area.
- A small amount (guidance: a pea-sized amount sufficient to cover the entire face) should be dotted onto affected areas and gently spread to cover the entire treatment area.
- Treatment course: do not exceed 12 weeks of continuous use without careful clinical re-evaluation.
- Therapeutic improvement may take several weeks.

### Administration instructions
- For external dermatological use only; avoid eyes, eyelids, lips and nostrils.
- Patients should wash hands after application.
- Minimise exposure to sunlight; use appropriate sunscreen (SPF ≥ 30) and protective clothing (e.g. hat). Avoid sunlamps/sunbeds.
- Avoid application to abraded, eczematous or sensitive skin areas. In case of accidental ocular contact, irrigate with large amounts of water.

### Concomitant topical products and cleansing
- Do not apply concurrently at the same time as other topical preparations (including cosmetics) because of possible incompatibility with tretinoin.
- Exercise particular caution with keratolytic agents (e.g. sulfur, salicylic acid, benzoyl peroxide, resorcinol) and chemical abrasives; the effects of such peeling agents should subside before initiating therapy.
- Avoid strong-drying medicated cleansers and abrasive soaps during therapy.

### Missed dose
- If a dose is missed, wait and apply the next dose at the usual time. Do not double dose.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to clindamycin, tretinoin, any excipient of the product, or to lincomycin.
- History of regional enteritis (Crohn’s disease), ulcerative colitis, or a history of antibiotic-associated colitis.
- Personal or family history of skin cancer.
- History of acute eczemas, rosacea, or perioral dermatitis.
- Pustular and deep cystic nodular acne varieties (e.g. acne conglobate, acne fulminans).
- Pregnancy.
- Women planning a pregnancy.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Not for oral, ophthalmic, intranasal or intravaginal use.
- Use with caution in patients with atopic dermatitis (atopic subjects).
- Avoid contact with mucous membranes, and with abraded or eczematous skin; apply cautiously to sensitive areas.
- Application of amounts greater than recommended or more frequent application may cause marked local irritation (redness, stinging, discomfort); if severe irritation occurs, interrupt use or reduce frequency.
- Photosensitivity may occur; patients should minimise sun exposure and use sunscreen. Discontinue if severe sunburn with erythema and peeling occurs until recovery.
- Avoid concurrent use of oral and topical antibiotics, particularly differing chemical classes, where possible.
- Tretinoin increases skin permeability; this may enhance penetration of other topically applied medicines.
- Excipients: methyl hydroxybenzoate and propyl hydroxybenzoate may cause (possibly delayed) allergic reactions; butylated hydroxytoluene may cause local skin reactions or irritation to eyes/mucous membranes.

### Antibiotic-associated colitis (Clostridium difficile)
- Antibiotic‑associated colitis, including C. difficile-associated disease (CDAD), has been reported with topical clindamycin products. Although percutaneous absorption is generally low, if prolonged or significant diarrhoea or abdominal cramps occur, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate for C. difficile infection (stool testing for C. difficile and toxin, colonoscopy if indicated). Consider appropriate management for colitis.

### Resistance
- Long-term use of topical clindamycin alone may select for resistant organisms and cause overgrowth of non‑susceptible bacteria or fungi. Clindamycin resistance prevalence may vary geographically; local resistance data and official guidance on antibacterial use should be consulted.
- Cross-resistance may occur with erythromycin and lincomycin.
- When applied together, tretinoin may increase follicular penetration of clindamycin; combination therapy may reduce but does not eliminate the risk of resistance.
- If clinically significant resistance or overgrowth occurs, discontinue therapy and choose an alternative treatment.

### Interactions requiring caution
- Use with neuromuscular blocking agents: clindamycin has neuromuscular blocking properties and may enhance effects of such agents—use with caution.
- Concomitant use with erythromycin-containing products: in vitro antagonism has been observed; simultaneous use is not recommended.
- Vitamin K antagonists (e.g. warfarin, acenocoumarol, fluindione): increased PT/INR and/or bleeding have been reported with clindamycin; monitor coagulation tests frequently if co‑administered.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs and symptoms of antibiotic-associated colitis (persistent diarrhoea).
- If a patient on a vitamin K antagonist receives topical clindamycin, check PT/INR frequently.
- Pregnancy avoidance counselling and contraception verification in women of childbearing potential (see Section 9).
- No specific laboratory test effects reported; routine laboratory monitoring not specified.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Erythromycin: in vitro antagonism observed between clindamycin and erythromycin; co-application is not recommended.
- Neuromuscular blocking agents: clindamycin may enhance neuromuscular blockade—use caution and monitor respiratory/muscle function.
- Tretinoin increases skin permeability, potentially increasing systemic/topical absorption of concurrently applied topical agents.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Systemic pharmacokinetic interactions are unlikely with topical administration because systemic absorption is generally low; however, when used with vitamin K antagonists (e.g. warfarin), increased PT/INR and bleeding have been reported with clindamycin—monitor coagulation closely if co‑administered.
- Concomitant topical medications, strong-drying cleansers, products with high alcohol concentration, corticosteroids and keratolytic agents may interact or increase irritation; avoid or use with caution.

### Clinical management notes
- Avoid simultaneous use of other topical acne treatments (keratolytics, abrasives) until effects of prior peeling agents have subsided.
- Monitor PT/INR in patients on vitamin K antagonists when topical clindamycin is used.
- If co‑administration with neuromuscular blockers is necessary, observe for increased neuromuscular blockade.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Contraindicated in pregnancy and in women planning pregnancy.
- Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 1 month after discontinuation.
- Animal data: topical administration in rabbits at high doses showed no maternal or foetal toxicity at doses up to specified multiples of anticipated clinical exposure (source reports rabbit dermal data); systemically administered tretinoin is a known human teratogen and oral retinoids are teratogenic in animals.
- Systemic exposure from proper topical use is generally low, but factors (e.g. damaged skin barrier or excessive use) may increase systemic absorption and risk.
- Pregnancy category (region-specific):
  - [Australian] Pregnancy Category D (drugs which have caused, are suspected to have caused or may be expected to cause an increased incidence of human foetal malformations or irreversible damage).

- Other regional pregnancy category systems: Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- It is not known whether tretinoin or topically applied clindamycin are excreted in human breast milk following topical use.
- Clindamycin has been found in breast milk after oral/parenteral administration; retinoids and metabolites are known to be secreted in breast milk after systemic administration.
- Recommendation: do not use in women who are breastfeeding.

### Fertility
- Animal data: topical administration in female rabbits up to high multiples of anticipated clinical exposure did not affect fertility in the reported study.
- Human fertility data for topical use: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Not recommended for children under 12 years of age.
- Efficacy and safety in adolescents (12–17 years) have been studied; adverse event profile similar to adults.

### Elderly use
- Safety and effectiveness in patients older than 65 years have not been established.

### Renal impairment
- Due to low systemic exposure after topical administration, moderate renal impairment is not expected to produce clinically significant systemic exposure; clindamycin and tretinoin serum concentrations after topical use have not been studied in renal impairment. In severe renal disease, individual assessment is advisable.

### Hepatic impairment
- Due to low systemic exposure after topical administration, moderate hepatic impairment is not expected to produce clinically significant systemic exposure; serum concentrations have not been studied in hepatic disease. Exercise individual clinical judgement in severe hepatic impairment.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Clindamycin: a lincosamide antibiotic that binds to the 50S bacterial ribosomal subunit and inhibits protein synthesis; bacteriostatic against susceptible organisms and has anti‑inflammatory properties. Clindamycin phosphate is a hydrolyzable prodrug converted to active clindamycin in vivo.
- Tretinoin: a topical retinoid with comedolytic, anti‑comedogenic and anti‑inflammatory effects. It reduces cohesion of follicular epithelial cells, increases cell turnover, promotes extrusion of comedones, and suppresses toll‑like receptor-mediated inflammation.
- Combination: tretinoin may increase follicular penetration of clindamycin, allowing complementary targeting of abnormal keratinisation, P. acnes proliferation and inflammation.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Percutaneous absorption: systemic exposure to clindamycin and tretinoin following recommended topical application is generally low/clinically negligible as reported in available sources.
- Detailed pharmacokinetic parameters (Cmax, Tmax, half‑life, volume of distribution, clearance) after topical administration: Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism and elimination following topical use: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Clinical anti‑acne effect arises from combined antimicrobial/anti‑inflammatory action of clindamycin and comedolytic/anti‑inflammatory effects of tretinoin.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Three randomized, double‑blind clinical trials including a total of 4,550 patients with acne vulgaris (inflammatory and non‑inflammatory lesions) were conducted comparing the combination product to clindamycin alone, tretinoin alone, and vehicle. Treatment was applied once daily for 12 weeks. Patients enrolled had 20–50 inflammatory facial lesions, 20–100 non‑inflammatory lesions, two or fewer nodules, and no cysts.

Key efficacy outcomes (median percent reduction in lesion counts at Week 12; meta‑analysis across studies):
- Inflammatory lesions (median percent reduction): combination 52.6%; clindamycin 46.4%; tretinoin 42.9%; vehicle 25.0%.
- Non‑inflammatory lesions (median percent reduction): combination 43.8%; clindamycin 27.5%; tretinoin 36.2%; vehicle 23.0%.
- Total lesions (median percent reduction): combination 46.3%; clindamycin 33.9%; tretinoin 39.6%; vehicle 22.2%.

Global severity outcomes:
- In two pivotal trials, the proportion of subjects judged "clear or almost clear" at Week 12 was higher with the combination versus vehicle and in some comparisons versus monotherapy; statistical significance varied by study and endpoint. In one trial comparing the combination to clindamycin alone, a higher proportion of patients achieved "clear, almost clear or at least 2‑grade improvement" with the combination (38% vs 32%; p = 0.002).

Adolescent subgroup (12–17 years):
- Median percent reductions at Week 12 were similar to the overall population; combination therapy produced greater median reductions in inflammatory, non‑inflammatory and total lesions versus monotherapy/vehicle in meta‑analysis.

- Safety profile in trials: most adverse events were mild and not necessarily considered treatment-related. Local skin reactions (erythema, dryness, burning, peeling) were observed and were generally manageable with temporary interruption or reduction in application frequency.

## 13. Overdose
- Topical overuse may cause marked local irritation, redness, peeling and discomfort. Management: discontinue product, gently wash the area with mild soap and lukewarm water; resume therapy after several days if appropriate.
- In cases of significant excess topical absorption or accidental ingestion, systemic clindamycin effects (abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea) and tretinoin‑related risks (including teratogenic risk if ingestion by women of childbearing potential) are possible. If accidental ingestion occurs, discontinue use and manage symptomatically; perform pregnancy testing in women of childbearing potential where appropriate.
- For suspected poisoning/overdose management, contact local poison control services as required.
