# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for **levocarnitine** injection. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should consult the local regulatory document to ensure compliance with regional legal and clinical requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport (amino acid derivative).
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   Intravenous injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    *   Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    *   **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    *   **Maintenance:** Daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    *   **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range 40–50 µmol/L). Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
*   Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
*   **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue treatment immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been reported to cause seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended for metabolic disorders, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine levels (target range: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
*   **Renal Impairment (Oral Precaution):** While this document covers the injection, it is noted that chronic high-dose **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Common (>1%):** Transient nausea, vomiting, body odor, and gastritis.
*   **Clinical Trial Data (ESRD Patients):** In placebo-controlled trials, the following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Cough, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus |
| **Urogenital** | Kidney failure |

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Category:** Not assigned (Descriptive). Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Clinicians should weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
*   **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, S. cerevisiae, S. pombe) were negative. No long-term animal studies exist for carcinogenic potential [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg (see Section 3).
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 (ESRD dosing) and Section 5 (TMA/TMAO accumulation precaution).
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
*   **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly post-dialysis), mean trough plasma levocarnitine increased from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While levocarnitine concentrations were successfully increased, the effects on clinical outcomes in this population have not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
*   **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
