# Editorial Product Information: Carteolol Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of carteolol hydrochloride ophthalmic solution, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis of available regulatory data and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Carteolol hydrochloride (a nonselective beta-adrenoceptor blocking agent).
- **Chemical Class:** Quinolone derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Carteolol Hydrochloride Ophthalmic Solution USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01ED05.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Ophthalmic solution 1% (10 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sandoz Inc.; Alcon Laboratories, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Intraocular Pressure (IOP) Reduction:** Indicated for the reduction of elevated intraocular pressure in patients with chronic open-angle glaucoma and intraocular hypertension [US].
- **Combination Therapy:** May be used as monotherapy or in combination with other IOP-lowering agents [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose:** One drop in the affected eye(s) twice daily [US].
- **Concomitant Therapy:** If target IOP is not achieved, clinicians may consider the addition of pilocarpine (or other miotics), epinephrine, dipivefrin, or systemic carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (e.g., acetazolamide) [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** To prevent contamination, do not touch the dropper tip to the eyelids or surrounding surfaces. The bottle should remain tightly closed when not in use and protected from light [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Respiratory Disease:** Bronchial asthma; a history of bronchial asthma; severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) [US].
- **Cardiac Disorders:** Sinus bradycardia; second- and third-degree atrioventricular (AV) block; overt cardiac failure; cardiogenic shock [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Systemic Absorption:** Like other topically applied ophthalmic agents, carteolol is absorbed systemically. Adverse reactions associated with systemic beta-blockers may occur, including severe respiratory and cardiac reactions [US].
- **Cardiac Failure:** Beta-blockade may precipitate more severe failure in patients with diminished myocardial contractility. At the first sign of cardiac failure, the drug should be discontinued [US].
- **Non-Allergic Bronchospasm:** Use with caution in patients with chronic bronchitis or emphysema, as beta-blockers may inhibit bronchodilation produced by endogenous/exogenous catecholamines [US].
- **Major Surgery:** Gradual withdrawal of beta-blockers prior to elective surgery is controversial but may be appropriate, as blockade impairs the heart's ability to respond to reflex stimuli. If necessary, effects can be reversed with beta-agonists (e.g., isoproterenol, dopamine) during surgery [US].
- **Diabetes Mellitus:** Beta-blockers may mask signs and symptoms of acute hypoglycemia (particularly tachycardia) and should be used with caution in patients with spontaneous hypoglycemia or labile diabetes [US].
- **Thyrotoxicosis:** May mask clinical signs of hyperthyroidism. Abrupt withdrawal should be avoided to prevent a thyroid storm [US].
- **Muscle Weakness:** Potential to potentiate symptoms consistent with myasthenia (e.g., diplopia, ptosis, generalized weakness) [US].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Patients with a history of severe atopy or anaphylaxis may be more reactive to allergen challenge while on beta-blockers and may be unresponsive to standard doses of epinephrine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Ocular Effects (~25%):** Transient eye irritation, burning, tearing, and conjunctival hyperemia/edema [US].
- **Occasional Ocular Effects:** Blurred or cloudy vision, photophobia, decreased night vision, ptosis, blepharoconjunctivitis, abnormal corneal staining, and decreased corneal sensitivity [US].
- **Systemic Effects (Occasional):**
    - **Cardiovascular:** Bradycardia, hypotension, arrhythmia, palpitations, syncope, heart block, congestive heart failure, and cerebral ischemia/CVA [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Dyspnea, bronchospasm (primarily in patients with pre-existing disease), and respiratory failure [US].
    - **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Headache, dizziness, insomnia, and depression [US].
    - **Other:** Asthenia, nausea, localized/generalized rash (hypersensitivity), sinusitis, and taste perversion [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Systemic Beta-blockers:** Use with caution in patients already receiving oral beta-adrenergic blocking agents due to potential additive effects on systemic blockade [US].
- **Catecholamine-depleting Agents:** Close observation is required if used with drugs such as reserpine, as additive effects may result in hypotension, marked bradycardia, vertigo, or syncope [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C [US]:** Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed increased resorptions and decreased fetal weights at doses significantly higher (>1000x) than the human dose. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Carteolol is excreted in animal breast milk. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk; caution is advised when administering to nursing mothers [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in rat or mouse studies at doses up to 150 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, beta-blocker precautions regarding cardiac and respiratory function are particularly relevant [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A nonselective beta-adrenergic antagonist with associated intrinsic sympathomimetic activity (ISA) and negligible membrane-stabilizing activity. It reduces IOP by a mechanism that has not been definitively demonstrated but involves lowering ocular hypertensive effects [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces both normal and elevated IOP without significantly affecting corneal sensitivity, tear secretion, or pupil size [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Systemic plasma levels were not detected following ocular instillation in the source clinical trials [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** In controlled domestic trials lasting 1.5 to 3 months, twice-daily administration produced a median IOP reduction of 22% to 25% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 15°C to 25°C (59°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Special Precautions:** Protect from light [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription drug) [US].
