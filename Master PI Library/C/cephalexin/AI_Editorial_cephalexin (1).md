# Editorial Product Information: cephalexin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **cephalexin**
- Chemical class: Semisynthetic cephalosporin (beta-lactam) antibiotic
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first approval/registration: 
  - 250 mg: 15 September 2000 [Australian]
  - 500 mg: 20 February 2007 [Australian]
- Date of last revision/update of source PI: 12 July 2024 [Australian]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
Cefalexin is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by susceptible strains of the listed organisms. Clinicians should obtain appropriate cultures and susceptibility testing prior to and during therapy where indicated.

- Respiratory tract infections caused by:
  - Streptococcus pneumoniae
  - Group A beta-haemolytic Streptococci (penicillin remains usual drug of choice for streptococcal infections; cefalexin can eradicate streptococci from the nasopharynx but data are limited regarding prevention of rheumatic fever)
- Bacterial sinusitis caused by:
  - Streptococci
  - Streptococcus pneumoniae
  - Staphylococcus aureus (methicillin-sensitive strains only)
- Otitis media due to:
  - Streptococcus pneumoniae
  - Staphylococci
- Skin and skin structure infections caused by:
  - Staphylococci and/or Streptococci
- Genitourinary tract infections (including acute prostatitis) caused by:
  - Escherichia coli
  - Proteus mirabilis
  - Klebsiella species

Notes:
- Cefalexin is not indicated for infections of the brain and spinal column; effectiveness in these sites has not been established.
- Local resistance patterns should be considered when selecting therapy.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
General principles:
- Intended for oral use.
- Cefalexin is acid-stable and may be given without regard to meals.
- Initiate appropriate culture and susceptibility testing prior to and during therapy where indicated.
- If daily doses greater than 4 g are required, consider switching to appropriate parenteral cephalosporins.
- Twice-daily dosing is not recommended when total daily dose exceeds 1 g.

Adults
- Usual adult dose: 250 mg every 6 hours (total 1 g/day).
- Dose range: 1–4 g daily in divided doses.
- For streptococcal pharyngitis/tonsillitis, mild uncomplicated urinary tract infections, and skin/soft-tissue infections: 500 mg every 12 hours may be used.
- For more severe infections or infections due to less susceptible organisms, larger doses within the 1–4 g/day range may be required.

Children
- Typical total daily dose: 25–50 mg/kg/day divided into multiple doses.
- For streptococcal pharyngitis (patients >1 year), tonsillitis, mild uncomplicated urinary tract infection, and skin/soft-tissue infections: total daily dose may be divided and administered every 12 hours.
- For otitis media: 75–100 mg/kg/day in four divided doses is recommended based on clinical studies.
- In severe infections, the recommended dosage may be doubled.
- Specific example dosing tables for oral suspension concentrations and weight bands were provided in the source PI. Exact suspension volumes/dosing conversions are not reproduced here; use available product-specific dosing instructions.

Administration instructions
- Oral administration only.
- May be taken with or without food.
- If signs of allergy occur, discontinue and treat appropriately.

Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to cephalosporins, including cefalexin.
- History of major allergic reaction to penicillins (experience of major penicillin allergy is listed as a contraindication).
- No other jurisdiction‑specific contraindications were specified in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity:
  - Prior to initiating therapy, evaluate history of hypersensitivity to cephalosporins, penicillins, or other allergens.
  - There is partial cross‑allergenicity between penicillins and cephalosporins; severe reactions including anaphylaxis have occurred with both classes.
  - If an allergic reaction occurs, discontinue cefalexin and treat with standard measures (e.g., adrenaline, antihistamines, corticosteroids) as clinically indicated.
- Clostridioides difficile–associated disease:
  - Pseudomembranous colitis has been reported with virtually all broad‑spectrum antibiotics, including cephalosporins. Consider the diagnosis in any patient who develops diarrhea or colitis during or after antibiotic therapy (may occur several weeks after cessation).
  - Mild cases may respond to discontinuation of the antibiotic; moderate to severe cases require specific therapy against C. difficile and supportive care (fluids, electrolytes, protein replacement). Avoid anti‑peristaltic agents (e.g., opiates, diphenoxylate/atropine) as they may worsen the course.
- Superinfection:
  - Prolonged therapy may result in overgrowth of non‑susceptible organisms; monitor and manage as appropriate.
- Neurotoxicity:
  - Cases of encephalopathy, seizures, and myoclonus have been reported with cephalosporins. Risk factors include advanced age, renal impairment, pre‑existing central nervous system disorders, and intravenous administration.
  - Consider withdrawal of cefalexin if neurotoxicity is suspected. Anticonvulsant therapy may be used if clinically indicated.
- Renal impairment:
  - Administer with caution in markedly impaired renal function; safe dosage may be lower than typically recommended. Careful clinical and laboratory monitoring is advised.
- Surgical considerations:
  - Indicated surgical procedures should be performed in conjunction with antibiotic therapy when appropriate.
- Severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCAR):
  - Stevens–Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS), acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP), and symmetrical drug‑related intertriginous and flexural exanthema (SDRIFE; "baboon syndrome") have been reported with beta‑lactam antibiotics. Discontinue cefalexin immediately if SCAR is suspected and consider alternative therapy.
- Laboratory test interference:
  - Quantitative urinary protein estimation using strong acids may be misleading due to precipitation of cefalexin.
  - False‑positive urine glucose tests may occur with Benedict's or Fehling's reagents or Clinitest®.
  - Positive direct Coombs' tests have been reported; this may affect transfusion cross‑matching and neonatal Coombs testing if the mother received cephalosporins before delivery.

Monitoring requirements
- Culture and susceptibility testing before and during therapy when indicated.
- Renal function tests when indicated.
- Monitor for signs of C. difficile infection in patients with diarrhea.
- In overdose or severe reactions, monitor haematological, renal, hepatic functions and coagulation status until stable.

Specific clinical precautions
- Exercise caution in penicillin‑sensitive patients due to potential cross‑reactivity.
- Consider alternative feeding arrangements for infants if the breastfeeding mother is receiving cefalexin (see Section 9).

## 8. Drug Interactions
Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Probenecid: Inhibits renal excretion of cephalexin (as with other beta‑lactams); coadministration reduces cephalexin renal clearance.
- Metformin: In healthy subjects given single doses of cefalexin (500 mg) and metformin, metformin Cmax and AUC increased by an average of 34% and 24%, respectively, and metformin renal clearance decreased by ~14%. The interaction with multiple‑dose administration has not been studied. Administration of a cephalosporin to a metformin‑treated patient may increase systemic exposure to metformin. Monitor for metformin‑related adverse effects and consider dose adjustment where appropriate.

Pharmacodynamic interactions
- No specific pharmacodynamic interactions detailed in the available sources beyond those related to altered antibiotic spectrum/resistance and potentiation of adverse effects with other nephrotoxic or neurotoxic agents (not specifically quantified in sources).

Clinically significant interactions — management notes
- When coadministered with probenecid, expect reduced renal clearance of cefalexin; dose adjustments may be required though specific adjustment recommendations are not provided in the available sources.
- When coadministered with metformin, monitor for increased metformin exposure and potential for metformin‑related adverse effects; multiple‑dose interaction effects are not defined.
- Use caution when combining with other agents that may predispose to seizures or neurotoxicity in at‑risk patients.

Jurisdiction‑specific interactions
- Not specified in available sources beyond the above.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
Pregnancy
- Effects on fertility: Not specified in available sources.
- Pregnancy category/system:
  - Pregnancy category: Category A [Australian] — laboratory animal and clinical experience show no evidence of teratogenicity; nonetheless, use with caution during pregnancy.
- Clinical recommendation: Administer during pregnancy only when clearly needed and after assessment of risks and benefits.

Breastfeeding
- Cefalexin is excreted in human milk. Caution is advised when administering to breastfeeding mothers; consider alternative feeding arrangements for the infant if clinically appropriate.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
Paediatric use
- Dosing: See Section 4 — typical total daily dose 25–50 mg/kg/day divided; otitis media 75–100 mg/kg/day divided into four doses; in certain indications dosing may be given twice daily as specified for weight bands in the source PI.
- Safety data: The source PI states "Paediatric use: No data available" for certain data elements; dosing guidance is provided but detailed paediatric safety/clinical trial data are not specified in available sources.

Elderly
- The source PI states "Use in the elderly: No data available." Exercise caution due to increased risk of neurotoxicity and possible reduced renal function; monitor renal function and consider dose adjustment although specific recommendations are not provided.

Renal impairment
- Cefalexin is primarily excreted unchanged by the kidney (glomerular filtration and tubular secretion). Administer with caution in patients with markedly impaired renal function; safe dosages may be lower than usual and clinical/laboratory monitoring is recommended.
- Specific dosing adjustments by creatinine clearance or eGFR were not provided in the available sources.

Hepatic impairment
- Specific dosing adjustments in hepatic impairment are not provided in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
Mechanism of action
- **Cephalexin** is bactericidal by inhibition of bacterial cell wall synthesis (beta‑lactam mechanism).

Pharmacodynamics
- Active in vitro against: beta‑haemolytic Streptococci, Staphylococci (including coagulase‑positive, coagulase‑negative, and penicillinase‑producing strains), Streptococcus (Diplococcus) pneumoniae, Escherichia coli, Proteus mirabilis, and Klebsiella species.
- Not active against most Enterobacter spp., Morganella morganii, Proteus vulgaris, Pseudomonas spp., or Acinetobacter calcoaceticus. Many Staphylococci exhibit cross‑resistance between cephalexin and methicillin‑type antibiotics.
- Susceptibility testing guidance and breakpoints:
  - Zone diameter (cephalothin discs) / MIC breakpoints:
    - Susceptible: zone ≥ 18 mm — MIC ≤ 8 μg/mL
    - Intermediate: zone 15–17 mm — MIC 1–16 μg/mL
    - Resistant: zone ≤ 14 mm — MIC > 16 μg/mL
  - Use standardised laboratory methods and quality control strains.

Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Rapidly and well absorbed after oral administration; acid‑stable and may be given without regard to meals.
- Peak serum concentrations: After single doses, mean peak serum levels at ~1 hour: 250 mg → ~9 μg/mL; 500 mg → ~18 μg/mL; 1 g → ~32 μg/mL. Measurable levels persist at ~6 hours post‑dose.
- Distribution: Not fully detailed in available sources.
- Metabolism: Not specified in available sources.
- Elimination: Primarily excreted unchanged in the urine by glomerular filtration and tubular secretion; over 90% excreted unchanged within 8 hours. Reported peak urinary concentrations (approximate) following single doses: 250 mg → ~1,000 μg/mL; 500 mg → ~2,200 μg/mL; 1 g → ~5,000 μg/mL.

Preclinical safety data
- Genotoxicity: Not specified in available sources.
- Carcinogenicity: Not specified in available sources.
