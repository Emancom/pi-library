# Editorial Product Information: Ceftazidime

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory and clinical information into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Ceftazidime (as pentahydrate).
*   **Chemical Class:** Third-generation cephalosporin antibiotic.
*   **Brand Names:** Ceftazidime-AFT [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** J01DD02.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Powder for injection in 1 g and 2 g vials [Australian].
*   **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or deep Intramuscular (IM) injection.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Ceftazidime** is indicated for the treatment of single and mixed infections caused by susceptible aerobic organisms, particularly where resistance to other antimicrobials is suspected or documented. It serves as an alternative to aminoglycosides in pseudomonal infections to avoid potential toxicity.

Specific [Australian-approved] indications include:
*   **Severe Systemic Infections:** Septicaemia, neonatal sepsis, bacteraemia, and infections in intensive care patients (e.g., infected burns).
*   **Respiratory Tract Infections:** Pneumonia, broncho-pneumonia, infected pleurisy, bronchiectasis, and bronchitis.
*   **Ear, Nose, and Throat Infections:** Otitis media and mastoiditis.
*   **Urinary Tract Infections (UTI):** Acute and chronic pyelonephritis, cystitis, urethritis, and infections associated with renal stones.
*   **Skin and Soft Tissue Infections:** Erysipelas, abscesses, cellulitis, infected burns/wounds, and mastitis.
*   **Gastrointestinal and Abdominal Infections:** Intra-abdominal abscesses and enterocolitis.
*   **Bone and Joint Infections:** Osteitis, osteomyelitis, septic arthritis, and infected bursitis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
Dosage is determined by the severity of infection, organism sensitivity, and the patient's age, weight, and renal status.

### General Dosing (Adults)
*   **Standard Range:** 1 g to 6 g daily, administered in divided doses every 8 or 12 hours.
*   **UTI and less severe infections:** 500 mg to 1 g every 12 hours.
*   **Moderate Infections:** 1 g every 8 hours or 2 g every 12 hours.
*   **Severe/Life-threatening Infections:** 2 g every 8 or 12 hours.
*   **Note:** Doses exceeding 1 g must be administered intravenously.

### Paediatric Dosing
*   **Infants and Children (>12 months):** 25 to 100 mg/kg/day in 2 or 3 divided doses (max 6 g/day). Doses up to the maximum may be used in cystic fibrosis or immunocompromised states.
*   **Neonates and Infants (<12 months):** 25 to 100 mg/kg/day administered in 2 divided doses.

### Administration Instructions
*   **Reconstitution:** Vials are supplied under a vacuum with carbon dioxide (CO2) as a protective gas. Reconstitution results in the release of CO2, creating positive pressure within the vial. 
*   **Diluents:** Water for Injections or 0.5% Lignocaine (for IM use only, provided no hypersensitivity exists).
*   **Compatibility:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride, 5% Dextrose, and Hartmann’s Solution. 
*   **Important:** Do **not** mix with aminoglycosides in the same syringe or giving set. Flush lines between ceftazidime and vancomycin to avoid precipitation.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to cephalosporin antibiotics.
*   History of major allergy to penicillins (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema, or urticaria) [Australian].
*   Hypersensitivity to Lignocaine (if used as a diluent for IM injection).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Careful inquiry regarding previous beta-lactam allergy is essential. Cross-reactivity may occur. Discontinue immediately if an allergic reaction occurs.
*   **Clostridium difficile-Associated Disease (CDAD):** Ranging from mild diarrhoea to fatal pseudomembranous colitis. Avoid drugs that delay peristalsis (e.g., opiates) if CDAD is suspected.
*   **Neurotoxicity:** Risk of encephalopathy, seizures, and myoclonus, particularly in elderly patients or those with renal impairment when doses are not appropriately adjusted.
*   **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** Including SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue if suspected.
*   **CNS Infections:** Ceftazidime achieves low concentrations in the CSF; use for meningitis or brain abscess is not currently advised [Australian].
*   **Resistance:** Resistance may develop during therapy, particularly with *Enterobacter* species.
*   **Sodium Content:** Contains approximately 52 mg (2.3 mEq) of sodium per gram of ceftazidime.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally infrequent.

| System Organ Class | Common (1–10%) | Uncommon to Very Rare (<1%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Local** | Phlebitis/thrombophlebitis (IV); Pain/inflammation (IM) | |
| **Hypersensitivity** | Pruritus, rash (maculopapular/urticarial), fever | Anaphylaxis, angioedema, SJS, TEN, Erythema multiforme |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain | Oral thrush, colitis, pseudomembranous colitis |
| **Neurological** | | Headache, dizziness, paraesthesia, bad taste, seizures/coma (in renal failure) |
| **Haematological** | Eosinophilia, positive Coombs’ test, thrombocytosis | Leucopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, haemolytic anaemia |
| **Hepatic** | Transient rise in enzymes (SGOT, SGPT, LDH, GGT, Alk Phos) | |
| **Renal** | | Transient elevations in BUN/serum creatinine |

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Chloramphenicol:** May be antagonistic in vitro; clinical significance is unknown but concurrent use should be considered with caution.
*   **Probenecid:** Unlike many other cephalosporins, concurrent oral probenecid does not significantly affect the serum levels or renal recovery of ceftazidime [Australian].
*   **Vancomycin:** Physical incompatibility; may precipitate in solution. Lines should be flushed between administrations.
*   **Sodium Bicarbonate:** Not recommended as a diluent due to potential instability.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. Safety in human pregnancy is not established. Animal studies show no evidence of teratogenicity. Use only if benefits clearly outweigh risks.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Ceftazidime is excreted in human milk in low concentrations. Caution is advised; use only if the expected benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the infant.
*   **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric Use
Effective in neonates for susceptible organisms. Note that the half-life in neonates can be 3–4 times longer than in adults.

### Elderly Use
Dosage should be adjusted based on renal function due to naturally reduced clearance in this population.

### Renal Impairment
Dosage must be reduced in patients with a GFR <50 mL/min to prevent neurotoxicity. 

**Recommended Maintenance Doses in Renal Insufficiency:**
*   **GFR 50-31 mL/min:** 1.0 g every 12 hours.
*   **GFR 30-16 mL/min:** 1.0 g every 24 hours.
*   **GFR 15-6 mL/min:** 0.5 g every 24 hours.
*   **GFR <5 mL/min:** 0.5 g every 48 hours.
*   **Haemodialysis:** Half-life is reduced to ~3 hours; the maintenance dose should be repeated after each dialysis period.

### Hepatic Impairment
No specific dose adjustment is required, but monitoring of hepatic function is advised if dysfunction exists. Avoid Lignocaine as a diluent in severe liver disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to target proteins. It is highly stable to most clinically significant beta-lactamases.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Negligible oral absorption; requires parenteral administration.
    *   **Distribution:** Widely distributed to bone, heart, bile, and soft tissues. Low CSF penetration. Protein binding is low (~10%).
    *   **Metabolism:** Not metabolised in the body.
    *   **Elimination:** Excreted 80–90% unchanged in the urine via glomerular filtration. Half-life is ~1.8 hours in adults with normal renal function.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   Not specified in available sources. The Australian PI notes that clinical trial experience indicates the drug is generally well tolerated, but specific pivotal trial data was not detailed.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Powder:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light.
*   **Reconstituted Solution:** Use immediately where possible. Potency is retained for 12 hours if refrigerated (2–8°C).
*   **Infusion Fluids:** In specific compatible fluids (e.g., 0.9% NaCl), solutions may be stored for 24 hours if refrigerated (2–8°C).
