# Editorial Product Information: Cyproheptadine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of cyproheptadine for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cyproheptadine hydrochloride (as sesquihydrate)
- **Chemical Class:** Serotonin and histamine antagonist (piperidine derivative)
- **Brand Names:** **PERIACTIN** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** R06AX02 (Antihistamines for systemic use)
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 4 mg tablets (white, flat, circular, bevel-edged, marked 'P4' on one side and scored on the other) [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
Cyproheptadine is indicated for the following:

- **Anti-allergic and Antipruritic Therapy:** Treatment of acute and chronic allergies and pruritic conditions, including:
  - Dermatitis (including neurodermatitis), eczema, and eczematoid dermatitis.
  - Hay fever, seasonal rhinitis, perennial allergic rhinitis, and vasomotor rhinitis.
  - Allergic conjunctivitis (due to inhalant allergens/foods), urticaria, and angioneurotic oedema.
  - Mild local allergic reactions to insect bites, drug/serum reactions, and dermatographism.
  - Anogenital pruritus and pruritus associated with chickenpox.
- **Adjunctive Anaphylaxis Management:** Used as therapy for anaphylactic reactions after acute manifestations have been controlled by adrenaline and other standard measures.
- **Vascular Headache and Migraine:** Prophylaxis or treatment of migraine and histamine cephalalgia [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The dosage should be individualised. Due to a duration of effect lasting four to six hours, daily requirements are typically divided into three doses.
- **Therapeutic Range:** 4 mg to 20 mg daily.
- **Initial Dose:** 4 mg three times daily.
- **Maintenance:** Most patients require 12 mg to 16 mg daily.
- **Maximum Dose:** 32 mg per day.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Migraine and Vascular Headache:**
  - **Initial:** 4 mg immediately. If necessary, repeat after 30 minutes.
  - **Acute Limit:** Do not exceed 8 mg in a 4-to-6-hour period.
  - **Maintenance:** 4 mg every 4 to 6 hours for responsive patients.

### Administration Instructions
Tablets are administered orally and may be taken with or without food.

### Missed Dose Instructions
Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Age:** Contraindicated in children under 6 years of age (due to risks of fatal respiratory depression and CNS events) [Australian].
- **Lactation:** Contraindicated in nursing mothers.
- **Acute Asthma:** Should not be used to treat an acute asthmatic attack.
- **Concurrent Therapy:** Use with Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs).
- **Underlying Conditions:**
  - Angle-closure glaucoma.
  - Stenosing peptic ulcer.
  - Symptomatic prostatic hypertrophy or bladder neck obstruction.
  - Pyloroduodenal obstruction.
- **Patient Status:** Elderly or debilitated patients [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known sensitivity to cyproheptadine or drugs of similar chemical structure.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Respiratory Health:** Antihistamines must not be used to treat lower respiratory tract symptoms, including acute asthma.
- **Anticholinergic Effects:** Due to its atropine-like action, use with caution in patients with:
  - History of bronchial asthma.
  - Increased intraocular pressure.
  - Hyperthyroidism.
  - Cardiovascular disease or hypertension.
- **CNS Depression:** May impair mental alertness. Patients should be warned against driving or operating machinery. Drowsiness may persist into the following day [Australian].
- **Paediatric Risks:** Overdosage in children can lead to hallucinations, CNS depression, convulsions, respiratory/cardiac arrest, and death. Conversely, young children may occasionally experience excitation.
- **Blood Dyscrasias:** Rare instances of blood dyscrasias have been reported with prolonged therapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequent adverse effects are **drowsiness** and **somnolence**, which often diminish after 3–4 days of continuous use.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, disturbed coordination, tremor, irritability, paraesthesia, neuritis, convulsions, headache. |
| **Psychiatric** | Confusion, restlessness, excitation, aggressive behaviour (especially in children <6 years), insomnia, hallucinations. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Dryness of mouth, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, epigastric distress. |
| **Hepato-biliary** | Cholestasis, hepatic failure, hepatitis, jaundice. |
| **Cardiac/Vascular** | Palpitation, tachycardia, extrasystoles, hypotension. |
| **Respiratory** | Thickening of bronchial secretions, dryness of nose/throat, wheezing, nasal stuffiness. |
| **Metabolism** | Increased appetite and weight gain, anorexia. |
| **Skin** | Excessive perspiration, urticaria, photosensitivity. |
| **Blood/Lymphatic** | Haemolytic anaemia, leucopenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia. |
| **Special Senses** | Blurred vision, diplopia, vertigo, tinnitus, acute labyrinthitis. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAO Inhibitors:** Prolong and intensify anticholinergic effects.
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with alcohol, hypnotics, sedatives, and anti-anxiety agents.
- **Serotonin-Enhancing Drugs:** Cyproheptadine may interfere with the action of serotonin-enhancing antidepressants.
- **Laboratory Tests:** May cause false-positive results for tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs) in urine or serum drug screens.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Australian Category A:** Drugs taken by a large number of pregnant women without proven increases in malformation frequency or harmful effects on the fetus.
  - **Clinical Note:** Use only if clearly needed; animal studies (up to 32x human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk; however, the risk of serious adverse reactions in infants is high.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (10x human dose) showed no effect on fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
  - **6–11 years:** Use only on the advice of a doctor/pharmacist. Dosing: 2 mg (at age 6) or 4 mg (age 7+) 2–3 times daily.
  - **< 6 years:** Contraindicated.
- **Elderly Use:** More susceptible to dizziness, sedation, and hypotension. Cyproheptadine is listed as a contraindication for elderly, debilitated patients in the Australian PI.
- **Renal Impairment:** Elimination is diminished; dosing adjustments may be necessary.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in detail, though hepatic failure and hepatitis are reported as adverse effects.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Cyproheptadine is a potent antagonist that competes with free serotonin and histamine for receptor sites. It also possesses anticholinergic and sedative properties.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Noted for rapid onset in migraine (relief within 1–2 hours).
- **Metabolism:** The primary metabolite in humans is a quaternary ammonium glucuronide conjugate.
- **Elimination:** At least 40% is excreted in the urine. 2–20% is excreted in stools (with <5.7% as unchanged drug). No unchanged drug is detectable in urine during chronic dosing.

### Pharmacodynamics
Animal models demonstrate it surpasses specific serotonin antagonists in activity. It uniquely inhibits histamine-induced gastric secretion, an effect not seen with standard antihistamines.

- **Summary:** Formal pivotal trial data summaries were not provided in the source documentation.
- **Clinical Observation:** Beneficial effects reported in patients with vascular headache who failed to respond to other agents [Australian].
