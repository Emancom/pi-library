# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a synthesis of clinical and regulatory data intended for professional reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should always refer to their local approved labelling.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN), a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection containing 1 g of levocarnitine per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg; however, doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].

### 3.2 ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight, administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine range: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before administration [US].
- **Preparation:** The solution is compatible and stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Precaution):** While this editorial concerns the injection, clinicians should note that high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of plasma carnitine levels is essential to guide therapy and prevent over- or under-treatment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials include [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), arrhythmia (2%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), dyspepsia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).

### 6.2 Postmarketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and laryngeal edema.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned in current text, but animal studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** The use of levocarnitine in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant should be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were observed in standard tests. Long-term animal studies to evaluate carcinogenic potential have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg body weight, applicable to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided in the source; however, general caution is advised due to the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function in this population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For the *injectable* form in ESRD, specific dosing and monitoring are required to manage carnitine deficiency effectively (see Section 3) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria. This provides the necessary substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) significantly increased plasma carnitine levels. Trough concentrations rose from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
