# Originality Audit Report: carbidopa and levodopa

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 68/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 6
- **Overall Assessment:** Concern

## Verbatim Copied Passages
The following passages are identical or near-identical to the source text, representing a failure to synthesize information into a new editorial voice.

1. **Section 1: Active Ingredients (Carbidopa)**
   - **Editorial:** "An inhibitor of aromatic amino acid decarboxylation (DOPA decarboxylase). It is a white, crystalline compound. Content is expressed in terms of anhydrous carbidopa."
   - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Page 1: "Carbidopa, an inhibitor of aromatic amino acid decarboxylase, is a white, crystalline compound... Tablet content is expressed in terms of anhydrous carbidopa..."
   - **Location:** Editorial Section 1.

2. **Section 3: Contraindications (MAOIs)**
   - **Editorial:** "These must be discontinued at least 14 days before starting carbidopa/levodopa. However, it may be used with the manufacturer’s recommended dose of selective MAO-B inhibitors (e.g., selegiline)."
   - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Page 2: "These inhibitors must be discontinued at least two weeks prior to initiating therapy... may be administered concomitantly with the manufacturer's recommended dose of a MAO inhibitor with selectivity for MAO type B, eg selegiline..."
   - **Location:** Editorial Section 3.

3. **Section 5: Dosage (Transferring from Levodopa)**
   - **Editorial:** "Levodopa must be discontinued at least 12 hours before starting the combination (24 hours for slow-release forms)."
   - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Page 2: "Levodopa should be discontinued at least 12 hours before... (24 hours for slow-release preparations of levodopa)."
   - **Location:** Editorial Section 5.

4. **Section 8: Use in Pregnancy**
   - **Editorial:** "There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Levodopa is known to cross the placental barrier. In animal studies (rabbits), the combination caused visceral and skeletal malformations."
   - **Source:** Document 2 (FDA), Page 10: "There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women... levodopa crosses the human placental barrier... Carbidopa and levodopa caused both visceral and skeletal malformations in rabbits..."
   - **Location:** Editorial Section 8.

5. **Section 9: Pharmacokinetics (Elimination)**
   - **Editorial:** "...approximately equal quantities, with 35% of urinary radioactivity appearing as unchanged drug within seven hours."
   - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Page 8: "...approximately equal quantities were excreted in the urine and the faeces... represented 35 percent of the total urinary radioactivity... essentially complete in seven hours."
   - **Location:** Editorial Section 9.

6. **Section 11: Storage Conditions (FDA)**
   - **Editorial:** "Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F), with excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F)."
   - **Source:** Document 2 (FDA), Page 15: "Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F)."
   - **Location:** Editorial Section 11.

## Close Paraphrases
- **Section 4: NMS Warning:** The editorial lists symptoms (fever, muscle rigidity, mental changes) in the exact order found in the TGA document (p. 3) under "A symptom complex resembling the neuroleptic malignant syndrome."
- **Section 5: Carbidopa Inhibition:** The statement regarding the requirement of 70mg to 100mg of carbidopa for "optimal inhibition of extracerebral decarboxylation" is a direct lift of clinical data phrasing from TGA Page 2 (Maintenance).
- **Section 7: Drug Interactions (Metoclopramide):** The description of speeding gastric emptying while impairing control via dopamine antagonism closely mirrors the FDA text (p. 9).

## Originality Strengths
- **Section 2 (Indications):** The editorial does a good job of merging the different indications from both agencies (noting the specific FDA indications for carbon monoxide/manganese poisoning) while using its own formatting.
- **Section 6 (Adverse Effects):** This section successfully categorizes side effects into "Common," "Serious," and "Cardiovascular" groups, which is a significant organizational improvement over the alphabetized or system-organ-class lists in the source PIs.
- **Section 10 (Mechanism of Action):** While technical, this section is a clean synthesis that explains the biological process without relying on the exact sentence structures of the source documents.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Harmonization:** The editorial explicitly identifies differences in clinical guidelines between the two regions (e.g., the 20% vs 25% levodopa dosage transfer rule).
- **Formatting:** The use of bold headers and bulleted lists provides a modern, "Reference-style" document that is easier to navigate than the dense regulatory PIs.
- **Omissions:** The editorial wisely omits specific manufacturer details (tablet colors, debossing codes, specific excipient names like "FD&C Blue No.2") to maintain a generic, clinical focus.
- **Metric Conversion:** Explicitly labels the 14-day MAOI washout period (Editorial) vs. "two weeks" (Source).

## Recommendation
**ASSESSMENT: CONCERN**

The editorial requires **revision**. While the structural synthesis is excellent, the writer has relied too heavily on verbatim "cut-and-paste" for technical descriptions, particularly in the **Active Ingredients, Pregnancy, and Pharmacokinetics** sections. In a regulatory context, high similarity in dosage instructions is expected, but general descriptions and metabolic data should be rewritten in the author's voice to avoid plagiarism.

**Required Changes:**
1. Rewrite the descriptions of Carbidopa and Levodopa in Section 1.
2. Paraphrase the Pregnancy and Breastfeeding warnings to move away from the "no adequate, well-controlled studies" boilerplate.
3. Rewrite the Pharmacokinetics Section 9 to summarize the data points rather than quoting the percentage results and timeframes in the exact sequence of the source text.
