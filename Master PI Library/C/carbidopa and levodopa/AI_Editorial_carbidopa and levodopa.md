# Unified Product Information: Carbidopa and Levodopa

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Generic Name:** Carbidopa and Levodopa
**Active Ingredients:**
*   **Carbidopa:** An inhibitor of aromatic amino acid decarboxylation (DOPA decarboxylase). It is a white, crystalline compound. Content is expressed in terms of anhydrous carbidopa.
*   **Levodopa:** An aromatic amino acid and the metabolic precursor of dopamine. It is a white to off-white crystalline powder.

## 2. Approved Indications
This combination therapy is indicated for the treatment of Parkinson’s disease and Parkinson’s syndrome. It is effective in managing core symptoms, including tremors, bradykinesia (slowness of movement), and muscular rigidity. 

Additional indications include:
*   Post-encephalitic parkinsonism [FDA].
*   Symptomatic parkinsonism resulting from carbon monoxide intoxication or manganese poisoning [FDA].

The medication may also be used to manage associated symptoms such as postural instability, sialorrhoea (excessive salivation), and dysphagia.

## 3. Contraindications
The use of carbidopa/levodopa is contraindicated in the following circumstances:
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to carbidopa, levodopa, or any inactive ingredients.
*   **Narrow-Angle Glaucoma:** Due to the risk of increased intraocular pressure.
*   **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use with non-selective MAOIs is contraindicated. These must be discontinued at least 14 days before starting carbidopa/levodopa. However, it may be used with the manufacturer’s recommended dose of selective MAO-B inhibitors (e.g., selegiline).
*   **Melanoma:** Because levodopa may activate malignant melanoma, the drug should not be used in patients with undiagnosed, suspicious skin lesions or a history of melanoma [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Dyskinesias:** Because carbidopa allows more levodopa to reach the brain, involuntary movements (dyskinesias) may occur at lower doses and more rapidly than with levodopa monotherapy. Dosage reduction may be required.
*   **Somnolence and Sudden Sleep Onset:** Patients have reported falling asleep during daily activities without prior warning. Caution is advised when driving or operating machinery. If sudden sleep onset occurs, therapy reduction or discontinuation should be considered.
*   **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Abrupt withdrawal or rapid dose reduction of antiparkinsonian agents can trigger a symptom complex resembling NMS (fever, muscle rigidity, mental changes). Patients should be tapered off the medication slowly.
*   **Psychiatric Effects:** All patients should be monitored for depression, suicidal ideation, hallucinations, and psychotic behavior. Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of severe psychotic disorders.
*   **Impulse Control Disorders:** Patients may experience intense urges to gamble, increased libido, or compulsive spending. Prescribers should specifically screen for these behaviors as patients may not recognize them as abnormal.
*   **Vitamin B6 Deficiency:** Treatment may reduce Vitamin B6 levels, potentially leading to seizures or neuropathy. Evaluation of B6 levels prior to and during therapy is recommended [FDA].
*   **Cardiovascular and Pulmonary Disease:** Use with caution in patients with severe cardiovascular disease, bronchial asthma, or a history of myocardial infarction with residual arrhythmias. Cardiac function should be monitored during initial dose titration.
*   **Melanoma Monitoring:** Epidemiological studies indicate Parkinson’s patients have a higher risk of melanoma. Frequent skin examinations by qualified professionals (e.g., dermatologists) are recommended.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Dosage must be individualized and titrated according to therapeutic response and tolerability.

**General Considerations:**
*   Optimal inhibition of extracerebral decarboxylation of levodopa typically requires 70 mg to 100 mg of carbidopa per day.
*   Standard antiparkinsonian drugs (excluding levodopa monotherapy) may be continued, though dose adjustments may be necessary.

**Initiation:**
*   **Starting Dose:** Typically one tablet of 100/25 mg (levodopa/carbidopa) three times daily. 
*   **Titration:** Dosage may be increased by one tablet every day or every other day as required, up to a maximum of eight tablets of 100/25 mg per day.
*   Alternatively, the 100/10 mg strength may be used 3–4 times daily, though this may not provide sufficient carbidopa for all patients [FDA].

**Transferring from Levodopa Monotherapy:**
*   Levodopa must be discontinued at least 12 hours before starting the combination (24 hours for slow-release forms).
*   The starting dose should provide approximately 20% [TGA] to 25% [FDA] of the previous daily levodopa dosage.

**Maintenance:**
*   If more levodopa is required, 250/25 mg tablets may be substituted. The maximum recommended daily dose is eight tablets of 250/25 mg (2 g levodopa and 200 mg carbidopa).

## 6. Adverse Effects
**Common:** Dyskinesias (choreiform or dystonic movements), nausea, dizziness, and dream abnormalities.

**Serious/Psychiatric:** Hallucinations, paranoid ideation, depression, confusion, and agitation.

**Cardiovascular:** Orthostatic hypotension, palpitations, cardiac irregularities, and syncope.

**Gastrointestinal:** Vomiting, anorexia, dry mouth, bitter taste, and rare instances of gastrointestinal bleeding or duodenal ulcer.

**Other reported effects:** Darkening of sweat, urine, or saliva; muscle cramps; blepharospasm (an early sign of overdosage); alopecia; and skin rash.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Antihypertensives:** May cause symptomatic postural hypotension; dose adjustments of the antihypertensive may be needed.
*   **Tricyclic Antidepressants:** Rare reports of hypertension and dyskinesia.
*   **Iron:** Ferrous sulfate or gluconate can chelate with the medication, reducing the bioavailability of both carbidopa and levodopa.
*   **Dopamine D2 Antagonists:** Phenothiazines, butyrophenones, and risperidone may reduce the therapeutic effect of levodopa.
*   **Isoniazid, Phenytoin, and Papaverine:** May reverse the beneficial effects of levodopa.
*   **Metoclopramide:** May increase levodopa bioavailability by speeding gastric emptying but may also impair disease control via dopamine antagonism [FDA].
*   **High-Protein Diet:** Absorption may be impaired in patients on high-protein diets due to competition with certain amino acids.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
**Pregnancy (Category B3 [TGA]):**
There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Levodopa is known to cross the placental barrier. In animal studies (rabbits), the combination caused visceral and skeletal malformations. Use in women of childbearing potential requires weighing the clinical benefits against potential fetal risks.

**Breastfeeding:**
Levodopa has been detected in human milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug, taking into account the importance of the medication to the mother.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
**Absorption:**
Levodopa is rapidly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract. Carbidopa's presence increases the plasma levels of levodopa and extends its measurable duration from four to eight hours.

**Distribution and Metabolism:**
*   Levodopa’s half-life is approximately 50 minutes without carbidopa, increasing to 1.5 hours when combined.
*   Carbidopa does not cross the blood-brain barrier; it inhibits only the peripheral decarboxylation of levodopa.
*   Levodopa is extensively metabolized into more than 30 metabolites, primarily dopamine, homovanillic acid, and vanillylmandelic acid.

**Elimination:**
Radioactive studies show carbidopa is excreted in urine and feces in roughly equal quantities, with 35% of urinary radioactivity appearing as unchanged drug within seven hours.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Parkinson’s disease symptoms are primarily caused by a depletion of dopamine in the corpus striatum. Levodopa relieves symptoms by crossing the blood-brain barrier and being converted into dopamine. 

Carbidopa is a peripheral decarboxylase inhibitor. It prevents the conversion of levodopa to dopamine in extracerebral tissues. This ensures more levodopa is available for transport to the brain and reduces peripheral side effects, such as nausea and vomiting, associated with dopamine formation in the systemic circulation.

## 11. Storage Conditions
Store the medication in a tightly closed, light-resistant container.
*   **TGA Guideline:** Store below 25°C, protected from light and moisture.
*   **FDA Guideline:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F), with excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
