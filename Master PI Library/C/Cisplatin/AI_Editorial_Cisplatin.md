# Editorial Product Information: Cisplatin

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **cisplatin** concentrated injection, intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official Product Information approved for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cisplatin (a platinum-based antineoplastic agent).
- **Brand Names:** **Cisplatin Accord** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Concentrated injection for infusion: 1 mg/mL.
    - Available in 10 mL (10 mg), 25 mL (25 mg), 50 mL (50 mg), and 100 mL (100 mg) amber glass vials [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Accord Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Cisplatin** is indicated as monotherapy or in combination with other cytotoxic agents for the treatment of:
- Metastatic non-seminomatous germ cell carcinoma.
- Advanced stage, refractory ovarian carcinoma.
- Advanced stage, refractory bladder carcinoma.
- Refractory squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children)
- **Monotherapy:** 
    - 50–100 mg/m² as a single IV infusion every 3–4 weeks (infused over 6–8 hours).
    - OR 15–20 mg/m²/day by slow IV infusion for 5 consecutive days, every 3–4 weeks.
- **Combination Therapy:** Doses vary based on the specific protocol. Common partners include:
    - *Testicular cancer:* Vinblastine, bleomycin, actinomycin D.
    - *Ovarian cancer:* Cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, hexamethylmelamine, fluorouracil.
    - *Head and neck cancer:* Bleomycin, methotrexate.

### Administration Instructions
- **Hydration Protocol:** Essential to mitigate nephrotoxicity.
    - **Pre-treatment:** 2 litres of 5% glucose in ½ to ⅓ normal saline over 2–4 hours.
    - **Post-treatment:** Maintain adequate hydration and urinary output for 24 hours following infusion.
- **Dilution:** Add to 1 litre of normal saline. Infusion must be completed within 24 hours of preparation.
- **Equipment Warning:** Do **NOT** use equipment containing aluminum parts (e.g., needles, syringes, catheters), as it reacts with cisplatin to form a black precipitate, inactivating the drug.
- **Stability:** Sensitive to bisulphite, metabisulphite, sodium bicarbonate, and fluorouracil.

### Dosage Adjustments
- Reduce dosage in patients with depressed bone marrow function.
- **Repeat Courses:** Do not administer until serum creatinine is <140 micromol/L and/or plasma urea is <9 mmol/L, and circulating blood elements reach acceptable levels (Platelets ≥100,000/mm³, WBC ≥4,000/mm³).

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **cisplatin** or other platinum-containing compounds.
- Pre-existing renal impairment (specifically contraindicated if serum creatinine >200 micromol/L [Australian]).
- Pre-existing hearing disorders.
- Bone marrow depression.
- Generalised infections.
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided sources; however, **cisplatin** is noted as a highly toxic drug with a narrow therapeutic index.
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Cumulative and dose-related; the major dose-limiting toxicity. Renal function must return to baseline before subsequent doses.
- **Myelosuppression:** Dose-related and cumulative. Nadirs for platelets and leucocytes typically occur between days 18–23. Severe anaemia may require red cell transfusions.
- **Ototoxicity:** Cumulative and may be irreversible. Hearing loss is most common in the 4000 to 8000 Hz range. Radiotherapy may exacerbate this effect.
- **Neurotoxicity:** Peripheral neuropathy (primarily sensory), seizures, and visual loss may occur, particularly after prolonged treatment. Development of significant symptoms generally contraindicates further use.
- **Anaphylaxis:** Can occur within minutes of administration; requires immediate access to adrenaline, corticosteroids, and antihistamines.
- **Vascular/Cardiovascular Toxicity:** Risk of venous thromboembolic events, myocardial infarction, and cerebrovascular accidents.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Prior to and during therapy, monitor: GFR, BUN, serum creatinine, electrolytes (Mg, Na, K, Ca), auditory function, CBC, liver function, and neurological status.

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea and vomiting (almost 100% of patients), leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, nephrotoxicity (elevated BUN/creatinine), hyperuricaemia, hypomagnesaemia, ototoxicity (tinnitus/hearing loss in up to 31%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Sepsis, arrhythmia, phlebitis at injection site. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anaphylactic-like reactions, impairment of spermatogenesis, gynaecomastia. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Acute leukaemia, seizures, optic neuritis, cortical blindness, cardiac arrest. |
| **Frequency Not Specified** | Stomatitis, anorexia, hiccups, diarrhoea, alopecia, myalgia, Raynaud’s phenomenon, pulmonary embolism. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Nephrotoxic/Ototoxic Agents:** Concurrent or sequential use of aminoglycosides or loop diuretics (e.g., frusemide) significantly increases risk.
- **Ifosfamide:** May enhance nephrotoxicity and hearing loss.
- **Anticonvulsants:** Cisplatin may reduce phenytoin serum levels to subtherapeutic ranges.
- **Anticoagulants:** Monitor INR closely when using coumarins/warfarin.
- **Paclitaxel:** Cisplatin administration prior to paclitaxel reduces paclitaxel clearance by 33%, potentially intensifying neurotoxicity.
- **Renally Excreted Drugs:** May alter elimination of bleomycin, methotrexate, and lithium.
- **Antigout Agents:** Adjustment of allopurinol or probenecid may be needed due to cisplatin-induced hyperuricaemia.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Potential for fetal harm, malformations, and irreversible damage. Use is not recommended unless benefits outweigh risks.
- **Contraception:** 
    - Females: Use effective contraception during and for at least 7 months post-therapy.
    - Males: Use effective contraception with partners for at least 4 months post-therapy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Cisplatin and metabolites are excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be avoided during and for 1 month after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** May cause cumulative, dose-dependent ovarian failure and premature menopause. In males, azoospermia and impaired spermatogenesis may occur (sometimes reversible). Fertility preservation advice is recommended before treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Delayed-onset hearing loss is a specific concern; long-term auditory follow-up is recommended.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available; however, hearing loss may be reported more frequently in very old patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in significant impairment (Cr >200 micromol/L). Requires intensive hydration and monitoring for all patients.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** High uptake in the liver; use with caution and monitor liver function tests (AST, ALT, alkaline phosphatase) periodically.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A platinum-based alkylating-like agent. The *cis*-isomer produces intra- and interstrand DNA cross-links, modifying DNA structure and inhibiting synthesis. It also inhibits protein and RNA synthesis to a lesser extent.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Concentrates in liver, kidneys, small intestine, and testes. >90% is protein-bound. Does not cross the blood-brain barrier to a significant extent.
    - **Metabolism:** Undergoes rapid non-enzymatic biotransformation.
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal. 15–25% excreted in the first 2–4 hours; 20–80% within 24 hours. The terminal half-life of total platinum is approximately 5 days or longer due to extensive tissue binding.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Concentrate:** Store below 25°C. Do **NOT** refrigerate or freeze (refrigeration may cause precipitation).
- **Protection:** Protect from light.
- **Precipitation:** If a precipitate forms due to cold exposure, redissolve by warming to recommended storage temperatures until the solution is clear.
