# Editorial Product Information: Cefoxitin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Cefoxitin sodium
- **Chemical Class:** Cephamycin (beta-lactam antibiotic)
- **Brand Names:** Cefoxitin Juno [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** J01DC01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** White to slightly yellow sterile powder for injection in 1 g and 2 g vials [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Intramuscular (IM).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Juno Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Cefoxitin is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by susceptible organisms, including:
- **Peritonitis** and other intra-abdominal/intra-pelvic infections.
- **Female genital tract infections.**
- **Septicaemia.**
- **Endocarditis.**
- **Urinary tract infections (UTI).**
- **Respiratory tract infections.**
- **Bone and joint infections.**
- **Skin and skin structure infections.**
- **Surgical Prophylaxis:** Prevention of post-operative infections in gastrointestinal, biliary, and genital tract procedures.
- **Adjunctive Therapy:** Surgical treatment of abscesses and infections complicating hollow viscus perforations.

Cefoxitin is effective against beta-lactamase-producing organisms resistant to penicillins or other cephalosporins. It is not recommended for the treatment of meningitis [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Uncomplicated Infections:** 1 g every 8 hours (occasionally every 6 hours; total 3–4 g/day).
- **Moderately Severe/Severe Infections:** 2 g every 8 hours (occasionally every 6 hours; total 6–8 g/day).
- **Infections Requiring Higher Doses:** 2 g every 4 hours or 3 g every 6 hours (total 12 g/day).
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** 12 g.

### Specific Dosing
- **Uncomplicated Gonorrhoea:** A single dose of 2 g IM administered concurrently with (or up to 1 hour before) 1 g oral probenecid.
- **Surgical Prophylaxis:** 2 g IM or IV administered 1 hour before the initial incision (IM) or just prior to surgery (IV), followed by 2 g every 6 hours for two doses.
- **Caesarean Section:** 2 g IV as soon as the umbilical cord is clamped; followed by 2 g IV or IM at 4 hours and 8 hours after the initial dose.

### Administration Instructions
- **Intravenous:** Reconstitute 1 g with 10 mL Water for Injections (WFI); 2 g with 10–20 mL WFI. Administer by slow injection over 3–5 minutes or via intermittent/continuous infusion.
- **Intramuscular:** Reconstitute 1 g with 2 mL of 0.5% or 1% lignocaine HCl (without adrenaline). If lignocaine is contraindicated, use WFI. Administer by deep injection into a large muscle mass; avoid blood vessels.
- **Neonatal Warning:** Diluents for neonates must be preservative-free. Benzyl alcohol is associated with toxicity in this population.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to cefoxitin sodium or the cephalosporin class of antibiotics.
- History of major allergic reactions to penicillin (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioneurotic oedema, urticaria).
- If using lignocaine as a diluent (IM), the contraindications for lignocaine apply.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Partial cross-allergenicity exists between cephamycins, cephalosporins, and penicillins. Severe reactions, including fatal anaphylaxis, have occurred.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** SJS, TEN, DRESS, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue immediately if SCAR is suspected.
- **Neurotoxicity:** Risk of encephalopathy, seizures, and myoclonus, particularly in the elderly or those with renal impairment.
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhoea (CDAD):** May range in severity from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis. Avoid drugs that delay peristalsis (e.g., opiates).
- **Superinfection:** Overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi, may occur.
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Exacerbation has been reported with cefoxitin use.

### Monitoring Requirements
- Renal function should be monitored, especially if high doses are used or if co-administered with potentially nephrotoxic agents (e.g., aminoglycosides).

### Laboratory Test Interference
- **Urinary Glucose:** May cause false-positive reactions with reducing substances (use glucose oxidase methods instead).
- **Serum Creatinine:** Falsely high values may occur using the Jaffe Technique if serum concentrations exceed 100 micrograms/mL. Do not draw samples within 2 hours of administration.
- **17-hydroxycorticosteroids:** High urine concentrations may interfere with Porter-Silber reaction measurements.
- **Coombs Test:** Positive results may develop, particularly in patients with azotaemia.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects occur in approximately 8.5% of cases and are generally mild and transient [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Thrombophlebitis/phlebitis (2.5% IV), local pain/induration (1.7% IV; common IM if WFI used), maculopapular rash/urticaria (1.7%). |
| **Rare (<0.1%) / Unknown** | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, hypotension, anaphylaxis, pruritus, eosinophilia, fever. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, haemolytic anaemia, bone marrow depression, jaundice, acute renal failure, and seizures (particularly in renal impairment).
- **Cephalosporin-Class Effects:** Erythema multiforme, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic nephropathy, aplastic anaemia, and haemorrhage.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** Competitively inhibits tubular secretion, resulting in higher and more prolonged serum concentrations of cefoxitin.
- **Aminoglycosides and Diuretics:** Concurrent use with gentamicin or frusemide may increase the risk of nephrotoxicity.
- **Anticoagulants:** Cephalosporins may affect the stability of anticoagulant control (e.g., warfarin, phenindione).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B1 (Animal studies show no evidence of foetal damage; human data limited). Use only if the clinician deems it essential and benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** Cefoxitin is excreted in human milk. Alternative feeding arrangements should be considered.
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Neonates (0–1 week):** 20–40 mg/kg every 12 hours.
    - **Neonates (1–4 weeks):** 20–40 mg/kg every 8 hours.
    - **Infants/Children (>1 month):** 20–40 mg/kg every 6 to 8 hours.
    - **Prophylaxis:** No paediatric data available; 30–40 mg/kg proposed at adult timing intervals.
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of neurotoxicity and renal dysfunction; monitor renal function and adjust dosage accordingly.
- **Renal Impairment:** Reduce maintenance dosage based on Creatinine Clearance (CrCl). Give a loading dose of 1–2 g first.

| Renal Function | CrCl (mL/min) | Dose | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mild | 80–30 | 1–2 g | Every 8–12 hours |
| Moderate | 29–10 | 1–2 g | Every 12–24 hours |
| Severe | <10 | 0.5–1 g | Every 12–24 hours |
| Dialysis | <5 | 0.5–1 g | Every 24–48 hours (dose after dialysis) |

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis.
- **Microbiology:** Structurally unique due to a 7-alpha methoxy group, providing high stability against beta-lactamases. Active against various aerobic/anaerobic Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms. Inactive against *Pseudomonas* spp, most enterococci, and MRSA.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak serum levels (1 g IV over 3 min) = 110.5 µg/mL. IM administration (1 g) = ~30 µg/mL at 30 minutes.
    - **Distribution:** Penetrates CSF but at concentrations lower than plasma.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted virtually unchanged by the kidneys (77–99% of IV dose recovered in urine). Serum half-life is approximately 50–60 minutes.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data provided in the available source document.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Reconstituted solutions maintain potency for 6 hours at 30°C or up to 96 hours at 2–8°C [Australian].
