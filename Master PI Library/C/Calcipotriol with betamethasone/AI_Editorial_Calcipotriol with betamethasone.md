# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a structured format.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational use by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from metabolic errors [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing chronic hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less frequently than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **ESRD:** Therapy may be prompted by trough concentrations below the normal range of 40 to 50 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and assess risks before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with known seizure history, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Thromboembolic Risk:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality):

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight increase/decrease |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, depression, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Other** | Pruritus, rash, taste perversion |

### Post-marketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis (less frequent) [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins, reducing the likelihood of protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, adequate and well-controlled human studies are lacking. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** [US]: Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the maternal benefits. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is indicated for metabolic disorders; refer to the weight-based dosing in Section 3 [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Injection:** Indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3).
    - **Oral:** Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency; accumulation of toxic metabolites may occur [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors or ESRD, levocarnitine clears accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical trials demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses administered three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels typically stabilised between 54 and 243 µmol/L (normal range 40–50 µmol/L) within 8 to 24 weeks of therapy. However, the specific impact on clinical outcomes or symptoms of carnitine deficiency in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL, stored at 25°C [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to [Australian] S4 if applicable).
