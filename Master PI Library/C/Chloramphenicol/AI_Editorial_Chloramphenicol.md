# Editorial Product Information: Chloramphenicol (Ophthalmic)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ophthalmic chloramphenicol for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information when prescribing or dispensing in a specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Chloramphenicol (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Broad-spectrum antibiotic (originally isolated from *Streptomyces venezuelae*).
- **Brand Names:** Cholfen [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (refer to S01AA01 for general ophthalmic use).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Eye Drops:** 0.5% w/v (5 mg/mL) [Australian].
    - **Eye Ointment:** 1% w/w (10 mg/g) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd (trading as Viatris) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Bacterial Conjunctivitis:** For the treatment of acute bacterial infections of the conjunctiva.
- **Superficial Ocular Infections:** For use under medical supervision in treating other superficial infections of the eye caused by chloramphenicol-sensitive organisms [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children $\ge$ 2 years)
- **Eye Drops:** Instil 1 or 2 drops into the affected eye(s) every two to six hours for the first 48 to 72 hours. The interval between doses may be increased thereafter.
- **Eye Ointment:** Apply 1.5 cm of ointment into the affected eye(s) every 3 hours.
- **Combined Therapy:** If using both forms, the drops may be used during the day and the ointment (1.5 cm) applied once at bedtime to provide continuous coverage.

### Administration Instructions
- **Duration:** Treatment should continue for at least 48 hours after the eye appears normal. Total treatment duration should not exceed 5 days except on medical advice [Australian].
- **Systemic Absorption Minimisation:** Apply gentle pressure to the tear duct (nasolacrimal occlusion) for approximately one minute immediately following the instillation of drops.
- **Contamination Prevention:** Avoid contact between the dropper/tube tip and the eye or any other surface.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to chloramphenicol or any excipients (e.g., phenylmercuric nitrate in drops; wool fat in ointment).
- History of toxic reaction to the drug [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hematologic Toxicity:** Bone marrow hypoplasia, including rare instances of irreversible aplastic anaemia and death, has been reported following local ophthalmic application. Chloramphenicol should be avoided if less potentially toxic agents are effective.
- **Corneal Healing:** Topical ophthalmic agents may delay the healing process of the cornea.
- **Microbial Overgrowth:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi. 
- **Contact Lens Wear:** Patients should not wear contact lenses during treatment. Hard/disposable lenses may be resumed after treatment; soft lenses should be avoided for 24 hours after treatment completion [Australian].
- **OTC Referral Criteria:** In the Australian context (S3), pharmacists must refer patients to a doctor/optometrist if they present with:
    - Photophobia or severe eye pain.
    - Vision loss or blurred vision.
    - Cloudy cornea or abnormal pupils.
    - History of welding without protection, glaucoma, or recent eye surgery (within 6 months).
    - Personal/family history of bone marrow disorders.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Frequency Not Defined:**
    - **Local:** Transient itching, burning, or stinging.
    - **Hypersensitivity:** Angioneurotic oedema, anaphylaxis, urticaria, fever, and various forms of dermatitis (vesicular/maculopapular). 
    - **Systemic:** Blood dyscrasias (aplastic anaemia, hypoplastic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, and granulocytopenia) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Dynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources for topical ophthalmic use.
- **Management:** While systemic absorption is limited, the potential for interactions should be considered if the patient is on concurrent systemic marrow-suppressant medications.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category A:** Chloramphenicol has been taken by a large number of pregnant women without a proven increase in the frequency of malformations.
    - **Risks:** Systemic chloramphenicol crosses the placenta and may cause "grey baby syndrome" or bone marrow suppression if given shortly before birth. However, topical ophthalmic use at recommended doses is not expected to pose a significant risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Systemic chloramphenicol is distributed into breast milk and poses a risk of bone marrow suppression to the infant. Limited absorption from ophthalmic use is generally considered low risk to the neonate [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Boron-containing excipients (boric acid/borax) in the eye drops have shown reduced fertility in animal studies; however, the human relevance at clinical doses is uncertain [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 2 years of age except on medical advice [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available; follow standard adult dosing.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data available for topical use; systemic absorption is minimal.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primarily bacteriostatic. It inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 50S ribosomal subunit, interfering with the transfer of activated amino acids from soluble RNA to ribosomes.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Chloramphenicol is absorbed systemically from the eye. Toxicity is generally associated with chronic exposure; dose-related toxicity from a single ocular exposure is unlikely [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific clinical trial data were detailed in the provided source document.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Eye Drops:** Store refrigerated between 2°C and 8°C until opened. Do not freeze. After opening, may be stored at room temperature (below 25°C) but must be discarded after 4 weeks. Protect from light.
- **Eye Ointment:** Store below 25°C. Discard 4 weeks after opening. Protect from light.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 3 (Pharmacist Only Medicine) [Australian].
