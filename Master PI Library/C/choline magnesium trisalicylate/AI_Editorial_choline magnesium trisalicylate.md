# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was developed using official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory information into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often utilised in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 to 4 hours; intervals should not be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be visually inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No contraindications are currently listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred—often within minutes of intravenous administration. These are most frequently reported in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a severe reaction occurs, therapy must be discontinued immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Commonly Reported (General):** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent effects include body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Frequency Data (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials):**
    - **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (30–49%), headache (22–37%), flu syndrome (25–40%), chest pain (12–15%), infection (15–24%), abdominal pain (5–21%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels at therapy initiation and following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified as a letter category in the current source.
    - **Data:** Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human surface area dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; however, supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following administration. Consider the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant versus the benefit of treatment for the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to the "Metabolic Disorders" subsection in Section 3 for dosing. Approved for use in infants and newborns [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in the source, though general caution is advised [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Conversely, high-dose oral levocarnitine is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg IV doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources [US].
