# Editorial Product Information: Carbidopa and Levodopa

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from the official regulatory filings of the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It combines the clinical guidance and safety data from both jurisdictions to provide a comprehensive reference for healthcare professionals. Where specific regional differences or unique data points exist, the contributing agency is noted in brackets.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Carbidopa and Levodopa (Immediate Release)
**Active Ingredients:** 
*   **Carbidopa:** An aromatic amino acid decarboxylase inhibitor.
*   **Levodopa:** An aromatic amino acid and the metabolic precursor of dopamine.

**Available Strengths:**
*   10 mg carbidopa / 100 mg levodopa
*   25 mg carbidopa / 100 mg levodopa
*   25 mg carbidopa / 250 mg levodopa

---

## 2. Approved Indications
Carbidopa/Levodopa is indicated for the management of:
*   Parkinson’s disease and syndrome.
*   Post-encephalitic parkinsonism [FDA].
*   Symptomatic parkinsonism resulting from carbon monoxide intoxication or manganese poisoning [FDA].

The combination is effective in alleviating major symptoms including bradykinesia, rigidity, and resting tremor. It is also utilized to manage associated symptoms such as dysphagia, sialorrhoea, and postural instability.

---

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use with non-selective MAOIs is contraindicated. These must be discontinued at least 14 days prior to starting carbidopa/levodopa. (Selective MAO-B inhibitors, such as selegiline, may be used concomitantly at recommended doses).
*   **Glaucoma:** Contraindicated in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma.
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to any component of the formulation.
*   **Melanoma:** Because levodopa may potentially activate malignant melanoma, this medication should not be used in patients with undiagnosed skin lesions or a history of melanoma [TGA/FDA].

---

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Sudden Sleep Onset and Somnolence
Patients have reported falling asleep during daily activities, sometimes without prior warning signs. Caution is advised when driving or operating machinery. If a patient experiences significant daytime somnolence, a dose reduction or discontinuation should be considered.

### Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)
Abrupt dose reduction or withdrawal of antiparkinsonian therapy has been associated with a symptom complex resembling NMS (fever, muscular rigidity, mental changes, and elevated serum creatine phosphokinase). Patients should be monitored closely during dosage changes.

### Impulse Control Disorders
Dopaminergic medications may cause patients to experience intense urges to gamble, increased libido, or other compulsive behaviors (e.g., binge eating or spending). Physicians should specifically query patients and caregivers about these behaviors, as patients may not recognize them as abnormal.

### Vitamin B6 Deficiency and Seizures [FDA]
Treatment may reduce Vitamin B6 levels. Seizures refractory to standard anticonvulsants but responsive to B6 supplementation have been reported. Levels should be evaluated periodically.

### Cardiovascular and Gastrointestinal Risk
Administer with caution to patients with severe cardiovascular disease or a history of myocardial infarction with residual arrhythmias. Cardiac function should be monitored during the initial titration phase. Use with caution in patients with a history of peptic ulcer due to the risk of upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage.

### Psychiatric Effects
All patients should be observed for the development of depression with suicidal tendencies, hallucinations, or psychotic behavior. Patients with a pre-existing major psychotic disorder should generally not be treated with carbidopa/levodopa.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Dosage must be individualized through careful titration. The goal is to provide at least 70 mg to 100 mg of carbidopa daily to ensure adequate saturation of peripheral dopa decarboxylase, which reduces the incidence of nausea and vomiting.

### Initiating Therapy
*   **Initial Dose:** Typically one 25/100 mg tablet three times daily.
*   **Titration:** Increase by one tablet daily or every other day as required, up to a maximum of eight 25/100 mg tablets per day.
*   **Alternative starting dose:** If using 10/100 mg tablets, the initial dose may be one tablet three or four times daily, though this may provide insufficient carbidopa for some patients [FDA].

### Conversion from Levodopa Monotherapy
Levodopa monotherapy must be discontinued at least 12 hours before starting the combination (24 hours for slow-release levodopa [TGA]). The starting dose of carbidopa/levodopa should provide approximately 20% to 25% of the previous levodopa daily dose.

### Maintenance and Maximum Dose
Maximum recommended dose is eight tablets of 25/250 mg per day (200 mg carbidopa and 2 g levodopa). Experience with daily carbidopa doses exceeding 200 mg is limited.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily due to the central neuropharmacologic activity of dopamine.

*   **Most Common:** Dyskinesias (choreiform or dystonic movements), nausea, and hallucinations.
*   **Cardiovascular:** Orthostatic hypotension, palpitations, cardiac irregularities, and syncope.
*   **Psychiatric/Neurological:** Confusion, insomnia, agitation, dream abnormalities, "on-off" phenomenon, and bradykinetic episodes.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Vomiting, anorexia, dry mouth, bitter taste, and dark-colored saliva (clinically insignificant but can stain clothing).
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Angioedema, urticaria, and pruritus.

---

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Antihypertensives:** May cause symptomatic postural hypotension; dose adjustments of the antihypertensive may be required.
*   **Tricyclic Antidepressants:** Rare reports of hypertension and dyskinesia when used concomitantly.
*   **Dopamine Antagonists:** Drugs such as phenothiazines, butyrophenones, risperidone, and isoniazid may reverse the therapeutic effects of levodopa.
*   **Iron:** Ferrous sulfate or gluconate can chelate with carbidopa/levodopa, significantly reducing bioavailability.
*   **Dietary Protein:** High protein intake may delay or impair absorption, as levodopa competes with certain amino acids for transport across the gut wall.
*   **Metoclopramide:** While it may increase bioavailability by accelerating gastric emptying, its dopamine-antagonistic properties may worsen disease control.

---

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**TGA Category B3.** Animal studies have shown visceral and skeletal malformations in rabbits at various doses. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Levodopa is known to cross the placental barrier. Use is only recommended when the potential benefits outweigh the risks to the fetus.

### Breastfeeding
Levodopa has been detected in human breast milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug, taking into account the mother's clinical need for therapy.

---

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Levodopa is rapidly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract. Carbidopa's bioavailability is approximately 99% relative to its co-administration with levodopa [FDA].
*   **Distribution:** Carbidopa does not cross the blood-brain barrier, ensuring its decarboxylase inhibitory action is limited to peripheral tissues. Levodopa readily crosses the blood-brain barrier.
*   **Metabolism:** Levodopa is extensively metabolized into over 30 metabolites, primarily dopamine, epinephrine, and norepinephrine.
*   **Elimination:** In the presence of carbidopa, the plasma half-life of levodopa increases from approximately 50 minutes to 1.5 hours.

---

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Symptoms of Parkinson's disease are linked to dopamine depletion in the corpus striatum. While dopamine cannot cross the blood-brain barrier, its precursor, levodopa, can. 

In the absence of a decarboxylase inhibitor, levodopa is rapidly converted to dopamine in peripheral tissues, leading to high systemic side effects (nausea) and low central nervous system delivery. Carbidopa inhibits this peripheral conversion, allowing a greater proportion of levodopa to reach the brain, where it is decarboxylated to functional dopamine. Carbidopa does not diminish the central effects of levodopa but significantly reduces peripheral side effects.

---

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 25°C (77°F).
*   FDA guidelines allow for excursions between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F).
*   Keep in a tightly closed, light-resistant container.
*   Protect from moisture.
