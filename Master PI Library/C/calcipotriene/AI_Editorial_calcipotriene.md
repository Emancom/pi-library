# Editorial Product Information: Calcipotriene

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Calcipotriene, a synthetic vitamin D3 derivative [(5Z,7E,22E,24S)-24-cyclopropyl-9,10-secochola-5,7,10(19),22-tetraene-1α,3β,24-triol].
- **Brand Names:** Calcipotriene Ointment (Generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Topical ointment, 0.005% (50 μg/g) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical dermatologic use only. Not for ophthalmic, oral, or intravaginal use [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Glenmark Generics Ltd. (India); Distributed by Taro Pharmaceuticals U.S.A., Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Plaque Psoriasis:** Indicated for the treatment of plaque psoriasis in adults [US].
- **Note:** Safety and efficacy in dermatoses other than psoriasis have not been established [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Apply a thin layer of ointment to the affected areas once or twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Rub the medication in gently and completely [US].
    - Hands must be washed thoroughly after application [US].
    - Avoid contact with the face or eyes [US].
- **Dosing Frequency:** Clinical trials suggest that while twice-daily dosing showed numerically higher improvement rates, it has not been shown to be statistically superior to once-daily dosing [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to calcipotriene or any components of the ointment base [US].
- **Hypercalcemia:** Patients with demonstrated hypercalcemia [US].
- **Vitamin D Toxicity:** Patients with evidence of vitamin D toxicity [US].
- **Site-Specific:** Calcipotriene should not be applied to the face [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Local Irritation:** May cause irritation of the treated lesions and surrounding uninvolved skin. Treatment should be discontinued if irritation develops [US].
- **Systemic Calcium Effects:** Use may lead to transient, rapidly reversible elevations in serum calcium. If serum calcium levels exceed the normal range, treatment must be discontinued until levels return to normal [US].
- **Photosensitivity:** Patients should avoid or limit excessive exposure to natural or artificial sunlight (including tanning beds and sun lamps). Calcipotriene may enhance the effect of ultraviolet radiation (UVR) to induce skin tumors [US].
- **Monitoring:** While not explicitly mandated for all patients, serum calcium levels should be monitored if systemic absorption is a concern [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily dermatological and occur at the site of application.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Burning, itching, skin irritation (approx. 10–15%) [US]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Erythema, dry skin, peeling, rash, dermatitis, worsening of psoriasis (including facial/scalp psoriasis) [US]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Skin atrophy, hyperpigmentation, hypercalcemia, folliculitis [US]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Systemic absorption leading to hypercalcemia is the primary serious risk associated with excessive or improper use [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Phototherapy:** Physicians may consider limiting or avoiding phototherapy in patients using calcipotriene due to a potential enhancement of UV-induced skin tumor formation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C:** There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. 
    - **Data:** Teratogenicity studies in rats and rabbits via the oral route showed increased skeletal abnormalities and fetal toxicity at doses roughly equal to human systemic exposure. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if calcipotriene is excreted in human milk. Caution is advised when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Studies in rats at oral doses up to 54 μg/kg/day showed no impairment of fertility or general reproductive performance [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established. Paediatric patients are at a higher risk of systemic adverse effects due to a larger skin surface area-to-body mass ratio [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies included approximately 12% of patients aged 65 or older. Analysis indicated that skin-related adverse events were significantly more severe in patients over 65 years compared to younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Calcipotriene is a synthetic analog of Vitamin D3. It regulates cell proliferation and differentiation, which helps manage the overproduction of keratinocytes seen in psoriasis [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 6% (± 3%) is absorbed systemically when applied to psoriasis plaques; 5% (± 2.6%) is absorbed when applied to normal skin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Absorbed active drug is converted to inactive metabolites within 24 hours of application. Metabolism occurs via pathways similar to the natural hormone [US].
    - **Excretion:** Metabolites are much less potent than the parent compound. Naturally occurring calcitriol is recycled via the liver and excreted in the bile [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Calcipotriene’s effect on calcium metabolism is the primary pharmacodynamic concern regarding systemic exposure [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Improvement typically begins after two weeks of therapy. 
- **Comparative Data:** In 8-week trials, 56.7% of once-daily patients and 70.0% of twice-daily patients showed "at least marked improvement." Complete clearing was observed in 6.4% (once-daily) and 11.3% (twice-daily) of patients [US].
- **Long-term Safety:** Open-label studies for up to one year showed skin irritation in 25% of patients and worsening of psoriasis in 10% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature between 15°C and 25°C (59°F–77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze [US].
- **Packaging:** Available in 60 g and 120 g aluminum tubes [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
