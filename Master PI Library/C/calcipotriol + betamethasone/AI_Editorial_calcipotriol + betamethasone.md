# Editorial Product Information: calcipotriol + betamethasone

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **calcipotriol + betamethasone**
  - Strength per gram (as described in available sources): calcipotriol 50 micrograms (as monohydrate) and betamethasone 500 micrograms (as dipropionate).
- Chemical class:
  - Calcipotriol: synthetic vitamin D3 analogue (antipsoriatic).
  - Betamethasone dipropionate: potent topical corticosteroid.
- ATC code: D05AX52.
- Route(s) of administration: topical (dermal/scalp).

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Topical treatment of scalp psoriasis in adults.
- Topical treatment of mild to moderate plaque (vulgaris) psoriasis on the body in adults.
- Not recommended for use in children and adolescents below 18 years due to lack of safety and efficacy data.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adults)
- Apply topically once daily to affected areas.
- Strength: 50 μg calcipotriol + 500 μg betamethasone per gram (as reported).

### Dosing by indication
- Scalp psoriasis:
  - Apply once daily for up to 4 weeks.
  - Typical amount: usually 1–4 g per day for the scalp (4 g approximates one teaspoon).
  - After 4 weeks, may be used as needed under medical supervision; intermittent courses of up to 52 weeks for scalp psoriasis have safety data.
- Body (mild to moderate plaque) psoriasis:
  - Apply once daily for up to 8 weeks.
  - If no response after 4 weeks of treatment on the body, treatment should be discontinued.
  - Treatment of body psoriasis should be stopped after 8 weeks because long-term efficacy beyond 8 weeks is not established.

### Maximum exposure limits (calcipotriol-containing products)
- Maximum recommended daily dose (calcipotriol component): 15 g.
- Maximum recommended weekly dose: 100 g.
- Total body surface area treated with calcipotriol should not exceed 30%.

### Administration instructions
- For topical use only. Not for ophthalmic use.
- Shake the container before use if formulation instructions indicate.
- When applied to scalp, optimal effect is achieved if hair is not washed immediately after application; product may remain on the scalp overnight or during the day as directed.
- Hands should be washed after application unless the hands are themselves being treated.
- Avoid applying to face and genital skin (areas are particularly sensitive to corticosteroids).
- Avoid occlusive dressings unless directed by a clinician (occlusion markedly increases corticosteroid absorption).

### Titration / duration rules
- Do not extend body treatment beyond 8 weeks; discontinue earlier if no response at 4 weeks.
- Scalp: up to 4 weeks, then intermittent use only under medical supervision.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List of all contraindications reported:
- Known hypersensitivity to calcipotriol, betamethasone, or any excipient listed in product formulation.
- Patients with known disorders of calcium metabolism.
- Due to corticosteroid content, use is contraindicated on skin conditions characterized by or associated with: viral lesions of the skin (e.g. herpes, varicella), fungal or bacterial skin infections, parasitic infections, skin manifestations related to tuberculosis or syphilis.
- Contraindicated in skin conditions including: perioral dermatitis, acne vulgaris, atrophic skin, striae atrophicae, fragility of skin veins, ichthyosis, acne rosacea, rosacea, ulceration and wounds, and in perianal and genital pruritus.
- Guttate, erythrodermic, exfoliative and pustular psoriasis.
- Severe renal insufficiency (contraindicated).
- Severe hepatic impairment (contraindicated).
- Known hypersensitivity to excipients with known effect:
  - Hydrogenated castor oil (contains peanut products) — caution in patients with peanut allergy.
  - Butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT) — may cause local skin reactions or eye/mucous membrane irritation.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Systemic corticosteroid effects (including hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal [HPA] axis suppression) can occur with topical corticosteroids, particularly with prolonged use, application to large areas, use under occlusion, or application to damaged skin or mucous membranes.
- Application under occlusion should generally be avoided unless supervised by a clinician because it significantly increases corticosteroid absorption.
- Avoid applying the product to large areas of damaged skin or to mucous membranes/skin folds because of increased systemic absorption.
- Repeated treatment of large body surface areas should be avoided because of risk of systemic adverse effects.
- Face and genital areas are particularly sensitive to corticosteroid adverse effects and should not be treated with this product.
- Concurrent topical corticosteroid therapy on the same area (e.g. scalp) should be avoided.
- Phototoxicity and stability of calcipotriol in sunlight/UV have not been established; patients should be advised to protect treated areas from direct sunlight and UV exposure (physical covering and/or sunscreens) and to avoid combining with UV therapy unless benefits outweigh risks.
- If lesions become secondarily infected, treat with appropriate antimicrobial therapy; stop topical corticosteroid treatment if the infection worsens.
- Discontinuation of topical corticosteroid therapy may result in rebound effects or, in psoriasis, the risk of generalised pustular psoriasis—continue medical supervision in the post-treatment period.
- With long-term or repeated use there is an increased risk of both local and systemic corticosteroid adverse effects; discontinue therapy if systemic corticosteroid-related adverse effects occur.
- Monitor for signs and symptoms of hypercalcaemia if dosing limits are exceeded or in high total exposures.
- Avoid application to the eyes and instruct patients to avoid accidental transfer to the face, mouth and eyes.
- Adverse reactions to excipients (e.g. contact dermatitis) may occur.

### Monitoring requirements
- Consider assessment of adrenal function (e.g. morning cortisol, ACTH stimulation testing) if topical treatment is prolonged, large areas are treated, occlusion is used, or clinical signs of adrenal suppression appear.
- Monitor serum calcium if excessive application or clinical symptoms suggest hypercalcaemia (polyuria, constipation, muscle weakness, confusion).
- Monitor blood glucose in diabetic patients as systemic corticosteroids can affect metabolic control.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid use in children and adolescents <18 years due to lack of safety/efficacy data and increased risk because of higher skin surface area-to-body mass ratio.
- Use with caution in elderly patients; increased sensitivity cannot be excluded.
- Contraindicated in severe renal or hepatic impairment; safety not established in less severe impairment.
- Advise breastfeeding mothers to avoid application to the breast area and to wash hands before handling the infant.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- Clinical trial programme included more than 6,000 patients, with >3,000 exposed to the combination product.
- Frequency definitions used in available sources:
  - Very common: ≥1/10
  - Common: ≥1/100 and <1/10
  - Uncommon: ≥1/1,000 and <1/100
  - Rare: ≥1/10,000 and <1/1,000
  - Very rare: <1/10,000

- Summary safety signals:
  - In scalp psoriasis studies, approximately 8% of patients experienced a non-serious adverse drug reaction possibly related to treatment.
  - Most frequently reported adverse reaction overall: pruritus.
  - Systemic corticosteroid-related adverse events (HPA axis suppression, Cushingoid features) have been reported with misuse/excessive exposure and with prolonged high-dose use; these are rare but can be severe.
  - Calcipotriol-related systemic effects: hypercalcaemia or hypercalciuria may very rarely occur (particularly if dose limits exceeded).

| Immune system disorders | Rare (≥1/10,000 and <1/1,000) | Hypersensitivity |
| Eye disorders | Uncommon (≥1/1,000 and <1/100) | Eye irritation |

|  | Rare (≥1/10,000 and <1/1,000) | Skin striae; skin exfoliation |

- Additional adverse reactions described as related to individual pharmacological classes:
  - Calcipotriol-related reactions: application site reactions, pruritus, skin irritation, burning and stinging sensation, dry skin, erythema, rash, dermatitis, eczema, aggravation of psoriasis, photosensitivity, transient changes in skin pigmentation, hypersensitivity reactions including very rare angioedema and facial oedema; very rarely systemic hypercalcaemia/hypercalciuria.
  - Betamethasone (potent topical corticosteroid) related reactions: local skin atrophy, telangiectasia, striae, folliculitis, hypertrichosis, perioral dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis, depigmentation, colloid milia; risk of generalised pustular psoriasis when treating psoriasis; risk of rebound on discontinuation after long-term use; rare but potentially severe systemic reactions (HPA axis suppression, Cushing’s syndrome, cataract, infections, impaired metabolic control of diabetes mellitus, increased intraocular pressure).
- Serious adverse effects highlighted:
  - HPA axis suppression and secondary adrenal insufficiency (may require gradual withdrawal and symptomatic treatment).
  - Cushing’s syndrome reported with prolonged misuse/excessive topical exposure.
  - Hypercalcaemia associated with excessive calcipotriol exposure.
  - Increased intraocular pressure and cataract with corticosteroid exposure near the eye.
- Reporting of suspected adverse reactions is important for ongoing benefit–risk monitoring.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- No formal interaction studies have been performed with the combination product.
- No clinical experience documented with concurrent systemic anti-psoriatic agents or with phototherapy (UV) in available sources.
- Pharmacodynamic considerations:
  - Concomitant use of other topical corticosteroids on the same area increases cumulative corticosteroid exposure and risk of local/systemic corticosteroid effects — avoid concurrent topical steroids on the treated area.
  - Systemic corticosteroid effects from topical absorption could theoretically interact with systemic corticosteroid-sensitive conditions (e.g. diabetes) — monitor metabolic control.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Management notes:
  - Avoid combining with other topical corticosteroids on the same site.
  - Use with UV therapy should be considered only if expected benefits outweigh risks due to limited data on phototoxicity and stability of calcipotriol in sunlight/UV.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category (as reported): Category B3 [Australian].
- There are no adequate human data on use during pregnancy. Animal studies with corticosteroids have shown reproductive toxicity (e.g. cleft palate, skeletal malformations) at systemic exposures; calcipotriol caused skeletal variations in rats at certain oral doses; the clinical relevance to humans is unknown.
- Recommendation: Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.

### Breastfeeding
- Betamethasone is excreted in breast milk; it is unknown whether topical administration of the combination results in significant transfer to human milk.
- Calcipotriol excretion in human milk: not specified in available sources.
- Recommendation: Caution should be exercised when prescribing to breastfeeding women. Avoid application to the breast area; mothers should wash hands thoroughly after application before handling the infant. Use only if potential benefit outweighs potential risk.

### Fertility
- Effects of the combination on fertility have not been investigated in animals.
- Oral calcipotriol studies in rats showed no impairment of male or female fertility at studied doses.
- Not specified in available sources for human fertility effects.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Not recommended for children and adolescents <18 years due to lack of safety and efficacy data.
- Children (particularly under 12 years) may be at increased risk of systemic adverse effects because of higher skin surface area-to-body mass ratio.

### Elderly use
- Clinical experience has not identified response differences in patients ≥65 years; however, some elderly individuals may have increased sensitivity. No specific dose adjustment provided.

### Renal impairment
- Safety has not been established in patients with renal impairment.
- Contraindicated in severe renal impairment.

### Hepatic impairment
- Safety has not been established in patients with hepatic impairment.
- Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- The combination pairs two complementary mechanisms:
  - Calcipotriol: synthetic vitamin D3 analogue that binds the active vitamin D receptor (VDR) and modulates keratinocyte proliferation and differentiation, aiming to normalise the epidermal growth characteristic of psoriasis. It has less effect than calcitriol on calcium metabolism.
  - Betamethasone dipropionate: a potent topical corticosteroid producing anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, vasoconstrictive and immunosuppressive effects; these effects are not curative of underlying disease but reduce inflammation and symptoms.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Calcipotriol induces differentiation and suppresses proliferation of keratinocytes without cytotoxicity.
- Betamethasone provides rapid anti-inflammatory and antipruritic effects; penetration and effect are enhanced substantially under occlusion (approximately tenfold increase in stratum corneum penetration noted).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Detailed systemic pharmacokinetic parameters (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination) are not specified in available sources.
- Clinical and pharmacodynamic data indicate systemic absorption can occur, particularly with:
  - Application over large surface areas,
  - Prolonged use,
  - Use on damaged skin or mucous membranes,
  - Use under occlusive dressings.
- Effects on cortisol serum levels and HPA axis have been observed in studies with extensive combined application to scalp and body.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Development programme included over 6,000 patients overall; >3,000 patients treated with the combination.

### Key scalp psoriasis trials
- Two randomised, double-blind, 8-week trials in patients with at least mild scalp psoriasis (total >2,900 patients) compared once-daily combination gel with betamethasone gel, calcipotriol gel, and in one trial vehicle alone. Primary endpoint: absent or very mild disease by Investigator’s Global Assessment (IGA) at week 8.
  - Efficacy findings (pooled presentation from reported data):
    - Week 2: patients achieving absent/very mild disease — combination ~53.2% vs betamethasone ~42.8% vs calcipotriol ~17.2% vs vehicle ~11.8%.
    - Week 8: combination ~69.8% vs betamethasone ~62.5% vs calcipotriol ~40.1% vs vehicle ~22.8%.
  - Combination product showed statistically significant superiority over individual components and vehicle for the primary endpoints and speed of onset.

- One randomized, investigator-blinded study (n=312) compared once-daily combination gel to calcipotriol solution twice daily for up to 8 weeks in patients with at least moderate scalp psoriasis.
  - At week 8, proportion clear or minimal disease: combination ~68.6% vs calcipotriol solution ~31.4% (statistically significant).

### Key body psoriasis trial
- Multicentre, randomised, double-blind trial in patients with mild to moderate plaque psoriasis on the body (n≈1,152 randomised across four arms: combination gel, betamethasone in vehicle, calcipotriol in vehicle, vehicle alone), treated once daily for 8 weeks. Primary outcome: clear or almost clear plus ≥2-step improvement on IGA.
  - Results:
    - Week 4: combination ~13.3% vs betamethasone ~12.5% vs calcipotriol ~5.2% vs vehicle ~2.1%.
    - Week 8: combination ~29.0% vs betamethasone ~21.5% vs calcipotriol ~14.6% vs vehicle ~6.3%.
  - Combination showed superior efficacy at week 8 versus comparators.

### Safety findings in trials
- Overall safety: the most common treatment-related adverse reaction was pruritus.
- Scalp studies: ~8% of patients had a non-serious adverse drug reaction possibly related to study medication; common scalp-related reactions included pruritus, and uncommon reactions included burning, irritation, folliculitis, dermatitis, erythema, acne, dry skin, exacerbation of psoriasis, rash, pustular rash, eye irritation.
- Body studies: adverse drug reactions occurred in small percentages; pruritus reported in ~1.5% in a controlled analysis for body application.

### Noted case reports / overdose
- Misuse example: a patient treated chronically with very high weekly doses (approx. 240 g/week of ointment; daily ~34 g) developed Cushing’s syndrome and, after abrupt cessation, developed pustular psoriasis. Overdose and chronic excessive use can lead to Cushingoid features and HPA suppression; abrupt withdrawal may precipitate severe rebound.
