# Editorial Product Information: Celecoxib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of celecoxib for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and administration guidelines into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Celecoxib
- **Chemical Class:** Diaryl-substituted pyrazole; non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID); selective cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** APX-CELECOXIB [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M01AH01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Hard capsules: 100 mg (white, opaque, blue bands, marked "100") [Australian].
    - Hard capsules: 200 mg (white, opaque, gold bands, marked "200") [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Symptomatic treatment** of osteoarthritis (OA), rheumatoid arthritis (RA), and ankylosing spondylitis (AS).
- **Primary dysmenorrhoea** in adults.
- **Short-term treatment of acute pain** in adults (e.g., post-surgery, musculoskeletal, or soft tissue injury).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
To mitigate cardiovascular risk, the lowest effective dose must be used for the shortest duration necessary. Long-term therapy should be reviewed every three months [Australian].

### Adult Dosing by Indication
- **Osteoarthritis:** 200 mg daily, administered as a single dose or in two divided doses.
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** 200 mg daily in two divided doses. For flares or exacerbations, the dose may be increased up to 400 mg daily for short-term management.
- **Ankylosing Spondylitis:** 200 mg daily (once daily or in two divided doses); this is the maximum recommended dose.
- **Primary Dysmenorrhoea:** 400 mg on the first day (single or divided dose), followed by 200 mg once daily on subsequent days. An additional 200 mg may be taken on any day if required. Maximum duration: 5 days.
- **Acute Pain (Post-surgical/Musculoskeletal):** Initial loading dose of 400 mg, followed by 200 mg once or twice daily as required. The effective dose is typically 200 mg twice daily. Maximum duration: 5 days.

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules may be taken with or without food.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to celecoxib, any excipients (including lactose), or sulfonamides.
- **NSAID Sensitivity:** History of asthma, urticaria, or allergic-type reactions after taking aspirin or other NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors).
- **Cardiovascular:** Peri-operative pain management in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery; unstable ischaemic heart disease; documented myocardial infarction (MI) or stroke within the last 3 months; congestive heart failure (NYHA Class II-IV).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Active peptic ulceration or gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding.
- **Renal:** Estimated creatinine clearance <30 mL/min.
- **Hepatic:** Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh score ≥10).
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Use with other non-aspirin NSAIDs [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Thrombotic Events:** All NSAIDs, including COX-2 inhibitors, carry an increased risk of serious, potentially fatal CV events (MI and stroke). Risk increases with dose, duration, and pre-existing CV disease.
- **Gastrointestinal Effects:** Risk of bleeding, ulceration, and perforation. Highest risk in the elderly, those with prior GI disease, or those using concomitant corticosteroids, anticoagulants, or alcohol.
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Rare but potentially fatal reactions include Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS). Onset usually occurs within the first month. New reports also include fixed drug eruption (FDE) and generalised bullous fixed drug eruption (GBFDE) [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** Celecoxib can cause new-onset hypertension or worsen existing disease. Blood pressure monitoring is required during initiation and throughout therapy.
- **Renal Effects:** Long-term use may cause renal papillary necrosis. Caution is required in patients with heart failure, liver dysfunction, or those taking ACE inhibitors/diuretics.
- **Hepatic Effects:** Borderline elevations in liver tests occur in up to 15% of patients. Severe reactions (jaundice, fatal hepatitis) are rare.
- **Corticosteroids:** Celecoxib is not a substitute for corticosteroids. Abrupt discontinuation of steroids may lead to disease exacerbation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (1–10%)
- **Nervous System:** Headache (15.8%), dizziness (2.0%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Dyspepsia (8.8%), diarrhoea (5.6%), abdominal pain (4.1%), nausea (3.5%), flatulence (2.2%).
- **Respiratory:** Upper respiratory tract infection (8.1%), sinusitis (5.0%), pharyngitis (2.3%), rhinitis (2.0%).
- **General:** Peripheral oedema (2.1%), injury (2.9%), insomnia (2.3%).
- **Skin:** Rash (2.2%).

### Uncommon to Rare (<1%)
- **Cardiovascular:** Myocardial infarction, angina pectoris, tachycardia, aggravated hypertension, arrhythmia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Intestinal obstruction, perforation, GI bleeding, pancreatitis, colitis, oesophageal perforation.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Cholelithiasis, hepatitis, jaundice, hepatic failure.
- **Renal:** Acute renal failure, nephrolithiasis, interstitial nephritis.
- **Haematological:** Thrombocytopenia, anaemia, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP2C9 Inhibitors (e.g., Fluconazole):** Can double celecoxib plasma concentrations; initiate at the lowest recommended dose.
- **CYP2C9 Inducers (e.g., Rifampicin, Carbamazepine):** May reduce celecoxib concentrations.
- **CYP2D6 Substrates:** Celecoxib is a moderate inhibitor of CYP2D6. May increase levels of drugs like metoprolol and dextromethorphan.
- **Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin, Apixaban):** Increased risk of bleeding. Monitor INR closely, especially in the elderly.
- **Antihypertensives (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, Diuretics, Beta-blockers):** NSAIDs may diminish the antihypertensive effect and increase the risk of acute renal failure (the "triple whammy" with ACEi/ARB and diuretics).
- **Lithium:** NSAIDs can increase steady-state lithium levels by ~17%.
- **Methotrexate:** Use with caution; monitor for methotrexate toxicity.
- **Aspirin:** Can be used with low-dose aspirin, but the risk of GI ulceration is significantly increased compared to celecoxib alone.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B3 [Australian].** Not recommended unless clinically essential. 
    - **Third Trimester:** Should be avoided (risk of premature closure of the ductus arteriosus).
    - **From 20 weeks gestation:** May cause fetal renal dysfunction leading to oligohydramnios and neonatal renal impairment. 
- **Breastfeeding:** Studies show very low transfer into breast milk. A decision should weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks to the infant.
- **Fertility:** NSAIDs may delay or prevent ovulation, potentially causing reversible infertility in women.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not approved for patients under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher incidence of adverse events. Start at the lowest dose in patients weighing <50 kg.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild: No adjustment required.
    - Moderate (Child-Pugh Class B): Reduce dose by 50%.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh Class C): Contraindicated.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild/Moderate: No adjustment generally required.
    - Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min): Contraindicated.
- **CYP2C9 Poor Metabolisers:** Use with caution; consider starting at a reduced dose due to potentially high plasma levels.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective inhibitor of cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2). It inhibits the conversion of arachidonic acid to prostaglandins involved in inflammation and pain, while sparing COX-1 (at therapeutic doses), thus preserving COX-1 mediated physiological processes in the stomach and platelets.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak levels at 2–3 hours. High-fat meals delay absorption by 1–2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (~97%); Vd of ~400 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP2C9 in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** <1% excreted unchanged in urine; 57% in faeces and 27% in urine as metabolites. Half-life is 4–15 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Cardiovascular Safety (PRECISION):** A large double-blind study (N=24,081) demonstrated non-inferiority of celecoxib (200–400 mg/day) compared to naproxen or ibuprofen regarding the composite endpoint of CV death, non-fatal MI, or non-fatal stroke.
- **Gastrointestinal Safety (CLASS):** A 12-month study in 8,000 patients showed that celecoxib (at supra-therapeutic doses) was associated with a lower incidence of symptomatic ulcers and complications compared to ibuprofen or diclofenac, particularly in those not taking concomitant aspirin.

## 12. Storage Conditions
