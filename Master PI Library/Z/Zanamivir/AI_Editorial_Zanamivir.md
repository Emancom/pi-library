# Editorial Product Information: Zanamivir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for information purposes and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Zanamivir, a potent and highly selective neuraminidase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Relenza Rotadisk** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AH01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Inhalation powder; each blister contains 5 mg of zanamivir. Prepared with 20 mg of lactose monohydrate (containing milk protein) as a carrier.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral inhalation only, via the **Diskhaler** device.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Treatment of Influenza:** Indicated for the treatment of infections caused by Influenza A and B viruses in adults and children aged 5 years and older. Treatment should be initiated within 48 hours of symptom onset [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis of Influenza:** 
    - **Post-exposure Prophylaxis:** To reduce transmission in households with an infected individual (adults and children $\ge$ 5 years) [Australian].
    - **Community Outbreak Prophylaxis:** Indicated during community outbreaks in specific circumstances where prophylaxis is justified (e.g., vaccine mismatch or pandemic) [Australian]. 
- **Note:** Zanamivir is not recommended for routine prophylaxis. Vaccination remains the primary method for influenza control.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children $\ge$ 5 years)
- **Treatment:** 10 mg (two 5 mg inhalations) twice daily for five days. Total daily dose is 20 mg.
- **Prophylaxis:** 10 mg (two 5 mg inhalations) once daily for 10 days.
- **Extended Prophylaxis:** The duration may be increased to 28 days if the exposure risk is prolonged [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Device Requirement:** Must only be administered using the provided **Diskhaler**.
- **Concurrent Inhaled Medications:** Patients using fast-acting bronchodilators should be advised to use them *prior* to zanamivir administration.
- **Supervision:** Paediatric use must be under adult supervision.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to zanamivir or any excipients (e.g., lactose).
- **Milk Protein Allergy:** Explicitly contraindicated in patients with a history of severe milk protein allergy due to the lactose monohydrate carrier [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Respiratory Complications (Bronchospasm):** There are reports of bronchospasm and decline in respiratory function following use. Zanamivir is not adequately assessed in patients with severe asthma or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). If used in these populations, patients must have a fast-acting bronchodilator available and be monitored closely.
- **Administration Limitations:** **Inhalation powder must not be used for nebulisation or mechanical ventilation.** Fatalities have occurred when the powder (containing lactose) obstructed the proper functioning of ventilator equipment [Australian].
- **Neuropsychiatric Events:** Post-marketing reports (primarily from Japan and in paediatric populations) indicate risks of delirium and abnormal behaviour leading to injury. Patients should be monitored for signs of abnormal behaviour; the risks and benefits of continuing treatment should be weighed if symptoms appear.
- **Bacterial Infections:** Zanamivir is specific to influenza viruses; it does not treat bacterial complications.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in clinical trials were generally similar between the zanamivir and placebo groups, often indistinguishable from influenza symptoms.

### Common Adverse Effects ($\ge$ 1.5% to 2%)
- **Neurological:** Headache.
- **Respiratory:** Cough, nasal signs/symptoms, throat and tonsil pain/discomfort, viral respiratory infections.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea.
- **General:** Malaise, fatigue, fever/chills.

### Post-marketing Experience (Very Rare < 0.01%)
- **Immune System:** Allergic-type reactions, including anaphylaxis/anaphylactoid reactions, facial and oropharyngeal oedema.
- **Respiratory:** Severe bronchospasm, dyspnoea.
- **Skin:** Rash, urticaria, severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCARs) including Erythema Multiforme, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis.
- **Nervous System:** Vasovagal-like reactions following inhalation in patients with fever or dehydration.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Zanamivir is excreted unchanged in the urine via glomerular filtration. It is not a substrate for P-glycoprotein (Pgp) and does not affect major cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes (1A2, 2A6, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, 3A4).
- **Clinically Significant Interactions:** Evaluations with common medications (aspirin, ibuprofen, paracetamol, codeine, and certain antibiotics) have shown no clinically significant interactions.
- **Renal Transport:** No evidence of interaction with organic anion, cation, or urate transporters.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** (Drugs which have been taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the human foetus having been observed).
    - **Recommendation:** Safety has not been established. Use is discouraged, particularly in the first trimester, unless the benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Animal studies show secretion in milk; however, human data are unavailable. Use in nursing mothers should be considered only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the infant.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no adverse effects on fertility or reproductive performance.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children $\ge$ 5 years. No dose modification required.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose modification required for patients $\ge$ 65 years, though clinical experience is limited.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose or frequency modification is required. While systemic exposure increases in severe impairment, the low systemic absorption from oral inhalation maintains safety margins.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Zanamivir is not hepatically metabolised; therefore, no dose modification is required.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Zanamivir inhibits viral neuraminidase, an enzyme essential for the release of newly formed influenza virions from infected cells. This prevents the spread of infection within the respiratory tract.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 4% to 17% of the inhaled dose is absorbed systemically. Peak serum concentrations are reached within 1–2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Widely deposited in the respiratory tract (approximately 77.6% in the oropharynx and 13.2% in the lungs). Protein binding is low (<10%).
    - **Metabolism:** None.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted unchanged in the urine with a serum half-life of 2.5 to 5.1 hours after oral inhalation.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Treatment Efficacy:** In five Phase III studies, zanamivir reduced the median time to alleviation of symptoms by 1.5 days compared to placebo in the influenza-positive population (p < 0.001).
- **High-Risk Population:** A pooled analysis showed a significant reduction in complications (29% in placebo vs 22% in zanamivir) and a 24% reduction in antibiotic use for complications.
- **Prophylaxis Efficacy:** 
    - **Household:** 79% to 81% protective efficacy in preventing symptomatic laboratory-confirmed influenza.
    - **Community:** 67% to 83% protective efficacy during outbreak periods.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Blisters should only be pierced immediately before inhalation using the Diskhaler.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
