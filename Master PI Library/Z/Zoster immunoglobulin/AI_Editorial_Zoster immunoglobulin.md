# Editorial Product Information: Human Zoster Immunoglobulin

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Human Zoster Immunoglobulin, synthesised for healthcare professionals to facilitate clinical decision-making. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Human Zoster Immunoglobulin (composed of at least 98% Immunoglobulin G [IgG]).
- **Chemical Class:** Plasma-derived fractionated immunoglobulin.
- **Brand Name(s):** Zoster Immunoglobulin-VF [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J06BB03 (Not specified in available source, but standard for this class).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile, preservative-free solution for injection containing 160 mg/mL of human plasma protein. Each vial contains at least 200 IU of antibodies to varicella-zoster virus [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** CSL Behring (Australia) Pty Ltd; manufactured from plasma collected by Australian Red Cross Lifeblood [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
[Australian-approved]: Prophylaxis against varicella (chickenpox) in patients meeting **all four** of the following criteria:

1.  **High-risk underlying condition:** Neoplastic disease (e.g., leukaemia, lymphoma), congenital or acquired immunodeficiency, or current immunosuppressive therapy (steroids or antimetabolites).
2.  **Significant exposure to chickenpox or shingles:** Defined as household contact, playmate contact (>1 hour indoors), hospital contact (same room or adjacent beds), or newborn contact (mother onset <5 days before or 48 hours after delivery).
3.  **Serological status:** Negative or unknown history of varicella.
4.  **Timing:** The product can be administered within 96 hours of exposure.

**Clinical Limitations:** This product is not indicated for the treatment of established varicella or zoster infections. It is generally not indicated for prophylaxis in immunodeficient patients with a known history of varicella, except for those undergoing bone marrow transplantation [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
Dosage is determined by patient body weight as follows [Australian]:

| Patient Weight (kg) | Dose (IU) | Number of Vials |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0–10 | 125 | 1 |
| 10.1–20 | 250 | 2 |
| 20.1–30 | 375 | 2 |
| 30.1–40 | 500 | 3 |
| > 40 | 600 | 3 |

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Bring the solution to room temperature before use. Do not use if the solution is turbid or contains sediment.
- **Technique:** Administer via slow, deep intramuscular injection. Aspirate prior to injection to ensure the needle is not in a blood vessel.
- **Volume limits:** If large volumes are required, doses should be divided and administered at different sites. This applies to volumes >2 mL in children $\le$ 20 kg and >5 mL in individuals >20 kg [Australian].
- **Additives:** Hyaluronidase or local anaesthetics may be added if desired [Australian].
- **Timing:** Administer as soon as possible after exposure, preferably within 96 hours.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known anaphylactic or severe systemic reactions to human immunoglobulin or any components (e.g., glycine) [Australian].
- **Selective IgA Deficiency:** Contraindicated in patients with IgA deficiency where anti-IgA antibodies are present, as the product contains trace amounts of IgA (<0.5 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Coagulation Disorders:** Severe thrombocytopenia or other clotting disorders that contraindicate intramuscular injections [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Route of Administration Warning:** **MUST NOT** be administered intravenously. Intravenous administration carries a significant risk of anaphylactic reactions [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rarely, a precipitous drop in blood pressure and anaphylaxis can occur even in patients who previously tolerated human immunoglobulin. 
- **Observation:** Patients should be monitored for at least 20 minutes post-administration. If accidental intravenous injection occurs, monitoring should extend to at least 1 hour [Australian].
- **Pathogen Safety:** As a plasma-derived product, the risk of transmitting infectious agents (including viruses and CJD) cannot be totally excluded. The manufacturing process includes pasteurisation (viral inactivation) and nanofiltration (virus removal), effective against HIV, HBV, HCV, HAV, and parvovirus B19 [Australian].
- **Trace IgA:** Contains <0.5 mg/mL IgA. May cause reactions in IgA-deficient patients.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Local Reactions
- Common: Tenderness, erythema (redness), and stiffness at the injection site, which may persist for several hours [Australian].

### Systemic Reactions
- **Occasionally reported:** Mild pyrexia, malaise, drowsiness, urticaria, and angioedema.
- **Rarely reported:** Allergic responses, skin lesions, headache, dizziness, nausea, and convulsions [Australian].

### Clinical Trial Data (Proxy Study)
In a comparative trial using Hepatitis B Immunoglobulin as a representative IM product, the following general reactions were noted in 58 subjects: malaise (34%), drowsiness (22%), and induration (12%) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Live Attenuated Vaccines:** Passively acquired antibodies may interfere with the immune response to live vaccines (e.g., measles, mumps, rubella, varicella). 
    - **Management:** Defer live vaccination for at least 3 months following immunoglobulin administration. 
    - If immunoglobulin is given within 2 weeks of vaccination, the vaccine efficacy may be compromised, and revaccination should be considered 3 months later [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** Passively transferred antibodies can lead to misleading positive results in serological tests (e.g., Coombs' test, anti-A, anti-B, anti-D) [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** Must not be mixed with other medicinal products unless specified [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category: Not assigned]. Safety in human pregnancy has not been established in controlled trials. Use with caution; should only be administered to pregnant women when clearly required [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been established in clinical trials. While immunoglobulins are excreted in breast milk, it is unknown if this applies to passively administered Zoster Immunoglobulin [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No reproductive toxicity studies have been conducted [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been formally established in paediatric-specific studies. However, spontaneous reporting does not indicate an over-representation of adverse events in this population [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific studies have not been conducted. Clinical experience has not identified differences in responses between the elderly and younger patients [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Provides passive immunity by increasing the titre of circulating antibodies against varicella-zoster virus, thereby preventing or modifying the severity of the infection in susceptible individuals [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Effective prophylaxis is associated with administration within 96 hours of exposure.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** In a study of a similar IM immunoglobulin product, maximal serum IgG concentration was reached after $8.0 \pm 5.5$ days. The estimated half-life of IgG was $27.2 \pm 6.6$ days [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Specific pivotal clinical trials for Zoster Immunoglobulin-VF were not conducted. Clinical data is largely extrapolated from a comparative trial of pasteurised vs. unpasteurised Hepatitis B Immunoglobulin, which demonstrated no significant clinical differences in in vivo behaviour following viral inactivation [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [Australian].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light.
- **Stability:** Use immediately after opening the vial as it contains no antimicrobial preservative [Australian].
