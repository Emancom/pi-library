# Editorial Product Information: Zopiclone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information regarding other regulatory jurisdictions (e.g., US FDA, European EMA, Health Canada) was not provided in the source material and is therefore not included in this harmonised overview.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information mandated for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Zopiclone (a cyclopyrrolone derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Non-benzodiazepine hypnotic; structurally unrelated to existing hypnotics but possessing a similar pharmacological profile to benzodiazepines.
- **ATC Code:** N05CF01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - **Oral Tablet:** 7.5 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Insomnia:** Short-term treatment (7–14 days) of insomnia [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** 7.5 mg administered orally shortly before retiring. This dose should not be exceeded. Based on clinical response, the dose may be reduced to 3.75 mg [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Zopiclone must be taken as a single intake and should not be re-administered during the same night.
- **Duration of Treatment:** Limited to 7–14 days. Any extension beyond this period requires a clinical re-evaluation of the patient due to the increased risk of abuse and dependence [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources; however, the requirement for a full night’s sleep (7–8 hours) prior to activity is emphasized.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to zopiclone or any excipients (e.g., lactose).
- Prior or concomitant use of alcohol.
- Myasthenia gravis.
- Severe impairment of respiratory function.
- Acute cerebrovascular accident.
- Sleep apnoea syndrome.
- Severe hepatic insufficiency.
- History of complex sleep behaviours (e.g., sleep-walking, sleep-driving) following zopiclone use.
- Paediatric patients (under 18 years of age) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Complex Sleep Behaviours:** Use may lead to sleep-walking, sleep-driving, and other activities performed while not fully awake. These behaviours can result in fatal injuries. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if a patient experiences such an event [Australian].
- **Dependence and Abuse:** Risk increases with dose and duration of treatment, particularly beyond four weeks. Caution is required in patients with a history of psychiatric disorders or substance abuse. Abrupt cessation after prolonged use may cause withdrawal symptoms.
- **Rebound Insomnia:** Withdrawal may lead to a transient recurrence of insomnia in an enhanced form; gradual dose tapering is recommended [Australian].
- **CNS Depression and Impairment:** Zopiclone can impair daytime function even if the patient feels fully awake. The risk of psychomotor impairment is higher if the drug is taken with less than a full night's sleep remaining or if performed activities require mental alertness within 12 hours of dosing.
- **Depression and Suicidality:** Zopiclone is not a treatment for depression and may mask its symptoms. An increased incidence of suicide attempts has been noted in patients using hypnotics.
- **Severe Anaphylactic Reactions:** Rare cases of angioedema involving the tongue, glottis, or larynx have been reported and can be fatal. Do not rechallenge patients who experience these reactions.
- **Respiratory Insufficiency:** Caution is advised as hypnotics can depress respiratory drive [Australian].
- **Haemolysis:** Reported in cases of overdose [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Categorisation
- **Common (>1%):** Taste alteration (bitter taste), dry mouth, drowsiness, headache, and fatigue.
- **Less Common/Rare (<1%):** 
  - **Nervous System:** Agitation, anxiety, anterograde amnesia, confusion, dizziness, tremor, nightmares, hallucinations, and ataxia.
  - **Gastrointestinal:** Heartburn, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, or constipation.
  - **Immune System:** Angioedema and anaphylactic reactions (very rare).
  - **Hepatobiliary:** Rare, minor increases in serum transaminases/alkaline phosphatase.
  - **Psychiatric:** Paradoxical reactions (e.g., irritability, aggression, delusion, anger), more common in the elderly [Australian].
- **Withdrawal Symptoms:** Muscle pain, tachycardia, tremors, sweating, and in severe cases, derealisation or seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Concomitant intake is contraindicated as it enhances the sedative effect of zopiclone.
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects occur with antipsychotics, hypnotics, anxiolytics, antidepressants, narcotic analgesics, antiepileptics, and anaesthetics.
- **Opioids:** Concurrent use increases the risk of profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death. Reserve for cases where alternative treatments are inadequate and use the lowest effective dose [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., erythromycin, clarithromycin, ketoconazole, ritonavir) Increase zopiclone plasma levels and may enhance hypnotic effects.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, St John's Wort) Decrease zopiclone plasma levels [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Not recommended. Zopiclone crosses the placenta. Use in the third trimester or during labour may cause neonatal hypothermia, hypotonia, and respiratory depression. Withdrawal symptoms may occur in neonates born to chronic users.
- **Breastfeeding:** Zopiclone and its metabolites are excreted in breast milk; use in nursing mothers is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed reduced male fertility at high doses (50 mg/kg/day). Human significance is unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated; safety and efficacy in children under 18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** More susceptible to sedation, ataxia, and confusion, increasing fall risk. Start with a lower dose (3.75 mg); do not exceed 7.5 mg [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Although accumulation is not typically seen, treatment should initiate at 3.75 mg [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In severe hepatic insufficiency, elimination is significantly reduced. Initiate at 3.75 mg; maximum 7.5 mg with caution [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A short-acting hypnotic that binds to the GABA-A receptor complex, though structurally distinct from benzodiazepines.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces sleep latency and nocturnal awakenings, increases sleep duration. Unlike benzodiazepines, it tends to increase or maintain stage 3 and 4 (slow-wave) sleep [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Rapid; Tmax approximately 1.75 hours. Bioavailability is ~76%.
  - **Distribution:** Approximately 45% protein bound.
  - **Metabolism:** Extensively hepatic; major metabolites are N-oxide (weakly active) and N-desmethyl (inactive).
  - **Elimination:** Primarily via hepatic metabolism. Elimination half-life is approximately 5 hours (prolonged to ~7 hours in the elderly and ~11.9 hours in hepatic insufficiency) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Special Precautions:** Protect from light [Australian].
