# Editorial Product Information: Zoledronic Acid

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) document: Australian (TGA: Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for information purposes only. It is not a substitute for the official, approved Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Zoledronic acid (anhydrous).
- **Chemical Class:** Nitrogen-containing bisphosphonate.
- **Brand Names:** Aclasta [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M05B A08.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for intravenous infusion; 5 mg zoledronic acid (anhydrous) in 100 mL solution [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Postmenopausal Osteoporosis:** Treatment to reduce the incidence of hip, vertebral, and non-vertebral fractures [Australian].
- **Osteoporosis in Men:** To increase bone mineral density [Australian].
- **Glucocorticoid-Induced Osteoporosis:** Treatment and prevention of bone mineral density loss associated with long-term systemic glucocorticoid use [Australian].
- **Paget’s Disease of Bone:** Treatment of bone involvement [Australian].
- **Fracture Prevention After Hip Fracture:** Treatment of patients over 50 years of age with a recent low-trauma hip fracture to reduce the incidence of subsequent fractures [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard dose is a single 5 mg intravenous infusion administered over at least 15 minutes.
- **Osteoporosis (Men, Postmenopausal, Glucocorticoid-induced) and Hip Fracture Prevention:** 5 mg administered once annually [Australian].
- **Paget’s Disease of Bone:** A single 5 mg infusion. 
    - **Retreatment:** May be considered in patients who relapse. It should not be administered sooner than 12 months after the initial dose. The mean remission period is approximately 7.7 years [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Must be administered via a separate vented infusion line at a constant rate.
    - **Hydration:** Patients must be appropriately hydrated before and after administration (e.g., two glasses of fluid), especially those on diuretic therapy or the elderly.
    - **Supplementation:** Ensure adequate Calcium and Vitamin D intake. In Paget’s disease, supplemental calcium (500 mg twice daily) is strongly advised for at least 10 days post-infusion [Australian].
- **Pre-treatment Management:** Post-dose symptoms (acute phase reaction) may be reduced by administering paracetamol shortly after the infusion [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to zoledronic acid, any excipients, or any bisphosphonates.
- **Hypocalcaemia.**
- **Severe Renal Impairment:** Creatinine clearance (CrCl) < 35 mL/min [Australian].
- **Ocular Disease:** Current or recent uveitis, or a history of bisphosphonate-associated uveitis [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Lactation.**

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Renal Effects:** Treatment has been associated with renal impairment and, rarely, acute renal failure. CrCl must be calculated prior to each dose. Use with caution in patients taking concomitant nephrotoxic drugs or diuretics.
- **Osteonecrosis of the Jaw (ONJ):** Primarily reported in oncology patients, but also occurs in osteoporosis. Perform a dental examination and appropriate preventive dentistry prior to treatment in patients with risk factors (e.g., poor oral hygiene, corticosteroids, chemotherapy).
- **Atypical Femoral Fractures:** Low-energy subtrochanteric and diaphyseal fractures have been reported. Evaluate any patient presenting with new thigh, hip, or groin pain.
- **Acute Phase Reaction:** Symptoms such as fever, myalgia, and headache typically occur within the first three days post-infusion.
- **Musculoskeletal Pain:** Severe and occasionally incapacitating bone, joint, and/or muscle pain has been reported.
- **Active Ingredient Overlap:** Do not use concurrently with other zoledronic acid-containing products used for oncology (e.g., Zometa) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Pyrexia (18.1%) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Nervous system: Headache, dizziness.
    - Gastrointestinal: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea.
    - Musculoskeletal: Myalgia, arthralgia, bone pain, back pain, pain in extremity.
    - General: Influenza-like illness, chills, fatigue, asthenia, malaise [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Conjunctivitis, eye pain, vertigo, hypertension, flushing, cough, dyspnoea, dyspepsia, abdominal pain, rash, muscle spasms, proteinuria, peripheral oedema.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Uveitis, episcleritis, iritis.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Renal failure, osteonecrosis of the jaw, and atrial fibrillation (serious AF events were increased in one trial: 1.3% vs 0.6% for placebo) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Use with caution when combined with drugs that impact renal function (e.g., aminoglycosides, diuretics causing dehydration) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Zoledronic acid is not systemically metabolised and does not inhibit cytochrome P450 enzymes. It is eliminated via renal excretion.
- **Incompatibilities:** The infusion solution must not come into contact with calcium-containing solutions or other divalent cation-containing solutions [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B3. Contraindicated. There are no data on use in pregnant women; animal studies show reproductive toxicity, including fetal malformations and dystocia.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if zoledronic acid is excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed decreased fertility in rats at 0.01 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children or adolescents under 18 years due to lack of safety/efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required. However, as decreased renal function is common in this population, close monitoring of renal function is necessary.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - CrCl $\geq$ 35 mL/min: No dose adjustment required.
    - CrCl < 35 mL/min: **Contraindicated** [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits osteoclast-mediated bone resorption by inhibiting the enzyme farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase (FPPS) in osteoclasts. It has a high affinity for mineralised bone.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributed to bone. Protein binding is approximately 30–60%.
    - **Metabolism:** Not metabolised in humans.
    - **Elimination:** Triphasic process with half-lives of $t_{1/2\alpha}$ 0.24 h, $t_{1/2\beta}$ 1.87 h, and a terminal elimination half-life $t_{1/2\gamma}$ of 146 hours. Approximately 39% of the dose is excreted unchanged in the urine within 24 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Postmenopausal Osteoporosis (HORIZON-PFT):** A 3-year study (n=7736) showed zoledronic acid 5 mg annually reduced the risk of new vertebral fractures by 70% and hip fractures by 41% [Australian].
- **Hip Fracture Prevention (HORIZON-RFT):** A study (n=2127) of patients with recent low-trauma hip fracture showed a 35% reduction in any new clinical fracture and a 28% reduction in all-cause mortality compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Paget’s Disease:** Two 6-month studies showed a 96% therapeutic response rate (normalisation of alkaline phosphatase) for a single 5 mg infusion compared to 74% for 30 mg oral risedronate [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- After opening, the solution is chemically and physically stable for 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C.
- The product should ideally be used immediately upon opening to maintain microbiological safety [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
