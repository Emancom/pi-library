# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise reference format. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any specific region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport; amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (not less than every 6 hours). Daily maintenance doses of 50 mg/kg are typical, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels (target: 35–60 µmol/L). Downward adjustments to 5 mg/kg may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted to concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s solution [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at this time [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Discontinue treatment immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine (oral or intravenous) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation):** Note that the safety of the *oral* formulation has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency; chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of plasma carnitine concentrations, blood chemistries, and vital signs is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis were reported in clinical trials without regard to causality.

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5% in ESRD Trials) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), flu syndrome (15–29%), headache (3–37%), asthenia (8–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%), fever (5–12%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), arrhythmia (3%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), dyspepsia (6–9%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%), insomnia (3–6%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%) |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress (laryngeal edema/bronchospasm) [US].
- Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) has been reported when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitor INR levels at therapy initiation and after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned in current labelling; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm.] Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if levocarnitine is excreted in human milk; however, studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated based on mg/kg, similar to adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided in the source; standard precautions for age-related organ dysfunction apply.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 for specific ESRD dosing. The injection is the preferred route for dialysis patients to avoid metabolite accumulation associated with oral dosing [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process provides substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain (primarily skeletal and cardiac muscle) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine clears accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitines, which are rapidly excreted. Plasma concentrations <20 µmol/L define carnitine deficiency [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine levels from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to steady-state trough levels between 54 and 180 µmol/L [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15% to 16% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
