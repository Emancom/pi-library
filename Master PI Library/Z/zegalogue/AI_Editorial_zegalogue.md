# Editorial Product Information: Zegalogue (Dasiglucagon)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Dasiglucagon (as dasiglucagon hydrochloride), a glucagon analogue.
- **Brand Name:** **Zegalogue** [US].
- **ATC Code:** H04AA01 (Glucagon and analogues).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
  - Single-dose autoinjector: 0.6 mg/0.6 mL [US].
  - Single-dose prefilled syringe: 0.6 mg/0.6 mL [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novo Nordisk Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Hypoglycemia:** Treatment of severe hypoglycemia in adult and pediatric patients with diabetes who are 6 years of age or older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Pediatrics ≥6 years):** A single 0.6 mg dose administered subcutaneously [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
  - Administer into the lower abdomen, buttocks, thighs, or outer upper arms [US].
  - Call for emergency medical assistance immediately after administration [US].
  - If the patient does not respond within 15 minutes, an additional 0.6 mg dose may be administered using a new device while awaiting emergency help [US].
  - Once the patient responds to treatment, oral carbohydrates should be provided to restore liver glycogen and prevent recurrence [US].
- **Preparation:** Inspect visually before use; the solution must be clear, colorless, and free of particles [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pheochromocytoma:** Contraindicated due to the risk of stimulating catecholamine release, which may cause a substantial increase in blood pressure [US].
- **Insulinoma:** Contraindicated due to the risk of stimulating exaggerated insulin release, which may lead to subsequent hypoglycemia [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Substantial Increase in Blood Pressure:** In patients with pheochromocytoma, dasiglucagon may cause a dangerous rise in blood pressure. If a patient with an undiagnosed tumor experiences such an increase, 5 to 10 mg of intravenous phentolamine mesylate is recommended for management [US].
- **Hypoglycemia in Patients with Insulinoma:** Administration may cause an initial rise in blood glucose but can trigger secondary hypoglycemia. Treat post-dose hypoglycemia with oral or intravenous glucose [US].
- **Hypersensitivity and Allergic Reactions:** Serious reactions, including generalized rash and anaphylactic shock (with hypotension and respiratory distress), have been reported with glucagon products [US].
- **Lack of Efficacy in Decreased Hepatic Glycogen:** Dasiglucagon relies on hepatic glycogen stores to raise blood glucose. It may be ineffective in patients in states of starvation, those with adrenal insufficiency, or those with chronic hypoglycemia. These patients should be treated with glucose [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typically transient and occur shortly after administration.

### Adults (within 12 hours)
| Adverse Reaction | Dasiglucagon (N=116) | Placebo (N=53) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nausea** | 57% | 4% |
| **Vomiting** | 25% | 2% |
| **Headache** | 11% | 4% |
| **Diarrhea** | 5% | 0% |
| **Injection site pain** | 2% | 0% |

### Pediatrics (6–17 years)
- Very Common (>10%): Nausea (65%), Vomiting (50%), Headache (10%) [US].
- Common (1–10%): Injection site pain (5%) [US].

### Other Noted Reactions
- Transient increases in blood pressure, heart rate, bradycardia, palpitations, and orthostatic intolerance [US].
- **Immunogenicity:** Less than 1% of patients developed treatment-emergent anti-drug antibodies (ADAs). No clinical safety or efficacy impact was observed, though long-term effects are unknown [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Beta-blockers:** May cause a transient increase in pulse and blood pressure following dasiglucagon administration [US].
- **Indomethacin:** May interfere with the ability to raise blood glucose or may paradoxically produce hypoglycemia [US].
- **Warfarin:** Dasiglucagon may enhance the anticoagulant effect of warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** There are no available human data. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no adverse developmental effects at 7 to 709 times the human dose. However, at very high doses (≥20 times human exposure), skeletal and visceral malformations were noted in rabbits alongside maternal toxicity. Untreated severe hypoglycemia is a significant risk to both mother and fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There is no information regarding the presence of dasiglucagon in human milk. As a peptide, it is expected to be degraded in the infant’s digestive tract and is unlikely to cause harm [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no impairment of male or female fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Pediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for patients ≥6 years old. Use is not recommended/established for those under 6 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65 and older to determine if they respond differently than younger adults [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically addressed in the available source, though hepatic glycogen stores are required for efficacy (see Section 5) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A glucagon receptor agonist that activates hepatic receptors to stimulate the breakdown of glycogen (glycogenolysis) and the release of glucose into the blood [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Mean peak plasma concentration (Cmax) of 5110 pg/mL reached in ~35 minutes (adults) and 3920 pg/mL in ~21 minutes (pediatrics) [US].
  - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is 47 L to 57 L [US].
  - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 30 minutes [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Cleared via proteolytic degradation in the blood, liver, and kidneys [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In adults, the mean glucose increase from baseline was 168 mg/dL at 90 minutes [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Trials (Trial A & B):** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in patients with type 1 diabetes. The median time to plasma glucose recovery (increase of ≥20 mg/dL) was **10 minutes** for dasiglucagon compared to 35–40 minutes for placebo [US].
- **Pediatric Trial (Trial C):** Conducted in patients aged 6–17 years. The median time to recovery was **10 minutes** for dasiglucagon vs. 30 minutes for placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigerated:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Do not freeze [US].
- **Room Temperature:** May be stored at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) for up to 12 months. Do not return to the refrigerator once stored at room temperature [US].
- **Handling:** Keep in the original protective case to protect from light. Discard after 12 months at room temperature or after the expiration date [US].
- **Latex Warning:** The autoinjector cap and prefilled syringe needle cover contain dry natural rubber [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
