# Editorial Product Information: Gatifloxacin (Ophthalmic)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Gatifloxacin (an 8-methoxyfluoroquinolone antimicrobial).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ZYMAXID** [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01AE06.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Ophthalmic solution 0.5% (5 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic use.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Allergan, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Bacterial Conjunctivitis:** Treatment of infections caused by susceptible strains of the following organisms [US]:
    - **Aerobic Gram-positive bacteria:** *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Staphylococcus epidermidis*, *Streptococcus mitis* group*, *Streptococcus oralis**, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*.
    - **Aerobic Gram-negative bacteria:** *Haemophilus influenzae*.
    - \**Efficacy for these specific organisms was studied in fewer than 10 infections.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:**
    - **Day 1:** Instill one drop into the affected eye(s) every two hours while awake, up to a maximum of 8 times.
    - **Days 2 through 7:** Instill one drop into the affected eye(s) two to four times daily while awake.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - For topical ophthalmic use only; not for injection [US].
    - Patients should be instructed to avoid contaminating the applicator tip with material from the eye, fingers, or other sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of hypersensitivity to gatifloxacin, other quinolones, or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reactions have been reported in patients receiving systemic quinolones. Topical gatifloxacin may cause hypersensitivity even after a single dose, including angioedema (pharyngeal, laryngeal, or facial), dyspnea, urticaria, and itching. Rare cases of **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome** have been reported with topical use. Therapy must be discontinued immediately if an allergic reaction occurs [US].
- **Growth of Resistant Organisms:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi. If superinfection occurs, discontinue use and initiate alternative therapy [US].
- **Corneal Endothelial Cell Injury:** Direct introduction of the solution into the anterior chamber of the eye may cause corneal endothelial cell injury [US].
- **Monitoring:** Clinical judgment should guide examination with the aid of magnification (e.g., slit-lamp biomicroscopy) and fluorescein staining where appropriate [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are categorised by frequency based on clinical study data (N=717) and postmarketing experience.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Worsening of conjunctivitis, eye irritation, dysgeusia (bitter taste), eye pain. |
| **Reported with other formulations** | Chemosis, conjunctival haemorrhage, dry eye, eye discharge, eyelid oedema, headache, increased lacrimation, keratitis, red eye, papillary conjunctivitis, reduced visual acuity. |
| **Postmarketing (Frequency unknown)** | Anaphylactic reactions, angioedema, blepharitis, dyspnea, eye pruritus, eye swelling (corneal/conjunctival), eye allergy, allergic dermatitis, nausea, pruritus, rash, urticaria, blurred vision. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Cross-resistance:** Cross-resistance has been observed between systemic gatifloxacin and some other fluoroquinolones. There is generally no cross-resistance between gatifloxacin and aminoglycosides, macrolides, or tetracyclines [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly letter-categorized in the current US labeling; however, "Risk Summary" states there are no available human data to inform drug-associated risks [US].
    - **Animal Data:** Oral administration in rats and rabbits during organogenesis did not produce adverse outcomes at clinically relevant doses. However, very high oral doses (≥150 mg/kg/day, ~600-fold the maximum human ophthalmic dose) produced teratogenic effects in rats (skeletal malformations, fetal weight reduction) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Gatifloxacin is excreted in the milk of lactating rats following oral administration. While systemic levels after topical ocular administration are very low, it is unknown if measurable levels are present in human milk. The benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rats at oral doses up to 200 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have been established for the treatment of bacterial conjunctivitis in paediatric patients aged **one year and older**. Safety in infants under one year has not been established [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy have been observed between elderly and younger patients [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources for the topical ophthalmic route.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Gatifloxacin inhibits **DNA gyrase** (essential for replication, transcription, and repair of bacterial DNA) and **Topoisomerase IV** (essential for partitioning chromosomal DNA during cell division).
- **Pharmacokinetics:** In a study of healthy males using an escalated dosing regimen (up to 2 drops 8 times daily for 3 days), serum gatifloxacin levels remained below the limit of quantification (5 ng/mL) at all time points [US].
- **Microbiology:** An 8-methoxyfluoroquinolone with a 3-methylpiperazinyl substituent. Resistance typically develops via multiple-step mutations at a frequency of $1 \times 10^{-7}$ to $10^{-10}$ [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Two randomised, double-masked, multicentre trials (N=658) evaluated patients aged 1–89 years.
- **Outcomes:** Gatifloxacin was clinically superior to its vehicle on Day 6.
    - **Clinical Success (Resolution of hyperemia/discharge):** 58% for gatifloxacin vs. 45% for vehicle.
    - **Microbiological Eradication:** 90% for gatifloxacin vs. 70% for vehicle [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 15°C to 25°C (59°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing [US].
- **Packaging:** Supplied in low-density polyethylene (LDPE) bottles with a controlled dropper tip.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
