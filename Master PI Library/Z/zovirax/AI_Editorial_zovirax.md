# Editorial Product Information: Zovirax (Aciclovir / Aciclovir and Hydrocortisone)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and US (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating data for topical **Zovirax** formulations. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or prescribing requirements of any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local PI for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Aciclovir (INN) [US: Acyclovir]; Hydrocortisone (INN) [Australian].
- **Chemical Class:** Aciclovir is a synthetic purine deoxynucleoside analogue; Hydrocortisone is a corticosteroid.
- **Brand Names:** 
    - **Zovirax Duo** [Australian]
    - **Zovirax Cream** [US]
- **ATC Code:** D06BB53 (Aciclovir, combinations) or D06BB03 (Aciclovir).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Topical Cream (Combination):** Aciclovir 5% w/w and Hydrocortisone 1% w/w [Australian].
    - **Topical Cream (Monotherapy):** Aciclovir 5% w/w (50 mg per gram) [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Topical (Cutaneous).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Haleon Australia [Australian].
    - Bausch Health US, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Recurrent Herpes Labialis (Cold Sores):** Treatment of recurrent cold sores on the lips and face in immunocompetent adults and adolescents aged 12 years and older. 
- **Ulcerative Lesion Reduction:** Specifically indicated to reduce the progression of cold sore episodes to ulcerative lesions and shorten healing time [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents $\ge$ 12 years):**
    - **Frequency:** Apply 5 times daily at approximately 4-hour intervals, omitting the night-time application.
    - **Duration:** 
        - [Australian]: Apply for 5 days. If lesions persist after 10 days, clinical consultation is advised.
        - [US]: Apply for 4 days.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Treatment must be initiated as soon as possible following the first signs or symptoms (e.g., tingling, itching, or redness) or when lesions first appear.
    - Apply a sufficient quantity to cover the affected area, including the outer margin of any lesions.
    - Users should wash hands before and after application and avoid unnecessary rubbing of lesions to prevent aggravating or transferring the infection.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to aciclovir, valaciclovir, hydrocortisone [Australian], or any excipient in the formulation.
- **Infection-specific:** Do not use on skin lesions caused by viruses other than herpes simplex, or for fungal, bacterial, or parasitic skin infections [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **External Use Only:** Application is restricted to cold sores on the lips and face. 
- **Mucous Membranes:** Not recommended for use on mucous membranes (e.g., eye, mouth, nose). Specifically, do not use for genital herpes.
- **Ophthalmic Care:** Avoid contact with the eyes.
- **Visual Disturbance:** Systemic and topical corticosteroid use may lead to visual disturbances. Patients reporting blurred vision or other symptoms should be referred for ophthalmic evaluation to rule out cataract, glaucoma, or rare conditions like central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR) [Australian].
- **Immunocompromised Patients:** Not recommended for use in patients with known immunosuppression [Australian]; safety and efficacy have not been established in this population [US].
- **Occlusion:** Do not use with occlusive dressings, plasters, or specialised cold sore patches [Australian].
- **Duration:** Avoid long-term continuous use; do not exceed the recommended treatment duration.
- **Excipients:** Contains cetostearyl alcohol (may cause contact dermatitis) and propylene glycol (may cause skin irritation).
- **Contact Sensitization:** Potential for irritation and contact sensitization exists [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are primarily localised to the site of application.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** ($\ge$1% to <10%) | Drying or flaking of the skin, dry lips, desquamation, pruritus [US]. |
| **Uncommon** ($\ge$0.1% to <1%) | Transient burning, tingling, or stinging; itching [Australian]. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Erythema, pigmentation changes, application site reactions (inflammation). |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Immediate hypersensitivity reactions including angioedema [Australian]. |
| **Not Known** | Vision blurred [Australian]; Anaphylaxis, contact dermatitis, eczema [US-Postmarketing]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Topical/Systemic Interactions:** No clinically significant interactions have been identified. 
- **Management:** Due to minimal systemic absorption, systemic drug interactions are considered unlikely [US]. Patients are advised to avoid applying other topical products to the affected area simultaneously [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3:** Use should only be considered when potential benefits outweigh unknown risks. Animal studies with systemic corticosteroids have shown foetal abnormalities, though human relevance is unestablished.
    - **US Perspective:** Minimal systemic absorption suggests maternal use is not expected to result in significant foetal exposure. Observational studies over decades have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage.
- **Breastfeeding:** Aciclovir and hydrocortisone pass into breast milk following systemic administration; however, the dose received by a nursing infant via topical maternal use is considered insignificant.
- **Fertility:** No human data available to evaluate the effect on fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Safety and efficacy have been established in adolescents $\ge$ 12 years of age.
    - Not recommended for children under 12 years due to a lack of safety and efficacy data.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects $\ge$ 65 years to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US]. No specific data available in [Australian] sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data available; however, systemic exposure is minimal following topical application.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Aciclovir:** A deoxynucleoside analogue that inhibits HSV-1 and HSV-2 DNA polymerase. It requires viral thymidine kinase for phosphorylation to its active form (aciclovir triphosphate), which then terminates the growing viral DNA chain.
    - **Hydrocortisone:** A mild corticosteroid that provides immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory effects to reduce the local inflammatory response in the lip.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The combination reduces the progression to ulcerative lesions and shortens healing time.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Systemic absorption is minimal in adults. In a study with 5x daily application for 4 days, plasma concentrations were mostly below the limit of detection (<0.01 μM).
    - **Distribution/Metabolism/Elimination:** Not clinically relevant for topical application due to limited systemic entry. Average daily urinary excretion was approximately 0.04% of the applied dose [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Zovirax Duo (Combination):** A double-blind trial (n=1443) showed that 58% of Zovirax Duo patients developed ulcerative lesions compared to 65% with aciclovir monotherapy and 74% with vehicle. Episode duration for ulcerative lesions was 5.7 days for the combination vs 6.5 days for vehicle.
- **Zovirax Cream (Monotherapy):** Two double-blind trials showed the mean duration of cold sore episodes was approximately 0.5 days shorter compared to vehicle (4.5 days vs 5 days). No significant difference was observed in the prevention of progression to ulcerative lesions for the monotherapy vs vehicle [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Do not refrigerate or freeze.
- **Shelf Life:** Expiry date is located on the packaging. 

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification:** Schedule 3 (Pharmacist Only Medicine).
- **US Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Medicine).
