# Editorial Product Information: ziv-aflibercept (Zaltrap)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** ziv-aflibercept (a recombinant fusion protein/VEGF inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** Zaltrap [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01XX44 (Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) inhibitor).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Injection (concentrate for solution), 25 mg/mL. Available as 100 mg/4 mL or 200 mg/8 mL single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis U.S. LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Colorectal Cancer (mCRC):** Indicated in combination with 5-fluorouracil, leucovorin, and irinotecan (FOLFIRI) for patients with mCRC that is resistant to or has progressed following an oxaliplatin-containing regimen [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 4 mg per kg of actual body weight, administered as a 1-hour IV infusion every 2 weeks [US].
- **Administration Sequence:** Administer ziv-aflibercept prior to any component of the FOLFIRI regimen [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Do **not** administer as an IV push or bolus.
    - Must be diluted in 0.9% NaCl or 5% Dextrose to a final concentration of 0.6 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL.
    - Administer via a 0.2-micron polyethersulfone filter. Do not use PVDF or nylon filters.
    - Do not mix with other drugs in the same infusion bag or line [US].
- **Dosage Modifications for Toxicity:**
    - **Permanent Discontinuation:** Required for severe hemorrhage, GI perforation, fistula formation, hypertensive crisis/encephalopathy, arterial thromboembolic events (ATE), nephrotic syndrome, thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA), or reversible posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome (RPLS).
    - **Temporary Suspension:** Suspend at least 4 weeks prior to elective surgery. Suspend for uncontrolled hypertension or proteinuria ≥2 g/24h. 
    - **Dose Reduction:** Upon resumption after suspension for hypertension or recurrent proteinuria, permanently reduce the dose to 2 mg/kg [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hemorrhage:** Increased risk of severe and sometimes fatal hemorrhagic events, including GI hemorrhage and intracranial hemorrhage. Do not initiate in patients with severe bleeding [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Can be fatal. Discontinue therapy immediately if perforation occurs [US].
- **Impaired Wound Healing:** Ziv-aflibercept impairs wound healing. Withhold for at least 4 weeks prior to elective surgery and 4 weeks following major surgery (and until the wound is fully healed) [US].
- **Fistula Formation:** Increased incidence of GI and non-GI fistulas (e.g., anal, enterocutaneous). Discontinue if fistulas develop [US].
- **Hypertension:** High incidence of Grade 3–4 hypertension. Monitor blood pressure every 2 weeks. Hypertensive crisis requires permanent discontinuation [US].
- **Arterial Thromboembolic Events (ATE):** Increased risk of TIA, CVA, and angina pectoris. Discontinue if an ATE occurs [US].
- **Proteinuria/Nephrotic Syndrome:** Monitor urine protein (dipstick/UPCR). Patients with UPCR >1 require 24-hour urine collection. Discontinue for nephrotic syndrome or TMA [US].
- **Neutropenia:** Higher incidence of febrile neutropenia and infection. Delay FOLFIRI/ziv-aflibercept until ANC ≥1.5 × 10⁹/L [US].
- **Diarrhea and Dehydration:** Increased incidence of severe diarrhea. Monitor elderly patients more closely [US].
- **Reversible Posterior Leukoencephalopathy Syndrome (RPLS):** Confirm diagnosis via MRI; discontinue therapy if it occurs [US].
- **Arterial Aneurysms:** Risk of aneurysms, dissections, and rupture [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (≥20%):** Leukopenia, diarrhea, neutropenia, proteinuria, AST/ALT increased, stomatitis, fatigue, thrombocytopenia, hypertension, weight loss, decreased appetite, epistaxis, abdominal pain, dysphonia, serum creatinine increased, headache [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Dehydration, urinary tract infection, venous thromboembolic events (9%), palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia syndrome, skin hyperpigmentation [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Hemorrhage (Grade 3–4: 3%), GI perforation (0.8%), Arterial Thromboembolic Events (2.6%), Neutropenic infection/sepsis (1.5%), Osteonecrosis of the jaw, Cardiac failure [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No dedicated drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted. No clinically important PK interactions were found between ziv-aflibercept and irinotecan (or its metabolite SN-38) or fluorouracil [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No Category Letter]. Based on animal studies and mechanism of action, ziv-aflibercept can cause fetal harm (embryotoxic and teratogenic). Verify pregnancy status before initiation [US].
- **Contraception:** Advise females of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during treatment and for 3 months after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established in humans. Advise against breastfeeding during treatment and for 1 month following the last dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggest impairment of reproductive function and fertility in both males and females; reversibility was observed within 18 weeks of cessation [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established. A study in patients aged 2–21 years showed the maximum tolerated dose was lower than that in adults with mCRC [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Patients ≥65 years had a higher incidence of diarrhea, dizziness, asthenia, weight loss, and dehydration compared to younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment recommended for mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment recommended for mild or moderate impairment. Not studied in patients with severe hepatic impairment (total bilirubin >3x ULN) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Acts as a soluble decoy receptor ("VEGF Trap"). It binds to VEGF-A, VEGF-B, and Placental Growth Factor (PlGF) with higher affinity than their native receptors, thereby inhibiting neovascularisation and decreasing vascular permeability [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** A study of 6 mg/kg every 3 weeks showed no large changes in QTc interval (mean increase <10 ms) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption/Distribution:** Steady state reached by the second dose.
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids by proteolysis.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life of free ziv-aflibercept is approximately 6 days (range 4–7 days) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **VELOUR Trial:** A randomised (1:1), double-blind, placebo-controlled study in 1226 patients with mCRC previously treated with oxaliplatin. 
    - **Outcome:** Addition of ziv-aflibercept to FOLFIRI significantly improved median Overall Survival (13.5 months vs 12.06 months; HR 0.817) and Progression-Free Survival (6.90 months vs 4.67 months; HR 0.758) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Keep in original carton to protect from light [US].
- **Diluted Solution:** May be stored refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 24 hours or at controlled room temperature (20°C to 25°C) for up to 8 hours [US].

- **Poison/Legal Classification:** [US]: Rx-only.
