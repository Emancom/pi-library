# Editorial Product Information: Ganciclovir Ophthalmic Gel

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ganciclovir (a nucleoside analog antiviral).
- **Chemical Class:** Guanosine derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Zirgan** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** S01AD09.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Ophthalmic gel, 0.15% (containing 1.5 mg of ganciclovir per gram of gel) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bausch & Lomb Incorporated [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Herpetic Keratitis:** Indicated for the treatment of acute dendritic ulcers caused by herpes simplex virus (HSV) infection [United States].
- **Pediatric Use:** Approved for patients aged 2 years and older [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric $\ge$ 2 years)
- **Initial Phase:** Instill 1 drop into the affected eye 5 times daily (approximately every 3 hours while awake) until the corneal ulcer is healed [United States].
- **Maintenance Phase:** Once the ulcer has healed, instill 1 drop 3 times daily for an additional 7 days [United States].

### Administration Instructions
- For topical ophthalmic use only.
- Patients should be advised to wash hands before use and avoid touching the dropper tip to any surface (including the eye or eyelid) to prevent contamination of the sterile gel.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- None identified in the provided source [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Topical Use Only:** This medication is not for injection or oral administration [United States].
- **Contact Lens Avoidance:** Patients should not wear contact lenses if they exhibit signs or symptoms of herpetic keratitis or during the active course of therapy with this medication [United States].
- **Risk of Contamination:** To maintain sterility, the tip of the container must not touch any surface.
- **Clinical Monitoring:** Patients should consult a physician if pain develops or if redness, itching, or inflammation worsens [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following adverse reactions were reported in clinical studies of ganciclovir ophthalmic gel 0.15%:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Blurred vision (60%), Eye irritation (20%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Punctate keratitis (5%), Conjunctival hyperemia (5%) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources. 
- **Editorial Note:** Given the minimal systemic absorption following topical ophthalmic administration (approximately 0.04% to 0.1% of systemic maintenance doses), the potential for systemic drug interactions is expected to be low [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in the current US labeling system. 
  - **Data:** There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal data using high-dose **intravenous** ganciclovir (1400–2400 times the human ocular dose) showed significant maternal toxicity, embryolethality, and teratogenicity (including cleft palate, hydrocephaly, and aplastic organs). Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if ganciclovir is excreted in human milk following topical ophthalmic use. Animal data indicate ganciclovir is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. Caution should be exercised when administered to nursing mothers [United States].
- **Fertility:** Based on animal studies (IV), ganciclovir may cause decreased fertility and hypospermatogenesis. It is unknown if topical ophthalmic administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to affect human fertility [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established in children 2 years of age and older. Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients below 2 years of age [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between geriatric and younger patients [United States].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; systemic exposure is expected to be minimal.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ganciclovir is a guanosine derivative that, once phosphorylated by viral and cellular thymidine kinases to ganciclovir triphosphate, inhibits DNA replication of HSV. This occurs via competitive inhibition of viral DNA polymerase and direct incorporation into the viral DNA primer strand, leading to chain termination [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** The estimated maximum daily ophthalmic dose (0.375 mg) results in minimal systemic exposure, representing a small fraction (0.04% to 0.1%) of doses typically seen with oral or intravenous administration [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Specifically active against herpes simplex viruses.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trial 1:** In an open-label, randomized, multicenter trial (n=164), ganciclovir 0.15% gel was non-inferior to acyclovir ophthalmic ointment 3%. Clinical resolution (healed ulcers) at Day 7 was 77% for ganciclovir vs. 72% for acyclovir [United States].
- **Pivotal Trial 2:** In three randomized, single-masked trials (n=213), clinical resolution at Day 7 was achieved in 72% of ganciclovir-treated patients versus 69% for those using acyclovir 3% ointment [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at $15^\circ\text{C}$ to $25^\circ\text{C}$ ($59^\circ\text{F}$ to $77^\circ\text{F}$) [United States].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze [United States].
- **Supplied:** 5 g sterile gel in a polycoated aluminum tube [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
