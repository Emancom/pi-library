# Editorial Product Information: Zoladex (Goserelin Acetate)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates information from the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI) documents:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Zoladex 10.8 mg implant.
    - **US (FDA)**: Zoladex 3.6 mg implant.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory and clinical data from multiple regions.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Goserelin acetate (a synthetic decapeptide analogue of LHRH/GnRH).
- **Brand Name**: **Zoladex**
- **ATC Code**: L02AE03
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - **3.6 mg Implant**: A 28-day sustained-release biodegradable cylinder [US, Australian].
    - **10.8 mg Implant**: A 3-month sustained-release biodegradable cylinder [Australian, US].
- **Route of Administration**: Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: 
    - **Australian**: AstraZeneca Pty Ltd.
    - **US**: TerSera Therapeutics LLC.

## 2. Approved Indications
**Prostate Cancer**
- Palliative treatment of advanced/metastatic carcinoma of the prostate [Australian, US].
- Management of locally confined (Stage B2-C/T2b-T4) prostate cancer, in combination with radiotherapy and/or flutamide [Australian, US].

**Endometriosis (3.6 mg strength only)**
- Management of endometriosis, including pain relief and reduction of lesions [US-approved; 10.8 mg is not indicated for this use in Australia].

**Endometrial Thinning (3.6 mg strength only)**
- Use as an endometrial-thinning agent prior to endometrial ablation for dysfunctional uterine bleeding [US-approved].

**Advanced Breast Cancer (3.6 mg strength only)**
- Palliative treatment of advanced breast cancer in pre- and perimenopausal women [US-approved].

**Note on Gender Restrictions**: The 10.8 mg implant is specifically **not** indicated for use in females in some jurisdictions due to insufficient evidence of reliable oestradiol suppression [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **3.6 mg Implant**: One implant injected subcutaneously every 28 days into the anterior abdominal wall [US].
- **10.8 mg Implant**: One implant injected subcutaneously every 3 months into the anterior abdominal wall [Australian].

### Indication-Specific Dosing (3.6 mg Strength)
- **Endometriosis**: Recommended duration is 6 months [US].
- **Endometrial Thinning**: One or two depots administered 4 weeks apart. If one depot is used, surgery is performed at 4 weeks; if two are used, surgery occurs 2–4 weeks after the second dose [US].
- **Prostate Cancer (Locally Confined)**: When used with flutamide and radiotherapy, treatment should begin 8 weeks prior to radiotherapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Site**: Anterior abdominal wall, below the navel line. 
- **Caution**: Extreme care must be taken to avoid the underlying inferior epigastric artery and its branches. Use extra caution in patients with a low BMI or those receiving full anticoagulation [Australian, US].
- **Technique**: Use an aseptic technique. Do not penetrate muscle or peritoneum. The **SafeSystem** syringe features a protective sleeve that activates upon full depression of the plunger to prevent needle-stick injury [Australian, US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to LHRH, LHRH agonist analogues, or any components of the delivery system [Australian, US].
- **Pregnancy**: **Zoladex** is contraindicated during pregnancy unless used for the palliative treatment of advanced breast cancer [US]. The 10.8 mg strength is contraindicated in all females in some regions [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Tumor Flare Phenomenon**: Initially, GnRH agonists cause a transient increase in serum testosterone (males) or oestrogen (females). This may result in temporary worsening of symptoms, bone pain, ureteric obstruction, or spinal cord compression. Patients at risk should be monitored closely during the first month [Australian, US].
- **Hyperglycaemia and Diabetes**: An increased risk of developing diabetes or worsening of glycaemic control has been reported in men. Blood glucose and/or HbA1c should be monitored periodically [Australian, US].
- **Cardiovascular Disease**: Increased risk of myocardial infarction, sudden cardiac death, and stroke has been reported in men. Monitor for cardiovascular signs and manage according to clinical practice [Australian, US].
- **QT/QTc Interval Prolongation**: Androgen deprivation therapy may prolong the QT interval. Benefits vs. risks should be weighed in patients with congenital long QT syndrome, congestive heart failure, or those taking QT-prolonging medications [Australian, US].
- **Bone Mineral Density (BMD)**: **Zoladex** causes a loss of BMD. In men, this may lead to osteoporosis or fractures. In women, loss may be partially reversible after cessation [Australian, US].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs)**: Cases of Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) have been reported. Discontinue permanently if confirmed [US].
- **Injection Site Injury**: Reports include pain, haematoma, and vascular injury. Rare cases of haemorrhagic shock requiring transfusion have occurred [Australian, US].
- **Hypercalcaemia**: Reported in patients with bone metastases [US].
- **Depression**: May occur or worsen in women; monitor mental health status [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Formal Studies**: No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been performed [Australian, US].
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs**: Concomitant use with medications known to prolong the QT interval or induce Torsade de Pointes must be carefully evaluated [Australian, US].
- **Laboratory Tests**: Treatment suppresses the pituitary-gonadal system; diagnostic tests of pituitary gonadotropic and gonadal functions may be misleading during therapy and for up to 12 weeks post-cessation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Category**: Not assigned a letter, but contraindicated unless used for breast cancer. Crosses the placenta in animal studies and increases pregnancy loss.
    - **Australian**: The 10.8 mg strength is not indicated for use in females.
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if goserelin is excreted in human milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility**: Suppression of gonadal function results in profound impairment of fertility, which is generally reversible upon cessation of therapy in animal models [Australian, US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian, US].
- **Elderly Use**: No dosage adjustments are necessary [Australian, US].
- **Renal Impairment**: No dosage adjustment is necessary despite an increased half-life in renal failure [Australian, US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No dosage adjustment is necessary for patients with hepatic impairment [Australian, US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Goserelin is a potent synthetic LHRH agonist. Acute administration stimulates LH and FSH release. Chronic administration inhibits gonadotrophin production, leading to a sustained suppression of pituitary LH and a reduction of sex hormones (testosterone in men, oestradiol in women) to castrate/postmenopausal levels [Australian, US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Castrate levels of testosterone are typically reached 2–4 weeks after the first dose and maintained with regular dosing [Australian, US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**: 
    - **Absorption**: Rapid initial absorption followed by continuous release. Peak concentrations occur within 24 hours [Australian] or between 0.5–1 hour [US] depending on the formulation/study.
    - **Distribution**: Apparent volume of distribution is 44 L in men and 20 L in women [US]. Protein binding is approximately 27% [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Hydrolysis of C-terminal amino acids is the primary clearance pathway [US].
    - **Elimination**: Serum half-life is approx. 4.2 hours (normal renal function), increasing to over 12 hours in severe renal impairment [Australian, US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Prostate Cancer**: Five Phase III trials (RTOG 85-31, EORTC 22863, etc.) demonstrated that adjuvant/neoadjuvant **Zoladex** combined with radiotherapy significantly improves disease-free survival and overall survival compared to radiotherapy alone [Australian].
- **Endometriosis**: Two controlled trials showed 3.6 mg **Zoladex** was as effective as danazol in relieving symptoms and reducing lesions, with amenorrhoea occurring in 80–92% of women [US].
- **Endometrial Thinning**: Trial 0022 showed significantly reduced endometrial thickness (1.50 mm vs 3.55 mm) and increased amenorrhoea rates compared to placebo [US].
- **Breast Cancer**: Trial SWOG-8692 showed an objective response rate of 22% for **Zoladex** compared to 12% for surgical oophorectomy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature**: Store below 25°C (77°F) [Australian, US].
- **Handling**: Ensure the seal is unbroken and the implant is visible in the applicator window before use.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification**: Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4).
- **US Classification**: Prescription Only (Rx-only).
