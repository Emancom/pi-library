# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial was developed using official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (chemical class: carrier molecule/amino acid derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (Note: typically A16AA01).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [United States].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less frequently than every 6 hours [United States].
- **Titration:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though therapy may require up to the highest recorded dose of 300 mg/kg [United States].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [United States].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [United States].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [United States].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [United States].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [United States].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic patients, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35–60 µmol/L) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Reactions:** Transient nausea and vomiting. Less frequent reactions include body odor and gastritis [United States].
- **Hemodialysis Population (≥5% Frequency):**
    - **General:** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia.
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea, vomiting.
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia.
    - **Nervous System:** Dizziness, paresthesia.
    - **Respiratory:** Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when co-administered with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended at the initiation of treatment or following dose adjustments [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: No specific category letter provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. Decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the risk of excess carnitine intake in the child against the benefit to the mother [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). See Section 3 for specific instructions [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and subsequent energy production [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, levocarnitine clears acylCoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted, potentially alleviating metabolic acidosis [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While increases in carnitine levels are demonstrated, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [United States].
