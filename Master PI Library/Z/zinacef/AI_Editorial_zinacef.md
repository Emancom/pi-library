# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, professional reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism (amino acid derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection containing 1 g of levocarnitine per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration Instructions:** For ESRD patients, the dose should be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [United States].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Acute Metabolic Crisis:** Treatment may begin with a loading dose, followed by equivalent doses administered every 3 or 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours. Daily maintenance doses thereafter are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [United States].
- **Maintenance (ESRD):** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are supra-normal [United States].

### Administration Notes
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Levocarnitine is compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** While the source states "None known," clinical warnings exist for serious hypersensitivity (see Section 5). [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug should be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with established seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L). In ESRD, monitor trough plasma levels (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in clinical trials of ESRD patients were often confounded by underlying pathology.

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent: gastritis and body odor.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (postmarketing) [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored upon initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but noted as use "only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. No adequate/controlled studies exist in pregnant women [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. In nursing mothers, the benefits to the mother must be weighed against potential risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients. Specific age-range safety profiles beyond metabolic indications are not detailed [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may result in the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a carrier molecule essential for transporting long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This facilitates the oxidation of fatty acids for energy production in tissues (excluding the brain), particularly skeletal and cardiac muscle [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic defects or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted. This helps alleviate autointoxication and metabolic acidosis [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the impact on clinical outcomes/symptoms in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of an opened vial as the formulation is preservative-free [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [United States].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
