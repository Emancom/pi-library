# Editorial Product Information: Zestril (Lisinopril)

## Editorial Note
- The following editorialised Product Information was synthesised from official regulatory documents provided by the **Australian** (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and the **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regional variations in prescribing and safety data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Lisinopril (as dihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic peptide derivative; Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Zestril**.
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets:** 2.5 mg [US], 5 mg [Australian; US], 10 mg [Australian; US], 20 mg [Australian; US], 30 mg [US], 40 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - **Atnahs Pharma Australia Pty Ltd** [Australian].
    - **Upsher-Smith Laboratories, LLC** [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Treatment of hypertension in adults and pediatric patients aged 6 years and older [US]. Used alone or as adjunct therapy. Note: Data are insufficient to support use in severe or renovascular hypertension [Australian].
- **Heart Failure:** Adjunct therapy for the treatment of systolic heart failure (typically in combination with diuretics and digitalis).
- **Acute Myocardial Infarction:** Reduction of mortality in haemodynamically stable patients within 24 hours of an acute myocardial infarction (MI).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer as a single daily dose, with or without food.

### Hypertension (Adults)
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg once daily. For patients on diuretics or those with volume/salt depletion, a starting dose of 5 mg [US] or 2.5 mg [Australian] should be used under medical supervision.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 20 mg to 40 mg daily.
- **Maximum Dose:** 80 mg daily has been used [US], though 40 mg is the common upper limit [Australian]. If blood pressure is not controlled at 40 mg, a low-dose diuretic (e.g., 12.5 mg hydrochlorothiazide) may be added.

### Hypertension (Pediatrics $\ge$ 6 years)
- **Initial Dose:** 0.07 mg/kg once daily (up to 5 mg total).
- **Maintenance:** Adjust based on response up to 0.61 mg/kg (up to 40 mg total) [US].
- **Contraindication:** Not recommended in children < 6 years or those with GFR < 30 mL/min [US].

### Heart Failure
- **Initial Dose:** 5 mg once daily. Use 2.5 mg once daily in patients with hyponatremia (serum sodium < 130 mEq/L) [US].
- **Titration:** Increase by no more than 10 mg increments at intervals of at least 2 weeks.
- **Target Dose:** Up to 40 mg once daily [US]; maintenance range of 5–20 mg/day [Australian].

### Acute Myocardial Infarction
- **Initial Dose:** 5 mg within 24 hours of MI onset, followed by 5 mg after 24 hours, 10 mg after 48 hours, then 10 mg daily for at least 6 weeks.
- **Low Systolic BP ($\le$ 120 mmHg):** Use a 2.5 mg starting dose. 
- **Hypotension Management:** If systolic BP $\le$ 100 mmHg, reduce the maintenance dose to 5 mg (with temporary reductions to 2.5 mg if needed). Withdraw treatment if prolonged hypotension (systolic BP < 90 mmHg for > 1 hour) occurs.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to lisinopril, other ACE inhibitors, or any excipient.
- **Angioedema History:** History of hereditary or idiopathic angioedema, or angioedema related to previous ACE inhibitor therapy.
- **Neprilysin Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with or within 36 hours of switching to/from a neprilysin inhibitor (e.g., sacubitril/valsartan).
- **Aliskiren:** Concomitant use with aliskiren-containing products in patients with diabetes mellitus or renal impairment (GFR < 60 mL/min).
- **Pregnancy:** Absolutely contraindicated in pregnancy [Australian; US].
- **Dialysis:** Use with high-flux polyacrylonitrile membranes (e.g., AN69) due to risk of anaphylactoid reactions [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Fetal Toxicity) [US]:** Exposure to ACE inhibitors during the second and third trimesters reduces fetal renal function and increases fetal/neonatal morbidity and death. Discontinue **Zestril** immediately upon detection of pregnancy.
- **Angioedema:** Can involve the face, extremities, or airway (larynx/glottis/tongue), which may be fatal. Black patients have a higher reported incidence than non-Black patients. Intestinal angioedema may present as abdominal pain.
- **Hypotension:** Most common in volume-depleted patients, those on high-dose diuretics, or those with severe heart failure. Monitor closely for 2 weeks after initiation or dose increases.
- **Renal Function:** Monitor periodically. Acute renal failure may occur, especially in patients with renal artery stenosis, severe heart failure, or volume depletion.
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Risk factors include renal insufficiency, diabetes, and use of potassium-sparing diuretics or salt substitutes.
- **Hepatic Failure:** ACE inhibitors are associated with a syndrome starting with cholestatic jaundice progressing to fulminant hepatic necrosis. Discontinue if jaundice or marked liver enzyme elevations occur.
- **Neutropenia/Agranulocytosis:** Use with caution in patients with collagen vascular disease or renal impairment; periodic haematologic monitoring is recommended [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (> 1% to 10%):** Headache, dizziness, cough, fatigue, asthenia, hypotension (especially in heart failure/MI patients), chest pain, diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, and rash.
- **Uncommon (0.1% to 1%):** Myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular accident (secondary to hypotension), palpitations, tachycardia, sleep disturbances, mood alterations, and vertigo [Australian].
- **Rare (< 0.1%):** Psoriasis, alopecia, urticaria, renal failure, gynaecomastia, hyponatremia, and SIADH.
- **Serious Effects:** Angioedema, anaphylactoid reactions, hepatic failure, and agranulocytosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Diuretics:** Potentiates hypotensive effects; risk of first-dose hypotension.
- **Potassium-Sparing Diuretics/Supplements/Salt Substitutes:** May lead to significant increases in serum potassium.
- **Lithium:** Increased serum lithium levels and toxicity; frequent monitoring required.
- **NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors):** May result in further deterioration of renal function (including acute renal failure) and attenuation of the antihypertensive effect, particularly in elderly or volume-depleted patients.
- **Dual Blockade of the RAAS:** Combining ACE inhibitors with ARBs or aliskiren increases the risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia, and renal impairment.
- **mTOR Inhibitors (e.g., temsirolimus) and Neprilysin Inhibitors:** Increased risk of angioedema.
- **Injectable Gold (Sodium Aurothiomalate):** Nitritoid reactions (flushing, nausea, hypotension) have been reported.
- **Antidiabetics:** May increase the blood-glucose-lowering effect, particularly in the first weeks of treatment.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** ACE inhibitors can cause fetal/neonatal morbidity and death when administered during pregnancy. 
    - **Fetal/Neonatal Risks:** Skull hypoplasia, anuria, hypotension, renal failure, and limb contractures. 
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety is not established. Lisinopril is present in rat milk; human data are unavailable. Advise against breastfeeding during treatment [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on reproductive performance were observed in rat studies at doses up to 300 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for hypertension in patients $\ge$ 6 years [US]. Not recommended in children < 6 years or those with GFR < 30 mL/min.
- **Elderly Use:** No general dosage adjustment is necessary [US], but pharmacokinetic data show doubled AUC and peak levels compared to younger patients; use with caution [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose adjustment required based on Creatinine Clearance (CrCl):
    - **CrCl > 30 mL/min:** No adjustment.
    - **CrCl 10–30 mL/min:** Halve the usual dose (Start at 2.5–5 mg).
    - **CrCl < 10 mL/min or Dialysis:** Start at 2.5 mg once daily.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dosing adjustments provided; however, monitor closely for jaundice or signs of liver failure.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive inhibition of ACE, preventing the conversion of Angiotensin I to the potent vasoconstrictor Angiotensin II. This leads to reduced vasopressor activity and decreased aldosterone secretion.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Peak blood pressure reduction occurs approximately 6 hours post-dose. Onset of action is typically within 1–2 hours.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 25% (range 6–60%); not affected by food.
    - **Distribution:** Does not bind to serum proteins other than ACE.
    - **Metabolism:** None.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted entirely unchanged in the urine.
    - **Half-life:** Effective accumulation half-life is 12.6 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **GISSI-3 Trial:** Large study in 19,394 MI patients showing that lisinopril initiated within 24 hours of MI reduced the 6-week risk of death by 11% compared to control.
- **ATLAS Trial:** Survival study in heart failure patients showing that high-dose lisinopril (up to 35 mg) was associated with a 12% risk reduction in death and all-cause hospitalisation compared to low-dose (up to 5 mg).
- **EUCLID Trial [Australian]:** Investigated lisinopril in normotensive IDDM patients; demonstrated a reduction in the urinary albumin excretion rate (AER).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Australian:** Store below 30°C.
- **US:** Store at controlled room temperature (20°C to 25°C). Protect from moisture, freezing, and excessive heat. Dispense in a tight container.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** 
    - **Australian:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4).
    - **US:** Rx-only.
