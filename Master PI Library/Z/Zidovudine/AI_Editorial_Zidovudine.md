# Editorial Product Information: Lamivudine and Zidovudine (COMBIVIR)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following provided regulatory source:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Australian Product Information — COMBIVIR (lamivudine and zidovudine) film-coated tablets.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Not all jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Japanese) were represented in the provided source material; information for those regions is marked as "Not specified" or omitted where data was unavailable.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients**: Lamivudine (150 mg) and Zidovudine (300 mg).
- **Chemical Class**: Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs).
- **Brand Name**: **COMBIVIR** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Scored, white to off-white, capsule-shaped film-coated tablets, engraved with “GXFC3” on both faces [Australian]. Each tablet contains 150 mg lamivudine and 300 mg zidovudine.
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: ViiV Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV Infection**: Indicated for use as monotherapy or in combination with other antiretroviral agents for the treatment of HIV infection [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Administration**: May be taken with or without food. While food reduces the peak plasma concentration ($C_{max}$) of lamivudine, the total absorption is not affected [Australian].
- **Adults and Adolescents (≥30 kg)**: One tablet twice daily (total daily dose: 300 mg lamivudine/600 mg zidovudine).
- **Paediatric Dosing (Weight-based)**:
    - **21 kg to <30 kg**: One-half tablet in the morning and one whole tablet in the evening.
    - **14 kg to <21 kg**: One-half tablet twice daily.
    - **<14 kg**: Not recommended; separate liquid or tablet formulations of the individual components must be used to allow for accurate dosing.
- **Administration Note**: Tablets should ideally be swallowed whole. For patients with swallowing difficulties, tablets may be crushed and added to a small amount of semi-solid food or liquid, provided the entire dose is consumed immediately [Australian].
- **Missed Dose**: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to lamivudine, zidovudine, or any excipient in the formulation.
- **Haematological Thresholds**: Due to the zidovudine component, **COMBIVIR** is contraindicated in patients with:
    - Abnormally low neutrophil counts (<0.75 x $10^9$/L).
    - Abnormally low haemoglobin levels (<7.5 g/dL or 4.65 mmol/L).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haematological Toxicity**: Zidovudine is associated with granulocytopenia and severe anaemia. This is more common in patients with advanced HIV or poor bone marrow reserve. 
    - *Monitoring*: Perform blood tests at least every two weeks for the first three months of therapy, and at least monthly thereafter in advanced disease.
- **Lactic Acidosis and Severe Hepatomegaly with Steatosis**: Reported with NRTI use, including fatal cases. Most cases occur in women. Treatment should be suspended if clinical or laboratory signs of lactic acidosis or hepatotoxicity develop.
- **Pancreatitis**: Treatment must be ceased immediately if clinical signs (e.g., abdominal pain) or laboratory abnormalities (elevated amylase) suggestive of pancreatitis occur.
- **Lipoatrophy**: Long-term zidovudine use is linked to loss of subcutaneous fat (face, limbs, buttocks). This may only be partially reversible upon switching to a zidovudine-free regimen.
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome**: Inflammatory reactions to latent opportunistic infections (e.g., CMV, Mycobacterium, PCP) may occur during the initial weeks of therapy.
- **Hepatitis B Co-infection**: Patients co-infected with HBV may experience a "flare" of hepatitis upon discontinuation of lamivudine. Periodic monitoring of liver function and HBV replication is required.
- **Osteonecrosis**: Patients should seek medical advice if they experience joint pain or stiffness.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event frequency is often difficult to distinguish from the underlying HIV disease progression.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache, nausea, malaise, fatigue |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhoea, vomiting, abdominal pain, fever, dizziness, neuropathy, insomnia, rashes, cough, nasal symptoms, musculoskeletal pain, LFT elevations (AST/ALT), hyperlactataemia |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anaemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, pancreatitis |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Pure red cell aplasia, lactic acidosis, rhabdomyolysis, gynaecomastia, nail/skin pigmentation, liver disorders (steatosis) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Nucleoside Antagonists**: Do not use in combination with **stavudine**, **zalcitabine**, or **emtricitabine** due to intracellular competitive inhibition [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**:
    - **Trimethoprim (as Co-trimoxazole)**: Increases lamivudine exposure by 40%. No dose adjustment is needed unless renal impairment is present.
    - **Sorbitol**: Co-administration of sorbitol-containing medicines reduces lamivudine levels; avoid chronic co-administration if possible.
    - **Probenecid/Glucuronidation Inhibitors**: May increase zidovudine levels by inhibiting metabolism or renal excretion.
- **Haematological/Nephrotoxic Agents**: Enhanced risk of zidovudine toxicity when used with dapsone, pyrimethamine, ganciclovir, amphotericin B, or cytotoxic chemotherapy.
- **Other**: **Ribavirin** antagonises the antiviral activity of zidovudine and is not recommended for co-administration [Australian]. **Clarithromycin** reduces zidovudine absorption; separate doses by 2 hours.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Category B3 [Australian]**: Animal studies show increased early embryonic deaths (lamivudine) and foetal resorptions (zidovudine) at high doses.
    - **Clinical Data**: The Antiretroviral Pregnancy Registry (APR) data show no increased risk of major birth defects compared to background rates. Use only if the benefit justifies the risk.
- **Breastfeeding**: HIV-infected women are advised not to breastfeed to avoid virus transmission. Both drugs are excreted in human milk at concentrations similar to maternal serum [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models. No human data on female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Approved for children weighing $\ge$ 14 kg. Not for use in infants <3 months due to insufficient data.
- **Elderly Use**: Use with caution due to age-related declines in renal function and haematological parameters.
- **Renal Impairment**: Not recommended for patients with a creatinine clearance $\le$ 50 mL/min, as the fixed-dose combination does not allow for the necessary dose reduction of individual components [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Use with caution. Patients with severe impairment or cirrhosis may require dose intervals that cannot be achieved with the fixed-dose tablet.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Both agents are phosphorylated intracellularly to active triphosphate metabolites. These act as competitive inhibitors of HIV reverse transcriptase and are incorporated into viral DNA, causing chain termination.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Rapidly and well absorbed. Bioavailability is 80–85% (lamivudine) and 60–70% (zidovudine).
    - **Distribution**: Both cross the blood-brain barrier and the placenta. Plasma protein binding is low ($\le$ 38%).
    - **Metabolism**: Zidovudine is primarily metabolised via hepatic glucuronidation to an inactive metabolite (GAZT). Lamivudine is mostly excreted unchanged.
    - **Elimination**: Primarily renal. The terminal half-life of lamivudine is 5–7 hours; zidovudine is approximately 1 hour.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CAESAR Study (NUCB3007)**: A randomised, double-blind study (n=1,840) showed that adding lamivudine to zidovudine-containing regimens reduced the risk of HIV progression or death by 57% (Hazard Ratio 0.427) [Australian].
- **ACTG320**: Demonstrated that a triple therapy regimen including lamivudine and zidovudine significantly reduced the incidence of AIDS-defining events and death compared to dual nucleoside therapy.
- **Surrogate Endpoints**: Studies NUCA3001 and NUCA3002 established that the combination significantly increased CD4+ counts and reduced viral load (HIV-RNA) compared to monotherapy.

- **Storage**: Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life**: Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Disposal**: Unused medicine should be returned to a pharmacy [Australian].
