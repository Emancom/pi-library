# Editorial Product Information: Zolmitriptan

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions: **Australian (TGA)** for oral formulations and **US (FDA)** for the nasal spray formulation.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of zolmitriptan for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical data across available delivery systems (oral, orally dispersible, and nasal).
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Zolmitriptan, a selective serotonin (5-HT$_{1B/1D}$) receptor agonist.
- **Brand Names:** **Zomig**, **Zomig Rapimelt** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated Tablets:** 2.5 mg and 5 mg [Australian].
    - **Orally Dispersible Tablets (ODT):** 2.5 mg [Australian].
    - **Nasal Spray (Metered):** 2.5 mg and 5 mg per 100 µL unit [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral; Nasal.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].
    - Amneal Pharmaceuticals LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Treatment of Migraine:** Indicated for the acute treatment of migraine with or without aura in adults. 
- **Paediatric Migraine:** Specifically indicated for the acute treatment of migraine with or without aura in paediatric patients aged 12 years and older [US-approved for nasal spray; not established for oral formulations in Australia].
- **Limitations of Use:**
    - Not for the prophylactic therapy of migraine.
    - Not indicated for the treatment of cluster headache.
    - Use only after a clear diagnosis of migraine has been established.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** 2.5 mg is the recommended starting dose across all formulations. 
- **Subsequent Dosing:** If the headache persists or recurs within 24 hours of an initial response, a second dose may be taken.
- **Minimum Dosing Interval:** 2 hours.
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** 10 mg in any 24-hour period.

### Formulation-Specific Instructions
- **Conventional Tablets:** Swallow whole with water.
- **Orally Dispersible Tablets:** Place on the tongue to dissolve; swallow with saliva. No water is required [Australian].
- **Nasal Spray:** Administered into one nostril. The device contains a single dose and must not be primed [US].

### Paediatric Dosing (Ages 12–17)
- **Nasal Spray:** Starting dose of 2.5 mg; maximum single dose of 5 mg; maximum daily dose of 10 mg [US].
- **Oral Formulations:** Efficacy not established; use is not recommended [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Ischemic Heart Disease:** History of myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease (angina pectoris, documented silent ischemia), or coronary artery vasospasm (including Prinzmetal’s angina).
- **Cerebrovascular Disease:** History of stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA).
- **Peripheral Vascular Disease:** Including ischemic bowel disease [US].
- **Uncontrolled Hypertension:** Moderate or severe hypertension, or mild uncontrolled hypertension.
- **Concurrent Medication Use:**
    - Use of another 5-HT$_1$ agonist (e.g., sumatriptan) or ergot-type medication (e.g., ergotamine, dihydroergotamine) within 24 hours.
    - Concurrent use of MAO-A inhibitors or use within 2 weeks of discontinuing an MAO-A inhibitor [US: 2 weeks; Australian: dose limit applies rather than absolute contraindication unless using high-dose moclobemide].
- **Specific Migraine Types:** Hemiplegic or basilar migraine [US].
- **Cardiac Conduction Disorders:** Symptomatic Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome or arrhythmias associated with other accessory conduction pathways.
- **Renal Impairment:** Creatinine clearance <15 mL/min [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to zolmitriptan or any component of the formulation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myocardial Ischemia/Infarction:** Serious cardiac events can occur even in patients without known cardiovascular disease. Cardiac evaluation is required for patients with multiple cardiovascular risk factors.
- **Cerebrovascular Events:** Cerebral haemorrhage and stroke have been reported; discontinue if an event occurs.
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening; risk increases with concomitant use of SSRIs or SNRIs. Monitor for mental status changes and neuromuscular aberrations.
- **Medication Overuse Headache (MOH):** Overuse (≥10 days/month) may exacerbate headaches; detoxification may be necessary.
- **Blood Pressure:** Transient increases in blood pressure may occur, more pronounced in the elderly.
- **Vasospasm Reactions:** Non-coronary vasospasm (e.g., peripheral ischemia, gastrointestinal ischemia, Raynaud’s syndrome) may occur.
- **Phenylketonuria:** Zomig Rapimelt contains aspartame (2.81 mg phenylalanine per tablet) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typically mild-to-moderate and transient.
- **Common (≥1% to <10%):**
    - **General:** Asthenia, heaviness, tightness, or pain in the throat, neck, limbs, or chest.
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness, somnolence, paresthesia, hyperaesthesia.
    - **Digestive:** Dry mouth, nausea, vomiting, dysphagia.
    - **Local (Nasal Only):** Unusual taste (up to 21%), nasal cavity discomfort, oropharyngeal pain [US].
- **Uncommon (0.1% to <1%):** Tachycardia, palpitations, transient increases in blood pressure.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, urticaria.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Myocardial infarction, angina pectoris, coronary vasospasm, and gastrointestinal infarction/necrosis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Ergot-type medications/Other Triptans:** Potential for additive vasospastic reactions; contraindicated within 24 hours of each other.
- **MAO-A Inhibitors:** Increases zolmitriptan exposure. 
    - [US]: Contraindicated if used within 14 days. 
    - [Australian]: Maximum zolmitriptan dose of 5 mg/24h; avoid if moclobemide dose exceeds 150 mg b.i.d.
- **Cimetidine:** Approximately doubles the half-life and AUC of zolmitriptan. Max dose 5 mg/24h if co-administered.
- **CYP1A2 Inhibitors (e.g., Fluvoxamine, Quinolones):** Potential for interaction; dose reduction to 5 mg/24h is recommended [Australian].
- **SSRIs/SNRIs:** Risk of serotonin syndrome.
- **St. John’s Wort:** May increase frequency of adverse effects.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - [Australian]: **Category B3**. Administration only if benefit outweighs potential risk to the foetus.
    - [US]: Based on animal data, may cause fetal harm (embryolethality and malformations in rats/rabbits at high doses).
- **Breastfeeding:** Zolmitriptan is excreted in the milk of lactating animals. No human data available. Exercise caution; consider the benefits of breastfeeding against the maternal clinical need for the drug.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment of fertility at doses >100 times human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - [US]: Approved for ages 12–17 (nasal spray). 
    - [Australian]: Not established/not recommended for patients <18 years.
- **Elderly (>65 years):** Safety and efficacy not systematically evaluated. Use is generally not recommended [Australian]. If used, start at the low end of the dosing range [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate:** No adjustment for oral [Australian]. Not recommended for moderate-to-severe [US nasal spray].
    - **Severe:** Maximum dose 5 mg in 24 hours for oral [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment required for CrCl >15 mL/min. Contraindicated if CrCl <15 mL/min [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective agonist at 5-HT$_{1B}$ and 5-HT$_{1D}$ receptors. This results in cranial vessel constriction and inhibition of the release of pro-inflammatory neuropeptides (CGRP, substance P, VIP).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed. Absolute bioavailability is ~40% (oral). Nasal spray bioavailability is ~102% relative to oral.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily converted by CYP1A2 and MAO-A to an active N-desmethyl metabolite (2–6 times more potent than the parent drug in animal models).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 60% excreted in urine and 30% in faeces. Mean elimination half-life is 2.5–3 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Nasal Spray (Study 1):** In adults, 5 mg nasal spray showed a 2-hour headache response of 69% compared to 31% for placebo [US].
- **Paediatric (Study 2):** In patients 12–17 years, 5 mg nasal spray showed a significantly higher 2-hour "no headache pain" rate (30%) compared to placebo (17%) [US].
- **Oral Formulations:** Pivotal studies showed 2.5 mg and 5 mg doses were similarly effective (63-65% response at 2 hours) and superior to 1 mg or placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Oral Tablets:** Store 2.5 mg tablets below 25°C. Store 5 mg and Rapimelt (ODT) tablets below 30°C [Australian].
- **Nasal Spray:** Store at controlled room temperature, 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **General:** Discard nasal units after a single use.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian (TGA):** Prescription Only Medicine (**Schedule 4**).
- **US (FDA):** Prescription Only (**Rx-only**).
