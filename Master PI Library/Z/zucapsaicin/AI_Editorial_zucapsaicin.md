# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** To provide a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals based on available regulatory documentation.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US: Baxter Healthcare Corporation].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Standard Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours (never less frequently than every 6 hours) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### Dosing in ESRD on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are high [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation lacks preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) carnitine levels (normal range 40 to 50 µmol/L) [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Commonly Observed:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
- **Less Frequent:** Body odor, nausea, and gastritis.

### Adverse Events in ESRD Clinical Trials (Frequency ≥5%)
The following table summarises events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in ESRD patients, regardless of causality [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Frequency (Levocarnitine 10–40 mg/kg) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain | 7% – 33% |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia | 6% – 20% |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea | 8% – 16% |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia | 5% – 9% |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, paresthesia, depression | 5% – 13% |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis | 7% – 23% |

- **Postmarketing Experience:** Seizures and serious hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis) have been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly assigned in text]. Reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; studies in dairy cows indicate concentration in milk increases with exogenous administration. Benefits to the mother should be weighed against risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosage is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, oral formulations should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels remain stable or increase over 8 to 24 weeks of therapy depending on the dose [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly), mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels increased from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations, its effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Use only clear, colorless solutions.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].
