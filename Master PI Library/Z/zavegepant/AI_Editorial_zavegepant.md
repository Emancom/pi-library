# Editorial Product Information: Zavegepant

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesises product information for zavegepant based on documentation from the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only United States (FDA) documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Zavegepant (as zavegepant hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **ZAVZPRET** [US].
- **ATC Code:** N02CD07.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Nasal spray: 10 mg zavegepant per unit-dose device [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intranasal.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Migraine Treatment:** Indicated for the acute treatment of migraine with or without aura in adults [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not indicated for the preventive treatment of migraine [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** 10 mg (one spray) administered as a single dose in one nostril as needed [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** 10 mg within a 24-hour period. The safety of treating more than 8 migraine attacks in a 30-day period is not established [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - For nasal use only.
    - The device is unit-dose and should not be tested, primed, or pressed before use, as this will result in loss of the dose.
    - Patients should clear their nostrils before use and keep their head level during delivery.
- **Missed Dose:** Not applicable, as this medication is used on an as-needed basis for acute attacks.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to zavegepant or any component of the formulation. Reported reactions include anaphylaxis and facial swelling [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions (anaphylaxis, urticaria, facial swelling) have occurred. If a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical therapy initiated [US].
- **Hypertension:** New-onset or worsening of pre-existing hypertension has been reported with CGRP antagonists. Blood pressure should be monitored, and discontinuation considered if an alternative etiology is not found or if blood pressure cannot be controlled [US].
- **Raynaud’s Phenomenon:** New-onset or recurrence/worsening of Raynaud’s phenomenon has been reported with CGRP antagonists, typically within 1.5 days of dosing. Patients should be monitored for digital temperature or colour changes and advised to discontinue use if symptoms develop [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic blood pressure monitoring is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials (Study 1 and 2) occurring in ≥2% of patients and more frequently than placebo:

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Taste Disorders*** | 18% |
| **Nausea** | 4% |
| **Nasal Discomfort** | 3% |
| **Vomiting** | 2% |

*\*Includes dysgeusia and ageusia.*

- **Uncommon/Rare Effects (<1%):** Hypersensitivity reactions, including facial swelling and urticaria [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Development of hypertension and Raynaud’s phenomenon has been identified following the use of small-molecule CGRP antagonists [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **OATP1B3 or NTCP Inhibitors:** Concomitant use may significantly increase zavegepant exposure. Avoid co-administration [US].
- **OATP1B3 or NTCP Inducers:** Concomitant use may decrease zavegepant exposure, potentially reducing efficacy. Avoid co-administration [US].
- **Intranasal Decongestants:** May decrease the absorption of zavegepant. If use is unavoidable, the decongestant should be administered at least 1 hour after zavegepant [US].
- **Other Medications:** No clinically significant interactions were observed with oral contraceptives (ethinyl estradiol) or sumatriptan [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified under current FDA labelling rules; however, the label states there are no adequate human data to evaluate developmental risks [US]. 
    - **Animal Data:** No adverse developmental effects were seen in rats or rabbits at exposures up to 4,000 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of zavegepant in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be considered alongside the mother's clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility or reproductive performance were observed in animal studies at 2,800 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65 and over. While limited data show no significant PK differences, caution is generally advised in the elderly [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild/Moderate:** No dose adjustment needed for CLcr ≥ 30 mL/min [US].
    - **Severe:** Avoid use in patients with CLcr < 30 mL/min [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A) / Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** No dose adjustment required [US].
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Avoid use, as it has not been studied in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Zavegepant is a competitive antagonist at the CGRP receptor, which is thought to play a key role in migraine pathophysiology [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentration ($C_{max}$) reached at 30 minutes. Absolute bioavailability is approximately 5% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is ~90%. Mean apparent volume of distribution ($V_z/F$) is ~1774 L [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4 and to a lesser extent by CYP2D6 (in vitro). Unchanged zavegepant is the major circulating component [US].
    - **Elimination:** Effective half-life is ~6.55 hours. Excreted primarily via the biliary/fecal route (80%) and minimally via the renal route (11%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Efficacy:** Efficacy was demonstrated in two randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (Study 1 and Study 2).
- **Primary Outcomes:** Statistically significant improvements were seen at 2 hours post-dose compared to placebo for:
    - **Pain Freedom:** 23.6% (Zavegepant) vs 14.9% (Placebo) [Study 1].
    - **Most Bothersome Symptom (MBS) Freedom:** 39.6% (Zavegepant) vs 31.1% (Placebo) [Study 1].
- **Secondary Outcomes:** Zavegepant showed superiority in pain relief at 2 hours and return to normal function [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature: 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Store in the original blister package until use [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
