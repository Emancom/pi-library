# Editorial Product Information: Zoster Vaccine Recombinant, Adjuvanted (SHINGRIX)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data, dosing, and safety information into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory guidelines for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Recombinant varicella zoster virus surface glycoprotein E (gE) antigen, adjuvanted with $AS01_B$.
- **Brand Name:** **SHINGRIX** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** J07BK03
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injectable suspension [Australian]. 
    - After reconstitution, a single 0.5 mL dose contains 50 mcg of the recombinant gE antigen.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prevention of Herpes Zoster (HZ):**
    - Indicated for adults aged 50 years and older [Australian].
    - Indicated for adults aged 18 years and older who are at increased risk of HZ due to immunodeficiency or immunosuppression resulting from known disease or therapy [Australian].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not indicated for the prevention of primary varicella infection (chickenpox) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** A two-dose series of 0.5 mL each.
- **Standard Schedule:** First dose at Month 0, followed by a second dose administered 2 to 6 months later [Australian].
- **Accelerated Schedule:** For individuals who are or will be immunodeficient or immunosuppressed and who would benefit from a shorter schedule, the second dose may be administered 1 to 2 months after the first [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer via intramuscular injection only, preferably in the deltoid region.
    - **Reconstitution (Vial/Vial presentation):** The Lyophilized gE Antigen Component (powder) must be reconstituted with the Adjuvant Suspension Component (liquid). Shake gently until dissolved. 
    - **Prefilled Syringe:** Does not require reconstitution; attach a sterile needle prior to administration.
    - Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration before use. The reconstituted vaccine is an opalescent, colorless to pale brownish liquid [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) to any component of the vaccine or following a previous dose of **SHINGRIX** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS):** Postmarketing observational studies have identified an increased risk of GBS during the 42 days following vaccination. Clinicians should weigh the benefits and risks when administering to patients with a history of GBS [Australian].
- **Anaphylaxis and Hypersensitivity:** Appropriate medical treatment and supervision must be available to manage potential anaphylactic reactions [Australian].
- **Syncope:** Fainting can occur in association with injectable vaccines. Procedures should be in place to prevent falling injuries and manage cerebral perfusion [Australian].
- **Thrombocytopenia/Coagulation Disorders:** As with other IM injections, use caution in patients with bleeding disorders or those on anticoagulant therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **Local:** Pain (up to 88%), redness (39%), swelling (31%).
    - **Systemic:** Myalgia (up to 57%), fatigue (57%), headache (51%), shivering (36%), fever (28%), and gastrointestinal symptoms (24%) [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - GBS (postmarketing).
    - Optic ischemic neuropathy was reported in rare instances (0.02%) in clinical trials, though a causal relationship is not established [Australian].
- **Note on Immunocompromised Patients:** Generally reported higher rates of local and systemic reactions (e.g., pain in auHSCT recipients up to 88%) [Australian].
- **Note on Age:** Incidence of solicited reactions was generally lower in subjects aged $\ge$ 70 years compared to those aged 50–69 years [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Concomitant Vaccine Administration:**
    - **Influenza Vaccine:** May be administered concomitantly with quadrivalent influenza vaccine; no interference in immune response was observed [Australian].
    - **Pneumococcal Vaccines:** May be administered with PNEUMOVAX 23 or PREVNAR 13. Concomitant administration with PNEUMOVAX 23 may increase reports of fever and shivering [Australian].
    - **dTpa Vaccine:** May be administered with BOOSTRIX. While immune response to most antigens was unaffected, the response to the pertactin (pertussis) antigen was reduced; the clinical significance is unknown [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category:** Not specified in available sources (The source states "Data are insufficient to establish if there is vaccine-associated risk in pregnant women") [Australian].
    - Animal studies in rats showed no adverse effects on fetal development or malformations.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if vaccine components are excreted in human milk. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be considered alongside the mother's clinical need [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effects on male or female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in individuals younger than 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No clinically meaningful differences in efficacy were observed across age groups. Efficacy remains high in those $\ge$ 70 years [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **SHINGRIX** is a non-live, recombinant vaccine. It is designed to induce an antigen-specific cellular and humoral immune response in individuals with pre-existing immunity against varicella zoster virus (VZV). It boosts VZV-specific immunity, which declines with age and immunosuppression [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics/Pharmacokinetics:** As a vaccine, standard pharmacokinetic studies (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination) are not applicable [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy in Adults $\ge$ 50 Years (Study 1):** In a randomized, placebo-controlled study, vaccine efficacy against HZ was 97.2% (95% CI: 93.7, 99.0) [Australian].
- **Efficacy in Adults $\ge$ 70 Years (Study 2 & Pooled):** Efficacy against HZ in subjects $\ge$ 70 years was 89.8%. Pooled analysis showed 91.3% efficacy in this age group.
- **Efficacy against Postherpetic Neuralgia (PHN):** Significant reduction in PHN incidence was observed (pooled efficacy of 88.8% in subjects $\ge$ 70 years) [Australian].
- **Immunocompromised Adults:** In auHSCT recipients $\ge$ 18 years, efficacy against HZ was 68.2% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated between 2°C and 8°C (36°F to 46°F).
- **Protection:** Store in the original package to protect from light.
- **Handling:** Do not freeze; discard if the vaccine has been frozen.
- **Reconstituted Stability:** Use immediately or store refrigerated and use within 6 hours. Discard if not used within 6 hours [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (S4) [Australian - standard classification for vaccines].
- **Classification:** Rx-only [US context mentioned in source].
