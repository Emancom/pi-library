# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesizes clinical data and regulatory instructions into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **Intravenous Solution:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    *   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    *   **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    *   **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
    *   **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    *   **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    *   **Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
*   Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
*   **Warfarin Interaction:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with warfarin. Monitor INR levels frequently [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### Other Serious/Notable Effects
*   **Body Odor:** Transient body odor has been reported with levocarnitine use [US].
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis [US].
*   **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (up to 15% in specific dose groups) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Pharmacodynamic interaction resulting in increased INR and potential bleeding risk. **Management:** Monitor INR after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].
*   **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** Chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned by letter, but noted as use only if clearly needed]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Animal data (dairy cows) indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits to the mother. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models at 3.8 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for inborn errors of metabolism is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available source [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For IV use in ESRD, see Section 3 [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production [US]. 
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    *   **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose, ~76% is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly cleared [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) plasma carnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging Study:** ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
*   **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
*   **Stability:** When mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
