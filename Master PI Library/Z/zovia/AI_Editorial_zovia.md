# Editorial Product Information: Ethynodiol Diacetate and Ethinyl Estradiol (Zovia 1/35)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the approved labelling in their specific region before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Ethynodiol diacetate (a progestogen) and ethinyl estradiol (an estrogen).
- **Brand Names:** **Zovia** 1/35 [US].
- **Chemical Class:** Combination Oral Contraceptive (COC).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:**
    - **Tablet:** Each active pale pink tablet contains 1 mg ethynodiol diacetate and 35 mcg ethinyl estradiol.
    - **Placebo:** White inert tablets containing no active ingredients [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mayne Pharma Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Contraception:** Indicated for the prevention of pregnancy in women who elect to use oral contraceptives [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Cycle:** One tablet is taken daily at the same time for 28 consecutive days [US].
- **Sequence:** 21 days of active (pale pink) tablets followed by 7 days of inert (white) tablets [US].

### Administration Schedules
- **Day 1 Start:** The patient takes the first tablet on the first day of menstrual flow. No backup contraception is required if started on Day 1 [US].
- **Sunday Start:** The patient takes the first tablet on the first Sunday after menstruation begins (if it begins on Sunday, start that day). A backup method of contraception (e.g., condoms, foam) must be used for the first 7 days of the first cycle [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions [US]
- **1 Active Tablet:** Take as soon as remembered. Take the next tablet at the regular time (may result in 2 tablets in one day).
- **2 Active Tablets (Week 1 or 2):** Take 2 tablets for 2 consecutive days. Use backup contraception for 7 days.
- **2 Active Tablets (Week 3) or 3+ Active Tablets:** 
    - *Day 1 Starters:* Discard the pack and start a new pack the same day.
    - *Sunday Starters:* Take 1 tablet daily until Sunday, then discard the pack and start a new pack.
    - *Note:* Use backup contraception for 7 days following the missed dose.
- **Placebo Tablets:** Discard the missed tablets and continue the schedule as planned.

## 4. Contraindications
**Zovia** is contraindicated in women with the following conditions [US]:
- Thrombophlebitis or thromboembolic disorders (active or history of).
- Cerebral vascular or coronary artery disease (active or history of).
- Myocardial infarction (active or history of).
- Known or suspected carcinoma of the breast (active or history of).
- Known or suspected estrogen-dependent neoplasia or carcinoma of the female reproductive organs (active or history of).
- Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding.
- History of cholestatic jaundice of pregnancy or jaundice with prior pill use.
- Hepatic adenomas, carcinomas, or benign liver tumors (active or history of).
- Known or suspected pregnancy.
- Concomitant use with Hepatitis C drug combinations containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Cigarette Smoking and Cardiovascular Risk [US]
- **Cigarette smoking increases the risk of serious cardiovascular side effects from combination oral contraceptive (COC) use.**
- This risk increases with age (particularly over 35 years) and with heavy smoking (15 or more cigarettes per day). 
- Women who use COCs should be strongly advised not to smoke.

### General Warnings
- **Thromboembolic Disorders:** COC use is associated with an increased risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE), pulmonary embolism, myocardial infarction, and stroke. Risk is highest during the first year of use [US].
- **Surgery:** Discontinue at least 4 weeks prior to and 2 weeks after elective surgery associated with increased risk of thromboembolism or during prolonged immobilisation [US].
- **Blood Pressure:** COCs may cause an increase in blood pressure; patients with a history of hypertension or renal disease should use alternative methods [US].
- **Ocular Lesions:** Discontinue immediately if there is unexplained partial or complete loss of vision, proptosis, diplopia, or papilledema [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** ALT elevations (>5x ULN) have been reported when used with certain Hepatitis C regimens. COCs should be discontinued prior to starting these treatments and can be restarted 2 weeks after completion [US].
- **Glucose/Lipid Metabolism:** COCs may decrease glucose tolerance and increase triglycerides. Monitor prediabetic and diabetic patients closely [US].
- **Headache:** New onset or exacerbation of migraine or development of severe recurrent headaches requires discontinuation and evaluation [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- Thrombophlebitis/thrombosis [US].
- Arterial thromboembolism and pulmonary embolism [US].
- Myocardial infarction and cerebral hemorrhage/thrombosis [US].
- Hypertension and gallbladder disease [US].
- Benign and malignant liver tumors [US].

### Common/Drug-Related Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, bloating [US].
- **Reproductive:** Breakthrough bleeding, spotting, change in menstrual flow, amenorrhea, temporary infertility post-discontinuation [US].
- **Skin/Breasts:** Melasma/chloasma (may persist), breast tenderness/enlargement/secretion [US].
- **General:** Weight changes, edema, mental depression, reduced tolerance to carbohydrates, vaginal candidiasis, intolerance to contact lenses [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Rifampin, barbiturates, phenylbutazone, phenytoin, and potentially griseofulvin, ampicillin, and tetracyclines may reduce COC efficacy and increase breakthrough bleeding [US].
- **HCV Treatments:** Concomitant use with ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir may cause significant ALT elevations [US].

### Laboratory Interactions
- COCs may increase prothrombin and factors VII, VIII, IX, and X; decrease antithrombin III; and increase thyroid-binding globulin (TBG) [US].
- Serum folate levels may be depressed [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X [US].** COCs should not be used during pregnancy. If pregnancy is confirmed, the drug must be discontinued immediately. There is no evidence of increased birth defects when COCs are taken inadvertently during early pregnancy [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts of steroids are excreted in breast milk. COCs may decrease the quantity and quality of milk. Nursing mothers are advised to use other forms of contraception until the child is completely weaned [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established in women of reproductive age. Use is expected to be similar in postpubertal adolescents under 16; use before menarche is not indicated [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not indicated in postmenopausal women. Healthy nonsmoking women over 40 may use COCs, though the lowest effective dose is recommended [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Steroids may be poorly metabolised; use with caution and discontinue if jaundice develops [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though hypertension risk is noted [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primarily acts by suppressing gonadotropins to inhibit ovulation. Secondary mechanisms include thickening cervical mucus (to inhibit sperm entry) and altering the endometrium (to reduce the likelihood of implantation) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not detailed in the source beyond identifying active moieties [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Source documents refer to extensive epidemiological studies and retrospective case-control/prospective cohort studies rather than specific pivotal Phase III trials. These studies establish the relative risks of cardiovascular disease and cancer associated with COC use [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule:** Rx-only [US].
- **STDs:** Patients must be counselled that this product does **not** protect against HIV infection (AIDS) or other sexually transmitted diseases [US].
- **Non-Contraceptive Benefits:** Long-term use of COCs (>35 mcg ethinyl estradiol) is associated with decreased risks of iron-deficiency anemia, dysmenorrhea, functional ovarian cysts, ectopic pregnancies, fibroadenomas, PID, endometrial cancer, and ovarian cancer [US].
