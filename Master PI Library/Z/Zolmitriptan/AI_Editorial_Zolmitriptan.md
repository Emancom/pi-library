# Editorial Product Information: Zolmitriptan

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **zolmitriptan** for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Prescribers must consult the current, locally approved PI before clinical use.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Zolmitriptan (5HT1B/1D receptor agonist).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Zolmitriptan [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N02CC03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 2.5 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Migraine:** Indicated for the acute treatment of migraine attacks with or without aura [Australian].
- **Note:** Zolmitriptan is not indicated for the prophylactic management of migraine [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults <65 years):**
    - **Initial Dose:** 2.5 mg.
    - **Recurrent Symptoms:** If symptoms persist or recur within 24 hours of the initial response, a second dose may be taken.
    - **Minimum Dosing Interval:** Do not take a second dose within 2 hours of the initial dose.
    - **Dose Titration:** If a 2.5 mg dose provides insufficient relief, subsequent attacks may be treated with 5 mg doses.
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** The total intake within a 24-hour period should not exceed 10 mg [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be swallowed whole with water and taken as early as possible after the onset of a migraine headache.

## 4. Contraindications
Zolmitriptan is contraindicated in patients with:
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to any component of the formulation.
- **Ischaemic Heart Disease:** History of myocardial infarction, Prinzmetal angina, coronary vasospasm, or peripheral vascular disease.
- **Cerebrovascular Disease:** History of cerebrovascular accident (CVA) or transient ischaemic attack (TIA).
- **Hypertension:** Moderate or severe hypertension, or mild uncontrolled hypertension.
- **Renal Impairment:** Severe impairment with creatinine clearance <15 mL/min [Australian].
- **Concomitant Medications:** 
    - Ergotamine or ergotamine derivatives.
    - Other 5HT1D receptor agonists.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Diagnosis Confirmation:** Only use when a clear diagnosis of migraine is established. Care must be taken to exclude other serious neurological conditions (e.g., subarachnoid haemorrhage).
- **Cardiac Evaluation:** Perform cardiovascular evaluation in patients with risk factors for ischaemic heart disease before initiating therapy. Atypical sensations over the precordium (tightness/pressure) may occur; if cardiac involvement is suspected, discontinue use and evaluate.
- **Cardiac Accessory Conduction Pathways:** Should not be used in patients with symptomatic Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome or associated arrhythmias.
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** A potentially life-threatening condition reported when triptans are combined with SSRIs or SNRIs. Symptoms include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular aberrations.
- **Medication Overuse Headache (MOH):** Excessive use of acute migraine treatments may lead to daily migraine-like headaches. Detoxification may be necessary.
- **Blood Pressure:** Transient increases in systemic blood pressure may occur, particularly in the elderly.
- **Driving/Machinery:** Somnolence may occur; caution is advised when performing skilled tasks.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typically mild to moderate, transient, and occur within 4 hours of dosing.

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (≥1% to <10%) | Nervous System | Dizziness, somnolence, paraesthesia, hyperaesthesia, headache. |
| | Gastrointestinal | Nausea, dry mouth, vomiting, abdominal pain, dysphagia. |
| | General/Cardiac | Palpitations, asthenia, heaviness/tightness/pain in chest, neck, throat, or limbs. |
| **Uncommon** (≥0.1% to <1%) | Cardiovascular | Tachycardia, syncope, transient BP increase, arrhythmias. |
| | Renal | Polyuria, increased urinary frequency. |
| **Rare** (≥0.01% to <0.1%) | Immune/Skin | Anaphylaxis, angioedema, urticaria, photosensitivity. |
| | Cardiovascular | Bradycardia, QT prolongation, postural hypotension. |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Cardiac/GI | Myocardial infarction, angina, ischaemic colitis, splenic infarction. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Ergotamine/Derivatives:** Increased risk of coronary vasospasm. Wait at least 24 hours after ergotamine use before taking zolmitriptan. Wait at least 6 hours after zolmitriptan use before taking ergotamine [Australian].
- **MAO-A Inhibitors (e.g., moclobemide):** Exposure to the active metabolite (N-desmethylzolmitriptan) increases 3-fold. **Dose Limit:** Maximum 5 mg zolmitriptan in 24 hours [Australian].
- **CYP1A2 Inhibitors (e.g., cimetidine, fluvoxamine, quinolones):** Cimetidine increases zolmitriptan half-life by 44%. **Dose Limit:** Maximum 5 mg zolmitriptan in 24 hours [Australian].
- **SSRIs/SNRIs:** Risk of Serotonin Syndrome; clinical monitoring is required.
- **St. John’s Wort:** Potential for increased pharmacodynamic adverse effects.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3 [Australian]**. Adequate and well-controlled human studies are absent. Use only if the expected benefit to the mother outweighs the potential risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Zolmitriptan is excreted in the milk of lactating animals. There is no human data. Caution should be exercised when administered to breastfeeding women.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Efficacy not established in patients aged 12–17 years; not evaluated in patients <12 years.
- **Elderly (>65 years):** Use is not recommended as safety and efficacy have not been systematically evaluated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - Mild/Moderate: No dosage adjustment required.
    - Severe: **Maximum dose 5 mg in 24 hours** [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required if CrCl is 5–39 mL/min. Contraindicated if CrCl <15 mL/min [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective agonist for 5HT1B and 5HT1D receptors. It induces vasoconstriction of pain-sensitive cranial blood vessels and inhibits the release of pro-inflammatory neuropeptides (CGRP, VIP, Substance P) via the trigeminal system.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (≥64% absorbed); bioavailability is ~40%. Food does not affect absorption.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily converted by CYP1A2 to an active N-desmethyl metabolite (which is 2–6 times more potent than the parent drug) and other inactive metabolites via MAO-A.
    - **Elimination:** Mean elimination half-life is 2.5–3 hours. Approximately 60% is excreted in urine and 30% in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal trials involving over 3,000 patients demonstrated that zolmitriptan (2.5 mg and 5 mg) significantly outperformed placebo.
- **Response Rates:** In Phase II studies, a 2-hour response rate was 61% (5 mg) vs. 21% (placebo).
- **Efficacy:** The 2.5 mg dose was found to be equi-effective to the 5 mg dose in Phase III studies, with a 63-65% response rate. Pain relief onset was typically observed within 1 hour.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Packaging:** Keep in the original container to protect from light/moisture.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
