# Editorial Product Information: Ziprasidone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- At the time of this publication, PI documents from other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison. Therefore, regional variations outside of the Australian context are marked as "Not specified in available sources."
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ziprasidone hydrochloride (atypical antipsychotic).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Ziprasidone [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05AE04 [Not specified in provided source].
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Hard gelatin capsules: 20 mg, 40 mg, 60 mg, and 80 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia:** Treatment of schizophrenia and related psychoses, prevention of relapse, and maintenance of clinical improvement during continuation therapy [Australian].
- **Bipolar I Disorder:** Monotherapy for the short-term treatment of acute manic or mixed episodes associated with Bipolar I disorder [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Capsules must be taken with food and swallowed whole. Do not chew, crush, or open capsules [Australian]. 
- **Bioavailability Note:** Bioavailability increases by up to 100% in the presence of food [Australian].

### Adult Dosing by Indication
- **Schizophrenia:** 
    - Recommended starting dose: 40 mg twice daily.
    - Dosage range: 40 mg to 80 mg twice daily. 
    - Titration: Adjustments should occur at intervals of no less than 2 days. The maximum dose (80 mg twice daily) may be reached by Day 3 [Australian].
    - Maintenance: Use the lowest effective dose; 20 mg twice daily may be sufficient for some patients [Australian].
- **Bipolar Mania:** 
    - Recommended starting dose: 40 mg twice daily on Day 1.
    - Dosage range: 40 mg to 80 mg twice daily.
    - Titration: May be adjusted based on clinical status up to 80 mg twice daily as early as Day 2 [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to ziprasidone or any excipients (e.g., lactose, sulfites) [Australian].
- Recent acute myocardial infarction [Australian].
- Uncompensated heart failure [Australian].
- Conditions predisposing to QT interval prolongation, including:
    - History of QT prolongation or congenital long QT syndrome [Australian].
    - Concomitant use of drugs known to prolong the QT interval [Australian].
    - Arrhythmias treated with Class IA or Class III antiarrhythmic drugs [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Prolongation:** Ziprasidone causes a mild to moderate increase in the QTc interval (mean increase 9–14 msec greater than other atypical antipsychotics). Risk of **Torsade de Pointes** and sudden death increases if the QTc exceeds 500 msec. Discontinue treatment if QTc remains >500 msec [Australian].
- **Increased Mortality in Elderly (Dementia-Related Psychosis):** Use of antipsychotics in this population is associated with an increased risk of death and cerebrovascular events (e.g., stroke). Ziprasidone is **not approved** for dementia-related psychosis [Australian].
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal; symptoms include hyperthermia, muscle rigidity, and autonomic instability. Discontinue all antipsychotics if suspected [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Reports of **DRESS** (Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms) and Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), which may be fatal. Discontinue immediately if SCARs occur [Australian].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Risk increases with long-term exposure. Consider dose reduction or discontinuation if signs appear [Australian].
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Identify risk factors before and during treatment [Australian].
- **Hyperglycaemia and Diabetes Mellitus:** Monitor patients with established diabetes or risk factors (e.g., obesity) for worsening glucose control [Australian].
- **Rash:** Occurred in approximately 5% of patients in trials. Discontinue if an alternative etiology cannot be identified [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Baseline and periodic serum potassium and magnesium in patients at risk for electrolyte disturbances [Australian].
    - Fasting blood glucose for patients with diabetes risk factors [Australian].
    - Fall risk assessments for patients on long-term therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data (Placebo-Controlled Trials)
| Body System | Common (1–10%) | Uncommon (0.1–1%) | Rare (<0.1%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous** | Akathisia (8.7%), Sedation (9.2%), Dizziness, Dystonia, Headache, Extrapyramidal disorder | Ataxia, Bradykinesia, Seizures, Hypersomnia, Paraesthesia | Akinesia, Paresis, Restless legs syndrome |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, Vomiting, Constipation, Dry mouth, Dyspepsia | Dysphagia, Flatulence, Gastritis | GORD, Diarrhoea |
| **Psychiatric** | Anxiety, Agitation, Restlessness | Nightmare, Libido decreased | Panic attack, Somnambulism |
| **Cardiac/Vascular** | Tachycardia (Post-marketing) | Palpitation, Prolonged QT, BBB | Torsade de pointes (Post-marketing) |
| **General** | Fatigue, Asthenia | Gait disturbance, Thirst | Pyrexia, Chest pain |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Torsade de pointes
- Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome
- DRESS and Stevens-Johnson Syndrome
- Syncope (reported in 0.6% of patients)
- Priapism
- Anaphylaxis/Angioedema

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **QT-prolonging Drugs:** Contraindicated (e.g., Class IA/III antiarrhythmics, certain antibiotics/antimalarials) [Australian].
    - **CNS Agents/Alcohol:** Increased risk of sedation and motor impairment [Australian].
    - **Dopamine Agonists:** Ziprasidone may antagonise their effects [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Ketoconazole):** May increase ziprasidone levels by ~35–40% [Australian].
    - **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Carbamazepine):** May decrease ziprasidone levels by ~35% [Australian].
    - **Lithium:** No pharmacokinetic interaction, but caution is advised due to potential additive cardiac conduction effects [Australian].
    - **Antacids:** No significant effect on ziprasidone pharmacokinetics [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category C** [Australian].
- **Pregnancy Clinical Notes:** Ziprasidone crosses the placenta. Neonates exposed during the third trimester are at risk of extrapyramidal symptoms or withdrawal (e.g., agitation, hypertonia, tremor, respiratory distress). Use only if benefits outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in breast milk at very low levels. Patients should **not** breastfeed while taking ziprasidone [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed increased time to copulation and impaired fertility in females at high doses; no effect on male fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not approved for use in patients <18 years of age. Increased incidence of sedation and somnolence observed in clinical trials for this age group [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment for schizophrenia in patients ≥65 years. Not systematically evaluated for bipolar mania in the elderly [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required. Ziprasidone is not removed by haemodialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. Consider lower doses in mild-to-moderate impairment (Child-Pugh A/B); clinical experience in severe impairment is lacking [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** High affinity for D2, D3, 5HT2A, 5HT2C, 5HT1A, 5HT1D, and α1-adrenergic receptors. It acts as an antagonist at D2, 5HT2A, and 5HT1D receptors and an agonist at 5HT1A. It also inhibits the synaptic reuptake of serotonin and noradrenaline [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak levels at 6–8 hours post-dose (with food) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** >99% protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via CYP3A4 and aldehyde oxidase [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 6–10 hours. Approximately 20% excreted in urine and 66% in faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Schizophrenia Maintenance:** In a 52-week trial, doses of 20 mg, 40 mg, and 80 mg twice daily were superior to placebo in preventing relapse [Australian].
- **Bipolar Mania:** Efficacy established in two 3-week placebo-controlled trials showing significant reduction in Mania Rating Scale (MRS) scores [Australian].
- **ZODIAC Study:** A large post-marketing safety study (n=18,239) found no significant difference in non-suicide mortality between ziprasidone and olanzapine over one year [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in the original container [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
