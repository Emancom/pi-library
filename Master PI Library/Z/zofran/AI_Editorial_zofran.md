## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ondansetron (as hydrochloride dihydrate or base)
- **Chemical Class:** Selective 5-HT$_3$ receptor antagonist
- **Brand Names:** Zofran, Zofran Zydis [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** A04AA01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets:** 4 mg, 8 mg, and 24 mg* [Australian]
    - **Oral Liquid:** 0.8 mg/mL [Australian]
    - **Zydis Wafers (Orally Disintegrating):** 4 mg and 8 mg [Australian]
    - **Injection:** 4 mg/2 mL and 8 mg/4 mL* [Australian]
    - **Suppositories:** 16 mg* [Australian]
    - *\*Note: 24 mg tablets, suppositories, and injections are noted as no longer marketed in some regions but remain described in the clinical reference material [Australian].*
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral, Intravenous (IV), Intramuscular (IM), Rectal
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (CINV):** Prevention and treatment of nausea and vomiting induced by cytotoxic therapy in adults and children (over 4 years for IV/oral).
- **Radiotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (RINV):** Prevention and treatment of nausea and vomiting induced by radiotherapy in adults.
- **Post-operative Nausea and Vomiting (PONV):** Prevention and treatment of post-operative nausea and vomiting in adults and children (aged 1 month to 17 years for injection; oral formulations approved for adults only) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Emetogenic Chemotherapy/Radiotherapy:** 8 mg via slow IV injection (not less than 30 seconds) immediately before treatment, OR 8 mg orally twice daily (first dose 2 hours before treatment), OR 16 mg rectally 2 hours before treatment.
- **Highly Emetogenic Chemotherapy:** A single 8 mg slow IV dose is standard. Doses up to 32 mg in 24 hours may be used based on severity. 
    - **Note:** Single IV doses must not exceed 16 mg and must be infused over at least 15 minutes [Australian].
- **Delayed Emesis:** 8 mg orally twice daily or 16 mg rectally once daily for up to 5 days post-treatment.
- **PONV Prevention:** 4 mg via IM or slow IV injection at induction of anaesthesia, OR 16 mg orally 1 hour prior to anaesthesia.
- **PONV Treatment:** 4 mg via IM or slow IV injection.

### Administration Instructions
- **Zydis Wafers:** Place on top of the tongue; dissolves in seconds; swallow with saliva.
- **IV Compatibility:** Stable for 7 days at room temperature when diluted with Sodium Chloride 0.9%, Glucose 5%, or Ringer's solution [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Concomitant Apomorphine:** Use with apomorphine hydrochloride is contraindicated due to reports of profound hypotension and loss of consciousness.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ondansetron or any component of the formulation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Prolongation:** Ondansetron causes dose-dependent prolongation of the QT interval. Cases of Torsade de Pointes have been reported. Avoid in patients with congenital long QT syndrome. Use with caution in patients with electrolyte abnormalities (hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia), congestive heart failure, or bradyarrhythmias.
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Reported with 5-HT$_3$ antagonists, particularly when used with other serotonergic drugs (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs).
- **Myocardial Ischaemia:** Cases have been reported, sometimes occurring immediately after IV administration. 
- **Intestinal Obstruction:** Ondansetron increases large bowel transit time; monitor patients with signs of subacute obstruction.
- **Phenylketonuria:** Wafers contain aspartame [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Constipation, sensation of warmth or flushing, local injection site reactions, xerostomia (oral), local burning (rectal) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Seizures, movement disorders (extrapyramidal reactions), arrhythmias, chest pain, hypotension, bradycardia, hiccups, asymptomatic LFT increases |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Immediate hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis), dizziness (rapid IV), transient visual disturbances (blurred vision), QTc prolongation |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Transient blindness (predominantly during IV administration; usually resolves within 20 mins), toxic skin eruptions (TEN) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Apomorphine:** Contraindicated (profound hypotension).
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Potent inducers (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampicin) significantly increase oral clearance and reduce ondansetron blood levels.
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of Serotonin Syndrome when used with SSRIs or SNRIs.
- **Tramadol:** Ondansetron may reduce the analgesic efficacy of tramadol.
- **Other:** No significant interactions observed with alcohol, temazepam, furosemide, or propofol.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Epidemiological studies suggest an increased risk of orofacial malformations (oral clefts) when administered during the first trimester. Ondansetron should not be used during the first trimester of pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in animal milk; breastfeeding is not recommended for mothers receiving ondansetron.
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility shown in animal studies (rats).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **CINV:** Approved for children >4 years. Recommended IV dose is 5 mg/m$^2$ followed by 4 mg orally twice daily.
    - **PONV:** Approved for children >1 month. Recommended IV dose is 0.1 mg/kg (max 4 mg). 
- **Elderly Use:** 
    - **CINV (<75 years):** Single IV dose must not exceed 16 mg (infused over 15 mins).
    - **CINV (≥75 years):** Single IV dose must not exceed 8 mg (infused over 15 mins).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In patients with moderate or severe hepatic dysfunction (Pugh score ≥7), the total daily dose should not exceed 8 mg.
- **Renal Impairment:** No alteration of daily dosage or frequency is required.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Highly selective 5-HT$_3$ receptor antagonist. It blocks the initiation of the vomiting reflex by antagonising 5-HT$_3$ receptors on vagal afferents in the gastrointestinal tract and the area postrema in the CNS.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Approximately 60% (oral).
    - **Peak Plasma Time:** ~1.5 hours (oral); ~6 hours (rectal).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism via CYP3A4, CYP2D6, and CYP1A2.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 4.1–11.6 hours (oral) and 2.5–6.1 hours (IV).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CINV:** Trials demonstrated that a 32 mg single IV dose was statistically superior to 8 mg in patients receiving high-dose cisplatin, though subsequent safety data led to a 16 mg single IV dose limit.
- **Pregnancy Safety:** A cohort study of 1.8 million pregnancies showed an adjusted relative risk of 1.24 for oral clefts in the first trimester.
- **PONV:** Pivotal trials in 1,049 paediatric patients showed ondansetron was significantly more effective than placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **General:** Store below 30°C.
- **Oral Liquid:** Store upright; do **not** refrigerate.
- **Injection:** Protect from light.
- **Diluted IV Solutions:** Should be used within 24 hours.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Animal Remedy)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine.
