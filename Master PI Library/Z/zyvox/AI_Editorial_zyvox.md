# Editorial Product Information: Linezolid

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and United States (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized editorial synthesis of official Product Information from the listed jurisdictions, designed as a coherent reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Linezolid (INN)
- **Chemical Class:** Oxazolidinone antibacterial
- **Brand Name:** **ZYVOX**
- **ATC Code:** J01XX08
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated Tablets:** 600 mg [Australian, US]; 400 mg [US].
    - **Granules for Oral Suspension:** 20 mg/mL (100 mg/5 mL) following reconstitution [Australian, US].
    - **Solution for Injection:** 2 mg/mL (available in 300 mL, 200 mL, and 100 mL bags) [Australian, US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion and Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]; Pharmacia & Upjohn Company LLC (subsidiary of Pfizer Inc) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**ZYVOX** is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by susceptible strains of designated Gram-positive microorganisms. It is not active against Gram-negative pathogens.

- **Vancomycin-resistant *Enterococcus faecium* (VRE) infections:** Including cases with concurrent bacteremia [Australian, US].
- **Nosocomial pneumonia:** Caused by *Staphylococcus aureus* (methicillin-susceptible and -resistant isolates) or *Streptococcus pneumoniae* [Australian, US].
- **Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP):** Caused by *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (including cases with concurrent bacteremia) or *Staphylococcus aureus* (methicillin-susceptible isolates only) [Australian, US].
- **Complicated skin and skin structure infections (cSSSI):** Including diabetic foot infections without concomitant osteomyelitis [US-approved]; caused by *S. aureus* (MRSA/MSSA), *S. pyogenes*, or *S. agalactiae* [Australian, US].
- **Uncomplicated skin and skin structure infections:** Caused by *S. aureus* (methicillin-susceptible isolates only) or *S. pyogenes* [US-approved; not explicitly listed as a separate Australian category].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
The oral formulations (tablets or suspension) may be used interchangeably with the IV formulation; due to ~100% bioavailability, no dose adjustment is required when switching between routes. IV infusion should be administered over 30 to 120 minutes.

### Adult and Adolescent (12 years and older)
- **Pneumonia and cSSSI:** 600 mg IV or orally every 12 hours for 10–14 days.
- **VRE Infections:** 600 mg IV or orally every 12 hours for 14–28 days.
- **Uncomplicated SSSI:**
    - Adults: 400 mg orally every 12 hours [US].
    - Adolescents: 600 mg orally every 12 hours [US].
- **Duration:** Maximum recommended duration is 28 days [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing (Birth through 11 years)
- **Pneumonia and cSSSI:** 10 mg/kg IV or orally every 8 hours for 10–14 days.
- **VRE Infections:** 10 mg/kg IV or orally every 8 hours for 14–28 days.
- **Uncomplicated SSSI:**
    - <5 years: 10 mg/kg orally every 8 hours [US].
    - 5–11 years: 10 mg/kg orally every 12 hours [US].
- **Neonatal Note (<7 days):** Most pre-term neonates (<34 weeks gestation) have lower clearance. Initial dosing should be 10 mg/kg every 12 hours. Increase to 10 mg/kg every 8 hours by day 7 of life or if clinical response is sub-optimal.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to linezolid or any excipients (e.g., aspartame in oral suspension [Australian]).
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use or use within two weeks of taking any medicinal product that inhibits MAO-A or MAO-B (e.g., phenelzine, isocarboxazid).
- **Unmonitored Hypertension/Sympathomimetics:** Unless monitored for blood pressure increases, avoid in patients with uncontrolled hypertension, pheochromocytoma, thyrotoxicosis, or those taking direct/indirect sympathomimetics, vasopressors, or dopaminergic agents.
- **Unmonitored Serotonergic Agents:** Unless closely observed for serotonin syndrome, avoid in patients with carcinoid syndrome or those taking SSRIs, tricyclic antidepressants, triptans, pethidine/meperidine, or buspirone.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression:** Anaemia, leucopenia, pancytopenia, and especially thrombocytopenia have been reported. **Weekly complete blood counts (CBC)** are required for all patients, particularly those treated for >2 weeks, those with pre-existing myelosuppression, or those with renal/hepatic impairment.
- **Peripheral and Optic Neuropathy:** Reported primarily with treatment >28 days. If visual impairment occurs (changes in acuity, color vision, or field), prompt ophthalmic evaluation is required.
- **Lactic Acidosis:** Reported with linezolid use. Patients with recurrent nausea, vomiting, or unexplained acidosis require immediate medical evaluation.
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potential for fatal outcomes when co-administered with serotonergic agents. Monitor for agitation, hyperreflexia, and hyperthermia.
- **Mortality Imbalance:** An investigational study in catheter-related bloodstream infections showed higher mortality in the linezolid arm compared to vancomycin/dicloxacillin/oxacillin, primarily associated with Gram-negative or mixed infections. Linezolid is **not approved** for catheter-related bloodstream infections.
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterials; ranges from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis.
- **Hypoglycemia:** Post-marketing cases of symptomatic hypoglycemia reported in patients with diabetes mellitus receiving insulin or oral hypoglycemics [US].
- **Hyponatremia/SIADH:** Monitor serum sodium levels, especially in the elderly and those taking diuretics.
- **Convulsions:** Reported rarely; often in patients with a history of seizures or risk factors.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 22% of adult patients experience adverse reactions [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>1%)** | Diarrhea, headache, nausea, vomiting, taste perversion/alteration, abnormal liver function tests, vaginal or oral candidiasis, dizziness, rash. |
| **Uncommon/Rare** | Thrombocytopenia, anaemia, leucopenia, pancytopenia, lactic acidosis, optic/peripheral neuropathy, convulsions, angioedema, anaphylaxis. |
| **Post-marketing** | Rhabdomyolysis, SIADH, superficial tooth and tongue discoloration (removable with dental cleaning), bullous skin disorders (SJS/TEN). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibition:** Linezolid is a reversible, non-selective MAOI.
- **Adrenergic Agents:** Potential for enhanced pressor response with pseudoephedrine or phenylpropanolamine. Initial doses of adrenaline (epinephrine) or dopamine should be reduced and titrated.
- **Tyramine:** Avoid ingestion of >100 mg tyramine (e.g., aged cheeses, soy sauce, red wine) due to potential for significant pressor response.
- **Serotonergic Agents:** Increased risk of Serotonin Syndrome when used with SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, or opioids (e.g., dextromethorphan, meperidine).
- **Rifampin:** Co-administration reduced linezolid Cmax by 21% and AUC by 32%; the clinical significance is unknown.
- **Cytochrome P450:** Linezolid does not inhibit or induce clinically significant CYP isoforms (1A2, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, 3A4). No interaction expected with warfarin or phenytoin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **Australian Category B3:** Limited data; animal studies show embryo-fetal toxicity. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk.
    - **US Perspective:** Available data have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. Animal data showed decreased pup survival and increased preimplantation loss at exposures equivalent to human levels.
- **Breastfeeding:** Linezolid passes into breast milk. The infant dose is estimated at 6% to 9% of the weight-adjusted maternal dose. Monitor breastfed infants for diarrhea and vomiting. Discontinuation of breastfeeding prior to administration was previously suggested [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Reversibly decreased fertility and altered spermatogenesis observed in male rats [Australian, US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Clearance is highest in infants and children (1 week to 11 years) and decreases with age.
- **Elderly:** No significant pharmacokinetic differences; no dose adjustment required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for any degree of renal insufficiency. However, metabolites (A and B) accumulate in severe impairment (CLcr <30 mL/min). Use with caution.
- **Haemodialysis:** Approximately 30% of a dose is removed during 3 hours of dialysis. **ZYVOX** should be administered **after** haemodialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild-to-moderate (Child-Pugh A or B) impairment. Not evaluated in severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Linezolid binds to the 23S ribosomal RNA of the 50S subunit. This prevents the formation of the functional 70S initiation complex, an essential stage of the bacterial translation process.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Time-kill studies show bacteriostatic activity against enterococci and staphylococci, and bactericidal activity against most strains of streptococci.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and extensive; 100% absolute bioavailability.
    - **Distribution:** Readily distributes to well-perfused tissues; 31% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via non-enzymatic oxidation of the morpholine ring into two inactive carboxylic acid derivatives (Metabolites A and B).
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 30% excreted renally as parent drug, 40% as metabolite B, and 10% as metabolite A. Elimination half-life is 5–7 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Nosocomial Pneumonia:** Trials showed clinical cure rates (~53–60%) equivalent to vancomycin.
- **VRE Infections:** High-dose (600 mg bid) showed superior cure rates (67%) compared to low-dose (200 mg bid; 52%) in VRE patients.
- **MRSA:** Comparable efficacy to vancomycin in treating MRSA pneumonia and skin infections.
- **Diabetic Foot Infections:** Linezolid 600 mg bid showed equivalent cure rates (~83%) to ampicillin/sulbactam or amoxicillin/clavulanate (~73%) in clinically evaluable patients.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets and Oral Suspension:** Store below 25°C. Keep bottle tightly closed and protect from light [Australian, US].
- **Oral Suspension (Reconstituted):** Discard if not used within 3 weeks [Australian].
- **Solution for Injection:** Store at 25°C (excursions permitted to 15–30°C). Do not freeze. Keep bags in overwrap and carton until ready to use to protect from light [Australian, US].
