# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the following Product Information (PI) source: United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism; chemically identified as (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours (and never less frequently than every 6 hours) [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight, administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Adjustment
- **Plasma Concentration:** In metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain a baseline plasma carnitine concentration before therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is advised; the target plasma free carnitine concentration should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose Titration (ESRD):** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations <40–50 µmol/L. Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- The formulation contains no preservative; any unused portion of an opened vial must be discarded [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with pre-existing conditions, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** While this PI concerns the injection, it notes that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), and flu syndrome (15–29%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), and chest pain (6–15%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), and nausea (5–12%).
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (10–18%) and paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), and dyspnea (3–11%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis (less frequent) [US].
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate and well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine's presence in human milk has not been specifically studied, though studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. The risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Dosage and Administration; approved for inborn errors of metabolism which often present in the pediatric population [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dosing for the elderly is not provided in the source documents.
- **Renal Impairment:** Indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety of high-dose *oral* carnitine in renal failure is cautioned due to metabolite accumulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters which are rapidly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis) showed that trough concentrations reached 54–180 µmol/L after one week (normal range 40–50 µmol/L). 
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** In a study of ~90 ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg, trough concentrations increased proportionally with the dose and remained stable through 24 weeks of therapy.
- **Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is demonstrated, the effect on specific signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency or clinical outcomes in ESRD has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
