# Editorial Product Information: Zolpidem

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA). [Note: Information from other jurisdictions (US, European, UK, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source files and is therefore not included in this synthesis.]
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of zolpidem tartrate, intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Zolpidem tartrate
- **Chemical Class:** Imidazopyridine (structurally unrelated to benzodiazepines)
- **Brand Names:** APO-Zolpidem [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** N05CF02
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 5 mg film-coated tablets: Pink, capsule-shaped [Australian]
    - 10 mg film-coated tablets: White, modified oval, scored [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Short-term treatment of insomnia:** Approved for use in adults where the insomnia is debilitating or is causing the patient severe distress [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults <65 years):** 10 mg to be taken immediately before retiring. The lowest effective daily dose must be used and should not exceed 10 mg [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Due to rapid onset of action, the drug should be taken immediately before bed. To ensure faster sleep onset, zolpidem should not be administered with or immediately after a meal [Australian].
- **Duration of Treatment:** Use should be limited to the shortest period possible, generally ranging from a few days to two weeks, with a maximum duration of four weeks including the tapering-off period [Australian].
- **Titration:** In adults under 65, the dose may be increased to 10 mg only if the 5 mg dose is inadequate and well-tolerated [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources, though immediate administration before a full night's sleep (7–8 hours) is recommended for efficacy and safety.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to zolpidem tartrate or any excipients [Australian].
- Obstructive sleep apnoea [Australian].
- Myasthenia gravis [Australian].
- Severe hepatic insufficiency [Australian].
- Acute and/or severe pulmonary insufficiency [Australian].
- Use in children under 18 years of age [Australian].
- Prior or concomitant intake with alcohol [Australian].
- History of complex sleep behaviours (e.g., sleepwalking, sleep-driving) after taking zolpidem [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [Australian]:** Zolpidem is associated with potentially dangerous complex sleep-related behaviours, including sleepwalking and sleep-driving. It must not be taken with alcohol. Use should be limited to four weeks maximum under close supervision.
- **CNS Depression:** May impair mental alertness and physical coordination. Patients should not drive or operate machinery for at least 8 hours after dosing.
- **Respiratory Function:** Caution is advised in patients with respiratory insufficiency, although studies have not shown respiratory centre depression at hypnotic doses [Australian].
- **Suicidality and Depression:** Use with caution in patients with symptoms of depression. Zolpidem may unmask pre-existing depression; suicidal tendencies may be present or uncovered during treatment [Australian].
- **Memory Impairment:** Anterograde amnesia may occur. To reduce risk, patients should ensure they have 7–8 hours of uninterrupted sleep [Australian].
- **Severe Injuries:** Drowsiness and decreased consciousness may lead to falls and subsequent severe injuries [Australian].
- **Angioedema:** Rare cases involving the tongue, glottis, or larynx have been reported. Patients experiencing this should not be rechallenged with the drug [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are dose-related, particularly for CNS-related events, and are more frequent in the elderly.

| Body System | Frequency | Adverse Events |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infections** | Common | Upper and lower respiratory tract infections |
| **Psychiatric** | Common | Drowsiness, hallucinations, agitation, nightmare, depression |
| | Uncommon | Confusion, irritability, restlessness, aggressiveness, somnambulism |
| | Rare | Delusion, rages, physical dependence |
| **Nervous System** | Common | Dizziness, headache, somnolence, exacerbated insomnia, amnesia |
| | Uncommon | Paraesthesia, tremor, speech disorder |
| | Rare | Ataxia, depressed level of consciousness |
| **Eye** | Uncommon | Diplopia, blurred vision |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Common | Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Uncommon | Elevated liver enzymes |
| | Rare | Hepatocellular or cholestatic liver injury |
| **General** | Common | Fatigue |
| | Rare | Falls, gait disturbances |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Contraindicated. Increases the risk of complex sleep behaviours and sedative effects [Australian].
- **Opioids:** Concomitant use increases the risk of profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects occur with antipsychotics, hypnotics, anxiolytics, antidepressants, and narcotic analgesics.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Inducers:** 
    - **Inhibitors:** Ketoconazole increases the sedative effect by reducing clearance. Fluvoxamine and ciprofloxacin may increase blood levels; concurrent use is not recommended [Australian].
    - **Inducers:** Rifampicin and St John's Wort significantly decrease blood levels and efficacy [Australian].
- **Warfarin/Digoxin:** No significant pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic interactions observed [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3:** Safety in human pregnancy is not established. Use is not recommended. Administration during late pregnancy or labour is associated with neonatal hypothermia, hypotonia, and respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Small quantities are excreted in breast milk; use in nursing women is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available; animal studies in rats showed reduced post-implantation survival but no impairment of fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children and adolescents under 18 years. Efficacy and safety have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly/Debilitated:** Increased sensitivity to zolpidem. **Dose: 5 mg at night.** This dose should not be exceeded [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment necessary, but close monitoring is required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** **Dose: 5 mg at night.** Contraindicated in severe hepatic insufficiency due to the risk of encephalopathy [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** An imidazopyridine that selectively binds to the omega-1 (BZ1) receptor subtype of the GABA-A receptor complex. This selectivity may explain the relative lack of myorelaxant and anticonvulsant effects at hypnotic doses [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; bioavailability ~70%. Tmax: 0.5–3 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 90% protein bound. 
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4, with minor contributions from CYP1A2 and CYP2D6. All metabolites are inactive.
    - **Excretion:** Eliminated via urine (56%) and faeces (37%). Elimination half-life is ~2.4 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Non-elderly Adults:** 11 placebo-controlled studies (n=1,606) showed that 10 mg zolpidem was superior to placebo in reducing sleep latency and improving sleep quality. Doses above 10 mg showed little increase in efficacy but increased adverse events [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Four studies (n=145) confirmed 5 mg as the optimal dose for efficacy and safety in this population [Australian].
- **Sleep Stages:** Clinical data indicate that zolpidem preserves deep sleep (stages 3 and 4) and has minimal effects on REM sleep at recommended doses [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions

- Keep in the original package [Australian].
- Shelf life is specified on the individual product packaging as per the ARTG listing [Australian].
