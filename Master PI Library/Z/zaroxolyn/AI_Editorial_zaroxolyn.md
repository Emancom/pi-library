# Editorial Product Information: Metolazone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory requirements. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Metolazone (a quinazoline diuretic/antihypertensive).
- **Brand Names:** **Zaroxolyn** [US].
- **ATC Code:** C03BA08 (not explicitly in source, but standard for metolazone).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 2.5 mg and 5 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** UCB, Inc. (Labeler/Repackager: Carilion Materials Management) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Edema:** Treatment of salt and water retention associated with:
    - Congestive heart failure [US].
    - Renal diseases, including nephrotic syndrome and states of diminished renal function [US].
- **Hypertension:** Treatment of high blood pressure, either as monotherapy or in combination with other antihypertensive classes [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Critical Administration Warning (Non-Interchangeability)
**Metolazone formulations are not all bioequivalent.** **Zaroxolyn** tablets (and bioequivalent generics) exhibit slow and incomplete bioavailability. They must **not** be interchanged with **Mykrox** tablets (a more rapidly and completely bioavailable formulation) on a mg-per-mg basis. Switching between these formulations requires dose titration [US].

### 3.2 General Dosing (Adult)
A single daily dose is recommended. Dosage should be individualized and titrated to the minimum effective level.

### 3.3 Dosing by Indication
- **Edema of Cardiac Failure:** 5 mg to 20 mg once daily [US].
- **Edema of Renal Disease:** 5 mg to 20 mg once daily [US].
- **Hypertension:** 2.5 mg to 5 mg once daily [US].
    - *Note:* In hypertension, initial response may take 3 to 4 days, while maximum effect may take 3 to 6 weeks [US].

### 3.4 Administration Instructions
- If therapeutic effect is achieved, dosage should be reduced to lower maintenance levels where possible [US].
- For patients with paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, higher doses may be required to ensure 24-hour diuresis [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Anuria [US].
- Hepatic coma or pre-coma [US].
- Known allergy or hypersensitivity to metolazone [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Rapid Electrolyte Depletion:** Rare reports of severe hyponatremia and/or hypokalemia occurring rapidly after initial dosing. Therapy should be discontinued and supportive measures initiated if symptoms of severe imbalance appear [US].
- **Hypokalemia:** Dose-related risk; particularly hazardous for digitalized patients or those with a history of ventricular arrhythmias. Regular serum potassium monitoring is required [US].
- **Cross-Allergy:** Possible sensitivity in patients allergic to sulfonamide-derived drugs, thiazides, or quinethazone [US].
- **Fluid and Electrolyte Imbalance:** Monitoring of serum electrolytes (sodium, potassium, chloride, magnesium) is essential, especially during high-dose therapy, low-salt diets, or concurrent illness (vomiting/diarrhea) [US].
- **Azotemia:** Prerenal azotemia may be precipitated. If azotemia and oliguria worsen in patients with severe renal disease, the drug should be discontinued [US].
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):** Thiazide-like diuretics may exacerbate or activate SLE [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild and transient, often representing extensions of the drug's pharmacological activity.

| Body System | Adverse Effects (Frequency not specified in source) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiovascular** | Orthostatic hypotension, chest pain, palpitations, venous thrombosis, excessive volume depletion. |
| **Central Nervous System** | Dizziness, headache, syncope, neuropathy, vertigo, paresthesias, psychotic depression, drowsiness, fatigue, restlessness. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea, constipation, pancreatitis, hepatitis, intrahepatic cholestatic jaundice. |
| **Dermatologic/Allergic** | Toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), photosensitivity, urticaria, pruritus, skin rashes. |
| **Hematologic** | Aplastic anemia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia. |
| **Metabolic** | Hypokalemia, hyponatremia, hyperuricemia (may precipitate gout), hyperglycemia, glycosuria, hypomagnesemia, hypercalcemia. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Loop Diuretics (e.g., Furosemide):** Co-administration can cause profound diuresis and electrolyte loss [US].
- **Lithium:** Diuretics reduce renal clearance of lithium, significantly increasing the risk of lithium toxicity [US].
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** Hypokalemia induced by metolazone increases the risk of digitalis-related arrhythmias [US].
- **Antihypertensives:** Potentiation of effect; dosage adjustment of concurrent agents may be necessary [US].
- **NSAIDs (e.g., Salicylates):** May decrease the antihypertensive efficacy of metolazone [US].
- **Curariform Drugs:** Diuretic-induced hypokalemia may enhance neuromuscular blockade (e.g., tubocurarine), potentially leading to respiratory depression. Discontinuation 3 days prior to surgery may be advisable [US].
- **Alcohol, Barbiturates, and Narcotics:** May potentiate orthostatic hypotension [US].
- **Corticosteroids/ACTH:** Increased risk of hypokalemia and salt/water retention [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category B.** Reproduction studies in animals showed no fetal harm. 
    - Metolazone crosses the placental barrier. 
    - Use only if clearly needed. Routine use for edema in healthy pregnant women is inappropriate and carries risks like fetal/neonatal jaundice and thrombocytopenia [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Metolazone appears in breast milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the drug's importance to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed reduced pregnancy rates and birth weights when males were treated with high doses (50 mg/kg) prior to mating [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Pediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in controlled trials. Limited experience suggests doses of 0.05 to 0.1 mg/kg once daily may provide diuresis, but response is inconsistent and prolonged use is not recommended [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of seniors. Dosing should be cautious, generally starting at the low end of the range due to higher frequencies of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Most of the drug is excreted renally; accumulation may occur. Use with caution in severe renal impairment. Metolazone remains effective at GFR <20 mL/min [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Risk of hypokalemia is increased; use with caution in severe liver disease [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A quinazoline diuretic that inhibits sodium reabsorption at the cortical diluting site and, to a lesser extent, the proximal convoluted tubule. This leads to increased excretion of sodium, chloride, water, potassium, magnesium, and phosphate [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak blood levels occur approximately 8 hours post-dose [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Only a small fraction is metabolized [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted unchanged in the urine [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of diuresis occurs within 1 hour and typically lasts for 24 hours or more [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Specific pivotal trial data summaries were not provided in the source PI. Clinical use is based on established diuretic and antihypertensive efficacy [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Protection:** Protect from light [US].
- **Safety:** Keep out of reach of children [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
