# Editorial Product Information: Zuclopenthixol

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial information is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of zuclopenthixol for healthcare professionals, synthesizing information across various pharmaceutical forms (hydrochloride, acetate, and decanoate).
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines. Prescribers should consult the current official PI in their specific jurisdiction before initiating treatment.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Zuclopenthixol (as hydrochloride, acetate, or decanoate).
- **Chemical Class:** Thioxanthene derivative; neuroleptic.
- **Brand Names:** **Clopixol** (Tablets), **Clopixol Acuphase** (Acuphase Injection), **Clopixol Depot** (Depot Injection).
- **ATC Code:** N05AF05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated Tablets:** 10 mg (as hydrochloride) [Australian].
    - **Acuphase Injection (Oily):** 50 mg/mL and 100 mg/2mL (as acetate) [Australian].
    - **Depot Injection (Oily):** 200 mg/mL (as decanoate) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral (Tablets); Intramuscular injection (Acuphase and Depot).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lundbeck Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Oral Tablets:**
    - Treatment of acute and chronic schizophrenia and other psychoses, particularly those presenting with hallucinations, delusions, thought disturbances, agitation, and aggressiveness.
    - Treatment of the manic phase of manic-depressive illness.
- **Acuphase Injection:**
    - Initial treatment of acute psychoses, mania, and exacerbations of chronic psychoses.
- **Depot Injection:**
    - Maintenance treatment of schizophrenia and other psychoses [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Dosage must be individualized according to the patient’s condition and response.

### Dosing by Formulation
- **Oral Tablets:**
    - **Acute Psychosis/Mania:** Initially 10–50 mg/day. Moderate to severe cases may start at 20 mg/day, increasing by 10–20 mg every 2–3 days as needed (max usually ≥75 mg/day).
    - **Chronic Psychosis:** Maintenance dose is typically 20–40 mg/day.
- **Acuphase Injection:**
    - **Dose:** 50–150 mg (1–3 mL) i.m., repeated if necessary every 2–3 days.
    - **Duration:** Not for long-term use; maximum duration of 2 weeks.
    - **Maximums:** Total of 4 injections or 400 mg per course.
- **Depot Injection:**
    - **Maintenance:** 200–400 mg (1–2 mL) i.m. every 2 to 4 weeks.

### Conversion and Titration
- **Oral to Depot:** Use the formula: (Oral daily dose in mg) × 8 = Depot i.m. dose in mg every two weeks. Oral medication should be tapered off during the first week after the first depot injection.
- **Acuphase to Oral/Depot:** Concomitantly with the last Acuphase injection, begin maintenance with oral (approx. 40 mg daily for a 100 mg Acuphase dose) or Depot (200–400 mg i.m. repeated every 2 weeks).

### Administration Instructions
- **Tablets:** May be taken with or without food; food increases bioavailability by ~20% but is not clinically significant.
- **Injections:** Intramuscular only. Acuphase and Depot (coconut oil-based) should not be mixed with sesame oil-based preparations.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to thioxanthenes (potential cross-sensitivity with phenothiazines).
- Acute intoxication (alcohol, barbiturates, or opiates).
- Circulatory collapse.
- Depressed level of consciousness (any cause), coma, or subcortical brain damage.
- Blood dyscrasias, leukopenia, or history of agranulocytosis.
- Phaeochromocytoma [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Potentially fatal; characterized by muscle rigidity, hyperthermia, and autonomic instability. Discontinue treatment immediately.
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** May cause QT prolongation and increases the risk of malignant arrhythmias. Use with caution in patients with bradycardia (<50 bpm), recent MI, or uncompensated heart failure.
- **Cerebrovascular Risk:** A 3-fold increase in stroke risk has been observed in elderly patients with dementia using certain antipsychotics. Zuclopenthixol is not approved for dementia-related behavioral disturbances.
- **Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS):** Common in early treatment; may require dose reduction or antiparkinsonian medication (not for routine prophylaxis).
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** Risk of irreversible, involuntary movements, particularly in elderly females on long-term therapy.
- **Seizure Threshold:** Use caution in patients with a history of convulsions.
- **Hematological:** Routine blood counts are advised during the first months of therapy due to reports of blood dyscrasias.
- **Venous Thromboembolism (VTE):** Identify risk factors before and during treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Dry mouth, somnolence, akathisia, hyperkinesia, hypokinesia.
- **Common (1–10%):** Tachycardia, palpitations, vertigo, accommodation disorder, constipation, vomiting, dyspepsia, fatigue, myalgia, tremor, dystonia, dizziness, headache, insomnia, depression, anxiety, pruritus.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Abdominal pain, nausea, injection site reaction, liver function test abnormalities, muscle rigidity, tardive dyskinesia, syncope, ataxia, erectile dysfunction.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis, QT prolongation, hyperprolactinaemia, hyperglycaemia, gynaecomastia, galactorrhoea, priapism.
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** Cholestatic hepatitis, jaundice, NMS, VTE.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic:**
    - **CNS Depressants:** Enhances effects of alcohol, barbiturates, and other sedatives.
    - **Antihypertensives:** May block the effects of guanethidine or similar compounds.
    - **Lithium:** Increases the risk of neurotoxicity.
    - **QT-prolonging Drugs:** Avoid concomitant use with Class Ia/III antiarrhythmics (quinidine, sotalol), certain macrolides, antihistamines, and quinolones.
    - **Dopamine Agonists:** May reduce the effects of levodopa.
- **Pharmacokinetic:**
    - **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** May decrease clearance of zuclopenthixol.
    - **TCAs:** Mutually inhibit metabolism with neuroleptics.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. Safety is not established. Use only if the benefit outweighs the risk. Neonates exposed in the third trimester are at risk for EPS and withdrawal symptoms (agitation, tremor, respiratory distress).
- **Breastfeeding:** Zuclopenthixol is excreted in breast milk (milk/serum ratio ~0.3). Breastfeeding is not recommended.
- **Fertility:** May cause hyperprolactinaemia, potentially impacting sexual function and fertility in both sexes.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and efficacy not established.
- **Elderly:** Dose reduction may be required. Increased risk of stroke and mortality in patients with dementia.
- **Renal Impairment:** In renal failure, reduce dose to half the usual dose and monitor closely [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in mild-to-moderate disease. In compromised hepatic function, give half the recommended dose and monitor serum levels.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent dopamine D1 and D2 receptor antagonist. It has a high affinity for both receptors, which mediates its antipsychotic and specific sedative effects.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Oral tablets ~49%.
    - **Distribution:** High protein binding (~98%); crosses the blood-brain and placental barriers.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via sulphoxidation, side chain N-dealkylation, and glucuronic acid conjugation; metabolites are inactive.
    - **Elimination:** Mainly via feces; ~10% via urine (only 0.1% unchanged).
    - **Half-life:** Oral tablets (~20 hours); Acuphase (~2–3 days); Depot (~19 days).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Minimum effective serum levels for clinical control are identified as:
    - **Acute Mania:** 5 ng/mL (12.5 nmol/L).
    - **Moderate Psychosis:** 3–4 ng/mL (7.5–10 nmol/L).
    - **Severe Psychosis:** 6–8 ng/mL (15–20 nmol/L) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light.
- **Injections:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light (keep in carton until use).
