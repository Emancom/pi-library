# Editorial Product Information: Zomacton (somatropin)

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **Zomacton** (somatropin) for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Somatropin (recombinant human growth hormone [rhGH]). It is a 191 amino acid polypeptide produced via recombinant DNA technology in *Escherichia coli*.
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Zomacton** [United States].
*   **ATC Code:** H01AC01.
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   **5 mg** lyophilized powder in a single-patient-use vial [United States].
    *   **10 mg** lyophilized powder in a single-patient-use vial [United States].
*   **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ferring Pharmaceuticals Inc. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
### Pediatric Indications [United States]:
*   **Growth Hormone Deficiency (GHD):** Treatment of growth failure due to inadequate endogenous GH secretion.
*   **Turner Syndrome:** Short stature associated with Turner syndrome.
*   **Idiopathic Short Stature (ISS):** Defined by a height standard deviation score (HSDS) $\leq$ -2.25 and growth rates unlikely to reach adult height in the normal range.
*   **SHOX Deficiency:** Short stature or growth failure associated with short stature homeobox-containing gene deficiency.
*   **Small for Gestational Age (SGA):** Children born SGA with no catch-up growth by age 2 to 4 years.

### Adult Indications [United States]:
*   **Growth Hormone Deficiency:** Replacement of endogenous GH in adults with GHD (either adult-onset or childhood-onset).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
*   Administer via subcutaneous injection to the abdomen, thigh, buttock, or back of the upper arm. 
*   **Injection Site Rotation:** Sites must be rotated to prevent the development of lipoatrophy [United States].
*   **Supervision:** Therapy should be supervised by a physician experienced in managing growth disorders [United States].

### Pediatric Dosing [United States]
Weekly dosages should be divided into equal doses administered 3, 6, or 7 days per week.
*   **GHD:** 0.18 mg/kg/week to 0.3 mg/kg/week.
*   **Turner Syndrome:** Up to 0.375 mg/kg/week.
*   **Idiopathic Short Stature:** Up to 0.37 mg/kg/week.
*   **SHOX Deficiency:** 0.35 mg/kg/week.
*   **SGA:** Up to 0.47 mg/kg/week. (Higher doses may be considered in very short patients [HSDS < -3]).

### Adult Dosing [United States]
Either a non-weight-based or weight-based regimen may be used:
*   **Non-weight-based:** Start at ~0.2 mg/day (range 0.15–0.3 mg/day). Increase by 0.1–0.2 mg/day every 1–2 months based on clinical response and serum IGF-1 levels.
*   **Weight-based:** Initial dose of 0.006 mg/kg daily. May increase to a maximum of 0.0125 mg/kg daily. (Not recommended for obese patients due to increased side effect risk).

### Reconstitution Instructions [United States]
*   **5 mg vial:** Reconstitute with 1–5 mL of Bacteriostatic Sodium Chloride (preserved with benzyl alcohol). For neonates or pregnant/lactating women, use 0.9% Sodium Chloride (preservative-free) and use only one dose per vial.
*   **10 mg vial:** Reconstitute with 1 mL of Bacteriostatic Water for Injection (preserved with metacresol). 
*   **Technique:** Aim diluent against the vial wall and swirl gently. **Do not shake.**

## 4. Contraindications [United States]
*   **Acute Critical Illness:** Patients with complications following open-heart or abdominal surgery, multiple accidental trauma, or acute respiratory failure.
*   **Prader-Willi Syndrome (Pediatric):** Specifically those who are severely obese, have a history of upper airway obstruction or sleep apnea, or severe respiratory impairment (risk of sudden death).
*   **Active Malignancy:** Therapy must be discontinued if evidence of active malignancy exists.
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to somatropin or any excipients (including benzyl alcohol in the 5 mg diluent and metacresol in the 10 mg diluent).
*   **Diabetic Retinopathy:** Active proliferative or severe non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy.
*   **Closed Epiphyses:** Pediatric patients in whom epiphyseal fusion has occurred.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Increased Mortality in Acute Critical Illness:** Significant increase in mortality reported in patients receiving pharmacologic doses of somatropin [United States].
*   **Sudden Death in Prader-Willi Syndrome:** Reported in pediatric patients with risk factors including obesity and respiratory infection. Note: **Zomacton** is not indicated for Prader-Willi syndrome [United States].
*   **Neoplasms:** Increased risk of second neoplasms (especially meningiomas) in childhood cancer survivors treated with radiation. Monitor for skin changes in all patients [United States].
*   **Glucose Intolerance/Diabetes:** Somatropin may decrease insulin sensitivity. Regular glucose monitoring is required, especially in patients with pre-existing diabetes or risk factors [United States].
*   **Intracranial Hypertension (IH):** Usually occurs within 8 weeks of initiation. Fundoscopic examination is required before and during treatment [United States].
*   **Fluid Retention:** Common in adults; may manifest as edema, arthralgia, or carpal tunnel syndrome. Usually dose-dependent and transient [United States].
*   **Hypoadrenalism:** Somatropin may inhibit the conversion of cortisone to cortisol. Patients may require initiation or increased doses of glucocorticoid replacement [United States].
*   **Hypothyroidism:** Growth response may be attenuated if hypothyroidism is untreated. Periodic thyroid function tests are necessary [United States].
*   **Slipped Capital Femoral Epiphysis (SCFE):** More frequent in patients experiencing rapid growth. Evaluate any child with a new onset of a limp or hip/knee pain [United States].
*   **Pancreatitis:** Should be considered in any patient developing persistent, severe abdominal pain [United States].
*   **Benzyl Alcohol Toxicity:** The 5 mg diluent contains benzyl alcohol, which is associated with "gasping syndrome" in neonates [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects ($\geq$ 5%)
| Pediatric (GHD) | Adult (Adult-Onset GHD) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Upper respiratory infection (32%) | Edema (21%) |
| Fever (16%) | Arthralgia (17%) |
| Pharyngitis (12%) | Paresthesia (17%) |
| Headache (11%) | Myalgia (14%) |
| Otitis media (10%) | Pain (14%) |

*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** Sudden death (Prader-Willi), intracranial hypertension, severe hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis), slipped capital femoral epiphysis, and pancreatitis [United States].
*   **Immunogenicity:** Specific antibodies to somatropin may develop; growth attenuation is rare unless binding capacity is high (>1.5 mg/L) [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Glucocorticoids:** Somatropin inhibits the enzyme 11$\beta$HSD-1, reducing the conversion of cortisone to active cortisol. Glucocorticoid replacement doses may need increasing [United States].
*   **Cytochrome P450 Metabolized Drugs:** Somatropin may increase the clearance of drugs metabolised by CP450 enzymes (e.g., corticosteroids, sex steroids, anticonvulsants) [United States].
*   **Oral Estrogen:** May reduce the IGF-1 response to somatropin; higher somatropin doses may be required in women on oral estrogen replacement [United States].
*   **Insulin/Hypoglycaemics:** Dose adjustments of anti-diabetic medications may be required due to somatropin’s effect on insulin sensitivity [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [United States]: No letter category assigned under current labelling rules. Available data do not report an association with major birth defects or miscarriage. Somatropin is not thought to cross the placenta. Caution: The 5 mg diluent contains benzyl alcohol; 0.9% NaCl should be used if treatment is necessary during pregnancy.
*   **Breastfeeding:** [United States]: No information on the presence of somatropin in human milk. Exogenous somatropin does not appear to increase normal breastmilk GH concentrations. Use with caution; use preservative-free diluent for the 5 mg vial if required.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Pediatric Use:** Approved for various growth failure conditions (see Indications). Safety and efficacy not established in patients with Prader-Willi syndrome [United States].
*   **Geriatric Use:** Patients $>65$ years may be more sensitive to somatropin; lower starting doses and smaller increments are recommended [United States].
*   **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific studies have been performed with **Zomacton** in these populations [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Binds to dimeric GH receptors, leading to intracellular signal transduction and induction of IGF-1. This promotes skeletal growth, increases linear growth rate, and mediates metabolic effects including protein synthesis and lipolysis [United States].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    *   **Bioavailability:** ~70% (Subcutaneous) [United States].
    *   **Peak Plasma Time ($T_{max}$):** ~4.5 hours [United States].
    *   **Metabolism:** Classical protein catabolism in both liver and kidneys [United States].
    *   **Elimination Half-life:** ~2.3 hours (Subcutaneous); 0.42 hours (Intravenous) [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Pediatric GHD:** A 2-year open-label study in 164 patients showed significant cumulative increases in height velocity (6.6 cm/yr at 24 weeks; 5.7 cm/yr at 12 months in treatment-naïve patients) [United States].
*   **Adult GHD:** Trials demonstrated an increase in lean body mass and a decrease in body fat mass. HDL cholesterol levels also normalised over 18 months of replacement therapy [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened Vials:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) in the original carton to protect from light [United States].
*   **Post-reconstitution:** 
    *   **5 mg vial:** Refrigerate (2°C to 8°C) for up to 14 days [United States].
    *   **10 mg vial:** Refrigerate (2°C to 8°C) for up to 28 days [United States].
*   **Do not freeze.**
