# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI when prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
  - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg [US].
  - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or infusion [US].
  - Loading Dose: Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
  - Frequency: Administered every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) [US].
  - Dose Range: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
  - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
  - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
  - Dose Adjustments: Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None [US].

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral vs. IV Administration:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating parenteral therapy [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].

- Transient nausea and vomiting [US].
- Body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. INR levels should be monitored at the initiation of treatment or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **US Category:** Not specified [US]. 
  - Animal data: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate human studies exist. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows suggest carnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Risks to the child must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 for weight-based dosing. In premature infants and newborns, secondary deficiency is defined as plasma concentrations below age-related normal levels [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing for ESRD patients on dialysis is established (see Section 3). High-dose oral therapy should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US]. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following intravenous bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg levocarnitine administered three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levels from approximately 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
