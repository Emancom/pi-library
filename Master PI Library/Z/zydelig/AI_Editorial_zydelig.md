# Editorial Product Information: Zydelig (idelalisib)

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictional Sources:** Australian (TGA) Product Information and United States (FDA) Prescribing Information.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the clinical and regulatory information for **Zydelig** (idelalisib) as provided by the contributing authorities. It is designed for use by healthcare professionals as a comprehensive clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information/Prescribing Information in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to their local regulatory documents for final prescribing guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Idelalisib.
- **Chemical Class:** Phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase p110δ (PI3Kδ) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Zydelig**.
- **ATC Code:** L01XX47.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **100 mg tablets:** Oval, film-coated, orange. Debossed with ‘GSI’ on one side and ‘100’ on the other.
    - **150 mg tablets:** Oval, film-coated, pink. Debossed with ‘GSI’ on one side and ‘150’ on the other.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Gilead Sciences (Australian and US).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL):** In combination with rituximab for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed CLL [Australian and US]. 
    - [US-specific]: Indicated for patients for whom rituximab alone would be considered appropriate therapy due to other co-morbidities.
    - [Australian-specific]: Indicated for patients for whom chemo-immunotherapy is not considered suitable.
- **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL) / Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma (SLL):** In combination with ofatumumab for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed CLL/SLL for whom chemo-immunotherapy is not considered suitable [Australian-approved; not US-approved]. 
- **Follicular Lymphoma (FL):** Monotherapy for the treatment of patients with follicular lymphoma refractory to at least two prior systemic therapies, including rituximab and an alkylating agent [Australian-approved; US accelerated approval removed 02/2022].
- **Limitations of Use:** **Zydelig** is not indicated or recommended for first-line treatment of any patient [US and Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** 150 mg orally, twice daily. 
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets should be swallowed whole. 
    - May be taken with or without food.
    - Treatment should continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
- **Missed Dose Instructions:**
    - If missed by <6 hours: Take the dose as soon as possible and return to the normal schedule.
    - If missed by >6 hours: Skip the dose and take the next dose at the usual time.
- **Dose Modifications:** Treatment should be interrupted for Grade 3 or 4 toxicities (e.g., hepatotoxicity, severe diarrhoea/colitis, pneumonitis, or neutropenia). Upon resolution, treatment may be resumed at a reduced dose of 100 mg twice daily. Permanent discontinuation is required for recurrent Grade 3+ toxicities, intestinal perforation, or life-threatening reactions.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to idelalisib or any excipient.
- [US-specific]: History of toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) with any drug.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US] / Serious Safety Notice [Australian]:** **Zydelig** is associated with fatal and/or serious toxicities, including hepatotoxicity, severe diarrhoea/colitis, pneumonitis, serious infections, and intestinal perforation. 
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Fatal and serious elevations in ALT/AST (>5x ULN) have occurred (16-18% of patients). Monitor ALT, AST, and bilirubin every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then monthly [Australian] or as clinically indicated [US]. Avoid concurrent use of other hepatotoxic drugs.
- **Diarrhoea/Colitis:** Severe diarrhoea (Grade 3+) occurs in approximately 18–20% of patients. Median time to onset is 1.9 months. It typically responds poorly to antimotility agents but may resolve with drug interruption and/or corticosteroids.
- **Pneumonitis:** Fatal cases have occurred. Monitor for new or worsening pulmonary symptoms (cough, dyspnea, hypoxia). Discontinue if symptomatic pneumonitis is diagnosed.
- **Serious Infections:** Increased risk of opportunistic infections, particularly *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia (PJP) and Cytomegalovirus (CMV).
    - **PJP Prophylaxis:** Required for all patients during treatment and for 2–6 months [Australian] or until 1 month [US] after discontinuation.
    - **CMV Monitoring:** Assess CMV status before treatment. Monthly clinical and laboratory (PCR) monitoring is recommended for patients with positive serology or history of infection. 
- **Intestinal Perforation:** Discontinue permanently if perforation is suspected or confirmed.
- **Severe Cutaneous Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), TEN, and DRESS have been reported. Discontinue permanently if these occur.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Diarrhoea/colitis, pneumonia, pyrexia, fatigue, rash, nausea, cough, abdominal pain, and vomiting.
- **Common (1–10%):** Sepsis, bronchitis, sinusitis, urinary tract infection, arthralgia, and insomnia.
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:**
    - **Hematologic:** Neutropenia (very common, Grade 3/4 in 42-58%), leukopenia, lymphocytopenia, and thrombocytopenia.
    - **Chemistry:** ALT/AST elevations (very common), hypertriglyceridemia, and hypoalbuminemia.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Pneumonia (23–30%), sepsis, and febrile neutropenia are among the most frequent serious events leading to death or discontinuation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Strong CYP3A Inducers:** Co-administration (e.g., rifampin, phenytoin, St. John’s Wort) reduces idelalisib AUC by ~75%. Avoid concurrent use.
    - **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors:** Co-administration (e.g., ketoconazole) increases idelalisib AUC by ~79-80%. Use alternative agents or monitor more frequently for toxicity.
    - **Effect of Idelalisib on other drugs:** Idelalisib and its primary metabolite (GS-563117) are strong CYP3A inhibitors. Co-administration with sensitive CYP3A substrates (e.g., midazolam) can increase substrate AUC by >5-fold. Avoid sensitive or narrow-therapeutic index CYP3A substrates (e.g., alfentanil, cyclosporine, ergotamine).
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Use caution when combining with other drugs known to cause hepatotoxicity or diarrhoea.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [Australian Category D]: Based on animal data, idelalisib may cause fetal harm and teratogenicity (e.g., hydrocephaly, vertebral agenesis).
    - [US-specific]: Advise females of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during treatment and for 1 month after the last dose. Advise males with female partners of reproductive potential to use contraception for 3 months after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been established. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 1 month after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential for hypospermatogenesis and decreased fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment required. However, patients ≥65 years experience a higher incidence of serious adverse reactions and treatment discontinuation compared to younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is necessary for patients with mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment (CrCl ≥15 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - AUC increases by ~60–70% in moderate/severe impairment. 
    - [US-position]: No dose adjustment for baseline impairment, but monitor closely.
    - [Australian-position]: Caution and intensified monitoring are recommended for severe impairment due to limited data.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Idelalisib inhibits PI3Kδ kinase, which is hyperactive in B-cell malignancies. By blocking this pathway, it inhibits proliferation, induces apoptosis, and disrupts homing/retention of malignant B-cells within lymphoid tissues and bone marrow.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is 1–1.5 hours. High-fat meals increase AUC by 36–40%.
    - **Distribution:** >84% protein bound. 
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via aldehyde oxidase and to a lesser extent CYP3A and UGT1A4. Primary metabolite is GS-563117 (strong CYP3A inhibitor).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~8.2 hours. Primarily excreted in feces (78%) and urine (14–15%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CLL (Study 312-0116):** A randomised, double-blind study of Zydelig + rituximab vs. placebo + rituximab. The Zydelig arm showed a significant improvement in Progression-Free Survival (PFS) (19.4 vs 6.5 months; HR 0.15).
- **CLL (Study 312-0119) [Australian-listed]:** Zydelig + ofatumumab showed superior PFS over ofatumumab alone (16.3 vs 8.0 months; HR 0.27).
- **iNHL/FL (Study 101-09):** A single-arm study in refractory indolent NHL/FL. The overall response rate (ORR) was 54-57% with a median duration of response of ~14 months. Note that three large trials (312-0123, 313-0124, 313-0125) were terminated early due to excess deaths in Zydelig-containing arms.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C (86°F). 
- Dispense only in the original container.
- Keep the bottle tightly closed and out of reach of children.
