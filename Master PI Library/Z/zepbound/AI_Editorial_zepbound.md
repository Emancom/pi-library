# Editorial Product Information: Tirzepatide

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tirzepatide for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing authority.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tirzepatide (a glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide [GIP] receptor and glucagon-like peptide-1 [GLP-1] receptor agonist).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Zepbound** [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Injection (Solution): 2.5 mg, 5 mg, 7.5 mg, 10 mg, 12.5 mg, and 15 mg per 0.5 mL.
  - Available as single-dose pens, single-dose vials, multi-dose vials, and single-patient-use **KwikPens** [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Eli Lilly and Company [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Weight Management:** Indicated as an adjunct to a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity for chronic weight management in adults with:
  - Obesity (initial body mass index [BMI] ≥30 kg/m²), or
  - Overweight (initial BMI ≥27 kg/m²) in the presence of at least one weight-related comorbid condition (e.g., hypertension, dyslipidaemia, type 2 diabetes mellitus, obstructive sleep apnea, or cardiovascular disease) [United States].
- **Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA):** Treatment of moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity [United States].
- **Limitations of Use:** Coadministration with other tirzepatide-containing products or GLP-1 receptor agonists is not recommended [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Administered subcutaneously once weekly at any time of day, with or without meals [United States].
- **Dose Escalation Schedule:**
  - Initial dose: 2.5 mg weekly for 4 weeks. (Note: 2.5 mg is for initiation and is not a maintenance dose).
  - Increase by 2.5 mg increments after at least 4 weeks at the current dose until the desired maintenance dose is reached [United States].
- **Maintenance Dosage:**
  - **Weight Reduction:** 5 mg, 10 mg, or 15 mg weekly.
  - **Obstructive Sleep Apnea:** 10 mg or 15 mg weekly [United States].
- **Maximum Dosage:** 15 mg subcutaneously once weekly [United States].
- **Administration Instructions:**
  - Inject into the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm. Rotate injection sites with each dose.
  - Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration (solution should be clear and colorless to slightly yellow).
- **Missed Dose Instructions:**
  - Administer as soon as possible within 4 days (96 hours) of the missed dose.
  - If >4 days have passed, skip the missed dose and resume the next dose on the regularly scheduled day.
  - The interval between two doses must be at least 3 days (72 hours) [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- Personal or family history of Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma (MTC) [United States].
- Patients with Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2) [United States].
- Known serious hypersensitivity to tirzepatide or any of its excipients [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Risk of Thyroid C-Cell Tumors [United States]:** Tirzepatide causes dose-dependent and treatment-duration-dependent thyroid C-cell tumors in rats. It is unknown whether it causes MTC or other C-cell tumors in humans. Routine monitoring of serum calcitonin is of uncertain value.
- **Severe Gastrointestinal Disease:** Use is associated with gastrointestinal adverse reactions, sometimes severe. Not recommended in patients with severe gastroparesis [United States].
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** Has been observed in patients treated with tirzepatide. If suspected, discontinue immediately and do not restart if confirmed [United States].
- **Acute Kidney Injury:** Volume depletion due to gastrointestinal adverse reactions may lead to AKI. Monitor renal function in patients reporting severe GI effects [United States].
- **Acute Gallbladder Disease:** Increased risk of cholelithiasis and cholecystitis [United States].
- **Hypoglycemia:** Risk is increased when used concomitantly with insulin secretagogues (e.g., sulfonylureas) or insulin. Dose reduction of these agents may be necessary [United States].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis, angioedema) have been reported [United States].
- **Diabetic Retinopathy in T2DM:** Rapid improvement in glucose control may result in temporary worsening of diabetic retinopathy. Monitor patients with a history of this condition [United States].
- **Pulmonary Aspiration with Anesthesia:** Delayed gastric emptying may increase the risk of aspiration during surgery. Instruct patients to inform providers of tirzepatide use prior to elective procedures [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
Reported in weight management trials [United States]:
- Nausea (25–29%)
- Diarrhea (19–23%)
- Vomiting (8–13%)
- Constipation (11–17%)
- Abdominal pain (9–10%)
- Dyspepsia (9–10%)
- Fatigue (5–7%)
- Injection site reactions (6–8%)
- Hair loss (4–5%)

### Serious and Other Notable Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Severe GI distress, eructation, flatulence, GERD, and abdominal distension.
- **Cardiovascular:** Mean increase in heart rate (1–3 bpm).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness, dysesthesia.
- **Postmarketing:** Ileus, intestinal obstruction, and acute renal failure [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Delayed Gastric Emptying:** Tirzepatide slows gastric emptying, potentially impacting the absorption of oral medications. This is most pronounced after the first dose [United States].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Efficacy may be reduced. Advise patients using oral contraceptives to switch to a non-oral method or add a barrier method for 4 weeks after initiation and for 4 weeks after each dose escalation [United States].
- **Narrow Therapeutic Index Drugs:** Monitor patients closely for drugs where absorption rate/timing is critical (e.g., warfarin) [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: PLLR Risk Summary] Available data are insufficient to evaluate drug-related risks of major birth defects or miscarriage. Weight loss provides no benefit during pregnancy and may cause fetal harm. Discontinue when pregnancy is recognized [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Tirzepatide was present in low/undetectable levels in human milk in a single-dose study (0.02% of maternal dose). Consider the developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother’s clinical need for the drug [United States].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed effects on reproductive organs (e.g., prolonged diestrus, decreased corpora lutea), but these were considered secondary to weight loss and reduced food intake [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in patients <18 years of age [United States].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger adults [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required. Monitor renal function in those with severe GI distress [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A long-acting GIP receptor and GLP-1 receptor agonist. It selectively binds to and activates both receptors, enhancing insulin secretion, increasing insulin sensitivity, reducing glucagon levels, and slowing gastric emptying to decrease food intake [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Bioavailability approximately 80%. Peak plasma concentration (Tmax) occurs in 8 to 72 hours.
  - **Distribution:** Highly bound to plasma albumin (99%).
  - **Metabolism:** Metabolized via proteolytic cleavage of the peptide backbone and beta-oxidation of the fatty diacid.
  - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 5 days. Excreted via urine and feces as metabolites [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Weight Management (Studies 1 & 2):** 72-week randomized trials showing significant mean body weight reduction compared to placebo (up to -20.9% in Study 1).
- **Intensive Lifestyle Intervention (Study 3):** Patients who achieved ≥5% weight loss during a 12-week lead-in further reduced weight on tirzepatide (-18.4% vs +2.5% for placebo).
- **Randomized Withdrawal (Study 4):** Patients remaining on tirzepatide for 88 weeks maintained weight loss compared to those switched to placebo at week 36.
- **OSA (Studies 5 & 6):** 52-week trials in patients with moderate-to-severe OSA and obesity showed a significant reduction in the Apnea-Hypopnea Index (AHI) compared to placebo [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F).
- **Room Temperature Stability:**
  - **Single-dose pens/vials:** May be stored unrefrigerated up to 30°C (86°F) for up to 21 days.
  - **Multi-dose vials/KwikPens:** May be stored unrefrigerated up to 30°C (86°F) for up to 30 days.
- **Handling:** Protect from light; do not freeze [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx) [United States].
