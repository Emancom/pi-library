# Editorial Product Information: Zaleplon

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of zaleplon clinical data for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the provided regulatory information into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for current legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Zaleplon (a nonbenzodiazepine hypnotic of the pyrazolopyrimidine class)
- **Chemical Name:** N-[3-(3-cyanopyrazolo[1,5-a]pyrimidin-7-yl)phenyl]-N-ethylacetamide
- **ATC Code:** N05CF03 (Not explicitly in source, but standard classification)
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - [US]: Oral capsules in 5 mg and 10 mg strengths.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** PD-Rx Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (Repackager); Unichem Laboratories Ltd. (Manufacturer) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Short-term treatment of insomnia:** Specifically indicated for patients who have difficulty with sleep onset. Effectiveness has been demonstrated for up to 30 days in controlled clinical trials [US].
- **Note:** It has not been shown to increase total sleep time or reduce the frequency of nocturnal awakenings [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** 10 mg once daily, taken immediately before bedtime. 
- **Low-Weight Adults:** 5 mg may be sufficient for some patients [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** 20 mg once daily. Doses above 20 mg have not been adequately evaluated and are not recommended [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Take immediately before retiring or after the patient has gone to bed and experienced difficulty falling asleep.
    - Do not take with or immediately after a high-fat/heavy meal, as this delays absorption and reduces efficacy for sleep onset [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Only take if the patient has time for a full night of sleep (7 to 8 hours) before becoming active again.

## 4. Contraindications
- **History of Complex Sleep Behaviors:** Patients who have previously experienced complex sleep behaviors (e.g., sleep-walking, sleep-driving) after taking zaleplon [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to zaleplon or any inactive ingredients (including FD&C Yellow No. 5/tartrazine in 5 mg capsules) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]: Complex Sleep Behaviors.** Zaleplon can cause sleep-walking, sleep-driving, and other activities while not fully awake. These may result in serious injury or death. Discontinue treatment immediately if these occur.
- **CNS Depression and Next-Day Impairment:** Risk of impaired psychomotor function. This risk increases if taken with less than 7–8 hours of sleep remaining, if higher than recommended doses are used, or if co-administered with other CNS depressants [US].
- **Need to Evaluate for Comorbidities:** If insomnia fails to remit after 7–10 days of treatment, this may indicate a primary psychiatric or medical illness that requires evaluation [US].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Rare cases of angioedema involving the tongue, glottis, or larynx have been reported and can be fatal. Do not rechallenge if these occur [US].
- **Abnormal Thinking and Behavioral Changes:** May include decreased inhibition, aggressiveness, agitation, hallucinations, and depersonalization [US].
- **Depression/Suicidal Ideation:** Use with caution in patients with signs of depression; suicidal tendencies may be present and protective measures may be required [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally dose-related. The following frequencies were observed in long-term (28–35 night) US trials:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Placebo (%) | Zaleplon 5–10 mg (%) | Zaleplon 20 mg (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **General** | Headache | 35 | 30 | 42 |
| | Abdominal pain | 3 | 6 | 6 |
| | Asthenia | 5 | 5 | 7 |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness | 7 | 7 | 9 |
| | Somnolence | 4 | 5 | 6 |
| | Amnesia | 1 | 2 | 4 |
| | Paresthesia | 1 | 3 | 3 |
| **Digestive** | Nausea | 7 | 6 | 8 |
| **Urogenital** | Dysmenorrhea | 2 | 3 | 4 |

- **Other Frequent (>1/100):** Back pain, chest pain, fever, migraine, constipation, dry mouth, dyspepsia, arthralgia, anxiety, depression, bronchitis, pruritus, rash, conjunctivitis [US].
- **Postmarketing Reports:** Nightmares and severe anaphylactic reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects occur with ethanol, imipramine, and thioridazine. Dosage adjustments may be necessary when combined [US].
- **Cimetidine (Nonspecific Enzyme Inhibitor):** Increases zaleplon $C_{max}$ and AUC by 85%. An initial dose of 5 mg is recommended in patients taking cimetidine [US].
- **Rifampin (CYP3A4 Inducer):** Reduces zaleplon $C_{max}$ and AUC by approximately 80%, potentially rendering zaleplon ineffective [US].
- **Erythromycin (CYP3A4 Inhibitor):** Increases zaleplon $C_{max}$ by 34% and AUC by 20% [US].
- **Note:** No significant pharmacokinetic interactions were observed with warfarin, digoxin, ibuprofen, or paroxetine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [US]: Not recommended for use during pregnancy. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at high doses showed reduced offspring growth and increased stillbirths, though no clear teratogenicity was evidenced at doses up to 49 times the MRHD.
- **Breastfeeding:** Zaleplon is excreted in breast milk (peak concentration at ~1 hour post-dose). Because the effects on nursing infants are unknown, breastfeeding mothers should not take zaleplon [US].
- **Fertility:** In rat studies, high doses (100 mg/kg/day) were associated with decreased fertility in females [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Recommended dose is 5 mg. Elderly patients may be more sensitive to hypnotic effects and are at higher risk of falls. Doses above 10 mg are not recommended [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild-to-moderate: No dosage adjustment required.
    - Severe: Not adequately studied; use with caution [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - Mild-to-moderate: Dose should be reduced to 5 mg due to increased plasma concentrations.
    - Severe: Not recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A nonbenzodiazepine that interacts with the GABA-BZ receptor complex. It binds selectively to the brain omega-1 receptor (alpha-1 subunit of the $GABA_A$ receptor), which modulates the chloride ion channel [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics [US]:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; $T_{max}$ ~1 hour. Bioavailability is ~30% due to significant first-pass metabolism.
    - **Distribution:** Lipophilic; volume of distribution ~1.4 L/kg. Plasma protein binding is ~60%.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized; <1% excreted unchanged. Primary pathway: aldehyde oxidase (to 5-oxo-zaleplon). Secondary pathway: CYP3A4 (to desethylzaleplon). All metabolites are inactive.
    - **Elimination:** Rapid elimination with a half-life ($T_{1/2}$) of ~1 hour. Approximately 70% of a dose is recovered in urine within 48 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Sleep Onset:** In 12 placebo-controlled trials, zaleplon 10 mg and 20 mg were superior to placebo in reducing time to sleep onset (TSO) and latency to persistent sleep (LPS) [US].
- **Duration of Effect:** Effectiveness for sleep onset was maintained for up to 5 weeks [US].
- **Tolerance/Withdrawal:** No evidence of pharmacodynamic tolerance was observed over 4 weeks. Rebound insomnia may occur, primarily on the first night after discontinuation, especially with the 20 mg dose [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature, 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** Dispense in a light-resistant container as defined in the USP [US].
