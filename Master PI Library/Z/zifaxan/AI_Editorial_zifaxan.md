# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information (PI) for levocarnitine injection. It is intended as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This editorial summary is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. This document is based on a single source from the US; additional regional nuances (e.g., Australian or European data) are not included in the source provided.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP). It is a carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Alimentary tract and metabolism; amino acids and derivatives).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Titration:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40 to 50 µmol/L. Adjustments should be guided by trough concentrations; downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may occur as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### General Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If severe, discontinue treatment and consider risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Accumulation of Metabolites:** Safety of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in renal impairment has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>5%) [US ESRD Trials]:**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), abdominal pain (9%), fever (7%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%).
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).
    - **Neurologic:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Rare/Serious:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, seizures [US].
- **Transient Reactions:** Nausea, vomiting, body odor, and gastritis have been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not formally categorised by letter in provided source [US]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. In nursing mothers, the benefits to the mother should be weighed against the risks of excess carnitine intake for the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as specified in Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used in ESRD patients post-dialysis. Note the warning regarding metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) with oral formulations in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical trials (9 to 24 weeks) in patients on hemodialysis demonstrated that IV administration of 20 mg/kg three times weekly significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels remained stable or increased over 24 weeks. However, the impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative.
- **Stability:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL concentration) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local jurisdiction) [US].
