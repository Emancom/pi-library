# Editorial Product Information: Ethosuximide

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and US (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data from multiple regulatory sources.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, legally binding PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ethosuximide, an anticonvulsant of the succinimide class.
- **Brand Name:** **Zarontin**.
- **ATC Code:** N03AD01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Capsules:** 250 mg [Australian, US]. 
        - Physical appearance: Clear oval soft gelatin capsules [Australian]; Orange, 8mm capsules imprinted with "P;D;237" [US].
    - **Syrup/Oral Solution:** 250 mg/5 mL [Australian, US].
        - Physical appearance: Clear, slightly yellowish to pinkish liquid [Australian]; Raspberry-flavoured base [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - **Australian:** Clinect Pty Ltd.
    - **US:** Parke-Davis (Division of Pfizer Inc.).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Petit mal (Absence) epilepsy:** Indicated for the control of absence seizures [Australian, US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Titration:** Dosage should be individualised based on patient response and plasma levels. A common method is to increase the daily dose by 250 mg every four to seven days until seizure control is achieved with minimal adverse effects [Australian, US].
- **Therapeutic Range:** 40–100 mg/L (mcg/mL). Dosages exceeding 1.5 g daily require strict medical supervision [Australian, US].

### Dosing by Age/Weight
- **Adults and Children:** Initial daily dose of approximately 20–30 mg/kg administered in two divided doses [Australian].
- **Pediatric (Age-based):** 
    - **3 to 6 years:** Initial dose is 250 mg (one capsule or 5 mL) per day [US].
    - **6 years and older:** Initial dose is 500 mg per day [US].
- **Maintenance:** The optimal dose for most pediatric patients is approximately 20 mg/kg/day [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Combination Therapy:** Ethosuximide may be administered with other anticonvulsants if other seizure types coexist with absence epilepsy [Australian, US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to ethosuximide, other succinimides (e.g., methsuximide), or any excipients in the formulation [Australian, US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** As with all antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), ethosuximide must be withdrawn slowly to avoid precipitating petit mal status [Australian, US].
- **Hematopoietic Effects:** Serious and potentially fatal blood dyscrasias (e.g., aplastic anaemia, agranulocytosis) have been reported. Periodic blood counts are required. Patients should report symptoms of infection (fever, sore throat) immediately [Australian, US].
- **Drug-Induced Immune Thrombocytopenia (DITP):** Onset typically occurs 1–3 weeks after initiation. Zarontin should be discontinued if DITP is suspected [US].
- **Serious Dermatologic Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) have been reported and can be fatal. Discontinue medication at the first sign of a rash unless the rash is clearly not drug-related [Australian, US].
- **Suicidal Behavior and Ideation:** AEDs increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behaviour. Monitoring for depression, suicidal ideation, or unusual changes in mood is essential [Australian, US].
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):** Cases of SLE have been reported; physicians should remain alert to this possibility [Australian, US].
- **Hepatic and Renal Effects:** Ethosuximide may cause functional changes in the liver and kidneys. Use with extreme caution in patients with known hepatic or renal disease. Periodic liver function studies and urinalysis are advised [Australian, US].
- **Mixed Epilepsy:** When used alone in mixed types of epilepsy, ethosuximide may increase the frequency of grand mal (tonic-clonic) seizures in some patients [Australian, US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies were not explicitly categorised by percentage in the source documents but are reported by system.*

- **Gastrointestinal (Frequent):** Anorexia, vague gastric upset, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, cramps, diarrhoea, weight loss, gingival hypertrophy, and swelling of the tongue [Australian, US].
- **Nervous System:** Drowsiness, dizziness, headache, ataxia, somnolence, lethargy, fatigue, irritability, hyperactivity, and disturbances in attention [Australian, US].
- **Psychiatric:** Aggression, night terrors, sleep disturbances, inability to concentrate, increased libido, depression, and rare cases of psychosis or paranoid delusions [Australian, US].
- **Hematologic:** Leukopenia, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, aplastic anaemia, eosinophilia, and thrombocytopenia [Australian, US].
- **Dermatologic:** Pruritic erythematous rashes, urticaria, hirsutism, SJS, and DRESS [Australian, US].
- **Other:** Myopia, vaginal haemorrhage/bleeding, and microscopic haematuria [Australian, US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Valproic Acid:** Reported to both increase and decrease ethosuximide serum levels [Australian, US].
- **Phenytoin:** Ethosuximide may elevate phenytoin serum levels [Australian, US].
- **CNS Depressants:** Alcohol and other sedating medications may enhance the depressant effects of ethosuximide [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic serum level determinations of concurrent AEDs may be necessary [Australian, US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Drugs which have caused, are suspected to have caused or may be expected to cause, an increased incidence of human fetal malformations or irreversible damage. 
    - **US Recommendation:** Physicians are advised to recommend that pregnant patients enroll in the North American Antiepileptic Drug (NAAED) Pregnancy Registry.
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Ethosuximide crosses the placenta. There is an increased risk of birth defects in children born to women on AEDs. However, therapy should not be discontinued abruptly as status epilepticus poses a severe risk to both mother and fetus [Australian, US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Ethosuximide is excreted in human milk. Because the effects on the nursing infant are unknown, use in nursing mothers only if benefits clearly outweigh risks [Australian, US].
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children below age 3 have not been established [US]. Weight-based dosing (20–30 mg/kg) is used for children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with extreme caution. Periodic monitoring of organ function is advised [Australian, US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ethosuximide suppresses the paroxysmal three-cycle-per-second spike and wave activity associated with absence seizures. It reduces the frequency of attacks by depressing the motor cortex and elevating the CNS threshold to convulsive stimuli [Australian, US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Specifically targets petit mal (absence) patterns.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not detailed in provided sources beyond the mention of therapeutic plasma levels (40–100 mg/L) [Australian, US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Suicidal Ideation Meta-analysis:** A pooled analysis of 199 placebo-controlled trials of 11 different AEDs showed a relative risk of 1.8 (95% CI: 1.2, 2.7) for suicidal behaviour or ideation. The estimated incidence was 0.43% in AED-treated patients compared to 0.24% in the placebo group [Australian, US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Capsules:** Store below 30°C [Australian]; Store at 25°C with excursions permitted to 15–30°C [US]. Protect from moisture and keep container tightly closed [Australian].
- **Syrup/Oral Solution:** Store below 25°C [Australian]; Store at 20–25°C [US]. Preserve in tight containers; protect from freezing and light [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
