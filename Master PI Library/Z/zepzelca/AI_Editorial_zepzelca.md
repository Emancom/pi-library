# Editorial Product Information: ZEPZELCA (Lurbinectedin)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates information from official Product Information documents provided by the **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)** and the **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized, clinically accurate overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This is an editorial synthesis and should not be substituted for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Lurbinectedin, a synthetic alkylating agent.
- **Brand Name**: **ZEPZELCA** [Australian, US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Lyophilized powder for injection; 4 mg single-dose vial.
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: 
    - **Australian**: Specialised Therapeutics Pharma Pty Ltd.
    - **US**: Jazz Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (under license from Pharma Mar, S.A.).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Extensive-Stage Small Cell Lung Cancer (ES-SCLC) Maintenance**: Indicated in combination with atezolizumab (with or without hyaluronidase-tqjs [US]) for the maintenance treatment of adult patients with ES-SCLC whose disease has not progressed following first-line induction therapy with atezolizumab, carboplatin, and etoposide.
- **Metastatic Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC)**: Indicated for adult patients with metastatic SCLC whose disease has progressed on or after platinum-based chemotherapy. 
    - *Note*: This indication is approved under **provisional approval** [Australian] and **accelerated approval** [US] based on overall response rate and duration of response. Continued approval may be contingent upon verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing**: The recommended dose is **3.2 mg/m²** administered via intravenous infusion over 60 minutes every 21 days.
- **Treatment Duration**: Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
- **Administration Sequence**: When administered on the same day as atezolizumab, the atezolizumab component must be administered first.
- **Pre-treatment Requirements**: 
    - Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) must be $\ge$ 1.5 x 10⁹/L [Australian] (1,500 cells/mm³ [US]).
    - Platelet count must be $\ge$ 100 x 10⁹/L [Australian] (100,000/mm³ [US]).
- **Premedication**: Prophylactic antiemetics (dexamethasone 8 mg IV and a serotonin antagonist like ondansetron 8 mg IV) are recommended prior to infusion.
- **G-CSF Prophylaxis**: Administration of primary G-CSF prophylaxis is recommended (especially in combination therapy) to reduce the risk of febrile neutropenia.
- **Administration Site**: A central venous line is recommended to reduce the risk of extravasation-related tissue necrosis [US; recommended in Australian for limited venous access].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity**: Contraindicated in patients with a history of significant drug allergy to lurbinectedin or any of its excipients [Australian].
- **US labeling**: No contraindications listed [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression**: **ZEPZELCA** can cause severe, life-threatening, or fatal myelosuppression, including neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia. Fatal sepsis has been reported. Monitor complete blood counts (CBC) before each dose.
- **Hepatotoxicity**: Elevations in ALT and AST are common and may be severe. Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) prior to initiation and periodically during treatment.
- **Extravasation and Tissue Necrosis**: Extravasation can lead to skin and soft tissue injury, including necrosis requiring debridement. If extravasation is suspected, immediately discontinue the infusion and remove the catheter.
- **Rhabdomyolysis**: Reports of rhabdomyolysis have occurred. Monitor creatine phosphokinase (CPK) levels prior to initiation and as clinically indicated.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity**: Based on animal studies, lurbinectedin can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to treatment.

- **Very Common (>10% Grade 3-4)**: Decreased lymphocytes (43%), decreased neutrophils (46%), decreased leukocytes (29%), decreased hemoglobin (10%).
- **Common (1-10% Grade 3-4)**: Increased ALT (4%), increased glucose (5%), decreased sodium (7%), decreased platelets (7%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A Modulators**: Lurbinectedin is a substrate of CYP3A4.
    - **Strong/Moderate Inhibitors**: (e.g., itraconazole, ketoconazole, grapefruit, erythromycin, diltiazem) Increase lurbinectedin exposure. Avoid coadministration. If a moderate inhibitor is unavoidable, reduce **ZEPZELCA** dose by 50% [US].
    - **Strong/Moderate Inducers**: (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St. John’s wort, bosentan) Decrease lurbinectedin exposure and efficacy. Avoid coadministration.
- **Food Interactions**: Avoid grapefruit products and Seville oranges during treatment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**:
    - **Australian Category D**: Lurbinectedin is suspected to cause an increased incidence of human fetal malformations or irreversible damage.
    - **US Recommendation**: Advise of potential risk to the fetus.
    - **Contraception**: Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 7 months after the final dose. Males with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception for 4 months after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding**: Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in breastfed children, patients should not breastfeed during treatment and for 2 weeks after the final dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age.
- **Elderly Use**: No overall differences in effectiveness were observed in patients $\ge$ 65 years. However, a higher incidence of serious adverse reactions (49% vs 26%) was noted in older patients receiving monotherapy [Australian, US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**:
    - **Mild**: No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate**: Reduce dose to **1.6 mg/m²** [US]; Effect unknown, use with caution [Australian].
    - **Severe**: Avoid use [US]; Effect unknown [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose adjustment for mild to moderate impairment (CrCl 30-89 mL/min). Not studied in severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min) or end-stage renal disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Lurbinectedin binds guanine residues in the minor groove of DNA, forming adducts that bend the DNA toward the major groove. This triggers a cascade that interferes with DNA-binding proteins and transcription factors, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis.
- **Pharmacokinetics**: 
    - **Distribution**: Highly protein bound (99%) to albumin and alpha-1-acid glycoprotein. Vd at steady state is 504 L.
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily metabolized by CYP3A4 *in vitro*.
    - **Excretion**: Terminal half-life is approximately 51 hours. 89% recovered in feces and 6% in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **IMforte Study**: A randomized, open-label trial (n=483) evaluating lurbinectedin + atezolizumab vs. atezolizumab alone as maintenance for ES-SCLC. The combination arm showed a median Overall Survival (OS) of 13.2 months vs. 10.6 months for the control (HR 0.73) and a median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) of 5.4 months vs. 2.1 months (HR 0.54).
- **Study B-005**: A multicenter, single-arm trial (n=105) in patients with SCLC who progressed after platinum chemotherapy. The investigator-assessed Overall Response Rate (ORR) was 35%, with a median Duration of Response (DOR) of 5.3 months.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials**: Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. 
- **Shelf Life**: 60 months for unopened vials [Australian].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution**: Stable for up to 24 hours (including infusion time) at room temperature/ambient light or refrigerated (2°C to 8°C).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification**: Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4).
- **US Classification**: Rx-only; Hazardous drug (requires special handling and disposal).
