# Editorial Product Information: ZENPEP (pancrelipase)

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA). 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of **ZENPEP** based on available regional Product Information (PI). It is designed as a concise, high-level reference for healthcare professionals.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis. Prescribers must consult the official PI approved in their specific jurisdiction (e.g., US FDA, Australian TGA) before clinical application. At the time of this editorial, only the United States PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pancrelipase (lipase, protease, and amylase).
- **Chemical Class:** Pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy (PERT) of porcine origin.
- **Brand Names:** **ZENPEP** [US].
- **ATC Code:** A09AA02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Delayed-release capsules containing enteric-coated pellets [US]:
    - 3,000 units lipase; 10,000 units protease; 14,000 units amylase.
    - 5,000 units lipase; 17,000 units protease; 24,000 units amylase.
    - 10,000 units lipase; 32,000 units protease; 42,000 units amylase.
    - 15,000 units lipase; 47,000 units protease; 63,000 units amylase.
    - 20,000 units lipase; 63,000 units protease; 84,000 units amylase.
    - 25,000 units lipase; 79,000 units protease; 105,000 units amylase.
    - 40,000 units lipase; 126,000 units protease; 168,000 units amylase.
    - 60,000 units lipase; 189,600 units protease; 252,600 units amylase.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aimmune Therapeutics, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Exocrine Pancreatic Insufficiency (EPI):** Treatment of EPI due to cystic fibrosis or other conditions in both adult and pediatric patients [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- Dosing is based on lipase units and must be individualised based on clinical symptoms (steatorrhea), body weight, and dietary fat content.
- **Maximum Dose:** Do not exceed 2,500 lipase units/kg/meal, 10,000 lipase units/kg/day, or 4,000 lipase units/g of fat ingested per day in patients >12 months of age [US].
- Total daily dosage should reflect three meals plus two or three snacks. Snacks should receive approximately half the mealtime dose.

### Dosing by Age Group [US]
- **Birth to 12 Months:** 3,000 lipase units (one capsule) per 120 mL of formula or per breastfeeding session.
- **12 Months to <4 Years:** Start with 1,000 lipase units/kg/meal.
- **4 Years to Adult:** Start with 500 lipase units/kg/meal.

### Administration Instructions
- **Capsules:** Must be swallowed whole. 
- **Sprinkling Option:** For patients unable to swallow whole, capsules may be opened and contents sprinkled on a small amount of soft, acidic food (e.g., applesauce, bananas, pears) with a pH of 4.5 or less.
- **Cautions:** Do NOT crush or chew the capsule or contents. This preserves the enteric coating and prevents oral mucosal irritation or enzyme inactivation.
- **Hydration:** Ensure adequate fluid intake during administration.
- **Infant Administration:** Contents can be sprinkled into the mouth or on acidic food. Follow immediately with formula or breast milk to ensure complete swallowing. Do not mix directly into a bottle of milk.

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, take the next dose with the next meal or snack. Do not double the dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Fibrosing Colonopathy:** A rare but serious condition (colonic stricture) associated with high-dose PERT, primarily reported in pediatric patients with cystic fibrosis under age 12. Risk increases with doses exceeding 6,000 lipase units/kg/meal [US].
- **Irritation of Oral Mucosa:** Potential for irritation if capsules are chewed or if pellets are retained in the mouth.
- **Hyperuricemia:** Pancreatic enzymes contain purines; high doses may increase blood uric acid levels. Use caution in patients with gout or renal impairment.
- **Risk of Viral Transmission:** As a porcine-derived product, the risk of transmitting infectious agents (porcine viruses) cannot be absolutely excluded, although no cases have been reported [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Monitor patients with known porcine protein allergies for symptoms of anaphylaxis, asthma, or hives.

| Headache | 15% | 0% |

### Other Reported Reactions
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Contusion, cough, early satiety, abdominal pain, and steatorrhea (noted in younger pediatric studies) [US].
- **Postmarketing Reports:** Blurred vision, distal intestinal obstruction syndrome (DIOS), flatulence, constipation, nausea, liver enzyme elevations, myalgia, muscle spasm, and various skin rashes (urticaria) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Lipases, proteases, and amylases are not substrates for CYP enzymes or transporters; therefore, mediated drug interactions are not expected [US].
- **Management:** No specific drug-drug interactions are highlighted in provided sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labeling rules]. Pancrelipase is minimally absorbed systemically; therefore, maternal use is not expected to result in fetal exposure. Published case reports have not identified a risk of major birth defects or miscarriage [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Pancrelipase is not expected to be excreted in human milk in clinically relevant amounts due to minimal maternal absorption. Use should consider the benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother’s clinical need for **ZENPEP**.
- **Fertility:** Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safe and effective. Specific dosing exists for infants birth to 12 months. High risk for fibrosing colonopathy in children <12 years on high-dose therapy [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not identify differences in responses between geriatric and younger patients.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically addressed; however, hyperuricemia monitoring is recommended for those with renal impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **ZENPEP** contains enzymes that catalyze the hydrolysis of fats (to monoglycerides, glycerol, and fatty acids), proteins (to peptides and amino acids), and starches (to dextrins and short-chain sugars).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Improves the coefficient of fat absorption (CFA) in patients with EPI [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Enzymes act locally in the gastrointestinal tract (duodenum and proximal small intestine) and are not absorbed in appreciable amounts. Pellets are formulated to release enzymes at a pH of 5.5 or greater.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1 (Aged 7–23 years):** A randomized, double-blind, crossover study (n=32) in CF patients with EPI. ZENPEP treatment resulted in a mean CFA of 88% compared to 63% for placebo (mean difference of 26 percentage points; p≤0.001) [US].
- **Study 2 (Aged 1–6 years):** An open-label, uncontrolled study (n=19). Results showed that switching from other PERT products to ZENPEP at similar doses maintained control of fat malabsorption, as measured by spot fecal fat testing [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at room temperature, 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Brief excursions up to 40°C (104°F) are permitted for 24 hours [US].
- **Moisture Protection:** Keep the bottle tightly closed and store in a dry place. Keep the desiccant in the bottle.
- **Repackaging:** If dispensed in an HDPE container, store at up to 30°C (86°F) for up to 6 months [US].
