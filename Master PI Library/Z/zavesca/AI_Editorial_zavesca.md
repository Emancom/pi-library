# Editorial Product Information: Zavesca (Miglustat)

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and US (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information (PI) for **Zavesca**, designed as a reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a clinical synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Miglustat (a synthetic analogue of D-glucose; N-alkylated imino sugar).
- **Chemical Class:** Glucosylceramide synthase inhibitor (substrate reduction therapy).
- **Brand Name:** **Zavesca**.
- **ATC Code:** A16AX06.
- **Dosage Form:** Hard capsules, 100 mg.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Janssen-Cilag Pty Ltd [Australian]
    - Actelion Pharmaceuticals US, Inc. (A Janssen Pharmaceutical Company) [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 1 Gaucher Disease (GD1):** Indicated for the monotherapy treatment of adult patients with mild to moderate Type 1 Gaucher disease for whom enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) is not a therapeutic option (e.g., due to hypersensitivity, allergy, or poor venous access).
- **Niemann-Pick Type C Disease (NPC):** Indicated for the treatment of progressive neurological manifestations in both adult and paediatric patients [Australian-approved; not US-approved for this indication].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Type 1 Gaucher Disease (Adults):** 100 mg three times daily at regular intervals.
- **Dose Adjustment:** In patients experiencing gastrointestinal adverse effects (diarrhea) or tremor, the dose may be temporarily reduced to 100 mg once or twice daily.

### Niemann-Pick Type C Disease [Australian]
- **Adults and Adolescents:** 200 mg three times daily.
- **Paediatrics (<12 years):** Dosing must be adjusted based on Body Surface Area (BSA):
    - >1.25 m²: 200 mg three times daily.
    - >0.88 – 1.25 m²: 200 mg twice daily.
    - >0.73 – 0.88 m²: 300 mg daily (divided into 2–3 doses).
    - >0.47 – 0.73 m²: 100 mg twice daily.
    - ≤0.47 m²: 100 mg once daily.

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Interaction:** It is generally recommended to take **Zavesca** away from food to improve gastrointestinal tolerance. Co-administration with food decreases maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) by 36% and delays Tmax by 2 hours, though the effect on overall absorption (AUC) is minimal (14% reduction) [US].
- **Dietary Modification:** Patients should reduce intake of foods high in disaccharides (sucrose, lactose) to mitigate osmotic diarrhea.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the next capsule should be taken at the next scheduled time; do not double the dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to miglustat or any of the capsule excipients [Australian]. 
- **General:** The US PI states there are no listed contraindications [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Reported in approximately 3% of patients [US]. All patients should undergo baseline and 6-monthly neurological evaluations. If symptoms like numbness, tingling, or pain develop, a risk-benefit reassessment is required.
- **Tremor:** Affects approximately 30–38% of GD1 patients and 58% of NPC patients. Usually starts within the first month and often resolves within 1–3 months. Dose reduction or discontinuation may be necessary.
- **Gastrointestinal Events:** Diarrhea and weight loss occur in >80% and >50% of patients, respectively. These are primarily due to the inhibition of intestinal disaccharidases. Evaluate for underlying disease if patients do not respond to dietary modification or anti-diarrheal medication (e.g., loperamide). Cases of Crohn’s disease have been reported post-marketing [Australian].
- **Platelet Count Reductions:** Mild reductions in platelet counts have been observed, particularly when switching from ERT to miglustat. Regular monitoring is recommended.
- **Growth Monitoring:** In paediatric/adolescent NPC patients, growth (weight and height) should be monitored, as reduced growth rates have been reported [Australian].
- **Severe Type 1 Gaucher Disease:** Efficacy and safety have not been evaluated in patients with haemoglobin <90g/L or platelet counts <50 x 10⁹/L [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are classified by frequency: Very common (≥10%) and Common (1–10%).

| System Organ Class | Very Common (≥10%) | Common (1–10%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea, flatulence, abdominal pain, weight loss, nausea, vomiting | Dyspepsia, constipation, dry mouth, bloating, faecal incontinence [Australian] |
| **Nervous System** | Tremor, headache, dizziness, paresthesia | Leg cramps, unsteady gait, amnesia, migraine, peripheral neuropathy |
| **Metabolism** | Decreased appetite | Lactose intolerance [Australian] |
| **Psychiatric** | | Insomnia, depression [Australian], memory loss |
| **Blood/Lymphatic** | | Thrombocytopenia, anaemia |
| **Musculoskeletal** | | Muscle spasms/cramps, back pain, arthralgia |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Imiglucerase (Cerezyme):** Co-administration may result in decreased exposure to miglustat (22% reduction in Cmax and 14% in AUC) [Australian]. Some data suggest miglustat may increase the clearance of imiglucerase by up to 70%, though these results are considered inconclusive [US].
- **Loperamide:** Population pharmacokinetic analysis indicates loperamide does not significantly alter miglustat pharmacokinetics.
- **Cytochrome P450 Enzymes:** In vitro studies show miglustat does not inhibit CYP1A2, 2A6, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, or 3A4; therefore, interactions with drugs metabolised by these enzymes are unlikely.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Miglustat has been shown to cause maternal and embryo-fetal toxicity in animal studies, including increased post-implantation loss and vascular anomalies. It should not be used during pregnancy.
    - **US Recommendation:** Based on animal data, miglustat may cause fetal harm. Effective contraception is required for women of child-bearing potential.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if miglustat is excreted in human milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment.
- **Fertility (Male):** Animal studies (rats) showed adverse effects on spermatogenesis and reduced fertility. While a small study in seven healthy men did not show effects on fertility [US], it is recommended that male patients use reliable contraception during treatment and for 3 months following discontinuation.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **NPC:** Approved for use in children with NPC (dosing based on BSA) [Australian]. 
    - **GD1:** Not recommended in children/adolescents with GD1 due to lack of experience [Australian]. The US PI states safety and effectiveness have not been established in any paediatric population [US].
- **Elderly (>70 years):** Limited data available. Use caution in dose selection, typically starting at the low end of the range.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild (CrCl 50–70 mL/min):** Start at 100 mg twice daily.
    - **Moderate (CrCl 30–50 mL/min):** Start at 100 mg once daily.
    - **Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min):** Use is not recommended.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically evaluated; however, as miglustat is primarily eliminated renally, hepatic impairment is not expected to significantly impact pharmacokinetics.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Miglustat is a competitive and reversible inhibitor of glucosylceramide synthase. It reduces the rate of glycosphingolipid biosynthesis (Substrate Reduction Therapy) to a level that can be effectively managed by the patient’s residual glucocerebrosidase enzyme.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; Tmax ~2 hours. Bioavailability is ~97%.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is 83–105 L. It does not bind to plasma proteins and crosses the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily eliminated unchanged.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted mainly via the kidneys (83% recovered in urine). The effective terminal half-life is 6–7 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **GD1 Studies:** Clinical efficacy was demonstrated in open-label, non-comparative trials and one active-controlled switch study from ERT. Key outcomes included significant reductions in liver and spleen volumes (mean reductions of 12% and 19% respectively at 12 months) and stabilization of haemoglobin and platelet counts over 24 months.
- **NPC Studies:** Efficacy was supported by a 12-month controlled study and retrospective surveys showing a reduction in the annualized progression rate of neurological symptoms, such as horizontal saccadic eye movement velocity and swallowing function [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Australian:** Store below 30°C.
- **US:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule (Australia):** Prescription Only Medicine (S4).
- **Legal Classification (US):** Prescription Only (Rx-only).
