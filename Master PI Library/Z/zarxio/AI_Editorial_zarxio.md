# Editorial Product Information: ZARXIO (filgrastim-sndz)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, intended to consolidate clinical data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Filgrastim-sndz, a human granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF).
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant DNA-derived glycoprotein (produced in *E. coli*).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ZARXIO** [US].
- **ATC Code:** L03AA02 (Filgrastim).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Single-dose Vial:** 300 mcg/mL; 480 mcg/1.6 mL (300 mcg/mL) [US].
    - **Single-dose Prefilled Syringe (with needle guard):** 300 mcg/0.5 mL; 480 mcg/0.8 mL [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection or intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sandoz Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**ZARXIO** is a leukocyte growth factor indicated to:
- **Decrease the incidence of infection** (febrile neutropenia) in patients with nonmyeloid malignancies receiving myelosuppressive anti-cancer drugs associated with significant severe neutropenia with fever [US].
- **Reduce time to neutrophil recovery** and duration of fever following induction/consolidation chemotherapy in patients with Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) [US].
- **Reduce the duration of neutropenia** and related clinical sequelae (e.g., febrile neutropenia) in patients with nonmyeloid malignancies undergoing myeloablative chemotherapy followed by Bone Marrow Transplantation (BMT) [US].
- **Mobilize autologous hematopoietic progenitor cells** (PBPC) into peripheral blood for collection by leukapheresis [US].
- **Chronic administration to reduce sequelae of severe neutropenia** (e.g., fever, infections, oropharyngeal ulcers) in symptomatic patients with congenital, cyclic, or idiopathic neutropenia [US].
- **Increase survival** in patients acutely exposed to myelosuppressive doses of radiation (Hematopoietic Syndrome of Acute Radiation Syndrome) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Myelosuppressive Chemotherapy:** 5 mcg/kg/day (SC or IV). Escalation in increments of 5 mcg/kg per cycle may be considered based on ANC nadir. Discontinue if ANC >10,000/mm³ after nadir. 
- **Bone Marrow Transplantation:** 10 mcg/kg/day as an IV infusion (≤ 24 hours). Titrate dose based on ANC recovery (reduce to 5 mcg/kg/day if ANC >1,000/mm³ for 3 days; resume 5 mcg/kg/day if ANC drops <1,000/mm³).
- **PBPC Mobilization:** 10 mcg/kg/day (SC). Administer for ≥4 days before first leukapheresis and continue until last collection.
- **Severe Chronic Neutropenia (SCN):**
    - **Congenital:** Starting dose 6 mcg/kg twice daily (SC).
    - **Cyclic/Idiopathic:** Starting dose 5 mcg/kg daily (SC).
- **Acute Exposure to Radiation:** 10 mcg/kg/day (SC).

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** Do not administer within 24 hours before or after cytotoxic chemotherapy [US].
- **Dilution:** If required for IV use, dilute only in 5% Dextrose. **Do not use saline**, as the product may precipitate. For concentrations <15 mcg/mL, Albumin (Human) must be added (2 mg/mL final concentration) to prevent adsorption to plastics.
- **Syringe Limitation:** Prefilled syringes are not designed for doses <0.3 mL (180 mcg) due to graduation visibility; use vials for smaller doses [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Patients with a history of serious allergic reactions to human granulocyte colony-stimulating factors, such as filgrastim products or pegfilgrastim products [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Splenic Rupture:** Fatal cases reported. Evaluate for left upper abdominal or shoulder pain.
- **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS):** Evaluate for fever and lung infiltrates. Discontinue if ARDS occurs.
- **Serious Allergic Reactions:** Including anaphylaxis; permanently discontinue therapy.
- **Sickle Cell Disorders:** Severe and fatal crises have occurred in patients with sickle cell disease.
- **Glomerulonephritis:** Consider dose reduction or interruption if suspected.
- **Capillary Leak Syndrome:** Characterized by hypotension, hypoalbuminemia, and edema; requires close monitoring.
- **MDS/AML Risk:** Monitor patients with SCN for cytogenetic abnormalities. Increased risk noted in breast and lung cancer patients receiving chemotherapy/radiotherapy.
- **Leukocytosis:** Monitor CBC; discontinue if WBC >100,000/mm³.
- **Aortitis:** Reported within the first week of therapy; monitor for fever and back pain.
- **Nuclear Imaging:** Transient positive bone-imaging changes may occur due to increased bone marrow activity.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (Incidence ≥5% over placebo)
- **Myelosuppressive Chemotherapy:** Pyrexia, pain, rash, cough, dyspnea, nausea, fatigue, thrombocytopenia.
- **AML:** Pain, epistaxis, rash.
- **BMT:** Rash, hypersensitivity, thrombocytopenia, hypertension, sepsis.
- **PBPC Mobilization:** Bone pain, pyrexia, headache.
- **SCN:** Bone pain, arthralgia, back pain, splenomegaly, anemia, epistaxis, diarrhea, alopecia.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Splenic rupture, ARDS, anaphylaxis, sickle cell crisis, glomerulonephritis, alveolar hemorrhage, capillary leak syndrome, and aortitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytotoxic Chemotherapy:** Do not use within 24 hours of chemotherapy due to the sensitivity of rapidly dividing myeloid cells [US].
- **Radiation Therapy:** Avoid simultaneous use with concurrent radiation.
- **Nuclear Imaging:** Bone-imaging agents may show transiently increased uptake in marrow [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified in new labeling format]. Observational studies in SCN patients have not established a clear association with major birth defects. Animal studies in rabbits showed embryo-fetal toxicity at doses 2–10 times the human dose. Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Filgrastim is secreted into human milk. Absorption by infants is unlikely, but clinical benefits should be weighed against potential infant risks.
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility observed in rats at doses up to 500 mcg/kg [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety/efficacy established in SCN and acute radiation exposure. PK in children is similar to adults. Long-term follow-up (up to 5 years) showed no adverse effects on growth or sexual maturation [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety/efficacy observed in patients ≥65 years during chemotherapy trials; insufficient data for other indications.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Higher serum concentrations noted in end-stage renal disease, but dose adjustments are not required for renal or hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** G-CSF regulates the production of neutrophils within the bone marrow. Filgrastim-sndz binds to specific surface receptors, stimulating proliferation, differentiation, and functional activation of the neutrophil lineage.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability 60%–70% after SC injection.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution ~150 mL/kg.
    - **Elimination:** Nonlinear PK; clearance is dependent on concentration and neutrophil count. Elimination half-life is ~3.5 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Small Cell Lung Cancer (Study 1):** Randomized trial showed filgrastim reduced the incidence of febrile neutropenia (40% vs 76% in placebo).
- **AML (Study 4):** Reduced median duration of severe neutropenia by 5 days compared to placebo.
- **SCN (Study 7):** 112 of 123 patients demonstrated a complete or partial ANC response to treatment.
- **Radiation Exposure:** Approval based on a non-human primate model where 60-day mortality was reduced from 59% (control) to 21% (filgrastim-treated) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store in refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from light. Do not shake. Avoid freezing; if frozen more than once, discard.
- **Room Temperature:** May be kept at room temperature (up to 25°C) for up to 8 days. Discard if not used within this period.
