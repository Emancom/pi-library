# Editorial Product Information: Zepatier (Elbasvir and Grazoprevir)

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the approved product information for Zepatier, intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial overview and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Elbasvir (HCV NS5A inhibitor) and Grazoprevir (HCV NS3/4A protease inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** Zepatier [US].
- **ATC Code:** J05AP54.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Fixed-dose combination film-coated tablets containing 50 mg elbasvir and 100 mg grazoprevir [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme LLC [US].
- **Physical Description:** Beige-colored, oval-shaped, film-coated tablet debossed with "770" on one side and plain on the other [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) Infection:** Indicated for the treatment of chronic HCV genotype 1 or 4 infection in adult and pediatric patients aged 12 years and older or weighing at least 30 kg [US].
- **Ribavirin Co-administration:** Indicated for use in combination with ribavirin for specific patient populations, such as those with baseline NS5A polymorphisms or those who have failed previous therapy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One tablet taken orally once daily, with or without food [US].
- **Testing Prior to Initiation:**
    - **HBV Screening:** Test all patients for evidence of current or prior Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) infection (HBsAg and anti-HBc) [US].
    - **Hepatic Testing:** Perform hepatic laboratory testing (ALT, etc.) prior to therapy [US].
    - **Resistance Testing:** For Genotype 1a, testing for baseline NS5A resistance-associated polymorphisms (at positions 28, 30, 31, or 93) is recommended to determine the need for ribavirin and treatment duration [US].
- **Treatment Duration and Regimens:**
    - **Genotype 1a (Treatment-naïve or PegIFN/RBV-experienced):** 12 weeks without baseline NS5A polymorphisms; 16 weeks with ribavirin if baseline NS5A polymorphisms are present [US].
    - **Genotype 1b (Treatment-naïve or PegIFN/RBV-experienced):** 12 weeks [US].
    - **Genotype 1a or 1b (PegIFN/RBV/PI-experienced):** 12 weeks with ribavirin [US].
    - **Genotype 4 (Treatment-naïve):** 12 weeks [US].
    - **Genotype 4 (PegIFN/RBV-experienced):** 16 weeks with ribavirin [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Patients should be advised not to take two doses at the same time to make up for a missed dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Moderate or Severe Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with Child-Pugh B or C cirrhosis [US].
- **Specific Drug Co-administration:**
    - **OATP1B1/3 Inhibitors:** Agents that significantly increase grazoprevir concentrations (e.g., Atazanavir, Darunavir, Lopinavir, Saquinavir, Tipranavir, Cyclosporine) [US].
    - **Strong CYP3A Inducers:** (e.g., Phenytoin, Carbamazepine, Rifampin, St. John’s Wort) [US].
    - **Efavirenz-containing regimens** [US].
- **Ribavirin Contraindications:** If used in combination, the contraindications to ribavirin also apply [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Risk of Hepatitis B Virus Reactivation [US]:** HBV reactivation has been reported in HCV/HBV co-infected patients receiving direct-acting antivirals (DAAs). This can result in fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure, and death. Monitor co-infected patients for hepatitis flare or reactivation during and after treatment.
- **ALT Elevations:** Elevations >5x ULN occurred in 1% of clinical trial subjects, typically at or after treatment week 8. Monitor hepatic labs at week 8 (and week 12 for 16-week regimens). Discontinue if ALT remains >10x ULN or if elevation is accompanied by signs of liver inflammation or increased bilirubin/INR [US].
- **Hepatic Decompensation/Failure:** Reported primarily in patients with advanced liver disease or cirrhosis. Monitor for signs of decompensation (ascites, encephalopathy, variceal hemorrhage) and discontinue if they occur [US].
- **Risk with Ribavirin:** Ensure adherence to ribavirin pregnancy avoidance and monitoring requirements when used in combination [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **With Zepatier Alone (12 weeks):**
    - **Common (>10%):** Fatigue, headache [US].
    - **Common (1–10%):** Nausea, insomnia, diarrhea [US].
- **With Zepatier + Ribavirin (16 weeks):**
    - **Common (>10%):** Fatigue, headache, anemia [US].
    - **Common (1–10%):** Nausea, insomnia, diarrhea, irritability, rash, pruritus, abdominal pain [US].
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Late ALT elevations (>5x ULN) and indirect bilirubin elevations (predominantly when used with ribavirin) [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Angioedema, hepatic decompensation, and hepatic failure [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Grazoprevir is a substrate of OATP1B1/3 and CYP3A. Elbasvir is a substrate of CYP3A. Both are substrates of P-gp [US].
- **Statins:**
    - **Atorvastatin:** Do not exceed 20 mg/day [US].
    - **Rosuvastatin:** Do not exceed 10 mg/day [US].
    - **Fluvastatin, Lovastatin, Simvastatin:** Use the lowest necessary dose and monitor for myopathy [US].
- **Immunosuppressants:** Co-administration with cyclosporine is contraindicated. Monitor tacrolimus blood levels frequently when co-administered [US].
- **HIV Medications:** Contraindicated with efavirenz or OATP1B1/3 inhibiting protease inhibitors. Co-administration with etravirine or cobicistat-containing regimens is not recommended [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **US Category:** Not specified in available sources (narrative labeling used). No adequate human data available. Animal studies at high exposures showed no evidence of adverse developmental outcomes [US].
    - **Combination with Ribavirin:** Contraindicated in pregnant women and in men whose female partners are pregnant [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk is unknown. In lactating rats, components were excreted in milk without effects on growth or development in pups. Consider maternal clinical need versus potential infant risk [US].
- **Fertility:** No effects on mating or fertility observed in rat studies at exposures significantly higher than the recommended human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children 12 years and older or weighing at least 30 kg. Safety and efficacy not established in children under 12 or those weighing <30 kg [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment required. Clinical trials included subjects 65 years and older; while no overall difference in efficacy was noted, higher plasma concentrations were observed in older subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for any degree of renal impairment, including patients on hemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild impairment (Child-Pugh A). Contraindicated in moderate or severe impairment (Child-Pugh B or C) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Elbasvir inhibits the HCV NS5A protein, essential for viral RNA replication and virion assembly. Grazoprevir inhibits the HCV NS3/4A protease, necessary for proteolytic cleavage of the HCV polyprotein [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak concentrations at 3 hours (elbasvir) and 2 hours (grazoprevir). Bioavailability is 32% and 27%, respectively [US].
    - **Distribution:** Both are highly protein-bound (>98%).
    - **Metabolism:** Partially eliminated via oxidative metabolism, primarily by CYP3A [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via feces (>90%), with <1% recovered in urine [US].
    - **Half-life:** Approximately 24 hours (elbasvir) and 31 hours (grazoprevir) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **C-EDGE TN:** Randomized, double-blind trial in treatment-naïve subjects. SVR12 achieved in 95% of Genotype 1 subjects [US].
- **C-EDGE COINFECTION:** Open-label trial in HCV/HIV-1 co-infected subjects. SVR12 achieved in 95% of Genotype 1 subjects [US].
- **C-SURFER:** Trial in subjects with Stage 4 or 5 chronic kidney disease. SVR12 achieved in 94% of subjects [US].
- **C-EDGE TE:** Randomized trial in treatment-experienced subjects. SVR12 achieved in 94% (12-week Zepatier) and 97% (16-week Zepatier + ribavirin) of Genotype 1 subjects [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep tablets in the original blister package until use to protect from moisture [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
