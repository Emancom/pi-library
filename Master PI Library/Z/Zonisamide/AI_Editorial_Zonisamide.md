# Editorial Product Information: Zonisamide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised, clinically focused overview of zonisamide for healthcare professionals, integrating information from available regional sources.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI applicable in a specific jurisdiction when prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Zonisamide (a benzisoxazole derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Antiepileptic; weak carbonic anhydrase inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** Zonegran [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N03AX15 (Not specified in provided source).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Hard Capsules:** 25 mg, 50 mg, and 100 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amdipharm Mercury (Australia) Pty Ltd (An ADVANZ PHARMA company) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Monotherapy:** Treatment of partial seizures (with or without secondary generalisation) in adults with newly diagnosed epilepsy who are either intolerant to other agents or where other agents are contraindicated [Australian].
- **Adjunctive Therapy:** Treatment of partial seizures (with or without secondary generalisation) in adult patients [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Zonisamide may be taken with or without food. The dose should be titrated based on clinical effect.

### Monotherapy (Newly Diagnosed Adults)
- **Initial Dose (Weeks 1+2):** 100 mg once daily.
- **Weeks 3+4:** 200 mg once daily.
- **Weeks 5+6:** 300 mg once daily.
- **Maintenance Dose:** Standard maintenance is 300 mg once daily. If required, the dose may be increased at two-weekly intervals in 100 mg increments.
- **Maximum Dose:** 500 mg daily [Australian].

### Adjunctive Therapy (Adults)
Dosing varies based on the presence of CYP3A4-inducing agents (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine):
- **With CYP3A4 Inducers:** 
    - Initial (Week 1): 50 mg/day in two divided doses.
    - Week 2: 100 mg/day in two divided doses.
    - Weeks 3-5: Increase at weekly intervals by 100 mg increments.
    - Maintenance: 300 to 500 mg per day (once daily or in two divided doses).
- **Without CYP3A4 Inducers or with Renal/Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Titration follows a slower schedule (e.g., 100 mg increments every two weeks) [Australian].

### Missed Dose and Withdrawal
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Withdrawal:** Must be tapered gradually to reduce the risk of breakthrough seizures. Clinical studies used 100 mg reductions at weekly intervals [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to zonisamide, other sulfonamides, or any product excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidal Ideation and Behaviour:** Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) increase the risk of suicidal thoughts. Patients should be monitored for depression, mood changes, or self-harm [Australian].
- **Serious Sulfonamide Reactions:** Potential for fatal reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), fulminant hepatic necrosis, agranulocytosis, and aplastic anaemia. Discontinue immediately at the first sign of a rash or hypersensitivity [Australian].
- **Metabolic Acidosis:** Zonisamide causes hyperchloremic, non-anion gap metabolic acidosis via carbonic anhydrase inhibition. Monitor serum bicarbonate at baseline and periodically (at least 6-monthly). Chronic acidosis may lead to nephrolithiasis, osteomalacia, or reduced growth in paediatric patients [Australian].
- **Kidney Stones:** Occurred in approximately 4% of clinical trial patients. Risk is higher in those with a personal/family history or those taking other carbonic anhydrase inhibitors. Ensure adequate hydration [Australian].
- **Acute Myopia and Secondary Angle Closure Glaucoma:** Reported in both adult and paediatric patients. Symptoms (decreased visual acuity, ocular pain) usually occur within hours to weeks of initiation. Discontinue immediately [Australian].
- **Hyperammonaemia and Encephalopathy:** Risk is increased when co-administered with other drugs like valproic acid. Measure ammonia levels if mental status changes occur [Australian].
- **Oligohidrosis and Hyperthermia:** Mainly reported in paediatric patients. Caution in warm weather and with drugs predisposing to heat-related disorders (e.g., anticholinergics) [Australian].
- **Cognitive Adverse Events:** May cause somnolence or difficulty concentrating. Patients should exercise caution when driving [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Tiers [Australian]
- **Very Common (>10%):** Somnolence, dizziness, anorexia, agitation/irritability, altered cognitive function, and decreased bicarbonate.
- **Common (1–10%):** Depression, anxiety, ataxia, confusion, diplopia, nystagmus, nausea, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, weight loss, rash, and fatigue.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Suicide attempt, hypersensitivity reactions.
- **Very Rare (<0.1%):** SJS/TEN, DRESS, agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia, pancreatitis, renal failure, acute closure glaucoma, and hyperthermia.

### Serious Adverse Effects
Sulfonamide-related skin/blood reactions and metabolic acidosis are the primary serious concerns.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Phenytoin, Carbamazepine, Phenobarbitone, Rifampicin):** Increase zonisamide clearance and decrease serum levels. Dose adjustments may be necessary if these are added or withdrawn [Australian].
- **Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors (e.g., Topiramate, Acetazolamide):** Concomitant use increases the risk of metabolic acidosis, kidney stones, and hyperammonaemia. This combination is not recommended [Australian].
- **Valproic Acid:** May increase the risk of hyperammonaemic encephalopathy [Australian].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Steady-state zonisamide (400 mg/day) did not affect the pharmacokinetics of ethinylestradiol or norethisterone in healthy subjects [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian].**
    - Zonisamide is teratogenic in animal models (cardiovascular, skeletal, and craniofacial defects). 
    - Human registry data suggest a higher risk of low birth weight (8%), pre-term birth (10%), and being small for gestational age (12%) compared to lamotrigine monotherapy. 
    - Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Zonisamide is excreted in human milk at concentrations similar to plasma. Because the drug has a long half-life, breastfeeding should not be resumed until one month after therapy completion [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed reduced corpora lutea and live fetuses; human effect is unknown [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children under 18 years have not been established. Use is **not recommended** due to risks of metabolic acidosis, heatstroke, and potential growth retardation [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited information; initiate with caution and use slower titration [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Zonisamide and its metabolites are excreted renally. Clearance is reduced (AUC increased by 35% when CrCl < 20 mL/min). Discontinue if acute renal failure or significant creatinine increase occurs [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use in severe impairment is not recommended. Caution and slower titration are required for mild to moderate impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Not fully elucidated. Appears to block voltage-sensitive sodium and calcium channels, disrupting synchronised neuronal firing. It also has a modulatory effect on GABA-mediated inhibition and facilitates dopaminergic and serotonergic neurotransmission [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Almost complete (100% bioavailability); peak levels in 2–5 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Extensively binds to erythrocytes; 40–50% plasma protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 (reductive cleavage) and N-acetylation [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is approximately 60 hours (reduced by CYP3A4 inducers). Primarily excreted in urine (62%) and faeces (3%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy Study:** A double-blind comparison with carbamazepine PR (n=583) showed zonisamide (300–500 mg/day) was non-inferior for 6-month seizure freedom (79.4% zonisamide vs 83.7% carbamazepine) [Australian].
- **Adjunctive Therapy:** Efficacy was demonstrated in four double-blind, placebo-controlled trials of up to 24 weeks. A median reduction in seizure frequency was observed with doses of 300–500 mg/day [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Unused medicine should be returned to a pharmacy for disposal [Australian].
