# Editorial Product Information: Vorinostat

## Editorial Note
*   **Jurisdictions Contributed:** This editorial synthesis incorporates data from the Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and United States (Food and Drug Administration) official Product Information documents.
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals to facilitate a consolidated clinical understanding of **vorinostat**.
*   **Disclaimer:** This text is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Vorinostat (INN)
*   **Chemical Class:** Histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor (hydroxamate)
*   **Brand Name:** **ZOLINZA**
*   **ATC Code:** L01CH01
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** 100 mg hard gelatin capsules. White, opaque capsules imprinted with "568" over "100 mg" within a radial bar in black ink.
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck Sharp & Dohme (Australia) Pty Limited [Australian]; Merck Sharp & Dohme LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Cutaneous T-cell Lymphoma (CTCL):** Treatment of cutaneous manifestations in patients with CTCL who have progressive, persistent, or recurrent disease following two prior systemic therapies [US]. The Australian indication specifies use "subsequent to prior systemic therapies" without quantifying a minimum of two, though trial data reflects a median of three prior therapies.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*   **Standard Adult Dose:** 400 mg orally once daily.
*   **Administration Instructions:**
    *   Administer with food.
    *   **ZOLINZA** capsules must not be opened or crushed. 
    *   Patients should maintain adequate hydration, consuming at least 2 L of fluid daily to prevent dehydration.
*   **Dose Modifications for Toxicity:**
    *   **First Reduction:** Decrease dose to 300 mg orally once daily with food.
    *   **Second Reduction:** Decrease dose to 300 mg once daily with food for 5 consecutive days each week.
    *   **Criteria:** Therapy should be adjusted or interrupted for intolerant patients or those experiencing severe adverse reactions (e.g., myelosuppression, gastrointestinal toxicity).
*   **Missed Dose:** Patients should skip a missed dose and take the next dose at the regularly scheduled time. They should not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **vorinostat** or any component of the formulation.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment [Australian; not explicitly listed as a contraindication in the US PI, though dosing is not recommended due to insufficient evidence].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Thromboembolism:** Pulmonary embolism (reported in ~5% of patients) and deep vein thrombosis have occurred. Risk is higher in patients with a prior history of thromboembolic events.
*   **Myelosuppression:** Dose-related thrombocytopenia and anaemia are common. Monitor CBC every 2 weeks for the first 2 months, then monthly.
*   **Gastrointestinal Toxicity:** Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea are frequent. Pre-existing symptoms must be controlled before starting therapy. Prompt use of antiemetics and antidiarrheals is recommended.
*   **Hyperglycaemia:** Observed in 69% of patients (severe in 5%). Monitor serum glucose every 2 weeks for the first 2 months and monthly thereafter, particularly in diabetic or pre-diabetic patients.
*   **Clinical Chemistry Abnormalities:** Potential for transient increases in serum creatinine and proteinuria (observed in ~50% of patients). Electrolytes (potassium, magnesium, calcium) should be corrected at baseline and monitored regularly.
*   **QTc Prolongation:** While supratherapeutic doses (800 mg) did not significantly prolong QTc in studies, clinicians should exercise caution in patients with pre-existing risk factors.
*   **HDAC Inhibitor Interaction:** Concomitant use with other HDAC inhibitors (e.g., valproic acid) increases the risk of severe thrombocytopenia and GI bleeding.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Coumarin-Derivative Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** Concomitant use may result in prolongation of Prothrombin Time (PT) and International Normalized Ratio (INR). Frequent INR monitoring is required.
*   **Valproic Acid/Other HDAC Inhibitors:** Additive risk of severe thrombocytopenia and gastrointestinal bleeding. Avoid combination or monitor platelet counts at least every 2 weeks.
*   **CYP450 Metabolism:** **Vorinostat** is not significantly metabolised by CYP450 enzymes and is not an inhibitor of these enzymes at clinically relevant concentrations; pharmacokinetic interactions via this pathway are unlikely.
*   **Transporters:** **Vorinostat** is not a substrate or inhibitor of P-glycoprotein (P-gp).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Classification:** Category D [Australian]; Embryo-fetal toxicity warning [US].
    *   **Recommendation:** Based on animal data showing crossed placenta and foetal malformations (e.g., gallbladder abnormalities in rabbits), **vorinostat** may cause foetal harm. Pregnancy must be excluded before starting therapy.
*   **Contraception:**
    *   **Females:** Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 6 months after the final dose.
    *   **Males:** Use effective contraception and avoid fathering a child during treatment and for at least 3 months after the final dose.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if **vorinostat** is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions, breastfeeding is not recommended during therapy and for at least 1 week after the last dose.
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate a potential for dose-dependent increases in corpora lutea and peri-implantation losses in females. Male fertility was unaffected in rats, though testicular degeneration was noted in dogs.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age.
*   **Elderly Use:** No significant differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients $\ge$65 years and younger patients. No dose adjustment is required.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically evaluated; however, renal excretion of unchanged drug is <1%. No dose adjustment is anticipated.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    *   **Mild to Moderate (Bilirubin 1–3x ULN or AST >ULN):** Reduce starting dose to 300 mg once daily with food [US].
    *   **Severe (Bilirubin >3x ULN):** Contraindicated [Australian]. There is insufficient evidence to recommend a starting dose; if used, extreme caution and dose reduction are necessary [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits HDAC1, HDAC2, HDAC3 (Class I), and HDAC6 (Class II). Inhibition leads to the accumulation of acetylated histones and transcription factors, causing cell cycle arrest, differentiation, or apoptosis in transformed cells.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Bioavailability is ~43%. A high-fat meal increases the extent of absorption (AUC) by 33% and delays $T_{max}$ but is not considered clinically significant.
    *   **Distribution:** Approximately 71% protein bound.
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily via glucuronidation and hydrolysis (non-CYP450 pathways). Two major inactive metabolites: O-glucuronide and 4-anilino-4-oxobutanoic acid.
    *   **Excretion:** Eliminated predominantly via metabolism; $<1\%$ recovered unchanged in urine. Terminal half-life is ~2 hours for **vorinostat** and its O-glucuronide metabolite.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Pivotal Phase IIb Study (N=74):** Open-label, multicenter trial in patients with advanced stage CTCL (Stage IIB and higher) refractory to at least two systemic therapies.
    *   **Primary Outcome:** Objective Response Rate (ORR) was 29.7%.
    *   **Time to Response:** Median 55–56 days.
    *   **Duration of Response:** Median not reached; estimated to exceed 6 months.
*   **Phase II Study (N=33):** Evaluated three different dosing regimens in patients refractory to or intolerant of conventional treatment. ORR was 24.2% across the population.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Storage:** Store below 30°C (86°F) [Australian]. The US PI specifies 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) with excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
*   **Handling:** **ZOLINZA** is a hazardous drug. Direct contact with the powder should be avoided. If a capsule is broken, wash skin or eyes immediately with copious water.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Australian Classification:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
*   **United States Classification:** Rx-only.
*   **Specific Handling:** Procedures for proper handling and disposal of anticancer drugs should be followed [US].
