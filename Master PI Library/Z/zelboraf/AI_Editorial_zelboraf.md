# Editorial Product Information: Zelboraf (vemurafenib)

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA), US (Food and Drug Administration - FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information from multiple regulatory authorities. It is intended as a high-level clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI of a specific jurisdiction when prescribing or dispensing. Clinicians must consult local regulations for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vemurafenib (as a co-precipitate of vemurafenib and hypromellose acetate succinate).
- **Chemical Class:** Low molecular weight, orally available kinase inhibitor (selective for mutated BRAF serine-threonine kinase).
- **Brand Name:** Zelboraf.
- **ATC Code:** L01XE15.
- **Dosage Form:** Film-coated tablets.
- **Strength:** 240 mg.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Roche Products Pty Limited [Australian]; Genentech, Inc. (Member of the Roche Group) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Unresectable or Metastatic Melanoma:** Indicated for the treatment of patients with unresectable stage IIIC [Australian] or stage IV metastatic melanoma positive for a **BRAF V600** mutation (specifically **BRAF V600E** [US]).
    - **Limitation of Use:** Not indicated for the treatment of patients with wild-type BRAF melanoma [US].
- **Erdheim-Chester Disease (ECD):** Indicated for the treatment of patients with Erdheim-Chester Disease with a **BRAF V600** mutation [US-approved; not specified in Australian source].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** 960 mg (four 240 mg tablets) twice daily.
- **Total Daily Dose:** 1920 mg.
- **Timing:** Doses should be taken approximately 12 hours apart.
- **Food Interaction:**
    - [Australian]: Take either at least 1 hour before or at least 2 hours after a meal (fasted state).
    - [US]: Take with or without a meal.
    - *Editorial Note:* US data indicates high-fat meals increase AUC and Cmax significantly; however, US instructions permit administration regardless of food. Australian instructions remain more conservative regarding the fasted state.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole with water; do not crush or chew.
- Treatment should continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

### Missed Doses and Vomiting
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it can be taken up to 4 hours before the next scheduled dose. Do not take both doses at the same time.
- **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after administration, do not take an additional dose. Resume treatment with the next scheduled dose.

### Dose Modifications
- Dose reductions below 480 mg twice daily are not recommended.
- **Intolerable Grade 2 or Grade 3 Toxicity:**
    - [Australian]: Interrupt until resolved to Grade 0–1. Resumption: Reduce dose by 240 mg twice daily.
    - [US]: Interrupt until resolved to Grade 0–1. Resumption: 1st appearance 720 mg twice daily; 2nd appearance 480 mg twice daily.
- **Grade 4 Toxicity:**
    - [Australian]: Permanent discontinuation OR interrupt until Grade 0–1 and resume at 480 mg twice daily.
    - [US]: Discontinue permanently OR interrupt until Grade 0–1 and resume at 480 mg twice daily.
- **New Primary Cutaneous Malignancies:** No dose modification or interruption is recommended.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to vemurafenib or any of its excipients [Australian].
- [US]: None listed.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **New Primary Malignancies:**
    - **Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma (cuSCC) and Keratoacanthoma:** High incidence (~20-24% in melanoma; ~40% in ECD). Perform dermatologic evaluation before initiation and every 2 months during therapy. Monitoring should continue for 6 months post-discontinuation.
    - **New Primary Melanoma:** Reported in clinical trials; managed by excision without dose adjustment.
    - **Non-Cutaneous SCC:** Rare cases in head/neck (tongue/tonsils). Monitor oral mucosa and lymph nodes every 3 months.
    - **Other Malignancies:** BRAF inhibitors may promote RAS-mutated malignancies.
- **Tumor Promotion in BRAF Wild-Type Melanoma:** Paradoxical activation of MAP-kinase signaling can occur in wild-type cells, increasing cell proliferation [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis and **DRESS** (Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms), have been reported. Discontinue permanently for severe reactions.
- **Dermatologic Reactions:** Rare cases of **SJS** (Stevens-Johnson syndrome) and **TEN** (Toxic epidermal necrolysis). Discontinue permanently for severe reactions.
- **QT Prolongation:** Exposure-dependent prolongation.
    - **Monitoring:** ECG and electrolytes (potassium, magnesium, calcium) before treatment, at day 15, monthly for 3 months, then every 3 months.
    - **Management:** Do not initiate if QTc > 500 ms. If QTc exceeds 500 ms during treatment, interrupt, correct electrolytes, and resume at a lower dose once < 500 ms. Permanent discontinuation is required if QTc > 500 ms and increases > 60 ms from baseline.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Liver injury (severe cases reported). Monitor transaminases, alkaline phosphatase, and bilirubin monthly.
- **Photosensitivity:** Mild to severe reactions. Advise patients to avoid sun exposure, use broad-spectrum UVA/UVB sunscreen, and SPF 30+ lip balm.
- **Ophthalmologic Reactions:** Uveitis, iritis, blurry vision, and retinal vein occlusion reported.
- **Radiation Toxicity:** Potential for radiation recall and sensitisation (can be fatal in visceral organs).
- **Renal Failure:** Creatinine elevations and acute kidney injury reported. Measure creatinine before and during treatment.
- **Dupuytren’s Contracture and Plantar Fascial Fibromatosis:** Reported cases are typically mild to moderate but can be disabling.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Arthralgia (53-67%), fatigue (38-54%), rash (37-52%), alopecia (36-45%), photosensitivity, nausea, diarrhoea, pruritus, skin papilloma, hyperkeratosis, headache, vomiting, dry skin, and GGT increased.
- **Common (1–10%):** cuSCC/Keratoacanthoma, basal cell carcinoma, palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia, pyrexia, dysgeusia, peripheral neuropathy, facial nerve paralysis, and QT prolongation.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Neutropenia, pancreatitis, retinal vein occlusion, SJS, TEN, and liver injury.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Sarcoidosis [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** cuSCC, serious hypersensitivity (DRESS, anaphylaxis), SJS/TEN, QT prolongation, and severe liver injury.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibition/Induction on Vemurafenib:**
    - **Strong Inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole):** Increase vemurafenib AUC by ~40%. Avoid or consider dose reduction.
    - **Strong Inducers (e.g., rifampin):** Decrease vemurafenib AUC by ~40%. Avoid or consider dose increase [US].
- **Vemurafenib Effect on CYP Substrates:**
    - **CYP1A2:** Potent inhibition. Increases tizanidine exposure by ~4.7-fold and caffeine by ~2.6-fold. Avoid sensitive substrates with narrow therapeutic indices (e.g., theophylline, tizanidine).
    - **CYP3A4:** Inducer. Decreases midazolam exposure by ~39%. May decrease efficacy of oral contraceptives.
    - **CYP2C9:** Increases S-warfarin exposure by ~18%. Exercise caution and monitor INR.
- **Transporters:**
    - **P-gp:** Inhibitor. Increases digoxin exposure by ~1.8-fold. Use caution with P-gp substrates.
    - **BCRP:** Vemurafenib is both a substrate and an inhibitor.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Ipilimumab:** Concurrent administration with Zelboraf is **not recommended** due to Grade 3 increases in transaminases and bilirubin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - [Australian]: **Category D.** (Suspected to cause fetal harm/malformations).
    - [US]: Embryo-fetal toxicity risk. Placental transfer has been reported.
    - **Contraception:** Effective contraception is required during and after treatment.
        - [Australian]: Continue for **6 months** post-discontinuation.
        - [US]: Continue for **2 weeks** after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug.
    - [US]: Advise against breastfeeding during treatment and for **2 weeks** after the final dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients < 18 years have not been established. [US] notes a small study (n=6) in adolescents (15–17 years) showed similar PK to adults.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is required for patients ≥ 65 years. However, older patients may be more susceptible to cuSCC, decreased appetite, and cardiac disorders.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. Insufficient data for severe impairment (creatinine clearance < 29 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. Insufficient data/caution advised for severe impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits mutated BRAF serine-threonine kinases (e.g., V600E). Prevents constitutive activation of the MAP-kinase pathway, thereby inhibiting cell proliferation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median Tmax ~3–4 hours. Significant accumulation occurs with BID dosing.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (>99%). Vd ~91 L [Australian] to 106 L [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily parent drug (95%). CYP3A4 is the primary metabolic enzyme in vitro.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (94%). Elimination half-life ~57 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Trial 1 (BRIM-3):** Phase III, open-label study comparing Zelboraf (960 mg BID) to dacarbazine (1000 mg/m²). Zelboraf showed significant improvement in Overall Survival (Hazard Ratio 0.44–0.47) and Progression-Free Survival (Median 5.3 vs 1.6 months).
- **Trial 2 (BRIM-2):** Phase II study in previously treated metastatic melanoma (n=132). Confirmed Best Overall Response Rate (BORR) was 52%.
- **Brain Metastases Study:** Phase II study (n=146) showed a BORR of ~18% in brain lesions for both previously treated and untreated cohorts.
- **Trial 4 (ECD):** Phase II study (n=22) in Erdheim-Chester Disease. Best Overall Response Rate was 54.5% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:**
    - [Australian]: Store below 30°C.
    - [US]: Store at 20°C–25°C (excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C).
- **Condition:** Store in the original container; protect from moisture. Keep container tightly closed.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:**
    - [Australian]: Prescription Only Medicine (**S4**).
    - [US]: **Rx-only**.
- **Mutation Testing:** Diagnosis of BRAF V600 mutation-positive status must be confirmed by a validated/approved assay (TGA-approved in Australia; FDA-approved in the US).
