# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Jurisdictions Contributing to this Editorial:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory information.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. The clinical details provided herein are based on the provided source for Levocarnitine Injection (USP).

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical class: Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [United States].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for intravenous injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [United States].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [United States].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    *   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
    *   **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours.
    *   **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, adjusted based on clinical response and plasma levels. The maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg [United States].
*   **ESRD Patients on Haemodialysis:**
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2–3 minute bolus.
    *   **Administration Timing:** Administer into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   **Dose Adjustments:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine concentrations [United States].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Visual Inspection:** Products should be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.
*   **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35–60 µmol/L) [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [United States].
*   **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients both with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
*   **Renal Impairment (Oral Metabolites):** While this PI covers the injection, it is noted that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients are often confounded by the underlying pathology.

### Very Common (>10%)
*   **General:** Injection site reaction (27–59%), Pain (21–49%), Flu syndrome (15–40%), Headache (3–37%), Infection (10–24%), Fever (5–12%), Asthenia (8–12%), Accidental injury (8–12%), Chest pain (6–15%).
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (14–21%), Hypotension (3–19%).
*   **Digestive:** Diarrhoea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%).
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), Dyspnoea (3–19%), Cough increase (10–18%), Rhinitis (6–11%).
*   **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), Paresthesia (3–12%).
*   **Metabolic:** Hypervolaemia (3–17%), Hypercalcaemia (6–15%).

### Common (1–10%)
*   **General:** Abdominal pain, Back pain, Allergic reaction.
*   **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, Palpitations, Arrhythmia, Atrial fibrillation.
*   **Digestive:** Anorexia, Constipation, Dyspepsia, Melena, Gastrointestinal disorder.
*   **Metabolic/Endocrine:** Hyperkalaemia, Weight increase/decrease, Peripheral oedema, Parathyroid disorder.
*   **Musculoskeletal/Skin:** Leg cramps, Myalgia, Pruritus, Rash.
*   **Special Senses:** Amblyopia, Eye disorder, Taste perversion.

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Anaphylaxis/Severe Hypersensitivity.
*   Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified]. Animal reproduction studies (rats/rabbits) using 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use in pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit [United States].
*   **Fertility:** No impairment observed in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on body weight (see Section 3).
*   **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for geriatric patients are not detailed in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose *oral* therapy is cautioned in renal insufficiency due to metabolite accumulation [United States].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating fatty acid oxidation and subsequent energy production in tissues (except the brain).
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces).
    *   **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Total body clearance is approximately 4.00 L/h [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels increased from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks. While increases in carnitine levels are proven, the direct effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial. The formulation contains no preservatives.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [United States].
