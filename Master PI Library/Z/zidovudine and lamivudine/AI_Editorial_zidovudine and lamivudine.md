# Editorial Product Information: Zidovudine and Lamivudine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdictions: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)** and the **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory guidance from multiple regions into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, current PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Lamivudine (INN) and Zidovudine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Nucleoside analogue reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs).
- **Brand Names:** **COMBIVIR** [Australian]; **Lamivudine and Zidovudine Tablets** (Generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** J05AR01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated, scored tablets containing 150 mg lamivudine and 300 mg zidovudine.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ViiV Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian]; Camber Pharmaceuticals, Inc. / Hetero Labs Limited [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated for the treatment of Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) infection.
    - [US]: Must be used in combination with other antiretroviral agents.
    - [Australian]: Indicated for use alone or in combination with other antiretroviral therapies.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents)
- **Weight ≥30 kg:** The recommended dose is one tablet (150 mg lamivudine/300 mg zidovudine) taken twice daily.

### Pediatric Dosing
- **Weight ≥30 kg:** One tablet twice daily [US, Australian].
- **Weight 21 kg to <30 kg:** One-half tablet (scored) in the morning and one whole tablet in the evening [Australian].
- **Weight 14 kg to 21 kg:** One-half tablet (scored) twice daily [Australian].
- **Weight <30 kg:** Not recommended as the fixed-dose combination prevents necessary dose adjustments [US].
- **Weight <14 kg:** Use of this fixed-dose tablet is not recommended; separate oral solutions should be used to ensure accurate weight-based dosing [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- May be administered with or without food.
- Tablets should ideally be swallowed whole; however, for patients unable to swallow, tablets may be crushed and added to a small amount of semi-solid food or liquid and consumed immediately [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a dose is missed, patients should take it as soon as they remember. They should not double the next dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to lamivudine, zidovudine, or any excipients.
- **Abnormal Haematological Indices:** Contraindicated in patients with abnormally low neutrophil counts (<0.75 x 10⁹/L) or abnormally low haemoglobin levels (<7.5 g/dL or 4.65 mmol/L) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Haematologic Toxicity:** Zidovudine has been associated with severe anaemia and neutropenia, particularly in advanced HIV disease.
- **Myopathy:** Symptomatic myopathy and myositis are associated with prolonged zidovudine use.
- **Lactic Acidosis and Severe Hepatomegaly with Steatosis:** Fatal cases have been reported with NRTIs. Suspend treatment if clinical or laboratory findings suggest lactic acidosis or hepatotoxicity.
- **Exacerbations of Hepatitis B (HBV):** Severe acute exacerbations of HBV have been reported in co-infected patients who discontinued lamivudine. Monitor hepatic function closely in these patients.

### General Warnings
- **Pancreatitis:** Use with caution in patients with a history of pancreatitis. Treatment should be stopped immediately if clinical signs (e.g., abdominal pain) or laboratory abnormalities (e.g., elevated amylase) occur.
- **Lipoatrophy:** Treatment with zidovudine is associated with the loss of subcutaneous fat (most evident in the face and limbs). Patients should be regularly assessed; switching to a non-zidovudine regimen may be necessary.
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome:** Inflammatory responses to residual opportunistic infections (e.g., MAC, CMV, PCP, TB) may occur during the initial phase of treatment. Autoimmune disorders (e.g., Graves’ disease) may also occur.
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** Use with extreme caution in patients with pre-existing bone marrow compromise (Hb <9 g/dL or neutrophil count <1000 cells/mm³).

### Monitoring Requirements
- Frequent blood counts (CBC) are recommended, especially for patients with advanced HIV. In symptomatic patients, testing every two weeks for the first three months is advised [Australian].
- Monitor liver function tests and HBV markers in co-infected patients.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are common and may be related to the drug or the underlying HIV disease.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache, nausea, malaise, fatigue, nasal signs/symptoms, cough, diarrhoea, musculoskeletal pain, neuropathy, insomnia. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Fever, chills, vomiting, abdominal pain/cramps, dyspepsia, dizziness, depressive disorders, skin rashes, myalgia, arthralgia. |
| **Serious/Rare** | Lactic acidosis, severe anaemia, neutropenia, pancytopenia, pure red cell aplasia, pancreatitis, rhabdomyolysis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (very rare). |

- **Haematological variation:** Frequencies of neutropenia and anaemia are significantly higher in patients with advanced HIV disease or those on higher doses of zidovudine [Australian, US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Trimethoprim/Sulphamethoxazole:** Increases lamivudine exposure by ~40%. No dose adjustment is needed unless renal impairment is present.
- **Atovaquone:** May decrease the rate of zidovudine metabolism, increasing zidovudine AUC by ~31%.
- **Probenecid:** Increases zidovudine AUC by ~106% by inhibiting glucuronidation and renal excretion.
- **Valproic Acid:** Increases zidovudine AUC by ~80%.
- **Sorbitol:** Co-administration may significantly decrease lamivudine concentrations; chronic co-administration should be avoided if possible.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Stavudine:** Zidovudine may inhibit the intracellular phosphorylation of stavudine. Concomitant use is not recommended.
- **Ribavirin:** Antagonizes the antiviral activity of zidovudine and increases the risk of anaemia. Co-administration is not advised.
- **Bone Marrow Suppressants:** Agents like ganciclovir, interferon alfa, dapsone, and systemic flucytosine may increase zidovudine toxicity.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [Australian]: **Category B3.** 
    - [US]: Human data from the Antiretroviral Pregnancy Registry (APR) show no significant difference in the risk of major birth defects compared to background rates. 
    - Zidovudine is proven to reduce maternal-foetal transmission of HIV.
- **Breastfeeding:** Health authorities recommend that HIV-infected mothers do not breastfeed to avoid postnatal transmission of HIV-1. Both drugs are excreted in human breast milk.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Fixed-dose combination not recommended for children <30 kg [US] or <14 kg [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Caution is advised due to the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not recommended for patients with **CrCl <50 mL/min**. Because it is a fixed-dose combination, patients requiring dose adjustments must use the individual components.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended [US] or advised to use separate components [Australian] for patients with hepatic impairment or cirrhosis, as zidovudine clearance may be reduced.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Both are NRTIs. They are intracellularly converted to active triphosphate metabolites which compete with natural substrates for incorporation into viral DNA, causing chain termination via the HIV reverse transcriptase enzyme.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; oral bioavailability is ~86% (lamivudine) and ~64% (zidovudine).
    - **Distribution:** Low plasma protein binding (<36–38%). Zidovudine has better CNS penetration (CSF:plasma ratio ~0.6) than lamivudine (~0.12).
    - **Metabolism:** Lamivudine is primarily excreted unchanged renally. Zidovudine is primarily metabolised by the liver to an inactive glucuronide (GAZT).
    - **Elimination:** Lamivudine half-life is 5–7 hours; zidovudine half-life is ~1 hour.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CAESAR Study (NUCB3007):** A pivotal trial in 1,840 HIV-infected adults. Adding lamivudine to zidovudine-containing regimens significantly reduced the risk of HIV progression or death (Hazard Ratio 0.427; p<0.0001) and reduced mortality by 60% compared to zidovudine-containing regimens without lamivudine.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian]; store at 20°C to 25°C [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
