# Editorial Product Information: Zetia (ezetimibe)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA). 
- **Note:** While this document aims to provide a comprehensive overview, it is based solely on available source documentation. Information from other major jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not specified in the provided source material and is therefore not included in this comparison.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ezetimibe, a dietary cholesterol absorption inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Zetia** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 10 mg tablets: White to off-white, capsule-shaped, debossed with "414" on one side [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Organon LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Zetia** is indicated as an adjunct to diet in the following scenarios [US]:
- **Primary Hyperlipidemia:** In combination with a statin, or as monotherapy when additional LDL-C lowering is required or statin therapy is not possible, to reduce elevated LDL-C in adults with primary hyperlipidemia (including heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia [HeFH]).
- **Pediatric HeFH:** In combination with a statin to reduce elevated LDL-C in pediatric patients aged 10 years and older.
- **Mixed Hyperlipidemia:** In combination with fenofibrate to reduce elevated LDL-C in adults.
- **Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia (HoFH):** In combination with a statin and other LDL-C lowering therapies to reduce elevated LDL-C in adults and pediatric patients aged 10 years and older.
- **Homozygous Familial Sitosterolemia:** To reduce elevated sitosterol and campesterol levels in adults and pediatric patients aged 9 years and older.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 10 mg orally once daily, taken with or without food [US].
- **Administration with Bile Acid Sequestrants:** Administer **Zetia** ≥2 hours before or ≥4 hours after taking a bile acid sequestrant to avoid reduced absorption [US].
- **Coadministration with Other Lipid-Lowering Agents:** When used with a statin or fenofibrate, follow the dosing and safety instructions for those specific products [US].
- **Assessment of Response:** LDL-C levels should be assessed as early as 4 weeks after initiation of therapy [US].
- **Missed Dose:** The dose should be taken as soon as remembered. Patients should not double the next dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to ezetimibe or any of the inactive ingredients (including anaphylaxis, angioedema, rash, and urticaria) [US].
- When used in combination with a statin, fenofibrate, or other lipid-lowering therapies, the contraindications for those agents also apply [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Liver Enzyme Abnormalities:** Increases in serum transaminases (ALT and/or AST ≥3 × ULN) have been reported. Liver enzyme testing should be performed as clinically indicated. If persistent elevations occur, withdrawal of **Zetia** should be considered [US].
- **Skeletal Muscle Effects:** **Zetia** may cause myopathy and rhabdomyolysis. The risk is increased when taken with statins or fibrates. Patients should promptly report unexplained muscle pain, tenderness, or weakness [US].
- **Combination Therapy Risks:** Refer to the respective PI for statins or fenofibrate when used in combination, as their specific warnings (e.g., hepatic or muscular) apply [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** **Zetia** is not recommended for patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (Incidence ≥2% and > Placebo/Comparator) [US]
| Setting | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Monotherapy** | Upper respiratory tract infection, diarrhea, arthralgia, sinusitis, pain in extremity, fatigue, influenza. |
| **With Statin** | Nasopharyngitis, myalgia, upper respiratory tract infection, arthralgia, diarrhea, back pain, influenza, pain in extremity, fatigue. |

### Post-Marketing Experience
The following reactions have been identified during post-approval use (frequency not determined) [US]:
- **Blood Disorders:** Thrombocytopenia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, pancreatitis, nausea.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Hepatitis, cholelithiasis, cholecystitis, elevations in liver transaminases (including >5 x ULN).
- **Immune System:** Hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis, angioedema, rash, urticaria).
- **Musculoskeletal:** Elevated CPK, myopathy/rhabdomyolysis.
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness, paresthesia, depression, headache.
- **Skin:** Erythema multiforme.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cyclosporine:** Concomitant use increases the exposure of both ezetimibe and cyclosporine. Monitoring of cyclosporine levels is required. The effect is more pronounced in patients with severe renal insufficiency [US].
- **Fibrates:** Coadministration with fibrates (other than fenofibrate) is not recommended. Both may increase cholesterol excretion into the bile, increasing the risk of cholelithiasis [US].
- **Bile Acid Sequestrants:** (e.g., cholestyramine) These agents decrease the mean exposure (AUC) of total ezetimibe by approximately 55%, potentially reducing efficacy [US].
- **Warfarin:** If added to warfarin, the International Normalized Ratio (INR) should be appropriately monitored [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not lettered; follows current FDA labeling standards]. There are insufficient data to evaluate drug-associated risks in humans. Animal studies showed increased incidences of skeletal findings (e.g., extra ribs) at high doses (~10–150 times human exposure). **Zetia** should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There is no information on the presence of ezetimibe in human milk. It is present in rat milk. Use should be considered only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of reproductive toxicity was observed in rat studies at doses up to 7 times the human exposure [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - HeFH/HoFH: Safety and efficacy established for ages 10 years and older [US].
    - Homozygous Sitosterolemia: Safety and efficacy established for ages 9 years and older [US].
    - Not recommended for children younger than the ages specified above or for other types of hyperlipidemia [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger patients. No dosage adjustment is necessary [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is necessary [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is necessary for mild hepatic impairment. **Zetia** is NOT recommended for patients with moderate or severe (Child-Pugh B or C) impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ezetimibe localizes to the brush border of the small intestine and inhibits the sterol transporter Niemann-Pick C1-Like 1 (NPC1L1). This inhibits the absorption of cholesterol and phytosterols, reducing the delivery of intestinal cholesterol to the liver, thereby increasing LDL-receptor expression and clearance of LDL from the blood [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed and extensively conjugated to an active glucuronide. Cmax for ezetimibe is reached in 4–12 hours. Food (high-fat) increases ezetimibe Cmax by 38% but does not affect the extent of absorption [US].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>90%) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized in the small intestine and liver via glucuronide conjugation (Phase II reaction). Minimal oxidative metabolism [US].
    - **Elimination:** Both ezetimibe and its glucuronide have a half-life of approximately 22 hours. Approximately 78% is excreted in feces and 11% in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Primary Hyperlipidemia:** In 12-week trials as monotherapy, **Zetia** significantly lowered LDL-C by approximately 18% compared to placebo. When added to on-going statin therapy, it provided an additional 25% reduction in LDL-C compared to statin monotherapy [US].
- **Pediatrics (HeFH):** A trial in patients aged 10–17 showed that **Zetia** coadministered with simvastatin significantly reduced LDL-C by 15% more than simvastatin alone [US].
- **Sitosterolemia:** An 8-week trial showed a 21% reduction in sitosterol and a 24% reduction in campesterol [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at controlled room temperature: 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- Protect from moisture [US].
