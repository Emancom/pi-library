# Editorial Product Information: Zeposia (ozanimod)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**
    - **US (Food and Drug Administration - FDA)**
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating global clinical data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory requirements for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ozanimod (as hydrochloride), a sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P) receptor modulator.
- **Brand Name:** **ZEPOSIA**
- **ATC Code:** L04AA38
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Hard capsules: 0.23 mg (230 mcg), 0.46 mg (460 mcg), and 0.92 mg (920 mcg) ozanimod.
    - [Australian: Each strength is expressed as the active moiety (230/460/920 mcg), equivalent to 250 mcg, 500 mcg, and 1.00 mg ozanimod hydrochloride respectively].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - [US: Celgene Corporation (a Bristol-Myers Squibb Company)].
    - [Australian: Bristol-Myers Squibb Australia Pty Ltd].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis (RMS):** 
    - Treatment of adult patients with relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis [Australian]. 
    - [US: Specifically includes clinically isolated syndrome, relapsing-remitting disease, and active secondary progressive disease].
- **Ulcerative Colitis (UC):** 
    - Treatment of adult patients with moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis. 
    - [Australian: For patients who had an inadequate response, lost response, or were intolerant to conventional or biological therapy].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Treatment Initiation and Titration
A 7-day dose escalation is required to minimize the risk of bradycardia and atrioventricular conduction delays.
- **Days 1–4:** 0.23 mg (230 mcg) once daily.
- **Days 5–7:** 0.46 mg (460 mcg) once daily.
- **Day 8 and thereafter:** 0.92 mg (920 mcg) once daily (Maintenance Dose).

### 3.2 Missed Dose Instructions
- **Within first 2 weeks:** If a dose is missed, the 7-day titration regimen must be restarted.
- **After first 2 weeks:** Continue with the next scheduled dose. 
- [Australian: Specifically notes that if >7 consecutive days are missed (Days 15–28) or >14 days are missed (after Day 28), the titration must be reinitiated].

### 3.3 Administration
- Capsules should be swallowed whole with or without food.
- **Assessments Prior to Initiation:**
    - Baseline ECG to screen for conduction abnormalities.
    - Complete Blood Count (CBC) (within 6 months).
    - Liver Function Tests (LFTs) (within 6 months).
    - [US: Baseline skin examination for malignancies].
    - Ophthalmic assessment in patients with risk factors (e.g., diabetes, uveitis).
    - Varicella Zoster Virus (VZV) antibody testing; vaccinate antibody-negative patients at least 1 month prior to treatment.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Recent Cardiac Events (last 6 months):** Myocardial infarction, unstable angina, stroke, TIA, decompensated heart failure requiring hospitalisation, or Class III/IV heart failure.
- **Cardiac Conduction Disorders:** Second-degree AV block Type II, third-degree AV block, or sick sinus syndrome (unless a functioning pacemaker is present).
- **Respiratory:** Severe untreated sleep apnoea.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ozanimod or any excipients.
- **Drug Interactions:** Concomitant use of monoamine oxidase (MAO) inhibitors [US-listed contraindication; Australian-listed as not recommended/precaution].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infections:** Reversible sequestration of lymphocytes increases infection risk. Delay initiation in patients with active infections. Monitor for 3 months after discontinuation.
- **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML):** Opportunistic JCV infection reported with S1P modulators. Withhold/discontinue at the first sign of PML.
- **Bradyarrhythmia and AV Conduction Delays:** Transient HR reduction occurs upon initiation. Cardiology consultation is advised for patients with significant QT prolongation or those on drugs that slow heart rate.
- **Liver Injury:** Transaminase elevations occur commony. Monitor LFTs periodically. Discontinue if significant liver injury is confirmed.
- **Macular Oedema:** Increased risk, particularly in patients with diabetes or uveitis. Perform funduscopic evaluation at baseline and if vision changes occur.
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Reported rarely. Discontinue if suspected.
- **Respiratory Effects:** Dose-dependent reductions in FEV1 and FVC. Assess pulmonary function if clinically indicated.
- **Rebound Effect:** Severe exacerbation of disease (MS) has been reported after stopping S1P modulators.
- **Cutaneous Malignancies:** Increased risk of basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and melanoma. Periodic skin examinations are recommended.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nasopharyngitis, Lymphopenia [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Upper respiratory tract infections.
    - Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and Gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT) increased.
    - Hypertension and Orthostatic hypotension.
    - Urinary tract infections.
    - Back pain.
    - Headache.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Macular oedema, Herpes zoster, Hypersensitivity (rash/urticaria).
- **Serious:** Rare cases of PRES, PML, and severe liver injury have been reported in the post-marketing setting.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAO Inhibitors:** Use of MAO inhibitors (e.g., selegiline, phenelzine) with **ZEPOSIA** is contraindicated [US] or not recommended [Australian] due to potential for hypertensive crisis.
- **CYP2C8 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., gemfibrozil) increase exposure of active metabolites; co-administration is not recommended.
- **CYP2C8 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin) decrease exposure and efficacy; co-administration should be avoided.
- **Vaccination:** Avoid live attenuated vaccines during treatment and for 3 months after stopping.
- **HR-Lowering Drugs:** Caution with beta-blockers or calcium channel blockers due to additive effects on heart rate.
- **Adrenergic/Sympathomimetic Agents:** Potential to increase blood pressure; monitor closely.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [Australian: Category D].
    - [US: Animal studies show embryofoetal death and malformations at clinically relevant doses]. 
    - Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for 3 months post-discontinuation.
- **Breastfeeding:** No human data. Present in rat milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, breastfeeding is not recommended.
- **Fertility:** No apparent effect on sperm count/motility or general fertility in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Safety and effectiveness in patients <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly (>65 years):** No dose adjustment required; however, use with caution due to increased infection risk and potential for reduced organ function.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild or Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** Titrate as normal, then use a maintenance dose of 0.92 mg (920 mcg) **every other day**.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment necessary.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selectively binds to S1P receptors 1 and 5. It blocks the egress of lymphocytes from lymph nodes, reducing peripheral lymphocyte counts and preventing their migration into the CNS and intestine.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by multiple systems (CYP3A4, CYP2C8, ALDH/ADH, NAT-2, MAO-B). Two major active metabolites, CC112273 and CC1084037, account for ~90% of total activity.
    - **Half-life:** Parent drug ~21 hours; major active metabolite (CC112273) ~11 days.
    - **Excretion:** Primarily via metabolites in faeces (37%) and urine (26%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Multiple Sclerosis:** Pivotal Phase 3 trials (SUNBEAM and RADIANCE) demonstrated that **ZEPOSIA** 0.92 mg significantly reduced the Annualised Relapse Rate (ARR) by 38–48% compared to interferon beta-1a over 1–2 years.
- **Ulcerative Colitis:** The TRUENORTH study showed significant improvement in clinical remission (18% vs 6% placebo at Week 10; 37% vs 19% at Week 52) and mucosal healing.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian]; [US: 20°C to 25°C (excursions permitted 15°C to 30°C)].
- **Special Precautions:** Store in the original package.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine). Subject to additional monitoring (Black Triangle).
- **United States:** Rx Only.
