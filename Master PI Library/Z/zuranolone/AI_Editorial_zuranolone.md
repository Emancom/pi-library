# Editorial Product Information: Zuranolone

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial information is derived from the official product labeling provided by the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA). 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **zuranolone** for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Zuranolone (neuroactive steroid gamma-aminobutyric acid [GABA] A receptor positive modulator).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ZURZUVAE** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral capsules: 20 mg, 25 mg, and 30 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Biogen MA Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Postpartum Depression (PPD):** Indicated for the treatment of PPD in adults [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Recommended Dosage:** 50 mg taken orally once daily in the evening for a 14-day treatment course [US].
- **Food Requirement:** Must be administered with fat-containing food (approximately 400 to 1,000 calories, with 25% to 50% fat) to ensure adequate absorption [US].
- **Duration:** Safety and efficacy beyond 14 days in a single course have not been established [US].

### Dosing Modifications
- **CNS Depressant Effects:** If somnolence or confusion occurs during the 14-day course, the dosage may be reduced to 40 mg once daily [US].
- **Concomitant Therapy:** May be used as monotherapy or as an adjunct to stable oral antidepressant therapy [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If an evening dose is missed, the patient should skip that dose and take the next dose at the regular time the following evening. Extra capsules should not be taken to compensate for a missed dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- No contraindications are listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- **Impaired Ability to Drive or Engage in Other Potentially Hazardous Activities [US]:** **Zuranolone** causes central nervous system (CNS) depression and driving impairment. Patients must be advised not to drive or operate machinery for at least 12 hours following administration for the duration of the 14-day course. Patients may not be able to self-assess their level of impairment.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **CNS Depressant Effects:** May cause somnolence, dizziness, and confusion. Concomitant use with alcohol, benzodiazepines, opioids, or tricyclic antidepressants increases the risk of psychomotor impairment and respiratory depression [US].
- **Suicidal Thoughts and Behavior:** As with other antidepressants, patients should be monitored for clinical worsening and the emergence of suicidal thoughts, particularly in those aged 24 and younger. Discontinuation should be considered if symptoms emerge [US].
- **Embryo-fetal Toxicity:** Animal studies indicate potential fetal harm (malformations and mortality). Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for one week after the final dose [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Monitor for signs of excessive CNS depression.
- Regular assessment for suicidal ideation and behavior [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions occurring in ≥5% of patients and at a higher rate than placebo include:
- Somnolence (36% vs 6% placebo) [US]
- Dizziness (13% vs 9% placebo) [US]
- Diarrhea (6% vs 2% placebo) [US]
- Fatigue (5% vs 2% placebo) [US]
- Nasopharyngitis (9% vs 3% placebo in Study 2) [US]
- Urinary tract infection (5% vs 4% placebo) [US]

Serious adverse reactions identified in clinical trials included confusional state (1%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use increases **zuranolone** exposure. Reduce **zuranolone** dosage to 30 mg once daily [US].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** Concomitant use significantly reduces **zuranolone** exposure and may decrease efficacy. Avoid co-administration [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **CNS Depressants (e.g., Alcohol, Benzodiazepines, Opioids):** Additive psychomotor impairment and sedation. Consider dosage reduction of **zuranolone** if concomitant use is unavoidable [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No assigned letter category]. Based on animal data, **zuranolone** may cause fetal harm. A pregnancy exposure registry is available to monitor outcomes [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Zuranolone** is excreted in human milk at low levels (relative infant dose <1%). The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need and potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed increased post-implantation loss and disrupted estrous cyclicity at high doses; no adverse effects on fertility were seen at doses associated with 4x the human exposure [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical experience in PPD is limited in this population. No clinically meaningful PK differences were observed in subjects up to 81 years of age [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild (eGFR 60–89 mL/min/1.73 m²): No dose adjustment [US].
    - Moderate or Severe (eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m²): Reduce dosage to 30 mg once daily [US].
    - End-stage renal disease (eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 m²) or dialysis: Not studied [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild or Moderate (Child-Pugh A or B): No dose adjustment [US].
    - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Reduce dosage to 30 mg once daily [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A receptors. The exact mechanism in PPD is not fully elucidated but is attributed to its modulation of GABAergic neurotransmission [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is 5 to 6 hours. High-fat meals significantly increase Cmax (approx. 4.3-fold) and AUC (approx. 2-fold) [US].
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution >500 L. Plasma protein binding >99.5% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 19.7 to 24.6 hours. Excreted via urine (45%) and feces (41%), primarily as metabolites [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Efficacy was demonstrated in two randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (Study 1 and Study 2) in women with PPD meeting DSM-5 criteria for a major depressive episode [US].
- **Primary Outcome:** Both studies showed a statistically significant improvement in depressive symptoms (measured by the HAMD-17 total score) at Day 15 compared to placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at room temperature between 20°C and 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** [US: Schedule IV (CIV) controlled substance due to abuse potential and physical dependence risk].
