# Editorial Product Information: Piperacillin and Tazobactam

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of piperacillin/tazobactam for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Piperacillin sodium (penicillin-class antibacterial) and tazobactam sodium (beta-lactamase inhibitor).
- **Brand Names:** **Zosyn** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01CR05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Frozen iso-osmotic solution for intravenous injection in single-dose GALAXY containers [US]:
    - 2.25 g (2 g piperacillin / 0.25 g tazobactam) in 50 mL.
    - 3.375 g (3 g piperacillin / 0.375 g tazobactam) in 50 mL.
    - 4.5 g (4 g piperacillin / 0.5 g tazobactam) in 100 mL.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Intra-abdominal Infections:** Treatment of appendicitis (complicated by rupture or abscess) and peritonitis caused by beta-lactamase producing *E. coli* or *Bacteroides fragilis* group [US]. Approved for adults and pediatric patients ≥2 months of age [US].
- **Nosocomial Pneumonia:** Treatment of moderate to severe nosocomial pneumonia caused by beta-lactamase producing *S. aureus* and susceptible strains of *A. baumannii*, *H. influenzae*, *K. pneumoniae*, and *P. aeruginosa*. For *P. aeruginosa*, use in combination with an aminoglycoside [US]. Approved for adults and pediatric patients ≥2 months of age [US].
- **Skin and Skin Structure Infections:** Treatment of uncomplicated and complicated infections (cellulitis, abscesses, ischemic/diabetic foot infections) caused by beta-lactamase producing *S. aureus* [US-approved; adult only].
- **Female Pelvic Infections:** Treatment of postpartum endometritis or pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) caused by beta-lactamase producing *E. coli* [US-approved; adult only].
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia:** Treatment of moderate severity pneumonia caused by beta-lactamase producing *H. influenzae* [US-approved; adult only].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Standard Dosage:** 3.375 g every 6 hours (totaling 13.5 g/day) for 7 to 10 days [US].
- **Nosocomial Pneumonia:** 4.5 g every 6 hours (totaling 18 g/day) in combination with an aminoglycoside for 7 to 14 days [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer via intravenous infusion over 30 minutes [US].
- For GALAXY containers: Thaw at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) or under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C). Do not force thaw. Do not refreeze [US].

### Renal Impairment (Adults)
Dosage should be adjusted based on creatinine clearance (CrCl) [US]:
| CrCl (mL/min) | Most Indications | Nosocomial Pneumonia |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| >40 | 3.375 g q6h | 4.5 g q6h |
| 20 to 40 | 2.25 g q6h | 3.375 g q6h |
| <20 | 2.25 g q8h | 2.25 g q6h |
| **Hemodialysis** | 2.25 g q12h + 0.75 g post-dialysis | 2.25 g q8h + 0.75 g post-dialysis |
| **CAPD** | 2.25 g q12h | 2.25 g q8h |

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** History of allergic reactions to any penicillin, cephalosporin, or beta-lactamase inhibitor [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal anaphylactic reactions have been reported. Discontinue immediately if an allergic reaction occurs [US].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), DRESS, and AGEP. Discontinue for progressive rashes [US].
- **Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis (HLH):** Reported in both pediatric and adult patients. Suspected cases require immediate discontinuation [US].
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Observed as an independent risk factor for renal failure in critically ill patients, with delayed recovery compared to other beta-lactams. Monitor renal function closely [US].
- **Hematologic Effects:** Bleeding manifestations, leukopenia, and neutropenia may occur, especially with prolonged therapy (>21 days). Periodic assessment of hematopoietic function is recommended [US].
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects:** Neuromuscular excitability or seizures may occur, particularly in patients with renal impairment or history of seizure disorders receiving high doses [US].
- **Rhabdomyolysis:** If signs (muscle pain, dark urine, elevated CPK) appear, discontinue the drug [US].
- **Electrolyte Effects:** Contains 2.84 mEq (65 mg) of sodium per gram of piperacillin. Monitor for hypokalemia in patients with low potassium reserves [US].
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterials; can range from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (Incidence >5%)
- Diarrhea, constipation, nausea, headache, and insomnia [US].

### Frequency in Clinical Trials (Adult Monotherapy)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (11.3%), Constipation (7.7%), Nausea (6.9%), Vomiting (3.3%), Dyspepsia (3.3%), Abdominal pain (1.3%) [US].
- **Nervous System:** Headache (7.7%), Insomnia (6.6%) [US].
- **Skin:** Rash (4.2%), Pruritus (3.1%) [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN, HLH, rhabdomyolysis, interstitial nephritis, seizures, and pancytopenia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Aminoglycosides:** Piperacillin can inactivate aminoglycosides *in vitro* and *in vivo* (notably in end-stage renal disease). Avoid Y-site co-administration with tobramycin. Amikacin and gentamicin are compatible via Y-site only under specific concentrations [US].
- **Vancomycin:** Co-administration may increase the incidence of acute kidney injury (AKI). Monitor renal function [US].
- **Probenecid:** Increases the half-life of piperacillin (by 21%) and tazobactam (by 71%) by inhibiting renal secretion [US].
- **Anticoagulants:** May affect the coagulation system or thrombocyte function. Monitor coagulation parameters more frequently [US].
- **Vecuronium/Non-depolarizing Muscle Relaxants:** Piperacillin may prolong neuromuscular blockade [US].
- **Methotrexate:** Clearance may be reduced, increasing the risk of methotrexate toxicity [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: No lettered category assigned. Human data are insufficient to determine risk for major birth defects or miscarriage. Animal studies in rats/mice during organogenesis at doses 1–3 times the human dose showed no structural abnormalities, though some fetotoxicity occurred at maternally toxic doses.
- **Breastfeeding:** Piperacillin is excreted in human milk. Tazobactam concentrations in milk have not been studied. Use should consider the developmental benefits of breastfeeding and the mother's clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in rat studies at doses similar to the maximum recommended human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for intra-abdominal infections and nosocomial pneumonia in patients ≥2 months of age. Dosing for patients <40 kg depends on age (2–9 months vs. >9 months) and indication [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety compared to younger patients, but dosage must be adjusted for renal impairment. Geriatric patients may be more sensitive to sodium loading [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Significant dose reduction required for CrCl ≤40 mL/min and dialysis patients [US]. (See Section 3).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment warranted in patients with hepatic cirrhosis [US].
- **Cystic Fibrosis:** Associated with increased incidence of fever and rash [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Piperacillin is a bactericidal antibiotic that inhibits septum formation and cell wall synthesis. Tazobactam is a beta-lactamase inhibitor (Molecular class A) that protects piperacillin from degradation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Approximately 30% protein bound. Widely distributed to tissues (intestinal mucosa, gallbladder, lung, etc.). Low CSF penetration in non-inflamed meninges [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Piperacillin is metabolized to a minor active metabolite; tazobactam to a single inactive metabolite [US].
  - **Elimination:** Primarily renal via glomerular filtration and tubular secretion. 68% of piperacillin and 80% of tazobactam excreted unchanged in urine [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Efficacy is most closely linked to the time plasma concentrations remain above the minimum inhibitory concentration (T > MIC) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Trials:** Phase 3 trials (n=2621) established baseline safety. Specific trials for nosocomial pneumonia compared Zosyn + aminoglycoside to imipenem/cilastatin + aminoglycoside, showing comparable safety and efficacy profiles [US].
- **Pediatric Trials:** A prospective trial in 542 pediatric patients (2–12 years) with intra-abdominal infections supported the safety and efficacy of an 8-hourly dosing regimen [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Frozen State:** Store at or below -20°C (-4°F) in original carton until use [US].
- **Thawed State:** Thawed solution is stable for 21 days under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C) or 24 hours at room temperature (20°C to 25°C). Do not refreeze [US].
