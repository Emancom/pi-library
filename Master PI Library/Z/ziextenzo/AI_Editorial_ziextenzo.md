# Editorial Product Information: Ziextenzo (pegfilgrastim)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and the United States (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **Ziextenzo** for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory requirements across these regions.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the local approved PI before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Pegfilgrastim. [US: pegfilgrastim-bmez].
- **Chemical Class:** Pegylated recombinant human granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Ziextenzo**.
- **ATC Code:** L03AA13.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile, preservative-free solution for injection in a single-dose pre-filled syringe: 6 mg / 0.6 mL.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian]; Sandoz Inc [US].
- **Description:** **Ziextenzo** is a biosimilar to the reference product, **Neulasta**. It consists of filgrastim (produced via *E. coli*) with a 20,000 dalton polyethylene glycol (PEG) molecule covalently bound to the N-terminal methionine residue.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reduction of Febrile Neutropenia:** Indicated to decrease the incidence of infection, as manifested by febrile neutropenia, in patients with non-myeloid malignancies receiving myelosuppressive anti-cancer drugs associated with a clinically significant incidence of febrile neutropenia.
- **Acute Radiation Syndrome [US-approved]:** To increase survival in patients acutely exposed to myelosuppressive doses of radiation (Hematopoietic Subsyndrome of Acute Radiation Syndrome).
- **Limitations of Use:** Not indicated for the mobilisation of peripheral blood progenitor cells (PBPC) for haematopoietic stem cell transplantation.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Cancer Chemotherapy:** 6 mg administered once per chemotherapy cycle.
- **Acute Radiation Syndrome [US]:** Two doses of 6 mg each, administered one week apart. The first dose should be given as soon as possible after suspected or confirmed exposure.

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** Administer approximately 24 hours after cytotoxic chemotherapy. **Ziextenzo** should not be administered in the period between 14 days before and 24 hours after administration of cytotoxic chemotherapy.
- **Preparation:** Allow the syringe to reach room temperature (15–30 minutes) before injection. Visually inspect for particulate matter or discolouration; do not use if observed. Do not shake.
- **Device:** The pre-filled syringe is designed for manual subcutaneous injection and includes an automatic needle guard.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- [US]: If a dose is missed, patients should contact their healthcare provider to discuss the timing of the next dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of serious hypersensitivity to pegfilgrastim, filgrastim, *E. coli*-derived proteins, or any component of the product. Reactions may include anaphylaxis.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Splenic Rupture:** Rare but potentially fatal cases have occurred. Evaluate patients reporting left upper abdominal or shoulder pain for splenomegaly or rupture.
- **Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS):** Reported in patients receiving G-CSF. Evaluate patients who develop fever, lung infiltrates, or respiratory distress. Discontinue if ARDS occurs.
- **Sickle Cell Crisis:** Severe and sometimes fatal crises have been associated with pegfilgrastim use in patients with sickle cell trait or disease. Discontinue use if a crisis occurs.
- **Glomerulonephritis:** Reported in patients receiving pegfilgrastim; typically resolves upon drug withdrawal. Monitor via urinalysis.
- **Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS) and Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML):** Associated with pegfilgrastim used in conjunction with chemotherapy/radiotherapy in breast and lung cancer patients. Monitor for signs and symptoms.
- **Capillary Leak Syndrome (CLS):** Characterised by hypotension, hypoalbuminemia, oedema, and hemoconcentration. Requires close monitoring and standard symptomatic treatment.
- **Aortitis:** May present with fever, abdominal pain, or increased inflammatory markers. Consider this diagnosis if symptoms occur without a known etiology. Discontinue if suspected.
- **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) [Australian]:** Rarely reported and can be life-threatening. Treatment must not be restarted if SJS occurs.
- **Leukocytosis:** White blood cell (WBC) counts of $\ge 100 \times 10^9/L$ have been observed. Monitoring of CBC is recommended.
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Monitor platelet counts.
- **Nuclear Imaging Interference:** Increased bone marrow haematopoietic activity may cause transient positive bone imaging changes.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Medullary bone pain (reported in 26%–31% of patients).
- **Common (1–10%):** Pain in extremity [US], myalgia, arthralgia, headache, nausea, fatigue, alopecia, diarrhoea, pyrexia, and musculoskeletal pain.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Splenic rupture, ARDS, serious allergic reactions (anaphylaxis), sickle cell crisis, glomerulonephritis, CLS, and SJS [Australian].
- **Laboratory Changes:** Spontaneously reversible elevations in LDH, alkaline phosphatase, and uric acid (mild-to-moderate) are common sequelae of bone marrow expansion.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Lithium:** May potentiate the release of neutrophils; although not specifically investigated, pharmacodynamic interaction is possible.
- **Chemotherapy:** Due to the sensitivity of rapidly dividing myeloid cells, do not use in the 24 hours following chemotherapy.
- **Bone Imaging:** Increased haematopoietic activity may interfere with bone scan results [US, Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [Australian: Category B3] (Drugs which have been taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the human fetus having been observed. Studies in animals have shown evidence of an increased occurrence of fetal damage, the significance of which is considered uncertain in humans).
    - [US]: Data are insufficient to establish a drug-associated risk. Animal studies in rabbits showed increased embryolethality and spontaneous abortions at 4 times the human dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if pegfilgrastim is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution; the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug.
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility were observed in rat studies at doses up to 13x [Australian] or 9x [US] the recommended human dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Safety and effectiveness are established for both cancer and ARDS indications.
    - [US]: For cancer patients $\ge 45$ kg, the dose is 6 mg. For those $< 45$ kg or for ARS, weight-based dosing is required; however, **Ziextenzo** syringes do not allow for doses less than 6 mg. 
    - [Australian]: Pharmacokinetics studied in sarcoma patients (0–21 years). Most common adverse reaction was bone pain.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between patients $\ge 65$ years and younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required. Studies show that renal dysfunction, including end-stage renal disease, has no effect on pharmacokinetics.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though not expected to affect pharmacokinetics.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A colony-stimulating factor that binds to specific cell surface receptors on haematopoietic cells, stimulating the proliferation, differentiation, and activation of neutrophils.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Median $T_{max}$ is 24 hours in cancer patients (range 22–120 hours).
    - **Distribution:** Limited to the plasma compartment.
    - **Elimination:** Non-linear, neutrophil-mediated clearance. Serum concentration declines rapidly at the onset of neutrophil recovery. Half-life ranges from 15 to 80 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Three randomised, double-blind trials in patients with solid tumours.
- **Results:** **Ziextenzo** demonstrated equivalence to the reference product (**Neulasta**) in reducing the duration of severe neutropenia (DSN). In Study 3, the incidence of febrile neutropenia was significantly lower in the pegfilgrastim group (1%) compared to placebo (17%).
- **ARS [US data]:** Approval based on animal models (rhesus macaques) where pegfilgrastim significantly increased 60-day survival (91%) compared to control (48%).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Freezing:** Do not freeze. If accidentally frozen once, thaw in the refrigerator. Discard if frozen more than once.
- **Room Temperature Exposure:** May be exposed to room temperature (20°C to 35°C) for a single period of up to 120 hours. Discard if left out longer.
- **Handling:** Do not shake.
