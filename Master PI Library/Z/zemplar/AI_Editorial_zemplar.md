# Editorial Product Information: Paricalcitol

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States**: US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for Zemplar (paricalcitol) capsules.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory information into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Paricalcitol (a synthetic vitamin D$_2$ analog).
- **Brand Names**: Zemplar [US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources (Note: H05BX02).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Soft gelatin capsules: 1 mcg and 2 mcg [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: AbbVie Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Hyperparathyroidism (SHPT)**: Indicated for the prevention and treatment of SHPT associated with:
    - Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) Stages 3 and 4 (Adults and paediatric patients $\ge$ 10 years) [US].
    - CKD Stage 5 in patients undergoing hemodialysis (HD) or peritoneal dialysis (PD) (Adults and paediatric patients $\ge$ 10 years) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adults: CKD Stages 3 and 4
Dosing is determined by baseline intact parathyroid hormone (iPTH) levels. Capsules may be administered daily or three times per week (TIW), but no more frequently than every other day [US].
- **Baseline iPTH $\le$ 500 pg/mL**: 1 mcg daily OR 2 mcg TIW.
- **Baseline iPTH > 500 pg/mL**: 2 mcg daily OR 4 mcg TIW.

### Adults: CKD Stage 5
Dosing is based on the following formula [US]:
- **Initial Dose (mcg)** = Baseline iPTH (pg/mL) / 80.
- **Frequency**: Administer orally TIW (no more frequently than every other day).
- **Requirement**: Initiate only after baseline serum calcium is $\le$ 9.5 mg/dL to minimise hypercalcemia risk.

### Paediatric Patients (Ages 10 to 16 Years)
- **CKD Stages 3 and 4**: 1 mcg TIW (no more frequently than every other day).
- **CKD Stage 5**: Initial dose (mcg) = Baseline iPTH (pg/mL) / 120, administered TIW.

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Dose Adjustments**: Should be individualized based on iPTH, serum calcium, and phosphorus. Titration intervals typically range from 2 to 4 weeks [US].
- **CKD 3/4 (Adults)**: Increase/decrease by 1 mcg daily or 2 mcg TIW depending on whether iPTH reduction is $<30\%$ (increase) or $>60\%$ (decrease/withhold).
- **CKD 5 (Adults)**: Titrate using the formula: Dose (mcg) = most recent iPTH / 80. If serum calcium is elevated, reduce dose by 2–4 mcg.
- **Missed Dose**: Not specified in available sources.

### Administration Instructions
- Capsules may be taken without regard to food [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Evidence of hypercalcemia [US].
- Evidence of vitamin D toxicity [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypercalcemia**: Excessive administration can lead to over-suppression of PTH, hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria, hyperphosphatemia, and adynamic bone disease. Acute hypercalcemia may exacerbate cardiac arrhythmias and seizures [US].
- **Digitalis Toxicity**: Hypercalcemia of any cause potentiates digitalis toxicity. Use caution when co-prescribing paricalcitol with digitalis compounds [US].
- **Laboratory Monitoring**: Monitor serum calcium, phosphorus, and iPTH closely. Initially, monitoring should occur every two weeks for 3 months, then monthly for 3 months, and every 3 months thereafter [US].
- **Estimated GFR (eGFR)**: Paricalcitol may increase serum creatinine and consequently decrease eGFR in pre-dialysis patients [US].
- **Aluminum Toxicity**: Avoid excessive use of aluminum-containing compounds (e.g., antacids, phosphate binders) as increased blood aluminum levels and bone toxicity may occur [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data (CKD Stages 3, 4, and 5 Adults)
- **Common ($\ge$ 5% and $>$ placebo)**: Diarrhea, nasopharyngitis, dizziness, vomiting, hypertension, hypersensitivity, nausea, and edema [US].
- **Other Reported Effects ($\ge$ 2%)**: Vertigo, abdominal discomfort, constipation, viral infection, fungal infection, arthritis, muscle spasms, headache, and rash [US].

### Frequency Data (Paediatric 10–16 Years)
- **Reported Effects**: Rhinitis (17%), nausea, conjunctivitis, micturition urgency, and asthma (all 6% in Stage 3/4) [US]. Hypercalcemia was reported in 23% of Stage 5 paediatric patients.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Postmarketing Experience**: Angioedema (including laryngeal edema) and increased blood creatinine [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors**: (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, atazanavir, grapefruit juice). Co-administration increases paricalcitol exposure. In clinical studies, ketoconazole doubled paricalcitol AUC. Dose adjustment may be required [US].
- **Bile Acid Sequestrants/Mineral Oil**: Cholestyramine or mineral oil may reduce intestinal absorption of paricalcitol. Paricalcitol should be taken at least 1 hour before or 4 to 6 hours after these agents [US].
- **Thiazide Diuretics/Calcium Supplements**: Concomitant use may increase the risk of hypercalcemia [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - [US]: Data are insufficient to inform drug-associated risks. Animal studies showed slightly increased embryofetal loss at doses causing maternal toxicity.
- **Breastfeeding**: 
    - [US]: Not recommended. Animal data show paricalcitol/metabolites are present in milk; risk of hypercalcemia in the infant exists.
- **Fertility**: No effect on fertility was observed in rat studies at doses up to 13 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy established in patients 10 to 16 years of age. Use in patients $<10$ years has not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use**: No overall differences in safety or efficacy observed between patients $\ge 65$ and younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No dose adjustment is required for patients with mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh). Severe impairment has not been evaluated [US].
- **Renal Impairment**: No special dose adjustments are required beyond the standard CKD stage-based protocols [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Paricalcitol is a synthetic vitamin D analog that binds to and activates the Vitamin D Receptor (VDR). This results in the selective activation of vitamin D responsive pathways, leading to the inhibition of PTH synthesis and secretion [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Reduces iPTH while increasing serum calcium and phosphorus. 
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Bioavailability is ~72% to 86%. Food does not significantly affect AUC but delays T$_{max}$ by ~2 hours [US].
    - **Distribution**: Extensively protein-bound ($\ge$ 99.8%).
    - **Metabolism**: Extensively metabolized via CYP3A4, UGT1A4, and mitochondrial CYP24 [US].
    - **Elimination**: Primarily hepatobiliary excretion (70% in feces, 18% in urine). Elimination half-life is 14 to 20 hours in CKD patients [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CKD Stages 3 and 4**: Three 24-week, double-blind trials (n=220) showed 91% of paricalcitol-treated patients achieved two consecutive $\ge$ 30% reductions in iPTH compared to 13% for placebo [US].
- **CKD Stage 5**: A 12-week study (n=88) showed 88% of paricalcitol patients achieved two consecutive weekly $\ge$ 30% reductions in iPTH compared to 13% for placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage**: Store at 25°C (77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life**: Not specified in available sources (refer to packaging).
