# Editorial Product Information: Zafirlukast

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of zafirlukast for healthcare professionals, synthesising data from the available official Product Information. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the US (FDA) source documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Zafirlukast (a synthetic, selective peptide leukotriene receptor antagonist).
- **Chemical Class:** Leukotriene receptor antagonist (LTRA).
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Generic: Zafirlukast).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - **Oral Tablets:** 10 mg and 20 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Strides Pharma Science Limited [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Asthma:** Indicated for the prophylaxis and chronic treatment of asthma in adults and children aged 5 years and older [US].
- **Important Limitation:** Not indicated for the reversal of bronchospasm in acute asthma attacks, including status asthmaticus [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children $\ge$ 12 Years)
- **Standard Dose:** 20 mg twice daily [US].

### Paediatric Dosing (Children 5 to 11 Years)
- **Standard Dose:** 10 mg twice daily [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Effect:** Zafirlukast must be taken at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after meals, as food significantly reduces bioavailability by approximately 40% [US].
- **Consistency:** Treatment should be continued regularly, even during symptom-free periods.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to zafirlukast or any of its inactive ingredients [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with hepatic impairment, including hepatic cirrhosis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Cases of life-threatening hepatic failure, fulminant hepatitis, and liver injury have been reported. Physicians should consider periodic serum transaminase testing, although this may not prevent serious injury. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if liver dysfunction is suspected (e.g., right upper quadrant pain, nausea, jaundice, flu-like symptoms). Patients with identified hepatic dysfunction must not be re-exposed to the drug [US].
- **Neuropsychiatric Events:** Reported in adults and children. Events include insomnia and depression. Prescribers should monitor for behavioral changes or mood disturbances [US].
- **Eosinophilic Conditions:** In rare cases, patients may present with systemic eosinophilia, eosinophilic pneumonia, or clinical features of vasculitis consistent with Churg-Strauss syndrome, often following reduction or withdrawal of oral steroid therapy [US].
- **Acute Bronchospasm:** Zafirlukast is not a bronchodilator and should not be used as rescue medication for acute episodes [US].

### Serious and Post-marketing Adverse Effects
- **Hepatobiliary:** Symptomatic hepatitis, hyperbilirubinemia, and rare cases of hepatic failure [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Urticaria, angioedema, and rashes (with or without blistering) [US].
- **Hematologic:** Agranulocytosis, bleeding, and bruising [US].
- **Musculoskeletal:** Arthralgia and myalgia [US].
- **Infection Risk:** In clinical trials, patients over 55 years of age experienced an increased frequency of infections (7.0% vs 2.9% for placebo), primarily respiratory in nature [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Coadministration results in a significant increase in prothrombin time (PT/INR) by approximately 35%. Prothrombin times should be monitored closely and anticoagulant doses adjusted accordingly [US].
- **Theophylline:** Coadministration may decrease zafirlukast plasma concentrations by approximately 30%. Rare reports of increased theophylline levels have occurred [US].
- **Aspirin:** Concurrent use may increase zafirlukast plasma levels by approximately 45% [US].
- **Erythromycin:** Coadministration decreases zafirlukast bioavailability and plasma levels by approximately 40% [US].
- **Fluconazole:** Increases zafirlukast plasma levels by approximately 58%. Use caution with other moderate/strong CYP2C9 inhibitors [US].
- **CYP3A4/2C9:** Zafirlukast inhibits CYP3A4 and CYP2C9 isoenzymes *in vitro*. Appropriate clinical monitoring is recommended when coadministering with substrates of these enzymes (e.g., cyclosporin, phenytoin, carbamazepine) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category B [US]:** No teratogenicity observed in animal studies at high doses. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled trials in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Zafirlukast is excreted in breast milk. Due to the potential for tumorigenicity shown in animal studies, zafirlukast should not be administered to nursing mothers [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment of fertility or reproduction was seen in rat studies at doses up to 2000 mg/kg [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children $\ge$ 5 years. Safety and efficacy in children < 5 years have not been established. Growth effects in children have not been determined [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance is reduced in patients $\ge$ 65 years, resulting in a 2- to 3-fold increase in $C_{max}$ and AUC. While generally well-tolerated, there is an increased risk of infection in patients > 55 years [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated due to significantly reduced clearance and increased risk of hepatotoxicity [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients with renal impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Zafirlukast is a selective and competitive receptor antagonist of leukotriene $D_4$ ($LTD_4$) and $E_4$ ($LTE_4$). It inhibits the inflammatory processes (edema, smooth muscle constriction) that contribute to asthma symptoms [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma concentrations reached in 3 hours. Bioavailability is reduced by food [US].
  - **Distribution:** > 99% protein bound (predominantly albumin). Minimal blood-brain barrier penetration [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized via CYP2C9 to hydroxylated metabolites [US].
  - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted in feces via biliary pathways (approx. 90%); urinary excretion accounts for 10%. Mean terminal half-life is approximately 10 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult/Adolescent Trials:** Three 13-week double-blind trials in 1380 patients ($\ge$ 12 years) demonstrated that 20 mg twice daily significantly improved daytime asthma symptoms, nighttime awakenings, and FEV1 compared to placebo [US].
- **Paediatric Trials:** Approval for children 5–11 years was based on extrapolation from adult efficacy data and pharmacokinetic comparisons showing similar drug disposition [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light and moisture. Store in the original air-tight container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:**
  - **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription drug) [US].
  - **Other regions:** Not specified in available sources.
