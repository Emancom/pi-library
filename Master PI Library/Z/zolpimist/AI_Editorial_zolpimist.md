# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of levocarnitine injection information, intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official PI or prescribing information in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].
- **Note:** Indications for other jurisdictions are not specified in available sources.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Initial Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Doses should be given every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Monitoring:** Daily doses generally range up to 50 mg/kg; however, doses as high as 300 mg/kg have been used [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- The formulation does not contain preservatives; discard any unused portion [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].
- Specific contraindications for other jurisdictions are not specified in available sources.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **General Precaution (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal impairment and may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine/TMA and trimethylamine-N-oxide/TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Effects (Clinical Trials):** Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed. Gastritis and body odor are less frequent [US].
- **ESRD Population (Frequency ≥5%):**
    - **General:** Abdominal pain (5-21%), accidental injury (8-12%), asthenia (8-12%), chest pain (6-15%), fever (5-12%), infection (10-24%), injection site reaction (27-38%), pain (21-35%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18-21%), hypotension (3-19%), tachycardia (5-9%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9-35%), vomiting (9-21%), nausea (5-12%) [US].
    - **Neurological:** Headache (3-37%), dizziness (10-18%), paresthesia (3-12%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15-27%), cough increase (10-18%), dyspnea (3-11%) [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations, the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models at 3.8 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by body weight (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical documents do not specify unique dose adjustments, though general caution is advised in the elderly due to potential decreased organ function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing for ESRD patients is specifically addressed (see Section 3). Oral levocarnitine is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with organic acidemias, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and gamma-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg thrice weekly post-dialysis showed that trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations reached 190 ± 55 µmol/L by 8 weeks (baseline: 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L). While carnitine levels increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Stability:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL at room temperature [US].

- **Legal Classification:**
