# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory data into a clinically focused reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines and official product labelling before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (Generic name); chemical class: carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Administered intravenously. For metabolic disorders, may be given as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
    - In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours [US].
    - Frequency: Administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - Daily maintenance doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - Adjustments: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately [US].
- **Seizures:** Reports exist of seizures occurring in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been noted [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality) include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), chest pain (12%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), abdominal pain (9%), asthenia (9%), fever (7%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), dyspepsia (5%), anorexia (5%) [US].
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), rhinitis (9%), dyspnea (7%) [US].
- **Other:** Hypercalcemia (9%), anemia (6%), leg cramps (4%), pruritus (5%) [US].

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity [US].
- Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly. INR monitoring is recommended after starting levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound; therefore, displacement interactions are unlikely [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/Not specified]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing humans are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased levocarnitine concentration in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), which applies to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific dose adjustments for ESRD patients are required (see Section 3). The risk of metabolite accumulation with **oral** forms is noted for patients with renal failure [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15% to 16% after correcting for endogenous levels [US].
- **ESRD Studies:** Efficacy has been demonstrated in increasing carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients; however, the impact of supplementation on specific clinical outcomes or signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of opened vials as the formulation is preservative-free [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
