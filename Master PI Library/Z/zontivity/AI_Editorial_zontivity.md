# Editorial Product Information: Vorapaxar

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Vorapaxar (present as vorapaxar sulfate).
- **Chemical Class:** Tricyclic himbacine-derived selective inhibitor of platelet aggregation; protease-activated receptor-1 (PAR-1) antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **ZONTIVITY** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 2.08 mg vorapaxar (equivalent to 2.5 mg vorapaxar sulfate) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** WraSer Pharmaceuticals, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reduction of Thrombotic Cardiovascular Events:** Indicated in patients with a history of myocardial infarction (MI) or peripheral arterial disease (PAD). It has been demonstrated to reduce the rate of a combined endpoint including cardiovascular death, MI, stroke, and urgent coronary revascularization [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One 2.08 mg tablet orally once daily [US].
- **Coadministration:** Should be used in combination with aspirin and/or clopidogrel according to their respective indications or standard of care. Clinical experience as a monotherapy or with other antiplatelet agents is limited [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** May be taken with or without food [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Treatment Duration:** In clinical trials, the median treatment duration was 2.3 years, with some patients treated for over 3 years [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **History of Cerebrovascular Events:** Contraindicated in patients with a history of stroke, transient ischemic attack (TIA), or intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) due to an increased risk of ICH [US].
- **Active Bleeding:** Contraindicated in patients with active pathological bleeding, such as ICH or peptic ulcer [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Bleeding Risk [US]:** 
    - **ZONTIVITY** increases the risk of bleeding, including ICH and fatal bleeding. 
    - Contraindicated in patients with a history of stroke, TIA, or ICH.
    - Antiplatelet agents in general increase bleeding risk; clinicians should monitor for signs of hemorrhage.
- **General Bleeding Risk:** Risk is proportional to the patient's underlying risk. Factors include older age, low body weight, reduced renal/hepatic function, and history of bleeding disorders.
- **Concomitant Medications:** Avoid use with anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin) or fibrinolytic therapy due to increased bleeding potential [US].
- **Irreversibility:** There is no known reversal agent. Due to a long half-life, significant platelet inhibition remains for approximately 4 weeks after discontinuation [US].
- **CYP3A Interactions:** Avoid concomitant use with strong CYP3A inhibitors or inducers (see Section 7) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reaction is bleeding.

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (ZONTIVITY vs. Placebo) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **GUSTO Moderate or Severe Bleeding** | 3.0% vs. 2.0% [US] |
| **Clinically Significant Bleeding** | 13.4% vs. 9.5% [US] |
| **Anemia** | 5.0% vs. 4.0% [US] |
| **Depression** | 2.4% vs. 2.1% [US] |
| **Rashes/Exanthemas** | 2.2% vs. 2.0% [US] |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Intracranial hemorrhage (0.4%) and fatal bleeding (0.2%) [US].
- **Other Effects:** Iron deficiency, retinopathy/retinal disorders, and diplopia/oculomotor disturbances were reported at rates <2% but significantly higher than placebo [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir) increase vorapaxar exposure. Concomitant use should be avoided [US].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine, St. John's Wort, phenytoin) decrease vorapaxar exposure, potentially reducing efficacy. Concomitant use should be avoided [US].
- **Anticoagulants:** Avoid use with warfarin or other anticoagulants due to increased bleeding risk [US].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** Vorapaxar is a weak inhibitor of intestinal P-gp; however, no dose adjustment is required for P-gp substrates like digoxin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified in letter format (PLR-aligned). 
    - **Recommendation:** Discontinue vorapaxar when pregnancy is detected. Use of alternative therapy with a shorter duration of action is advised due to the long half-life of vorapaxar [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on presence in human milk. Secretion into rat milk is confirmed. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions (bleeding) in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in animal studies at exposures up to 67 times the human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger patients. However, as age is a general risk factor for bleeding, caution is advised [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required in patients with any degree of renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild or moderate impairment. Use is **not recommended** in patients with severe hepatic impairment due to increased inherent bleeding risk [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reversible antagonist of PAR-1 on platelets. It inhibits thrombin-induced and TRAP-induced platelet aggregation. While binding is reversible, the extremely long half-life makes the effect effectively irreversible [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Achieves ≥80% inhibition of TRAP-induced aggregation within one week. Inhibition of ~50% persists 4 weeks after cessation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** ~100% absolute bioavailability. Peak concentration (Tmax) at 1 hour [US].
    - **Distribution:** Vd ~424 L; highly protein bound (≥99%), primarily to albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 and CYP2J2. Major active metabolite is M20 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily via feces (84%). Effective half-life is 3–4 days; terminal elimination half-life is approximately 8 days [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **TRA 2°P TIMI 50:** A multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled study (n=26,449). 
    - **Outcome:** In patients with history of MI or PAD (excluding those with prior stroke/TIA), vorapaxar significantly reduced the primary composite endpoint of CV death, MI, stroke, and urgent coronary revascularization (10.1% vs 11.8% in placebo; HR 0.83; p<0.001) [US].
    - **Safety Note:** Trial participants with a history of stroke or TIA experienced a significantly higher rate of ICH, leading to the contraindication in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Bottles/Blisters:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Store in the original package. Keep the bottle tightly closed and do not remove the desiccant to protect from moisture [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison schedule/legal classification not specified in standard format, but restricted to prescription use [US].
