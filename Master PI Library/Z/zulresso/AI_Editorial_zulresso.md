# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in the transport of long-chain fatty acids.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism leading to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Standard Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours).
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response [US].

### Dosing in ESRD on Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Preparation:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before use.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Contraindications:** None known at this time [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These may occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Metabolites):** While this injection is used in renal failure, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (frequency ≥5%) include:
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%), arrhythmia (2%) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), abdominal pain (9%), nausea (8%), vomiting (15%) [US].
- **Neurological/Nervous:** Headache (22%), dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), insomnia (4%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%) [US].
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), fever (7%), asthenia (9%), flu syndrome (25%) [US].
- **Other:** Body odor and gastritis have been reported less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels regularly after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly required [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight, similar to adults (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. No dose adjustment for the injection was specified for other stages of renal impairment, though caution is advised regarding oral metabolite accumulation in severe renal failure [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine helps clear accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following intravenous administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Data:** In ESRD patients, a dose of 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis resulted in a significant increase in trough levocarnitine levels (from ~21 µmol/L baseline to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks) [US].
- **Dose-Response:** A dose-ranging study (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed a dose-proportional increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in dialysis patients, the specific effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) has not been definitively determined in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** As the formulation contains no preservatives, any unused portion of a single-dose vial must be discarded.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL at room temperature [US].
