# Editorial Product Information: Zejula (Niraparib)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates information from the following Product Information (PI) documents:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Zejula (niraparib) Product Information
    - **US (FDA)**: Zejula (niraparib) Prescribing Information
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data from multiple jurisdictions into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI. Clinicians must refer to the specific approved PI within their jurisdiction for prescribing purposes.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Niraparib (as tosilate monohydrate).
- **Chemical Class**: Poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name**: **Zejula**.
- **ATC Code**: L01XK02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**:
    - **Capsules**: 100 mg [Australian].
    - **Tablets**: 100 mg [Australian, US]; 200 mg [US]; 300 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer**: GlaxoSmithKline (GSK).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **First-Line Maintenance Treatment**:
    - For the maintenance treatment of adult patients with advanced high-grade [Australian] or epithelial [US] ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer who are in response (complete or partial) following first-line platinum-based chemotherapy.
    - **Note**: The US approval is restricted to patients whose cancer is associated with homologous recombination deficiency (HRD)-positive status, defined by either a deleterious/suspected deleterious *BRCA* mutation and/or genomic instability [US-approved; not specified in Australian indication text].
- **Recurrent Maintenance Treatment**:
    - **Relapsed Disease**: For the maintenance treatment of adult patients with platinum-sensitive relapsed high-grade serous [Australian] epithelial [US] ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer who are in response (complete or partial) to platinum-based chemotherapy.
    - **Note**: The US approval for recurrent maintenance is restricted to patients with deleterious or suspected deleterious germline *BRCA*-mutated (gBRCAmut) status [US-approved; not specified in Australian indication text].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **First-Line Ovarian Cancer Maintenance**:
    - **Body weight <77 kg OR baseline platelet count <150,000/µL**: 200 mg once daily.
    - **Body weight ≥77 kg AND baseline platelet count ≥150,000/µL**: 300 mg once daily.
- **Recurrent Ovarian Cancer Maintenance**: 300 mg once daily.

### Administration Instructions
- Take at approximately the same time each day. 
- Bedtime administration may help manage nausea.
- Swallow whole; do not chew, crush, or split [US].
- May be taken with or without food.

### Missed Dose and Vomiting
- If a dose is missed, the patient should take the next dose at the regularly scheduled time. 
- If a patient vomits, an additional dose should not be taken.

### Duration of Treatment
- Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to niraparib or any excipients [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: Contraindicated during treatment and for one month after the final dose [Australian; US lists as a warning rather than a formal contraindication].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelodysplastic Syndrome/Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (MDS/AML)**: Cases, some fatal, have been reported. Most patients had received prior platinum chemotherapy or other DNA-damaging agents. Discontinue **Zejula** permanently if MDS/AML is confirmed.
- **Bone Marrow Suppression**: Thrombocytopenia, anaemia, and neutropenia are very common. Do not start treatment until the patient has recovered from prior chemotherapy-induced haematologic toxicity (≤ Grade 1).
    - **Monitoring**: Weekly Complete Blood Counts (CBC) for the first month, then monthly for the next 11 months, and periodically thereafter.
- **Hypertension and Cardiovascular Effects**: Hypertension, including hypertensive crisis, has occurred. Pre-existing hypertension must be adequately controlled before initiation.
    - **Monitoring**: Blood pressure and heart rate monitoring at least weekly for the first two months, then monthly for the first year.
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)**: Rare neurologic disorder (0.1%). Symptoms include seizures, headache, and visual disturbances. Confirm by brain imaging (MRI). Discontinue if PRES occurs.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity**: Based on mechanism of action, niraparib can cause fetal harm.
- **Lactose/Excipients**: Capsules contain lactose [Australian]. The capsule shell contains tartrazine, which may cause allergic reactions [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally most frequent in the first few months of treatment.

| System Organ Class | Very Common (>10%) | Common (1–10%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Blood/Lymphatic** | Thrombocytopenia, Anaemia, Neutropenia, Leukopenia | Neutropenic infection |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, Vomiting, Constipation, Abdominal pain, Diarrhoea, Dyspepsia | Stomatitis, Dry mouth, Mucositis |
| **Nervous System** | Headache, Dizziness | Dysgeusia |
| **Psychiatric** | Insomnia | Anxiety, Depression, Cognitive impairment |
| **Vascular** | Hypertension | Tachycardia, Palpitations |
| **General** | Fatigue, Asthenia | Peripheral oedema |
| **Metabolism** | Decreased appetite | Hypokalaemia |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Back pain, Arthralgia | Myalgia |

- **Serious Adverse Effects**: MDS/AML, Hypertensive crisis, PRES, and severe pancytopenia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**:
    - **CYP Enzymes**: Niraparib is not a clinically relevant inhibitor or inducer of major CYP enzymes (*in vitro*). Minimal oxidative metabolism.
    - **Transporters**: Niraparib inhibits MATE1 and MATE2K. Co-administration with **metformin** may increase metformin exposure (>2-fold increase predicted). Monitor glycaemia closely.
    - **Uptake Transporters**: Niraparib inhibits OCT1 *in vitro*; the clinical relevance of this is considered possible.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions**:
    - **Vaccines/Immunosuppressants**: Combination not studied. Use with caution.
    - **Cytotoxic Agents**: Limited data; use caution when combining with other myelosuppressive drugs.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**:
    - **Australian Category D**: Niraparib can cause fetal harm.
    - **Contraception**: Females of reproductive potential must use highly effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after the final dose.
    - **Testing**: Verify pregnancy status before initiation.
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if niraparib is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is contraindicated [Australian] or not advised [US] during treatment and for 1 month after the last dose.
- **Fertility**: Animal studies showed decreased spermatogenesis. Findings were largely reversible in dogs but not rats.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy not established in children or adolescents under 18 years.
- **Elderly Use**: No dose adjustment required for patients ≥65 years. Data for those ≥75 years are limited.
- **Renal Impairment**:
    - **Mild to Moderate**: No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe/ESRD (Haemodialysis)**: No data available; use with caution.
- **Hepatic Impairment**:
    - **Mild**: No dose adjustment required.
    - **Moderate**: Recommended starting dose is **200 mg once daily** (total bilirubin ≥1.5 to 3 x ULN).
    - **Severe**: No data available; use with caution.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Niraparib inhibits PARP-1 and PARP-2 enzymes, which are critical for DNA repair. This leads to increased formation of PARP-DNA complexes, resulting in DNA damage and cell death, particularly in cells with existing DNA repair deficiencies (synthetic lethality).
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Absolute bioavailability ~73%. $T_{max}$ within 5 hours.
    - **Distribution**: 83% protein bound (mainly albumin). $V_d$ is approximately 1200 L.
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily via carboxylesterases to the inactive metabolite M1, which then undergoes glucuronidation.
    - **Elimination**: Mean terminal half-life is 44–54 hours. Elimination occurs via hepatobiliary and renal routes (47.5% in urine, 38.8% in faeces).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PRIMA Study**: Double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=733) in first-line maintenance. 
    - **Result**: Significant improvement in Progression-Free Survival (PFS) in the HRD-positive population (21.9 vs 10.4 months; HR 0.43) and the overall population (13.8 vs 8.2 months; HR 0.62).
- **NOVA Study**: Double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=553) in recurrent maintenance.
    - **Result**: Significant PFS benefit in both the gBRCAmut cohort (21.0 vs 5.5 months; HR 0.27) and the non-gBRCAmut cohort (9.3 vs 3.9 months; HR 0.45).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Capsules**: Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Tablets**: Store below 30°C [Australian]. US specifies 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) with excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C.
- Store in the original container.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia**: Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4). Subject to additional monitoring (Black Triangle).
- **United States**: Rx-only.
