# Editorial Product Information: Zanubrutinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA). *Note: While the scope of this project typically encompasses multiple international agencies, this specific editorial synthesis is based on the provided Australian Product Information (TGA) for Brukinsa.*
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of zanubrutinib for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Zanubrutinib (a small-molecule inhibitor of Bruton’s tyrosine kinase [BTK]).
- **Chemical Class:** Antineoplastic agent; BTK inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **BRUKINSA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EL03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Hard Capsules:** 80 mg zanubrutinib (white to off-white, opaque, marked "ZANU 80").
    - **Film-Coated Tablets:** 160 mg zanubrutinib (oval, blue, scored, debossed with "zanu"). [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** BeOne Medicines AUS Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Waldenström’s macroglobulinaemia (WM):** Treatment of adult patients who have received at least one prior therapy, or as first-line treatment for patients unsuitable for chemo-immunotherapy.
- **Mantle cell lymphoma (MCL):** Treatment of adult patients who have received at least one prior therapy. [Provisional approval in Australia based on objective response rate].
- **Marginal zone lymphoma (MZL):** Treatment of adult patients who have received at least one prior anti-CD20-based therapy. [Provisional approval in Australia based on objective response rate].
- **Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL) / Small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL):** Monotherapy for adult patients, including those with deletion 17p and/or TP53 mutation.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 320 mg daily.
- **Dosing Regimen:** May be administered as 320 mg once daily or 160 mg twice daily.
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Take with or without food.
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole with water; do not open, break, or chew.
    - Tablets should be swallowed whole with water; however, they are scored and may be split in half if an 80 mg dose is required. Do not chew or crush.
- **Missed Dose:** Take as soon as possible on the same day; return to normal schedule the following day.
- **Dose Modifications for Toxicity:**
    - **First occurrence (Grade 3/4):** Interrupt therapy. Resume at 320 mg/day (or 160 mg BID) once resolved to ≤ Grade 1.
    - **Second occurrence:** Interrupt therapy. Resume at 160 mg/day (or 80 mg BID) once resolved.
    - **Third occurrence:** Interrupt therapy. Resume at 80 mg once daily once resolved.
    - **Fourth occurrence:** Permanently discontinue.
- **Dose Modifications for Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - Mild to Moderate: No adjustment. 
    - Severe impairment: 80 mg orally twice daily.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None listed** in current available sources.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haemorrhage:** Serious and fatal events (e.g., intracranial, gastrointestinal) have occurred. Bleeding of any grade occurs in ~50% of patients. Discontinue if intracranial haemorrhage occurs. Consider withholding 3–7 days pre- and post-surgery.
- **Infections:** Fatal and non-fatal infections (bacterial, viral, fungal) including pneumonia and opportunistic infections (e.g., HBV reactivation, herpes zoster). Prophylaxis should be considered for at-risk patients.
- **Cytopenias:** Grade 3/4 neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and anaemia are common. Monitor Complete Blood Counts (CBC) regularly.
- **Second Primary Malignancies:** Reported in ~15% of patients, most commonly skin cancers (BCC and SCC). Advise sun protection and routine skin checks.
- **Cardiac Arrhythmias:** Atrial fibrillation and flutter (Grade 3+ in ~2%). Monitor for palpitations, dizziness, and syncope, especially in patients with cardiac risk factors or acute infections.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Reported infrequently (<0.5%), primarily in CLL. Assess baseline risk (tumour burden) and hydrate/monitor as appropriate.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Rare cases of drug-induced liver injury (DILI). Evaluate bilirubin and transaminases at baseline and throughout treatment.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A Inhibitors:** 
    - **Strong (e.g., posaconazole, voriconazole):** Reduce zanubrutinib dose to 80 mg once daily.
    - **Moderate (e.g., fluconazole, diltiazem, erythromycin):** Reduce zanubrutinib dose to 80 mg twice daily.
- **CYP3A Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use with strong (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin) or moderate (e.g., efavirenz, bosentan) inducers, as zanubrutinib exposure is significantly reduced (AUC decreased by 93% with rifampicin).
- **Effect of Zanubrutinib on other drugs:** 
    - **CYP3A substrates:** May decrease concentrations (e.g., midazolam AUC decreased by 47%).
    - **CYP2C19 substrates:** May decrease concentrations (e.g., omeprazole AUC decreased by 36%).
    - **P-gp substrates:** May increase concentrations (e.g., digoxin Cmax increased by 34%).
- **Gastric Acid Reducing Agents:** No clinically significant effect on zanubrutinib pharmacokinetics.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Zanubrutinib can cause embryofetal harm based on animal data (heart malformations). Confirm pregnancy status before initiation.
- **Contraception:** Advise females of reproductive potential and males with female partners of reproductive potential to use highly effective contraception during treatment and for **one week** after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety not established. Advise against breastfeeding during treatment and for **two weeks** after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** No observed effects on fertility in animal models, though morphological sperm abnormalities and increased post-implantation loss were noted at very high doses (19–32x human exposure).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients <18 years.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients ≥65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild/Moderate (CrCl ≥30 mL/min): No adjustment.
    - Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min) or Dialysis: Limited data; monitor closely for adverse reactions.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - Mild/Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B): No adjustment.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Recommended dose is 80 mg twice daily. Monitor closely.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Zanubrutinib is a potent, irreversible inhibitor of BTK. It forms a covalent bond with a cysteine residue in the BTK active site, inhibiting B-cell receptor (BCR) signalling, which is critical for malignant B-cell proliferation and survival.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 100% median steady-state BTK occupancy is maintained in peripheral blood mononuclear cells over 24 hours at the 320 mg/day dose.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median Tmax is 2 hours. High-fat meals do not significantly alter AUC or Cmax.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is ~94%. Vd is ~522 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A.
    - **Elimination:** Mean half-life (t½) is 2–4 hours. 87% excreted in faeces (38% unchanged) and 8% in urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **WM (ASPEN trial):** Phase 3 trial vs. ibrutinib. While zanubrutinib showed higher VGPR+CR rates (28% vs 19%), the difference was not statistically significant. Zanubrutinib demonstrated a lower rate of atrial fibrillation compared to ibrutinib.
- **CLL/SLL (SEQUOIA & ALPINE trials):** SEQUOIA (treatment-naïve) showed superior PFS vs bendamustine+rituximab. ALPINE (relapsed/refractory) demonstrated superior ORR and PFS compared to ibrutinib, with significantly lower rates of atrial fibrillation/flutter.
- **MCL:** Single-arm studies (BGB-3111-206) showed an ORR of 83.7% with 68.6% Complete Response.
- **MZL (MAGNOLIA trial):** ORR of 68% in relapsed/refractory patients.
