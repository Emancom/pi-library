# Editorial Product Information: Everolimus (**Zortress**)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **Zortress** for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory guidance into a single clinically relevant reference.
- **Editorial Note:** This document is an expert synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Everolimus (a macrolide immunosuppressant).
- **Chemical Class:** Mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** **Zortress** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Oral Tablets:** 0.25 mg, 0.5 mg, 0.75 mg, and 1 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Kidney Transplantation:** For the prophylaxis of organ rejection in adult patients at low-to-moderate immunologic risk. Must be used in combination with basiliximab induction, reduced-dose cyclosporine, and corticosteroids [US-approved].
- **Liver Transplantation:** For the prophylaxis of allograft rejection in adult patients. Administration should begin no earlier than 30 days post-transplant. Must be used in combination with reduced-dose tacrolimus and corticosteroids [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Kidney Transplant:** Initial dose of 0.75 mg orally twice daily (1.5 mg/day) as soon as possible after transplantation [US].
- **Liver Transplant:** Initial dose of 1 mg orally twice daily (2 mg/day) starting no earlier than 30 days post-transplantation [US].

### Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM)
- **Everolimus Target Range:** Trough concentrations should be maintained between **3 and 8 ng/mL** (using LC/MS/MS assay) [US].
- **Monitoring Frequency:** Adjustments should be based on trough levels obtained 4–5 days after a dose change. If levels are <3 ng/mL, double the daily dose. If levels are >8 ng/mL on two consecutive measures, reduce the dose by 0.25 mg BID [US].
- **Calcineurin Inhibitor (CNI) Adjustment:** Use with **reduced-dose** cyclosporine (Kidney) or **reduced-dose** tacrolimus (Liver) is mandatory to minimize nephrotoxicity [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole with water; do not crush or chew [US].
- Administer consistently with or without food, approximately 12 hours apart, and at the same time as the CNI [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **everolimus**, **sirolimus**, or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Management:** Only physicians experienced in immunosuppressive therapy and transplant management should prescribe.
- **Malignancies and Infections:** Increased susceptibility to serious infections and the development of malignancies (e.g., lymphoma, skin cancer).
- **Kidney Graft Thrombosis:** Increased risk of arterial and venous thrombosis, usually within 30 days post-transplant.
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Increased risk with standard doses of cyclosporine; reduced-dose cyclosporine is required.
- **Heart Transplantation:** **Not recommended** due to increased mortality observed in clinical trials, often associated with serious infections within the first three months.

### General Warnings
- **Angioedema:** Increased risk when used concomitantly with ACE inhibitors [US].
- **Wound Healing and Fluid Accumulation:** Potential for delayed healing, dehiscence, incisional hernia, lymphocele, and generalized fluid accumulation (e.g., pericardial/pleural effusions) [US].
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD):** Non-infectious pneumonitis, including fatal cases, has occurred. Monitor for cough or dyspnea [US].
- **Hyperlipidemia:** Significant elevations in serum cholesterol and triglycerides may require anti-lipid therapy [US].
- **Proteinuria:** Risk increases with higher everolimus trough concentrations [US].
- **Polyoma Virus Infections:** Risk of BK virus-associated nephropathy and JC virus-associated progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) [US].
- **New Onset Diabetes After Transplant (NODAT):** Monitor blood glucose levels [US].
- **Hereditary Disorders:** Patients with galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption should not take **Zortress** [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Kidney Transplant:** Peripheral edema (45%), constipation (38%), hypertension (30%), nausea (29%), anemia (26%), urinary tract infection (22%), and hyperlipidemia (21%) [US].
- **Liver Transplant:** Diarrhea (19%), headache (19%), peripheral edema (18%), hypertension (17%), nausea (14%), pyrexia (13%), abdominal pain (13%), and leukopenia (12%) [US].

### Common (1–10%)
- **General:** Stomatitis/mouth ulceration (8%), dyspepsia, vomiting, insomnia, tremor, acne, and epistaxis [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Lymphoma and other malignancies.
- Serious viral, bacterial, and fungal infections (including CMV and BK virus).
- Thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA), TTP, and HUS (especially when combined with cyclosporine) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4/P-gp Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir) are not recommended. Moderate inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, verapamil) require close monitoring and possible dose reduction [US].
- **CYP3A4/P-gp Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., rifampin, St. John’s Wort) are not recommended as they significantly decrease everolimus concentrations [US].
- **Cannabidiol:** May increase everolimus levels; monitor for toxicity and consider dose reduction [US].
- **Grapefruit/Grapefruit Juice:** Avoid entirely as it inhibits CYP3A4 metabolism [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **ACE Inhibitors:** Increased risk of angioedema [US].
- **CNIs:** Cyclosporine increases everolimus levels (requires TDM). Tacrolimus has little to no effect on everolimus PK [US].
- **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors:** Increased risk of rhabdomyolysis when used with cyclosporine and everolimus; monitor closely [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but stated as "may cause fetal harm"]. Based on animal data, everolimus is embryo-fetal toxic at exposures below clinical doses. Females of reproductive potential must use highly effective contraception during treatment and for **8 weeks** after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants. It is unknown if everolimus is excreted in human milk [US].
- **Fertility:** Male infertility (azoospermia or oligospermia) and female infertility (amenorrhea) may occur and may be related to the drug's anti-proliferative effects [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients <18 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data in patients ≥65 years. No specific dose adjustment is currently recommended based on age [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** Reduce initial dose by approximately one-third [US].
    - **Moderate/Severe (Child-Pugh B or C):** Reduce initial dose by approximately one-half. Subsequent titration must be based on TDM [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Everolimus binds to the intracellular protein FKBP-12, forming a complex that inhibits the mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1). This inhibition blocks cytokine-stimulated T and B cell proliferation by stopping the cell cycle transition from G1 to S phase [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is 1–2 hours. A high-fat meal reduces Cmax by 60% and AUC by 16% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Blood-to-plasma ratio is concentration-dependent (17–73%). Plasma protein binding is ~74% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP3A4 in the liver and intestinal wall [US].
    - **Elimination:** Majority (80%) excreted in feces; 5% in urine. Half-life is approximately 30 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Kidney Transplant Trial:** A 24-month study showed that **Zortress** 1.5 mg/day with reduced-dose cyclosporine was non-inferior to mycophenolic acid with standard-dose cyclosporine regarding efficacy failure (treated BPAR, graft loss, death, or loss to follow-up) [US].
- **Liver Transplant Trial:** A 24-month study demonstrated that **Zortress** initiated 30 days post-transplant with reduced-dose tacrolimus was comparable to standard-dose tacrolimus. However, total elimination of tacrolimus resulted in higher rejection rates and is not recommended [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light and moisture. Dispense in the original child-resistant blister pack [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
