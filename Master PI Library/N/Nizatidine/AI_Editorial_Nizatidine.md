# Editorial Product Information: Nizatidine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI. Clinicians should consult the approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nizatidine (a histamine $H_2$-receptor antagonist).
- **Brand Name(s):** **NIZAC** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 150 mg capsules (Size 2, dark yellow cap/pale yellow body) [Australian].
    - 300 mg capsules (Size 1, brown cap/pale yellow body) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Active Duodenal Ulcer:** Treatment for up to 8 weeks; most patients heal within 4 weeks [Australian].
- **Maintenance of Duodenal Ulcer:** Prophylactic therapy at reduced dosage following healing of an active ulcer; studies do not exceed 12 months of continuous use [Australian].
- **Benign Gastric Ulcer:** Treatment for up to 8 weeks [Australian].
- **Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD):** Treatment for up to 12 weeks for oesophagitis (including erosive and ulcerative forms) and associated heartburn [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Active Duodenal Ulcer:** 150 mg twice daily OR 300 mg once daily in the evening [Australian].
- **Benign Gastric Ulcer:** 150 mg twice daily OR 300 mg once daily in the evening [Australian].
- **Maintenance of Duodenal Ulcer:** 150 mg once daily in the evening [Australian].
- **Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease:** 150 mg twice daily [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Prior to treating gastric ulcers, malignancy must be excluded [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **nizatidine** or any excipients [Australian].
- History of hypersensitivity to other $H_2$-receptor antagonists (cross-sensitivity has been observed) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastric Malignancy:** Symptomatic response to therapy does not rule out the presence of gastric cancer [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Nizatidine is partially metabolised in the liver. It is not recommended for use in patients with hepatic failure. In patients with uncomplicated hepatic dysfunction, disposition remains similar to normal subjects [Australian].
- **Pneumonia Risk:** Epidemiological data suggest an increased risk (relative risk 1.63) of community-acquired pneumonia in current users of $H_2$-receptor antagonists [Australian].
- **Nosocomial Infections:** Potential for bacterial colonisation of the stomach in ICU patients receiving acid suppressants [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Clinical monitoring is advised in patients with renal or hepatic impairment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Nizatidine is generally well tolerated with a safety profile comparable to other $H_2$-blockers.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Sweating, urticaria (significantly higher than placebo) [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Anaemia (0.2% vs 0% placebo) [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hepatitis, jaundice, cholestatic/mixed injury, reversible mental confusion, serum sickness, anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal oedema [Australian]. |
| **Frequency Not Defined** | Somnolence, dizziness, headache, gastrointestinal upset (diarrhoea, constipation, nausea, vomiting), myalgia, arthralgia, gynaecomastia, impotence (equal to placebo rates) [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Rare cases of fatal thrombocytopenia (in patients with history of drug-induced thrombocytopenia), leucopenia, and agranulocytosis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Metabolism:** Nizatidine does not inhibit the cytochrome P-450 system; interactions with theophylline, warfarin, phenytoin, or diazepam are not expected [Australian].
- **pH-Dependent Absorption:** May reduce the absorption of drugs requiring an acidic gastric environment [Australian].
- **Aspirin:** Concurrent high-dose aspirin (3,900 mg/day) may result in increased serum salicylate levels when taken with **nizatidine** 150 mg BID [Australian].
- **Vitamin $B_{12}$:** Chronic use may impair absorption of protein-bound Vitamin $B_{12}$, potentially leading to deficiency [Australian].
- **Antacids:** Concomitant use with aluminium/magnesium antacids may decrease **nizatidine** absorption by approximately 10% [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at high oral doses showed no evidence of impaired fertility or teratogenicity. However, high IV doses in rabbits produced foetal cardiac and skeletal anomalies. Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Approximately 0.1% of the oral dose is secreted in human milk. Due to observed growth depression in animal pups, a decision should be made whether to cease nursing or the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on reproductive performance were observed in rat studies up to 650 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Healing rates are similar to younger patients. However, dose reduction may be necessary due to age-related decline in renal function [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage must be reduced for moderate to severe insufficiency:
    - **Active Ulcer (CrCl 20-50 mL/min):** 150 mg daily.
    - **Active Ulcer (CrCl < 20 mL/min):** 150 mg every 2 days.
    - **Maintenance (CrCl 20-50 mL/min):** 150 mg every 2 days.
    - **Maintenance (CrCl < 20 mL/min):** 150 mg every 3 days [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended for use in hepatic failure [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive, reversible inhibitor of histamine at $H_2$-receptors, primarily on gastric parietal cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Inhibits basal and nocturnal acid secretion for up to 12 hours. Also inhibits secretion stimulated by food, caffeine, and pentagastrin. Does not significantly affect pepsin activity or basal serum gastrin [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** >70%.
    - **Peak Plasma Time ($T_{max}$):** 0.5 to 3 hours.
    - **Protein Binding:** ~35% (primarily to $\alpha$1-acid glycoprotein).
    - **Metabolism:** <7% metabolised (N2-monodesmethyl-nizatidine is the active metabolite).
    - **Elimination:** >90% excreted in urine (60% as unchanged drug) within 12 hours. Half-life is 1 to 1.5 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Duodenal Ulcer:** Healing rates are significantly higher with 300 mg nocte or 150 mg BID compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** 150 mg nocte significantly reduced ulcer recurrence for up to 1 year [Australian].
- **GERD:** Clinical trials showed **nizatidine** 150 mg BID yielded higher healing rates for erosive oesophagitis at 6 weeks (21-32%) and 12 weeks (29%) compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
