# Editorial Product Information: Normal Immunoglobulin Subcutaneous (Human) 20%

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided for this specific harmonisation task. Where data is absent from the provided Australian source, it is noted as "Not specified in available sources."
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI and should not be used as the sole basis for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Normal Immunoglobulin Subcutaneous (Human) 20% (contains at least 98% IgG).
- **Chemical Class:** Human polyvalent Immunoglobulin G (IgG).
- **Brand Name(s):** **CUVITRU** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Solution for subcutaneous (SC) injection (200 mg/mL). Available in vials of:
    - 1.0 g / 5 mL
    - 2.0 g / 10 mL
    - 4.0 g / 20 mL
    - 8.0 g / 40 mL
    - 10.0 g / 50 mL
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Takeda Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**CUVITRU** is indicated as replacement therapy in both adult and paediatric patients for:
- Primary immunodeficiency diseases (PID).
- Symptomatic hypogammaglobulinaemia secondary to underlying disease or treatment.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Dosing must be individualised based on pharmacokinetic and clinical response.
- **Target Trough Level:** At least 5 to 6 g/L (aiming for the age-specific reference interval).
- **Loading Dose:** At least 0.2 to 0.5 g/kg (1.0 to 2.5 mL/kg). This may be divided over several days (max daily dose 0.1 to 0.15 g/kg).
- **Maintenance Dose:** Once steady state is achieved, doses are administered at repeated intervals to reach a cumulative monthly dose of approximately 0.3 to 1.0 g/kg.

### Switching Instructions
- **From Subcutaneous IG (SCIG):** The initial weekly dose of **CUVITRU** should be the same as the previous SCIG weekly dose.
- **From Intravenous IG (IVIG):** The initial weekly dose is calculated by dividing the previous IVIG dose (in grams) by the number of weeks between IVIG doses. Treatment should begin one week after the last IVIG infusion.

### Administration Instructions
- **Method:** Subcutaneous infusion via infusion pump (device-assisted) or manual syringe administration.
- **Anatomic Sites:** Abdomen, thigh, upper arm, or lateral hip. Use 1 to 4 sites simultaneously, at least 4 inches apart.
- **Maximum Volume:** In adults, doses >30 mL may be divided; in infants/children, change sites every 5–15 mL.
- **Infusion Rates:**
    - **Device-assisted:** Initially 10 mL/h/site. If tolerated, increase at 10-minute intervals to a maximum of 20 mL/h/site for the first two infusions. Subsequent infusions may be increased as tolerated.
    - **Manual:** Proposed maximum rate of 1–2 mL per minute.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known anaphylactic or severe hypersensitivity reactions to the active substance or any excipients (e.g., glycine).
- Patients with severe IgA deficiency and a history of hypersensitivity to human immunoglobulin treatment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Route of Administration:** **Strictly for subcutaneous use.** Intravascular injection may lead to shock.
- **Infusion Monitoring:** Monitor patients closely throughout the infusion. Naive patients or those switching products require monitoring during the first infusion and for one hour thereafter. Others should be observed for 20 minutes post-administration.
- **Hypersensitivity:** True hypersensitivity is rare but can occur, particularly in patients with anti-IgA antibodies. **CUVITRU** contains trace amounts of IgA (≤ 280 mcg/mL).
- **Thromboembolism:** Arterial and venous events (MI, stroke, DVT, PE) have been reported. Use caution in patients with risk factors (age, hypertension, diabetes, history of vascular disease, hyperviscosity). Ensure adequate hydration.
- **Renal Complications:** Although **CUVITRU** does not contain sucrose, severe renal adverse reactions (acute renal failure, ATN) have been reported with IVIG. Monitor at-risk patients.
- **Aseptic Meningitis Syndrome (AMS):** May occur, usually within hours to 2 days of treatment. More frequent in females.
- **Hemolysis:** May contain blood group antibodies (hemolysins) that can induce a positive Coombs test and, rarely, hemolysis.
- **Transmissible Agents:** As a product of human plasma, the risk of infectious agents (including unknown pathogens) cannot be totally excluded, though manufacturing steps (solvent/detergent, nanofiltration, low pH incubation) are effective for enveloped and non-enveloped viruses.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Treatment is generally well tolerated; local reactions are common but mostly mild.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction (per subject) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache, Diarrhoea, Nausea, Fatigue, Local site reactions (erythema, pain/discomfort) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, Migraine, Somnolence, Hypotension, Abdominal pain, Pruritus, Urticaria, Myalgia, Site swelling/pruritus/bruising |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Burning sensation, Lower abdominal pain, Site oedema, Anti-GAD antibody positive, Coombs direct test positive |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Aseptic meningitis, anaphylactic shock (rare), and thromboembolic events.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Live Attenuated Vaccines:** Immunoglobulin administration may impair the efficacy of vaccines (measles, rubella, mumps, varicella) for 6 weeks to 3 months. For measles, this may persist for up to 1 year. Vaccination should occur at least 3 months after **CUVITRU** administration.
- **Laboratory Interference:** Passively transferred antibodies may result in misleading positive serological results (e.g., Hepatitis A, B, measles). 
- **Fungal Assays:** May lead to false-positive results in beta-D-glucan assays for fungal infections.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category: Not specified]. Safety has not been established in controlled trials. Immunoglobulins cross the placenta, especially during the third trimester. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Immunoglobulins are excreted in human milk. A risk-benefit assessment is required; however, clinical experience suggests no harmful effects on the neonate are expected.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects anticipated based on the nature of the protein.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Evaluated in patients aged 2 to <18 years. Posology is the same as adults (weight-based). Not evaluated in children <2 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Evaluated in 12 subjects ≥65 years. No overall differences in safety or efficacy. Use minimum infusion rate practicable in those at risk of renal or thrombotic events.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dose adjustments provided; however, monitor patients at risk of renal failure closely.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Provides a broad spectrum of IgG antibodies against infectious agents, mimicking the action of normal plasma. It acts as replacement therapy to restore low IgG levels to the normal range.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak serum levels (Cmax) are achieved approximately 3 days after subcutaneous administration.
    - **Distribution:** IgG sub-class distribution is similar to native human plasma (IgG1 ≥ 56.9%).
    - **Elimination:** IgG and IgG-complexes are degraded by the reticuloendothelial system.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 170903 (European):** A phase 2/3 study in 48 PID patients (aged ≥2 years). Demonstrated that monthly doses of 0.3–1.0 g/kg maintained effective trough levels (median 8.26 g/L). The annualised rate of acute serious bacterial infections (ASBIs) was 0.022.
- **Study 170904 (North American):** Evaluated 74 subjects using an adjusted dose (145% of the previous IVIG dose) to maintain equivalent AUC. The annualised ASBI rate was 0.012.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Protection:** Keep the vial in the outer carton to protect from light.
- **Handling:** Allow vials to reach room temperature (up to 90 minutes) before use. Do not shake. Use within 2 hours after drawing into a syringe.
