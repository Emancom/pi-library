# Editorial Product Information: Nabiximols

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of Nabiximols, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredients:** Nabiximols (standardised extract of *Cannabis sativa* L. leaf and flower).
*   **Components:** Each 100 microlitre spray contains 2.7 mg delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) and 2.5 mg cannabidiol (CBD).
*   **Chemical Class:** Cannabinoid receptor agonists/Cannabinoids.
*   **Brand Name:** **Sativex** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** N02BG10.
*   **Dosage Form:** Pump-actuated metered-dose oromucosal spray.
*   **Route of Administration:** Oromucosal use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Jazz Pharmaceuticals ANZ Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Multiple Sclerosis (MS) Spasticity:** Indicated as add-on treatment for symptom improvement in adult patients with moderate to severe spasticity due to MS who have not responded adequately to other anti-spasticity medications and who demonstrate clinically significant improvement during an initial trial of therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
*   **Initial Trial:** Treatment must be initiated and supervised by a specialist neurologist or rehabilitation physician. Response should be reviewed after four weeks; therapy should be discontinued if a 20% improvement in spasticity-related symptoms (on a 0–10 Numeric Rating Scale) is not achieved [Australian].
*   **Maintenance:** The median dose in clinical trials was 8 sprays per day. The total dose must not exceed 12 sprays per 24-hour period [Australian].

### Titration Schedule
A titration period is required to reach the optimal dose. The afternoon/evening dose should be taken between 4 pm and bedtime; the morning dose should be taken between waking and midday.
*   **Days 1–2:** 1 spray (evening).
*   **Days 3–4:** 2 sprays (evening).
*   **Days 5–6:** 1 spray (morning), 2 sprays (evening).
*   **Gradual Increase:** Increase by one spray per day up to a maximum of 12 sprays. 
*   **Administration Interval:** At least a 15-minute gap between sprays. No more than 7 sprays should be administered within any 3-hour period [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Priming:** Shake gently and prime the pump before first use (or if unused for >21 days) until a fine spray appears.
*   **Application:** Spray onto different sites on the oromucosal surface (changing the site each time).
*   **Food Intake:** Administration should be standardised in relation to food to minimise bioavailability variability [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   Hypersensitivity to cannabinoids or any excipients (ethanol absolute, propylene glycol, peppermint oil).
*   Known or suspected history (or family history) of schizophrenia or other psychotic illnesses.
*   History of severe personality disorder or significant psychiatric disorder (excluding depression associated with the underlying condition).
*   Breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [Australian]
**Psychiatric Adverse Events:** The maximum recommended dose must not be exceeded. High doses increase the risk of serious psychiatric events, including psychosis, hallucinations, delusions, and homicidal or suicidal ideation.

### General Precautions
*   **Cardiovascular:** Not recommended in serious cardiovascular disease. Initial dose introduction may cause alterations in pulse rate and blood pressure; fainting episodes have been reported.
*   **Psychiatric:** Disorientation, euphoria, and dissociation are common. Discontinue immediately if suicidal ideation occurs.
*   **Falls Risk:** Reduction in spasticity combined with CNS effects may increase the risk of falls, especially where muscle strength is insufficient to maintain posture.
*   **Epilepsy:** Caution is advised in patients with a history of recurrent seizures.
*   **Abuse and Dependence:** Potential for abuse is moderate at recommended doses but increases with higher doses. Dependence is considered unlikely following abrupt withdrawal.
*   **Excipients:** Contains approximately 50% v/v ethanol (up to 0.04g per actuation), which may be harmful in severe alcohol use disorder.

### Monitoring
*   Regular inspection of the oral mucosa is advised in long-term use. If lesions or persistent soreness occur, interrupt treatment until resolved [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are most frequent in the first four weeks and often resolve with continued treatment or dose reduction.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dizziness (25%), Fatigue (12.5%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Anorexia, increased appetite, depression, disorientation, euphoria, dissociation, amnesia, disturbance in attention, dysgeusia, somnolence, vertigo, blurred vision, constipation, diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, dry mouth, mouth ulceration, oral pain/discomfort, application site pain, asthenia, feeling drunk, malaise, falls. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Tachycardia, hypertension, pharyngitis, tooth discolouration, mucosal exfoliation, fainting. |

*   **Serious Effects:** Transient toxic psychosis has been observed with supratherapeutic dosing (18 sprays twice daily) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
*   **CYP450 Metabolism:** THC and CBD are metabolised by CYP3A4 and 2C9.
    *   *Inhibitors:* Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, clarithromycin) increase THC and CBD exposure; new dose titration may be required.
    *   *Inducers:* Strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, St John’s Wort) may reduce plasma concentrations of cannabinoids.
*   **UGT Enzymes:** Nabiximols inhibits UGT1A9 and UGT2B7 in vitro. Caution is required with drugs solely metabolised by these enzymes (e.g., propofol).
*   **P-glycoprotein:** CBD may inhibit intestinal P-gp; caution is advised with substrates like digoxin.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
*   **CNS Depressants:** Additive sedative and muscle-relaxing effects occur with hypnotics, sedatives, and alcohol.
*   **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Nabiximols may reduce the effectiveness of systemically acting hormonal contraceptives. Women must use an additional barrier method during therapy and for three months after discontinuation [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B2.** There is insufficient human experience. Animal studies showed reduced foetal weights and skeletal variants at maternotoxic doses. Use only if benefits outweigh risks to the foetus.
*   **Breastfeeding:** **Contraindicated.** Cannabinoids are concentrated in breast milk and may affect the growth and development of infants [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** Rat studies showed no effect on fertility at high doses, though inconsistent effects on male reproductive parameters have been noted in other cannabinoid studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children or adolescents under 18 years due to a lack of safety and efficacy data.
*   **Elderly Use:** Caution is required. Patients >65 years in clinical trials were three times more likely to experience CNS adverse events compared to placebo.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment needed for mild impairment. Cautious use and frequent clinical evaluation are required for moderate or severe hepatic impairment.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied; effects may be exaggerated or prolonged. Frequent clinical review is recommended.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
THC acts as a partial agonist at CB1 (CNS-based) and CB2 (immune-based) receptors. CBD has low affinity for these receptors but modulates intracellular calcium and microglial activity, providing neuroprotective effects. The combination reduces limb stiffness and improves motor function in MS models.

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Plasma levels appear within 15 minutes. Mean Cmax for THC is ~4 ng/mL (following 10.8 mg dose) reached at 45–120 minutes. High inter-patient variability exists.
*   **Distribution:** Highly lipophilic; distributes rapidly into body fat. Protein binding is ~97%.
*   **Metabolism:** Hepatic first-pass metabolism is significant. THC is converted to 11-OH-THC (psychoactive) and then to THC-COOH. 
*   **Elimination:** Terminal half-life for THC is ~2–5 hours; however, release from fatty tissues results in a prolonged terminal elimination phase (24–36 hours). Approximately one-third is excreted in urine, the remainder in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Pivotal Trials:** Efficacy was assessed using a 0–10 Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) for spasticity.
*   **Responder Analysis:** In a randomized withdrawal study, patients who achieved a ≥20% NRS improvement during a 4-week lead-in (48% of the cohort) were randomized. Treatment failure (20% increase in NRS) occurred in 44% of the Nabiximols group vs 94% of the placebo group (Hazard Ratio 0.335) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened:** Store refrigerated (2°C to 8°C). Store upright. Keep away from heat and direct sunlight.
*   **In-use:** Once opened, refrigerated storage is not mandatory, but do not store above 25°C.
*   **Shelf-life:** Use within 42 days of opening [Australian].
