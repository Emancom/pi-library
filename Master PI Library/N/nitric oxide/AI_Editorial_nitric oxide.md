# Editorial Product Information: Nitric Oxide for Inhalation

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from official regulatory filings provided by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) for INOmax® and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for Noxivent™. Information has been combined to provide a comprehensive global overview. Where clinical indications or safety thresholds differ between agencies, the more conservative or detailed information is provided and labeled accordingly.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
*   **Product Names:** INOmax®, Noxivent™
*   **Active Ingredient:** Nitric oxide (gaseous)
*   **Chemical Formula:** NO
*   **Concentration:** Typically supplied in cylinders at 800 ppm (parts per million) balanced with nitrogen (99.92%).

## 2. Approved Indications
Nitric oxide is a pulmonary vasodilator intended for use in conjunction with ventilatory support and other appropriate therapies.

*   **Hypoxic Respiratory Failure (Neonatal):** Indicated for the treatment of term and near-term (>34 weeks gestation) neonates presenting with hypoxic respiratory failure associated with clinical or echocardiographic evidence of pulmonary hypertension. The primary goals are to improve oxygenation and reduce the requirement for extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO).
*   **Perioperative Pulmonary Hypertension (Cardiac Surgery):** Indicated to selectively reduce pulmonary arterial pressure in pediatric and adult patients undergoing heart surgery [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
Nitric oxide is contraindicated in the following populations:
*   Neonates who are known to be dependent on right-to-left shunting of blood.
*   Neonates with significant left-to-right shunting of blood [TGA].
*   Patients with a known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of the excipients (nitrogen).

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Rebound Pulmonary Hypertension
Therapy must not be discontinued abruptly. Sudden withdrawal may cause a rapid increase in pulmonary artery pressure (PAP) and a decrease in systemic oxygenation, potentially leading to cardiopulmonary collapse. If rebound occurs, therapy should be reinstated immediately and weaned more gradually.

### Methemoglobinemia
Nitric oxide combines with hemoglobin to form methemoglobin, which cannot transport oxygen. Methemoglobin levels are dose-dependent and should be monitored within 1–8 hours of initiation and periodically thereafter. If levels exceed 2.5% [TGA] or 5% [FDA], the dose should be reduced.

### Nitrogen Dioxide (NO₂) Toxicity
In the presence of oxygen, nitric oxide rapidly oxidizes to NO₂, which can cause airway inflammation and alveolar damage. Inspired NO₂ must be monitored continuously. The concentration should be maintained below 0.5 ppm; if NO₂ exceeds 1 ppm [TGA] or 3 ppm [FDA], the delivery system must be assessed and the dose adjusted.

### Left Ventricular Dysfunction and Heart Failure
Patients with pre-existing left ventricular dysfunction may experience a worsening of heart failure, increased pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP), and pulmonary edema when treated with nitric oxide. Use in these patients requires a careful benefit-risk assessment and close hemodynamic monitoring.

### Clinical Non-Response
In clinical trials for Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN), approximately 30–45% of patients did not respond to therapy. If oxygenation does not improve after 4–6 hours, alternative "rescue" therapies, such as ECMO, should be considered.

### Congenital Diaphragmatic Hernia (CDH)
Nitric oxide has not demonstrated efficacy in patients with CDH and may be associated with poor outcomes in this specific population [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Neonatal Hypoxic Respiratory Failure
*   **Standard Dose:** 20 ppm.
*   **Duration:** Treatment is typically maintained for up to 96 hours [TGA] or 14 days [FDA], or until the underlying desaturation has resolved. 
*   **Weaning:** Once oxygenation improves and ventilator support is reduced, the dose should be tapered gradually (e.g., down to 5 ppm, then 1 ppm) before discontinuation.

### Cardiac Surgery (Perioperative) [TGA]
*   **Pediatric (0–17 years):** Starting dose of 10 ppm, which may be increased up to 20 ppm if required.
*   **Adults (>17 years):** Range of 10–20 ppm.
*   **Duration:** Typically 24–48 hours, though it may be used for up to 7 days in some clinical settings.

### Administration Method
Nitric oxide must be administered via a calibrated, non-pulsatile delivery system that ensures a constant concentration throughout the respiratory cycle. The system must measure inspired NO, NO₂, and FiO₂ continuously. Backup battery power and an independent manual ventilation system (for suctioning or transport) must be available.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Most Common:** Hypotension (observed in approximately 13–14% of patients).
*   **Neonatal Trials (CINRGI):** Other effects occurring at >5% incidence compared to placebo include withdrawal-related rebound hypoxemia, atelectasis, hematuria, hyperglycemia, and sepsis/bacteremia.
*   **Long-term Safety:** Follow-up at 18–24 months generally shows no significant differences in neurodevelopmental outcomes, though some data suggest a higher incidence of sensorineural hearing loss or cerebral palsy in certain study cohorts [TGA].
*   **Occupational Exposure:** Hospital staff accidentally exposed to nitric oxide have reported headaches, dizziness, chest discomfort, and dry throat.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Nitric Oxide Donors:** Concurrent use of agents like sodium nitroprusside, nitroglycerine, or prilocaine may increase the risk of developing methemoglobinemia.
*   **Vasodilators:** The use of nitric oxide with sildenafil or other cGMP/cAMP-acting vasodilators may result in additive effects on pulmonary artery pressure and central circulation [TGA].
*   **Surfactant:** Experimental data suggest NO may react with surfactant proteins; however, the clinical significance of this in neonates remains unclear.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy Category:** B2 [TGA]. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if nitric oxide causes fetal harm or affects reproductive capacity.
*   **Lactation:** It is unknown if nitric oxide or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Due to the acute, intensive-care nature of this therapy, use in breastfeeding mothers is generally not applicable.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Nitric oxide is absorbed systemically via the lungs.
*   **Distribution:** It enters the systemic circulation and binds with 60–100% oxygen-saturated hemoglobin. 
*   **Metabolism:** It combines with oxyhemoglobin to form methemoglobin and nitrate. Within the lungs, it can also react with oxygen and water to form NO₂ and nitrite.
*   **Excretion:** Nitrate is the primary metabolite and is excreted in the urine, with a clearance rate matching the glomerular filtration rate.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Nitric oxide is a compound produced endogenously by various cells. When inhaled, it diffuses across the alveolar-capillary membrane and binds to the heme moiety of cytosolic guanylate cyclase in vascular smooth muscle. This activates the enzyme, increasing levels of cyclic guanosine 3’,5’-monophosphate (cGMP), which induces smooth muscle relaxation.

Because inhaled nitric oxide is rapidly inactivated by hemoglobin in the blood, its vasodilatory effect is selective to the pulmonary vasculature, with minimal impact on systemic blood pressure. It improves oxygenation by dilating vessels in well-ventilated areas of the lung, thereby redistributing blood flow to areas with better ventilation-perfusion (V/Q) matching.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
*   **Safety:** Cylinders must be stored in well-ventilated areas and protected from physical shock, falls, and sources of ignition. All local regulations regarding high-pressure gas vessels must be observed.
*   **Shelf Life:** 3 years [TGA].
