# Editorial Product Information: Netarsudil and Latanoprost Ophthalmic Solution

## Editorial Note
- Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial: United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of netarsudil and latanoprost ophthalmic solution for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Netarsudil (as dimesylate) and Latanoprost.
- **Chemical Class:** Rho kinase inhibitor and Prostaglandin $F_{2\alpha}$ analogue.
- **Brand Names:** **ROCKLATAN** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile ophthalmic solution containing netarsudil 0.02% (0.2 mg/mL) and latanoprost 0.005% (0.05 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alcon Laboratories, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reduction of Intraocular Pressure (IOP):** Indicated for the reduction of elevated IOP in patients diagnosed with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The recommended regimen is one drop instilled into the affected eye(s) once daily, specifically in the evening [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - If multiple topical ophthalmic medications are being used, they should be administered at least five (5) minutes apart [US].
    - To prevent contamination, patients should avoid touching the dropper tip to the eye, eyelids, or any other surfaces [US].
- **Maximum Frequency:** Dosing should not exceed once daily, as more frequent administration of prostaglandin analogues may diminish the IOP-lowering effect or cause a paradoxical rise in IOP [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, treatment should resume with the next scheduled dose the following evening [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Epithelial Corneal Edema:** Netarsudil has been associated with corneal edema (described as bullous or honeycomb-like), particularly in patients with existing corneal endothelial compromise or following ocular surgery. This typically resolves upon drug discontinuation [US].
- **Pigmentation:** Latanoprost can cause increased pigmentation of the iris, periorbital tissue (eyelids), and eyelashes. Iris pigmentation changes result from increased melanin content in melanocytes and are likely to be permanent. Long-term effects beyond five years are currently unknown [US].
- **Eyelash Changes:** Potential changes include increased length, thickness, number, and pigmentation of lashes. These changes are typically reversible after stopping treatment [US].
- **Intraocular Inflammation:** Use with caution in patients with a history of uveitis or iritis; avoid use during active intraocular inflammation [US].
- **Macular Edema:** Reports of macular edema (including cystoid macular edema) exist for latanoprost. Caution is advised in aphakic patients, pseudophakic patients with a torn posterior lens capsule, or those with known risk factors [US].
- **Herpetic Keratitis:** Use with caution in patients with a history of herpetic keratitis. Use should be avoided in active Herpes Simplex keratitis [US].
- **Bacterial Keratitis:** Risk of bacterial infection associated with contamination of multiple-dose containers [US].
- **Contact Lenses:** Lenses should be removed prior to instillation and may be reinserted 15 minutes afterward, as the preservative benzalkonium chloride may be absorbed by soft lenses [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following adverse reactions were identified in clinical trials [US]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Conjunctival hyperemia (59%), instillation site pain (20%), corneal verticillata (15%), conjunctival hemorrhage (11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Eye pruritus, reduced visual acuity, increased lacrimation, instillation site discomfort, blurred vision |

**Additional Adverse Effects (Individual Components):**
- **Netarsudil:** Instillation site erythema, corneal staining, eyelid erythema.
- **Latanoprost:** Foreign body sensation, punctate keratitis, burning/stinging, eyelid margin crusting, photophobia, upper respiratory tract infections (nasopharyngitis/influenza), and myalgia/arthralgia/back pain.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Thimerosal:** In vitro studies indicate precipitation occurs when **ROCKLATAN** is mixed with eye drops containing thimerosal. If used concurrently, space administration by at least five minutes [US].
- **Prostaglandin Analogues:** Concurrent use of two or more prostaglandins is not recommended, as it may reduce the efficacy of IOP lowering [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** No lettered category provided (PLLR format). 
    - **Summary:** There are no adequate, well-controlled human studies. Animal data for netarsudil showed embryofetal lethality and malformations in rats and rabbits at high intravenous doses (≥0.3 mg/kg/day). Latanoprost showed embryofetal lethality in rabbits at 80 times the human dose. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential fetal risk [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if netarsudil, latanoprost, or their metabolites are excreted in human milk. Systemic exposure following topical application is low, but the developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies have not shown impaired fertility for either component [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy have been observed between geriatric and younger adult populations [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments are not provided in the source documentation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Netarsudil:** A Rho kinase inhibitor that reduces IOP by increasing aqueous humor outflow through the trabecular meshwork [US].
    - **Latanoprost:** A prostanoid selective FP receptor agonist that increases uveoscleral outflow of aqueous humor [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Netarsudil is absorbed through the cornea and metabolized by ocular esterases to its active metabolite, AR-13503. Latanoprost is a prodrug absorbed through the cornea and hydrolyzed to latanoprost acid [US].
    - **Distribution:** Netarsudil's volume of distribution in humans is approximately 0.16 L/kg. Latanoprost acid is detectable in aqueous humor for 4 hours and plasma for 1 hour post-dose [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Netarsudil undergoes esterase-mediated metabolism in the eye. Latanoprost acid reaching systemic circulation is primarily metabolized by the liver via fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation [US].
    - **Elimination:** Latanoprost acid is rapidly eliminated from plasma ($t_{1/2} = 17$ min). Metabolites are primarily excreted via the kidneys [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study Summaries:** Two randomized, controlled trials (Studies 301 and 302) evaluated patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension (baseline IOP < 36 mmHg).
- **Outcomes:** **ROCKLATAN** administered once daily showed an average IOP reduction that was 1 to 3 mmHg greater than monotherapy with either netarsudil 0.02% or latanoprost 0.005% alone [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Bottles:** Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Protect from light [US].
- **Opened Bottles:** Once opened, the product may be stored at 2°C to 25°C (36°F to 77°F) for up to 6 weeks. If refrigerated after opening, it may be used until the expiration date [US].
- **Shipping:** During transit, the product may withstand temperatures up to 40°C (104°F) for a maximum of 14 days [US].
