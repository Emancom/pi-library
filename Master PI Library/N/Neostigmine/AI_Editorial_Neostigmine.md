# Editorial Product Information: Glycopyrronium bromide and Neostigmine methylsulfate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for information purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Glycopyrronium bromide (also known as glycopyrrolate) and Neostigmine methylsulfate.
- **Chemical Class:** Glycopyrronium is a synthetic quaternary ammonium antimuscarinic agent; Neostigmine is a quaternary ammonium anticholinesterase.
- **Brand Names:** **GLYCONEO 0.5/2.5** [Australian].
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile, preservative-free solution for injection. Each 1 mL contains 0.5 mg glycopyrronium bromide and 2.5 mg neostigmine methylsulfate (1:5 ratio) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** KSJ Pharmatech [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reversal of Neuromuscular Blockade:** Indicated for the reversal of residual effects of non-depolarising (competitive) neuromuscular blocking agents [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- The product is a fixed-dose combination (1:5 ratio). If alternative ratios are clinically required, individual monotherapy products must be used [Australian].
- **Adults:** The standard dose is 0.016 mL/kg administered intravenously. This is equivalent to 0.008 mg/kg of glycopyrronium bromide and 0.04 mg/kg of neostigmine methylsulfate. Alternatively, a ratio of 0.2 mg glycopyrrolate per 1 mg neostigmine may be used [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** Total doses exceeding 2 mL are not recommended, as excessive neostigmine may induce a depolarising neuromuscular block [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer only when the first twitch response is significantly greater than 10% of baseline or when a second twitch is present [Australian].
- Do not dilute the solution prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion; the product contains no antimicrobial preservatives [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to glycopyrronium bromide, neostigmine methylsulfate, or any excipients.
- Mechanical obstruction of the gastrointestinal or urinary tracts.
- Concurrent administration with suxamethonium, as neostigmine potentiates the depolarising effects of this agent [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Monitoring:** Neuromuscular function monitoring must be available and utilised before considering extubation.
- **Cardiovascular Status:** Use with extreme caution in patients with unstable cardiovascular status, including severe bradycardia, arrhythmias, coronary artery disease, recent myocardial infarction, or hypertension. Anticholinergics may induce ventricular arrhythmias during inhalation anaesthesia [Australian].
- **Respiratory Disease:** Use caution in patients with bronchospasm. While neostigmine causes bronchoconstriction, the combination with glycopyrrolate generally results in bronchodilation [Australian].
- **Intestinal Surgery/Conditions:** Caution is advised in patients with intestinal anastomosis (risk of rupture), severe ulcerative colitis, or toxic megacolon [Australian].
- **Ocular Effects:** Use with caution in patients with glaucoma; increases in intraocular pressure have been reported [Australian].
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Large doses of quaternary ammonium compounds can block end-plate nicotinic receptors, which must be considered in this population [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data [Australian]
Based on clinical trial data (N=225), the following adverse reactions were reported:

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Nausea | 7.6% |
| Dry mouth | 3.1% |
| Increased/decreased heart rate | 3.1% |
| Muscle weakness | 1.3% |
| Abdominal pain / Vomiting | 0.9% |
| Prolonged neuromuscular blockade | 0.9% |
| Bradycardia | 0.4% |
| Hypertension | 0.4% |

### Serious and Systemic Effects
- **Immune System:** Hypersensitivity, angioedema, and anaphylactic reactions.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, cardiac arrest, heart block, and prolonged QTc interval (associated with glycopyrrolate).
- **Respiratory:** Bronchospasm, increased secretions, or respiratory arrest (associated with neostigmine overdose).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness, convulsions, or mental confusion (particularly in the elderly) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antimuscarinics:** Increased risk of side effects (dry mouth, blurred vision) when used with MAOIs, tricyclic antidepressants, amantadine, or clozapine [Australian].
- **Corticosteroids:** Concurrent use may increase intraocular pressure. Corticosteroids may also diminish the anticholinesterase effects of neostigmine [Australian].
- **Depolarising Relaxants:** Neostigmine prolongs Phase I blocks induced by suxamethonium [Australian].
- **Antibiotics/Anaesthetics:** Aminoglycosides, local anaesthetics, and certain general anaesthetics may interfere with neuromuscular transmission, potentially requiring higher neostigmine doses [Australian].
- **Other:** Quinine, lithium, and beta-blockers may reduce the effectiveness of neostigmine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Australian Category B2.** Safety has not been established. Glycopyrrolate crosses the placental barrier in very small amounts. 
  - Neostigmine may cause uterine irritability and induce premature labour near term. Use only if benefits outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Anticholinergics may suppress lactation. It is unknown if glycopyrrolate is excreted in milk; neostigmine excretion is negligible. Caution is advised [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use (1 month to 12 years):** Dosing is 0.016 mL/kg. Paradoxical hyperexcitability may occur with glycopyrrolate. Increased sensitivity to anticholinergics is noted in patients with Down’s syndrome or spastic paralysis [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Use with caution, starting at the lower end of the dosing range due to higher frequencies of hepatic, renal, or cardiac impairment. Glycopyrrolate is less likely than atropine to cause postoperative confusion [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Duration of effect may be prolonged as both agents are primarily excreted renally. Dosage adjustments may be necessary [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Neostigmine inhibits acetylcholinesterase, allowing acetylcholine to accumulate at the neuromuscular junction. Glycopyrrolate is a muscarinic antagonist that prevents the unwanted cholinergic effects of neostigmine (e.g., bradycardia, secretions) [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of action is within one minute intravenously. Vagal blocking persists for 2–3 hours; antisialogogue effects persist up to 7 hours [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
  - **Distribution:** Glycopyrrolate has a rapid distribution phase (t1/2 = 5 min). Neither agent readily crosses the blood-brain barrier due to their quaternary ammonium structures.
  - **Elimination:** Glycopyrrolate is >80% excreted unchanged in urine (terminal t1/2 = 1.7 hours). Neostigmine is 50% excreted unchanged in urine, with an elimination t1/2 of 47–60 minutes [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Recovery time from a second twitch (T2) to a Train-of-Four Ratio (TOFR) > 0.9 was approximately 17 minutes after rocuronium or vecuronium administration [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep ampoules in the outer carton to protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
