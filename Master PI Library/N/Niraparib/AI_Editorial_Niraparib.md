# Editorial Product Information: Niraparib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of niraparib for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing authority.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Niraparib (as tosilate monohydrate), a poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **ZEJULA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XK02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Hard capsules: 100 mg (white body with purple cap).
    - Film-coated tablets: 100 mg (grey, oval-shaped).
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **First-line Maintenance:** Monotherapy for the maintenance treatment of adults with advanced high-grade epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer who have achieved a complete or partial response following completion of first-line platinum-based chemotherapy [Australian].
- **Recurrent Maintenance:** Monotherapy for the maintenance treatment of adults with platinum-sensitive relapsed high-grade serous epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer who are in response (complete or partial) to platinum-based chemotherapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment should be initiated and supervised by a physician experienced in oncology. Capsules or tablets should be swallowed whole, with or without food, preferably at the same time each day. Administering the dose at bedtime may help manage nausea.

### Dosing by Indication
- **First-line Maintenance:**
    - Patients weighing $\ge$ 77 kg AND with baseline platelets $\ge$ 150,000/µL: 300 mg (three 100 mg capsules/tablets) once daily.
    - Patients weighing < 77 kg OR with baseline platelets < 150,000/µL: 200 mg (two 100 mg capsules/tablets) once daily.
- **Recurrent Maintenance:** 300 mg once daily.

### Dose Adjustments for Toxicity
- **Dose Reduction Levels:** 
    - Starting dose 300 mg/day $\rightarrow$ 200 mg/day $\rightarrow$ 100 mg/day $\rightarrow$ Discontinue.
    - Starting dose 200 mg/day $\rightarrow$ 100 mg/day $\rightarrow$ Discontinue.
- **Haematological Toxicity:** Withhold niraparib for Grade 3 or 4 reactions. Monitor weekly until recovery. If platelets do not recover to $\ge$ 100,000/µL or neutrophils/haemoglobin do not recover within 28 days, discontinue the drug. 
- **Non-Haematological Toxicity:** For Grade $\ge$ 3 reactions persisting despite prophylaxis, withhold for up to 28 days. Resume at a reduced dose.

### Missed Dose
If a dose is missed, the patient should take the next dose at the regularly scheduled time. No compensatory double dose should be taken.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to niraparib or any excipients.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated during treatment and for one month after the final dose [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Haematological Reactions:** Very common, including thrombocytopenia, anaemia, and neutropenia. Complete blood counts (CBC) must be monitored weekly for the first month, then monthly for the next 10 months, and periodically thereafter.
- **Myelodysplastic Syndrome/Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (MDS/AML):** Reported in approximately 1% of patients, sometimes fatal. Most cases occurred in patients previously treated with platinum chemotherapy or other DNA-damaging agents. Discontinue if MDS/AML is confirmed.
- **Hypertension and Cardiovascular Effects:** Hypertension, including hypertensive crisis, has been reported. Pre-existing hypertension must be controlled before starting. Monitor blood pressure and heart rate weekly for two months, then monthly for the first year.
- **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES):** Rare (0.09% incidence). Symptoms include seizures, headache, and visual disturbances. Diagnosis requires MRI. Discontinue niraparib if PRES occurs.
- **Excipients:** Capsules contain **lactose monohydrate** (avoid in patients with hereditary galactose intolerance/Lapp lactase deficiency) and **tartrazine** (may cause allergic reactions) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Metabolism:** Minimal oxidative metabolism by CYPs. Niraparib is primarily metabolised by carboxylesterases (CEs) to form the inactive metabolite M1.
- **Transporters:** Niraparib inhibits MATE1 and MATE2K. Co-administration with **metformin** may increase metformin exposure (> 2-fold); monitor blood glucose closely.
- **Induction:** Niraparib weakly induces CYP1A2 in vitro. Use caution with narrow therapeutic index substrates of CYP1A2 (e.g., clozapine, theophylline).
- **Substrate Status:** Niraparib is a substrate of P-gp and BCRP, but clinically significant interactions with inhibitors/inducers of these transporters are considered unlikely.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Based on its mechanism, niraparib may cause fetal harm or death. A pregnancy test is required before treatment. Females of childbearing potential must use highly effective contraception during and for 6 months after treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated during and for one month after the last dose due to unknown excretion in human milk.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed decreased spermatogenesis. Reversibility was noted 2–4 weeks after cessation of dosing in dogs, but not rats.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children < 18 years not established; no data available.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients $\ge$ 65 years. Limited data for those $\ge$ 75 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. No data for severe impairment (CLcr < 30 mL/min) or end-stage renal disease; use with caution.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild impairment. For **moderate hepatic impairment**, the recommended starting dose is **200 mg once daily** [Australian]. No data for severe impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Potent inhibitor of PARP-1 and PARP-2. It prevents DNA repair, particularly in cells with deficiencies in homologous recombination (e.g., BRCA1/2 mutations), leading to apoptosis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability ~73%. Peak plasma levels reached in ~3 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 83% protein bound; large volume of distribution (1206 L).
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life is 44 to 54 hours (approx. 2 days). Eliminated via hepatobiliary and renal routes (47.5% in urine, 38.8% in faeces).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PRIMA Trial:** A Phase 3 study in first-line maintenance. Niraparib significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS) compared to placebo in both the HR-deficient population (21.9 vs 10.4 months; HR 0.43) and the overall population (13.8 vs 8.2 months; HR 0.62).
- **NOVA Trial:** A Phase 3 study in recurrent maintenance. Niraparib significantly improved PFS in the germline BRCA-mutated cohort (21.0 vs 5.5 months; HR 0.27) and the non-gBRCAmut cohort (9.3 vs 3.9 months; HR 0.45).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Capsules:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Tablets:** Store below 30°C in the original container [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (**Schedule 4**) [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring in Australia (Black Triangle Scheme) [Australian].
