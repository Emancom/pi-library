# Editorial Product Information: Nafarelin Acetate

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based solely on the provided Product Information from the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is designed to consolidate clinical information into a single, structured reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Nafarelin acetate (a synthetic decapeptide analogue of gonadotrophin-releasing hormone [GnRH]).
*   **Brand Name:** **SYNAREL** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** H01CA02.
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Nasal spray solution containing nafarelin acetate equivalent to 2 mg/mL of nafarelin base. Each metered actuation delivers 200 µg of nafarelin base [Australian].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intranasal.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Endometriosis:** Hormonal management of visually proven endometriosis, including the reduction of endometriotic lesions and relief of associated pain [Australian].
*   **Controlled Ovarian Stimulation:** Use in controlled ovarian stimulation programmes prior to in-vitro fertilisation (IVF), performed under the supervision of an infertility specialist [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Endometriosis
*   **General Dose:** 400 µg daily, administered as one spray (200 µg) to one nostril in the morning and one spray to the other nostril in the evening [Australian].
*   **Initiation:** Treatment should commence between days 2 and 4 of the menstrual cycle [Australian].
*   **Titration:** If symptoms persist and the patient does not achieve amenorrhoea at the 400 µg dose, the dosage may be increased to 800 µg daily (one spray in each nostril twice daily) [Australian].
*   **Duration:** Recommended duration is six months. Retreatment is not recommended due to a lack of safety data; a minimum of two years between treatment courses is suggested if retreatment is considered [Australian].

### Controlled Ovarian Stimulation (IVF)
*   **General Dose:** 800 µg daily, administered as one spray in each nostril in the morning and one spray in each nostril at night [Australian].
*   **Protocol:** Typically involves a "long protocol" starting in either the early follicular phase (day 2) or mid-luteal phase (usually day 21). Treatment continues through the "flare effect" until pituitary down-regulation is achieved (typically within 4 weeks) [Australian].
*   **Maintenance:** Once down-regulated, dose is maintained alongside gonadotrophins until hCG administration [Australian].
*   **Discontinuation:** If down-regulation is not achieved within 12 weeks, therapy should be discontinued [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Pump Priming:** The metered spray pump must be primed before the first use.
*   **Nasal Decongestants:** If required, decongestants should be administered at least 30 minutes *after* nafarelin to avoid impaired absorption [Australian].
*   **Missed Doses:** Patients should consult their physician if doses are missed; for IVF patients, a pregnancy test is required before continuing treatment [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   Hypersensitivity to GnRH, GnRH agonist analogues, or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].
*   Undiagnosed abnormal vaginal bleeding [Australian].
*   Pregnancy or the potential to become pregnant during treatment [Australian].
*   Breastfeeding [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
*   **Bone Density Reduction:** Nafarelin induces a hypoestrogenic state which may result in a decrease in bone mineral density (BMD). Full reversibility of bone loss is not established. Use with caution in women at high risk for reduced bone mass (e.g., chronic glucocorticoid use, strong family history of osteoporosis, weight loss/athletic amenorrhoea) [Australian].
*   **Ovarian Cysts:** Functional ovarian cysts may form during the first two months of therapy, particularly if initiated in the follicular phase [Australian].
*   **Clinical Flare:** A transient increase in estradiol secretion during the first weeks of therapy may cause a temporary "flare-up" of endometriotic symptoms [Australian].
*   **Intercurrent Rhinitis:** Allergic or infectious rhinitis may impair nasal absorption [Australian].
*   **Monitoring:** Diagnostic tests of pituitary-gonadal function may be misleading if performed during or within 4–8 weeks of discontinuing therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily associated with hypoestrogenism.
*   **Very Common (>10%):** Hot flushes, libido changes, vaginal dryness, headaches, emotional lability, acne, myalgia, decreased breast size, and nasal irritation [Australian].
*   **Common (1–10%):** Insomnia, weight changes, oedema, hirsutism, and seborrhoea [Australian].
*   **Other Reported Effects:** Paraesthesia, depression, arthritic symptoms, alopecia, and blood pressure changes [Australian].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Drug sensitivity symptoms (shortness of breath, chest pain, urticaria, rash, pruritus) occurred in approximately 0.2% of patients [Australian].
*   **Serious Effects:** 
    *   **Bone Density Loss:** Average vertebral trabecular bone density decreased by 8.7% after 6 months of treatment, with only partial recovery post-treatment [Australian].
    *   **Ovarian Hyperstimulation Syndrome (OHSS):** A risk when used in conjunction with gonadotrophins for IVF [Australian].
    *   **Respiratory:** Interstitial pneumonitis and pulmonary fibrosis have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Nafarelin is degraded by peptidases, not cytochrome P-450 enzymes; therefore, P-450 mediated drug-drug interactions are not expected [Australian].
*   **Protein Binding:** Nafarelin is 80% bound to plasma proteins (albumin); interactions at the protein-binding level are unlikely [Australian].
*   **Nasal Decongestants:** May reduce absorption if used concurrently. Must be used at least 30 minutes after nafarelin administration [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D**. Nafarelin is contraindicated in pregnancy. Pregnancy must be excluded before treatment initiation. Non-hormonal contraception must be used during therapy. If pregnancy occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately and the patient informed of potential fetal risks [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if nafarelin is excreted in human milk. Due to unknown effects on the infant, nafarelin should not be used by breastfeeding women [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** Studies in rats showed full reversibility of fertility suppression after treatment cessation [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** For endometriosis, clinical experience is limited to women aged 18 years and over [Australian]. Use in central precocious puberty was not detailed in the provided source.
*   **Elderly Use:** No data available [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Specific data are unavailable. No specific dosage adjustments are currently recommended, though extra risks are not anticipated [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Specific data are unavailable. No specific dosage adjustments are currently recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** A potent GnRH agonist. Initial administration stimulates the release of pituitary gonadotrophins (LH and FSH), increasing steroidogenesis. Continuous administration (3–4 weeks) leads to pituitary desensitisation and suppression of gonadal steroidogenesis [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    *   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed after intranasal dosing; peak plasma concentration at 26 minutes (range 5–60 mins) [Australian].
    *   **Bioavailability:** Average of 2.8% (range 1.2–5.6%) [Australian].
    *   **Distribution:** 78–84% bound to plasma proteins (primarily albumin) [Australian].
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily degraded by peptidases; metabolites are thought to be inactive [Australian].
    *   **Elimination:** Recovered in both urine (44–56%) and faeces (19–44%) after subcutaneous dosing [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Endometriosis:** Studies demonstrated relief of symptoms (pelvic pain, dyspareunia, dysmenorrhoea) and reduction in the size of endometrial implants. Amenorrhoea was achieved in approximately 90% of patients after 120 days of treatment at 400 µg daily [Australian].
*   **IVF:** In a study of 160 women, 100% achieved down-regulation using 400 µg BD. Clinical pregnancy rates in pivotal trials ranged from 23% to 31% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
*   **Conditions:** Protect from light. Store the container in an upright position. Do not refrigerate or freeze [Australian].
*   **Shelf Life:** 24 months [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **Australia:** Schedule 4 (S4) - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
