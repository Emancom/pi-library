# Editorial Product Information: Naltrexone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Note:** At the time of publication, PI documents from other major jurisdictions (e.g., US FDA, European EMA, Health Canada) were not provided for comparison. Consequently, this document reflects the harmonised position of the available Australian data.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference; it is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Naltrexone hydrochloride (a synthetic congener of oxymorphone; pure opioid antagonist).
- **Brand Names:** ARX-NALTREXONE [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 50 mg film-coated, oval, biconvex tablets with a break line [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Alcohol Dependence:** Indicated as part of a comprehensive treatment program to support abstinence and reduce relapse.
- **Opioid Dependence:** Adjunctive therapy for the maintenance of formerly opioid-dependent patients who have ceased opioid use (e.g., heroin, morphine).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Alcohol Dependence:** 50 mg once daily is the recommended dose for most patients [Australian].
- **Opioid Dependence:** 
    - **Induction:** Do not initiate until the patient has been opioid-free for 7–10 days. 
    - **Initial Dose:** 25 mg. If no withdrawal signs occur, the patient may be increased to 50 mg daily thereafter.
- **Alternative Dosing:** While 50 mg every 24 hours is standard, flexible dosing or extended intervals may be used, though the degree of blockade may be reduced. 

### Administration Instructions
- **Opioid-Free Verification:** Abstinence should be verified by urine analysis and clinical assessment.
- **NARCAN (Naloxone) Challenge Test:** Should be performed if occult opioid use is suspected.
    - **Intravenous:** 0.2 mg initially; if no withdrawal signs after 30 seconds, inject a further 0.6 mg. Observe for 20 minutes.
    - **Subcutaneous:** 0.8 mg; observe for 20 minutes.
    - **Interpretation:** If signs of withdrawal (nausea, dysphoria, yawning, sweating, etc.) appear, the test is positive; do NOT start naltrexone.

## 4. Contraindications
- Patients currently receiving opioid analgesics.
- Patients currently dependent on opioids (acute withdrawal may ensue).
- Patients in acute opioid withdrawal.
- Individuals with a positive urine screen for opioids or those who failed the naloxone challenge.
- History of hypersensitivity to naltrexone or any component of the formulation (cross-sensitivity with naloxone or phenanthrene opioids is unknown).
- **Acute hepatitis or liver failure** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Naltrexone can cause hepatocellular injury when given in excessive doses. It is contraindicated in acute hepatitis or liver failure. The margin of safety is narrow (approximately five-fold between therapeutic and hepatotoxic doses).
- **Unintended Precipitation of Abstinence:** To avoid severe withdrawal, patients must be opioid-free for 7–10 days.
- **Overcoming Blockade:** Patients should be warned that attempting to overcome the naltrexone blockade by taking large doses of opioids is extremely dangerous and can lead to fatal overdose (respiratory arrest, circulatory collapse).
- **Post-Treatment Sensitivity:** After stopping naltrexone, patients may be more sensitive to lower doses of opioids than previously used, increasing the risk of overdose.
- **Suicide Risk:** Substance abuse patients, with or without depression, have an increased risk of suicide; naltrexone treatment does not eliminate this risk.
- **Emergency Analgesia:** If opioid analgesia is required in an emergency, regional analgesia or non-opioid options are preferred. If opioids must be used, the dose required will be higher, and respiratory depression may be deeper and more prolonged.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse effects occur early in therapy and are transient.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Nausea, headache, anxiety, nervousness, abdominal pain/cramps, difficulty sleeping, low energy, joint/muscle pain. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Dizziness, vomiting, fatigue, loss of appetite, diarrhoea, constipation, increased thirst, irritability, skin rash, delayed ejaculation. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Chest pain, nasal congestion, cough, palpitations, tachycardia, excessive gas, tremors, acne, alopecia, depression, blurred vision, tinnitus. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Hepatotoxicity (dose-related), precipitated withdrawal syndrome (may include mental status changes, hallucinations, and severe fluid loss).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Opioid-containing medications:** Naltrexone antagonises the effects of opioid-containing cough/cold preparations, antidiarrheals, and analgesics.
- **Sedating medications:** Concomitant use with neuroleptics, benzodiazepines, or sedating antidepressants is not recommended [Australian].
- **Acamprosate:** Co-administration significantly increases the rate and extent of acamprosate absorption.
- **Disulfiram:** Safety of concomitant use is unknown; combined use of two potentially hepatotoxic agents is generally discouraged.
- **Thioridazine:** Reports of lethargy and somnolence when combined with naltrexone.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3 [Australian]**. Animal studies show increased early foetal loss at doses ≥30 mg/kg. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Naltrexone and its metabolite are excreted in rat milk. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed increased pseudo-pregnancy and decreased pregnancy rates at high doses (100 mg/kg). Male fertility was unaffected in animal models.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established; use is not recommended.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available.
- **Renal Impairment:** Naltrexone and its metabolites are excreted primarily in urine; caution is recommended.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Bioavailability is significantly increased in patients with liver cirrhosis (5- to 10-fold increase in AUC). Extreme caution is required due to the drug's hepatotoxic potential.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A pure opioid antagonist that competitively binds to opioid receptors, blocking the subjective effects of exogenous opioids. In alcoholism, the mechanism is not fully understood but likely involves the endogenous opioid system.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** A 50 mg dose blocks 25 mg of IV heroin for 24 hours. Blockade lasts 48 hours at 100 mg and 72 hours at 150 mg.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and nearly complete (96%) via the GI tract. Peak plasma levels occur within one hour.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive first-pass metabolism (bioavailability 5–40%). The major active metabolite is **6-beta-naltrexol**.
    - **Distribution:** Low protein binding (21%).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (53–79% of dose). Mean half-life: 4 hours (parent), 13 hours (6-beta-naltrexol).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Alcohol Dependence:** Placebo-controlled 12-week trials (50 mg/day) demonstrated superior abstention rates (51% vs 23%) and lower relapse rates compared to placebo when used as an adjunct to psychotherapy.
- **Opioid Dependence:** Clinical trials confirmed complete blockade of the euphoric effects of opioids, though efficacy in preventing recidivism is often limited by poor medication compliance.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
- **Packaging:** PVC/PVdC/Al or PVC/PE/PCTFE/Al blister packs.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
