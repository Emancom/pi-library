# Editorial Product Information: **norethisterone + mestranol**

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **norethisterone + mestranol**
- Chemical class: Combined hormonal contraceptive (progestogen + oestrogen)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Oral contraception (prevention of pregnancy).

## 4. Dosage and Administration

General dosing (adult)
- Typical regimen described: 21 days of active **norethisterone + mestranol** tablets followed by 7 days of inactive tablets (placebo) to complete a 28‑day cycle. Active tablet strength described in available sources: norethisterone 1 mg + mestranol 50 µg per active tablet.

Administration instructions
- Take one active tablet daily at approximately the same time each day (intervals not to exceed 24 hours). Preferably take at bedtime.
- After 21 consecutive active tablets, take one placebo/inactive tablet daily for seven days; withdrawal bleeding usually occurs within 2–4 days after the last active tablet.
- Begin the next pack on the day after the last tablet of the previous pack (i.e. do not skip days between packs), starting with an active tablet.

Initiation and switching
- First cycle (start): Begin on day 1 of the menstrual cycle (first day of bleeding); take the active tablet corresponding to that day and continue daily.
- Switching from another 28‑day oral contraceptive: Finish current 28‑day pack, then start this product on the next day (take an active tablet corresponding to the day of week). Use a non‑hormonal additional contraceptive method until seven consecutive active tablets have been taken during the first cycle with the new product.
- Switching from a 21‑day oral contraceptive pack: Wait seven days after the last active tablet from the 21‑day pack, then start this product on the eighth day by taking an active tablet. Use a non‑hormonal additional contraceptive method until seven consecutive active tablets have been taken during the first cycle.
- Postpartum/postabortum use:
  - Can be initiated postpartum in non‑lactating women or postabortum as soon as the first normal menstrual period following a normal biphasic cycle occurs.
  - If avoiding any further pregnancy is medically required, initiation must occur by the 12th (but not before the 7th) day postpartum, or immediately postabortum, or by the 5th day postabortum at the latest.
  - When administered in the immediate postpartum/postabortum period, the increased risk of thromboembolic disease must be considered.

Titration schedules
- Not specified in available sources.

Missed dose instructions
- If < 12 hours late taking an active tablet: take it immediately and continue as usual (no additional contraceptive precautions required).
- If > 12 hours late taking an active tablet: continue taking tablets daily as usual (ignore the missed tablet[s]) but use additional non‑hormonal contraception (not rhythm/temperature methods) for the next seven days. If these seven days extend into the inactive tablet interval, start a new pack immediately after the last active tablet of the current pack (i.e. skip inactive tablets), which may delay the next withdrawal bleeding and result in no bleed until end of the second pack.
- Missing one or more inactive tablets: protection is maintained provided active tablets are started on the appropriate day of the next pack.
- If adherence has been poor (missed one or more active tablets or started later than recommended), consider the possibility of pregnancy at the time of the first missed period before resuming the product.
- Continuous intake of active tablets from two packs: a withdrawal bleed should occur at the end of the second pack; if it does not, pregnancy should be ruled out before resuming.

Vomiting or diarrhoea
- Mild laxatives do not impair effectiveness. If vomiting or diarrhoea occurs during or shortly after tablet intake, contraceptive reliability may be jeopardised.
- Do not interrupt tablet taking to avoid premature withdrawal bleeding. Use additional non‑hormonal contraception during the episode and for seven days after recovery. If these seven days extend into the inactive tablet interval, start a new pack after the last active tablet (skip inactive tablets). If gastrointestinal upset is protracted, consider alternative methods of contraception.

Concurrent medication
- If taking drugs that may interact with **norethisterone + mestranol**, continue the tablet regimen but employ a non‑hormonal contraceptive method during the interacting treatment and for seven days after stopping the interacting drug. If these seven days extend into the inactive tablet interval, start a new pack immediately after the last active tablet (skip inactive tablets).
- If interacting medication is used chronically, consider using an alternative contraceptive method.

## 5. Contraindications
Do not use **norethisterone + mestranol** in the presence of any of the following conditions. If any of these conditions appear for the first time during use, stop the product immediately.

- Presence or risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE), including:
  - Current VTE (on anticoagulants) or history of deep venous thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE) or other thrombotic disorder.
  - Known hereditary or acquired predisposition for VTE (e.g. Activated Protein C resistance including Factor V Leiden, antithrombin III deficiency, protein C deficiency, protein S deficiency).
  - Major surgery with prolonged immobilisation.
  - High risk of VTE due to multiple risk factors.
- Presence or risk of arterial thromboembolism (ATE), including:
  - Current ATE or history of ATE (e.g. myocardial infarction or stroke) or prodromal condition (e.g. angina pectoris or transient ischaemic attack).
  - Known hereditary or acquired predisposition for arterial thromboembolism (e.g. hyperhomocysteinaemia, antiphospholipid antibodies such as anticardiolipin antibodies and lupus anticoagulant).
  - Headaches with focal neurological symptoms (such as aura) including haemiplegic migraine.
  - High risk of ATE due to multiple risk factors or the presence of one serious risk factor such as diabetes with vascular symptoms, uncontrolled hypertension, severe dyslipoproteinaemia, or sickle cell anaemia.
- Pancreatitis or history of pancreatitis if associated with severe hypertriglyceridaemia.
- Presence or history of active hepatic disease until liver function values have returned to normal, history of cholestatic jaundice or pruritus in pregnancy, jaundice with prior oral contraceptive use, Dubin–Johnson syndrome, or Rotor syndrome.
- Presence or history of liver tumours (benign or malignant).
- Known or suspected sex steroid‑influenced malignancies (e.g. of the genital organs or breasts).
- Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding.
- Known or suspected pregnancy.
- History of herpes in pregnancy.
- History of otosclerosis with exacerbations in pregnancy.
- Concomitant use with certain hepatitis C antiviral combinations (specifically ombitasvir, paritaprevir, ritonavir and dasabuvir) is contraindicated.
- Hypersensitivity to any of the ingredients.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources as a jurisdictional boxed/black‑box warning. (Serious vascular risks are emphasised under general warnings.)

General warnings and precautions
- Combined oral contraceptives (COCs) increase the risk of venous and arterial thrombotic and thromboembolic events (e.g. myocardial infarction, stroke, DVT, PE). These events are rare in average‑risk women but can be serious and occasionally fatal.
- Use the lowest effective doses of oestrogen and progestogen compatible with contraceptive needs to minimise thrombotic risk.
- The risk of VTE is highest during the first year of use and may be higher when a COC is restarted after a break of ≥4 weeks.
- Baseline VTE risk increases with age, obesity, and smoking. The risk of VTE in pregnancy and in the postpartum period is higher than that associated with COC use.
- Products containing > 50 µg ethinylestradiol are associated with greater VTE risk; products containing < 35 µg have lower risk. It is unknown how mestranol compares to ethinylestradiol regarding VTE risk.
- Among available progestogens, norethisterone is associated with a lower VTE risk compared with some others; prescribe the lowest effective norethisterone dose tolerated.
- If multiple VTE or ATE risk factors are present, the combined risk may be greater than the sum of individual risks; avoid COC use when the benefit–risk balance is unfavourable.
- In women at risk of prolonged immobilisation (major surgery, leg/pelvic surgery, neurosurgery, major trauma), discontinue the product in advance (in elective surgery, at least four weeks before surgery) and do not resume until two weeks after full remobilisation. Consider antithrombotic prophylaxis if not discontinued in advance.
- If hereditary thrombophilia is suspected, refer to a specialist before prescribing.
- Because the immediate postpartum period has increased thromboembolism risk, do not start combined oral contraceptives earlier than day 28 after delivery in a non‑lactating woman or after a second‑trimester abortion.

Monitoring requirements
- Baseline history and physical exam to identify contraindications and risk factors for VTE/ATE (age, smoking status, hypertension, obesity, family history).
- Monitor for signs and symptoms of thrombotic events during use and inform patients about recognition (see below).
- If pregnancy is suspected (e.g. missed withdrawal bleed after continuous active tablet use for two packs), rule out pregnancy before resuming.

Specific clinical precautions
- Advise women to stop the product immediately and seek medical attention if signs of thrombosis or thromboembolism occur.
- Use caution in women with risk factors for arterial disease (age > 35 years, smoking, hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, obesity, diabetes, migraine, positive family history for arterial thromboembolism, and other cardiovascular risk conditions).
- Be alert for new or worsening headaches, particularly migraine with focal neurologic symptoms — consider immediate discontinuation if these occur.

Signs and symptoms requiring immediate evaluation
- Venous thromboembolism (DVT): unilateral leg swelling, pain or tenderness, increased warmth, redness or discoloration.
- Pulmonary embolism (PE): sudden unexplained shortness of breath, rapid breathing, sudden cough (possibly with haemoptysis), sharp chest pain, severe light‑headedness, rapid/irregular heartbeat.
- Arterial events (stroke/TIA): sudden numbness/weakness of face/arm/leg (especially one side), sudden trouble walking/dizziness/loss of balance/coordination, sudden confusion/trouble speaking/understanding, sudden visual disturbances, sudden severe or prolonged headache, loss of consciousness.
- Myocardial infarction: chest pain/pressure, discomfort radiating to back/jaw/arm, sweating, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, extreme weakness, shortness of breath, rapid/irregular heartbeat.

Persistence of risk
- Some epidemiological studies (performed with higher‑dose estrogen formulations, ≥ 50 µg) suggest persistence of increased risk of myocardial infarction or cerebrovascular disease for years after discontinuation in certain populations (e.g. prolonged past use in older age groups). These data were derived from formulations with higher estrogen doses; applicability to this product is uncertain.

Other precautions
- Extremely rare thromboses have been reported in other vascular beds (hepatic, mesenteric, renal, retinal).

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The full adverse event listing for **norethisterone + mestranol** products is not provided in the available sources. The following adverse/outcome events and frequencies are described in the available material; other adverse reactions are not specified in the provided source excerpts.

- Serious adverse reactions described in available sources:
  - Venous thromboembolism (DVT, PE) — VTE increases with COC use. Estimated absolute risk per year (examples reported for COCs):
    - Women not using combined hormonal contraception and not pregnant: about 2 per 10,000 women per year.
    - Women using a COC containing levonorgestrel, norethisterone or norgestimate: about 5–7 per 10,000 women per year.
    - Women using a COC containing etonogestrel or norelgestromin: about 6–12 per 10,000 women per year.
    - Women using a COC containing drospirenone, gestodene, desogestrel or cyproterone: about 9–12 per 10,000 women per year.
    - For some progestogens (e.g. chlormadinone, dienogest, nomegestrol) risk estimates were not available.
    - VTE may be fatal in 1–2% of cases.
  - Arterial thromboembolic events (myocardial infarction, ischemic/hemorrhagic stroke, TIA) — increased risk associated with COC use; frequency depends on individual risk factors and is higher in older, smoking, hypertensive, or otherwise at‑risk women.
  - Extremely rare: thromboses in hepatic, mesenteric, renal or retinal veins and arteries.

## 8. Drug Interactions

Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions described in available sources
- Concomitant medications that may reduce contraceptive efficacy or otherwise interact:
  - Use of drugs that interact with norethisterone or mestranol: patients should continue taking tablets but employ additional non‑hormonal contraception during the interacting treatment and for seven days after stopping the interacting drug. If these seven days extend into the inactive tablet interval, start a new pack immediately after the last active tablet (skip inactive tablets).
  - If interacting drugs are used chronically, alternative contraceptive methods should be considered.

- Specific contraindicated interaction:
  - Concomitant use with certain hepatitis C antiviral combinations (ombitasvir, paritaprevir, ritonavir and dasabuvir) is contraindicated.

- Interactions related to GI upset:
  - Vomiting or diarrhoea may reduce contraceptive reliability; manage by using additional contraception during GI illness and for seven days after recovery.

Clinically significant interactions and management notes
- For any medication known or suspected to reduce systemic hormonal contraceptive concentrations (e.g. enzyme inducers) or otherwise interact: employ an additional barrier method during concomitant therapy and for seven days after; if the interacting medication is long‑lasting (e.g. enzyme induction), consider a non‑hormonal alternative method.
- For chronic interacting therapy where additional contraception is impractical or unreliable, advise switching to or adding a non‑hormonal contraceptive method.
- If an interacting medication must be stopped and seven days of additional protection would extend into the inactive interval, start a new pack after active tablets as described above.

- Other specific interaction details (e.g. with enzyme inducers, antibiotics, herbal products): Not specified in available sources beyond the general guidance above.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

Pregnancy
- Known or suspected pregnancy: **norethisterone + mestranol** is contraindicated.
- Initiation postpartum/postabortum:
  - Can be prescribed postpartum in non‑lactating mothers or postabortum as soon as the first normal menstrual period following a normal biphasic cycle occurs.
  - If further pregnancy is contraindicated, initiation must be by day 12 postpartum (but not before day 7), immediately postabortum, or by day 5 postabortum at the latest.
  - Consider increased thromboembolic risk when initiating in the immediate postpartum/postabortum period.
