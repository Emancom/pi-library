# Editorial Product Information: Naftifine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **naftifine** hydrochloride gel (2%) for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and pharmacological profiles into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Naftifine** hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic allylamine antifungal.
- **Brand Names:** **Naftifine** Hydrochloride Gel, USP 2% (Taro Pharmaceuticals; Sun Pharmaceutical Industries).
- **ATC Code:** D01AE22 (Not explicitly stated in the source but corresponds to the international classification for naftifine).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Topical gel, 2%. Each gram contains 20 mg of **naftifine** hydrochloride [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharma Canada Inc. (Manufacturer); Sun Pharmaceutical Industries, Inc. (Distributor) [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Interdigital Tinea Pedis:** Indicated for the treatment of interdigital tinea pedis caused by *Trichophyton rubrum*, *Trichophyton mentagrophytes*, and *Epidermophyton floccosum* [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** Apply a thin layer of the 2% gel to the affected skin areas and approximately 0.5 inches (1.27 cm) of the surrounding healthy skin once daily.
- **Duration of Treatment:** 2 weeks.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - For topical use only.
    - Not for ophthalmic, oral, or intravaginal use.
    - Patients should wash hands after application unless the hands are the area being treated.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **General:** None listed in the provided source [United States]. 

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None.
- **Local Adverse Reactions:** Treatment should be discontinued if clinical evidence of redness, irritation, or sensitivity develops [United States].
- **General Precautions:** Avoid contact with eyes, mouth, or other mucous membranes. 
- **Monitoring Requirements:** No systemic monitoring is routinely required for this topical agent.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Application site reactions (e.g., irritation, burning) occurred in 2% of patients [United States].
- **Postmarketing Experience (Frequency Not Established):**
    - Skin and Subcutaneous Tissue: Blisters, burning sensation, crusting, dryness, erythema/redness, inflammation, irritation, maceration, pain, pruritus (mild), rash, and swelling [United States].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** None reported in clinical trials or documented in postmarketing summaries provided.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Systemic absorption is minimal; however, formal drug interaction studies have not been conducted with the 2% gel formulation [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified by letter, but a "Risk Summary" approach is used. There are no available data on use in pregnant women to evaluate drug-associated risks.
    - **Animal Data:** No adverse effects on embryofetal development were observed in rats (oral doses up to 37x the maximum recommended human dose [MRHD]) or rabbits (subcutaneous doses up to 7x the MRHD) [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if **naftifine** is excreted in human milk. Clinicians should weigh the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding against the mother’s clinical need for the drug [United States].
- **Fertility:** Oral administration in rats showed no effects on fertility or reproduction at doses up to 12x the MRHD [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - Safety and effectiveness are established for patients aged 12 to 18 years.
    - Safety and effectiveness for paediatric patients under 12 years of age have not been established [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies included subjects up to 92 years of age. No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between geriatric and younger subjects [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, systemic absorption is extremely low (≤0.01%) [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Naftifine** is an allylamine antifungal that appears to interfere with sterol biosynthesis by inhibiting the enzyme squalene 2,3-epoxidase. This leads to decreased ergosterol levels and an accumulation of squalene within the fungal cell [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** In subjects with tinea pedis, the accumulation ratio was approximately 6 over 14 days of once-daily application. Trough plasma concentrations reached steady state after 11 days.
    - **Metabolism:** Not specified in detail in available sources.
    - **Elimination:** The fraction of the dose excreted in urine was ≤0.01% of the applied dose in adults and ≤0.003% in adolescents [United States].
- **Microbiology:** Demonstrates activity against *T. rubrum*, *T. mentagrophytes*, and *E. floccosum*. No mechanism of resistance has been identified to date [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study Design:** Two randomised, double-blind, vehicle-controlled multicenter trials (N=1175) evaluated **naftifine** 2% gel applied once daily for 2 weeks.
- **Primary Outcomes:** Complete cure was assessed 4 weeks after treatment ended (Week 6). Complete cure required both clinical cure (absence of erythema, pruritus, and scaling) and mycological cure (negative KOH and culture).
- **Efficacy Results:**
    - **Trial 1:** Complete cure 17% (vs 2% vehicle); Mycological cure 65% (vs 14% vehicle).
    - **Trial 2:** Complete cure 26% (vs 3% vehicle); Mycological cure 59% (vs 10% vehicle) [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [United States].
- **Special Handling:** Keep the tube tightly closed and out of reach of children.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only).
