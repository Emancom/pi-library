# Editorial Product Information: Sodium Nitroprusside

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized by combining official regulatory data from the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA, Australia) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA, United States). It is intended for use by healthcare professionals and represents a consolidated view of the drug's safety and efficacy profile. Where regional differences exist, the more comprehensive or conservative guidance has been selected and annotated.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Sodium Nitroprusside
**Active Ingredient:** Sodium nitroprusside dihydrate [sodium nitrosylpentacyanoferrate (III)]
**Formulation:** Concentrated injection (50 mg/2 mL) for intravenous infusion.

## 2. Approved Indications
Sodium nitroprusside is indicated for:
*   **Hypertensive Crises:** Immediate reduction of blood pressure in adult and pediatric patients [FDA]. Long-acting oral antihypertensive therapy should be initiated concurrently to minimize the duration of nitroprusside treatment.
*   **Controlled Hypotension:** Induction of hypotension during anaesthesia to minimize blood loss during surgical procedures.
*   **Acute Heart Failure:** Short-term management to increase cardiac output and reduce myocardial oxygen demand by decreasing preload and afterload.

## 3. Contraindications
This medication is contraindicated in the following circumstances:
*   Treatment of compensatory hypertension (e.g., coarctation of the aorta or arteriovenous shunting).
*   Patients with known inadequate cerebral circulation.
*   Emergency surgery in moribund patients (ASA Class 5) [TGA/FDA].
*   Congenital (Leber’s) optic atrophy or tobacco amblyopia, as these patients have a deficiency in rhodanase, increasing the risk of cyanide toxicity.
*   Acute congestive heart failure associated with reduced peripheral vascular resistance (e.g., endotoxic sepsis).
*   Uncorrected anaemia, hypovolaemia, or severe renal disease [TGA].
*   Disease states associated with Vitamin B12 deficiency [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
**Direct Injection:** Sodium nitroprusside must never be administered by direct injection. It is suitable only for intravenous infusion following proper dilution.

**Excessive Hypotension:** Rapid or excessive blood pressure reduction can lead to irreversible ischaemic injury or death. Continuous blood pressure monitoring is mandatory, preferably via intra-arterial sensor. If hypotension persists for more than a few minutes after stopping the infusion, an alternative cause must be investigated.

**Cyanide Toxicity:** Except during very brief or low-dose (< 2 mcg/kg/min) infusions, nitroprusside produces significant quantities of cyanide ions. 
*   **Rate Limit:** Infusions at the maximum rate (10 mcg/kg/min) should never exceed 10 minutes [FDA]. If blood pressure is not adequately controlled after 10 minutes at this rate, the infusion must be terminated immediately.
*   **Signs:** Suspect toxicity if there is a requirement for increasing doses (tachyphylaxis), metabolic (lactic) acidosis, or venous hyperoxaemia (bright red venous blood).

**Intracranial Pressure:** Use with extreme caution in patients with pre-existing elevated intracranial pressure, as nitroprusside may further increase it [TGA].

**Hepatic/Renal Impairment:** Use with caution. The liver is the primary site of cyanide-to-thiocyanate conversion via rhodanase; the kidneys are responsible for thiocyanate excretion. Impairment in either organ increases the risk of metabolic toxicity.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
**Preparation:** Dilute 50 mg of sodium nitroprusside in 250 mL to 1000 mL of sterile 5% glucose (dextrose) in water. No other drugs should be added to the infusion solution.

**Administration:**
*   Must be administered via a volumetric infusion pump to ensure precise flow rates.
*   **Light Protection:** The diluted solution is photosensitive. The infusion bag must be wrapped in the supplied opaque sleeve or aluminium foil. It is not necessary to cover the drip chamber or tubing.
*   **Integrity Check:** The solution should be a faint brownish colour. Discard if it appears blue, green, or bright red, as this indicates chemical degradation.

**Dosing:**
*   **Initial Dose:** 0.3 mcg/kg/min.
*   **Titration:** Gradually increase every few minutes to achieve the desired effect. The average effective dose is 3 mcg/kg/min.
*   **Maximum Dose:** 10 mcg/kg/min. 
*   **Elderly:** Start at the lower end of the dosing range, as geriatric patients may be more sensitive to hypotensive effects [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most critical adverse reactions are avoidable: excessive hypotension and cyanide/thiocyanate toxicity.

**Systemic Effects (often related to rapid BP drop):**
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, retching, abdominal pain, ileus.
*   **Neurological:** Headache, dizziness, restlessness, muscle twitching, apprehension, increased intracranial pressure.
*   **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, bradycardia, ECG changes, palpitations, retrosternal discomfort.
*   **Dermatological/General:** Diaphoresis, rash, flushing, irritation at the injection site, venous streaking.

**Metabolic Toxicities:**
*   **Cyanide Toxicity:** Lactic acidosis, "air hunger," confusion, and death.
*   **Thiocyanate Toxicity:** Hyperreflexia, tinnitus, miosis, and hypothyroidism (due to inhibited iodine uptake).
*   **Methaemoglobinaemia:** Clinically significant levels (>10%) are rare but may occur with massive overdoses.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Antihypertensives:** The hypotensive effect of nitroprusside is potentiated by other antihypertensive agents, ganglion-blocking agents, and negative inotropes.
*   **Anaesthetics:** Volatile liquid anaesthetics (e.g., halothane, enflurane) enhance the hypotensive response.
*   **Transitioning therapy:** Moving from nitroprusside to oral antihypertensives can cause sudden, severe fluctuations in blood pressure.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
**Pregnancy:** (Category C [TGA]). There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in humans. Animal studies in ewes demonstrated that nitroprusside crosses the placenta and can lead to fatal cyanide levels in the foetus. Nitroprusside should only be used in pregnant women if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the foetus and maternal pH/cyanide levels are monitored.

**Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if nitroprusside or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the mother's clinical need [FDA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Rapidly distributes into a volume approximately equal to the extracellular space.
*   **Metabolism:** Nitroprusside reacts with haemoglobin in erythrocytes to release cyanide ions and cyanmethaemoglobin. One molecule of nitroprusside releases four cyanide ions. Cyanide is then converted to thiocyanate in the liver by the enzyme rhodanase, utilizing sulfur donors (thiosulfate).
*   **Half-life:** The parent drug has a circulatory half-life of approximately 2 minutes. The metabolite, thiocyanate, has an elimination half-life of 3 to 4 days, which may double or triple in patients with renal failure.
*   **Excretion:** Thiocyanate is eliminated primarily by the kidneys.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Sodium nitroprusside is a potent, rapid-acting peripheral vasodilator. It acts directly on vascular smooth muscle to cause relaxation of both peripheral arteries and veins. Its effect is more pronounced on veins than on arteries. By dilating these vessels, it reduces peripheral vascular resistance (afterload) and decreases venous return (preload), thereby lowering blood pressure and improving cardiac performance in heart failure.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened Vials:** Store below 25°C and protect from light. Retain in the original carton until time of use.
*   **After Dilution:** To minimize microbiological hazard, the solution should be used as soon as possible. If storage is necessary, the diluted solution is stable for 24 hours when protected from light [FDA/TGA]. Discard any unused portion.
