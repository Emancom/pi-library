# Editorial Product Information: Nivolumab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for nivolumab. It is designed as a concise, clinically accurate reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s current approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nivolumab (rch), a fully human monoclonal immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4) anti-PD-1 antibody.
- **Chemical Class:** Antineoplastic agent; monoclonal antibody.
- **Brand Name:** Opdivo [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01FF01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 10 mg/mL concentrate solution for infusion. Available in 40 mg/4 mL, 100 mg/10 mL, and 240 mg/24 mL single-use vials.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bristol-Myers Squibb [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Nivolumab is approved for the following indications [Australian]:

### Melanoma
- **Adjuvant Treatment:** Monotherapy for adults and adolescents (≥12 years) with completely resected Stage IIB, IIC, III, or IV melanoma.
- **Unresectable or Metastatic:** Monotherapy or in combination with ipilimumab.

### Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC)
- **Neoadjuvant Treatment:** In combination with platinum-doublet chemotherapy for resectable NSCLC.
- **Metastatic/Recurrent:** First-line treatment in combination with ipilimumab and two cycles of platinum-doublet chemotherapy (for patients without EGFR/ALK aberrations).
- **Advanced/Metastatic:** Monotherapy for squamous or non-squamous NSCLC with progression on or after prior chemotherapy (and targeted therapy if EGFR/ALK positive).

### Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC)
- **First-line:** In combination with ipilimumab (intermediate/poor-risk) or in combination with cabozantinib.
- **Advanced:** Monotherapy after prior anti-angiogenic therapy.

### Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma (cHL)
- Relapsed or refractory disease after autologous stem cell transplant (ASCT) and treatment with brentuximab vedotin.

### Other Solid Tumours
- **Malignant Pleural Mesothelioma (MPM):** First-line in combination with ipilimumab.
- **Squamous Cell Carcinoma of the Head and Neck (SCCHN):** Monotherapy for recurrent/metastatic disease progressing after platinum-based therapy.
- **Urothelial Carcinoma (UC):** Adjuvant treatment for high-risk muscle-invasive UC; first-line in combination with cisplatin/gemcitabine; or monotherapy for locally advanced/metastatic disease after platinum therapy.
- **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC):** Monotherapy after sorafenib or first-line in combination with ipilimumab.
- **Oesophageal Cancers:** Adjuvant treatment of resected oesophageal or GOJ cancer after neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy; first-line unresectable/metastatic OSCC (monotherapy, combo with chemo, or combo with ipilimumab).
- **Gastric/GOJ/Oesophageal Adenocarcinoma:** First-line in combination with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-based chemotherapy.
- **Colorectal Cancer (CRC):** MSI-H or dMMR metastatic CRC in combination with ipilimumab.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Nivolumab is administered as a 30-minute IV infusion.
- **Monotherapy:** 3 mg/kg every 2 weeks, 240 mg every 2 weeks, or 480 mg every 4 weeks.
- **Combination with Ipilimumab:** Varies by indication. Common regimens include nivolumab 1 mg/kg + ipilimumab 3 mg/kg every 3 weeks (Melanoma, HCC) or nivolumab 3 mg/kg + ipilimumab 1 mg/kg (RCC, MPM, OSCC).
- **Combination with Chemotherapy:** Typically 360 mg every 3 weeks or 240 mg every 2 weeks.

### Administration Instructions
- Do not administer as an IV push or bolus.
- Must be administered via a dedicated line using an in-line, sterile, non-pyrogenic, low protein binding filter (pore size 0.2–1.2 μm).
- Compatible with PVC, non-PVC, or polyolefin containers and glass bottles.
- Flush the line with 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose after administration.

### Treatment Modifications
- **Dose escalation or reduction is not recommended.**
- Withhold or permanently discontinue doses based on the severity and type of immune-related adverse reactions (irARs). Specific guidelines are provided for pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, nephritis, endocrinopathies, and skin reactions.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to nivolumab or any of the excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [Australian]
**IMMUNE-RELATED ADVERSE REACTIONS WITH NIVOLUMAB AND IPILIMUMAB COMBINATION THERAPY**
- irARs occur more frequently and with greater severity in combination therapy compared to monotherapy.
- irARs can involve any organ system and may be fatal.
- Early diagnosis and management (often involving corticosteroids) are essential.
- Monitor patients at least prior to each dose.

### General Warnings
- **Immune-related Adverse Reactions (irARs):** May occur during treatment or weeks/months after discontinuation. Includes pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, nephritis/renal dysfunction, endocrinopathies (thyroid, hypophysitis, adrenal insufficiency, diabetes), and skin reactions (SJS/TEN).
- **HSCT Complications:** Increased risk of severe graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) in cHL patients receiving nivolumab before or after allogeneic HSCT.
- **Infusion Reactions:** Severe reactions require permanent discontinuation. Premedication may be used for mild/moderate cases.
- **Multiple Myeloma:** Increased mortality was observed when PD-1 inhibitors were added to thalidomide analogues plus dexamethasone (not an approved indication).

### Monitoring Requirements
- Liver function tests (LFTs), serum creatinine, and thyroid function tests (TFTs) at baseline and periodically during treatment.
- Monitor for clinical signs of pneumonitis, colitis, and neurological deterioration.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are mostly immune-related and typically resolve with appropriate management.

### Monotherapy Frequency [Australian]
- **Very Common (>10%):** Fatigue (29%), rash (18.8%), pruritus (15.2%), diarrhoea (14.8%), nausea (10.5%), and various lab abnormalities (increased LFTs, creatinine, lipase; anaemia, lymphopaenia).
- **Common (1–10%):** Infusion-related reactions, hypothyroidism/hyperthyroidism, decreased appetite, peripheral neuropathy, headache, dizziness, dyspnoea, colitis, stomatitis, musculoskeletal pain, and pyrexia.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Pneumonitis, adrenal insufficiency, hepatitis, renal failure, and pancreatitis.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Fatalities have been reported due to pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, and myocarditis.
- Rare but severe: Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), encephalitis, and Myocarditis-Myositis-Myasthenia Gravis Overlap Syndrome.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal PK interaction studies have been conducted. As a monoclonal antibody, nivolumab is not metabolised by CYP450 enzymes; interactions via this pathway are not expected.
- **Systemic Immunosuppressants:** Use of baseline corticosteroids/immunosuppressants should be avoided if possible as they may interfere with the pharmacodynamic activity. However, they can be used to treat irARs once treatment has commenced without impairing nivolumab efficacy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian] **Category D.** Nivolumab is an IgG4 known to cross the placenta. Animal studies show increased fetal loss and neonatal mortality. Use is not recommended unless clinical benefit outweighs potential risk. Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 5 months after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if nivolumab is secreted in human milk. A risk to newborns/infants cannot be excluded. A decision must be made to discontinue breastfeeding or the drug.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in patients ≥12 years weighing ≥40 kg for the adjuvant treatment of melanoma. Use in children <12 years is not established.
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy between patients ≥65 years and younger patients. No dose adjustment is required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild or moderate impairment. Data in severe renal impairment are insufficient.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild or moderate impairment. Nivolumab has not been studied in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C); use with caution.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Nivolumab binds to the PD-1 receptor on T-cells, blocking its interaction with ligands PD-L1 and PD-L2. This inhibits the negative signal that downregulates T-cell activity, thereby potentiating T-cell immune responses against tumours.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Mean volume of distribution at steady state is 6.6 L.
- **Metabolism:** Expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways.
- **Elimination:** Geometric mean elimination half-life is approximately 25 days. Steady state is reached by 12 weeks with Q2W dosing.
- **Clearance:** Decreases over time by approximately 24-26% in certain populations; this is not considered clinically relevant.

## 11. Clinical Trials
Efficacy has been demonstrated across multiple Phase 2 and Phase 3 "Checkmate" and "Attraction" trials [Australian]:
- **Melanoma:** CA20976K (Adjuvant), CA209067 (First-line monotherapy vs. combo with ipilimumab).
- **NSCLC:** CA209816 (Neoadjuvant), CA2099LA (First-line metastatic).
- **RCC:** CA209214 (First-line combo), CA2099ER (Combo with cabozantinib).
- **cHL:** CA209205 and CA209039 (Relapsed/Refractory).
- **UC:** CA209274 (Adjuvant), CA209901 (First-line metastatic).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store in a refrigerator (2°C to 8°C). Do not freeze. Keep in the original package to protect from light.
- **Unopened Vials:** May be stored at room temperature (up to 25°C) for up to 48 hours.
- **Prepared Infusion:** Administration must be completed within 7 days of preparation if stored at 2°C to 8°C (protected from light). A maximum of 8 hours of this period can be at room temperature.
