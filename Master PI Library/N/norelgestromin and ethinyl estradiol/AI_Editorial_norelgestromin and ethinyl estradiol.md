# Editorial Product Information: Norelgestromin and Ethinyl Estradiol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of norelgestromin and ethinyl estradiol transdermal systems for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical use in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Norelgestromin (NGMN) and Ethinyl Estradiol (EE).
- **Chemical Class:** NGMN is a progestin; EE is an estrogen.
- **Brand Names:** **Xulane** [US].
- **ATC Code:** G03AA13 (Norelgestromin and ethinyl estradiol).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:**
    - **Transdermal System (Patch):** 150 mcg/day norelgestromin and 35 mcg/day ethinyl estradiol [US].
    - **Specifications:** Each 14 cm² patch contains 4.86 mg NGMN and 0.53 mg EE [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Transdermal.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mylan Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Contraception:** Indicated for the prevention of pregnancy in women with a Body Mass Index (BMI) < 30 kg/m² for whom a combined hormonal contraceptive (CHC) is appropriate [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Effectiveness may be reduced in women weighing 198 lbs (90 kg) or more [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Cycle Length:** A 28-day (4-week) cycle.
- **Regimen:** One patch is applied weekly for three consecutive weeks (21 days), followed by a patch-free week (7 days) during which withdrawal bleeding is expected [US].
- **Consistency:** Each new patch should be applied on the same day of the week (the "Patch Change Day"). Only one patch should be worn at any time [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Application Sites:** Apply to the upper outer arm, abdomen, buttock, or back. Avoid areas where the patch may be rubbed by tight clothing (e.g., the waistband).
- **Restrictions:** Do **not** apply to the breasts. Do not apply to cut, irritated, or oily skin. Do not use lotions, creams, or make-up at the site.
- **Site Rotation:** Rotate application sites; do not apply a new patch to the exact same location as the previous one [US].

### Initial Start Schedules
- **First Day Start:** Apply the first patch within the first 24 hours of the menstrual period. No backup contraception is required.
- **Sunday Start:** Apply the first patch on the first Sunday after the menstrual period begins. Use non-hormonal backup contraception for the first 7 days of the first cycle [US].

### Missed Dose/Detachment Instructions
- **Partial/Complete Detachment:**
    - **< 24 hours:** Try to reapply; if it does not adhere, replace immediately. No backup required.
    - **≥ 24 hours (or unknown duration):** Stop the current cycle and start a new 4-week cycle immediately. Use non-hormonal backup for the first 7 days of the new cycle.
- **Forgetting to Change Patch (Middle of Cycle):**
    - **1–2 days late (up to 48 hours):** Apply new patch immediately; the next patch should be applied on the usual Change Day.
    - **> 2 days late (48 hours or more):** Start a new 4-week cycle immediately. Use non-hormonal backup for 7 days [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Vascular Risk:** High risk of arterial or venous thromboembolic events (VTE/ATE), including current or past deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, cerebrovascular disease, or coronary artery disease [US].
- **Obesity:** BMI ≥ 30 kg/m² [US].
- **Smoking:** Women over 35 years of age who smoke [US].
- **Hypertension:** Uncontrolled hypertension or hypertension with vascular disease [US].
- **Hepatic Disease:** Liver tumors (benign or malignant) or active liver disease [US].
- **Oncology:** Current diagnosis or history of breast cancer or other estrogen- or progestin-sensitive malignancies [US].
- **Neurology:** Headaches with focal neurological symptoms or migraine with aura (specifically contraindicated in women > 35 years with any migraine) [US].
- **Bleeding:** Undiagnosed abnormal uterine bleeding [US].
- **Pregnancy:** Known or suspected pregnancy [US].
- **Drug-Specific:** Co-administration with Hepatitis C drug combinations containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **Cigarette Smoking and Serious Cardiovascular Events:** Smoking increases the risk of serious cardiovascular events. This risk increases with age (especially > 35 years) and the number of cigarettes smoked.
- **BMI Contraindication:** Xulane is contraindicated in women with a BMI ≥ 30 kg/m² due to a higher risk of VTE compared to lower-BMI users.

### General Warnings
- **Thromboembolic Disorders:** Discontinue if an arterial or venous thrombotic event occurs. Higher estrogen exposure (AUC) with the transdermal system may increase VTE risk compared to some oral contraceptives [US].
- **Surgical Risk:** Discontinue at least 4 weeks before and 2 weeks after major surgery associated with an increased risk of thromboembolism [US].
- **Liver Enzyme Elevations:** ALT elevations > 5x ULN were observed in clinical trials when CHCs were used with certain Hepatitis C regimens [US].
- **Gallbladder Disease:** CHC users have a small increased relative risk of developing gallbladder disease.
- **Carbohydrate and Lipid Effects:** Monitor prediabetic and diabetic women; CHCs may decrease glucose tolerance [US].
- **Headache:** Evaluate new, recurrent, persistent, or severe headaches and discontinue if indicated [US].

The most common reactions (≥ 5%) reported in clinical trials (n = 3,322) include [US]:

| **Abdominal pain** | 8.1% |

| **Vaginal bleeding/menstrual disorders** | 6.4% |
| **Mood, affect, and anxiety disorders** | 6.3% |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Myocardial infarction, stroke, and pulmonary embolism.
- Hepatic adenomas and gallbladder disease.
- Hypertension [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Substances Decreasing Plasma Concentrations
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Drugs such as phenytoin, barbiturates, carbamazepine, rifampin, topiramate, and St. John’s wort may decrease plasma concentrations, potentially leading to breakthrough bleeding or contraceptive failure. Use backup contraception during and for 28 days after stopping the inducer [US].

### Substances Increasing Plasma Concentrations
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Itraconazole, voriconazole, and fluconazole may increase hormone concentrations.
- **Other:** Atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, ascorbic acid, and acetaminophen may increase EE levels [US].

### Effects on Other Drugs
- **Lamotrigine:** CHCs significantly decrease lamotrigine plasma levels, potentially reducing seizure control; dose adjustments may be necessary.
- **Thyroid Replacement:** CHCs increase thyroid-binding globulin, requiring increased doses of thyroid hormone replacement in some patients [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated [US]. Epidemiological studies show no increased risk of birth defects from inadvertent use during early pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended [US]. Estrogen-containing contraceptives can reduce milk production. Small amounts of steroids pass into breast milk.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in women of reproductive age. Use before menarche is not indicated [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not studied in postmenopausal women; not indicated [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in active liver disease [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No specific studies conducted [US].
- **Body Mass Index (BMI):** Contraindicated in women with BMI ≥ 30 kg/m² [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primary action is the suppression of gonadotropins, leading to inhibition of ovulation. Secondary mechanisms include thickening of cervical mucus and endometrial changes [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Steady state is reached within 2 weeks. Total estrogen exposure (AUC) is ~60% higher than oral EE 35 mcg, but peak concentration (Cmax) is ~25% lower.
    - **Metabolism:** NGMN is the active progestin. It is metabolised by the liver; metabolites include norgestrel.
    - **Elimination:** Following patch removal, half-lives are approximately 28 hours (NGMN) and 17 hours (EE). Metabolites are eliminated via renal and fecal pathways [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** In three Phase 3 trials (n = 3,330), the pregnancy rate was 1.07 per 100 woman-years for women aged 18 to 35. 
- **Weight Factor:** 5 of the 15 pregnancies in these trials occurred in women with a baseline weight ≥ 198 lbs, suggesting reduced efficacy in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Store patches in their protective pouches. Apply immediately upon removal from the pouch. Do **not** refrigerate or freeze [US].
- **Disposal:** Fold the used patch so the sticky sides adhere together. Place in a sturdy container and dispose of in the trash. Do not flush [US].
