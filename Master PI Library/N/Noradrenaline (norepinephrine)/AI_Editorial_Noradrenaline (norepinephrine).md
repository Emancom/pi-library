# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administration should occur as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Metabolic Crisis:** In severe cases, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and no less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Dose Adjustments (ESRD):** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are high [US].
- **Target Concentrations:** For metabolic disorders, plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are no listed contraindications for levocarnitine injection in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These can occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].

### Clinical Precautions
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may result in the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (frequency $\ge 5\%$) regardless of causality [US]:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, fever, abdominal pain, asthenia |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, hyperkalemia, peripheral edema |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, paresthesia, depression, insomnia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Leg cramps |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash |
| **Special Senses** | Amblyopia, taste perversion |
| **Urogenital** | Kidney failure, urinary tract infection |

**Serious/Notable Effects:**
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, transient nausea/vomiting, and "fishy" body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. While supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied, studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for pediatric patients with metabolic disorders are consistent with adult weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source, though general clinical monitoring applies.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Special caution is advised regarding the use of *oral* formulations in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg post-dialysis three times weekly increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg) demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
