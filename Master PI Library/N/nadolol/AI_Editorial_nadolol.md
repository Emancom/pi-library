# Editorial Product Information: Nadolol

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **nadolol** for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Nadolol** (Synthetic nonselective beta-adrenergic receptor blocking agent).
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Generic tablets provided as source).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 20 mg, 40 mg, and 80 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amneal Pharmaceuticals NY LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Angina Pectoris:** Indicated for the long-term management of patients with angina pectoris [US].
- **Hypertension:** Indicated for the treatment of hypertension, either alone or in combination with other agents (particularly thiazide-type diuretics), to reduce blood pressure and associated cardiovascular risk [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Dosage must be individualised through titration. Tablets may be administered regardless of food intake [US].
- **Angina Pectoris:**
    - **Initial Dose:** 40 mg once daily.
    - **Titration:** Increase in 40 mg to 80 mg increments every 3 to 7 days until optimal response or significant bradycardia occurs.
    - **Maintenance:** Generally 40 mg to 80 mg once daily. 
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 160 mg or 240 mg daily may be used; safety of doses exceeding 240 mg has not been established [US].
- **Hypertension:**
    - **Initial Dose:** 40 mg once daily.
    - **Titration:** Increase in 40 mg to 80 mg increments until optimal blood pressure is reached.
    - **Maintenance:** Generally 40 mg to 80 mg once daily.
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 240 mg or 320 mg once daily may be required [US].
- **Discontinuation:** If treatment is to be stopped, the dose must be reduced gradually over 1 to 2 weeks [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Patients should be advised on the proper course for a missed dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Bronchial asthma [US].
- Sinus bradycardia [US].
- Conduction block greater than first degree [US].
- Cardiogenic shock [US].
- Overt cardiac failure [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Exacerbation of Ischemic Heart Disease):** Abrupt withdrawal in patients with coronary artery disease may lead to exacerbated angina, myocardial infarction, or ventricular dysrhythmias. Therapy should be tapered over 1 to 2 weeks. If angina worsens, therapy should be temporarily reinstituted [US].
- **Cardiac Failure:** Beta-blockade may precipitate more severe failure in patients with congestive heart failure. Use with caution in well-compensated patients (e.g., those on digitalis/diuretics). **Nadolol** does not abolish the inotropic effect of digitalis [US].
- **Nonallergic Bronchospasm:** Patients with chronic bronchitis or emphysema should generally not receive beta-blockers; use with extreme caution if necessary [US].
- **Diabetes and Hypoglycemia:** **Nadolol** may mask early warning signs of acute hypoglycemia (e.g., tachycardia). It also reduces insulin release in response to hyperglycemia, necessitating dose adjustments of antidiabetic drugs [US].
- **Thyrotoxicosis:** May mask clinical signs of hyperthyroidism (e.g., tachycardia). Abrupt withdrawal may precipitate thyroid storm [US].
- **Major Surgery:** Chronic beta-blockade should not be routinely withdrawn prior to surgery, but clinicians should be aware of the impaired ability of the heart to respond to reflex adrenergic stimuli [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse effects are mild and transient.

| System Organ Class | Common (1–10%) | Uncommon to Rare (<1%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiovascular** | Bradycardia (<60 bpm), Symptomatic bradycardia (<40 bpm; 2%), Peripheral vascular insufficiency/Raynaud type (2%), Heart failure/Hypotension/Rhythm disturbances (1%) | First and third-degree heart block |
| **Central Nervous System** | Dizziness or fatigue (2%) | Paresthesias, sedation, behavioural changes (0.6%), Reversible mental depression, hallucinations, disorientation, short-term memory loss |
| **Respiratory** | Not specified | Bronchospasm (0.1%), cough, nasal stuffiness |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Not specified | Nausea, diarrhea, constipation, vomiting, bloating (0.1–0.5%), Ischemic colitis, elevated liver enzymes |
| **Dermatologic/Allergic** | Not specified | Rash, pruritus (0.1–0.5%), Reversible alopecia, Pemphigoid rash, laryngospasm |
| **Other** | Not specified | Dry mouth/eyes, impotence/decreased libido, weight gain, tinnitus, blurred vision (0.1–0.5%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenic purpura, and mesenteric arterial thrombosis have been reported with beta-blockers, though causal relationships to **nadolol** are not always established [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **General Anesthetics:** May exaggerate hypotension [US].
- **Antidiabetic Drugs:** May cause hypoglycemia or hyperglycemia; requires dose adjustment of insulin or oral agents [US].
- **Catecholamine-depleting Drugs (e.g., Reserpine):** Additive effect; monitor for hypotension and excessive bradycardia (vertigo, syncope) [US].
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** Both agents slow AV conduction; concomitant use increases bradycardia risk [US].
- **Epinephrine:** Patients on beta-blockers may be unresponsive to standard doses of epinephrine used for anaphylaxis [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter, but US source notes evidence of embryo- and fetotoxicity in animal studies (rabbits) at 5 to 10 times the human dose. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk. Neonates may exhibit bradycardia and hypoglycemia if the mother receives **nadolol** at parturition [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Nadolol** is excreted in human milk. Due to potential infant risks, a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources (General caution for renal function typically applies).
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage interval adjustment is required as **nadolol** is excreted principally by the kidneys [US]:

| Creatinine Clearance (mL/min/1.73m²) | Dosage Interval (Hours) |
| :--- | :--- |
| >50 | 24 |
| 31 to 50 | 24 to 36 |
| 10 to 30 | 24 to 48 |
| <10 | 40 to 60 |

- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (Note: **Nadolol** is not metabolised by the liver) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Nadolol** is a nonselective beta-adrenergic receptor blocker. It competes with agonists for both beta-1 (cardiac) and beta-2 (bronchial/vascular) receptors. It lacks intrinsic sympathomimetic activity (ISA) and has no membrane-stabilizing activity [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces heart rate and cardiac output, inhibits isoproterenol-induced tachycardia, and reduces reflex orthostatic tachycardia. It decreases myocardial oxygen demand [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Variable; average 30%. Peak serum concentrations occur in 3 to 4 hours. Food does not affect absorption [US].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 30% protein bound. Low lipophilicity (minimal crossing of the blood-brain barrier) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Not metabolised by the liver [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted unchanged principally by the kidneys. Half-life is 20 to 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hypertension:** Controlled studies showed doses of 40 to 320 mg/day decrease both standing and supine blood pressure for 24 hours. While various classes of antihypertensives reduce cardiovascular mortality, specific risk reduction trials for **nadolol** tablets were not cited in the source [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from light and avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Keep bottle tightly closed [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
