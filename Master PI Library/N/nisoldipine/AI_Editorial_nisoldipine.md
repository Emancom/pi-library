# Editorial Product Information: Nisoldipine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial is based on Product Information (PI) documentation provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the clinical and regulatory profile of nisoldipine for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial summary and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction for prescribing purposes.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nisoldipine (dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker).
- **Chemical Class:** 3,5-pyridinedicarboxylic acid, 1,4-dihydro-2,6-dimethyl-4-(2-nitrophenyl)-, methyl 2-methylpropyl ester.
- **Brand Names:** **SULAR** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Extended-Release Tablets:** 8.5 mg, 17 mg, and 34 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Covis Pharma US, Inc [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Indicated for the treatment of hypertension, either as monotherapy or in combination with other antihypertensive agents [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Initial dose is typically 17 mg orally once daily.
- **Titration:** Dosage may be increased by 8.5 mg at weekly intervals or longer to achieve desired blood pressure control. 
- **Maintenance Dose:** Usually ranges from 17 mg to 34 mg once daily. Doses exceeding 34 mg/day are not recommended [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Must be taken on an empty stomach (1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal). 
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not crush, chew, or divide.
    - Avoid administration with grapefruit juice due to significant interaction risks.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to nisoldipine or other dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Increased Angina and/or Myocardial Infarction:** Rarely, patients with severe obstructive coronary artery disease have experienced increased frequency, duration, or severity of angina or acute myocardial infarction upon starting therapy or during dosage increases [US].
- **Hypotension:** As a vasodilator, nisoldipine may cause symptomatic hypotension. Close monitoring is required during initiation and titration, particularly in patients already taking other antihypertensives [US].
- **Congestive Heart Failure:** Use with caution in patients with heart failure or compromised ventricular function, particularly when combined with beta-blockers, as safety in these populations has not been fully established [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Nisoldipine is extensively metabolised by the liver. Patients with cirrhosis may exhibit 5-fold higher plasma concentrations; cautious dosing and monitoring are required [US].
- **Tartrazine Sensitivity:** The 17 mg tablet contains FD&C Yellow No. 5 (tartrazine), which may cause allergic reactions (including bronchial asthma) in susceptible individuals, particularly those with aspirin hypersensitivity [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole) should generally be avoided. 
    - **Cimetidine:** Increases nisoldipine AUC and $C_{max}$ by 30–45% [US].
    - **Grapefruit Juice:** Significantly interferes with metabolism, increasing $C_{max}$ approx. 3-fold and AUC approx. 2-fold [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** 
    - **Phenytoin:** Reduces nisoldipine plasma levels to undetectable concentrations; coadministration should be avoided [US].
- **Quinidine:** Nisoldipine may increase plasma quinidine levels by 20%, while quinidine may decrease nisoldipine AUC by 26% [US].
- **Beta-blockers:** Propranolol may attenuate heart rate increases associated with immediate-release nisoldipine; antihypertensive effects may be enhanced when used with atenolol [US].
- **No Significant Interaction:** No significant interactions were observed with ranitidine, warfarin, or digoxin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** [US: Pregnancy data not explicitly categorized by letter but detailed below]. 
    - **Risk Summary:** There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies showed fetotoxicity and increased resorption at maternally toxic doses (5–16 times the MRHD). Developmental abnormalities (forelimb/vertebral) were seen in monkeys at 30 times the MRHD. Nisoldipine should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if nisoldipine is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the importance of the drug to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in rats at doses up to 5 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Patients over 65 years typically exhibit 2-fold to 3-fold higher plasma concentrations. A lower starting dose ($\le$ 8.5 mg daily) is recommended with cautious titration [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics are not significantly altered in mild-to-moderate renal impairment; dose adjustments are not required [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Bioavailability is significantly increased (4–5 fold) in patients with liver cirrhosis. A lower starting dose ($\le$ 8.5 mg daily) is recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A dihydropyridine calcium channel antagonist that inhibits the transmembrane influx of calcium into vascular smooth muscle and cardiac muscle. It is highly selective for vascular smooth muscle, leading to systemic vasodilation and reduced peripheral vascular resistance [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is approximately 5%. Significant food effect: high-fat meals increase $C_{max}$ by up to 245% and decrease AUC by 25% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Very high protein binding (<1% unbound) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised, primarily by hydroxylation in the gut wall and liver. CYP3A4 is believed to be the major enzyme involved. One metabolite is active but only 10% as potent as the parent drug [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 13.7 hours. 60–80% is excreted in urine, mostly as metabolites; only traces are found unchanged [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces blood pressure in a dose-related manner. Chronic use results in sustained decreases in vascular resistance with minimal effect on heart rate or cardiac conduction (except for mild shortening of the sinus cycle) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hypertension Efficacy:** Studied in 5 double-blind, placebo-controlled trials. Monotherapy produced sustained reductions in supine and standing blood pressure over 24 hours. Trough/peak ratios ranged from 70% to 100% [US].
- **Combination Therapy:** When added to atenolol, nisoldipine doses of 17 mg and 34 mg produced additional blood pressure reductions of 12/6 and 19/8 mmHg, respectively [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light and moisture. Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers [US].

- **Legal Classification:**
