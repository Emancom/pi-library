# Editorial Product Information: Nilotinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Nilotinib-Teva (Nilotinib) Capsules.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs). 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Nilotinib (as hydrochloride dihydrate), a tyrosine kinase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s)**: Nilotinib-Teva [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: L01EA03 (not explicitly in source, but standard for Nilotinib).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: 
    - Hard capsules: 150 mg and 200 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Teva Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Newly Diagnosed Ph+ CML-CP**: Treatment of adult patients with newly diagnosed Philadelphia chromosome-positive chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML) in chronic phase (CP) [Australian].
- **Resistant or Intolerant Ph+ CML-CP/AP**: Treatment of adult patients with chronic phase (CP) or accelerated phase (AP) Philadelphia chromosome-positive CML who are resistant to or intolerant of prior therapy, including imatinib [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Newly Diagnosed Ph+ CML-CP**: 300 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Resistant or Intolerant Ph+ CML-CP and CML-AP**: 400 mg twice daily [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing**: Doses should be taken approximately 12 hours apart.
- **Food Interaction**: Must be taken on an empty stomach. No food should be consumed for at least 2 hours before the dose and at least 1 hour after the dose is taken.
- **Method**: Capsules must be swallowed whole with water; do not crush or dissolve.
- **Missed Dose**: If a dose is missed, the patient should not take an additional dose but should wait until the next scheduled dose.

### Treatment Discontinuation (Treatment-Free Remission - TFR)
Discontinuation may be considered in eligible Ph+ CML-CP patients who have achieved a sustained deep molecular response (MR 4.5) for at least one year, following a minimum of 3 years of nilotinib therapy. This process requires frequent molecular monitoring (BCR-ABL transcript levels) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to nilotinib or any of the excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myelosuppression**: Frequently associated with Grade 3/4 thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and anaemia, particularly in resistant/intolerant patients or those in accelerated phase. Monitor CBC every two weeks for the first two months, then monthly [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation**: Nilotinib has the potential to prolong the QT interval. Baseline ECG is required, followed by an ECG 7 days after initiation and periodically thereafter. Correct hypokalaemia or hypomagnesaemia prior to administration [Australian].
- **Sudden Death**: Uncommon cases (0.1 to 1%) have been reported, potentially linked to ventricular repolarisation abnormalities [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Events**: Reports of peripheral arterial occlusive disease (PAOD), ischemic heart disease, and ischemic cerebrovascular events. Monitor cardiovascular status and manage risk factors (e.g., blood pressure, lipids) [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity**: Risk of increased bilirubin, ALT, and AST levels. Monitor liver function monthly [Australian].
- **Metabolic Abnormalities**: Increases in serum cholesterol and blood glucose have been reported. Monitor lipid profiles and glucose levels before and during therapy [Australian].
- **HBV Reactivation**: Screening for Hepatitis B is required before initiation. Carriers must be monitored during and for several months after therapy [Australian].
- **Fluid Retention**: Severe forms (e.g., pleural effusion, pulmonary oedema) have been observed [Australian].
- **Excipients**: Contains lactose; not recommended for patients with rare hereditary problems of galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Table (Non-haematologic)
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Rash, pruritus, headache, nausea, fatigue, alopecia, myalgia, upper abdominal pain, constipation, diarrhoea [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Muscle spasms, arthralgia, peripheral oedema, vomiting, asthenia, dizziness, insomnia, anxiety, skin papilloma [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pancreatitis, myocardial infarction, cardiac failure, pleural effusion, pulmonary oedema, febrile neutropenia [Australian]. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Sudden death.
- Grade 3/4 Myelosuppression (Neutropenia up to 42% in CML-AP) [Australian].
- Ischemic cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events.
- Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors**: (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir) Increase nilotinib concentration. Avoid concomitant use; if essential, consider dose interruption [Australian].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers**: (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St. John’s Wort) Decrease nilotinib concentration and efficacy. Avoid concomitant use [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates**: Nilotinib may increase the systemic exposure of drugs primarily metabolised by CYP3A4, especially those with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., alfentanil, cyclosporine) [Australian].
- **Warfarin**: Potential for interaction (CYP2C9); monitor INR, especially when initiating nilotinib [Australian].
- **pH-Dependent Solubility**: Solubility decreases as pH increases. Avoid antacids within 2 hours of a dose. H2 blockers should be administered 10 hours before or 2 hours after nilotinib [Australian].
- **Grapefruit Products**: Avoid as they inhibit CYP3A4 [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category D** [Australian]. Can cause foetal harm. Use in pregnancy is not recommended. Women of childbearing potential must use highly effective contraception during and for 2 weeks after treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: Not recommended. Safety not established in humans; animal studies show excretion in milk. Avoid breastfeeding during treatment and for 2 weeks after the final dose [Australian].
- **Fertility**: No effects on sperm count/motility in animal studies. Human data is not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents under 18 have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No major differences in safety or efficacy in patients ≥ 65 years compared to younger adults [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: Not studied in patients with impaired renal function. Nilotinib and its metabolites are not significantly renally excreted [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Use with caution. Exposure (AUC) increases in patients with hepatic impairment (19% to 35% depending on severity) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Selective inhibitor of BCR-ABL tyrosine kinase activity. It binds to the ATP-binding site, inhibiting the proliferation of CML cell lines [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Approximately 30% absorbed. Peak concentrations reached in 3 hours. Bioavailability increases significantly with food [Australian].
    - **Distribution**: 98% plasma protein bound. Blood-to-serum ratio of 0.68 [Australian].
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily hepatic via CYP3A4 [Australian].
    - **Elimination**: >90% eliminated within 7 days, mainly in faeces (93%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Newly Diagnosed CML (ENESTnd)**: A Phase III trial comparing nilotinib (300 mg or 400 mg BID) to imatinib (400 mg QD). Nilotinib 300 mg BID showed a significantly higher Major Molecular Response (MMR) rate at 12 months (44.3%) compared to imatinib (22.3%) [Australian].
- **Resistant/Intolerant CML**: A Phase II study in patients failing imatinib showed major cytogenetic response (MCyR) in 59% of CP patients and a confirmed haematological response in 55% of AP patients [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- Store in original package to protect from moisture [Australian].
