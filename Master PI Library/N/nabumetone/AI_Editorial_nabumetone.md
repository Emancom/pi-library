# Editorial Product Information: Nabumetone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of nabumetone for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for the most current legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nabumetone (a non-acidic naphthylalkanone prodrug).
- **Brand Names:** Nabumetone (Generic), various manufacturers including ANI Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].
- **ATC Code:** M01AX01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - **Oral Tablets:** 500 mg and 750 mg film-coated tablets [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ANI Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Osteoarthritis (OA):** Relief of signs and symptoms of osteoarthritis [US].
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):** Relief of signs and symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:**
  - **Recommended Starting Dose:** 1,000 mg taken as a single daily dose, with or without food.
  - **Titration:** Some patients may require 1,500 mg to 2,000 mg per day for symptomatic relief.
  - **Frequency:** May be administered as a single dose or in twice-daily divided doses.
  - **Maximum Dose:** 2,000 mg per day. Doses exceeding this limit have not been studied [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
  - Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration possible.
  - In patients weighing less than 50 kg, clinical response should be monitored before increasing beyond 1,000 mg daily [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources; standard clinical practice suggests taking as soon as remembered unless the next dose is imminent.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to nabumetone or any excipients.
- **Aspirin Sensitivity:** History of asthma, urticaria, or allergic-type reactions following aspirin or other NSAID use.
- **Surgical Setting:** Use in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery [US].
- **History of Serious Skin Reactions:** Contraindicated in patients with previous serious skin reactions to NSAIDs [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [US]:**
  - **Cardiovascular (CV) Thrombotic Events:** NSAIDs cause an increased risk of serious CV thrombotic events, including fatal myocardial infarction and stroke. Risk may occur early in treatment and increases with duration of use.
  - **Gastrointestinal (GI) Risk:** Increased risk of serious GI adverse events including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation of the stomach or intestines, which can be fatal. Elderly patients are at higher risk.
- **General Warnings:**
  - **Hepatotoxicity:** Borderline elevations of liver tests occur in up to 15% of patients. Severe reactions (e.g., jaundice, fatal hepatitis) are rare but require immediate discontinuation if suspected.
  - **Hypertension:** NSAIDs can lead to new-onset or worsening of pre-existing hypertension; blood pressure should be monitored.
  - **Heart Failure and Edema:** Avoid use in patients with severe heart failure unless benefits outweigh risks. Monitor for fluid retention.
  - **Renal Effects:** Long-term use may lead to renal papillary necrosis. Monitor patients with impaired renal function, heart failure, or liver dysfunction.
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Discontinue at the first appearance of skin rash (e.g., Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis, DRESS).
- **Anaphylactoid Reactions:** May occur in patients without known prior exposure.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily gastrointestinal in nature.

| Frequency | Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>10%)** | Gastrointestinal | Diarrhea (14%), Dyspepsia (13%), Abdominal pain (12%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Gastrointestinal | Constipation, flatulence, nausea, positive stool guaiac, dry mouth, gastritis |
| | Central Nervous System | Dizziness, headache, fatigue, insomnia, nervousness, somnolence |
| | Dermatologic | Pruritus, rash |
| | Special Senses | Tinnitus |
| | Miscellaneous | Edema |
| **Uncommon (<1%)** | Multiple | Anorexia, jaundice, gastric/duodenal ulcer, GI bleeding, hepatic failure, depression, confusion, photosensitivity, vasculitis, weight gain, dyspnea, thrombocytopenia, renal failure |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Myocardial infarction, stroke, GI perforation/hemorrhage, exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), and anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **ACE Inhibitors/ARBs:** NSAIDs may diminish antihypertensive effects.
- **Aspirin:** Increases risk of GI events. Concomitant use reduces 6MNA protein binding; not generally recommended.
- **Diuretics (Loop/Thiazide):** Reduced natriuretic effect; monitor for signs of renal failure.
- **Lithium:** Increased plasma lithium levels (mean 15% increase) due to reduced renal clearance.
- **Methotrexate:** Potential for enhanced methotrexate toxicity due to competitive inhibition of renal accumulation.
- **Warfarin:** Synergistic effect on GI bleeding. 6MNA may displace warfarin from protein binding sites; use extreme caution [US].
- **Antacids:** Aluminum-containing antacids do not significantly affect the bioavailability of the active metabolite (6MNA) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **First/Second Trimester:** Use only if potential benefit justifies fetal risk.
  - **$\ge$ 20 Weeks Gestation:** Avoid use if possible. If necessary, limit to the lowest dose/shortest duration. Monitor amniotic fluid via ultrasound if treatment exceeds 48 hours due to risk of oligohydramnios/fetal renal dysfunction [US].
  - **$\ge$ 30 Weeks Gestation (Third Trimester):** **Contraindicated** due to risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if nabumetone is excreted in human milk (6MNA is excreted in rat milk). Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug based on maternal importance [US].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, nabumetone did not impair male or female fertility at clinical doses [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher steady-state plasma concentrations of 6MNA are observed compared to younger subjects. Use caution and monitor closely for GI and renal toxicity [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
  - **Mild (CrCl $\ge$50 mL/min):** No dosage adjustment generally required.
  - **Moderate (CrCl 30–49 mL/min):** Start with 750 mg once daily; maximum 1,500 mg daily [US].
  - **Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min):** Start with 500 mg once daily; maximum 1,000 mg daily [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Biotransformation may be reduced in severe impairment (e.g., cirrhosis). Use with caution [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Nabumetone is a prodrug. It undergoes rapid hepatic biotransformation to **6-methoxy-2-naphthylacetic acid (6MNA)**, a potent inhibitor of prostaglandin synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Well absorbed (>80%). Food increases the rate of absorption but not the extent.
  - **Distribution:** 6MNA is >99% protein bound.
  - **Metabolism:** Nabumetone is not detectable in plasma; it is converted to 6MNA (35%) and other unidentified metabolites (50%). No evidence of enterohepatic recirculation.
  - **Elimination:** Approximately 80% recovered in urine over 168 hours.
  - **Half-life:** Approximately 24 hours (increases to ~30h in elderly and ~39h in moderate renal impairment) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Osteoarthritis:** Double-blind trials (n=1,047) for 6 weeks to 6 months. Nabumetone 1,000 mg/day was comparable to naproxen 500 mg/day and aspirin 3,600 mg/day in efficacy [US].
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Trials (n=770) for 3 weeks to 6 months demonstrated comparability to naproxen 500 mg/day and aspirin 3,600 mg/day [US].
- **Endoscopy Studies:** In several trials, nabumetone was associated with significantly fewer endoscopically detected GI lesions (>3 mm) compared to naproxen, indomethacin, or ibuprofen [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F).
- **Packaging:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers with child-resistant closures [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Human Prescription Drug).
- **Australia/Europe/UK/Canada:** Not specified in provided source.
