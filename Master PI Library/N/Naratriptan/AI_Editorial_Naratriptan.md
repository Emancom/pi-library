# Editorial Product Information: Naratriptan

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of naratriptan, synthesised for healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Naratriptan (as hydrochloride)
- **Chemical Class:** Selective 5-hydroxytryptamine-1 (5-HT1) receptor agonist (Triptan)
- **Brand Name(s):** Naramig [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 2.5 mg film-coated, green, D-shaped, biconvex tablets [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Migraine:** Indicated for the acute treatment of migraine attacks, with or without aura [Australian].
- **Note:** Naratriptan is not indicated for the management of hemiplegic, basilar, or ophthalmoplegic migraine. It should not be used prophylactically [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing (18–65 years)
- **Initial Dose:** A single 2.5 mg tablet taken as early as possible after the onset of a migraine headache. It remains effective if taken at a later stage.
- **Recurrence:** If symptoms recur, a second 2.5 mg dose may be taken, provided there is a minimum interval of four (4) hours between doses.
- **Maximum Dose:** The total daily dose must not exceed 5 mg (two 2.5 mg tablets) in any 24-hour period.
- **Non-responders:** If a patient does not respond to the first dose, a second dose is unlikely to provide benefit for the same attack.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed whole with water.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to naratriptan or any excipients.
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** History of myocardial infarction, ischaemic heart disease, Prinzmetal's angina/coronary vasospasm, or signs/symptoms consistent with ischaemic heart disease.
- **Cerebrovascular/Peripheral Vascular Disease:** History of cerebrovascular accident (CVA), transient ischaemic attack (TIA), or peripheral vascular disease.
- **Hypertension:** Uncontrolled hypertension.
- **Renal Impairment:** Severely impaired renal function (creatinine clearance < 15 mL/min) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severely impaired hepatic function (Child-Pugh grade C) [Australian].
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Concurrent use of other 5-HT1 receptor agonists (e.g., sumatriptan).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Evaluation:** Naratriptan should not be administered to patients with unrecognised cardiac disease without prior evaluation. This includes postmenopausal women, males over 40, and those with cardiovascular risk factors.
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potential for life-threatening serotonin syndrome, particularly when used with SSRIs or SNRIs. Symptoms include altered mental status, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular abnormalities.
- **Medication Overuse Headache (MOH):** Overuse of acute migraine treatments (>10 days/month) may lead to exacerbation of headache. Withdrawal of treatment may be necessary.
- **Sulphonamide Hypersensitivity:** Naratriptan contains a sulphonamide component; a theoretical risk of hypersensitivity exists in patients with known sulphonamide allergies.
- **Ophthalmic Effects:** Naratriptan binds to melanin and may accumulate in melanin-rich tissues (e.g., the eye) over time. While no retinal toxicity was noted in trials, clinicians should be aware of possible long-term effects.
- **Neurological Conditions:** Ensure a clear diagnosis of migraine; exclude other serious neurological conditions (e.g., CVA, TIA) in patients presenting with atypical symptoms.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typically of short duration and usually mild to moderate.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Paresthesia, dizziness, drowsiness/sleepiness, nausea, vomiting, malaise, fatigue, sensations of tingling/heat, and pain/heaviness in the chest or throat. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Sensations of pressure or tightness. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Ischaemic colitis, somnolence, hypersensitivity reactions (including anaphylaxis). |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Coronary artery vasospasm, transient ischaemic ECG changes, angina, myocardial infarction, and peripheral vascular ischaemia. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Clinicians should monitor for coronary vasospasm, serious cardiac events, and anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **5-HT1 Agonists:** Concomitant use with other triptans is contraindicated due to additive vasospastic risks.
- **Ergotamine and Derivatives:** Concomitant administration of ergotamine, dihydroergotamine, or methysergide is not recommended.
- **SSRIs/SNRIs:** Risk of Serotonin Syndrome; clinical observation is required if co-administration is warranted.
- **St John’s Wort:** May increase the incidence of side effects when used with 5-HT1 agonists.
- **Metabolic Interactions:** Significant interactions via Cytochrome P450 isoenzymes or MAO inhibitors are unlikely, as naratriptan metabolism is low and does not involve a specific isoenzyme.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Australian Category B3:** Naratriptan and/or its metabolites cross the placenta in animal studies. Increased post-implantation loss and skeletal variations were noted at high doses in rabbits/rats. 
  - Use in humans is not recommended unless the potential benefit to the mother outweighs the risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Naratriptan and/or its metabolites are secreted into the milk of lactating rats. It is recommended that breastfeeding be discontinued for 24 hours following naratriptan administration to minimise infant exposure [Australian].
- **Fertility:** High-dose animal studies showed reduced litter sizes and testicular/ovarian atrophy at exposures >500 times the human clinical exposure.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
  - **Adolescents (12–17 years):** Efficacy not demonstrated due to a high placebo response in clinical trials. 
  - **Children (<12 years):** No data available; use is not recommended.
- **Elderly Use (>65 years):** Safety and efficacy not established. A moderate decrease in clearance (approx. 26%) has been observed in subjects aged 65–77.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
  - **Mild/Moderate:** Maximum daily dose is a single 2.5 mg tablet. 
  - **Severe (CrCl < 15 mL/min):** Contraindicated.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
  - **Mild/Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** Maximum daily dose is a single 2.5 mg tablet. 
  - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Contraindicated.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective 5-HT1B and 5-HT1D receptor agonist. It is thought to act by constricting intracranial blood vessels and inhibiting trigeminal nerve activity.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma concentrations reached in 2–3 hours. Oral bioavailability is 74% in women and 63% in men.
  - **Distribution:** Low plasma protein binding (29%). Volume of distribution is 170 L.
  - **Metabolism:** Minimal metabolism by various CYP450 isoenzymes.
  - **Elimination:** Predominantly renal; 50% recovered as unchanged drug and 30% as inactive metabolites. Mean elimination half-life is 6 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Data from seven clinical studies involving 3,907 adult patients demonstrated that 2.5 mg naratriptan provides significant headache relief compared to placebo. 
- **Comparison to Sumatriptan:** In trials using sumatriptan 100 mg as a comparator, naratriptan 2.5 mg showed a lower incidence of migraine recurrence (4 to 24 hours post-dose) but similar 24-hour overall efficacy.
- **Long-term Safety:** An open-label, 12-month study showed that naratriptan remained well-tolerated and effective over repeated use.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 3 years from the date of manufacture.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
