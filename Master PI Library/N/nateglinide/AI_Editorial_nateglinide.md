# Editorial Product Information: Nateglinide

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a coherent reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for full prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nateglinide.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; Glinide (non-sulfonylurea insulin secretagogue).
- **Brand Names:** Nateglinide (generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - Oral tablets: 60 mg and 120 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories Limited [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus:** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Nateglinide should not be used in patients with type 1 diabetes mellitus or for the treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard maintenance dose is 120 mg taken three times daily [US].
- **Initial Dosing:** In patients who are near their glycemic goal (HbA1c) when initiating therapy, a dose of 60 mg three times daily may be used [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Doses should be administered 1 to 30 minutes before meals [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a meal is skipped, the scheduled dose of **nateglinide** must also be skipped to reduce the risk of hypoglycemia [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to **nateglinide** or any of its inactive ingredients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources [US].
- **Hypoglycemia:** All glinides can cause hypoglycemia, which may be severe (leading to seizures, coma, or death). The risk is increased by skipping meals, strenuous exercise, or co-administration with other antidiabetic agents [US].
- **Monitoring:** Regular self-monitoring of blood glucose is essential. Increased frequency of monitoring is recommended in patients with renal or hepatic impairment and those with reduced symptomatic awareness of hypoglycemia (e.g., elderly, patients with autonomic neuropathy) [US].
- **Macrovascular Outcomes:** No clinical studies have established conclusive evidence of macrovascular risk reduction with **nateglinide** [US].
- **Concentration and Reaction Time:** Ability to drive or operate machinery may be impaired by hypoglycemic episodes [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in placebo-controlled trials (12 to 64 weeks) occurring at a rate $\ge$ 2%:

| Adverse Reaction | Nateglinide (%) (N=1441) | Placebo (%) (N=458) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Upper Respiratory Infection | 10.5 | 8.1 |
| Back Pain | 4.0 | 3.7 |
| Flu Symptoms | 3.6 | 2.6 |
| Dizziness | 3.6 | 2.2 |
| Arthropathy | 3.3 | 2.2 |
| Diarrhea | 3.2 | 3.1 |
| Accidental Trauma | 2.9 | 1.7 |
| Bronchitis | 2.7 | 2.6 |
| Coughing | 2.4 | 2.2 |

- **Hypoglycemia:** Non-severe hypoglycemia occurred in 2.4% of patients compared to 0.4% in the placebo group [US].
- **Weight Gain:** Patients experienced a statistically significant mean increase in weight compared to placebo (1.0 kg for 60 mg TID; 1.6 kg for 120 mg TID) [US].
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Increases in mean uric acid levels were observed when used as monotherapy or in combination with metformin [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience (Serious/Rare):**
    - Hypersensitivity reactions: Rash, itching, and urticaria.
    - Hepatobiliary disorders: Jaundice, cholestatic hepatitis, and elevated liver enzymes.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2C9 Inhibitors:** (e.g., amiodarone, fluconazole, voriconazole, sulfinpyrazone) may increase **nateglinide** exposure and hypoglycemia risk [US].
- **CYP Inducers:** (e.g., rifampin, phenytoin, St John’s Wort) may reduce the glucose-lowering effect [US].
- **Protein Binding:** **Nateglinide** is highly protein-bound (98%). While in vitro studies with drugs like warfarin or furosemide showed no influence on binding, clinicians should evaluate individual cases prudently [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Potentiating Hypoglycemic Effect:** NSAIDs, salicylates, MAOIs, non-selective beta-blockers, anabolic hormones, and alcohol. Dose reductions and frequent monitoring may be required [US].
- **Reducing Hypoglycemic Effect:** Thiazides, corticosteroids, thyroid products, and sympathomimetics. Dose increases may be required [US].
- **Blunting Hypoglycemia Signs:** Beta-blockers, clonidine, guanethidine, and reserpine may mask the autonomic symptoms of hypoglycemia [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: No specific category listed, but data are insufficient to identify drug-associated risks]. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus. Animal studies at high doses (27x MRHD) showed no teratogenicity in rats, but increased gallbladder agenesis was noted in rabbits [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Use is not recommended. **Nateglinide** is excreted in rat milk; there are no data on its presence in human milk or effects on the breastfed infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in rat studies at doses up to 600 mg/kg [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients $\ge$ 65 years and younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients with mild to severe renal impairment. In dialysis patients, drug exposure was reduced by 49%, but a metabolite (M1) may accumulate [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild impairment. Use with caution in moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment as these populations have not been studied [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Lowers blood glucose by stimulating insulin secretion from pancreatic beta-cells. It interacts with ATP-sensitive potassium ($K+_{ATP}$) channels, causing depolarization and calcium influx, leading to insulin release. The effect is glucose-dependent and diminishes at low glucose levels [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~73%. $T_{max}$ occurs within 1 hour when taken before meals [US].
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is ~10 L; 98% protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP2C9 (70%) and CYP3A4 (30%) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Average half-life is ~1.5 hours. Excreted mostly in urine (83%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy:** In a 24-week trial, 120 mg TID reduced HbA1c by 0.5% from a baseline of 8.1% compared to a 0.2% increase in the placebo group [US].
- **Active Comparator:** In a study against glyburide (10 mg daily), patients switched to **nateglinide** showed statistically significant increases in HbA1c and FPG, suggesting **nateglinide** was less potent than the sulfonylurea at those doses [US].
- **Combination Therapy:** The addition of **nateglinide** to metformin or rosiglitazone resulted in statistically significant HbA1c reductions compared to monotherapy. However, adding **nateglinide** to glyburide produced no additional clinical benefit [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight container [US].
