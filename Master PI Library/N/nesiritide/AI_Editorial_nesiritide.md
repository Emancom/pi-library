# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection, synthesising available regulatory data for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP). It is a carrier molecule essential for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection (solution); 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and no less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Adjustments:** Dose modifications (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation lacks preservatives [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Perform weekly and monthly monitoring, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Contraindications:** None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, often within minutes of intravenous administration. These are most frequently reported in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a severe reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately. Re-administration should only occur after a careful risk-benefit analysis and under close monitoring [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulations):** While not specific to the injection, the safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trials Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), infection (15%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), arrhythmia (2%), atrial fibrillation (2%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%), depression (5%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **General:** Body odor, transient nausea/vomiting, and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, adequate, well-controlled human studies are absent. Use during pregnancy only if clearly required [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies on levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers have not been conducted. While carnitine levels in cow's milk increase following administration, the risk to a nursing infant from excess intake must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing guidelines for metabolic disorders are applicable to paediatric patients; see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 for specific ESRD dosing. The accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) is a concern with chronic high-dose oral therapy in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].
    - **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, a dose of 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Trough concentrations remained stable or increased over the study duration. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability (Admixture):** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentrations). Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
