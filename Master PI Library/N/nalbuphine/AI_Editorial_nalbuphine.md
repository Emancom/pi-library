# Editorial Product Information: Nalbuphine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the provided official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official PI and should not be used as the sole basis for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nalbuphine hydrochloride (a synthetic opioid agonist-antagonist of the phenanthrene series).
- **Brand Name(s):** Nalbuphine Hydrochloride Injection (distributed by Henry Schein, Inc.) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 10 mg/mL and 20 mg/mL.
    - Available in 10 mL multiple-dose vials (containing methylparaben and propylparaben as preservatives) and 1 mL single-dose ampuls (preservative-free) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV), Intramuscular (IM), Subcutaneous (SC). The product label also identifies subconjunctival use for specific preparations [United States].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured for Somerset Therapeutics, LLC; Repackaged by Henry Schein, Inc. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Management of pain severe enough to require an opioid analgesic and for which alternative treatments are inadequate [United States].
- Supplement to balanced anesthesia [United States].
- Preoperative and postoperative analgesia [United States].
- Obstetrical analgesia during labor and delivery [United States].

**Limitations of Use:** Due to the risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, nalbuphine should be reserved for patients for whom non-opioid options have failed or are not tolerated. It should not be used for extended periods unless the pain remains severe and alternatives remain inadequate [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The usual recommended dose is 10 mg for a 70 kg individual, administered every 3 to 6 hours as necessary. 
- **Maximum Dose:** In non-tolerant individuals, the single maximum dose is 20 mg, with a total maximum daily dose of 160 mg [United States].
- **Balanced Anesthesia Supplement:** Requires higher doses. Induction doses range from 0.3 mg/kg to 3 mg/kg IV (administered over 10–15 minutes). Maintenance doses of 0.25 to 0.5 mg/kg may be given as single IV administrations as required [United States].
- **Administration Instructions:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Titration:** Dosage must be individualised based on pain severity, patient response, and prior analgesic experience. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration.
- **Safe Reduction/Discontinuation:** To avoid withdrawal in physically dependent patients, taper the dose gradually by 25% to 50% every 2 to 4 days [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- Significant respiratory depression [United States].
- Acute or severe bronchial asthma in an unmonitored setting or without resuscitative equipment [United States].
- Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, including paralytic ileus [United States].
- Hypersensitivity to nalbuphine or any of the excipients (e.g., sodium citrate, citric acid, or parabens in multi-dose vials) [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [United States]:**
    - **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Nalbuphine exposes users to the risks of opioid addiction, which can lead to overdose and death.
    - **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur, particularly during initiation or following dose increases.
    - **Risks from Concomitant Use with Benzodiazepines or Other CNS Depressants:** Combined use may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.
- **Opioid-Induced Hyperalgesia (OIH) and Allodynia:** Paradoxical increased pain may occur; this requires dose reduction or opioid rotation [United States].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, usually after more than one month of therapy.
- **Severe Hypotension:** Nalbuphine may cause syncope and orthostatic hypotension, particularly in ambulatory patients or those with compromised blood volume.
- **Increased Intracranial Pressure (ICP):** Use with extreme caution in patients with head injuries or brain tumors, as CO2 retention can further increase ICP.
- **Biliary Tract Disease:** May cause spasm of the sphincter of Oddi and increase serum amylase.
- **Seizure Disorders:** May increase seizure frequency in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequent adverse reaction is **sedation (36%)**. 

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Sweaty/clammy (9%), nausea/vomiting (6%), dizziness/vertigo (5%), dry mouth (4%), headache (3%). |
| **Uncommon (≤1%)** | Nervousness, depression, restlessness, euphoria, hostility, confusion, hallucinations, hypertension, hypotension, bradycardia, tachycardia, cramps, dyspnea, itching, urinary urgency, blurred vision. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, pulmonary edema, loss of consciousness, and fetal death (when used during labor) [United States].
- **Psychotomimetic Effects:** While reported (unreality, depersonalization), these occur less frequently than with pentazocine [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants (Benzodiazepines, Alcohol, etc.):** Additive pharmacodynamic effect increasing the risk of respiratory depression and death. 
- **Serotonergic Drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, Triptans, MAOIs):** Increased risk of potentially life-threatening serotonin syndrome [United States].
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Use is not recommended within 14 days of MAOI therapy due to risk of serotonin syndrome or opioid toxicity [United States].
- **Muscle Relaxants:** Nalbuphine may enhance neuromuscular blocking action and increase respiratory depression.
- **Diuretics:** Opioids may reduce diuretic efficacy by inducing antidiuretic hormone release.
- **Anticholinergic Drugs:** Increased risk of urinary retention and severe constipation/paralytic ileus.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy [United States]:** Data are insufficient to inform a drug-associated risk for major birth defects or miscarriage. 
    - **Labor and Delivery:** High placental transfer. Maternal use during labor is associated with **severe fetal bradycardia**, respiratory depression at birth, apnea, and cyanosis. Naloxone may reverse these effects. A sinusoidal fetal heart rate pattern has also been reported [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Nalbuphine is excreted in human milk in small amounts (<1% of maternal dose). Monitor infants for excess sedation and respiratory depression [United States].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years have not been established [United States].
- **Geriatric Use (≥65 years):** Increased sensitivity to adverse effects. Dose selection should be cautious, generally starting at the low end of the range, due to decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Substantially excreted by the kidneys. Use with caution and in reduced amounts [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Metabolized in the liver. Use with caution and in reduced amounts [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Nalbuphine is a mixed agonist-antagonist; it acts as an agonist at kappa (κ) opioid receptors and an antagonist at mu (μ) opioid receptors [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Analgesic potency is equivalent to morphine on a milligram basis up to 30 mg. Unlike pure mu-agonists, nalbuphine exhibits a "ceiling effect" for respiratory depression at doses above 30 mg [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Onset:** 2–3 minutes (IV); <15 minutes (SC/IM).
    - **Duration of Action:** 3 to 6 hours.
    - **Half-life:** 5 hours.
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Likely hepatic metabolism; renal excretion [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Specific pivotal trial summaries were not detailed in the available source, though safety data from 1066 patients in clinical studies was cited for adverse effect frequencies [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15°–30°C (59°–86°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from excessive light. Store in the original carton until the contents have been used [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [United States].
- **Poison Schedule:** The document indicates management strategies equivalent to a **Schedule II (C-II)** drug for storage and control to prevent abuse and diversion [United States].
