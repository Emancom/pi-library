# Editorial Product Information: Nitrazepam

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority:
    *   Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulations and the current approved PI for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Nitrazepam, a member of the benzodiazepine class.
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Alodorm** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** N05CD02 (Nitrazepam).
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 5 mg tablets (flat, bevel-edged, white, marked “NM/5” and “G”) [Australian].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd (trading as Viatris) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Insomnia:** Management of insomnia of both organic and inorganic origin [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Standard Dose:** 5 mg to 10 mg (1 to 2 tablets) administered immediately before retiring.
*   **In-patients:** The dosage may be increased up to 20 mg if clinically necessary [Australian].

### Special Populations
*   **Elderly Patients:** 2.5 mg to 5 mg (½ to 1 tablet) [Australian].
*   **Paediatric Patients:** Not approved for use as a hypnotic in children [Australian].

### Administration and Duration
*   **Duration:** Prescription should generally be limited to short periods (e.g., 2 to 4 weeks). Continuous long-term use is not recommended due to the development of tolerance and dependence.
*   **Tapering:** Following prolonged use, withdrawal must be gradual. Tapering schedules ranging from four weeks to four months may be required depending on the degree of dependence [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to benzodiazepines.
*   Chronic obstructive airway disease (COPD) with incipient respiratory failure.
*   Severe hepatic insufficiency [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Concomitant Use with Opioids:** Concurrent use may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death. Reserve for cases where alternative treatments are inadequate; use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration.
*   **Dependence and Tolerance:** Tolerance to sedative effects may develop within one week. Use may lead to physical and psychological dependence. Abrupt discontinuation can trigger withdrawal symptoms (insomnia, anxiety, palpitations, tremors, and in severe cases, convulsions or psychosis).
*   **Somnambulism and Complex Behaviours:** "Sleep-driving," preparing food, or other complex behaviours while not fully awake have been reported. The risk increases with alcohol or other CNS depressants. Discontinue if these occur.
*   **Amnesia:** Transient amnesia or memory impairment may occur.
*   **Hypotension:** Caution is advised in patients where a drop in blood pressure could lead to cardiac or cerebral complications, particularly the elderly.
*   **Myasthenia Gravis:** Nitrazepam may exacerbate muscle weakness.
*   **Acute Narrow-Angle Glaucoma:** Use with caution due to potential atropine-like side effects.
*   **Respiratory Function:** Caution in patients with respiratory depression or COPD, as benzodiazepines may increase carbon dioxide tension and decrease arterial oxygen tension.
*   **Psychiatric Conditions:** Not recommended as primary therapy for depression or psychosis. May uncover suicidal tendencies or cause paradoxical reactions (aggression, agitation, hallucinations). Discontinue if paradoxical reactions occur.
*   **Angioedema:** Rare cases of angioedema involving the tongue, glottis, or larynx have been reported; these can be fatal and require emergency intervention.
*   **Lactose Intolerance:** Contains lactose; avoid in patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Nitrazepam is generally well tolerated, with most effects relating to CNS depression.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** | Drowsiness, dizziness, fatigue, ataxia, headache, confusion, vertigo, morning "hangover," slurred speech, memory impairment, reduced alertness, muscle weakness, double vision, and rebound insomnia. |
| **Rare/Uncommon** | Hypotension, faintness, palpitation, rash, pruritus, gastrointestinal disturbances, and changes in libido. |
| **Very Rare** | **Serious:** Paradoxical reactions (excitement, hallucinations, hyperactivity), retrograde amnesia, delirium tremens, and hypothermia. |

*   **Pediatric/Elderly Note:** Bronchial hypersecretion and excessive salivation leading to aspiration pneumonia have occurred, particularly in infants, young children, and bedridden elderly patients (observed at doses of 0.7 mg/kg/day) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects occur with alcohol, barbiturates, antipsychotics, sedatives, tricyclic antidepressants, MAO inhibitors, skeletal muscle relaxants, and anaesthetics.
*   **Opioids:** Increased risk of fatal respiratory depression and sedation.
*   **Disulfiram and Cimetidine:** Inhibit oxidative metabolism, potentially increasing nitrazepam plasma levels and enhancing response.
*   **Anticholinergics:** Potentiation of atropine-like effects.
*   **Anticonvulsants:** Interactions may alter serum concentrations of either the benzodiazepine or the anticonvulsant; frequent monitoring is recommended.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy (Category C):** Benzodiazepines cross the placenta. Use may cause hypotonia, respiratory depression, and hypothermia in the neonate. Prolonged use during pregnancy may lead to neonatal withdrawal symptoms. Avoid during pregnancy and delivery [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Nitrazepam is excreted in breast milk. Use with caution as it may cause drowsiness and feeding difficulties in the infant [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** No human data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Not approved for use as a hypnotic. Risk of bronchial hypersecretion is significant in this group.
*   **Elderly Use:** Increased susceptibility to sedative effects, confusion, and ataxia, which raises the risk of falls. Lower doses (2.5–5 mg) are required.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Caution is required; dosage reduction may be necessary as metabolites are primarily excreted renally.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe insufficiency. In mild to moderate cases, use with caution; dosage reduction and periodic liver function tests are recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** A benzodiazepine agonist that facilitates the action of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), the primary inhibitory neurotransmitter in the brain. It possesses sedative, anxiolytic, anticonvulsant, and muscle relaxant properties.
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** Induces sleep lasting 6 to 8 hours when taken at recommended doses.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed from the GI tract; peak plasma levels reached in ~2 hours (range 0.5–5 hours).
    *   **Distribution:** Lipophilic, crosses the blood-brain barrier and placenta. Approximately 87% protein-bound.
    *   **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by the liver, primarily to 7-aminonitrazepam and 7-acetamido nitrazepam (weakly active).
    *   **Excretion:** Primarily renal (70% within 120 hours). Elimination half-life averages 27 hours (range 16–48 hours). Half-life is significantly longer in the elderly and in cerebrospinal fluid [Australian].
