# Editorial Product Information: Neomycin and Polymyxin B Sulfates (Irrigation)

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory data into a single clinical reference. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredients:** Neomycin Sulfate (Aminoglycoside class) and Polymyxin B Sulfate (Polypeptide class).
*   **Brand Names:** Neomycin and Polymyxin B Sulfates Solution for Irrigation, USP [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **Sterile Concentrated Solution:** Each 1 mL contains neomycin sulfate equivalent to 40 mg neomycin base and 200,000 units polymyxin B sulfate [US].
    *   **Available sizes:** 1 mL ampules and 20 mL multiple-dose vials (contains 0.1% methylparaben as a preservative) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Urinary bladder irrigation only. **NOT FOR INJECTION.**
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** XGen Pharmaceuticals DJB, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Prevention of Bacteriuria and Septicemia:** Indicated for short-term use (up to 10 days) as a continuous irrigant or rinse in the urinary bladder of abacteriuric patients to help prevent bacteriuria and gram-negative rod septicemia associated with the use of indwelling catheters [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Standard Dilution:** 1 mL of the concentrated solution must be added to 1,000 mL of isotonic saline solution [US].
*   **Administration:** Administered via a three-way catheter or other system permitting continuous bladder irrigation using strict aseptic technique [US].
*   **Duration:** Limited to 10 days [US].

### Dosing by Indication
*   **Continuous Irrigation:** For most patients, the inflow rate of the 1,000 mL saline/antibiotic mixture should be adjusted to a slow drip to deliver approximately 1,000 mL every 24 hours [US].
*   **Adjustment for High Urine Output:** If urine output exceeds 2 liters per day, the inflow rate should be adjusted to deliver 2,000 mL of the solution in a 24-hour period [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   The rinse must be continuous; inflow should not be interrupted for more than a few minutes [US].
*   Preparation must use strict aseptic techniques.
*   Use of a sterile lubricant is recommended during catheter insertion [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to neomycin, the polymyxins, or any ingredient in the formulation [US].
*   **Cross-Sensitivity:** A history of hypersensitivity or serious toxic reaction to any aminoglycoside may contraindicate use due to known cross-sensitivity [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
*   Not specified in available sources; however, "NOT FOR INJECTION" is prominently highlighted [US].

### General Warnings
*   **Restricted Site of Use:** Use is strictly limited to the urinary bladder. It should not be used for irrigation at any other site [US].
*   **Systemic Absorption Risk:** Prophylactic bladder care should not be administered where there is a possibility of systemic absorption (e.g., vesical rupture or operative procedures on the bladder wall) [US].
*   **Cumulative Toxicity:** If absorbed, neomycin and polymyxin B are both nephrotoxic and neurotoxic. Their toxic potentials are additive [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
*   **Ototoxicity:** Absorbed neomycin can destroy fibers of the acoustic nerve, potentially causing permanent bilateral deafness [US].
*   **Neuromuscular Blockade:** Both antibiotics have additive neuromuscular blocking effects [US].
*   **Superinfection:** Use may result in the overgrowth of nonsusceptible organisms, including fungi [US].
*   **Monitoring:** Urine specimens should be collected for urinalysis, culture, and susceptibility testing during treatment to monitor for resistant organisms [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Local Irritation:** Irritation of the urinary bladder mucosa has been reported [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Neomycin may occasionally cause skin sensitization; however, topical application to mucous membranes rarely results in local or systemic hypersensitivity [US].
*   **Systemic Toxicity (Rare if used as directed):** Signs of ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity have been reported following systemic absorption [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    *   **Other Nephrotoxic/Neurotoxic Drugs:** Potential for additive toxicity if used concurrently with other systemic aminoglycosides or polymyxins [US].
    *   **Neuromuscular Blocking Agents:** Potential for additive neuromuscular blockade if systemic absorption occurs [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Category D [US]:** Aminoglycosides can cause fetal harm when administered to pregnant women. They cross the placenta and have been linked to irreversible bilateral congenital deafness in children. Patients should be apprised of potential hazards to the fetus [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients have not been established [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Elderly patients are considered at higher risk for toxicity if systemic absorption occurs [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Patients with impaired renal function are at increased risk of toxicity following systemic absorption [US].
*   **Dehydrated Patients:** Increased risk for development of toxicity [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
*   **Neomycin:** An aminoglycoside that inhibits protein synthesis in susceptible microorganisms [US].
*   **Polymyxin B:** A polypeptide that increases the permeability of bacterial cell wall membranes [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** In the intact urinary bladder, absorption is clinically insignificant. A serum level of 0.1 mcg/mL (well below toxic levels) was observed in a minority of patients in clinical studies [US].
*   **Absorption from Denuded Tissue:** Significant systemic absorption can occur when applied to open wounds, burns, or denuded bladder surfaces [US].

### Microbiology
*   **Bactericidal Activity:** Active *in vitro* against *Escherichia coli, Staphylococcus aureus, Haemophilus influenzae, Klebsiella, Enterobacter, Neisseria* species, and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* [US].
*   **Resistance:** Not active against *Serratia marcescens* and *Streptococci* [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Summary:** Source data mentions a study of 33 patients receiving the rinse solution where neomycin serum levels were monitored. Levels remained clinically insignificant (0.1 mcg/mL) in the three patients where the drug was detectable [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Concentrate:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
*   **Prepared Solution:** Following dilution, the prepared irrigation solution should be stored at 4°C and used within 48 hours to reduce contamination risk [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **US:** Rx-only [US].
*   **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified for other jurisdictions in available sources.
