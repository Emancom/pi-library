# Editorial Product Information: Netarsudil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Netarsudil dimesylate.
- **Chemical Class:** Rho kinase (ROCK) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Rhopressa** [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01EX05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Ophthalmic solution 0.02% (0.2 mg/mL). Each mL contains 0.285 mg netarsudil mesylate [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alcon Laboratories, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Elevated Intraocular Pressure (IOP):** Indicated for the reduction of elevated IOP in patients diagnosed with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** One drop instilled into the affected eye(s) once daily, specifically in the evening [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - To minimize contamination, avoid contact between the dropper tip and the eye, surrounding structures, or any other surfaces [US].
    - If using multiple topical ophthalmic drugs, allow at least a 5-minute interval between the administration of each product [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, patients should continue with the next scheduled dose in the evening. Doubling the dose or twice-daily administration is not recommended as it is poorly tolerated [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None identified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Epithelial Corneal Edema:** Reports of "honeycomb" or bullous corneal edema have occurred, particularly in patients with pre-existing corneal stromal edema or following ocular procedures that compromise endothelial function. This condition typically resolves upon cessation of treatment [US].
- **Bacterial Keratitis:** Risk of bacterial keratitis is associated with the use of multi-dose containers, often due to inadvertent contamination by patients with concurrent corneal disease or epithelial surface disruption [US].
- **Contact Lens Use:** Netarsudil 0.02% contains benzalkonium chloride, which may be absorbed by soft contact lenses. Lenses must be removed prior to instillation and may be reinserted 15 minutes post-administration [US].
- **Monitoring:** Patients should be advised to seek immediate medical advice if they develop intercurrent ocular conditions (trauma, infection) or undergo ocular surgery [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily local and ocular.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Conjunctival hyperemia (53%), corneal verticillata (~20%), instillation site pain (~20%), conjunctival hemorrhage (~20%) [US]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Instillation site erythema, corneal staining, blurred vision, increased lacrimation, eyelid erythema, and reduced visual acuity (reported in 5–10% of patients) [US]. |
| **Postmarketing** | Epithelial corneal edema (honeycomb/bullous) [US]. |

- **Clinical Note on Corneal Verticillata:** Typically appears within 4 weeks of daily dosing. It does not generally result in functional visual changes and most cases resolve upon treatment discontinuation [US].
- **Discontinuation Rate:** Approximately 6% of patients in clinical trials discontinued therapy due to conjunctival hyperemia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources. Systemic exposure is negligible following topical application [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Use with other IOP-lowering agents requires a 5-minute administration interval [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** No lettered category assigned in current labeling. 
    - **Clinical Summary:** There are no adequate data on use in pregnant women. Systemic exposure is low. Animal studies (IV administration) showed embryofetal lethality and malformations (e.g., umbilical hernia, skeletal issues) at doses significantly exceeding (at least 126-fold) the recommended human ophthalmic dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data regarding the presence of netarsudil in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. The low systemic exposure following topical administration suggests minimal risk, but the benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies evaluating the effect of netarsudil on fertility have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy have been observed between geriatric and younger adult populations [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Netarsudil is a Rho kinase inhibitor. It reduces IOP by increasing the outflow of aqueous humor primarily through the trabecular meshwork. The exact molecular mechanism remains fully uncharacterized [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Intended for local action in the eye to reduce IOP [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Systemic levels of netarsudil and its active metabolite (AR-13503) are generally below the limit of quantitation (0.1 ng/mL) after once-daily topical dosing [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolized by local esterases in the eye [US].
    - **Elimination:** Not specified in available sources due to low systemic levels.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Three randomized, controlled trials (Studies 301, 302, and 304) evaluated netarsudil 0.02% once daily in patients with open-angle glaucoma or ocular hypertension [US].
- **Efficacy:**
    - Demonstrated IOP reductions of up to 5 mmHg [US].
    - In patients with baseline IOP < 25 mmHg, the reduction was comparable to timolol 0.5% twice daily [US].
    - In patients with baseline IOP ≥ 25 mmHg, netarsudil was less effective than timolol 0.5% (difference up to 3 mmHg in favor of timolol) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Initial Storage:** Store unopened bottles refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Post-Opening:** After opening, the bottle may be kept at 2°C to 25°C (36°F to 77°F) for up to 6 weeks. If refrigerated post-opening, it is stable until the expiration date [US].
- **Shipping:** During shipment, the product may be maintained at temperatures up to 40°C (104°F) for a maximum of 14 days [US].
