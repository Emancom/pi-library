# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States PI was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (marketed by Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL. Store at room temperature (25°C) for up to 24 hours in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate appropriate medical care [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Plasma Concentrations:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to therapy. For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is advised (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Dialysis Monitoring:** In ESRD, monitor trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations (normal range: 40 to 50 µmol/L) [US].
- **Warfarin Therapy:** Reports of increased INR have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitor INR levels frequently after initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequently confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Very Common (>10%) and Common (1–10%)
Based on clinical trials in dialysis patients, the following were reported at a frequency $\ge$ 5% [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), infection (15%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), fever (7%), flu syndrome (25%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), nausea (8%), vomiting (15%).
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity [US].
- Seizures [US].

### Other Reported Effects
- Transient nausea and vomiting.
- Gastritis and body odor (less frequent) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Potentiation of the hypoprothrombinemic effect of warfarin has been reported. Monitor INR levels [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources. Levocarnitine is not protein-bound [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a specific letter category in provided text, but states "use only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of carnitine following exogenous administration. The risks to the infant of excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 (Dosage and Administration). Dosing is weight-based.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Evaluated extensively in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency; chronic high doses of oral formulations in these patients may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (faeces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels increased from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8 [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** In patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg, trough concentrations remained stable or increased over 24 weeks. While carnitine concentrations increased, the specific effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
