# Originality Audit Report: nystatin and triamcinolone

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 68/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** Yes
- **Overall Assessment:** Concern

## Verbatim Copied Passages
The following passages are identical or near-identical to the source PI documents:

1.  **Approved Indications (TGA):**
    - **Editorial:** "Relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of dermatoses that are likely to become infected or are already infected."
    - **Source (Doc 1 - TGA, p. 1):** "indicated for the relief of the inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of dermatoses likely to become or which are already infected."
    - **Location:** Section 2.

2.  **Dosage and Administration (FDA):**
    - **Editorial:** "Apply twice daily (morning and evening) by gently and thoroughly massaging the preparation into the skin."
    - **Source (Doc 2 - FDA, p. 5):** "usually applied to the affected areas twice daily in the morning and evening by gently and thoroughly massaging the preparation into the skin."
    - **Location:** Section 3.

3.  **Adverse Effects (List):**
    - **Editorial:** "burning, itching, irritation, dryness, folliculitis, hypertrichosis, acneform eruptions, hypopigmentation, perioral dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis, skin maceration, secondary infection, skin atrophy, striae, and miliaria."
    - **Source (Doc 1, p. 3; Doc 2, p. 5):** This is a verbatim list in the exact order found in both PIs under "Adverse Reactions."
    - **Location:** Section 6.

4.  **Paediatric Adverse Effects:**
    - **Editorial:** "Linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, low plasma cortisol, and intracranial hypertension (bulging fontanelles, headaches, bilateral papilloedema)."
    - **Source (Doc 1, p. 4; Doc 2, p. 5):** "Manifestations of adrenal suppression in children include linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, low plasma cortisol levels... Manifestations of intracranial hypertension include bulging fontanelles, headaches, and bilateral papilledema."
    - **Location:** Section 9.

5.  **Pharmacology (Neomycin Excretion):**
    - **Editorial:** "Absorbed neomycin is excreted unchanged by the kidneys with a half-life of 2–3 hours."
    - **Source (Doc 1, p. 5):** "Once absorbed neomycin is rapidly excreted unchanged through the kidneys. The half-life is approximately 2 to 3 hours."
    - **Location:** Section 10.

## Close Paraphrases
1.  **Visual Disturbance Warning:** Section 5 (Warnings) follows the structure and specific disease list (cataract, glaucoma, CSCR) of the TGA PI (p. 2) very closely, though it condenses the text.
2.  **HPA Axis Suppression:** Section 5 (Systemic Absorption) uses the standard regulatory phrasing for HPA axis suppression found in both documents. While standard, the specific string of "Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycaemia, and glucosuria" is a direct carry-over.
3.  **Nystatin Mechanism:** Section 10 (Mechanism of Action) for Nystatin: "Binds to sterols in the fungal cell membrane, altering permeability and causing leakage of intracellular components" is a minor variation of the TGA/FDA phrasing ("binding to steroids/sterols... leakage of intracellular components").

## Originality Strengths
- **Harmonisation Analysis:** The editorial successfully identifies and highlights the major difference between the two products (the presence of Neomycin and Gramicidin in the Australian formulation versus only Nystatin/Triamcinolone in the US version).
- **Structure:** The editorial does not follow the layout of either source PI, instead creating a custom synthesis that categorizes information by jurisdiction where necessary.
- **Editorial Note:** The introductory section is original writing that provides necessary context for the comparison.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Ingredient Comparison:** The editorial explicitly flags "TGA only" or "FDA" for specific components, whereas the PIs only list what is in their specific product.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor Synthesis:** Merges sponsorship information from different markets into a single identification section.
- **Storage Synthesis:** Converts different temperature formatting into a single comparative list.
- **Clinical Trial Summary:** The editorial synthesises the FDA "Nystatin and Triamcinolone Acetonide" clinical section (p. 3) into a concise summary of results rather than copying the study parameters.

## Recommendation
**REVISION NEEDED.** 

While a high degree of technical accuracy is required, the editorial contains several long strings of verbatim text (specifically in Indications, FDA Dosing, and Paediatric effects) that should be rewritten in the editor's voice. 

**Required changes:**
1.  **Indications:** Rephrase the TGA indication (e.g., "Used to treat skin conditions involving inflammation and itching where a bacterial infection is present or suspected").
2.  **Dosing:** Rephrase the FDA dosing instruction to avoid the "gently and thoroughly massaging" string.
3.  **Adverse Effects:** Break up the long verbatim list of local reactions or categorize them (e.g., "Skin-related reactions include irritation, dryness, and thinning of the skin...") to avoid the exact "burning, itching, irritation..." sequence.
4.  **Paediatrics:** Rephrase the symptoms of intracranial hypertension and growth issues to avoid the verbatim medical list.
