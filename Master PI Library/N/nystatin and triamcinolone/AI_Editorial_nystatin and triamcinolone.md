# Editorial Product Information: Nystatin and Triamcinolone (with Neomycin and Gramicidin)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) documents from the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA, Australia) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA, United States).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of nystatin and triamcinolone acetonide combinations for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Significant differences exist in the number of active ingredients between the Australian and United States formulations.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** 
    - **Nystatin** (polyene antifungal) and **Triamcinolone acetonide** (synthetic corticosteroid) [FDA; TGA].
    - **Neomycin sulfate** (aminoglycoside antibiotic) and **Gramicidin** (antibiotic) [TGA only].
- **Brand Names:** Kenacomb [TGA]; Nystatin and Triamcinolone Acetonide Cream, USP [FDA].
- **ATC Code:** D07CA01 (Corticosteroids, moderately potent, combinations with antibiotics).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Ointment [TGA]:** 100,000 IU/g nystatin, 1 mg/g (0.1%) triamcinolone acetonide, 2.5 mg/g neomycin (as sulfate), and 0.25 mg/g gramicidin.
    - **Cream [FDA]:** 100,000 USP units/g nystatin and 1 mg/g triamcinolone acetonide. 
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical (External Use Only).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [TGA]; Macleods Pharmaceuticals/NuCare Pharmaceuticals [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cutaneous Candidiasis:** Indicated for the treatment of mild to severe manifestations of cutaneous candidiasis [FDA].
- **Infected Dermatoses:** Relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of dermatoses that are likely to become infected or are already infected [TGA].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:**
    - **[TGA]:** Apply to the affected area 2 to 3 times daily.
    - **[FDA]:** Apply twice daily (morning and evening) by gently and thoroughly massaging the preparation into the skin.
- **Administration Instructions:** For dermatological/external use only. Avoid contact with the eyes.
- **Duration of Therapy [FDA]:** Treatment should be discontinued if symptoms persist after 25 days of therapy.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to triamcinolone, nystatin, neomycin, or gramicidin.
- Ophthalmic use.
- **Viral and Tuberculous Lesions [TGA]:** Tuberculous lesions and most viral skin lesions (e.g., Herpes simplex, vaccinia, varicella).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Warnings:** 
    - Discontinue use if sensitivity or irritation develops. 
    - Corticosteroids may mask hypersensitivity reactions to the anti-infective components [TGA].
- **Systemic Absorption:** Topical corticosteroids can be absorbed systemically, potentially leading to reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycaemia, and glucosuria. 
- **Occlusive Dressings:** Avoid occlusive dressings as they significantly increase percutaneous absorption and the risk of sensitivity reactions [FDA; TGA]. 
- **Ototoxicity and Nephrotoxicity [TGA]:** Due to neomycin content, avoid use on extensive skin damage or conditions where absorption is possible, as it may cause ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity.
- **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi other than *Candida*.
- **Visual Disturbance [TGA]:** Systemic or topical corticosteroid use may lead to visual disturbances, including cataract, glaucoma, or central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR). Blurred vision should prompt ophthalmological referral.
- **Monitoring:** Periodic evaluation for HPA axis suppression (e.g., urinary free cortisol, ACTH stimulation test) is recommended if large doses or large surface areas are treated.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Frequency (Uncommon/Infrequent):**
    - **Local Effects:** Burning, itching, irritation, dryness, folliculitis, hypertrichosis, acneform eruptions, hypopigmentation, perioral dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis, skin maceration, secondary infection, skin atrophy, striae, and miliaria.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - **Neomycin Sensitisation:** Delayed-type hypersensitivity reactions and sensitisation [TGA].
    - **Systemic Toxicity:** Ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity with extensive use [TGA].
    - **Eye Disorders:** Blurred vision (post-marketing) [TGA].
    - **Acneform Eruption:** Reported in approximately 1% of patients in clinical studies [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No specific data available in sources.
- **General Note:** The corticosteroid component may mask the signs of allergic reactions to other components.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **[TGA]: Category D.** Aminoglycosides (like neomycin) cross the placenta and carry a risk of selective uptake by fetal kidneys and eighth cranial nerve damage. 
    - **[FDA]:** No specific letter category listed. Notes that corticosteroids are generally teratogenic in animals at low doses. Use only if potential benefits justify the potential risk to the fetus. Do not use extensively or for prolonged periods in pregnant patients.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if topical administration results in detectable quantities in breast milk. Caution is advised. While systemic corticosteroids are secreted into breast milk, they are unlikely to affect the infant at normal doses.
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies to evaluate mutagenic or carcinogenic potential or impairment of fertility have not been performed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Children are more susceptible to HPA axis suppression and Cushing’s syndrome due to a higher skin surface area to body weight ratio. 
    - **Adverse Effects in Children:** Linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, low plasma cortisol, and intracranial hypertension (bulging fontanelles, headaches, bilateral papilloedema) [TGA; FDA].
    - **Recommendation:** Limit treatment to the minimum amount and shortest duration necessary. 
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [TGA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Triamcinolone acetonide:** Anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive actions.
    - **Nystatin:** Binds to sterols in the fungal cell membrane, altering permeability and causing leakage of intracellular components. Effective against *Candida* species.
    - **Neomycin [TGA]:** Inhibits protein synthesis in gram-negative organisms (not active against *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*).
    - **Gramicidin [TGA]:** Alters cell membrane permeability of gram-positive organisms.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Triamcinolone can be absorbed through intact skin; absorption is increased by inflammation or occlusive dressings. Nystatin and gramicidin are not absorbed through intact skin/mucosa. Neomycin is absorbed through inflamed skin.
    - **Metabolism/Excretion:** Absorbed corticosteroids are metabolised in the liver and excreted by the kidneys. Absorbed neomycin is excreted unchanged by the kidneys with a half-life of 2–3 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Nystatin/Triamcinolone Combination [FDA]:** Clinical studies in patients with cutaneous candidiasis showed that the combination provides a faster and more pronounced clearing of erythema and pruritus than nystatin alone during the first few days of treatment.
- **General:** No additional trial data specified in source documents.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **[FDA]:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F). Avoid freezing.
- **[TGA]:** Store below 25°C.
- **Container:** Available in 15 g and 30 g aluminium tubes [TGA]; 30 g tubes [FDA].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia [TGA]:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States [FDA]:** Rx only.
