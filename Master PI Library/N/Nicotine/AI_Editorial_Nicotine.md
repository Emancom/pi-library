# Editorial Product Information: Nicotine (Chewing Gum)

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nicotine (3-[(2S)-1-methylpyrrolidin-2-yl]pyridine)
- **Chemical Class:** Tobacco alkaloid; Nicotinic acetylcholine receptor agonist.
- **Brand Names:** Nicabate Gum [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** N07BA01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Coated chewing gum: 2 mg [Australian]
    - Coated chewing gum: 4 mg [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Buccal (oral mucosa)
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Haleon [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- Relief of nicotine withdrawal symptoms, including cravings, associated with smoking cessation [Australian].
- Smoking reduction strategy for individuals unable or unready to stop smoking abruptly, serving as a step toward complete cessation [Australian].
- *Note:* Use in conjunction with a behavioural support programme is recommended to improve success rates [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults $\ge$ 18 years)
Dosing is individualised based on the user’s nicotine dependence and withdrawal symptoms [Australian]:
- **Smokers of <20 cigarettes/day:** 2 mg gum.
- **Smokers of >20 cigarettes/day:** 4 mg gum.

### Administration Instructions
- **Chew and Rest Technique:** Patients must chew one piece slowly until the taste becomes strong (approx. 1 minute), then "park" the gum between the cheek and gum. When the taste fades, chewing should be resumed until the taste returns, then parked again. This cycle is repeated for approximately 30 minutes [Australian].
- **Dietary Restrictions:** Do not eat or drink while chewing. Acidic beverages (e.g., coffee, soft drinks, fruit juice) lower mouth pH and significantly reduce buccal absorption. Avoid these drinks for 15 minutes prior to use [Australian].

### Treatment Schedule (Abrupt Cessation)
The typical course lasts at least 3 months [Australian]:
- **Step 1 (Weeks 1–12):** 1 piece whenever there is an urge to smoke. Typical daily use: 8–12 pieces (2 mg) or 8–10 pieces (4 mg).
- **Step 2 (Next 2 weeks):** Gradually reduce to 4–6 pieces/day.
- **Step 3 (Final 2 weeks):** Reduce to 1–3 pieces/day, then zero.
- **Maximum Dose:** Do not exceed 1 piece per hour. Max 20 pieces/day (2 mg) or 10 pieces/day (4 mg) [Australian].

### Gradual Cessation (Reduce to Quit)
- Use gum between smoking episodes to prolong smoke-free intervals and reduce cigarette consumption.
- If a reduction in cigarettes is not achieved after 6 weeks, consult a healthcare professional.
- A quit attempt should ideally begin within 6 months of starting treatment [Australian].

### Combination Therapy
- Nicotine 2 mg gum may be combined with Nicabate 21 mg patches for smokers who have relapsed on monotherapy or have breakthrough cravings [Australian].
- **Dosing:** Minimum 4 pieces/day; maximum 12 pieces/day when used with patches. 
- *Warning:* 4 mg gum should NOT be used in combination with patches [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Non-smokers [Australian].
- Children under 12 years of age [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to nicotine or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Use with caution in patients with recent myocardial infarction, severe dysrhythmia, or CVA. Haemodynamically unstable patients should be encouraged to quit via non-pharmacological means first. If NRT is used, it should be under medical supervision [Australian].
- **Seizures:** Use with caution in patients with epilepsy or those on anti-convulsant therapy; convulsions have been reported in association with nicotine [Australian].
- **Diabetes Mellitus:** Monitor blood glucose closely upon initiation. Nicotine-induced catecholamine release can affect carbohydrate metabolism and peripheral vasoconstriction may delay insulin absorption [Australian].
- **Phaeochromocytoma and Hyperthyroidism:** Use caution as nicotine triggers catecholamine release [Australian].
- **GI Disease:** Swallowed nicotine may exacerbate oesophagitis, gastritis, or peptic ulcers [Australian].
- **Dental:** Gum may be difficult to use with dentures or TMJ disease; it may loosen fillings or dental implants [Australian].
- **Toxicity in Children:** Doses tolerated by adults can be fatal to small children. Ensure products are stored out of reach [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Smoking Cessation Effects:** Stopping smoking (with or without NRT) may increase the plasma concentration of drugs metabolised by CYP1A2 (e.g., **theophylline, clozapine, ropinirole, tacrine, imipramine, olanzapine**). Dose adjustments may be required [Australian].
- **Adenosine:** Nicotine may enhance the haemodynamic effects of adenosine, potentially increasing blood pressure, heart rate, and angina-type pain response [Australian].
- **Insulin:** Smoking cessation may increase subcutaneous insulin absorption, necessitating dose reduction [Australian].
- **Other:** Adjustments may be needed for nifedipine, adrenergic agonists, or blockers due to changes in circulating catecholamines [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category D [Australian]**.
- **Pregnancy:** Nicotine is harmful to the fetus, affecting breathing and circulation. Ideally, cessation should be achieved without NRT. If unsuccessful, NRT is considered less risky than continued smoking (due to lack of tar and carbon monoxide), but should be used for only 2–3 months and initiated early in pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Nicotine passes into breast milk. Exposure via NRT is small but present. To minimise infant exposure, use intermittent NRT (gum) immediately after breastfeeding rather than before [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children <12 years. In adolescents (12–17 years), use only if the benefit of abstinence outweighs the risk of continued smoking, and limit duration to 10–14 weeks unless reassessed by a professional [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No specific data available; however, the adult dosing schedule includes the elderly [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution in moderate to severe impairment; nicotine clearance may be decreased [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution in severe renal impairment or haemodialysis; clearance of nicotine and its metabolites may be reduced [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Nicotine acts as an agonist at nicotinic receptors in the peripheral and central nervous systems. Chewing gum provides a controlled source of nicotine to alleviate withdrawal symptoms associated with the abrupt cessation of tobacco use.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed through the buccal mucosa. Blood levels are demonstrable within 5–7 minutes, peaking at 30 minutes [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Low plasma protein binding (4.9%–20%). Crosses the blood-brain barrier and placenta [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic; also occurs in lungs and kidneys. Major metabolite is **cotinine** (half-life 15–20 hours) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal. Elimination half-life is approximately 2 hours [Australian].
