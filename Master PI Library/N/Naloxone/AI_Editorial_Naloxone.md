# Editorial Product Information: Oxycodone Hydrochloride / Naloxone Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** To provide a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals. This document serves as an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.
- **Note on Sources:** Information provided in this document is derived exclusively from the Australian PI; where international variations are typically noted, they are absent here due to the single source provided.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** **Oxycodone hydrochloride** (opioid agonist) and **naloxone hydrochloride** (opioid antagonist).
- **Chemical Class:** Opioid analgesic and opioid antagonist.
- **Brand Names:** **ARX-OXYCODONE/NALOXONE** [Australian].
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Modified-release (MR) tablets containing oxycodone HCl/naloxone HCl dihydrate in the following ratios: 2.5 mg/1.25 mg, 5 mg/2.5 mg, 10 mg/5 mg, 15 mg/7.5 mg, 20 mg/10 mg, 30 mg/15 mg, 40 mg/20 mg, 60 mg/30 mg, and 80/40 mg.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mundipharma Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Pain:** Management of severe pain where opioid responsiveness is expected and continuous, long-term daily treatment is required, and other treatment options (non-opioids) have failed or are contraindicated.
- **Opioid-Induced Constipation (OIC):** Prophylaxis and/or therapy of constipation associated with opioid treatment (provided by the naloxone component).
- **Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS):** Second-line symptomatic treatment for patients with severe to very severe idiopathic RLS following the failure of dopaminergic therapy [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Schedule:** Administered 12-hourly. Tablets must be swallowed whole and never broken, chewed, or crushed (risk of fatal oxycodone dose release).
- **Maximum Dose:** 160 mg oxycodone / 80 mg naloxone daily [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Analgesia (Opioid-Naïve):** Usually starts at 10 mg/5 mg every 12 hours. Lower strengths (2.5 mg/1.25 mg or 5 mg/2.5 mg) may be used for initial titration.
- **Analgesia (Opioid-Tolerant):** Patients may start on higher doses based on previous opioid exposure. High-strength tablets (60 mg/30 mg and 80 mg/40 mg) are for opioid-tolerant patients only.
- **Restless Legs Syndrome:** Usual starting dose for opioid-naïve patients is 5 mg/2.5 mg every 12 hours. Titrate weekly if required.

### Conversion and Titration
- **Opioid Rotation:** Use conversion tables to determine the "oral oxycodone equivalent." Conservative initial dosing is recommended.
- **Tapering:** When discontinuing, the dose should be reduced gradually (e.g., 10% to 25% every 2 to 4 weeks) to prevent withdrawal symptoms.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to oxycodone, naloxone, or any excipients.
- Moderate to severe hepatic impairment.
- Severe respiratory depression, acute respiratory disease, or significantly elevated blood CO2.
- Severe Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) or cor pulmonale.
- Uncontrolled bronchial asthma.
- Non-opioid induced paralytic ileus.
- Suspected surgical abdomen or delayed gastric emptying.
- Alcoholism or delirium tremens.
- Concurrent administration of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or within 2 weeks of cessation.
- Pregnancy and lactation [Australian].
- History of opioid abuse in RLS patients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [Australian]
- **Hazardous and Harmful Use:** Risk of addiction, abuse, and misuse leading to overdose and death.
- **Life-threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur; monitoring is critical during initiation and dose escalation.
- **CNS Depressants:** Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or alcohol may cause profound sedation, coma, and death.
- **Limitations of Use:** Reserved for cases where alternative treatments are inadequate.

### General Precautions
- **Opioid Use Disorder (OUD):** Repeated use may lead to physical/psychological dependence and OUD.
- **Sleep-Related Breathing Disorders:** Can cause central sleep apnoea (CSA) and hypoxemia.
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with long-term use; monitoring for nausea, fatigue, and low blood pressure is advised.
- **Hyperalgesia:** An unexplained increase in pain may require dose reduction or opioid rotation.
- **Biliary Tract:** Oxycodone may cause spasm of the sphincter of Oddi; use with caution in pancreatitis or biliary disease.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typical of full opioid agonists, though naloxone reduces the incidence of opioid-induced constipation.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Fatigue, headache, somnolence, nausea, constipation, hyperhidrosis. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Dizziness, insomnia, abdominal pain, dry mouth, vomiting, dyspepsia, pruritus, rash, hot flush, decreased appetite. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Anxiety, depression, restlessness, palpitations (withdrawal context), visual impairment, dyspnoea, flatulence, hepatic enzyme increase, muscle spasms, urinary urgency. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Increased appetite, urticaria. |
| **Not Known** | Adrenal insufficiency, central sleep apnoea, dental caries, biliary colic, erectile dysfunction, amenorrhoea. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** (e.g., Benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, cannabis, alcohol) Increase risk of fatal respiratory depression and sedation.
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., Clarithromycin, ketoconazole, grapefruit juice) May increase oxycodone plasma levels.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., Rifampicin, carbamazepine, St John’s Wort) May decrease oxycodone efficacy.
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** (e.g., Paroxetine, quinidine) May increase oxycodone concentrations.
- **Anticholinergic Agents:** Increased risk of urinary retention and severe constipation.
- **MAOIs:** Potential for fatal interactions; contraindicated [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Agents:** Risk of serotonin toxicity when used with SSRIs or SNRIs.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. Oxycodone and naloxone cross the placenta. Contraindicated due to risk of neonatal withdrawal syndrome and respiratory depression.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Oxycodone passes into breast milk (milk:plasma ratio 3.4:1). Risks of sedation and respiratory depression in the infant.
- **Fertility:** Prolonged opioid use may cause androgen deficiency, leading to low libido, erectile dysfunction, or infertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for use in children or adolescents under 18 years of age.
- **Elderly Use:** Plasma concentrations are nominally higher (~18%). Dose should be reduced to 1/3 or 1/2 of the usual dose in infirm or debilitated patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** Plasma concentrations of both drugs increase. Initiate at 1/3 to 1/2 of the usual dose with cautious titration. Contraindicated in severe impairment if naloxone exposure is a concern.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in moderate to severe impairment. In mild impairment, initiate at 1/3 to 1/2 of the usual dose.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Oxycodone:** Full agonist at mu, kappa, and delta opioid receptors, providing systemic analgesia.
    - **Naloxone:** Competitive opioid antagonist. Due to high first-pass metabolism, oral bioavailability is <3%, allowing it to localise its antagonist effect in the gut to reduce constipation without reversing systemic analgesia.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Mean bioavailability of oxycodone is ~50%. 
    - **Metabolism:** Oxycodone is metabolised via CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 to noroxycodone and oxymorphone. Naloxone is metabolised in the liver to naloxone-3-glucuronide.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted in urine and faeces. Elimination half-life of oxycodone is ~4.8 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pain and Constipation (Studies 3001 & 3006):** 12-week trials demonstrated that the combination provided equivalent analgesia to oxycodone CR but with significant improvement in bowel function as measured by the Bowel Function Index (BFI).
- **Restless Legs Syndrome (Study OXN3502):** A 12-week trial in patients with severe idiopathic RLS (failing dopaminergic therapy) showed a significant decrease in IRLS scale scores compared to placebo (p<0.001).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Presented in PVC/Al blister packs.
- **Special Handling:** Keep out of reach of children; accidental ingestion can be fatal.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 8 (Controlled Drug) [Australian].
