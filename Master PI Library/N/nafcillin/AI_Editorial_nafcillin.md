# Editorial Product Information: Nafcillin

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nafcillin sodium (a semisynthetic penicillinase-resistant penicillin derived from 6-aminopenicillanic acid).
- **Brand Names:** Nafcillin for Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** J01CF02 (not explicitly stated in source, but standard for nafcillin).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Pharmacy Bulk Package (not for direct infusion): 10 grams as a dry powder in bottles for reconstitution [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** WG Critical Care, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Staphylococcal Infections:** Treatment of infections caused by penicillinase-producing staphylococci that have demonstrated susceptibility to the drug [US].
- **Empiric Therapy:** Initiation of therapy in suspected cases of resistant staphylococcal infections prior to the availability of susceptibility results [US].
- **Note on Limitations:** Nafcillin should not be used for infections caused by organisms susceptible to penicillin G. Therapy should be switched to another agent if susceptibility testing indicates the organism is not a resistant *Staphylococcus* [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosage:** 500 mg every 4 hours [US].
- **Severe Infections:** 1 gram every 4 hours [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Administer via slow IV infusion over at least 30 to 60 minutes to minimize the risk of vein irritation, thrombophlebitis, and extravasation [US].
    - Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Duration of Therapy:**
    - Varies by severity of infection and clinical response [US].
    - **Severe Staphylococcal Infections:** Minimum of 14 days [US].
    - **General:** Continue for at least 48 hours after the patient is afebrile and asymptomatic, with negative cultures [US].
    - **Endocarditis/Osteomyelitis:** May require significantly longer durations [US].
- **Pharmacy Bulk Package Instructions:** Reconstitute with 93 mL of Sterile Water for Injection or 0.9% Sodium Chloride to reach a concentration of 100 mg/mL. This must be further diluted before infusion. Once the bulk package closure is penetrated, fluid transfer must be completed within 4 hours [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Penicillin Hypersensitivity:** History of a hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reaction to any penicillin [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal anaphylactic reactions have been reported. These are more common in individuals with a history of penicillin or cephalosporin sensitivity, or multiple allergens. Immediate emergency treatment (epinephrine, oxygen, steroids, airway management) is required for anaphylaxis [US].
- **Clostridium difficile-Associated Diarrhea (CDAD):** Reported with nearly all antibacterial agents, ranging from mild diarrhea to fatal colitis. Monitor patients who present with diarrhea post-antibiotic use; symptoms may occur up to two months after treatment [US].
- **Extravasation and Tissue Necrosis:** Severe tissue necrosis and sloughing can occur with subcutaneous extravasation. Use of the intravenous route should be restricted to short-term therapy (24 to 48 hours) when possible due to the risk of thrombophlebitis [US].
- **Hepatic and Renal Caution:** As the liver is the primary route of clearance, caution is required in patients with concomitant hepatic and renal dysfunction. Monitor serum levels to avoid neurotoxicity [US].
- **Sodium Content:** Contains 65.8 mg (2.9 mEq) of sodium per gram of nafcillin. This may be clinically significant in patients requiring sodium restriction, such as those with congestive heart failure [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity (0.7% to 10%):** Immediate reactions (urticaria, pruritus, laryngospasm, hypotension, death) usually occur within 20 minutes. Delayed reactions (serum sickness-like symptoms, fever, malaise, myalgia, arthralgia) occur after 48 hours or up to 4 weeks [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, stomatitis, black or "hairy" tongue, and pseudomembranous colitis [US].
- **Hematologic:** Agranulocytosis, neutropenia, and bone marrow depression [US].
- **Renal:** Renal tubular damage and interstitial nephritis (manifested by rash, fever, eosinophilia, hematuria, proteinuria, and renal insufficiency) [US].
- **Local Reactions:** Pain, swelling, inflammation, phlebitis, and skin sloughing at the injection site [US].
- **Neurological:** Neurotoxic reactions (similar to penicillin G) with very high doses, particularly in patients with renal or hepatic failure [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Tetracycline:** A bacteriostatic antibiotic that may antagonize the bactericidal effect of nafcillin; concurrent use should be avoided [US].
- **Warfarin:** High-dose nafcillin (e.g., 2g q4h) may decrease the anticoagulant effect of warfarin. Monitor prothrombin time closely during and for up to 30 days after nafcillin discontinuation [US].
- **Cyclosporine:** Concurrent use may result in subtherapeutic cyclosporine levels; monitor levels in organ transplant patients [US].
- **Probenecid:** Increases and prolongs nafcillin plasma concentrations by reducing clearance [US].
- **Laboratory Interactions:** Nafcillin in urine can cause a false-positive reaction for protein when using the sulfosalicylic acid test (not seen with dipstick) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **US Category B.** Reproduction studies in rats (40x human dose) and mice (20x human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Penicillins are excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** One-generation studies in rats and mice showed no maternal or fetal abnormalities [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness for intravenous nafcillin have not been established. Due to immature hepatic and renal function, neonates and infants may experience impaired excretion and abnormally high serum levels [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Clinical studies did not include enough subjects ≥65 years to determine if they respond differently. Dosing should be cautious (low end of range) due to higher frequencies of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction. Elderly patients are at higher risk of thrombophlebitis and salt loading [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is generally necessary for isolated renal failure. Hemodialysis does not accelerate nafcillin clearance [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is significantly decreased in cirrhosis and biliary obstruction. If renal dysfunction is also present, dose adjustments based on serum level monitoring are required [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial cell wall biosynthesis during active multiplication [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed in bile, pleural, amniotic, and synovial fluids. Crosses the blood-brain barrier only with inflamed meninges.
    - **Protein Binding:** High (approximately 89.9% ± 1.5%), mainly to albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Primarily eliminated via non-renal routes (hepatic inactivation and biliary excretion). Only ~30% is excreted unchanged in the urine [US].
    - **Half-life:** 33 to 61 minutes (IV route) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study:** In a study of five healthy adults, a 500 mg IV dose over seven minutes resulted in a mean plasma concentration of 30 mcg/mL at 5 minutes post-injection, with a mean AUC of 18.06 mcg•h/mL [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Sterile Powder:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution Stability (at 100 mg/mL):**
    - **Room Temp (25°C):** 24 hours (in Water or 0.9% NaCl).
    - **Refrigerated (4°C):** 7 days.
    - **Frozen (-15°C):** 90 days.
- **Handling:** Once the Pharmacy Bulk Package is breached, use within 4 hours [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
