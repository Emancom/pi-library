# Editorial Product Information: Nitrofurantoin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of nitrofurantoin for use by healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nitrofurantoin (a synthetic antibacterial nitrofuran derivative).
- **Brand Names:** APO-NITROFURANTOIN [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01XE01 (Note: Not explicitly stated in the source but standard for this molecule).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Hard gelatin capsules (macrocrystals): 50 mg and 100 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian].
- **Physicochemical Properties:** Lemon yellow crystals or fine powder. Solubility increases with urine pH. The macrocrystalline form exhibits slower absorption and reduced gastrointestinal intolerance compared to standard tablets without compromising clinical efficacy [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs):** Treatment of infections such as cystitis and pyelitis caused by susceptible pathogens [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis:** Long-term suppressive therapy or prevention of UTIs [Australian].
- **Clinical Limitations:** Not indicated for the treatment of prostatitis or cortical/perinephric abscesses, as the drug does not achieve therapeutic plasma concentrations [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Administration:** Nitrofurantoin should be taken with food or milk to improve absorption and minimise gastrointestinal side effects [Australian].
- **Adult Dosing:**
    - **Acute UTI:** 50–100 mg four times daily. The maximum daily dose is 400 mg [Australian].
    - **Prophylaxis:** 50 mg or 100 mg at bedtime (nocte) [Australian].
- **Paediatric Dosing (Infants > 1 month):**
    - Calculated as 5–7 mg/kg body weight per 24 hours, administered in four divided doses [Australian].
- **Duration of Therapy:** For acute infections, therapy should continue for at least one week or for at least 3 days after urine sterility is confirmed [Australian].
- **Dose Titration:** For long-term prophylaxis, the lowest effective dose should be determined [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Renal Impairment:** Anuria, oliguria, or significant impairment defined as a creatinine clearance (CrCl) < 60 mL/min or clinically elevated serum creatinine [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to nitrofurantoin or other furan derivatives [Australian].
- **Pregnancy/Neonatal Use:** 
    - Contraindicated in pregnant women during labour and delivery or when the onset of labour is imminent [Australian].
    - Contraindicated in infants under one month of age due to the risk of haemolytic anaemia from immature enzyme systems (glutathione instability) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Pulmonary Reactions:** Acute, sub-acute, or chronic reactions may occur and can be life-threatening. Chronic reactions (e.g., interstitial pneumonitis or fibrosis) develop insidiously, typically in patients on therapy for >6 months. Monitoring of pulmonary status is mandatory for long-term users [Australian].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Rare but potentially fatal hepatic reactions (hepatitis, cholestatic jaundice, hepatic necrosis) have occurred. Onset of chronic active hepatitis may be insidious; monitor liver function tests (LFTs) periodically [Australian].
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** May become severe or irreversible; fatalities have been reported. Increased risk exists in patients with renal impairment, anaemia, diabetes mellitus, vitamin B deficiency, or debilitating disease. Discontinue immediately if numbness or tingling occurs [Australian].
- **Haemolytic Anaemia:** Linked to G6PD deficiency. Discontinue if haemolysis occurs [Australian].
- **Clostridioides difficile-associated Diarrhoea (CDAD):** Pseudomembranous colitis has been reported; consider in patients presenting with diarrhoea during or after treatment [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** May cause false-positive or spuriously high readings for serum bilirubin, urine creatinine, serum urea, and urine glucose (if using copper reduction methods). Glucose oxidase tests (e.g., Clinistix) are unaffected [Australian].
- **Urine Discolouration:** Patients should be advised that the drug may cause a harmless brownish discolouration of the urine [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Very Common | Nausea, anorexia, emesis (dose-related) |
| | Common/Uncommon | Abdominal pain, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, flatulence, constipation |
| **Neurological** | Rare/Not Specified | Peripheral neuropathy (including optic neuritis), headache, dizziness, nystagmus, vertigo, benign intracranial hypertension |
| **Respiratory** | Rare | Acute/Chronic pulmonary hypersensitivity (fever, chills, cough, dyspnoea, fibrosis) |
| **Hepatic** | Rare | Hepatitis, cholestatic jaundice, chronic active hepatitis |
| **Haematological** | Rare | Haemolytic anaemia, agranulocytosis, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, aplastic anaemia |
| **Dermatological** | Rare | Exfoliative dermatitis, erythema multiforme (inc. Stevens-Johnson syndrome), alopecia |
| **Hypersensitivity** | Rare | Anaphylaxis, lupus-like syndrome, angioedema, arthralgia |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antacids:** Reduce the potency of nitrofurantoin; do not co-administer [Australian].
- **Uricosurics (Probenecid/Sulfinpyrazone):** Inhibit renal tubular secretion of nitrofurantoin, increasing serum levels (potential toxicity) and decreasing urinary levels (reduced efficacy) [Australian].
- **Urinary pH Modifiers:** Acidifying drugs decrease excretion (increasing potential toxicity), while alkalinising drugs increase excretion (potentially reducing antibacterial effect) [Australian].
- **Phenobarbitone:** May inhibit the action of nitrofurantoin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category A** (for short-term therapy). 
    - Studies have not shown a potential for birth defects, but the drug crosses the placenta. It is contraindicated at term due to the risk of neonatal haemolytic anaemia [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in breast milk in negligible amounts. However, caution is advised, and the risk of haemolytic anaemia in infants < 1 month of age should be considered [Australian].
- **Fertility:** High doses in animals caused testicular atrophy. In humans, nitrofurantoin may decrease sperm counts and produce abnormal testicular histology [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in infants < 1 month old. Approved for use in older children with weight-based dosing [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data provided, though the requirement for adequate renal function (CrCl > 60 mL/min) is a critical limiting factor for this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated if CrCl is < 60 mL/min due to lack of efficacy (failure to reach therapeutic urine levels) and increased risk of systemic toxicity [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, but monitoring is required due to risks of hepatotoxicity [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Presumed to interfere with multiple bacterial enzyme systems. It is bacteriostatic at low concentrations and bactericidal at higher concentrations [Australian].
- **Microbiology:** Active against *E. coli*, *Staph. aureus*, and *Enterococci*. *Ps. aeruginosa* and many *Proteus* and *Klebsiella* strains are insensitive [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Well absorbed orally; peak plasma levels reached in 1–2 hours. Plasma levels are low (< 2.5 µg/mL) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 25–60% protein bound. Crosses the placenta and enters breast milk [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via nitro group reduction [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Rapid renal excretion (both glomerular filtration and tubular secretion); approximately 35–38% excreted unchanged. Plasma half-life is approximately 20 minutes [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific pivotal trial data summaries provided in the available source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** 2 years [Australian].
- **Special Handling:** Protect from light; nitrofurantoin darkens on exposure and decomposes upon contact with metals other than stainless steel or aluminium [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (S4) - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
