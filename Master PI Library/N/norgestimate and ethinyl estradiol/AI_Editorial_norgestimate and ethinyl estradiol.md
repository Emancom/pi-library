# Editorial Product Information: Norgestimate and Ethinyl Estradiol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of norgestimate and ethinyl estradiol for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and prescribing safety information.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for regional legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Norgestimate (progestin) and Ethinyl Estradiol (estrogen).
- **Chemical Class:** Combined Oral Contraceptive (COC).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Mono-Linyah** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Active tablets:** 0.250 mg norgestimate and 0.035 mg ethinyl estradiol (e.g., blue, round, biconvex, coated tablets) [US].
    - **Inert tablets:** Non-hormonal placebo (e.g., white, round, biconvex tablets) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Northstar Rx LLC; A-S Medication Solutions [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Pregnancy Prevention:** Indicated for use by females of reproductive potential to prevent pregnancy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- One tablet is taken orally at the same time every day, with or without food.
- Tablets must be taken in the order directed on the blister pack.

### Initiation Schedules [US]
- **Day 1 Start:** Take the first active tablet on the first day of menses. Take one active tablet daily for 21 days, followed by one inert tablet daily for 7 days.
- **Sunday Start:** Take the first active tablet on the first Sunday after the onset of menses. Use additional non-hormonal back-up contraception (e.g., condoms) for the first 7 days of the first cycle.

### Switching from Other Methods [US]
- **Other Oral Contraceptive:** Start on the day the next pack of the previous contraceptive would have started.
- **Transdermal Patch or Vaginal Ring:** Start on the day the next application or insertion was scheduled.
- **Injection:** Start on the day the next injection was scheduled.
- **IUD/IUS:** Start on the day of removal. If not removed on Day 1 of the cycle, use back-up contraception for the first 7 days.
- **Post-Abortion/Miscarriage:** 
    - *First Trimester:* Start immediately. 
    - *Second Trimester:* Do not start until 4 weeks post-event due to thromboembolic risk.
- **Post-Childbirth:** Start no earlier than 4 weeks postpartum in non-breastfeeding women.

### Missed Dose Instructions [US]
- **One active tablet missed:** Take as soon as remembered; continue taking one tablet daily.
- **Two active tablets missed (Week 1 or 2):** Take two tablets as soon as possible and two tablets the next day. Use back-up contraception for 7 days.
- **Two active tablets missed (Week 3) OR three or more missed (any week):**
    - *Day 1 Starters:* Discard the pack and start a new pack immediately.
    - *Sunday Starters:* Take one tablet daily until Sunday, then discard the pack and start a new pack.
    - *Note:* Use back-up contraception for 7 days after the missed doses.

## 4. Contraindications
- **High risk of arterial or venous thrombotic diseases:** Including smokers >35 years of age, current or history of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE), inherited/acquired hypercoagulopathies, cerebrovascular disease, coronary artery disease, or thrombogenic valvular/rhythm diseases [US].
- **Uncontrolled hypertension** or hypertension with vascular disease [US].
- **Diabetes mellitus** with vascular involvement [US].
- **Headaches with focal neurological symptoms** or migraine with aura; also contraindicated in women >35 with any migraine [US].
- **Liver disease:** Including acute viral hepatitis, severe cirrhosis, or liver tumors (benign or malignant) [US].
- **Undiagnosed abnormal uterine bleeding** [US].
- **Breast cancer:** Current diagnosis or history of hormone-sensitive breast cancer [US].
- **Concomitant Hepatitis C therapy:** Use with regimens containing ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir, with or without dasabuvir [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **Cigarette Smoking and Serious Cardiovascular Events:** Cigarette smoking increases the risk of serious cardiovascular events from COC use. This risk increases with age (particularly over 35 years) and with the number of cigarettes smoked. COC use is strictly contraindicated in women over 35 who smoke.

### Clinical Precautions
- **Thromboembolic Disorders:** Discontinue if an arterial or venous thrombotic event occurs. Discontinue at least 4 weeks before and 2 weeks after major surgery or prolonged immobilisation.
- **Liver Enzyme Elevations:** Discontinue if jaundice occurs. Concomitant use with certain Hepatitis C antivirals may cause significant ALT elevations.
- **Hypertension:** Monitor blood pressure regularly. Discontinue if blood pressure rises significantly in women with well-controlled hypertension.
- **Metabolic Effects:** Monitor prediabetic and diabetic women for altered glucose tolerance. Consider alternative methods for women with uncontrolled dyslipidaemia.
- **Headache:** Evaluate new, recurrent, persistent, or severe headaches. Discontinue if migraine increases in frequency or severity.
- **Bleeding Irregularities:** Unscheduled bleeding or spotting is common, especially in the first 3 months. Evaluate for malignancy or pregnancy if bleeding is persistent.
- **Depression:** Carefully observe patients with a history of depression; discontinue if it recurs to a serious degree.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (≥2%) [US]
| Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- |
| Headache/Migraine | 32.9% |
| Vaginal infection | 8.4% |
| Abdominal/Gastrointestinal pain | 7.8% |
| Genital discharge | 6.8% |
| Breast issues (pain, enlargement, discharge) | 6.3% |
| Mood disorders (depression, mood altered) | 5.0% |
| Flatulence | 3.2% |
| Nervousness | 2.9% |
| Rash | 2.6% |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Myocardial infarction, stroke, and venous thromboembolism (VTE).
- Hepatic adenomas and gallbladder disease.
- Anaphylactic reactions [Postmarketing].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Drugs like phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampin, and St. John’s Wort can decrease COC plasma concentrations, leading to breakthrough bleeding or contraceptive failure. Back-up contraception is required during and for 28 days after use of enzyme inducers [US].
- **Colesevelam:** Reduces the absorption of ethinyl estradiol. Administer COCs at least 4 hours apart from colesevelam [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin) may increase hormone levels [US].
- **Effect of COCs on Other Drugs:** COCs may increase levels of cyclosporine, theophylline, and tizanidine, and decrease levels of lamotrigine (potentially reducing seizure control), acetaminophen, and morphine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not indicated for use during pregnancy. If pregnancy is confirmed, discontinue the medication. Epidemiologic data have not shown an increased risk of birth defects when COCs are taken inadvertently during early pregnancy [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** COCs can reduce milk production. This is less likely once lactation is well-established. Non-hormonal methods are generally preferred until the infant is weaned [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for post-pubertal adolescents. Not indicated before menarche [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not studied in postmenopausal women; not indicated for this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in active liver disease or significantly impaired liver function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics not specifically studied in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primary effect is the suppression of ovulation via the reduction of gonadotropins (LH and FSH). Secondary mechanisms include changes in cervical mucus (increasing difficulty for sperm entry) and the endometrium (reducing likelihood of implantation) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Norgestimate (NGM) and ethinyl estradiol (EE) are rapidly absorbed. NGM is a pro-drug, rapidly converted via first-pass metabolism to norelgestromin (NGMN) and norgestrel (NG) [US].
    - **Distribution:** NGMN and NG are highly protein-bound (>97%), primarily to albumin and SHBG [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism via CYP450 enzymes.
    - **Elimination:** Metabolites are excreted in both urine (47%) and faeces (37%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- In three US clinical trials involving 1,651 women (aged 18–38) over 24,272 cycles, the pregnancy rate was approximately 1 pregnancy per 100 women-years [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
- **Light:** Protect from light [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
