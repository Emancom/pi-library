# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours. Daily maintenance doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). This should be administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Adjustment
- **Plasma Monitoring:** Therapy should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Normal levels range between 40 µmol/L and 50 µmol/L. 
- **Dose Titration:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) in dialysis patients may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment based on plasma levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with reports of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine Warning):** While this document concerns the IV formulation, it is noted that chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for dialysis patients (occurring at ≥5%) regardless of causality include:

- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - *General:* Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), flu syndrome (25–29%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), asthenia (8–12%), chest pain (6–15%).
    - *Cardiovascular:* Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%).
    - *Digestive:* Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
    - *Respiratory:* Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%), rhinitis (6–11%).
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, fever, tachycardia, arrhythmia, hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, anemia, dizziness, insomnia, and pruritus [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended following the initiation of levocarnitine or dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins, reducing the likelihood of protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category B (implied)]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, no adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy for metabolic disorders are established; dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing adjustments are required for ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). Safety and efficacy of the *oral* formulation have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing the necessary substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with organic acidopathies, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on dialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly, mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine increased from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L by week 8. While supplemental carnitine effectively increases plasma concentrations, its effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population remains undetermined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) at room temperature [US].
