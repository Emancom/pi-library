# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising data from the provided Product Information.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer intravenously via slow injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Given as a slow bolus or infusion. In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required. The maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Monitoring and Adjustment:** Dosing should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Normal levels are 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L. Downward dose adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on these levels [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Cases have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** For patients with metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting, body odor, and gastritis [US].
- **ESRD Clinical Trial Data (Frequency ≥5%):**
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%), Chest pain (6–15%), Tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
    - **Neurological:** Headache (3–37%), Dizziness (10–18%), Paresthesia (3–12%) [US].
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Abdominal pain (5–21%), Vomiting (9–21%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
    - **Other:** Injection site reactions (27–38%), Fever (5–12%), Asthenia (8–12%) [US].

### Postmarketing Experience
- Seizures and serious hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis) have been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concurrently with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but descriptive]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, no adequate studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies on levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers are unavailable. In dairy cows, levocarnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. Clinicians should weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation for the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients; see Section 3 [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Trough concentrations remained stable or increased over time [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only [US].
