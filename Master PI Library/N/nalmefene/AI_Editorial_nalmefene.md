# Editorial Product Information: Nalmefene

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals based on available regulatory documentation.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nalmefene hydrochloride (an opioid antagonist; a 6-methylene analogue of naltrexone).
- **Brand Name(s):** **ZURNAI** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Injection: 1.5 mg nalmefene base (equivalent to 1.7 mg nalmefene hydrochloride) in a 0.5 mL pre-filled, single-dose auto-injector [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) or Subcutaneous (SC) injection into the outer thigh.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Knoa Pharma LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Opioid Overdose:** Indicated for the emergency treatment of known or suspected opioid overdose (natural or synthetic) in adults and paediatric patients aged 12 years and older, manifesting as respiratory and/or central nervous system (CNS) depression [US].
- **Note:** Intended for immediate administration in settings where opioids may be present; not a substitute for professional emergency medical care [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Paediatric Patients $\ge$ 12 Years)
- **Initial Dose:** 1.5 mg (single auto-injector) administered by IM or SC injection into the anterolateral aspect of the thigh [US].
- **Administration:** Can be injected through clothing if necessary. Do not prime or test the device prior to use [US].

### Repeat Dosing
- **Observation:** If the desired response is not achieved after 2 to 5 minutes, or if the patient responds and subsequently relapses into respiratory depression, administer an additional 1.5 mg dose using a new auto-injector [US].
- **Frequency:** Additional doses may be given every 2 to 5 minutes until emergency medical assistance arrives [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Place the needle guard against the outer thigh, press firmly until a "click" is heard, and hold for 3 seconds.
- Confirm delivery by checking that the viewing window has turned completely orange [US].
- Seek emergency medical care immediately after the first dose.
- Place the patient in the recovery position (on their side) while awaiting assistance [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to nalmefene hydrochloride or any inactive ingredients (magnesium chloride, hydrochloric acid) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Recurrent Respiratory and CNS Depression:** The duration of action of some opioids may exceed that of nalmefene. Continued surveillance is essential, and repeat doses may be required [US].
- **Limited Efficacy with Partial Agonists:** Reversal of respiratory depression caused by partial agonists (e.g., buprenorphine) or mixed agonist/antagonists (e.g., pentazocine) may be incomplete and require higher or repeated doses [US].
- **Precipitation of Severe Opioid Withdrawal:** In opioid-dependent patients, administration may precipitate acute withdrawal symptoms (e.g., tachycardia, hypertension, gastrointestinal distress, agitation). In neonates, this withdrawal can be life-threatening [US].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Abrupt reversal of opioid effects, particularly postoperatively, may result in tachycardia, hypertension, seizures, ventricular arrhythmias, pulmonary oedema, or cardiac arrest. Increased risk is noted in patients with pre-existing CV disease [US].
- **Risk of Overdose from Attempts to Overcome Blockade:** Patients may attempt to overcome the antagonist effect with high doses of opioids, leading to fatal intoxication [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are reported based on healthy volunteer studies and clinical settings (postoperative and overdose) [US].

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea (18%), Feeling hot (25%), Headache (18%), Dizziness (15.9%–17.4%), Vomiting (11.4%–13%), Allodynia (11.4%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Palpitations (9.1%), Tinnitus (9.1%), Chills (1%–13.6%), Tachycardia (5%), Hypertension (5%), Postoperative pain (4%), Fever (3%), Ear discomfort (6.8%), Irritability (6.8%), Hot flush (6.8%), Vasodilatation (1%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Not specifically categorised; see "Incidence < 1%" below. |

- **Incidence < 1%:** Bradycardia, arrhythmia, diarrhoea, dry mouth, somnolence, depression, agitation, tremor, confusion, myoclonus, pharyngitis, pruritus, and urinary retention [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Abrupt withdrawal syndrome, pulmonary oedema, cardiovascular instability (ventricular fibrillation/tachycardia), and encephalopathy [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Opioid Agonists:** Nalmefene antagonises the therapeutic and toxic effects of all opioid agonists.
- **Partial Agonists/Mixed Agonists-Antagonists:** May require more frequent dosing due to slow dissociation from receptors (e.g., buprenorphine) [US].
- **Cardiovascular-active Drugs:** Increased risk of adverse CV effects when used following reversal of opioids in patients taking drugs with similar CV profiles [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter category in provided source [US]. 
    - **Risk Summary:** No available data on use in pregnant women. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no embryo-fetal toxicity at doses significantly higher than the human dose. Opioid overdose is a medical emergency; life-sustaining treatment should not be withheld [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data on the presence of nalmefene in human milk or effects on the breastfed infant. Nalmefene is present in rat milk. Caution is advised, but emergency use should not be withheld [US].
- **Fertility:** Oral administration in rats up to 1200 mg/m²/day did not affect fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for patients $\ge$ 12 years. Not indicated for use in children under 12 [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness observed between elderly and younger patients, though higher systemic exposure may occur due to decreased organ function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance is reduced (approx. 25–27%) in end-stage renal disease (ESRD). However, no dose adjustment is required for single-dose emergency administration [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is reduced by approximately 28.3%. No dose adjustment is recommended for acute use [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive antagonist at opioid receptors. It has no opioid agonist activity [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reverses respiratory depression, sedation, and hypotension induced by opioids. It has a longer duration of action than naloxone at fully reversing doses [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median $T_{max}$ is 0.25 hours (IM). Bioavailability is approx. 113%–115% relative to IM/IV 1 mg injections [US].
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributed; 45% protein bound. Crosses the blood-brain barrier (80% receptor occupancy within 5 minutes) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via hepatic glucuronide conjugation to an inactive metabolite. Trace N-dealkylation also occurs [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 9.07 hours. Primarily excreted in urine (<5% unchanged) and feces (17%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacodynamic Study (NAL1004):** A study in 24 opioid-experienced, non-dependent subjects using an opioid-induced respiratory depression (OIRD) model (fentanyl-induced). Nalmefene 1.5 mg IM showed onset of reversal between 2.5 to 5 minutes, with full recovery of respiratory drive between 5 and 15 minutes [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Study (NAL1005):** A relative bioavailability study in healthy volunteers comparing the 1.5 mg auto-injector to 1.0 mg IM and IV nalmefene [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions permitted between 5°C to 40°C (41°F to 104°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze or refrigerate. Protect from light. Keep in the carton until use [US].
- **Maintenance:** Inspect the viewing window every 30 days. The solution should be clear and colorless to light yellow; replace if discolored or contains particulates [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (N/A for US jurisdiction).
