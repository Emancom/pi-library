# Editorial Product Information: Natamycin

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of natamycin ophthalmic suspension for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for regional compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Natamycin (also known as pimaricin).
- **Chemical Class:** Tetraene polyene antifungal.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Natacyn** [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01AA10.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile ophthalmic suspension 5% (50 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Harrow Eye, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Fungal Keratitis:** Treatment of keratitis caused by susceptible organisms, including *Fusarium solani* [US].
- **Fungal Blepharitis:** Treatment of blepharitis caused by susceptible organisms [US].
- **Fungal Conjunctivitis:** Treatment of conjunctivitis caused by susceptible organisms [US].
- **Susceptible Organisms:** Includes species of *Candida*, *Aspergillus*, *Cephalosporium*, *Fusarium*, and *Penicillium* [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Fungal Keratitis:** 
    - **Initial dose:** Instill one drop into the conjunctival sac at 1-hour or 2-hour intervals [US].
    - **Maintenance:** After 3 to 4 days, the frequency may be reduced to one drop 6 to 8 times daily.
    - **Duration:** Therapy typically continues for 14 to 21 days or until the active keratitis resolves. Gradual reduction of dosage (at 4 to 7-day intervals) is recommended to ensure the elimination of replicating organisms [US].
- **Fungal Blepharitis and Conjunctivitis:**
    - **Initial dose:** One drop 4 to 6 times daily may be sufficient [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Shake well before using** [US].
- Avoid touching the dropper tip to any surface to prevent contamination.
- Inspect the container before use; use only if the container is undamaged.
- **Contact Lenses:** Patients should be advised not to wear contact lenses if signs and symptoms of fungal ocular infection are present [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to natamycin or any other component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Route of Administration:** For topical ophthalmic use only. **Not for injection** [US].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** If no clinical improvement is observed after 7 to 10 days of treatment, it suggests the infection may be caused by a non-susceptible microorganism. Therapy should be re-evaluated based on laboratory studies and clinical exam [US].
- **Ocular Sequestration:** The suspension regularly adheres to areas of epithelial ulceration or retains in the fornices [US].
- **Fungal Endophthalmitis:** Effectiveness as a single agent in fungal endophthalmitis has not been established [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies of adverse effects for natamycin are not established, as the following events were identified through voluntary post-marketing reports in a population of unknown size [US]:
- **Ocular:** Eye irritation, eye pain, eye edema, eye hyperemia (redness), corneal opacity, change in vision, foreign body sensation, tearing, and paresthesia.
- **Systemic:** Allergic reaction, dyspnea (shortness of breath), and chest pain.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources. Systemic absorption is not expected following topical administration, which limits the potential for systemic drug interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned in the latest labeling; however, the source states animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if natamycin can cause fetal harm or affect reproduction capacity. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known whether natamycin is excreted in human milk. Caution is advised when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies to evaluate the impairment of fertility have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between geriatric and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Natamycin is a polyene antifungal that binds to the sterol moiety (ergosterol) of the fungal cell membrane. This binding alters membrane permeability, leading to the depletion of essential cellular constituents and subsequent cell death [US].
### Pharmacodynamics
- **Activity:** Predominantly fungicidal; activity is dose-related.
- **Antibacterial Activity:** Natamycin is not effective against gram-positive or gram-negative bacteria [US].
### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Systemic absorption is not expected following topical application. GI absorption is very poor.
- **Distribution:** Topical administration produces effective concentrations within the corneal stroma but fails to reach effective levels in the intraocular fluid [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Clinical Trial Data:** Specific pivotal trial summaries are not provided in the primary source document. Clinical use is based on established in vitro activity against specific fungal pathogens and post-marketing experience [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 2°C to 24°C (36°F-75°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Protect from light and excessive heat [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx Only (Prescription Only). NDC: 82667-012-05 [US].
- **Australia:** Noted as "FDA (Australia)" in the user request, though Australian regulations are typically managed by the TGA. In Australia, ophthalmic antifungals are generally Prescription Only Medicines (Schedule 4).
