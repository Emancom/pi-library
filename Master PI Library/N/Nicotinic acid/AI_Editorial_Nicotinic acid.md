# Editorial Product Information: Nicotinic Acid

## Editorial Note
- This editorial PI has been developed using official Product Information from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nicotinic acid (also known as Niacin)
- **Chemical Class:** Water-soluble B-complex vitamin
- **Brand Names:** Nicotinic Acid [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C10AD02
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 250 mg tablets (white, bevelled edge with a breakline) [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd (trading as Viatris) [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hyperlipidaemia and Hypertriglyceridaemia:** Adjunctive therapy (with diet and other measures) for the treatment of hyperlipidaemia, hypertriglyceridaemia, and Frederickson-Lees Levy hyperlipoproteinaemia types II, IIB, III, IV, and V [Australian].
- **Pellagra:** Treatment of nicotinic acid deficiency. (Note: In the Australian context, this is typically limited to special clinical situations or non-nutritional factors) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Administration:** Tablets must be taken orally after meals to minimize gastric irritation.
- **Pellagra:** 250 mg (one tablet) twice daily [Australian].
- **Hyperlipidaemia/Hypertriglyceridaemia:** 
    - Initial dose: 250 mg three times daily.
    - Titration: Increase by 250 mg increments every fourth day until a target daily dose of 3 g to 4.5 g is achieved.
    - Dosage should be individualised based on plasma cholesterol and triglyceride response [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hepatic Dysfunction:** Nicotinic acid may exacerbate existing liver impairment [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Active or history of peptic ulcer (large doses) [Australian].
- **Metabolic:** Overt diabetes mellitus, gout, or hyperuricaemia [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular:** Recent myocardial infarction (MI); patients who exhibit a sudden fall in peripheral vascular resistance; heart disease; arterial bleeding [Australian].
- **Other:** Gallbladder disease; glaucoma; severe idiosyncratic reactions to nicotinic acid [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Monitoring:** Vasodilation typically occurs within 20 minutes of administration and lasts up to 60 minutes. Therapy must be withdrawn immediately if the patient experiences a myocardial infarction [Australian].
- **Hepatic Function:** Frequent monitoring of liver function tests (LFTs) is mandatory during therapy to detect potential adverse hepatic effects [Australian].
- **Glucose Tolerance:** Nicotinic acid may decrease glucose tolerance. Regular glucose tolerance testing is advised; diabetic patients may require adjustments to diet or hypoglycaemic therapy [Australian].
- **Uric Acid Levels:** Long-term therapy is associated with elevated uric acid levels; frequent monitoring is advised to prevent precipitation of gout [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Irritation:** Nicotinic acid stimulates histamine release, increasing gastric acid secretion. Close supervision is required for patients prone to GI irritation [Australian].
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** 
    - May cause false elevations in fluorometric determinations of urinary catecholamines.
    - May produce false-positive results for urinary glucose (using Benedict's reagent) and blood bilirubin tests [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by system organ class. Frequency data is not explicitly provided in available sources.

- **Cardiovascular:** Acute flushing (very common following dose), sensation of heat, pounding in the head, headache, hypotension. Atrial fibrillation and other arrhythmias may occur in patients with coronary heart disease (CHD) [Australian].
- **Dermatological:** Pruritus, skin dryness with mild exfoliation, brown pigmentation, hyperkeratosis, urticaria, rash, and furunculosis. These effects are generally reversible upon cessation of therapy [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, heartburn, flatulence, and activation of peptic ulcers [Australian].
- **Hepatic:** Cholestatic jaundice, elevated LFTs, hepatomegaly, ascites, patchy fibrosis, necrosis, and lymphocyte infiltration [Australian].
- **Endocrine/Metabolic:** Increased insulin requirements in diabetics, hypothyroidism, hyperuricaemia [Australian].
- **Nervous System:** Nervousness [Australian].
- **Special Senses:** Toxic amblyopia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Statins (Lovastatin, Pravastatin, Simvastatin):** Increased risk of myopathy, rhabdomyolysis, and acute renal failure. Clinical monitoring is essential [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives / Adrenergic Blocking Agents:** Additive vasodilating effects may lead to significant postural hypotension [Australian].
- **Anti-hyperglycaemic Therapy:** Nicotinic acid induces hyperglycaemia; dosage of insulin or oral hypoglycaemics may require adjustment [Australian].
- **Aspirin:** May reduce the severity of flushing/warmth but may also increase and prolong nicotinic acid concentrations, potentially increasing toxicity [Australian].
- **Clonidine:** May reduce skin flushing associated with nicotinic acid [Australian].
- **Colestipol:** May reduce the absorption of nicotinic acid. However, combined use has been shown to lower cholesterol more effectively than colestipol alone [Australian].
- **Isoniazid:** May increase nicotinic acid requirements [Australian].
- **Nicotine (Transdermal):** Concurrent use may increase the frequency of flushing and dizziness [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** Case reports indicate a risk of delirium (paranoid ideation/asterixis) and lactic acidosis [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Contraindicated.** Australian Category B2 (Limited human data; animal studies inadequate, but no evidence of increased fetal damage). Nicotinic acid should not be used in pregnant women or women of childbearing age [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** **Contraindicated** [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available; use with caution [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic dysfunction. Monitor closely for signs of renal failure if used with statins [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reduces serum cholesterol and triglycerides by inhibiting the synthesis of very low-density lipoproteins (VLDL), the precursors to LDL. Proposed mechanisms include inhibition of hepatic apolipoprotein B-100 synthesis, increased lipoprotein lipase activity, and reduced free fatty acid mobilisation from adipose tissue [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Large doses cause vasodilation via prostacyclin mediation. It also stimulates histamine release, increasing gastric motility [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed from the GI tract.
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed into body tissues.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised in the liver to nicotinamide (at physiological doses). In therapeutic doses, a significant portion is excreted unchanged.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life and time to peak serum concentration are both approximately 45 minutes [Australian].
