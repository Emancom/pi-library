# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in provided source (Generic: Levocarnitine Injection, USP) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - Maximum recorded dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration Timing:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Adjustment:** Dose adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **United States:** None known.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Plasma carnitine levels should be obtained prior to therapy.
    - Recommended monitoring includes weekly and monthly blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic high-dose oral administration (not specific to the IV formulation) in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequently confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **Body as a Whole:** Pain (generic), abdominal pain, headache, infection, injection site reaction.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension.
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea, vomiting.
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea (in ESRD populations).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness.

### Common (1–10%)
- **Digestive:** Nausea, gastritis, constipation, dyspepsia, anorexia.
- **Cardiovascular:** Chest pain, tachycardia, palpitations.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes.
- **Nervous:** Insomnia, paresthesia, depression, anxiety.
- **Skin:** Pruritus, rash.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis** and severe hypersensitivity.
- **Seizures** (new onset or exacerbation).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound, suggesting a low potential for displacement-based interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter grade in the provided text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefit of supplementation for the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were found in standard tests (Salmonella, Saccharomyces, Schizosaccharomyces) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is consistent with adult weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg). See Section 3.
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety of high-dose **oral** levocarnitine in renal failure is cautioned due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose, approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean plasma carnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to trough levels of 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effects on specific clinical outcomes in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only.
