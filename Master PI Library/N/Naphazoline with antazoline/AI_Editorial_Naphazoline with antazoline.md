# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document has been synthesized from official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acids.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer intravenously as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
- **Loading Dose:** Often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Timing:** Administer into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels.
- Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L).

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and assess the risk-benefit ratio before re-challenge [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy.
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality):
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), headache (3–37%), fever (5–12%), chest pain (6–15%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%).
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Dizziness (10–18%), insomnia (3–6%), depression (5–6%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).

### Post-Marketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis.
- **General:** Body odor (transient).
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Animal reproductive studies (rats and rabbits) using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following exogenous administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 2). Oral formulations should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation (see Section 5).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic/fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15–16% [US].
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly), mean trough concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Trough concentrations showed a dose-proportional increase across 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg regimens over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability (Mixed):** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) when stored at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Single-Use:** Discard any unused portion as the vial contains no preservative.
