# Editorial Product Information: Naldemedine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Note:** At the time of this editorial's publication, official PI documents for other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison. Clinical recommendations for those regions should be sought from local regulatory authorities.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Naldemedine (present as naldemedine tosylate).
- **Chemical Class:** Peripherally-acting mu-opioid receptor antagonist (PAMORA); derivative of naltrexone.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Symproic** [US].
- **ATC Code:** A06AH05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 0.2 mg film-coated tablets [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Collegium Pharmaceutical, Inc. (previously BioDelivery Sciences International Inc) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Opioid-Induced Constipation (OIC):** Treatment of OIC in adult patients with chronic non-cancer pain, including patients with chronic pain related to prior cancer or its treatment who do not require frequent (e.g., weekly) opioid dosage escalation [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosage:** 0.2 mg orally once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - May be taken with or without food [US].
    - Prior alteration of the existing analgesic regimen is not required before starting naldemedine.
    - Effectiveness may be reduced in patients who have been receiving opioids for less than 4 weeks.
- **Discontinuation:** Naldemedine should be discontinued if the opioid pain medication is discontinued.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, standard clinical practice suggests taking the dose as soon as remembered unless it is nearly time for the next dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Gastrointestinal Obstruction:** Known or suspected GI obstruction or patients at increased risk of recurrent obstruction, due to the potential for GI perforation [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of hypersensitivity reaction to naldemedine (reactions have included rash and bronchospasm) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Cases of GI perforation, including fatal outcomes, have been reported with PAMORAs. Use is contraindicated in patients with known/suspected obstruction. Exercise caution in patients with conditions that impair GI wall integrity (e.g., GI cancer, diverticulitis, Crohn’s disease, or those receiving chemotherapy/radiation). Monitor for severe or worsening abdominal pain and discontinue immediately if perforation is suspected [US].
- **Opioid Withdrawal:** While naldemedine is designed for peripheral action, symptoms consistent with opioid withdrawal (e.g., hyperhidrosis, chills, diarrhea, abdominal pain, tachycardia) have occurred. Patients with disruptions to the blood-brain barrier (BBB) may be at increased risk of central withdrawal or reduced analgesia [US].
- **Monitoring:** Monitor for symptoms of withdrawal and severe GI adverse events.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally gastrointestinal in nature. Frequencies are derived from 12-week and 52-week clinical trials [US].

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (Naldemedine 0.2mg) | Frequency (Placebo) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Abdominal Pain** | 8% – 11% | 2% – 5% |
| **Diarrhea** | 7% | 2% – 3% |
| **Nausea** | 4% – 6% | 2% – 5% |
| **Vomiting** | 3% | 2% |
| **Gastroenteritis** | 2% – 3% | 1% |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Gastrointestinal perforation and severe opioid withdrawal.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Rare reports of bronchospasm and rash [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A Inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, St. John’s Wort):** Significantly decrease naldemedine plasma concentrations. **Avoid concomitant use** [US].
- **Moderate CYP3A Inducers (e.g., efavirenz):** May decrease exposure by approximately 43%. Clinical significance is unknown.
- **Strong/Moderate CYP3A Inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole, fluconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir):** Increase naldemedine plasma concentrations. **Monitor for adverse reactions** [US].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Inhibitors (e.g., cyclosporine, amiodarone, quinidine):** Increase naldemedine concentrations. **Monitor for adverse reactions** [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Other Opioid Antagonists:** Potential for additive receptor antagonism and increased risk of opioid withdrawal. **Avoid concomitant use** [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (Current US labeling uses descriptive risk summary). 
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Naldemedine crosses the placenta. Use during pregnancy may precipitate opioid withdrawal in the fetus due to the immature fetal blood-brain barrier. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of naldemedine in human milk. Radioactivity was detected in rat milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions (including withdrawal) in the infant, it is advised to either discontinue breastfeeding or discontinue the drug. If the drug is stopped, breastfeeding may be resumed **3 days after the final dose** [US].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies (rats), no effect on fertility was observed at doses up to 17,000 times the human AUC, though a prolongation of the diestrous phase was noted at high doses [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between geriatric and younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients with mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment, or those with End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) requiring haemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild/Moderate (Child-Pugh A or B):** No dosage adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** **Avoid use** due to a lack of clinical data [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Naldemedine is a peripherally-acting mu-opioid receptor antagonist. It contains a side chain that increases its molecular weight and polar surface area, which, combined with being a P-gp substrate, limits its ability to cross the blood-brain barrier. This allows it to treat OIC without interfering with centrally-mediated opioid analgesia [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is ~0.75 hours (fasted). A high-fat meal delays Tmax to 2.5 hours but does not meaningfully change the total AUC.
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is 93% to 94%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by **CYP3A** to nor-naldemedine, with a minor contribution from **UGT1A3**.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 11 hours. Excretion is 57% via urine and 35% via feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Studies 1 and 2 (COMPOSE-1 and -2):** Replicate 12-week, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in patients with OIC and chronic non-cancer pain. 
- **Primary Endpoint:** Responder analysis (at least 3 SBMs/week and an increase of ≥1 SBM/week from baseline).
- **Results:** Responder rates were significantly higher for naldemedine (48% and 53%) compared to placebo (35% and 34%) [US].
- **Study 3:** A 52-week long-term safety study which confirmed that the safety profile remained consistent over extended use [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Store in a light-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only; not a controlled substance.
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
