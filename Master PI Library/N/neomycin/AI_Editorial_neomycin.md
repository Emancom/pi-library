# Unified Editorial Product Information: Neomycin

**Editorial Note:** This document represents a unified synthesis of Product Information (PI) for neomycin, derived from regulatory filings provided by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). This editorial PI combines data regarding neomycin as used in human topical combination therapy [TGA] and neomycin sulfate used as a mono-therapy for oral veterinary administration [FDA]. Readers should note the distinct indications and safety profiles associated with different routes of administration and target species.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Active Ingredient:** Neomycin (as sulfate)

This substance is available in multiple pharmaceutical forms, including:
*   **Topical Ointment (Human):** Often formulated in combination with triamcinolone acetonide, nystatin, and gramicidin [TGA].
*   **Soluble Powder (Veterinary):** Concentrated powder for oral administration via drinking water or drench [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Human Use (Topical):**
Indicated for the management of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of various dermatoses where a secondary bacterial infection—susceptible to neomycin—is present or considered likely [TGA].

**Veterinary Use (Oral):**
*   **Cattle (excluding veal calves), Swine, Sheep, and Goats:** Treatment and control of colibacillosis (bacterial enteritis) caused by *Escherichia coli* susceptible to neomycin sulfate [FDA].
*   **Turkeys:** Control of mortality associated with susceptible *E. coli* organisms [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to neomycin or other aminoglycoside antibiotics [TGA].
*   **Ophthalmic Use:** Neomycin preparations intended for skin or oral use are strictly contraindicated for ophthalmic application [TGA].
*   **Specific Lesions (Topical Human):** Contraindicated in tuberculous skin lesions and most viral skin infections, including Herpes simplex, vaccinia, and varicella [TGA].
*   **Specific Veterinary Exclusions:** Not for use in calves to be processed for veal. Not for use in female dairy cattle 20 months of age or older [FDA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
**General Safety:**
If irritation or sensitivity occurs during treatment, therapy should be discontinued. Chronic or prolonged use may lead to the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi [TGA].

**Systemic Toxicity:**
Due to the inherent risks of nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity associated with aminoglycosides, neomycin should not be applied to extensive areas of damaged skin or any site where significant systemic absorption is possible [TGA].

**Human Safety (Veterinary Product):**
Oral soluble powder formulations of neomycin sulfate are strictly not for use in humans. Keep out of reach of children [FDA].

**Veterinary Residue Warnings:**
Strict withdrawal periods must be observed prior to slaughter to prevent illegal drug residues in meat:
*   **Turkeys:** 0 days
*   **Cattle:** 1 day
*   **Sheep:** 2 days
*   **Swine and Goats:** 3 days [FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
**Human Topical Application:**
Apply a thin layer of ointment to the affected area two to three times daily [TGA]. The use of occlusive dressings is generally discouraged as it increases the risk of sensitivity and systemic absorption [TGA].

**Veterinary Oral Administration:**
The standard dose is 10 mg of neomycin sulfate per pound of body weight per day.
*   **Duration:** Turkeys (5 days); Cattle, swine, sheep, and goats (maximum of 14 days) [FDA].
*   **Method:** Administer in drinking water or as a drench. Fresh medicated water should be prepared daily [FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
**Local Reactions (Topical):**
Reported effects include burning, itching, irritation, dryness, and allergic contact dermatitis. If used with corticosteroids, it may also cause skin atrophy, striae, or folliculitis [TGA].

**Systemic Reactions:**
Ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity may occur if the drug is absorbed through damaged skin or large surface areas [TGA].

**Veterinary Observations:**
If clinical symptoms (fever, depression) persist after 2-3 days of oral treatment, the diagnosis should be re-evaluated by a veterinarian. Systemic levels are generally low via the oral route due to poor GI absorption [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
Specific drug interaction studies for topical or veterinary oral neomycin were not identified in the source documents [TGA, FDA]. However, healthcare providers should be aware of the potential for additive toxicity if used concurrently with other systemic aminoglycosides.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
**Pregnancy (Category D):**
Aminoglycosides cross the placental barrier. There is evidence of selective uptake by the fetal kidney and potential for damage to the eighth cranial nerve (ototoxicity) following in-utero exposure. While systemic levels from topical use are typically lower, the potential for fetal harm exists if significant absorption occurs [TGA].

**Breastfeeding:**
It is unknown if topical neomycin is excreted in human breast milk in significant amounts. Caution is advised when prescribing to nursing women [TGA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
**Absorption:**
*   **Skin:** Neomycin is generally not absorbed through intact skin but can be absorbed through inflamed or denuded areas [TGA].
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Absorption from the GI tract is very low [FDA].

**Distribution and Excretion:**
Once absorbed systemically, neomycin is rapidly excreted unchanged by the kidneys. The half-life is approximately 2 to 3 hours [TGA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Neomycin is an aminoglycoside antibiotic that exerts bactericidal activity against various gram-negative and some gram-positive organisms. It acts by binding to the bacterial ribosome, thereby inhibiting protein synthesis. It is notably active against *Escherichia coli* but is not effective against *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* or viruses [TGA, FDA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Topical Ointment:** Store below 25°C [TGA].
*   **Soluble Powder:** Store in a dry place between 20°C and 25°C (68°F–77°F). Brief excursions between 15°C and 40°C are permitted. Ensure partially used containers are securely closed to prevent caking [FDA].
