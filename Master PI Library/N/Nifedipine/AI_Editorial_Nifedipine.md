# Editorial Product Information: Nifedipine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of nifedipine controlled-release formulations for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian (TGA) source was available for analysis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nifedipine (a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker).
- **Brand Names:** Addos XR [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C08CA05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Controlled-release (XR) tablets available in 30 mg and 60 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Management of high blood pressure [Australian].
- **Chronic Stable Angina Pectoris:** Prophylactic treatment [Australian].
- **Note:** This formulation is not suitable for the acute treatment of angina due to its modified-release profile [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Therapy should be individualised and adjusted gradually based on clinical response.
- **Hypertension:**
    - Initial dose: 30 mg once daily.
    - Starting dose of 20 mg may be considered if medically indicated (though 20 mg tablets are not available under the Addos XR brand) [Australian].
    - Titration: Usually over 7 to 14 days. Dose may be increased up to 120 mg daily [Australian].
- **Chronic Stable Angina:**
    - Initial dose: 30 mg once daily.
    - Maximum dose: 90 mg once daily. Experience with doses exceeding 90 mg in angina patients is limited [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole with liquid. They must not be chewed, crushed, or broken. May be taken independently of meals.
- **Food Restrictions:** **Grapefruit juice must be avoided** as it inhibits nifedipine metabolism.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to nifedipine, other dihydropyridines, or any excipients [Australian].
- **Pregnancy and Lactation:** Contraindicated throughout pregnancy and during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Status:** Cardiogenic shock; within the first 8 days following an acute myocardial infarction [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Patients with a Kock pouch (ileostomy after proctocolectomy) [Australian].
- **Drug Interactions:** Concomitant use with rifampicin [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Excessive Hypotension:** Caution is required in patients with severe hypotension (systolic BP < 90 mmHg).
- **Paradoxical Angina/MI:** Rare cases of increased angina frequency or myocardial infarction have been reported shortly after initiation or dose escalation, particularly in patients with severe obstructive coronary artery disease [Australian].
- **Beta-blocker Withdrawal:** Nifedipine provides no protection against the effects of abrupt beta-blocker withdrawal; such withdrawal must be gradual.
- **Congestive Heart Failure:** Use with caution in patients with poor cardiac reserve.
- **Peripheral Oedema:** Dose-dependent peripheral oedema (arteriolar vasodilation) occurs in approximately 10% (at 30 mg) to 33% (at 120 mg) of patients [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Obstruction:** Because the tablet is non-deformable, caution is required in patients with pre-existing severe GI narrowing. Rare cases of bezoars requiring surgery have occurred [Australian].
- **Shortened GI Transit Time:** Efficacy may be reduced in conditions like chronic diarrhoea or short bowel syndrome where transit time is <18–22 hours [Australian].
- **Diabetes:** May theoretically impair glucose metabolism.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trial data (n=9,566) [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Headache, dizziness, palpitations, constipation, feeling unwell, asthenia, peripheral oedema. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Anxiety, sleep disorders, chest pain, tachycardia, angina pectoris, diarrhoea, dry mouth, dyspepsia, nausea, vomiting, transient increase in liver enzymes, muscle cramps, joint swelling, dyspnoea, pruritus, rash, polyuria, erectile dysfunction. |
| **Rare** (0.01–0.1%) | Urticaria, substernal chest pain, anorexia, gingivitis, gum hyperplasia, arthralgia, hypaesthesia, maculopapular rash, sweating. |

- **Serious/Post-marketing effects:** Agranulocytosis, leukopenia, anaphylactic reactions, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), exfoliative dermatitis, and intestinal obstruction [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP3A4)
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., Macrolides, Protease inhibitors, Azole antifungals, Fluoxetine, Nefazodone, Cimetidine). These may increase nifedipine plasma levels; blood pressure monitoring and dose reduction may be required [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** 
    - **Rifampicin:** Strongly reduces nifedipine bioavailability; concomitant use is **contraindicated** [Australian].
    - **Antiepileptics:** Phenytoin, carbamazepine, and phenobarbital may reduce nifedipine efficacy.
- **Grapefruit Juice:** Increases nifedipine concentrations; effect can last up to 3 days after ingestion.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Antihypertensives:** Potentiation of BP-lowering effects (e.g., diuretics, ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, PDE5 inhibitors).
- **Digoxin:** Nifedipine may reduce digoxin clearance; monitor digoxin levels [Australian].
- **Quinidine:** Nifedipine may decrease quinidine levels; conversely, quinidine withdrawal may increase nifedipine levels.
- **Tacrolimus:** Levels may be increased by nifedipine; monitor tacrolimus concentrations.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Contraindicated. Animal studies show teratogenic, embryotoxic, and fetotoxic effects (e.g., digital anomalies, malformations). Potential for fetal hypoxia and intrauterine growth retardation.
- **Breastfeeding:** Nifedipine passes into breast milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued if nifedipine treatment is necessary [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Reversible biochemical changes in spermatozoa have been noted in isolated cases of IVF, potentially impairing sperm function [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Use with caution due to higher risk of hypotension or cerebrovascular insufficiency; lower doses may be required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is reduced, and half-life is prolonged in severe liver disease. Careful monitoring is required; dose reduction may be necessary [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No substantial pharmacokinetic changes detected compared to healthy volunteers [Australian].
- **Ethnic Groups:** South Asian patients may have higher AUC and slower elimination; therapy should be initiated at low doses [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the transmembrane influx of calcium ions through voltage-dependent channels into cardiac muscle and vascular smooth muscle. This results in peripheral arterial vasodilation, reduced peripheral resistance, and decreased afterload.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Near-complete absorption (>90%). Controlled-release tablets exhibit zero-order absorption kinetics, reaching a plateau at ~6 hours [Australian].
    - **Bioavailability:** ~86% relative to IR formulations.
    - **Distribution:** ~95% protein bound (albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Almost completely metabolised in the liver via CYP3A4 to inactive metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** 60–80% excreted renally as metabolites; 5–15% via bile/faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Angina Clinical Trials:** Pivotal studies showed nifedipine XR (30 mg and 60 mg) improved exercise tolerance test (ETT) parameters. 60 mg once daily was found to be as effective as atenolol 100 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** In patients on beta-blockers, nifedipine XR up to 90 mg daily was more effective than modified-release nitrates in improving ETT parameters measured 22–24 hours post-dose [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Sensitivity:** Nifedipine is sensitive to light; tablets should remain in original packaging until use.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
