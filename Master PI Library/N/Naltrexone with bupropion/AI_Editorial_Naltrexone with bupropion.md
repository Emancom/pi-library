# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the US-based documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]; Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd.

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Titration:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustments:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase seizure frequency or severity in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** In patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis, chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Clinical Outcome in ESRD:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the specific effect on clinical outcomes (signs and symptoms) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine treatment or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a specific letter category in provided text, but states "use only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant of excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have been established for the treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. High doses of *oral* formulations are cautioned in renal impairment due to metabolite (TMA/TMAO) accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, a 20 mg/kg dose increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations; trough levels typically reach 54–180 µmol/L after one week of therapy (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients undergoing hemodialysis (20 mg/kg three times weekly) showed a significant increase in mean plasma levocarnitine concentrations from 20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis baseline) to trough levels of 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of an opened vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
