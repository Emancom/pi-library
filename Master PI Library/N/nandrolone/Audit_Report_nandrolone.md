## Close Paraphrases

- **Section 10 (Pharmacokinetics):** The list of metabolites ("19-norandrosterone, 19-noretiocholanolone, and 19-norepiandrosterone") is a direct extraction, though these are chemical names that cannot be reasonably paraphrased.
- **Section 5 (Oedema):** The list of electrolytes ("sodium, chloride, potassium") follows the specific sequence in both TGA and FDA documents.
- **Section 2 (Indications):** The list of indications for the TGA product is a direct transcription of the bullet points on Page 2 of Document 1.

## Originality Strengths

- **Structural Synthesis:** The editorial successfully merges two very different documents—one which focused heavily on renal anemia (FDA) and one with broad anabolic/palliative indications (TGA). 
- **Tabular Format:** The Adverse Effects section (Section 6) is an original synthesis. It takes the disparate "Hepatobiliary," "Genitourinary," and "Endocrine" lists from both documents and organizes them into a Frequency/System Organ Class table that does not exist in the source files.
- **Regulatory Harmonisation:** The writer successfully contrasted the Schedule 4 (Australia) and Schedule III (USA) statuses, providing clear value-add for a global clinical audience.

## Differences from Source PIs

- **Excipient Comparison:** The editorial explicitly contrasts the use of arachis (peanut) oil in the Australian product versus sesame oil in the US product, which is a critical clinical detail for allergy management not highlighted as a comparison in either source.
- **Merged Dosing:** Section 3 combines the specific weekly ranges from the FDA (50–100 mg) with the fixed doses from the TGA (100 mg), creating a more comprehensive reference for clinicians.
- **Omission of Instructional Fluff:** The editorial removes administrative details (e.g., "vials should be inspected visually for particulate matter") to focus on clinical data.
- **ATC Code Note:** The editorial notes the absence of the ATC code, showing an independent attempt to verify data outside the provided PDFs.

## Recommendation

**PASS**

The editorial is a high-quality synthesis. While there are instances of verbatim technical phrases, they generally involve clinical warnings or laboratory interactions where precise wording is required for regulatory and safety accuracy. The overall structure, the comparative excipient analysis, and the harmonized adverse effects table demonstrate significant original effort and professional "value-add" beyond simple copying. No revision is required.
