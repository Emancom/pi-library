# Editorial Product Information: Nitazoxanide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian (TGA), European (EMA), Canadian (Health Canada)) was not provided in the source material; therefore, clinical recommendations and regulatory statuses from those regions are "Not specified in available sources."
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nitazoxanide (a synthetic antiprotozoal).
- **Chemical Class:** Pyruvate:ferredoxin oxidoreductase (PFOR) enzyme inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** Nitazoxanide Tablets (e5 Pharma) [US]; Alinia (reference product mentioned in text) [US].
- **ATC Code:** P01AX11.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Tablet:** 500 mg (round, yellow, film-coated, debossed with 'ZNN') [US].
    - **Oral Suspension:** 100 mg/5 mL (mentioned as available for paediatric use) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** e5 Pharma, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Diarrhea caused by *Giardia lamblia*:** Indicated for patients 12 years of age and older [US].
- **Diarrhea caused by *Cryptosporidium parvum*:** Indicated for patients 12 years of age and older [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Nitazoxanide has not been shown to be effective for the treatment of diarrhea caused by *C. parvum* in HIV-infected or immunodeficient patients [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents $\ge$ 12 years):** 
    - 500 mg (one tablet) administered orally every 12 hours for 3 days [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Must be taken with food [US].
- **Paediatric Dosing ($\le$ 11 years):** 
    - Nitazoxanide 500 mg tablets should **not** be administered to children 11 years of age or younger because the tablet strength exceeds the recommended paediatric dose [US]. These patients should be treated with the oral suspension.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known prior hypersensitivity to nitazoxanide or any other ingredient in the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Immunocompromised Patients:** Nitazoxanide has not been studied for *G. lamblia* in HIV-infected/immunodeficient patients and has shown no superiority to placebo for *C. parvum* in these populations [US].
- **General Caution:** The pharmacokinetics of nitazoxanide have not been studied in patients with compromised renal or hepatic function [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reaction rates were evaluated in 2177 HIV-uninfected subjects (aged 12 months and older).

| Frequency | Adverse Event [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common ($\ge$ 2%)** | Abdominal pain, headache, chromaturia (discoloration of urine), nausea |
| **Postmarketing/Spontaneous** | Diarrhea, gastroesophageal reflux disease, dizziness, dyspnea, rash, urticaria |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** None specifically highlighted as "serious" in the clinical trial data provided; however, hypersensitivity is a contraindication.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Protein Binding:** The active metabolite, tizoxanide, is highly protein-bound (>99.9%). Concurrent administration with other highly plasma protein-bound drugs with narrow therapeutic indices (e.g., **warfarin**) may lead to competition for binding sites. 
- **Management:** Monitor for adverse reactions when co-administering with highly protein-bound drugs [US].
- **Cytochrome P450:** In vitro studies indicate tizoxanide does not significantly inhibit CYP450 enzymes [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly letter-coded, but follows "Risk Summary" format. 
    - **Data:** There are no adequate data in pregnant women. Animal studies (rats and rabbits) at 30 and 2 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) respectively showed no evidence of teratogenicity or fetotoxicity [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data are available regarding the presence of nitazoxanide in human milk or its effects on the infant or milk production. Consider the clinical need for the drug alongside the potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility at doses approximately 20 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Safety and efficacy established for children $\ge$ 1 year. 
    - Tablets (500 mg) are only for $\ge$ 12 years [US].
    - Safety and efficacy in infants < 1 year have not been studied [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects $\ge$ 65 years. Caution is advised due to the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function in this population [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics have not been studied in these populations [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Antiprotozoal activity is believed to be due to interference with the pyruvate:ferredoxin oxidoreductase (PFOR) enzyme-dependent electron transfer reaction, which is essential to anaerobic energy metabolism. This inhibits the growth of *C. parvum* sporozoites/oocysts and *G. lamblia* trophozoites [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Following oral administration, the parent drug is not detected in plasma. It is rapidly hydrolyzed to the active metabolite, tizoxanide [US].
    - **Food Effect:** Bioavailability increases significantly with food (AUC increases ~two-fold, $C_{max}$ increases ~50%) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Hydrolysis to tizoxanide followed by glucuronidation [US].
    - **Elimination:** Tizoxanide is excreted in urine, bile, and feces. Approximately 2/3 is excreted in feces and 1/3 in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Giardia lamblia (Adults/Adolescents):** Two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials showed clinical response rates of 85% to 100% following a 3-day course of nitazoxanide tablets compared to 30% to 44% for placebo [US].
- **Cryptosporidium parvum (Adults/Adolescents):** A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial showed a 96% clinical response rate with nitazoxanide tablets versus 41% for placebo [US].
- **Paediatrics:** Trials in children (1–11 years) using oral suspension showed clinical response rates (85% to 88%) comparable to those seen in adults and superior to placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** HDPE bottles of 6 tablets [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only medicine (Rx only) [US].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
