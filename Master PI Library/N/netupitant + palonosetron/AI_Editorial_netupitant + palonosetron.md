# Editorial Product Information: netupitant + palonosetron

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **netupitant + palonosetron**
- Chemical class: Not specified in available sources for netupitant; palonosetron is described as a 5-HT3 receptor antagonist class agent.
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Prevention of acute and delayed nausea and vomiting associated with initial and repeat courses of highly emetogenic cancer chemotherapy.
- Prevention of acute and delayed nausea and vomiting associated with initial and repeat courses of moderately emetogenic cancer chemotherapy.
- Note: Dexamethasone is recommended concomitantly with netupitant + palonosetron for these indications.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- One oral dose containing 300 mg netupitant and 500 microgram palonosetron administered approximately one hour prior to the start of each chemotherapy cycle.

### Dosing by indication
- Same single-dose regimen is used for prevention of both acute and delayed chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting for moderately and highly emetogenic chemotherapy.

### Administration instructions
- Administer orally; swallow the capsule/tablet intact (specific physical descriptions omitted).
- Can be taken with or without food.
- Concomitant dexamethasone is recommended. When dexamethasone is co-administered, the oral dexamethasone dose should be reduced by approximately 50% because netupitant is a moderate CYP3A4 inhibitor and increases dexamethasone exposure.

### Titration schedules
- Not applicable.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to palonosetron or netupitant or to any excipient in the product.
- **Contraindicated during pregnancy.**
- Note on excipients: product contains sorbitol and sucrose and may contain trace lecithin derived from soya. Patients with hereditary fructose intolerance, glucose‑galactose malabsorption, or sucrose‑isomaltase insufficiency should not take this product. Patients with known hypersensitivity to peanut or soya should be monitored for allergic reactions.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity: Reactions to palonosetron may occur in patients who have had hypersensitivity to other selective 5-HT3 receptor antagonists.
- Use only to prevent nausea/vomiting associated with chemotherapy administration; should not be used to prevent/treat nausea and vomiting outside the context of chemotherapy administration days.
- Constipation: Palonosetron may increase large bowel transit time; monitor patients with a history of constipation or signs of subacute intestinal obstruction.
- QT prolongation: A thorough ECG study showed no clinically important ECG effects with the combination at studied doses; nevertheless, caution is advised when co‑administered with other QT‑prolonging agents or in patients with risk factors for QT prolongation (personal/family history, electrolyte abnormalities, congestive heart failure, bradyarrhythmias, conduction disturbances, or concurrent use of anti‑arrhythmics). Correct hypokalaemia and hypomagnesemia prior to administration.
- Serotonin syndrome: Reports have occurred with 5-HT3 antagonists alone or in combination with other serotonergic drugs (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs). Monitor for signs and symptoms of serotonin syndrome if combined with serotonergic agents.
- CYP3A4 interactions: Netupitant is a moderate inhibitor of CYP3A4 with a long half-life; systemic exposure of CYP3A4 substrates (including some chemotherapies and dexamethasone) may increase when given with or after netupitant—monitor for toxicity.
- P-glycoprotein and transporter interactions: Netupitant is an inhibitor of P-gp and BCRP in vitro; metabolite M2 is a P-gp substrate. Use caution with P-gp substrates (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran, colchicine) and with oral substrates of UGT2B7 (e.g., zidovudine, valproic acid, morphine).
- Hepatic impairment: Netupitant exposure increases with hepatic impairment; use with caution in severe hepatic impairment (Child–Pugh score ≥ 9). No dosage adjustment for mild‑to‑moderate hepatic impairment (Child–Pugh 5–8).
- Renal impairment: No dose adjustment necessary in mild to moderate renal impairment. Use in severe renal impairment or end‑stage renal disease should be avoided.
- Women of childbearing potential: Women must not be pregnant at time of treatment; perform pregnancy test in pre‑menopausal women prior to treatment. Effective contraception must be used during therapy and for one month after the last dose.
- Lactation: Unknown whether components are excreted in human milk; breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment and for one month after the last dose.
- Elderly: No dosage adjustment necessary, but elderly subjects showed modestly higher exposures.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor ECG and electrolytes in patients at risk for QT prolongation.
- Monitor for signs and symptoms of increased exposure to concomitant CYP3A4‑metabolised chemotherapies (e.g., increased toxicity) and to dexamethasone (reduce dose by ~50%).
- Monitor for constipation and intestinal obstruction signs.
- Monitor for serotonin syndrome when combined with serotonergic agents.
- Consider monitoring plasma concentrations and clinical status when co‑administering with narrow therapeutic index CYP3A4 or P‑gp substrates.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Netupitant:
  - Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4; is a substrate and a moderate inhibitor of CYP3A4 at the 300 mg dose. Half‑life approximately 88 hours; CYP3A4 inhibition may persist for over 4 days.
  - Co‑administration with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) increases netupitant exposure (ketoconazole increased netupitant AUC by ~140% and Cmax by ~25% in a study). Palonosetron AUC/Cmax increased modestly (~10–15%) with ketoconazole.
  - Co‑administration with strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampicin) decreases netupitant exposure markedly (rifampicin reduced netupitant Cmax by ~62% and AUC by ~82%) and reduced palonosetron exposure modestly — concomitant strong inducers can reduce efficacy.
  - Netupitant increases exposure of CYP3A4 substrates (examples below).
  - Netupitant is an in vitro inhibitor of P‑gp and BCRP; metabolite M2 is a P‑gp substrate. Use caution with P‑gp substrates (digoxin, dabigatran, colchicine).
  - In vitro inhibition of transporters BSEP, MRP2 and uptake transporters (OATP1B1/1B3, OAT1/3, OCT1/2) by netupitant is unlikely at the clinical dose.

- Palonosetron:
  - Eliminated by renal excretion and metabolic pathways mediated by multiple CYP enzymes. In vitro studies indicate palonosetron is not an inhibitor of CYP1A2, CYP2A6, CYP2B6, CYP2C9, CYP2D6, CYP2E1, or CYP3A4/5 (CYP2C19 not investigated) and does not induce CYP1A2, CYP2D6, or CYP3A4/5; therefore clinically significant CYP‑mediated interactions from palonosetron are unlikely.

### Noted clinically significant interactions and management
- Dexamethasone: Netupitant increases dexamethasone exposure (AUC increased ~1.7–2.4‑fold during a 4‑day dexamethasone regimen). Reduce dexamethasone dose by approximately 50% when co‑administered.
- Chemotherapeutic agents metabolised by CYP3A4: Exposure increases have been observed:
  - Docetaxel exposure increased by ~37% when co‑administered.
  - Etoposide exposure increased by ~21%.
  - No consistent effect observed with cyclophosphamide.
  - Monitor for increased chemotherapy‑related adverse reactions and consider dose adjustments per clinical judgement.
- Benzodiazepines and sedatives metabolised by CYP3A4: Midazolam exposure increased ~2.4‑fold; erythromycin increased ~1.3‑fold. Consider dose adjustments and monitor for enhanced sedative effects for agents like midazolam, alprazolam, triazolam.
- Oral contraceptives: Co‑administration with a single dose of ethinylestradiol/levonorgestrel increased levonorgestrel AUC by ~1.4‑fold without significant ethinylestradiol change; clinical effect on hormonal contraceptive efficacy is unlikely.
- Strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampicin): May markedly reduce netupitant and palonosetron exposure and decrease efficacy — avoid concomitant use where possible.
- Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole): Increase netupitant exposure; exercise caution when co‑administered.
- Serotonergic agents: Concomitant use with SSRIs, SNRIs, or other serotonergic drugs may increase risk of serotonin syndrome; monitor patients.
- UGT2B7 substrates: In vitro data suggest caution when co‑administering netupitant with UGT2B7 substrates (e.g., zidovudine, valproic acid, morphine).

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Contraindicated in pregnancy.**
- Pregnancy testing: Pre‑menopausal women should have a pregnancy test prior to treatment.
- Animal data summarized in sources indicate developmental and reproductive findings with palonosetron and netupitant at certain doses; animal studies are insufficient to determine combination reproductive toxicity fully. (Details reported in non‑clinical toxicology; see source PI for full descriptions.)

### Breastfeeding
- It is not known whether netupitant or palonosetron are excreted in human milk. A risk to the infant cannot be excluded. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment and for one month after the last dose.

### Fertility
- Palonosetron and netupitant non‑clinical fertility data:
  - Palonosetron: High oral doses in rats showed no effect on fertility at exposures up to >30‑fold human AUC; testicular degeneration observed at high dose in longer toxicity studies.
  - Netupitant: Daily oral administration up to three times the human AUC in rats showed no effects on fertility. (Non‑clinical details provided in source PI.)

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and efficacy in children have not been established. No data are available.

### Elderly use
- No dose adjustment is necessary for elderly patients. Population PK and clinical data did not reveal relevant differences in safety or efficacy; elderly healthy subjects showed modestly higher netupitant and palonosetron exposures (mean increases described in PK section).

### Renal impairment
- Mild to moderate renal impairment: No dosage adjustment necessary.
- Severe renal impairment or end‑stage renal disease: Use should be avoided.
- Palonosetron exposure increased approximately 28% in severe renal impairment compared to healthy subjects; renal excretion of netupitant is negligible and netupitant PK has not been studied in renal impairment.

### Hepatic impairment
- Mild or moderate hepatic impairment (Child–Pugh 5–8): No dosage adjustment necessary.
- Severe hepatic impairment (Child–Pugh ≥ 9): Limited data; netupitant exposure is substantially increased in moderate and severe impairment and, because no dose adjustment is possible with the fixed‑dose combination, use with caution in severe hepatic impairment.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Palonosetron: referenced as a 5-HT3 receptor antagonist.
- Netupitant: Not specified in available sources as to mechanism of action.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Netupitant:
  - Elimination primarily by hepatic metabolism mediated by CYP3A4; renal excretion negligible.
  - Netupitant is a substrate and moderate inhibitor of CYP3A4. Reported half‑life about 88 hours; inhibitory effects on CYP3A4 can persist for over 4 days.
  - Hepatic impairment increases netupitant exposure: reported relative increases in Cmax and AUC in mild and moderate impairment (examples: mild increases ~11–28% across parameters; moderate increases ~70–143% across parameters — individual variability noted).
  - Elderly healthy subjects (>65 years) had mean netupitant AUC0–∞ and Cmax increases of approximately 25% and 36%, respectively, compared with younger adults.
- Palonosetron:
  - Eliminated via renal excretion and multiple metabolic pathways (mediated by multiple CYP enzymes).
  - Palonosetron is not an inhibitor of several major CYP enzymes in vitro (CYP1A2, CYP2A6, CYP2B6, CYP2C9, CYP2D6, CYP2E1, CYP3A4/5).
  - Total systemic exposure increased by ~28% in severe renal impairment relative to healthy subjects.
  - Elderly healthy subjects had mean palonosetron AUC0–∞ and Cmax increases of approximately 37% and 10%, respectively, compared with younger adults.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Not specified in available sources beyond palonosetron being a 5‑HT3 antagonist and the ECG study noting no clinically important effects on cardiac repolarisation for the combination at studied doses.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Safety dataset summary:
  - Total of 1,169 cancer patients evaluated for safety with the combination; 782 patients exposed for at least 4 cycles and 321 for at least 6 cycles, up to a maximum of 12 chemo cycles.
  - All patients received a single oral dose of the combination approximately one hour prior to each chemotherapy cycle; dexamethasone was co‑administered in the studies referenced.
  - Investigator‑assessed drug‑related adverse reactions occurred in approximately 13% of patients; 3.7% discontinued due to adverse reactions.
