# Editorial Product Information: Norfloxacin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory data into a structured format for quick reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Norfloxacin (a synthetic fluoroquinolone antibacterial).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Norfloxacin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J01MA06.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 400 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Urinary Tract Infections (UTI):** Treatment of complicated and uncomplicated infections in adults caused by susceptible organisms [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Infections:** Treatment of adults with shigellosis and traveller’s diarrhoea [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):**
    - **Urinary Tract Infection:** 400 mg twice daily for 7 to 10 days. For uncomplicated lower UTI, 400 mg twice daily for 3 days may be used, though 7 days provides higher eradication rates [Australian].
    - **Gastrointestinal Infection:** 400 mg twice daily for 5 days [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** 800 mg per day [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets should be taken with a glass of water, either 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal [Australian].
    - Patients must maintain adequate hydration.
    - **Timing with Cations:** Do not take within 2 hours of multivitamins, iron, zinc, antacids (magnesium/aluminium), sucralfate, or didanosine [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to norfloxacin, any component of the formulation (including soya beans), or any chemically related quinolone antibacterials [Australian].
- **Paediatrics:** Use is contraindicated in children and adolescents under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Use is contraindicated in pregnant women [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [Australian]:** **Serious Disabling and Potentially Irreversible Adverse Reactions.** Fluoroquinolones are associated with disabling and persistent reactions involving the musculoskeletal (tendonitis, rupture), nervous (neuropathy), and psychiatric systems. Use should be reserved for infections where no alternative exists.
- **Tendon Effects:** Increased risk of inflammation and rupture, particularly in the elderly, those with renal impairment, solid organ transplant recipients, or those on concomitant corticosteroids. Discontinue at the first sign of pain or inflammation [Australian].
- **Aortic Aneurysm and Dissection:** Increased risk reported, especially in the elderly. Use only after careful benefit-risk assessment in patients with pre-existing risk factors (e.g., Marfan syndrome, hypertension, atherosclerosis) [Australian].
- **Cardiac Disorders:** Potential for QT interval prolongation. Use with caution in patients with bradycardia, hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, or those taking Class IA or III antiarrhythmics [Australian].
- **Nervous System Disorders:** Use with caution in patients with a history of seizures or Myasthenia Gravis (may exacerbate muscle weakness). Sensory/sensorimotor polyneuropathy may occur [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Reactions:** May occur after the first dose, including toxic psychosis, suicidal ideation, hallucinations, and confusion [Australian].
- **C. difficile-associated Disease:** Range from mild diarrhoea to fatal pseudomembranous colitis. Discontinue if suspected [Australian].
- **Dysglycaemia:** Risk of hypoglycaemia (including coma) or hyperglycaemia, particularly in diabetic patients [Australian].
- **Photosensitivity:** Patients should avoid excessive sunlight [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (1–10%):** Nausea (2.8%), headache (2.7%), dizziness (1.8%), cholestatic hepatitis, rhabdomyolysis [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Fatigue, rash, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, somnolence, depression, insomnia, constipation, flatulence, heartburn, leucopenia, eosinophilia, elevation of ALT/AST [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Tendon rupture, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, anaphylaxis, pseudomembranous colitis, pancreatitis, haemolytic anaemia (especially in G6PD deficiency), and interstitial nephritis [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Tendon rupture, aortic dissection, hypoglycaemic coma, peripheral neuropathy, and Torsades de pointes [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP1A2 Inhibition:** Norfloxacin inhibits CYP1A2. Concomitant use increases levels of **theophylline**, **caffeine**, **clozapine**, **ropinirole**, and **tizanidine**. Monitor levels/toxicity closely [Australian].
- **Cations/Antacids:** Absorption is significantly reduced by magnesium, aluminium, calcium, iron, and zinc. Observe a 2-hour dosing gap [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** May enhance anticoagulant effects; monitor prothrombin time (INR) [Australian].
- **NSAIDs:** Concomitant use may increase the risk of CNS stimulation and seizures [Australian].
- **Nitrofurantoin:** May antagonise the antibacterial effect of norfloxacin in the urinary tract; combination is not recommended [Australian].
- **Cyclosporin:** Norfloxacin may increase serum cyclosporin levels [Australian].
- **Glibenclamide:** Risk of severe hypoglycaemia when co-administered [Australian].
- **Probenecid:** Reduces urinary excretion of norfloxacin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3 [Australian].** Animal studies show embryonic loss and arthropathy in immature animals. Norfloxacin should not be used in pregnant women [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Quinolones are known to pass into breast milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision should be made to discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, or wait 24–48 hours after the last dose before nursing [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were noted in mice at doses up to 500 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated under 18 years due to the risk of arthropathy in weight-bearing joints [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** More susceptible to tendon rupture and aortic dissection. Dose adjustment is required if renal function is impaired [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - CrCl > 30 mL/min/1.73 m²: Standard dosing.
    - CrCl $\le$ 30 mL/min/1.73 m²: 400 mg once daily.
    - Not recommended for anuric patients [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, but monitoring is advised due to reports of cholestatic hepatitis [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial DNA synthesis by targeting DNA gyrase (topoisomerase II), preventing ATP-dependent DNA supercoiling and promoting double-stranded DNA breakage [Australian].
- **Microbiology:** Active against aerobic Gram-negative (e.g., *E. coli, Klebsiella, Pseudomonas*) and some Gram-positive (e.g., *S. aureus, S. saprophyticus*) bacteria. Generally inactive against anaerobes [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 30–40% absorbed; peak plasma levels in ~1 hour. Food decreases absorption [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 10–15% protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Partially metabolised to six metabolites with less potency [Australian].
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal (glomerular filtration and tubular secretion). Effective half-life is 3–4 hours, increasing to 8.6–11.5 hours in severe renal impairment [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Pivotal trial details are not explicitly itemized in the source document; however, clinical data from 1,127 subjects established an overall adverse event incidence of 10.7%, with the drug being generally well-tolerated for UTIs and GI infections [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Container:** Available in HDPE bottles of 14 tablets [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].**
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [Australian].
