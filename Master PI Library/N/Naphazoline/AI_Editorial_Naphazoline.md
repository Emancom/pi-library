# Editorial Product Information: Naphazoline

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of naphazoline hydrochloride eye drops, synthesised from available official Product Information. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for regional compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Naphazoline hydrochloride
- **Chemical Class:** Imidazoline derivative; sympathomimetic alpha-adrenergic agonist
- **Brand Name(s):** Albalon [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Ophthalmic solution (eye drops), 1 mg/mL (0.1% w/v) [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical Ocular
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AbbVie Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Topical Ocular Vasoconstriction:** For the relief of redness or congestion of the eye through local vasoconstriction [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- Instill 1 or 2 drops into the affected eye(s) every three to four hours [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Nasolacrimal Occlusion:** To minimise systemic absorption, patients should be instructed to apply pressure to the tear duct immediately following administration [Australian].
- **Contamination Prevention:** Avoid touching the dropper tip to the eyelids, surrounding ocular areas, or any other surface to prevent contamination of the solution.
- **Infection Control:** Use of the bottle by more than one person may lead to the spread of infection [Australian].

### Duration of Treatment
- Treatment should not exceed 14 consecutive days unless directed by a medical professional [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to naphazoline hydrochloride or any excipient in the formulation.
- **Glaucoma:** Specifically contraindicated in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma or an anatomically narrow angle [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Serious Eye Conditions:** This preparation should not be used in the presence of glaucoma (other than narrow-angle, which is contraindicated) or other serious ocular conditions without medical supervision [Australian].
- **Rebound Hyperemia:** Prolonged or frequent use, particularly in the presence of an inflamed eye, may lead to increased systemic absorption and potentially result in rebound congestion or systemic effects [Australian].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Cardiovascular and Endocrine Disease:** Use with caution in patients with hypertension, cardiac disease, hyperthyroidism, or hyperglycaemia (diabetes) [Australian].
- **Urinary Retention:** Administer with caution to patients with prostatic enlargement, as naphazoline may exacerbate difficulties in micturition [Australian].
- **Symptom Persistence:** If ocular conditions do not respond within 48 hours or if symptoms worsen/recur, treatment should be discontinued and the patient re-evaluated by a physician [Australian].

### Contact Lens Use
- **Benzalkonium Chloride:** Formulations containing benzalkonium chloride may discolour soft (hydrophilic) contact lenses. Lenses should be removed prior to instillation, with a minimum 15-minute wait time before reinsertion [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are not defined in available sources due to voluntary post-marketing reporting.

### Ocular Effects
- Pupillary dilation (mydriasis)
- Increased intraocular pressure
- Eye irritation and pain
- Eye oedema
- Ocular hyperemia
- Transient blurred vision upon instillation

### Systemic Effects (due to absorption)
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, cardiac irregularities (arrhythmias).
- **Central Nervous System:** Drowsiness, CNS depression.
- **Metabolic:** Hyperglycaemia.
- **Immune System:** Hypersensitivity reactions, including allergic dermatitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use or use within 14 days of an MAOI may precipitate a severe hypertensive crisis [Australian].
- **Antidepressants:** The pressor effect of naphazoline may be potentiated by tricyclic antidepressants or maprotiline [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Concurrent use with methyldopa may increase the pressor response of naphazoline [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category: Not formally assigned in provided text]. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if naphazoline causes fetal harm or affects reproductive capacity. Use only if clearly needed [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known whether naphazoline is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children under 12 years of age. In infants and young children, absorption may lead to severe CNS depression, potentially resulting in coma and a marked reduction in body temperature [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data regarding use in the elderly are not available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Naphazoline is an imidazoline-class sympathomimetic. It acts directly upon alpha-adrenergic receptors in the vascular smooth muscle of the conjunctiva, resulting in vasoconstriction [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Characterised by a relatively long duration of action compared to other topical vasoconstrictors [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** No specific pharmacokinetic data (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination) are available in the provided sources. Systemic absorption is known to occur, particularly if the conjunctiva is inflamed or if nasolacrimal occlusion is not performed.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data were provided in the source documents.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Light Protection:** Protect from light [Australian].
- **Stability:** Do not use if the solution becomes cloudy or changes colour.
- **Shelf Life:** 3 years; discard any unused contents 4 weeks after first opening the bottle [Australian].
