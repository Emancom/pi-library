# Editorial Product Information: Naproxen Sodium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Naproxen sodium; a propionic acid derivative (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug, NSAID) [Australian].
- **Brand Names:** ANAPROX 550 [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M01AE02 (Naproxen).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Australian:** Film-coated tablet containing 550 mg of naproxen sodium (equivalent to 500 mg naproxen). Tablets are oblong, dark blue, engraved "NPS 550", and break-marked on both faces.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Atnahs Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Migraine Attacks:** Management of acute migraine pain and associated symptoms [Australian].
- **Rheumatological Conditions:** Treatment of rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, and ankylosing spondylitis [Australian].
- **Acute Gout:** Management of acute gouty episodes [Australian].
- **Pain Management:** Relief of acute and/or chronic pain states featuring an inflammatory component [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
The lowest effective dose should be utilised for the shortest possible duration consistent with individual patient treatment goals [Australian].

### Specific Indications [Australian]
- **Acute Migraine Headache:** 
    - Initial dose: 825 mg at the first sign of an impending headache.
    - Supplemental dose: 275 mg to 550 mg may be administered at least one hour after the initial dose if required.
    - Maximum daily dose: 1375 mg.
- **Acute Pain States (with inflammatory component):** 
    - Initial dose: 550 mg.
    - Subsequent dosing: 275 mg every six to eight hours as needed.
    - Maximum daily dose: 1375 mg.
- **Chronic Inflammatory Conditions (RA, OA, AS):** 
    - General range: 550 mg to 1100 mg daily, administered in two divided doses.
    - Starting dose: Should not be less than 550 mg daily.
    - Maintenance: Gradually titrate up to 1100 mg daily based on patient response.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed with liquid. 
- Concomitant food intake may delay the rate of absorption but does not affect the total extent of absorption [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to naproxen/naproxen sodium.
- **Cross-Reactivity:** History of asthma, rhinitis, nasal polyps, or urticaria following the use of aspirin or other NSAIDs [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Disease:** Active or history of peptic ulceration, chronic dyspepsia, active GI bleeding, or perforation (including those related to previous NSAID therapy) [Australian].
- **Cardiac Surgery:** Management of perioperative pain in the setting of Coronary Artery Bypass Graft (CABG) surgery [Australian].
- **Organ Impairment:** Severe heart failure, severe hepatic impairment, or creatinine clearance <30 mL/min [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Third trimester of pregnancy [Australian].
- **Pediatrics:** Children under 2 years of age (safety not established) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Cardiovascular Thrombotic Events
NSAIDs may increase the risk of serious cardiovascular (CV) thrombotic events, myocardial infarction, and stroke, which can be fatal. This risk may increase with dose and duration of use. Patients with existing CV disease or CV risk factors are at higher risk [Australian].

### Gastrointestinal (GI) Effects
Serious GI toxicity, including inflammation, bleeding, ulceration, and perforation, can occur at any time without warning. Risk factors include advanced age, history of GI disease, smoking, alcoholism, and concomitant use of oral corticosteroids or anticoagulants [Australian].

### Severe Skin Reactions
NSAIDs can rarely cause life-threatening cutaneous reactions, including Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS). Treatment should be discontinued at the first appearance of a skin rash or hypersensitivity sign [Australian].

### Renal and Hepatic Effects
- **Renal:** Long-term NSAID use can result in renal papillary necrosis. Use caution in patients with impaired renal function, heart failure, or liver dysfunction, as well as those on diuretics or ACE inhibitors [Australian].
- **Hepatic:** Borderline elevations of liver tests may occur. Severe hepatic reactions (jaundice, fatal hepatitis) have been reported [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Blood pressure should be monitored closely during the initiation and throughout the course of NSAID therapy [Australian].
- Patients on long-term treatment should be regularly reviewed for efficacy and risk factors [Australian].
- Hemoglobin should be checked frequently in patients with initial values ≤10 g/dL on long-term therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (3–9%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Constipation, heartburn, abdominal pain, nausea.
- **CNS:** Headache, dizziness, drowsiness.
- **Dermatologic:** Pruritus, skin eruptions, ecchymoses.
- **Special Senses:** Tinnitus.
- **Cardiovascular:** Oedema, dyspnoea.

### Uncommon (1–3%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Dyspepsia, diarrhoea, stomatitis.
- **CNS:** Vertigo, light-headedness.
- **Cardiovascular:** Palpitations.
- **Other:** Sweating, purpura, visual/hearing disturbances, thirst.

### Rare/Serious (<1%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** GI bleeding, haematemesis, melaena, peptic ulceration, fatal hepatitis.
- **Renal:** Renal failure, interstitial nephritis, haematuria, hyperkalaemia.
- **Hematologic:** Agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia, eosinophilia, thrombocytopenia.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants/Anti-platelets (e.g., Warfarin, Aspirin):** Increased risk of serious GI bleeding and prolonged bleeding time. Use with extreme caution [Australian].
- **Probenecid:** Significantly prolongs naproxen half-life (14 to 37 hours) and increases plasma levels [Australian].
- **Methotrexate:** Reduced tubular secretion may increase methotrexate toxicity [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives (ACE inhibitors, ARBs, Beta-blockers):** Naproxen may reduce antihypertensive efficacy and increase the risk of renal impairment ("triple whammy" when combined with diuretics) [Australian].
- **Lithium:** Inhibition of renal lithium clearance, leading to increased plasma lithium levels [Australian].
- **SSRIs:** Increased risk of GI bleeding [Australian].
- **Sucralfate/Cholestyramine:** May delay the absorption of naproxen [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category C** [Australian].
- **Third Trimester:** Contraindicated; may cause premature closure of the ductus arteriosus and neonatal renal impairment [Australian].
- **First/Second Trimester:** Use only if benefits outweigh risks. From 20 weeks gestation, NSAID use may cause fetal renal dysfunction leading to oligohydramnios [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Naproxen is excreted in breast milk (~1% of plasma concentration). Use is **not recommended** in lactating mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May impair female fertility; withdrawal of treatment is recommended for women having difficulty conceiving [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 5 years of age. Contraindicated in infants under 2 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of fatal GI events. Use the lowest effective dose; unbound plasma fraction of naproxen is higher in this population [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min). Monitor creatinine clearance closely in patients with moderate impairment; consider dose reduction [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use the lowest effective dose. Increased risk of toxicity in chronic alcoholic liver disease and cirrhosis [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibition of prostaglandin synthetase (cyclooxygenase). Demonstrates anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antipyretic properties [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and complete; peak plasma levels in 1–2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution ~0.09 L/kg; >99% albumin-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Hepatic metabolism to 6-0-desmethyl naproxen.
    - **Elimination:** ~95% excreted in urine. Elimination half-life is approximately 14 hours [Australian].
