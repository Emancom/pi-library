# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official PI and should not be used as the sole basis for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport; amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic, Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for intravenous injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with an inborn error of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - A loading dose is often employed in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustment: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may manifest within minutes of intravenous administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before considering re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been reported [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
    - **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations (normal range 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of the patient populations (ESRD or metabolic errors).

### Frequency >5% (ESRD Population) [US]
| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, anorexia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |

### Serious and Other Notable Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Other:** Body odor (less frequent) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned in current PI; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. Decisions should weigh the maternal benefit against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant; discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is applicable to paediatric patients; specifically studied in premature infants and newborns with secondary deficiency [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Injection:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis.
    - **Note on Oral Levocarnitine:** High-dose oral therapy in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]), which are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors or ESRD, it promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** In healthy volunteers, a 20 mg/kg slow IV bolus followed a two-compartment model.
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical trials in ESRD patients undergoing hemodialysis demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly significantly increased pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. However, the effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources [US].
