# Editorial Product Information: Nelfinavir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for definitive prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nelfinavir mesylate (HIV-1 protease inhibitor).
- **Brand Names:** **Viracept** [US].
- **ATC Code:** J05AE04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated Tablets:** 250 mg and 625 mg (expressed as nelfinavir free base) [US].
    - **Oral Powder:** 50 mg/g (as nelfinavir free base) in 144 g multi-use bottles [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Inc / Agouron Pharmaceuticals, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated for the treatment of HIV-1 infection in combination with other antiretroviral agents [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents ≥13 years)
The recommended oral dosage is either:
- **1250 mg twice daily** (e.g., two 625 mg tablets or five 250 mg tablets BID).
- **750 mg three times daily** (e.g., three 250 mg tablets TID) [US].

### Pediatric Dosing (2 to <13 years)
Weight-based dosing is required using either the oral powder or 250 mg tablets:
- **Twice Daily:** 45 to 55 mg/kg per dose.
- **Three Times Daily:** 25 to 35 mg/kg per dose.
- **Maximum Dose:** Should not exceed the adult maximum of 2500 mg per day [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Requirement:** **Viracept** must be taken with a meal to ensure optimal absorption [US].
- **Swallowing Difficulties:** 
    - Tablets may be dissolved in a small amount of water; the resulting cloudy liquid should be consumed immediately. 
    - Oral powder can be mixed with water, milk, formula, soy milk, or dietary supplements. It should not be mixed with acidic foods or juices (e.g., orange juice, apple sauce) due to a resulting bitter taste.
    - Reconstituted mixtures must be consumed within 6 hours if refrigerated [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Patients should take a missed dose as soon as possible and return to the normal schedule. If a dose is skipped, the patient should not double the next dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
**Viracept** is contraindicated for coadministration with drugs highly dependent on CYP3A for clearance, where elevated concentrations may cause serious or life-threatening events, or drugs that significantly reduce nelfinavir exposure:
- **Alpha 1-adrenoreceptor antagonists:** Alfuzosin.
- **Antiarrhythmics:** Amiodarone, quinidine.
- **Antimycobacterials:** Rifampin.
- **Antipsychotics:** Lurasidone, pimozide.
- **Ergot Derivatives:** Dihydroergotamine, ergotamine, methylergonovine.
- **GI Motility Agents:** Cisapride.
- **Herbal Products:** St. John's wort (*Hypericum perforatum*).
- **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors:** Lovastatin, simvastatin.
- **PDE5 Inhibitors:** Sildenafil (when used for pulmonary arterial hypertension).
- **Sedative/Hypnotics:** Triazolam, oral midazolam [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Drug Interactions:** Nelfinavir is a potent CYP3A inhibitor. Initiation in patients taking drugs metabolized by CYP3A may increase plasma concentrations of those drugs, leading to severe or fatal events [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended for use in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C) [US].
- **Phenylketonuria:** The oral powder contains 11.2 mg of phenylalanine per gram (from aspartame) [US].
- **Diabetes Mellitus/Hyperglycemia:** New-onset diabetes or exacerbation of pre-existing diabetes has been reported with protease inhibitors. In some cases, diabetic ketoacidosis occurred [US].
- **Hemophilia:** Increased spontaneous bleeding (hematomas/hemarthrosis) has been reported in patients with Type A and B hemophilia [US].
- **Fat Redistribution:** Changes in body fat distribution (central obesity, "buffalo hump," peripheral wasting) have been observed with antiretroviral therapy [US].
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome:** Patients may develop an inflammatory response to indolent or residual opportunistic infections (e.g., MAC, CMV, PCP, TB) during the initial phase of treatment [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Adults and Adolescents (≥13 years)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Diarrhea (up to 20% in BID regimens) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Nausea, flatulence, rash [US].
- **Uncommon/Rare (<1%):** Abdominal pain, asthenia, fever, headache, pancreatitis, vomiting, hypersensitivity reactions (including bronchospasm and edema) [US].

### Pediatric Patients (2 to <13 years)
- **Common (≥2%):** Diarrhea, leukopenia/neutropenia, rash, anorexia, and abdominal pain [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- QTc prolongation and Torsades de Pointes (postmarketing), jaundice, metabolic acidosis, and bilirubinemia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Nelfinavir as a CYP3A Inhibitor:** Increases concentrations of drugs such as calcineurin inhibitors (cyclosporine, tacrolimus), certain statins (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin), and PDE5 inhibitors [US].
- **Nelfinavir as a Substrate:** Metabolized by CYP3A and CYP2C19.
    - **PPIs (e.g., Omeprazole):** Significantly decrease nelfinavir concentrations; concomitant use may lead to loss of virologic response [US].
    - **Anticonvulsants:** Carbamazepine, phenobarbital, and phenytoin may decrease nelfinavir levels [US].

### Management Notes
- **Warfarin:** Monitor INR closely upon initiation or cessation.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Ethinyl estradiol levels are decreased; alternative non-hormonal contraception is advised [US].
- **Colchicine:** Use is contraindicated in patients with renal or hepatic impairment who are also taking **Viracept** [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Use only if potential benefit justifies the risk]. Hepatic adverse events (including liver failure) have been reported in pregnant patients. Data from the Antiretroviral Pregnancy Registry (APR) showed a 3.9% rate of major birth defects with first-trimester exposure, compared to 2.7–4.2% in reference populations [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** HIV-infected mothers should be instructed not to breastfeed to avoid the risk of postnatal HIV transmission and potential serious adverse reactions in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no effects on mating or fertility at systemic exposures comparable to human therapeutic levels [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for children ≥2 years. Reliable dosing for children <2 years has not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects ≥65 years to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment needed for mild impairment (Child-Pugh A). **Contraindicated** in moderate to severe impairment (Child-Pugh B or C) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Nelfinavir is an HIV-1 protease inhibitor that prevents the cleavage of the *gag* and *gag-pol* polyprotein, resulting in the production of immature, non-infectious virions [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is not determined. Food increases the AUC by 2- to 5-fold [US].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>98%) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP3A and CYP2C19. One major oxidative metabolite has antiviral activity comparable to the parent drug [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 3.5 to 5 hours. Excreted primarily in feces (87%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 511 (Treatment-Naïve):** A double-blind, randomized trial comparing zidovudine/lamivudine (ZDV/3TC) alone vs. ZDV/3TC + nelfinavir (500 mg or 750 mg TID). At 48 weeks, 86% of the 750 mg TID group achieved HIV RNA <400 copies/mL [US].
- **Study 542 (BID vs TID):** An open-label trial comparing 1250 mg BID vs. 750 mg TID. Both regimens showed comparable efficacy, with roughly 60% of patients achieving HIV RNA <400 copies/mL at 48 weeks [US].
- **Pediatric Studies (556 and PACTG 337):** Demonstrated efficacy in children ≥2 years, though drug exposure was highly variable [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep container tightly closed. Dispense in the original container [US].
