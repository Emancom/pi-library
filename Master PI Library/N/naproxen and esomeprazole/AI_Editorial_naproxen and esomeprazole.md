# Editorial Product Information: Naproxen and Esomeprazole Magnesium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- At the time of compilation, official PI documents for other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian (TGA), European (EMA), Canadian (Health Canada)) were not provided for comparison.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and must not be substituted for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the local regulatory authority’s current approved documentation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Naproxen (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug [NSAID]) and Esomeprazole Magnesium (proton pump inhibitor [PPI]).
- **Chemical Class:** Naproxen is a propionic acid derivative; Esomeprazole is a substituted benzimidazole.
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in provided source (Generic synthesis).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - [US]: Delayed-release tablets containing 375 mg naproxen/20 mg esomeprazole or 500 mg naproxen/20 mg esomeprazole. The tablets consist of an enteric-coated naproxen core surrounded by an immediate-release esomeprazole magnesium layer.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** [US]: Distributed by DocRx, Inc. (manufactured by Dr. Reddy's Laboratories).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis, Osteoarthritis, and Ankylosing Spondylitis:** For the symptomatic relief of signs and symptoms in adults [US].
- **Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis (JIA):** For the relief of signs and symptoms in adolescent patients 12 years of age and older weighing at least 38 kg [US].
- **Gastric Ulcer Risk Reduction:** Specifically indicated to decrease the risk of developing naproxen-associated gastric ulcers in patients requiring NSAID therapy for the above conditions [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Not recommended for the initial treatment of acute pain due to delayed naproxen absorption. It should not be substituted with single-ingredient products of naproxen and esomeprazole [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Principle:** Use the lowest effective naproxen dose for the shortest duration necessary to meet clinical goals.
- **Adult Dosing:** One tablet twice daily (using either the 375 mg/20 mg or 500 mg/20 mg strength) [US].
- **Adolescent Dosing (12+ years):**
    - Weight 38 kg to <50 kg: 375 mg/20 mg twice daily [US].
    - Weight $\ge$50 kg: 375 mg/20 mg or 500 mg/20 mg twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole with liquid. 
    - **Do not split, chew, crush, or dissolve.**
    - Administer at least 30 minutes before meals [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Take as soon as remembered unless it is nearly time for the next dose. Do not double doses [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to naproxen, esomeprazole, substituted benzimidazoles (e.g., omeprazole), or any excipients.
- **Aspirin/NSAID Sensitivity:** History of asthma, urticaria, or allergic-type reactions following aspirin or other NSAID use [US].
- **Surgical Setting:** Use in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery [US].
- **Drug Interactions:** Concomitant use of rilpivirine-containing products [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]:** 
    - **Cardiovascular (CV) Risk:** NSAIDs increase the risk of serious, potentially fatal CV thrombotic events, including myocardial infarction and stroke. Risk may occur early in treatment and increases with duration of use.
    - **Gastrointestinal (GI) Risk:** NSAIDs cause an increased risk of serious, potentially fatal GI adverse events, including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation. The elderly and those with prior peptic ulcer disease/GI bleeding are at higher risk.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Elevations in ALT/AST occur in approximately 1% of patients. Discontinue immediately if clinical signs of liver disease develop or systemic manifestations (e.g., eosinophilia, rash) occur [US].
- **Hypertension:** NSAIDs can lead to new-onset or worsening of pre-existing hypertension; monitor blood pressure closely.
- **Renal Effects:** Long-term use may result in renal papillary necrosis. Avoid use in advanced renal disease. Monitor function in those with heart failure or hepatic impairment [US].
- **Anaphylactic Reactions:** May occur even in patients without prior exposure.
- **Skin Reactions:** Risk of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS). Discontinue at the first appearance of a skin rash [US].
- **PPI-Related Precautions:**
    - **Acute Tubulointerstitial Nephritis:** May occur at any point during PPI therapy.
    - **Clostridium difficile-associated diarrhea:** PPIs may increase the risk.
    - **Bone Fracture:** Long-term, high-dose therapy is associated with increased risk of osteoporosis-related fractures.
    - **Lupus Erythematosus:** Both cutaneous (CLE) and systemic (SLE) forms reported.
    - **Nutritional Deficiencies:** Long-term use (>3 years) may lead to Vitamin B12 malabsorption; prolonged use may cause hypomagnesemia [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>2%):**
    - [US Clinical Trials]: Gastritis (17%), Diarrhea (6%), Upper respiratory tract infection (5%), Flatulence (4%), Abdominal pain upper (4%), Constipation (4%), Headache (3%), Dizziness (3%), Peripheral edema (3%), Urinary tract infection (2%), Dysgeusia (2%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Myocardial infarction, stroke, GI bleed/perforation, renal failure, hepatotoxicity, SJS/TEN, and anaphylaxis [US].
- **PPI-Specific:** Fundic gland polyps (risk increases with use >1 year) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets:** Increased risk of bleeding. Naproxen may interfere with the antiplatelet effect of low-dose aspirin.
- **Clopidogrel:** Esomeprazole reduces the exposure to the active metabolite of clopidogrel; avoid concomitant use [US].
- **Antihypertensives:** NSAIDs may diminish the efficacy of ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and beta-blockers.
- **Digoxin:** Both components may increase digoxin levels; monitor concentrations [US].
- **Lithium:** NSAIDs reduce renal clearance, increasing lithium levels.
- **Methotrexate:** PPIs and NSAIDs may elevate and prolong methotrexate serum levels, increasing toxicity risk.
- **Antiretrovirals:** Contraindicated with rilpivirine. Esomeprazole significantly reduces levels of atazanavir and nelfinavir [US].
- **CYP2C19/3A4:** Strong inducers (e.g., St. John’s Wort, rifampin) can substantially decrease esomeprazole levels [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **First/Second Trimester:** Use only if clearly needed.
    - **$\ge$20 Weeks Gestation:** Avoid use as NSAIDs may cause fetal renal dysfunction leading to oligohydramnios and, in some cases, neonatal renal impairment [US].
    - **$\ge$30 Weeks Gestation:** Contraindicated/Avoided due to the risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Naproxen is excreted in human milk (approx. 1% of plasma concentration). Esomeprazole presence is likely but human data is limited. Benefits should be weighed against potential infant risk [US].
- **Fertility:** NSAIDs may delay or prevent ovulation; consider withdrawal in women having difficulty conceiving [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety/efficacy established only for JIA in adolescents $\ge$12 years and $\ge$38 kg. Not established for children <12 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of GI and CV complications. Use the lowest effective dose. Increased unbound fraction of naproxen noted in the elderly [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild/Moderate:** Consider dose reduction.
    - **Severe:** Avoid use [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Advanced (CrCL <30 mL/min):** Not recommended [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:**
    - **Naproxen:** Reversible inhibition of COX-1 and COX-2 enzymes, leading to decreased prostaglandin synthesis.
    - **Esomeprazole:** Irreversible inhibition of the H+/K+-ATPase (proton pump) in gastric parietal cells, suppressing acid secretion.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Naproxen bioavailability is 95%. Esomeprazole is rapidly absorbed (Tmax 0.43–1.2h). Food significantly delays naproxen Tmax (by 10h) and reduces esomeprazole AUC (by 52%) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Naproxen is metabolised via CYP2C9/1A2. Esomeprazole is extensively metabolised by CYP2C19 and CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Naproxen half-life is ~15h; Esomeprazole half-life is ~1–1.5h. Both primarily excreted via urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Gastric Ulcer Prevention:** Two pivotal 6-month studies (Study 1 and 2) compared the combination 500/20 mg BID to enteric-coated naproxen 500 mg BID. The combination significantly reduced the cumulative incidence of gastric ulcers (4.1% and 7.1% vs. 23.1% and 24.3% respectively) [US].
- **Osteoarthritis:** Two 12-week studies confirmed efficacy in reducing pain and improving physical function compared to placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Store in the original container. Keep bottle tightly closed to protect from moisture [US].
