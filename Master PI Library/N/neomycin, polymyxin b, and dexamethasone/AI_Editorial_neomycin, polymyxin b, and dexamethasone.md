# Editorial Product Information: Neomycin, Polymyxin B Sulfates, and Dexamethasone

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for a topical ophthalmic combination of neomycin, polymyxin B, and dexamethasone. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredients:** 
    *   Neomycin Sulfate (Aminoglycoside antibiotic)
    *   Polymyxin B Sulfate (Cyclic lipopeptide antibiotic)
    *   Dexamethasone (Corticosteroid)
*   **Brand Names:** Maxitrol (Original reference brand), distributed/relabelled by NuCare Pharmaceuticals and Bausch + Lomb [US].
*   **ATC Code:** S01CA01
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   **Ophthalmic Ointment:** Each gram contains neomycin sulfate (equivalent to 3.5 mg neomycin base), polymyxin B sulfate (10,000 units), and dexamethasone (0.1% or 1 mg) [US].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** NuCare Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (Relabeler); Bausch + Lomb (Manufacturer) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Steroid-responsive inflammatory ocular conditions:** For use when a corticosteroid is indicated and where a concomitant bacterial infection or a high risk of such infection exists.
*   **Inflammatory conditions of the ocular segment:** Including the palpebral and bulbar conjunctiva, cornea, and anterior segment of the globe.
*   **Chronic anterior uveitis.**
*   **Corneal injury:** Resulting from chemical, radiation, or thermal burns, or penetration of foreign bodies.
*   **Infective Conjunctivitides:** Use is accepted to reduce oedema and inflammation when the inherent risks of steroid use are deemed acceptable.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Ophthalmic Ointment:** Apply a small amount (approximately 1/2 inch) into the conjunctival sac(s) up to three or four times daily [US].

### Administration Instructions
1.  Tilt the head back.
2.  Gently pull down the lower eyelid to create a small pocket.
3.  Place the ointment into the pocket; ensure the tube tip does not touch the eye or any other surface to maintain sterility.
4.  Look downward before closing the eye.

### Clinical Management
*   **Prescription Limitations:** Not more than 8 g should be prescribed for the initial order. Renewal of the prescription should only occur after clinical examination with magnification (e.g., slit lamp biomicroscopy) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Viral Infections:** Epithelial herpes simplex keratitis (dendritic keratitis), vaccinia, varicella, and most other viral diseases of the cornea and conjunctiva [US].
*   **Mycobacterial Infections:** Mycobacterial infection of the eye [US].
*   **Fungal Diseases:** Fungal infections of ocular structures [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known or suspected hypersensitivity to any component of the formulation. Hypersensitivity to the antibiotic components occurs at a higher rate than other ingredients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Route Limitation:** Not for injection [US].
*   **Ocular Hypertension/Glaucoma:** Prolonged use may result in glaucoma, damage to the optic nerve, and defects in visual acuity and fields of vision. Intraocular pressure (IOP) must be monitored routinely if used for 10 days or longer [US].
*   **Cataract Formation:** Prolonged use may lead to posterior subcapsular cataract formation.
*   **Delayed Healing:** Steroids may delay wound healing and increase the incidence of bleb formation following cataract surgery [US].
*   **Secondary Infection:** Prolonged use may suppress the host immune response, increasing the risk of secondary fungal or bacterial ocular infections. In acute purulent conditions, steroids may mask or enhance existing infection [US].
*   **Corneal Perforation:** In diseases causing thinning of the cornea or sclera, topical steroids have been known to cause perforation [US].
*   **Neomycin Sensitization:** Cutaneous sensitization to neomycin may occur. Cross-hypersensitivity to other aminoglycosides should be considered. If severe hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis, rash, etc.) occurs, discontinue use immediately [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions may be attributed to the steroid component, the anti-infective component, or the combination.

| Organ System | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Ocular (Steroid-related)** | Elevation of IOP, glaucoma, optic nerve damage, posterior subcapsular cataract formation, delayed wound healing, keratitis, conjunctivitis, corneal ulcers, conjunctival hyperaemia. |
| **Ocular (Anti-infective)** | Allergic sensitization/hypersensitivity reactions. |
| **Infection** | Secondary bacterial infection; fungal infections of the cornea (suspect in persistent corneal ulceration). |
| **Post-marketing (Serious)** | Ulcerative keratitis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, headache [US]. |

*   **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Management:** While specific drug-drug interactions are not listed in the provided PI, the document notes that the combination formulation is used to ensure compatibility and patient compliance when both an antimicrobial and a steroid are required [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **US Category:** Not explicitly letter-coded in text, but notes dexamethasone is teratogenic in mice and rabbits. 
*   **Clinical Summary:** Prolonged or repeated corticoid use during pregnancy is linked to increased risk of intra-uterine growth retardation. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus. Infants born to mothers receiving substantial doses should be monitored for hypoadrenalism [US].

### Breastfeeding
*   **Clinical Summary:** Systemic corticosteroids appear in human milk and can suppress growth. It is unknown if topical ophthalmic administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable quantities in milk. Exercise caution when administering to a nursing woman [US].

### Fertility
*   **Data:** Polymyxin B has been reported to impair equine sperm motility; effects on human fertility are unknown. No long-term animal studies for carcinogenesis or mutagenesis have been conducted with polymyxin B [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients have not been established [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** No overall clinical differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between elderly and younger adult patients [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** 
    *   **Dexamethasone:** Suppresses the inflammatory response to various agents and likely delays healing.
    *   **Anti-infectives (Neomycin & Polymyxin B):** Provide activity against common bacterial eye pathogens including *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Escherichia coli*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Klebsiella/Enterobacter* species, *Neisseria* species, and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*.
*   **Microbiology Notes:** This product does **not** provide adequate coverage against *Serratia marcescens* or *Streptococci* (including *Streptococcus pneumoniae*) [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Storage Temperature:** Store between 15°C and 25°C (59°F–77°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Keep tube tightly closed when not in use. Avoid touching the tube tip to any surface to prevent contamination. Keep out of reach of children.
