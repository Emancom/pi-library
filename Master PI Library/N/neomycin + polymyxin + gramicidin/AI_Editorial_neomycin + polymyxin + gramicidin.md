# Editorial Product Information: neomycin + polymyxin + gramicidin

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **neomycin + polymyxin + gramicidin**
- Chemical class:
  - Neomycin: aminoglycoside antibiotic
  - Polymyxin B: polymyxin antibiotic (cationic polypeptide)
  - Gramicidin (gramicidin D): peptide antibiotic (gramicidin A/B/C mixture)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Ophthalmic (topical)

## 2. Regulatory History
- [US] Initial abbreviated new drug application (ANDA) marketing start date: 01/31/1996.
- [US] Product marketing start date recorded: 06/29/2016.
- Date of last revision/update of product information: August 2022; additional relabel revision noted 02/2026. [US]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Topical treatment of superficial bacterial infections of the external eye and adnexa caused by susceptible organisms, including:
  - Conjunctivitis
  - Keratitis and keratoconjunctivitis
  - Blepharitis and blepharoconjunctivitis
- Indications are stated generically; jurisdiction‑specific variations are not specified in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### Strengths (per mL)
- Neomycin sulfate: equivalent to 1.75 mg neomycin base per mL
- Polymyxin B sulfate: equivalent to 10,000 polymyxin B units per mL
- Gramicidin: 0.025 mg per mL
- Preservative: thimerosal 0.001% (0.01 mg/mL)
- Inactives: sodium chloride, alcohol (0.5%), Poloxamer 188, propylene glycol, purified water; pH adjusted as needed.

### General dosing (adults)
- Instill one to two drops into the affected eye(s) every 4 hours for 7 to 10 days.
- For severe infections, dosage may be increased to up to two drops every hour.

### Dosing by indication
- No separate, indication-specific dosing schedules beyond the general and severe-infection guidance are specified.

### Administration instructions
- Instruct patients to avoid allowing the dispenser tip to touch the eye, eyelids, fingers or any surface.
- Do not inject into the eye (see Warnings).
- Do not share the product with others.
- If symptoms persist, worsen, or if a rash/allergic reaction develops, discontinue use and consult a physician.

### Titration and missed dose
- Titration schedules: Not specified in available sources.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to neomycin, polymyxin B, gramicidin, thimerosal, or any component of the formulation.
- No other contraindications are specified in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- **Not for injection into the eye.** This product must never be introduced directly into the anterior chamber or administered by subconjunctival injection.

### General warnings and precautions
- Topical antibiotics, particularly neomycin, may cause sensitization. Sensitization manifestations may include itching, reddening and edema of the conjunctiva and eyelid; a sensitization reaction may present as failure to heal.
- Prolonged use may result in overgrowth of nonsusceptible organisms, including fungi; if superinfection occurs, initiate appropriate treatment.
- Bacterial resistance to the combination may develop with prolonged or repeated use.
- Contamination of multi‑dose ocular products by the patient has been associated with bacterial keratitis, particularly in patients with corneal disease or epithelial disruption.
- Allergic cross‑reactions among aminoglycoside antibiotics can occur; prior sensitization to related agents may preclude future use of some aminoglycosides.

### Monitoring requirements
- During prolonged therapy, periodically examine the patient for signs of sensitization and for evidence of superinfection.
- If purulent discharge, increased inflammation, or increased pain occur, discontinue therapy and re-evaluate.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid use in patients with known hypersensitivity to any component.
- After a sensitization reaction, avoid further applications of products containing the same ingredients.
- Patients should be instructed on proper handling to minimize contamination risk.

## 7. Adverse Effects

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamic and immunologic interactions
- Allergic cross‑reactivity: Sensitization to neomycin may cross-react with other aminoglycoside antibiotics and could prevent use of kanamycin, paromomycin, streptomycin, and possibly gentamicin for future infections.

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- No specific concomitant drug interactions or interaction management strategies are described in the available sources beyond the potential for cross‑sensitivity among aminoglycosides.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Adequate animal reproductive studies have not been conducted with neomycin, polymyxin B, or gramicidin.
- It is not known whether this product can cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman or can affect reproductive capacity.
- Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.

### Pregnancy classification systems
- No jurisdiction‑specific pregnancy category is provided in the available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- It is not known whether neomycin, polymyxin B, or gramicidin are excreted in human milk.
- Because many drugs are excreted in human milk, exercise caution when administering to nursing women.

### Fertility
- Neomycin: in vitro treatment of cultured lymphocytes increased chromosome aberrations at the highest concentration tested; effects on human carcinogenesis and mutagenesis are unknown.
- Polymyxin B: reported to impair motility of equine sperm; effects on human fertility are unknown.
- Long-term carcinogenicity/mutagenicity studies: Not conducted for polymyxin B or gramicidin. Overall effects on human fertility and carcinogenesis are unknown.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Neomycin: Aminoglycoside that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to ribosomal RNA, causing misreading of mRNA and bactericidal activity against many gram‑positive and gram‑negative organisms.
- Polymyxin B: Disrupts gram‑negative bacterial cell membranes by interacting with membrane phospholipids, increasing membrane permeability and producing bactericidal effects.
- Gramicidin: Forms transmembrane channels in lipid bilayers, increasing membrane permeability to inorganic cations and exerting bactericidal activity primarily against gram‑positive organisms.
- Combined spectrum: Overlapping antibacterial activity from the three agents provides a broad topical spectrum.

### Microbiology / spectrum of activity
- The combination is stated to be active against: Staphylococcus aureus; streptococci including Streptococcus pneumoniae; Escherichia coli; Haemophilus influenzae; Klebsiella‑Enterobacter species; Neisseria species; and Pseudomonas aeruginosa.
- The product does not provide adequate coverage against Serratia marcescens.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption, systemic distribution, metabolism and elimination parameters are not specified in the available sources for topical ophthalmic administration.

### Pharmacodynamics
