# Originality Audit Report: neratinib

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 92/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 2
- **Overall Assessment:** Pass

## Verbatim Copied Passages
1. **Dosing String:** "240 mg (six 40 mg tablets) taken orally once daily"
   - **Source:** Matches Document 1 (TGA, Section 4.2) and Document 2 (FDA, Section 2.2).
   - **Location:** Editorial Section 5, under "General Dosing."
   - *Auditor Note:* While verbatim, this is a factual medical specification (dosage) that allows for very little linguistic variation in a clinical context.

2. **Pharmacokinetic Peak:** "peak plasma levels reached at 2 to 8 hours"
   - **Source:** Document 2 (FDA, Section 12.3) states "reached in the range of 2 to 8 hours."
   - **Location:** Editorial Section 9, under "Absorption."
   - *Auditor Note:* Near-verbatim. The phrase is a standard reporting of clinical data.

## Close Paraphrases
1. **Hepatotoxicity Monitoring:** "Monthly for the first three months of treatment. Every three months thereafter, or as clinically indicated."
   - **Source:** Document 2 (FDA, Section 5.2). The source says "monthly for the first 3 months of treatment, then every 3 months while on treatment and as clinically indicated." The editorial slightly simplifies this but retains the exact structure.
2. **Missed Doses:** "Missed doses should not be replaced. The patient should resume the regular schedule the following day."
   - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA, Section 4.2) and Document 2 (FDA, Section 2.2). The phrasing is standard across both regulatory documents; the editorial replaces "next scheduled daily dose" with "the following day."

## Originality Strengths
- **Regulatory Harmonization:** The editorial performs an excellent synthesis of conflicting information regarding hepatic impairment. It correctly identifies that the TGA classifies Child-Pugh C as a "Contraindication" while the FDA treats it as a "Dose Modification," and it uses bracketed attributions (e.g., **[TGA]**, **[FDA]**) to clarify these regional differences.
- **Structural Innovation:** The editorial does not follow the linear numbering of either the TGA or FDA documents. It reorganizes information into an "Editorial Note" and more user-friendly sub-headings like "Diarrhea Management Strategies," which are not present in the sources.
- **Condensation of Tables:** Complex tables regarding loperamide prophylaxis and dose escalation (Table 1 and 2 in both sources) are condensed into clear, instructional prose in Section 5.
- **Mechanism of Action:** Section 10 provides a superior synthesis, using the phrase "pan-ErbB tyrosine kinase family," which is a high-level clinical summary of the more granular receptor lists found in the sources.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Unified Format:** The editorial combines the TGA’s "Extended Adjuvant" focus with the FDA’s "Metastatic" indication, creating a single reference point that does not exist in the source files.
- **Omission of Trial Specifics:** The editorial removes exhaustive clinical trial data (iDFS percentages, hazard ratios, and subgroup analysis tables) in favor of a summary of clinical management.
- **Formatting for Readability:** Uses bolding and bullet points to highlight the most "significant adverse reaction" (diarrhea), whereas the PIs use a more standard, flat regulatory structure.
- **Clarification of Excipients:** Explicitly mentions "neratinib maleate" vs "neratinib" (48.31 mg vs 40 mg) in the formulation section, which is often buried in the "Description" or "Qualitative Composition" sections of the source PIs.

## Recommendation
**PASS**

The editorial is a high-quality, original synthesis. It successfully navigates the complex task of merging two different regulatory frameworks (TGA and FDA) into a cohesive document. The few instances of near-verbatim text are limited to essential clinical data (dosages and timeframes) where accuracy is more critical than linguistic variety. No evidence of "patchwriting" or lazy copying was found. No revision is required.
