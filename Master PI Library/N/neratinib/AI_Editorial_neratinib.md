# Editorial Product Information: Neratinib (Nerlynx)

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from official regulatory documentation provided by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Where clinical guidelines or regulatory restrictions differ between agencies, the most comprehensive or conservative information has been included and attributed to the specific source agency.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** NERLYNX  
**Active Ingredient:** Neratinib (as neratinib maleate)  
**Formulation:** 40 mg film-coated tablets. Each tablet contains neratinib maleate, equivalent to 40 mg of neratinib (approximately 48.31 mg of neratinib maleate).

## 2. Approved Indications
Nerlynx is a kinase inhibitor indicated for:

*   **Extended Adjuvant Treatment:** As a standalone therapy for adult patients with early-stage, HER2-overexpressed/amplified breast cancer, following the completion of adjuvant trastuzumab-based therapy.
*   **Advanced or Metastatic Breast Cancer:** In combination with capecitabine for adult patients with HER2-positive metastatic disease who have previously undergone two or more anti-HER2 based regimens in the metastatic setting **[FDA]**.

## 3. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to neratinib or any of the inactive tablet excipients.
*   **Severe Hepatic Impairment:** Specifically patients classified as Child-Pugh C **[TGA]**. (Note: The FDA suggests a dose reduction for this group rather than a total contraindication; see Section 5).
*   **Drug Interactions:** Concurrent use with medicinal products that are strong inducers of the CYP3A4/P-gp isoform of cytochrome P450, such as carbamazepine, phenytoin, rifampin, or St John’s Wort **[TGA]**.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Diarrhea
Diarrhea is the most significant adverse reaction associated with neratinib, often occurring early (within the first week) and potentially leading to dehydration, hypotension, and renal failure. Management requires mandatory anti-diarrheal prophylaxis or a proactive dose-escalation strategy.

### Hepatotoxicity
Neratinib has been linked to increased liver enzymes. Liver function tests (ALT, AST, and total bilirubin) should be monitored:
*   Monthly for the first three months of treatment.
*   Every three months thereafter, or as clinically indicated.
*   Treatment should be withheld for Grade 3 abnormalities and permanently discontinued for Grade 4 abnormalities.

### Left Ventricular Dysfunction
HER2 inhibition has been associated with decreased left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF). While neratinib has not been studied in patients with significant cardiac history or low baseline LVEF, cardiac monitoring is recommended for patients with known cardiac risk factors **[TGA]**.

### Embryo-Fetal Toxicity
Neratinib can cause fetal harm. Healthcare providers must advise patients of the potential risk to the fetus and the necessity of effective contraception.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
The standard recommended dose is **240 mg (six 40 mg tablets) taken orally once daily**. Tablets must be swallowed whole with food, preferably at the same time every morning. Treatment for extended adjuvant therapy should continue for one year or until disease recurrence.

### Diarrhea Management Strategies
Physicians should employ one of two strategies to improve tolerability:
1.  **Loperamide Prophylaxis:** Initiate 4 mg loperamide three times daily for the first two weeks, followed by twice daily for weeks 3–8, then as needed to maintain 1–2 bowel movements per day.
2.  **Dose Escalation:** Start at 120 mg daily for Week 1, increase to 160 mg daily for Week 2, and reach the 240 mg maintenance dose by Week 3.

### Dose Modifications for Hepatic Impairment
*   **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A or B):** No initial adjustment required.
*   **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Reduce the starting dose to 80 mg daily **[FDA]**. (Note: Contraindicated by TGA).

### Missed Doses
Missed doses should not be replaced. The patient should resume the regular schedule the following day.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile varies slightly depending on whether neratinib is used as a single agent or in combination.

*   **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (reported in >90% of patients without prophylaxis), nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, stomatitis, and dyspepsia.
*   **Hepatobiliary:** Elevated ALT/AST levels and increased blood bilirubin.
*   **Skin and Subcutaneous:** Rash, nail disorders (including paronychia and discoloration), skin fissures, and dry skin.
*   **General:** Fatigue, muscle spasms, decreased appetite, and weight loss.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Gastric Acid Reducing Agents
Neratinib solubility is pH-dependent.
*   **Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs):** Concomitant use should be avoided.
*   **H2-Receptor Antagonists:** Take neratinib at least 2 hours before or 10 hours after the H2-antagonist.
*   **Antacids:** Separate dosing by at least 3 hours.

### CYP3A4 and P-gp Interactions
*   **Strong/Moderate Inhibitors:** Avoid concomitant use (e.g., ketoconazole, fluconazole, erythromycin) as they significantly increase neratinib exposure. If an inhibitor must be used, reduce the neratinib dose to 40 mg daily.
*   **P-gp Substrates:** Neratinib may increase concentrations of P-gp substrates (e.g., digoxin, dabigatran). Monitor these patients for increased toxicity.

### Dietary Interactions
Grapefruit and pomegranate (including juices) should be avoided as they may increase plasma concentrations of the drug.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**Category D [TGA].** There are no adequate data on use in pregnant women. Animal studies indicate embryofetal lethality and skeletal abnormalities at exposures below clinical levels. Females of reproductive potential should have a pregnancy test before starting treatment and must use highly effective contraception during treatment and for one month after the final dose.

### Contraception for Males
Male patients with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for three months following the final dose.

### Lactation
It is unknown if neratinib is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for one month after the last dose.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Slow absorption with peak plasma levels reached at 2 to 8 hours. Absorption and systemic exposure (AUC) increase significantly with a high-fat meal.
*   **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>98%), primarily to albumin and alpha-1 acid glycoprotein.
*   **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized in the liver by the CYP3A4 enzyme system.
*   **Elimination:** The mean half-life is approximately 7 to 17 hours. Excretion is almost entirely fecal (approx. 97%).

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Neratinib is a quinazoline-based, irreversible inhibitor of the pan-ErbB tyrosine kinase family. It binds to the intracellular domains of EGFR (ERBB1), HER2 (ERBB2), and HER4 (ERBB4). This binding inhibits autophosphorylation and downstream signaling pathways (MAPK and AKT), effectively suppressing tumor cell proliferation in HER2-expressing cancers.

## 11. Storage Conditions
Store the tablets below 25°C. The bottle should be kept tightly closed, and the desiccant canister should remain inside to protect the medicine from moisture.
