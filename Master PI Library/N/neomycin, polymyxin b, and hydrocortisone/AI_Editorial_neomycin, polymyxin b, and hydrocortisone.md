# Editorial Product Information: Neomycin, Polymyxin B, and Hydrocortisone (Otic)

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for the combination of neomycin, polymyxin B, and hydrocortisone otic suspension. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a synthesis of available regulatory documents and does not replace the official, legally approved PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredients:** 
    *   **Neomycin sulfate** (Aminoglycoside antibiotic)
    *   **Polymyxin B sulfate** (Polypeptide antibiotic)
    *   **Hydrocortisone** (Corticosteroid)
*   **Brand Names:** Neomycin and Polymyxin B Sulfates and Hydrocortisone Otic Suspension [US].
*   **ATC Code:** S02CA05 (Corticosteroids and anti-infectives in combination).
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile otic suspension containing:
    *   Neomycin sulfate equivalent to 3.5 mg neomycin base/mL.
    *   Polymyxin B sulfate equivalent to 10,000 units/mL.
    *   Hydrocortisone 10 mg (1%)/mL.
*   **Route of Administration:** Auricular (Otic) use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** A-S Medication Solutions [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Superficial Bacterial Infections:** Treatment of superficial infections of the external auditory canal caused by organisms susceptible to the included antibiotics [US].
*   **Post-Surgical Infections:** Treatment of infections occurring in mastoidectomy and fenestration cavities caused by susceptible organisms [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
*   **Therapy Duration:** Treatment must be limited to 10 consecutive days to minimize the risk of ototoxicity and sensitization [US].
*   **Preparation:** The suspension must be shaken well before each use [US].
*   **Cleansing:** The external auditory canal should be cleaned and dried with a sterile cotton applicator before instillation [US].

### Adult Dosing
*   **Standard Dose:** Instill 4 drops into the affected ear 3 to 4 times daily [US].

### Paediatric Dosing
*   **Standard Dose:** 3 drops are suggested due to the smaller capacity of the ear canal, administered 3 to 4 times daily [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Positioning:** The patient should lie with the affected ear upward. After instillation, this position should be maintained for 5 minutes to facilitate penetration into the canal [US].
*   **Cotton Wick Method:** If preferred, a sterile cotton wick may be inserted into the canal and saturated with the suspension. The wick should be kept moist by adding more suspension every 4 hours and must be replaced at least every 24 hours [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to neomycin, polymyxin B, hydrocortisone, or any excipients (including thimerosal) [US].
*   **Viral Infections:** Presence of herpes simplex, vaccinia, or varicella infections in the ear [US].
*   **Tympanic Membrane Perforation:** Use is contraindicated if the tympanic membrane is perforated due to the risk of ototoxicity [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Ototoxicity:** Neomycin may induce permanent sensorineural hearing loss via cochlear damage (destruction of hair cells in the organ of Corti). The risk increases with prolonged use; therefore, treatment is strictly limited to 10 days. Patients should be under close clinical observation [US].
*   **Cutaneous Sensitization:** Neomycin sulfate may cause skin sensitization, often manifesting as low-grade reddening, swelling, dry scaling, and itching, or simply a failure to heal. If sensitization or irritation occurs, discontinue use immediately. Sensitized patients should avoid neomycin-containing products thereafter [US].
*   **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may lead to the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi. If the infection does not improve after one week, cultures and susceptibility testing should be repeated [US].
*   **Cross-Reactions:** Allergic cross-reactions may occur, potentially precluding the future use of other aminoglycosides such as kanamycin, paromomycin, streptomycin, and possibly gentamicin [US].
*   **General Precaution:** Do not use in the eyes [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are not defined in the source document due to the lack of a treated patient denominator.

*   **Allergic/Dermatological:** Allergic sensitization (most frequent), burning, itching, irritation, dryness, folliculitis, hypertrichosis, acneiform eruptions, hypopigmentation, perioral dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis, maceration of skin, skin atrophy, striae, and miliaria [US].
*   **Ototoxicity:** Sensorineural hearing loss [US].
*   **Nephrotoxicity:** Reported with topical use of antibiotic combinations containing neomycin [US].
*   **Middle Ear Effects:** Stinging and burning have been reported rarely if the drug gains access to the middle ear [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **General Note:** Clinicians should consider the potential for cumulative toxicity if used in conjunction with systemic aminoglycosides.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter grade in the source, but contains class warnings.
*   **Risk Summary:** Topical corticosteroids (0.5%–15% concentration) have demonstrated teratogenic effects in rabbits and mice in animal studies. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].

### Breastfeeding
*   **Risk Summary:** Hydrocortisone is excreted in human milk following oral administration. Because systemic absorption may occur after topical application, caution should be exercised when administered to a nursing woman [US].

### Fertility
*   **Data:** Long-term animal studies (rats, rabbits, mice) showed no evidence of carcinogenicity from oral corticosteroids [US]. Impact on human fertility is not specified.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness for otitis externa have been established in paediatric patients [US]. (See Section 3 for dosing).
*   **Geriatric Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects. No identified differences in responses between elderly and younger patients have been reported [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, neomycin-associated nephrotoxicity is a noted risk [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
*   **Hydrocortisone:** A corticosteroid that suppresses the inflammatory response and may inhibit the body's defense mechanism against infection [US].
*   **Neomycin and Polymyxin B:** Antibacterial components that provide action against susceptible organisms [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
*   **Susceptible Microorganisms:** *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Escherichia coli*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Klebsiella-Enterobacter* species, *Neisseria* species, and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* [US].
*   **Non-Susceptible Microorganisms:** The product does not provide adequate coverage against *Serratia marcescens* and streptococci, including *Streptococcus pneumoniae* [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Systemic absorption of hydrocortisone may occur following topical application [US]. Specific absorption data for neomycin and polymyxin B via the otic route is not specified.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 15° to 25°C (59° to 77°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Keep the container tightly closed. Avoid contaminating the bottle tip with material from the ear, fingers, or other sources to preserve sterility [US].
