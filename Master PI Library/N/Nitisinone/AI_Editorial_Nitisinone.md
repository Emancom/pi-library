# Editorial Product Information: Nitisinone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: NITYR (nitisinone) tablets.
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data, dosing protocols, and safety information. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their specific region before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Nitisinone; a competitive inhibitor of 4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate dioxygenase.
- **Chemical Class**: Cyclohexanedione.
- **Brand Names**: **NITYR** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: A16AX04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Oral tablets: 2 mg, 5 mg, and 10 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer**: Orpharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hereditary Tyrosinaemia Type 1 (HT-1)**: Indicated for the treatment of patients with confirmed HT-1. This therapy must be used in conjunction with a restricted diet deficient in tyrosine and phenylalanine [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing
- **Initial Dose**: 1 mg/kg body weight/day, administered in two divided doses [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose**: 2 mg/kg body weight/day. This should be considered the absolute ceiling for all patients [Australian].

### 3.2 Dose Adjustment and Titration
- Dosing must be individualised based on biochemical monitoring.
- If urine succinylacetone is still detectable one month after initiation, the dose should be increased to 1.5 mg/kg/day. 
- Further titration to 2 mg/kg/day may be required based on an evaluation of all biochemical parameters (plasma/urine succinylacetone, liver function tests, and alpha-fetoprotein).
- If the biochemical response is satisfactory, subsequent adjustments should be based solely on body weight gain [Australian].

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
- **Consistency**: May be taken with or without food; however, it is recommended that if treatment is initiated with food, this routine should be maintained [Australian].
- **Pediatric/Infant Administration**:
    - **Applesauce**: Tablets may be crushed between two metal spoons and mixed with one teaspoon of applesauce. The mixture must be administered immediately or stored at room temperature for no more than 2 hours.
    - **Oral Syringe**: Tablets may be disintegrated in a 5-mL oral syringe with water (2.6 mL for one tablet; 5 mL for two). Disintegration requires approximately 60 minutes. The resulting suspension is stable for 24 hours at room temperature, protected from sunlight.
    - **Precaution**: Do not administer the suspension via a baby bottle [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to nitisinone or any of the tablet excipients (lactose monohydrate, glyceryl dibehenate).
- **Lactation**: Mothers receiving nitisinone treatment must not breast-feed [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ocular and Cutaneous Toxicity**: Inadequate dietary restriction of tyrosine and phenylalanine leads to high plasma tyrosine levels. Levels exceeding 500 μmol/L are associated with:
    - Ocular effects: Corneal ulcers, opacities, keratitis, conjunctivitis, and photophobia.
    - Skin effects: Painful hyperkeratotic plaques on palms and soles.
    - Neurological effects: Variable developmental delay and intellectual disability.
- **Dietary Compliance**: If tyrosine levels exceed 500 μmol/L, dietary restriction must be intensified. Dose reduction of nitisinone is **not** recommended to manage hypertyrosinaemia, as this may lead to clinical deterioration of the underlying metabolic defect.
- **Liver Monitoring**: Regular liver imaging (ultrasound, CT, or MRI) and LFTs are mandatory. Increasing alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) levels or new liver nodules require urgent evaluation for hepatic malignancy, a known risk in HT-1.
- **Haematological Monitoring**: Reversible thrombocytopenia and leucopenia have been reported. Platelet and WBC counts should be monitored regularly.
- **Monitoring Frequency**: Clinical and laboratory visits should occur at least every 6 months; more frequent monitoring is required during dose titration or if adverse events occur [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Frequency Summary
| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (≥1/100 to <1/10) | Blood/Lymphatic | Thrombocytopenia, leucopenia, granulocytopenia |
| | Eye | Conjunctivitis, corneal opacity, keratitis, photophobia, eye pain |
| **Uncommon** (≥1/1,000 to <1/100) | Eye | Blepharitis |
| | Skin | Exfoliative dermatitis, rash, pruritus |

### 6.2 Serious Adverse Effects
- **HT-1 Related Risks**: Hepatic failure (6.4%), hepatic neoplasm (malignant 4.4%, unspecified 3.2%), and porphyric crises are associated with the underlying disease state but remain critical considerations during nitisinone therapy [Australian].
- **Other (<1%)**: Encephalopathy, septicaemia, and gastrointestinal haemorrhage.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Interactions**: Nitisinone is metabolised in vitro by CYP3A4. Dose adjustments may be necessary when co-administered with potent inhibitors or inducers of this enzyme.
- **CYP Inhibition**: In vitro studies indicate that nitisinone is not expected to inhibit CYP1A2, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, or 3A4 [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Category B3 [Australian]**: There are no adequate data in pregnant women. Nitisinone should only be used if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the foetus. Animal studies have demonstrated embryotoxicity and foetal abnormalities (e.g., skeletal malformations, umbilical hernia) at doses near or below the maximum clinical dose.
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if nitisinone is excreted in human milk. Due to potential risks to the suckling infant (including corneal opacities seen in animal models), breastfeeding is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Fertility**: Animal data showed prolonged mating periods and increased post-implantation loss at doses twice the maximum clinical dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Most clinical data involve pediatric patients (median age 9 months at enrolment). Dosing is weight-based.
- **Elderly Use**: No data available; safety and efficacy have not been established in patients over 65 years.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: Specific dosing adjustments for renal or hepatic impairment are not provided in available sources; however, close monitoring of liver function is standard for the indication [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Nitisinone is a competitive inhibitor of 4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate dioxygenase (4-HPPD), the enzyme preceding fumarylacetoacetate hydrolase (FAH) in the tyrosine catabolic pathway. By blocking this pathway, it prevents the accumulation of toxic intermediates (maleylacetoacetate and fumarylacetoacetate) which otherwise convert to succinylacetone, a potent toxin and inhibitor of porphyrin synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Food delays Tmax (from 3h fasting to 6h fed) but does not clinically affect total exposure (AUC).
    - **Distribution**: Mean volume of distribution is 0.3 L/kg in children and 0.07 L/kg in adults.
    - **Elimination**: Terminal half-life is approximately 52 to 60 hours. Clearance is estimated at 0.0956 L/kg/day [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study**: An open-label, uncontrolled study of 207 patients (ages 0 to 21.7 years) demonstrated significant improvements in survival compared to historical controls treated with diet alone. 
- **Survival Data**: In patients where treatment was initiated before 2 months of age, 4-year survival was 88%. For those initiated after 6 months of age, survival reached 93–97%.
- **Biochemical Response**: Urine succinylacetone normalized in >90% of patients within the first week of treatment.
- **Cancer Risk**: Early initiation (before 12 months of age) resulted in a 13.5-fold reduction in the risk of developing hepatocellular carcinoma compared to dietary restriction alone [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions**: Keep in the original bottle to protect from light.
- **In-use Stability**: Reconstituted suspension in a capped oral syringe is stable for 24 hours at room temperature [Australian].
