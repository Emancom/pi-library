# Editorial Product Information: Nebivolol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, UK, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source material and is therefore noted as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nebivolol hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Beta-adrenoceptor antagonist (selective).
- **Brand Names:** **APO-Nebivolol** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets:** 1.25 mg, 5 mg, and 10 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Essential Hypertension:** Management of high blood pressure [Australian].
- **Chronic Heart Failure (CHF):** Treatment of stable chronic heart failure as an adjunct to standard therapy in patients aged 70 years or older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Hypertension
- **General Dose:** 5 mg once daily, preferably at the same time each day [Australian].
- **Onset of Action:** Blood pressure reduction is evident after 2 weeks; optimal effect may require 4 weeks [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** May be used as monotherapy or with 12.5–25 mg hydrochlorothiazide [Australian].

### Chronic Heart Failure (CHF)
- **Titration Schedule:** Must be initiated with gradual up-titration at 1–2 weekly intervals based on patient tolerance [Australian]:
    - 1.25 mg once daily, increased to:
    - 2.5 mg once daily, then:
    - 5 mg once daily, then:
    - **Maximum Dose:** 10 mg once daily.
- **Clinical Requirements:** Patients must have stable CHF without acute failure for the preceding six weeks. Standard therapies (diuretics, digoxin, ACE inhibitors, or ARBs) should be stabilised for two weeks prior to starting **nebivolol** [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets may be taken with or without food. A consistent approach (with or without meals) is recommended [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **nebivolol** hydrochloride or any excipients [Australian].
- Hepatic insufficiency or impaired liver function [Australian].
- Acute heart failure, cardiogenic shock, or episodes of decompensation requiring intravenous inotropic therapy [Australian].
- Sick sinus syndrome (including sino-atrial block) [Australian].
- Second and third-degree heart block in patients without a pacemaker [Australian].
- History of bronchospasm or asthma [Australian].
- Untreated phaeochromocytoma [Australian].
- Metabolic acidosis [Australian].
- Bradycardia (heart rate < 60 bpm prior to therapy) [Australian].
- Hypotension (systolic blood pressure < 100 mmHg) [Australian].
- Severe peripheral circulatory disturbances [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Do not discontinue therapy abruptly, especially in patients with ischaemic heart disease. Dosage should be tapered over 1–2 weeks to avoid exacerbation of angina or myocardial infarction [Australian].
- **Anaesthesia:** Inform anaesthetists of **nebivolol** use due to the risk of bradyarrhythmias, hypotension, and decreased reflex ability to compensate for blood loss. If blockade must be interrupted, discontinue at least 24 hours prior to surgery [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular:** May induce or worsen bradycardia. Use with caution in patients with first-degree heart block or Prinzmetal’s angina [Australian].
- **Metabolic/Endocrinological:** May mask symptoms of hypoglycaemia (tachycardia, palpitations) in diabetic patients and symptoms of tachycardia in hyperthyroidism [Australian].
- **Respiratory:** Use with caution in chronic obstructive pulmonary disorders if airway constriction is aggravated [Australian].
- **Sensitivity to Allergens:** May increase sensitivity to allergens and the severity of anaphylactic reactions; adrenaline response may be reduced [Australian].
- **Lactose:** Contains lactose monohydrate; avoid in patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Hypertension (Incidence ≥ 1%)
| Adverse Event | **Nebivolol** 5 mg (%) | Placebo (%) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Headache | 6.0 | 11.1 |
| Dizziness | 4.8 | 2.3 |
| Fatigue | 4.3 | 2.3 |
| Paraesthesia | 2.6 | 0.3 |
| Nausea | 1.9 | 2.1 |
| Dyspnoea | 1.2 | 2.1 |

### Chronic Heart Failure (SENIORS Trial)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Dizziness (14.4%), Bradycardia (10.6%) [Australian].
- **Common (1-10%):** Aggravated cardiac failure (19.0% - noted as similar to placebo 21.3%), hypotension (6.9%), fatigue (6.3%) [Australian].

### Post-Marketing and Other Reactions
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Nightmares, depression, impaired vision, bradycardia, heart failure, slowed AV conduction, hypotension, intermittent claudication, bronchospasm, dyspepsia, flatulence, vomiting, pruritus, rash, impotence [Australian].
- **Very Rare (<0.1%):** Syncope, aggravated psoriasis [Australian].
- **Serious/Isolated Reports:** Angioneurotic oedema, abnormal hepatic function, acute pulmonary oedema, thrombocytopenia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Class I Antiarrhythmics & Calcium Channel Blockers (Verapamil/Diltiazem type):** Not recommended; risk of potentiated negative inotropic effects and AV conduction disturbances [Australian].
- **Centrally Acting Antihypertensives (e.g., clonidine, methyldopa):** Not recommended; may worsen heart failure. Abrupt withdrawal may cause rebound hypertension [Australian].
- **Insulin and Antidiabetics:** Masked symptoms of hypoglycaemia [Australian].
- **Anaesthetics (Volatile halogenated):** May increase risk of hypotension and attenuate reflex tachycardia [Australian].
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** May increase AV conduction time [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors (e.g., paroxetine, fluoxetine, quinidine):** May increase **nebivolol** plasma levels, increasing the risk of bradycardia and adverse events [Australian].
- **Cimetidine:** Increases peak plasma concentrations of **nebivolol** by approximately 21–23% [Australian].
- **Ranitidine/Antacids:** No significant effect on pharmacokinetics if **nebivolol** is taken with meals and antacids between meals [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C.** 
    - Beta-blockers reduce placental perfusion, potentially leading to intrauterine growth retardation, death, or premature delivery. If used, the newborn must be closely monitored for 3 days post-delivery for hypoglycaemia and bradycardia [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Animal studies show excretion in breast milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, **nebivolol** decreased fertility in rats at high doses (160 mg/kg/day). Human studies showed variable effects on testosterone levels between genders but no significant effect on other hormones [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is not recommended as studies in children and adolescents below 18 years have not been conducted [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** 
    - **Hypertension:** Recommended starting dose of 2.5 mg daily for patients > 65 years. Caution in patients > 75 years [Australian].
    - **CHF:** No specific adjustment required beyond standard titration; trial data was established in patients $\ge$ 70 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Hypertension:** Recommended starting dose is 2.5 mg daily. 
    - **CHF:** No adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment. Not recommended in severe renal insufficiency (serum creatinine $\ge$ 250 µmol/L) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated due to limited data [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Nebivolol** is a racemate of two enantiomers. The *d*-enantiomer is a competitive and selective beta-1 receptor antagonist. The *l*-enantiomer has mild vasodilating properties through interaction with the L-arginine/nitric oxide pathway [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; unaffected by food [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via alicyclic and aromatic hydroxylation, N-dealkylation, and glucuronidation. Metabolism via aromatic hydroxylation is subject to CYP2D6 polymorphism. Bioavailability is ~13% in fast metabolisers and virtually complete in slow metabolisers [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life of enantiomers is ~10 hours in fast metabolisers and 3–5 times longer in slow metabolisers. Excreted in urine (38%) and faeces (48%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hypertension:** Five dose-finding trials (n=1106) and seven therapeutic dose trials (n=1493) confirmed 5 mg significantly reduces systolic and diastolic blood pressure compared to placebo [Australian].
- **SENIORS Study (CHF):** A trial of 2128 patients ($\ge$ 70 years) showed **nebivolol** as an add-on therapy significantly reduced the composite endpoint of all-cause mortality or cardiovascular hospital admission (Hazard Ratio 0.86). The benefit was independent of age, gender, or left ventricular ejection fraction [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions

- Shelf life is specified on the packaging [Australian].
