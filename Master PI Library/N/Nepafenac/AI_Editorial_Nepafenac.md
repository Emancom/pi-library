# Editorial Product Information: Nepafenac

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved documentation before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nepafenac (a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory prodrug).
- **Chemical Class:** Phenylacetic acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** **ILEVRO** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** S01BC10.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Ophthalmic suspension (eye drops), 3 mg/mL (0.3%) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Postoperative Inflammation and Pain:** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of pain and inflammation associated with cataract surgery [Australian-approved].
- **Postoperative Macular Oedema:** Reduction in the risk of postoperative macular oedema following cataract surgery in patients with non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The standard dose is one drop in the conjunctival sac of the affected eye(s) once daily.
- **Dosing Schedule by Indication:**
    - **Pain and Inflammation:** Begin 1 day prior to surgery, continue on the day of surgery, and through the first 14 days of the postoperative period.
    - **Macular Oedema Risk Reduction (Diabetic Patients):** Begin 1 day prior to surgery, continue on the day of surgery, and for up to 60 days postoperatively as directed by the clinician.
- **Surgical Day Supplement:** An additional drop must be administered 30 to 120 minutes before the surgical procedure.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Shake the bottle well before use.
    - Remove the tamper-evident snap collar if loose after opening the cap.
    - To limit systemic absorption, nasolacrimal occlusion (pressing on the tear duct) and gentle eyelid closure are recommended following instillation.
    - If using multiple ophthalmic medications, wait at least 5 minutes between products; ointments should be applied last.
    - Avoid contaminating the dropper tip by not touching the eyelids or surrounding surfaces.
- **Missed Dose:** Administer a single drop as soon as remembered. Do not double the dose to compensate for a missed one.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to nepafenac, any excipients, or other non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) [Australian].
- **Cross-Sensitivity:** Patients who experience asthma, acute rhinitis, or urticaria precipitated by acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin) or other NSAIDs [Australian].
- **Contact Lenses:** Use is contraindicated while wearing soft contact lenses due to the presence of benzalkonium chloride [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Ocular Surface Effects:** Topical NSAIDs may cause keratitis. In susceptible patients, prolonged use may lead to epithelial breakdown, corneal thinning, erosion, ulceration, or perforation. These events can be sight-threatening. Use should be discontinued immediately if corneal epithelial breakdown is evident.
- **Delayed Healing:** Topical NSAIDs may slow the healing process. Caution is advised when used concomitantly with topical corticosteroids, as the risk of healing complications increases.
- **Increased Bleeding:** There is a potential for increased bleeding of ocular tissues (e.g., hyphaemas) during surgery. Use with caution in patients with known bleeding tendencies or those taking medications that prolong bleeding time.
- **Masking of Infection:** Anti-inflammatory properties may mask the signs of an acute ocular infection.
- **Sunlight Exposure:** Patients should be advised to avoid direct sunlight during treatment.
- **Contact Lens Wear:** Generally not recommended during the postoperative period following cataract surgery.
- **Excipient-Specific Caution:** Contains benzalkonium chloride, which may cause eye irritation, punctate/toxic ulcerative keratopathy, and discolouration of soft contact lenses.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally ocular and occurred in 0.1% to 1% of patients in clinical trials.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Punctate keratitis (specifically in diabetic patients) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Keratitis, foreign body sensation, eye pain, corneal epithelium defect, eyelid margin crusting |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypersensitivity, dizziness, headache, iritis, choroidal effusion, corneal deposits, ocular discomfort, dry eye, blepharitis, eye discharge, eye pruritus, allergic conjunctivitis, increased lacrimation, conjunctival hyperaemia, nausea, allergic dermatitis, cutis laxa (dermatochalasis) |
| **Not Known** | Corneal perforation, impaired healing, corneal opacity, corneal scar, reduced visual acuity, eye swelling, ulcerative keratitis, corneal thinning, blurred vision, increased blood pressure, vomiting |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP Inhibition:** In vitro data suggest a very low potential for interactions mediated by cytochrome P450 (CYP1A2, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, and 3A4) [Australian].
- **Protein Binding:** Interaction potential via protein binding is unlikely due to low systemic exposure [Australian].
- **Prostaglandin Analogues:** Concomitant use is not recommended due to limited data and potentially conflicting mechanisms of action.
- **Topical Steroids:** Increased risk of corneal healing issues when used together.
- **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets:** Potential for increased risk of ocular haemorrhage when used with medications that prolong bleeding time.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Nepafenac crosses the placenta in animal studies and is associated with reproductive toxicity at high oral doses. It is not recommended during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using contraception.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if nepafenac is excreted in human milk. Excretion occurs in rat milk. While systemic exposure is negligible, use is not recommended during lactation.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed decreased sperm motility at high exposures but no impairment of overall fertility or reproductive performance.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established; use is not recommended.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available; however, no dose adjustments are expected to be necessary.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not studied in this population. No dose adjustment is warranted as systemic exposure is very low.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in this population. No dose adjustment is warranted as elimination is primarily via biotransformation.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Nepafenac is a prodrug. Upon topical administration, it penetrates the cornea and is converted by ocular tissue hydrolases into amfenac. Amfenac inhibits cyclooxygenase (COX-1 and COX-2) enzymes, thereby suppressing prostaglandin synthesis.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Clinically, it reduces inflammation and pain without significantly impacting intraocular pressure.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Low steady-state plasma concentrations are reached (Cmax < 1.13 ng/mL).
    - **Distribution:** Distributed locally to both the anterior and posterior segments of the eye.
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly bioactivated to amfenac, followed by more polar metabolites involving hydroxylation and glucuronide conjugation.
    - **Excretion:** Primarily via urine (~85%) and faeces (~6%) following oral administration.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Postoperative Pain and Inflammation:** Two masked, placebo-controlled trials (n=3462) demonstrated that nepafenac 0.3% was superior to vehicle. Inflammation clearance at Day 14 was 65–68% for nepafenac vs. 25–35% for vehicle. Pain-free rates were ~90% for the nepafenac group [Australian].
- **Macular Oedema in Diabetic Patients:** Two studies in patients with non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy showed a significantly lower incidence of macular oedema with nepafenac (4.1% integrated) compared to vehicle (15.9% integrated). More patients achieved a 15-letter improvement in visual acuity compared to the control group [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Protection:** Keep protected from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any remaining suspension 4 weeks after the bottle is first opened [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
