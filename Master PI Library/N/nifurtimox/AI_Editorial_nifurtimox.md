# Editorial Product Information: Nifurtimox

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of nifurtimox for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinically relevant reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active ingredient:** Nifurtimox (a nitrofuran antiprotozoal).
- **Brand name(s):** **Lampit** [US].
- **ATC code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage form(s) and strength(s):** 
    - Functionally scored film-coated tablets: 30 mg and 120 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/sponsor:** Bayer HealthCare Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chagas Disease (American Trypanosomiasis):** Indicated for the treatment of Chagas disease caused by *Trypanosoma cruzi* in pediatric patients from birth to less than 18 years of age, weighing at least 2.5 kg [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Pediatric)
Nifurtimox must be administered three times daily with food. The total daily dose is determined by body weight, and the recommended treatment duration is 60 days [US].

### Dosing by Body Weight [US]
| Body Weight Group | Total Daily Dose |
| :--- | :--- |
| 41 kg or greater | 8 to 10 mg/kg |
| Less than 41 kg | 10 to 20 mg/kg |

### Administration Instructions
- **Tablets:** May be taken whole or split by hand at the scored line. Do not use mechanical tablet-splitting devices [US].
- **Slurry Preparation:** For patients unable to swallow tablets, a slurry may be prepared by dispersing the tablet in approximately 2.5 mL of water in a spoon. The tablet typically disintegrates in less than 30 seconds. The slurry must be administered immediately with food [US].
- **Alcohol:** Consumption of alcohol is contraindicated during treatment [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible with food. If the next scheduled dose is less than 3 hours away, the missed dose should be skipped. Do not double the dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to nifurtimox or any excipients [US].
- Consumption of alcohol during the course of treatment [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Genotoxicity and Carcinogenicity:** Nifurtimox has demonstrated genotoxicity in humans, in vitro systems, and rodents. A study in pediatric patients showed a 13-fold increase in chromosomal aberrations. While carcinogenicity is observed with similar nitrofurans, human data for nifurtimox are currently unavailable [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Animal data suggest potential fetal harm (abortions, fetal death, malformations). Pregnancy testing is required prior to initiation in females of reproductive potential [US].
- **Neurological and Psychiatric Conditions:** Treatment may worsen pre-existing conditions (e.g., brain injury, seizures, psychiatric disease, serious behavioural alterations). Close medical supervision is required for these patients [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Severe reactions, including angioedema, hypotension, dyspnea, and serious skin reactions, have been reported. Discontinue immediately if serious hypersensitivity occurs [US].
- **Decreased Appetite and Weight Loss:** Patients should have their weight checked every 14 days, as dose adjustments may be necessary [US].
- **Porphyria:** Nitrofuran derivatives may trigger acute porphyria attacks. Administer with caution and under close supervision in affected patients [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 64.5% of pediatric patients reported adverse reactions in clinical trials [US].

### Common Adverse Effects (Frequency ≥1% to >10%) [US]
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Vomiting (14.6%), Abdominal pain (13.2%), Headache (12.8%), Decreased appetite (10.5%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea (8.2%), Pyrexia (7.3%), Rash (5.5%), Diarrhea (4.6%), Weight decreased (2.7%), Dizziness (2.7%), Anemia (2.7%), Eosinophilia (2.3%), Urticaria (2.3%) |

### Serious and Postmarketing Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and Angioedema [US].
- **Skin:** Drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS) [US].
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Seizures, amnesia, polyneuropathy, psychotic behaviour, agitation, and sleep disorders [US].
- **Other:** Muscle weakness, tremors, and syncope [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Concomitant use may increase the incidence and severity of adverse effects common to nitrofurans. Alcohol is contraindicated during treatment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** In vitro studies indicate nifurtimox is not a substrate, inducer, or inhibitor of major CYP450 enzymes or transporters (P-gp, BCRP, OATs, OCTs, MATEs). No clinical drug interaction studies have been conducted [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not recommended for the treatment of chronic Chagas disease during pregnancy due to embryo-fetal toxicity risks. For acute symptomatic Chagas disease, treatment should be evaluated on a case-by-case basis [US].
- **Contraception:** 
    - **Females:** Use effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months after the final dose [US].
    - **Males:** Use condoms with female partners of reproductive potential during treatment and for 3 months after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Nifurtimox is present in human milk (estimated infant dose <15% of the pediatric dose). Monitor infants for vomiting, rash, decreased appetite, pyrexia, and irritability [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate nifurtimox may impair male fertility, which was not fully reversible in some cases [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in patients from birth to <18 years weighing ≥2.5 kg. Not established in those weighing <2.5 kg [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Information not specified in available sources (limited to pediatric indication).
- **Renal Impairment:** Effect on PK is unknown; however, blood concentrations are increased in patients with End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) requiring hemodialysis. Close medical supervision is required [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Effect on PK is unknown. Close medical supervision is required [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A nitrofuran antiprotozoal. It is activated by Type I and Type II nitroreductases, producing toxic metabolites and reactive oxygen species that induce DNA damage and cell death in *T. cruzi* [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is approximately 4 hours (fed). Food increases $C_{max}$ by 68% and AUC by 71% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Crosses the blood-brain and placental barriers. Plasma protein binding is 42% [US].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is 2.4 to 3.6 hours. Approximately 44% is recovered in urine, primarily as metabolites [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Trial 1 (NCT02625974):** A prospective, randomized, double-blind trial in 330 pediatric patients (Argentina, Bolivia, Colombia). The 60-day regimen was compared to a 30-day regimen. 
- **Outcomes:** The 60-day regimen was superior, with serological response rates (defined by lysate ELISA) of 32% vs 19% at 1 year post-treatment. Seroconversion to negative (confirmed by three assays) was 81.8% in infants (<8 months) and 20% in those aged 8 months to <2 years at the 4-year follow-up [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Store in the original bottle with the child-resistant closure. Do not remove the desiccant packet. Protect from moisture [US].
