# Editorial Product Information: Nimodipine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nimodipine (a 1,4-dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Nimotop** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C08CA06.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Tablet:** 30 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
    - **Concentrated Solution for Infusion:** 10 mg/50 mL (0.2 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral; Intravenous infusion (via central catheter); Intracisternal instillation (experimental/uncontrolled use) [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Boucher & Muir Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Haemorrhage (SAH):** Prophylaxis and treatment of ischaemic neurological deficits resulting from cerebral vasospasm following a ruptured intracranial aneurysm. Use is indicated for patients in good neurological condition post-ictus (e.g., Hunt and Hess Grades I-III) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Intravenous Infusion
- **Initial Phase:** Administer 1 mg/hour (5 mL/hour) for the first 2 hours.
- **Maintenance Phase:** If tolerated well (no severe blood pressure reduction), increase the dose to 2 mg/hour (10 mL/hour).
- **Low Body Weight:** Patients weighing significantly less than 70 kg or those with labile blood pressure should start at 0.5 mg/hour (2.5 mL/hour) [Australian].
- **Administration Note:** Must be administered as a continuous infusion via a central catheter using an infusion pump and a three-way stopcock for co-infusion [Australian].

### 3.2 Oral Administration
- **Sequential Therapy:** Following 5–14 days of intravenous therapy, it is recommended to transition to 60 mg (2 x 30 mg tablets) every 4 hours (total 360 mg/day) for approximately 7 days.
- **Oral-only Therapy:** If no intravenous therapy is used, the recommended dose is 60 mg every 4 hours for 10–14 days [Australian].

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
- **IV Compatibility:** Must be co-infused with compatible solutions (e.g., 0.9% NaCl, 5% Glucose, Hartmann’s solution) at a ratio of approximately 1:4 (40 mL/h of co-infusate).
- **Incompatibility:** Do **not** use PVC tubing, as nimodipine is adsorbed by PVC; use polyethylene (PE), polypropylene (PP), or polyurethane (PU) instead.
- **Tablets:** Swallow whole with liquid, regardless of meals. **Grapefruit juice must be avoided** [Australian].
- **Alkaline Mixtures:** Do not administer alkaline mixtures within 2 hours of oral nimodipine due to potential hydrolysis [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to nimodipine or any excipients.
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** Concomitant use of oral nimodipine with rifampicin, or antiepileptics such as phenobarbital, phenytoin, or carbamazepine is contraindicated as they significantly reduce nimodipine efficacy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypotension:** Nimodipine can cause a dose-related decrease in blood pressure (reported in 5–7% of patients). Use with caution if systolic BP is <100 mmHg [Australian].
- **Intracranial Pressure:** Caution is advised in patients with generalised cerebral oedema or severely raised intracranial pressure [Australian].
- **Cardiac Risks:** Consider the benefit-risk ratio in patients with unstable angina or within 4 weeks of an acute myocardial infarction [Australian].
- **Ethanol Content:** The intravenous formulation contains 24.7% (v/v) ethanol (up to 50 g per daily dose), which must be considered in patients with alcoholism, impaired alcohol metabolism, epilepsy, or liver disease [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of blood pressure, pulse, and ECG is recommended, particularly during dose escalation or in patients with hepatic/renal impairment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are classified by frequency as follows:
- **Uncommon (≥ 0.1% to < 1%):** 
    - Thrombocytopaenia
    - Allergic reactions (rash)
    - Headache
    - Tachycardia
    - Hypotension and vasodilation
    - Nausea
- **Rare (≥ 0.01% to < 0.1%):**
    - Bradycardia
    - Ileus (intestinal pseudo-obstruction)
    - Transient increase in liver enzymes
    - Infusion site reactions (including thrombo-phlebitis)
- **Post-Marketing Reports:** Nausea and vomiting [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### 7.1 Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP3A4 Pathway)
- **Inhibitors:** Nimodipine plasma levels are increased by macrolide antibiotics (e.g., erythromycin), anti-HIV protease inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir), azole antimycotics (e.g., ketoconazole), nefazodone, fluoxetine (50% increase), cimetidine (50% increase), and valproic acid. 
- **Inducers:** Rifampicin and certain anticonvulsants (phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbital) significantly lower nimodipine levels and are contraindicated [Australian].
- **Food:** Grapefruit juice inhibits nimodipine metabolism and should be avoided for at least 4 days prior to treatment [Australian].

### 7.2 Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Antihypertensives:** Potentiation of blood-pressure-lowering effects (e.g., diuretics, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors).
- **Beta-blockers (IV):** Simultaneous intravenous use may lead to mutual potentiation of negative inotropic effects and potential heart failure [Australian].
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Concomitant use with aminoglycosides or cephalosporins may deteriorate renal function [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category C.** Nimodipine may cause fetal hypoxia due to maternal hypotension. Animal studies show embryotoxicity (reduced fetal weight). Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Nimodipine and its metabolites appear in breast milk at concentrations similar to maternal plasma. Breastfeeding is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In vitro cases suggest calcium channel blockers may lead to reversible biochemical changes in spermatozoa, potentially impairing sperm function [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data provided, though clearance may be affected by age-related renal/hepatic changes [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is substantially reduced, and bioavailability is doubled. Dose should be reduced to 30 mg (one tablet) every 4 hours or 0.5 mg/hour (2.5 mL/hour) for IV [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Patients with severe renal insufficiency should be closely monitored. Intravenous treatment requires careful surveillance if the patient is also receiving nephrotoxic drugs [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Nimodipine is a 1,4-dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker with preferential cerebral vasodilator activity. It inhibits calcium ion transfer into smooth muscle and neurons, reducing vasospasm-induced ischaemia [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Orally, nimodipine is almost completely absorbed but undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism, resulting in a bioavailability of 3–30%.
    - **Distribution:** Highly lipophilic; crosses the blood-brain barrier. Protein binding is >95%.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via the CYP3A4 system in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic decline; terminal half-life is 1.2–1.8 hours (IV) and 5–10 hours (oral). Approximately 50% is excreted via kidneys (as metabolites) and 30% via faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SAH Efficacy:** Clinical studies demonstrate reduced severity of neurological deficits and lower mortality in patients with Hunt and Hess Grades I-III. While clinically effective, arteriographic evidence has not consistently shown that nimodipine prevents the physical spasm of large arteries [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Concentrated Solution for Infusion:** Store below 25°C. Protect from direct sunlight. Stable for 10 hours in diffuse daylight if in the manufacturer’s light-protective brown glass vial or protected by opaque coverings [Australian].
- **Tablets:** Store in original container in a dry place, protected from light.
    - Blister (PP/Al or PVC/PVDC/Al): Store below 25°C.
    - Blister (PA/Al/PVC/Al): Store below 30°C [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
