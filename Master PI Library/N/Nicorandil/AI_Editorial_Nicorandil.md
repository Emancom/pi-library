## 4. Dosage and Administration
- General adult dosing:
  - Recommended therapeutic dose: 10 to 20 mg twice daily; usual starting dose 10 mg twice daily (preferably morning and evening). A lower starting dose of 5 mg twice daily may be used in patients prone to headache or other adverse reactions. Dosage should be titrated to the minimum effective dose [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].
- Dosing by indication: Not specified beyond chronic stable angina pectoris dosing above [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].
- Administration instructions:
  - Oral administration of tablets. Nicorandil tablets may be taken with meals; food delays absorption (~16%) but does not change extent of absorption [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Sections 4.2; 4.5].
- Titration schedules:
  - Start at the lowest effective dose and titrate to effect; a lower initial dose (5 mg twice daily) may reduce early headache incidence [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.2].
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List of contraindications reported in the provided source (every item retained and attributed):
- Known or idiosyncratic hypersensitivity to nicorandil, nicotinamide, nicotinic acid or any excipient [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.3].
- Cardiogenic shock [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.3].
- Hypotension; severe hypotension; patients at risk of developing severe hypotension including acute myocardial infarction with acute left ventricular failure and low filling pressures, and hypovolaemia [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.3].
- Cardiac decompensation [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.3].
- Acute pulmonary oedema [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.3].
- Concomitant use with any soluble guanylate cyclase stimulators (e.g., riociguat) because of risk of serious blood pressure fall [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.3].
- Concomitant use with phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (e.g., sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil) due to risk of severe hypotension [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.3].

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- Boxed/warning-style highlights:
  - **Ulceration risk:** Nicorandil is associated with gastrointestinal, skin, mucosal, ocular and mouth ulcerations which may be severe and refractory to treatment until nicorandil is discontinued; persisting ulceration should prompt permanent discontinuation [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
  - **Concomitant use with PDE5 inhibitors or soluble guanylate cyclase stimulators is contraindicated** because of risk of serious blood pressure lowering [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Sections 4.3; 4.5].
- General warnings and precautions:
  - Gastrointestinal ulcerations may present after short or prolonged treatment and can progress to perforation, fistulation, abscess or haemorrhage; patients with diverticular disease may be at particular risk of fistula or perforation [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
  - Concomitant corticosteroid or NSAID (including aspirin) use has been associated with gastrointestinal perforation/haemorrhage; use with caution when combining these agents [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Sections 4.4; 4.5].
  - Eye ulcerations (conjunctival and corneal) have been reported; patients should be counselled about ocular symptoms and monitored; discontinue nicorandil if ulceration occurs [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
  - Mouth ulceration/stomatitis/persistent buccal ulceration: discontinue drug and manage appropriately if these occur [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
  - Heart failure: use with caution in patients with NYHA class III or IV due to limited data [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
  - Blood pressure effects: use cautiously in patients with low systolic blood pressure (e.g., <100 mm Hg), hypovolaemia or conditions predisposing to hypotension; nicorandil may potentiate antihypertensive agents [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
  - Glaucoma: use with caution [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
  - Hepatic impairment: nicorandil is primarily metabolised in the liver; minor PK changes were observed in cirrhosis but severe liver disease may require dose reduction — cannot be excluded [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
  - Renal impairment: no dosage adjustment is necessary based on the available PK data, as nicorandil disposition was not significantly altered by reduced GFR in the studied groups [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
  - Hyperkalaemia: nicorandil has been associated with hyperkalaemia (rarely severe); use with care when co-administered with agents that raise potassium, especially in moderate–severe renal impairment [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
  - G6PD deficiency: use cautiously because organic nitrate metabolism may form nitrites and could precipitate methemoglobinemia in G6PD-deficient patients [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- Monitoring requirements and actions:
  - Prompt recognition and withdrawal of nicorandil if ulceration (GI, ocular, oral, skin) occurs; consider ENT/ophthalmology/gastroenterology referral as clinically indicated [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
  - Monitor blood pressure and signs of hypotension, especially when used with other vasodilators or antihypertensives [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Sections 4.4; 4.5].
  - Monitor potassium in patients receiving concomitant agents that increase potassium, particularly with renal impairment [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
  - Laboratory safety monitoring: the provided PI states no specific laboratory monitoring data available; transient abnormalities could have been missed in trials [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 5.1].

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - Cimetidine (400 mg twice daily for 7 days) did not significantly change nicorandil PK and dose adjustment may not be necessary [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
  - Rifampicin (600 mg/day) did not significantly modify nicorandil pharmacokinetics except for slight decrease of t1/2ß; dose adjustment may not be necessary [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- Pharmacodynamic and clinically significant interactions:
  - Nitrate-containing agents: additive hypotensive effects possible due to nicorandil’s nitrate moiety; use caution [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
  - Phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (e.g., sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil): concomitant use is contraindicated because of potentiation of hypotension [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Sections 4.3; 4.5].
  - Soluble guanylate cyclase stimulators (e.g., riociguat): concomitant use is contraindicated due to risk of serious fall in blood pressure [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Sections 4.3; 4.5].
  - Corticosteroids and NSAIDs (including aspirin): concomitant use has been associated with gastrointestinal perforation/haemorrhage; use with caution [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Sections 4.4; 4.5].
  - Warfarin/acenocoumarol: co-administration did not affect anticoagulation effect in the studies cited [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
  - Beta-blockers, calcium antagonists, digoxin, digoxin/furosemide: no pharmacological or PK interaction observed in animal or clinical studies referenced [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
  - Tricyclic antidepressants, other antihypertensive drugs, alcohol: nicorandil may potentiate their effects (possibility not excluded) — use caution [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
  - Dapoxetine: prescribe with caution due to possible reduced orthostatic tolerance when combined with nicorandil [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.5].
- Management notes:
  - Avoid contraindicated combinations (PDE5 inhibitors, sGC stimulators). Monitor blood pressure when initiating or combining with other vasodilators or antihypertensives. Consider GI risk when combining with corticosteroids/NSAIDs. Monitor potassium if combining with agents that increase potassium. [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Sections 4.3–4.5].

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Fertility:
  - Animal data: Nicorandil did not affect fertility in male and female rats at oral doses up to 100 mg/kg/day [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.6].
- Pregnancy:
  - Pregnancy category: Category B3 [Australian classification] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.6].
  - Human data: Nicorandil has not been studied in pregnant women; animal studies show no teratogenicity but increased pre-implantation loss at 40 mg/kg/day and increased fetal mortality at 100 mg/kg/day in rats; use in pregnancy only if considered essential by the physician [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.6].
- Breastfeeding:
  - It is not known whether nicorandil is excreted in human milk; animal studies showed increased perinatal mortality at 50 mg/kg/day; nicorandil is not recommended during breastfeeding [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.6].

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Not recommended for use in children; safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Sections 4.2; 4.4].
- Elderly use:
  - No dosage adjustment required based on PK studies in elderly vs younger adults; however, as with all medicines, the lowest effective dose should be used [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Sections 4.2; 4.4].
- Renal impairment:
  - In PK studies across GFR groups, renal impairment did not significantly alter disposition of nicorandil; no dosage adjustment necessary based on available data [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- Hepatic impairment:
  - In cirrhotic patients, oral AUC was less and t½ longer compared with controls (AUC after oral dosing was less; t½ 1.6 h vs 1.1 h for controls in the cited study); nicorandil is primarily metabolised in the liver, and dose reduction in patients with severe liver disease cannot be excluded [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].
- Cardiac failure:
  - Use with caution in patients with heart failure NYHA III–IV due to limited data [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

## 11. Pharmacology
- Mechanism of action:
  - Nicorandil exerts anti-anginal effects via a dual mechanism: opening ATP-sensitive potassium (KATP) channels causing arterial vasodilation and decreased afterload; and via its nitrate moiety increasing intracellular cyclic GMP leading to venous dilation and decreased preload. It has direct effects on coronary arteries and spasmolytic activity [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 5.1].
- Pharmacodynamics:
  - Improves myocardial oxygen balance and increases post-stenotic myocardial blood flow; does not have direct negative inotropic effects at therapeutic doses; activation of vascular KATP channels causes smooth muscle relaxation [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 5.1].
- Pharmacokinetics:
  - Absorption: Rapid oral absorption with Tmax about 30–60 minutes; absolute bioavailability ~75% [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 5.2].
  - Dose proportionality: Plasma concentrations and AUC are proportional to dose across 5–40 mg [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 5.2].
  - Distribution, metabolism and elimination: Nicorandil is primarily metabolised in the liver; renal impairment does not significantly alter nicorandil disposition; specific clearance and half-life parameters are documented in the PI (see hepatic impairment notes) [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Sections 5.1; 5.2].
  - Effects in special populations (hepatic cirrhosis): oral AUC was less and t½ longer in cirrhotic patients vs controls in a cited study; clinical implications require caution for severe hepatic disease [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 4.4].

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Summary of clinical efficacy evidence reported in the source PI:
  - Exercise tolerance studies: Nicorandil 10–20 mg twice daily provided antianginal efficacy comparable to several other anti-anginal agents (diltiazem, nifedipine, isosorbide mononitrate/dinitrate, propranolol, metoprolol, atenolol) in trials generally ≤3 months and in patients with relatively frequent angina (<5 attacks/week) [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 5.1].
  - Combination therapy: Trend toward increased anti-anginal efficacy when nicorandil was added to β-blockers or calcium channel blockers, though not statistically significant in cited comparisons [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 5.1].
  - Vasospastic angina: Nicorandil at least as effective as nifedipine in preventing coronary spasm in a provocation test (methylergometrine) [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 5.1].
  - Long-term data: Uncontrolled long-term studies suggest maintained efficacy without tolerance up to 2 years; data on myocardial infarction and mortality effects are limited [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 5.1].
- Trial design and primary outcomes: Specific pivotal trial names, sizes, and primary endpoints are not detailed beyond the summaries above in the provided source [Australian] [Source: TGA PI, Section 5.1].
