# Editorial Product Information: Triamcinolone Acetonide, Nystatin, Neomycin Sulfate, and Gramicidin

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination topical therapy containing triamcinolone acetonide, nystatin, neomycin sulfate, and gramicidin. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate synthesis of available regulatory information.
*   **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredients:** 
    *   Triamcinolone acetonide (synthetic corticosteroid)
    *   Nystatin (antifungal)
    *   Neomycin as sulfate (aminoglycoside antibiotic)
    *   Gramicidin (antibiotic)
*   **Brand Name(s):** Kenacomb [Australian]
*   **ATC Code:** D07CA01 (Corticosteroids, moderately potent, combinations with antibiotics)
*   **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    *   **Topical Ointment:** Each gram contains triamcinolone acetonide 1 mg (0.1%), neomycin (as sulfate) 2.5 mg, gramicidin 0.25 mg, and nystatin 100,000 IU [Australian].
*   **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical (Dermatological)
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
*   Relief of inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of dermatoses that are either infected or likely to become infected [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*   **General Dosing (Adult):** Apply a thin layer to the affected areas 2 to 3 times daily [Australian].
*   **Administration Instructions:** 
    *   For external skin use only.
    *   Avoid contact with eyes [Australian].
    *   Do not use occlusive dressings (e.g., tight-fitting bandages, plastic pants, or incontinence garments) unless directed by a physician, as this increases the risk of systemic absorption and sensitivity reactions [Australian].
    *   In inguinal areas, the preparation should be applied sparingly with loose-fitting clothing worn over the site [Australian].
*   **Missed Dose Instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to triamcinolone acetonide, nystatin, neomycin, or gramicidin [Australian].
*   Ophthalmic use [Australian].
*   Tuberculous lesions of the skin [Australian].
*   Viral skin lesions, specifically Herpes simplex, vaccinia, and varicella [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **General Warnings:** If irritation or sensitivity develops, therapy must be discontinued. Note that corticosteroids may mask hypersensitivity reactions to the anti-infective components [Australian].
*   **Nephrotoxicity and Ototoxicity:** Due to the potential for systemic absorption of neomycin, this preparation should not be used on patients with extensive skin damage or in conditions where significant absorption is likely [Australian].
*   **Superinfection:** Prolonged use may lead to the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi other than *Candida*. If superinfection occurs, therapy should be discontinued and appropriate antimicrobial treatment initiated [Australian].
*   **Systemic Steroid Effects:** Reversible hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression, Cushing’s syndrome, hyperglycaemia, and glucosuria may occur, particularly with high doses, use over large surface areas, or prolonged treatment. If HPA suppression is detected, withdrawal should be attempted or the frequency of application reduced [Australian].
*   **Visual Disturbance:** Both systemic and topical corticosteroid use have been associated with visual disturbances, including blurred vision, cataracts, glaucoma, or rare conditions such as central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR). Patients reporting these symptoms should be referred to an ophthalmologist [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally reported as infrequent or uncommon. 

*   **Local/Dermatological (Infrequent):** Burning, itching, irritation, dryness, folliculitis, hypertrichosis, acneform eruptions, hypopigmentation, perioral dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis, skin maceration, secondary infection, skin atrophy, striae, and miliaria [Australian].
*   **Sensitivity:** Delayed-type hypersensitivity and sensitisation have been reported with neomycin, particularly after prolonged use. Irritation and contact dermatitis may occur with nystatin [Australian].
*   **Systemic Effects:** 
    *   **Neomycin:** Ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity (when applied to large surfaces or damaged skin) [Australian].
    *   **Corticosteroid:** HPA axis suppression (see Section 5 and 9) [Australian].
*   **Post-marketing:** Blurred vision [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No specific data available in the source documentation [Australian].
*   **Laboratory Tests:** Lack of therapeutic response may necessitate repeating KOH smears or cultures. Urinary free cortisol and ACTH stimulation tests are useful for evaluating HPA axis suppression [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. 
    *   Aminoglycosides (such as neomycin) cross the placenta. Evidence suggests selective uptake by the fetal kidney and potential damage to immature nephrons. Eighth cranial nerve damage has been reported with in-utero exposure to aminoglycosides. Corticosteroids should only be used if the potential benefit justifies the risk [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if topical administration results in significant systemic absorption into breast milk. While systemically administered steroids are secreted into breast milk, they are unlikely to affect the infant at normal doses. Caution is advised when prescribing to nursing women [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies regarding carcinogenic or mutagenic potential or impairment of fertility have not been conducted [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** 
    *   Children are more susceptible to HPA axis suppression and Cushing’s syndrome due to a higher skin surface area to body weight ratio. 
    *   Reported effects include linear growth retardation, delayed weight gain, and intracranial hypertension (manifesting as bulging fontanelles, headaches, and bilateral papilloedema). 
    *   Treatment should be limited to the minimum amount and shortest duration required for efficacy [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
*   **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though the risk of neomycin-induced nephrotoxicity is noted (see Section 5).

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:**
    *   **Triamcinolone acetonide:** A topical corticosteroid with anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties [Australian].
    *   **Nystatin:** An antifungal that binds to steroids in the fungal cell membrane, altering permeability and causing leakage of intracellular components. It is active against *Candida albicans* [Australian].
    *   **Neomycin:** An aminoglycoside that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis; active against various gram-negative organisms (excluding *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*) [Australian].
    *   **Gramicidin:** An antibiotic that increases cell membrane permeability in many gram-positive organisms [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Corticosteroids can be absorbed through intact skin, with absorption increased by inflammation or occlusive dressings. Neomycin is absorbed through inflamed skin. Nystatin and gramicidin are not absorbed through intact skin or mucous membranes [Australian].
    *   **Distribution:** Absorbed corticosteroids bind to plasma proteins in varying degrees [Australian].
    *   **Metabolism/Elimination:** Corticosteroids are metabolised in the liver and excreted by the kidneys and bile. Absorbed neomycin is excreted unchanged by the kidneys with a half-life of 2 to 3 hours [Australian].
