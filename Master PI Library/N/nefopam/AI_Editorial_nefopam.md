# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, professional reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [United States].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should be every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less frequent than every 6 hours (q6h).
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [United States].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations, typically starting as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Treatment should be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before initiating parenteral therapy.
    - Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [United States].
    - Monitor INR in patients receiving concomitant warfarin therapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting. In hemodialysis patients, frequently reported events (regardless of causality) include hypertension (18-21%), diarrhea (9-35%), headache (3-37%), and injection site reactions (27-38%) [United States].
- **Less Common (1-10%):** Body odor, gastritis, abdominal pain, asthenia, fever, and pharyngitis [United States].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
    - Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
    - Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not explicitly lettered in current labeling; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.]
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels. The benefits of supplementation to the mother should be weighed against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 [United States].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided source [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normal plasma free carnitine levels are 40–50 µmol/L; levels below 20 µmol/L are defined as deficiency [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean trough levocarnitine levels increased from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While the increase in concentrations is proven, the effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC plastic bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [United States].
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [United States].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available US sources.
