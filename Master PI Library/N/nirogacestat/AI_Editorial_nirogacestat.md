# Editorial Product Information: Nirogacestat

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- At the time of this publication, official PI documents from other major jurisdictions (e.g., Australian (TGA), European (EMA), Canadian (Health Canada)) were not provided for comparison.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, legally approved PI in a specific jurisdiction when prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nirogacestat (as nirogacestat hydrobromide).
- **Chemical Class:** Gamma secretase inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **OGSIVEO** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral tablets:** 100 mg and 150 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** SpringWorks Therapeutics, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Desmoid Tumors:** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with progressing desmoid tumors who require systemic treatment [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 150 mg administered orally twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - May be taken with or without food.
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not break, crush, or chew.
    - If a patient vomits or misses a dose, the next dose should be taken at its regularly scheduled time; do not double the dose.
- **Dose Modifications for Adverse Reactions:**
    - **General Strategy:** For Grade 3 or 4 adverse reactions (or persistent intolerable Grade 2), withhold treatment until resolved to Grade ≤1 or baseline. Treatment may be resumed at a reduced dose of 100 mg twice daily [US].
    - **Diarrhea (Grade 3/4):** Withhold until resolved, then resume at 100 mg twice daily.
    - **Hepatotoxicity (ALT/AST >3x to 5x ULN):** Withhold until resolved to <3x ULN, then resume at 100 mg twice daily.
    - **Hepatotoxicity (ALT/AST >5x ULN):** Permanently discontinue [US].
    - **Electrolyte Abnormalities (Grade 3/4 Hypophosphatemia or Hypokalemia):** Withhold until resolved, then resume at 100 mg twice daily.
- **Concomitant Gastric Acid Reducing Agents:**
    - Avoid proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) and H2-receptor antagonists.
    - If an antacid is required, stagger administration (2 hours before or 2 hours after the **OGSIVEO** dose) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None:** There are no listed contraindications in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Diarrhea:** Highly common (84%) and can be severe (Grade 3 in 16%). Median onset is 9 days. Monitor patients and treat with anti-diarrheal medications promptly [US].
- **Ovarian Toxicity:** May impair female reproductive function and fertility. In clinical trials, 75% of females of reproductive potential experienced ovarian toxicity. Effects may include premature menopause and amenorrhea. Monitor for symptoms of estrogen deficiency [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** ALT or AST elevations occur frequently. Monitor liver function tests regularly [US].
- **Non-Melanoma Skin Cancers:** Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (2.9%) and basal cell carcinoma (1.4%) have occurred. Perform dermatologic examinations prior to initiation and routinely during treatment [US].
- **Electrolyte Abnormalities:** Decreased phosphate (65%) and potassium (22%) are common. Monitor levels regularly and supplement as needed [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Based on its mechanism of action and animal data, nirogacestat can cause fetal harm [US].
- **Pediatric Growth Plate Risk:** Epiphyseal disorder (widening of the growth plate) has been reported in pediatric patients with open growth plates [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong/Moderate CYP3A Inhibitors:** Avoid concomitant use. Strong inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole) significantly increase nirogacestat exposure (AUC), increasing the risk of toxicity. Avoid grapefruit, Seville oranges, and starfruit [US].
- **Strong/Moderate CYP3A Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use. Strong inducers (e.g., rifampin) significantly decrease nirogacestat exposure, potentially reducing efficacy [US].
- **Gastric Acid Reducing Agents:** PPIs and H2-blockers decrease nirogacestat concentrations and should be avoided. Stagger antacids by 2 hours [US].
- **Effect of Nirogacestat on Other Drugs:**
    - **CYP3A Substrates:** Nirogacestat increases exposure of CYP3A substrates (e.g., midazolam). Avoid use with substrates where small concentration changes lead to serious toxicity [US].
    - **CYP2C19 Substrates:** Nirogacestat decreases exposure of CYP2C19 substrates, potentially reducing their efficacy [US].
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Nirogacestat may reduce the effectiveness of hormonal contraceptives. Advise the use of an additional barrier method [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** No lettered category provided (current US labeling). 
    - **Summary:** Can cause fetal harm. Animal data show embryo-fetal death and toxicity at exposures below the human recommended dose. Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential prior to initiation [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on presence in human milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for **1 week** after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate potential for impaired fertility in both males and females (ovarian atrophy, reduced testes weight, decreased sperm motility) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established. Use is associated with a risk of epiphyseal disorder in patients with open growth plates [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65 and over (only 4% of trial population) to determine if they respond differently than younger adults [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically significant PK differences were observed in patients with mild or moderate renal impairment (eGFR ≥41 mL/min/1.73m²). Data are unavailable for severe impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for patients with mild or moderate (Child-Pugh B) hepatic impairment. The effect of severe hepatic impairment is unknown [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Nirogacestat is a selective gamma secretase inhibitor. It blocks the proteolytic activation of the Notch receptor, which, when dysregulated, can drive the growth of desmoid tumors [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Higher exposure is associated with an increased risk of Grade 3 hypophosphatemia. No clinically significant QTc prolongation was observed at the recommended dose [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 19%. Median Tmax is 1.5 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 99.6% protein bound (primarily to albumin and alpha-1 acid glycoprotein).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP3A4 (85%).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 23 hours. Excreted mainly in feces (38%) and urine (17%, with <1% unchanged) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **DeFi Trial:** A phase 3, international, multicenter, randomized (1:1), double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 142 adult patients with progressing desmoid tumors.
- **Primary Outcome:** Progression-free survival (PFS).
- **Results:** Nirogacestat demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in PFS compared to placebo (HR 0.29; 95% CI: 0.15, 0.55; p<0.001). The Objective Response Rate (ORR) was 41% for nirogacestat versus 8% for placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Excursions:** Permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** Supplied in blister cards containing 14 tablets [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only. 
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
