# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 
*   *Note: While the user instructions referenced a document for Netupitant/Palonosetron, the source material provided for analysis was exclusively for **Levocarnitine Injection, USP**. This editorial reflects the provided Levocarnitine data.*

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP)
*   **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport; (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US]
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection, solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Treatment of acute and chronic deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    *   **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    *   **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** Starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    *   **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    *   **Adjustments:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels, as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
*   Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable when mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL. Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].

### Monitoring
*   **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** Prior to therapy, obtain baseline plasma concentrations. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended. The target plasma free carnitine concentration should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of injection. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
*   **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
*   **Oral Formulation Precaution:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High doses of the oral formulation in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% regardless of causality) include [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Frequency (≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain | Common |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations | Common |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, dyspepsia, anorexia | Common |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema | Common |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression | Common |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, rhinitis, dyspnea | Common |
| **Skin** | Pruritus | Common |

*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, seizures [US].
*   **Other reported effects:** Transient nausea/vomiting, body odor, and gastritis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned - "Use only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
*   **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, Saccharomyces, Schizosaccharomyces) were negative [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders); specific age-range safety profiles are not further delineated in the source [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Dosing for ESRD on hemodialysis is provided (see Section 3). Oral levocarnitine is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters that are quickly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces) [US].
*   **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times per week after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
*   **Dose-Ranging:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
*   **Handling:** Vials are single-dose; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
