# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory information into a single clinical reference.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (generic).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from genetic metabolic disorders [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing chronic haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses over 24 hours. 
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours. 
    - Maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD on Haemodialysis:** 
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). 
    - Administration: Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Monitor plasma carnitine levels weekly or monthly. Target free carnitine concentrations between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **ESRD:** Initiation of therapy may be indicated if pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levels fall below 40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These may occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor for recurrence if re-administration is attempted [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine has not been established in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD. Chronic oral dosing may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** Clinical monitoring should include vital signs, blood chemistries, and plasma carnitine levels [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a letter grade in current labeling, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation has not been specifically studied in nursing mothers. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. Benefits to the mother should be weighed against potential risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg) as described in Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source; general caution regarding dose selection is standard for this population.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Trough level monitoring is required for dose adjustment. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV administration of 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) plasma carnitine levels from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion as the product contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL at room temperature [US].
