# Editorial Product Information: Nitrous Oxide

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis intended for use by healthcare professionals. It provides a clinically accurate overview of the properties, administration, and safety profile of **nitrous oxide**.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI used for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Nitrous oxide** (N2O)
- **Chemical Class:** Inhalational anaesthetic gas
- **Brand Names:** BOC Gases Nitrous Oxide [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** N01AX13
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Compressed medicinal gas, 99.0% v/v (minimum 98.0% v/v) [Australian]
- **Routes of Administration:** Inhalation
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** BOC Gases Australia Limited [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **General Anaesthesia:** Indicated in adults and children as an adjuvant to other volatile or intravenous anaesthetics [Australian].
- **Analgesia:** Indicated in adults and children for analgesic use (when administered with oxygen), such as in dentistry and obstetrics [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **General Anaesthesia:** Administered via inhalation using suitable anaesthetic apparatus in concentrations up to 70%, with oxygen as the balance. It should not be used as a sole agent due to risks of hypoxia and inadequate depth of anaesthesia [Australian].
- **Analgesia:** Administered via a suitable face mask in concentrations up to 50%, with oxygen as the balance [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- Dosing must be individualised for children. The potential for increased sedation and compromised protective reflexes must be considered in this population [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Oxygen Requirements:** Must never be administered with less than 21% oxygen; in anaesthetic practice, a minimum of 30% oxygen is required [Australian].
- **Ventilation:** Must only be administered in rooms with proper ventilation and/or scavenging equipment to maintain ambient levels below 25 ppm [Australian].
- **Diffusion Hypoxia:** To prevent diffusion hypoxia at the conclusion of administration, nitrous oxide should be washed out with oxygen, and 100% oxygen provided via face mask for at least 20 minutes during recovery [Australian].
- **Personnel:** Should only be administered by medical personnel trained in the appropriate techniques [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Oxygen Levels:** Administration without at least 30% oxygen [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to **nitrous oxide** or any component of the gas [Australian].
- **Air Entrapment/Expansion:** Any condition where air is trapped in a body cavity and expansion would be dangerous, including:
    - Intracranial air, severe head trauma, or air encephalography [Australian].
    - Pneumothorax (artificial, traumatic, or spontaneous) [Australian].
    - Pneumopericardium [Australian].
    - Air or gas embolus [Australian].
    - Decompression sickness or following a recent dive [Australian].
    - Severe bullous emphysema [Australian].
    - During myringoplasty or in cases of an occluded middle ear [Australian].
    - Cysts or gross abdominal distension [Australian].
    - Maxillofacial injuries [Australian].
    - Cardiopulmonary bypass [Australian].
    - Within specific timeframes following intraocular gas injection (e.g., SF6 or C3F8) until the gas is completely absorbed, to avoid potential blindness [Australian].
- **Metabolic/Systemic:**
    - Vitamin B12 or folic acid deficiency, or genetic perturbations in these systems [Australian].
    - Cardiac failure or severely impaired cardiac function [Australian].
    - Intoxicated patients [Australian].
    - Persistent signs of confusion or increased intracranial pressure [Australian].
    - Decreased consciousness or co-operability when used for analgesia [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Abuse and Addiction:** Cases of addiction and abuse have been reported. Withdrawal may cause delirium [Australian].
- **Vitamin B12 Inactivation:** **Nitrous oxide** inactivates vitamin B12, interfering with methionine synthesis and DNA formation. Prolonged use (exceeding 6 hours) or frequent use (more than once every 4 days) requires clinical supervision and haematological monitoring for megaloblastic changes and leukopenia [Australian].
- **Occupational Exposure:** Chronic exposure in inadequately ventilated rooms may lead to reduced fertility and neurological or bone marrow impairment in medical personnel [Australian].
- **Pressure Effects:** **Nitrous oxide** passes into gas-containing spaces faster than nitrogen passes out, leading to increased pressure in middle ears, bowel distension, and increased pressure in tracheal tube/catheter balloons [Australian].
- **Hypotension:** Use with caution in patients with severe hypotension [Australian].
- **Fire Risk:** Do not use during laser surgery in the airways due to the risk of explosive fire [Australian].
- **Cold Burns:** Contact with evaporating liquid can cause cold burns to the skin and eyes [Australian].

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Bone marrow depression, severe megaloblastic anaemia (in some cases fatal), and subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord following prolonged or repeated use [Australian].
- Diffusion hypoxia and asphyxia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Methotrexate:** Concomitant use should be avoided. **Nitrous oxide** potentiates the effects of methotrexate on folate metabolism, potentially leading to severe, unpredictable myelosuppression, stomatitis, and neurotoxicity [Australian].
- **Volatile Anaesthetics:** **Nitrous oxide** reduces the MAC (Minimum Alveolar Concentration) of other volatile agents by up to 50% [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants (e.g., Benzodiazepines, Morphine):** Concomitant use may increase sedation, respiratory depression, and hypotensive effects [Australian].
- **Opioids:** Concurrent use, especially high-dose fentanyl, may lead to circulatory depression, decreased heart rate, and decreased cardiac output [Australian].
- **Second Gas Effect:** The rapid uptake of **nitrous oxide** from the lungs accelerates the uptake of concomitantly administered inhalational anaesthetics [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category A:** Drugs which have been taken by a large number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age without any proven increase in the frequency of malformations or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the fetus having been observed [Australian].
    - Inhibition of methionine synthase may cause adverse effects during early pregnancy. Animal studies have shown teratogenic effects with high concentrations/prolonged exposure [Australian].
    - Chronic occupational exposure for operating room personnel may increase the risk of spontaneous abortion [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if the drug is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Reduced fecundity has been reported in female dental assistants and midwives exposed to unscavenged or high levels of **nitrous oxide** [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use with caution. Animal studies suggest that anaesthetic agents (including those blocking NMDA receptors) used for longer than 3 hours during periods of rapid brain growth may result in neuronal loss and cognitive deficits. The clinical significance in humans is undetermined [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** These patients are more susceptible to the effects of **nitrous oxide** [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Nitrous oxide** is a weak inhalational anaesthetic and potent analgesic. It blocks NMDA receptors and potentially influences GABA activity [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset and recovery are rapid. MAC is >100%. Analgesia is typically achieved at 25% concentration; unconsciousness usually requires 70% [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed via inhalation [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Low blood:gas partition coefficient (0.47) allows for rapid rise in alveolar concentration [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Rapidly eliminated, primarily via the lungs [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No data provided in the available source [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 45°C in a dry, well-ventilated area away from sources of ignition [Australian].
- **Handling:** Store cylinders upright. Ensure hands and equipment are free of oils, greases, and tarry substances due to the risk of spontaneous combustion [Australian].
- **Disposal:** Cylinders must be returned to the supplier [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Restricted Medicine) [Australian].
