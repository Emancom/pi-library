# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference only. It does not replace the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (L-carnitine); chemical class: carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Subsequent daily maintenance doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised as clinically required [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). 
    - Administration should occur as a slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels <40–50 µmol/L.

### Administration and Titration
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended.
- **Dose Adjustments:** In dialysis patients, downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough carnitine levels [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These can occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued and appropriate medical intervention initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients both with and without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a known seizure disorder, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been reported [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In patients with renal impairment, chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine (not specifically the IV formulation) may lead to the accumulation of metabolites trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are potentially toxic [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event frequency is often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.
- **Common (1–10%):** Nausea, vomiting, and transient abdominal pain.
- **General Disorders:** Body odor, asthenia, fever, injection site reactions (33%), and pain.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia, and palpitations.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, diarrhea (16%), and dyspepsia.
- **Neurological:** Headache (22%), dizziness (13%), and paresthesia.
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase, and dyspnea.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%) and hypervolemia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/letter-system retired]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response, use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after administration. The benefits of maternal carnitine must be weighed against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders follows the same weight-based guidelines (50 mg/kg) as adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources; however, dose selection should generally be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. IV levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis, but monitoring for metabolite accumulation is advised [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It acts as a carrier molecule that facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine clears accumulated acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is then rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** In a study of 12 ESRD patients, a 20 mg/kg IV dose three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing. 
- **Handling:** Vials are single-dose and contain no preservative; discard any unused portion after opening [US].
