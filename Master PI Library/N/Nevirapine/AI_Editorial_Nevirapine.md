# Editorial Product Information: Nevirapine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of nevirapine for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nevirapine (a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor [NNRTI]).
- **Brand Names:** Nevirapine Viatris [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AG01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 200 mg uncoated tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd trading as Viatris [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated for the treatment of HIV-1 infection in adults, always in combination with at least two other antiretroviral agents [Australian].
- **Note:** Resistance emerges rapidly if nevirapine is used as monotherapy or in dual-agent regimens [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Initial Dosing (Lead-in Period):** 200 mg once daily for the first 14 days. This lead-in period is mandatory to reduce the incidence of rash [Australian].
- **Maintenance Dosing:** 200 mg twice daily, in combination with at least two additional antiretroviral agents [Australian].
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Lead-in Interruption:** 
    - If a rash occurs during the 14-day lead-in, do not escalate the dose until the rash resolves.
    - If the lead-in dose (200 mg once daily) is continued beyond 28 days, an alternative regimen must be sought.
    - If nevirapine is interrupted for >7 days, the lead-in schedule must be restarted [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Patients should take the missed dose as soon as possible, but must not double the next dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Clinically significant hypersensitivity to nevirapine or any excipients [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe hepatic dysfunction (Child-Pugh Class C) or pre-treatment AST/ALT >5x Upper Limit of Normal (ULN) until baseline levels stabilise <5x ULN [Australian].
- **Re-administration Bans:** Must not be reintroduced in patients who previously discontinued nevirapine for:
    - Severe rash or rash with constitutional symptoms.
    - Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) or toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN).
    - Hypersensitivity reactions or clinical hepatitis due to nevirapine.
    - Recurrence of liver abnormalities upon previous re-challenge [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance, total lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption should not take this medicine (contains lactose) [Australian].
- **Concomitant Medications:** St John’s Wort must not be used concurrently due to risk of decreased nevirapine concentrations [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources, though intensive monitoring is mandated for the first 18 weeks.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** The first 18 weeks are critical. Serious, life-threatening, and fatal hepatotoxicity (including fulminant hepatitis) has occurred. Risk is highest in the first 6 weeks but persists throughout therapy [Australian].
- **Cutaneous Reactions:** Severe skin reactions, including fatal SJS and TEN, occur most frequently in the first 6 weeks. Any rash with constitutional symptoms (fever, blistering, oral lesions) requires immediate and permanent discontinuation [Australian].
- **CD4+ Threshold Risks:** Risk of symptomatic hepatic events is significantly higher in treatment-naïve patients with higher CD4+ counts at initiation:
    - **Adult Females:** >250 cells/mm³ [Australian].
    - **Adult Males:** >400 cells/mm³ [Australian].
    - Nevirapine should only be initiated in these patients if the benefits clearly outweigh the risks.
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Perform Liver Function Tests (LFTs) prior to initiation and at frequent intervals (e.g., every 2 weeks for the first month, then monthly) [Australian].
    - Monitor more frequently if AST/ALT >2.5x ULN.
- **Immune Reactivation Syndrome:** May occur during the initial phase of combination treatment, leading to inflammatory responses to residual opportunistic infections [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** 
    - Rash (occurring in approximately 9% of patients in combination regimens) [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Granulocytopenia.
    - Hypersensitivity (angioedema, urticaria, anaphylaxis).
    - Headache.
    - Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea.
    - Hepatitis (1.2%), abnormal LFTs (ALT, AST, GGT, bilirubin).
    - Myalgia.
    - Fatigue, pyrexia [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):**
    - Anaemia.
    - SJS (0.3%), angioedema.
    - Arthralgia.
    - Jaundice [Australian].
- **Rare (<0.1%):**
    - Liver failure/fulminant hepatitis (may be fatal).
    - TEN (may be fatal) [Australian].
- **Post-marketing:** Peripheral neuropathy, paraesthesia, and redistribution of body fat (lipodystrophy) have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Profile:** Nevirapine is an inducer of CYP3A and CYP2B. It may significantly decrease plasma concentrations of drugs metabolised by these enzymes [Australian].
- **Contraindicated/Not Recommended:**
    - **NNRTIs:** Efavirenz, etravirine, rilpivirine (co-administration not recommended).
    - **Antifungals:** Ketoconazole (not recommended).
    - **Antibiotics:** Rifampicin (not recommended; consider rifabutin).
    - **Antivirals:** Elvitegravir/cobicistat.
    - **Protease Inhibitors (without Ritonavir):** Fosamprenavir, saquinavir, atazanavir [Australian].
- **Management Required:**
    - **Methadone:** May trigger narcotic withdrawal; monitor and increase methadone dose as needed.
    - **Warfarin:** Complex interaction; monitor coagulation levels closely.
    - **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Nevirapine may decrease effectiveness of oral/implanted hormones. Use of Depo-medroxyprogesterone acetate (DMPA) is acceptable without adjustment, but other hormonal methods should not be used as the sole method of contraception [Australian].
    - **Protease Inhibitors (PIs):** Lopinavir/ritonavir dose may need to be increased to 533/133 mg (4 capsules) twice daily [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** B3 [Australian].
- **Clinical Data:** Data from the Antiretroviral Pregnancy Registry indicate no increased risk of major birth defects or foeto/neonatal toxicity. Nevirapine readily crosses the placenta [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Nevirapine is excreted in breast milk. It is generally recommended that HIV-1 infected women do not breastfeed to avoid postnatal transmission of HIV-1 [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Impaired fertility was observed in female rats at doses equivalent to human exposure. No human data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing and approval not detailed for children in this source; refers to adult dosing (16 years and older) [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics do not appear to change with age (range 19–68 years). Monitor carefully as for all adults [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **CLcr ≥20 mL/min:** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Haemodialysis:** An additional 200 mg dose is recommended following each dialysis session to offset increased clearance [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild/Moderate (Child-Pugh A/B):** Use with caution.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Nevirapine binds directly to HIV-1 reverse transcriptase (RT) and blocks RNA-dependent and DNA-dependent DNA polymerase activities by disrupting the enzyme's catalytic site. It does not inhibit HIV-2 RT or eukaryotic DNA polymerases [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed (>90%); peak concentrations at 4 hours. Not affected by food or antacids.
    - **Distribution:** Highly lipophilic; crosses the blood-brain barrier (CSF levels ~45% of plasma). Protein binding ~60%.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively biotransformed via CYP3A and CYP2B isoenzymes.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (81.3% as glucuronidated metabolites); terminal half-life decreases from ~45 hours to ~25–30 hours after autoinduction [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ACTG 241:** Triple therapy (Nevirapine + AZT + ddI) showed significant benefit over double therapy (AZT + ddI) in patients with prior nucleoside therapy regarding CD4+ counts and viral DNA.
- **ACTG 193a:** Demonstrated that nevirapine-based triple therapy significantly delayed HIV progression or death compared to dual therapy (82 weeks vs 62 weeks).
- **BI Trial 1046:** In treatment-naïve patients, nevirapine + AZT + ddI showed significantly better virologic response (45% vs 6%) compared to AZT + ddI alone [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Packaging:** Store in the original container (HDPE bottle) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
