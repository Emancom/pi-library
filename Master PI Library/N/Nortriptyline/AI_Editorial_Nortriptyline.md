# Editorial Product Information: Nortriptyline

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates information from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for healthcare professionals to provide a clear, concise, and clinically accurate summary of nortriptyline product information.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial consolidation and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current PI for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nortriptyline hydrochloride (Tricyclic antidepressant).
- **Brand Names:** Allegron [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets:** 10 mg (white, round, plain) and 25 mg (orange, round, scored) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depression:** Treatment of major depression [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended usual dose is 25 mg administered three or four times daily. 
- **Titration:** Therapy should be initiated at a low dosage and increased gradually based on clinical response and tolerability.
- **Maximum Dose:** Dosages exceeding 100 mg per day are not recommended [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** Following clinical remission, medication may be required for an extended period at the lowest effective dose to maintain stability.
- **Administration Instructions:** If minor adverse reactions occur, the dosage should be reduced. Serious adverse effects or allergic manifestations require prompt discontinuation.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concurrent use is contraindicated. A minimum of 14 days must elapse after discontinuing an MAOI before initiating nortriptyline to avoid hyperpyretic crises, severe convulsions, and fatalities.
- **Myocardial Infarction:** Use is contraindicated during the acute recovery period following a myocardial infarction.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to nortriptyline.
- **Cross-sensitivity:** Potential cross-sensitivity exists with other dibenzazepine-class tricyclic antidepressants.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidality and Clinical Worsening:** Depression is associated with an inherent risk of suicide. Patients of all ages should be monitored for clinical worsening, suicidal ideation, or unusual behavioural changes, particularly during the first few months of therapy or following dose adjustments.
- **Brugada Syndrome:** Nortriptyline may unmask Brugada syndrome (a hereditary sodium channelopathy). It should generally be avoided in patients with a known or suspected family history of Brugada syndrome, cardiac arrest, or sudden death.
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Use with extreme caution under close supervision due to the risk of sinus tachycardia, prolonged conduction time, arrhythmias, and stroke.
- **Bipolar Disorder/Mania:** Antidepressant monotherapy may precipitate a mixed or manic episode in patients at risk for bipolar disorder. Patients should be screened for bipolar risk factors prior to treatment.
- **Seizure Threshold:** Nortriptyline is known to lower the convulsive threshold; patients with a history of seizures require close monitoring.
- **Anticholinergic Effects:** Use with caution in patients with glaucoma or a history of urinary retention.
- **Hyperthyroidism:** Great care is required in hyperthyroid patients or those on thyroid medication due to increased risk of cardiac arrhythmias.
- **Psychiatric Effects:** Use in schizophrenic patients may exacerbate psychosis or activate latent symptoms. Overactive or agitated patients may experience increased anxiety.
- **Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT):** Concurrent use may increase clinical hazards.
- **Surgery:** If possible, discontinue the drug several days prior to elective surgery.
- **Motor Coordination:** May impair the mental and physical abilities required for hazardous tasks (e.g., driving or operating machinery).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects common to the tricyclic antidepressant class should be considered even if not specifically reported for nortriptyline.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypotension, hypertension, tachycardia, palpitation, MI, arrhythmias, heart block, stroke, unmasking of Brugada Syndrome. |
| **Psychiatric** | Confusional states (especially in the elderly), hallucinations, disorientation, delusions, anxiety, restlessness, agitation, insomnia, panic, nightmares, hypomania, exacerbation of psychosis. |
| **Neurological** | Numbness, tingling, paraesthesias, incoordination, ataxia, tremors, peripheral neuropathy, extrapyramidal symptoms, seizures, EEG changes, tinnitus. |
| **Anticholinergic** | Dry mouth (rarely sublingual adenitis/gingivitis), blurred vision, mydriasis, constipation, paralytic ileus, urinary retention, urinary tract dilation. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, epigastric distress, diarrhoea, peculiar taste, stomatitis, abdominal cramps, black tongue. |
| **Haematologic** | Bone marrow depression (agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia), eosinophilia, purpura, thrombocytopenia. |
| **Allergic** | Skin rash, petechiae, urticaria, itching, photosensitization, oedema (face/tongue), drug fever. |
| **Endocrine/Metabolic** | Gynaecomastia (male), galactorrhoea (female), altered libido, impotence, testicular swelling, blood sugar fluctuations, SIADH, hyponatraemia. |
| **Other** | Jaundice, hepatitis, weight changes, perspiration, flushing, urinary frequency, nocturia, drowsiness, dizziness, fatigue, headache, parotid swelling, alopecia. |

**Withdrawal Symptoms:** Abrupt cessation after prolonged use may cause nausea, headache, and malaise (not indicative of addiction).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cimetidine:** May significantly fluctuate or increase nortriptyline serum levels, leading to serious anticholinergic symptoms.
- **SSRIs (e.g., Fluoxetine):** Fluoxetine may increase nortriptyline plasma levels more than twofold. Consideration must be given to the long half-life of fluoxetine metabolites during drug conversion.
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors/Substrates:** Nortriptyline is metabolised by the cytochrome P450 isoenzyme P450IID6 (CYP2D6). Co-administration with other drugs metabolised by or inhibiting this enzyme (e.g., quinidine, phenothiazines, Type 1C antiarrhythmics) requires caution and potential dose reduction.
- **Reserpine:** May produce a "stimulating" effect in depressed patients when combined with TCAs.
- **Anticholinergic and Sympathomimetic Drugs:** Close supervision and dose adjustment required.
- **Guanethidine:** Antihypertensive effects may be blocked by nortriptyline.
- **Alcohol:** Potentiating effects may occur, increasing the risk of suicide attempt or overdose.
- **Chlorpropamide:** A case of significant hypoglycaemia has been reported when nortriptyline was added to chlorpropamide therapy in a Type II diabetic patient.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C [Australian]:** Drugs which, owing to their pharmacological effects, have caused or may be suspected of causing harmful effects on the human foetus or neonate without causing malformations.
    - Safe use in pregnancy has not been established. Benefits must outweigh potential hazards.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safe use during lactation has not been established; potential benefits must be weighed against risks to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies have yielded inconclusive results regarding fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children. Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents (<18 years) for the treatment of depression have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Lower dosages (25 to 50 mg/day in divided doses) are recommended. Older patients may have higher concentrations of the active metabolite 10-hydroxy-nortriptyline, which has been associated with cardiotoxicity despite normal parent drug levels.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources (though jaundice and liver necrosis are noted as adverse effects).
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The precise mechanism of mood elevation is unknown. It is not an MAOI. It interferes with the transport, release, and storage of catecholamines. It inhibits the activity of histamine, 5-hydroxytryptamine (serotonin), and acetylcholine.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases the pressor effect of noradrenaline but blocks the pressor effect of phenethylamine.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption/Distribution/Excretion:** Not specified in available sources.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised by CYP2D6. Genetic "poor metabolisers" (3-10% of the population) will have higher than expected plasma concentrations.
    - **Therapeutic Range:** Optimal response is associated with plasma concentrations of 50 to 150 mcg/L. Levels above this may increase adverse events.
