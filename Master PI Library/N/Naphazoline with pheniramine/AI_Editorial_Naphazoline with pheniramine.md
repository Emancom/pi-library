# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the provided Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, pharmacology, and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
    - Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h); never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Up to 300 mg/kg has been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Adjustment:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; contains no preservative [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If severe, discontinue treatment and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit assessment [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with existing conditions, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### Specific Precautions
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring:** INR should be monitored in patients receiving concomitant warfarin [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% frequency, regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, palpitations, tachycardia, vascular disorder |
| **Digestive** | Anorexia, constipation, diarrhea, dyspepsia, nausea, vomiting |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight increase/decrease |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash |
| **Urogenital** | Kidney failure |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].
- **Less Frequent Effects:** Body odor and gastritis have been noted [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used with levocarnitine. **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk. **Management:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the child against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg (see Section 3). Safety and efficacy in children are established within the context of metabolic disorders [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety of oral levocarnitine not evaluated in renal failure; see Section 5 regarding metabolite accumulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US]. 

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with metabolic acidopathies, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted. This prevents the "autointoxication" caused by the accumulation of acylCoA compounds [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times per week after dialysis showed that trough levels (pre-dialysis) rose from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability (Admixture):** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL). Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Packaging:** Supplied in cartons of 10 x 5 mL single-dose vials [US].
