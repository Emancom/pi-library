# Editorial Product Information: Natalizumab

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for natalizumab (specifically the biosimilar **Tyruko**). It is designed as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals to ensure safe and effective use across different clinical settings.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a professional synthesis and does not replace the official, legally approved Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s current PI for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Natalizumab
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant humanised IgG4 monoclonal antibody produced in Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells.
- **Brand Names:** **Tyruko** [Australian] (Note: Tyruko is a biosimilar to the reference product **Tysabri**).
- **ATC Code:** L04AA23
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated solution for intravenous (IV) infusion; each 15 mL single-use vial contains 300 mg natalizumab (20 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsing Remitting Multiple Sclerosis (RRMS):** Indicated as monotherapy to delay the progression of physical disability and reduce the frequency of relapse [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 300 mg administered once every four weeks via intravenous infusion [Australian].
- **Preparation:** 
    - Withdraw 15 mL of concentrate and dilute in 100 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride.
    - Gently invert to mix; do **not** shake.
    - Use only 0.9% Sodium Chloride as the diluent [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer over approximately one hour.
    - Do **not** administer as a bolus or push.
    - Post-infusion, flush the line with 0.9% Sodium Chloride.
    - Use of filtration devices has not been evaluated [Australian].
- **Monitoring:**
    - Observe patients during the infusion and for one hour thereafter for signs of hypersensitivity.
    - After the first 12 doses, subsequent post-infusion observation should be based on clinical judgment [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not explicitly detailed; however, regular 4-week intervals are recommended to maintain efficacy and reduce the risk of antibody formation [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML):** Current or historical PML [Australian].
- **Immunocompromised States:** Patients with increased risk for opportunistic infections, including those currently receiving immunosuppressive therapies or those with systemic medical conditions (e.g., HIV, organ transplant, active malignancy) [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to natalizumab, any of its excipients, or CHO-cell products [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** Concomitant use with other immunomodulatory agents (e.g., beta-interferons, glatiramer acetate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [Australian]
**Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML):** Natalizumab is associated with an increased risk of PML, an opportunistic viral infection (JC virus) of the brain that may lead to death or severe disability. Therapy must be withheld immediately at the first signs of PML. Risk factors include:
1. Presence of anti-JCV antibodies.
2. Treatment duration exceeding 2 years.
3. Prior use of immunosuppressants.

### General Warnings
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Spontaneous reports of severe liver injury, including markedly elevated hepatic enzymes and bilirubin, have occurred as early as six days after the first dose. Discontinue if liver injury is suspected [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity/Anaphylaxis:** Reactions (e.g., urticaria, dizziness, fever, hypotension) occur in <1% of patients, typically during or within one hour of infusion. If anaphylaxis occurs, discontinue permanently [Australian].
- **Other Opportunistic Infections:** Increased risk for herpes simplex and varicella zoster encephalitis/meningitis, and acute retinal necrosis (ARN). ARN can lead to blindness; refer for retinal screening if eye symptoms occur [Australian].
- **Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome (IRIS):** Occurs in most patients after natalizumab withdrawal following a PML diagnosis. Characterised by rapid neurological worsening [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Anti-JCV Antibody Testing:** Required prior to treatment and recommended every 6 months for antibody-negative patients. 
- **MRI Monitoring:** A baseline MRI (usually within 3 months) is required. Routine MRIs should be performed at least yearly; more frequent MRIs (every 3–6 months) are recommended for high-risk patients [Australian].
- **Laboratory Tests:** Standard monitoring for MS, plus liver function tests for patients with a history of liver disease [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in clinical trials (Study 1 - Monotherapy) at an incidence $\ge$ 1% higher than placebo:

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache (38%), Fatigue (27%), Arthralgia (19%), Urinary tract infection (21%), Lower respiratory tract infection (17%), Gastroenteritis (11%), Vaginitis (10%), Depression (19%), Pain in extremity (16%), Abdominal discomfort (11%), Diarrhoea (10%), Rash (12%), Nasopharyngitis, Dizziness, Nausea |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Chest discomfort, Acute hypersensitivity, Seasonal allergy, Rigors, Weight increase/decrease, Herpes, Tonsillitis, Abnormal LFTs, Dermatitis, Pruritus, Irregular menstruation, Vertigo, Urinary urgency, Pyrexia, Vomiting, Urticaria |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | PML, Serious hypersensitivity (Anaphylaxis) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** PML, serious opportunistic infections, hepatotoxicity, and clinical hypersensitivity [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Immunosuppressants/Antineoplastics:** Safety and efficacy of combination therapy are not established. Concurrent use may increase infection risk [Australian].
- **Corticosteroids:** Short courses for MS relapses were associated with a similar infection rate to placebo-treated patients in clinical trials [Australian].
- **Interferons/Glatiramer Acetate:** Use in combination is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug interaction studies were performed. Natalizumab is not expected to interact via cytochrome P450 enzymes [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C:** Use only if clearly needed. If a patient becomes pregnant, discontinuation should be considered.
    - **Neonatal Effects:** Cases of anaemia and thrombocytopenia have been reported in infants born to women exposed to natalizumab during pregnancy. Monitoring of neonatal haematological parameters (platelets, Hb) is recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Natalizumab is detected in human milk. The decision to discontinue breastfeeding or the drug should consider the benefit of the drug to the mother and the potential risk to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (guinea pigs) showed reduced female fertility at high doses (18x human exposure); no effect on male fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of patients >65 years to determine if they respond differently [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No formal studies conducted. Population pharmacokinetics suggest no dose adjustment is necessary [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Natalizumab binds to the $\alpha$4-subunit of $\alpha$4$\beta$1 and $\alpha$4$\beta$7 integrins on the surface of leukocytes (except neutrophils). It inhibits the adhesion of leukocytes to vascular cell adhesion molecule-1 (VCAM-1) and MadCAM-1, preventing leukocyte transmigration into inflamed parenchymal tissue [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Cmax:** 110 $\pm$ 52 $\mu$g/mL (repeat 300 mg dose).
    - **Half-life:** Estimated median terminal half-life of 26.8 days.
    - **Steady State:** Achieved at approximately 24 weeks.
    - **Clearance:** Increases with body weight and the presence of anti-drug antibodies (ADA) [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Natalizumab increases circulating leukocytes (lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils) but does not affect neutrophil counts [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1 (Monotherapy):** A 2-year trial in 942 RRMS patients. Natalizumab 300 mg reduced the annualised relapse rate (0.23 vs 0.73 for placebo) and reduced the risk of sustained disability progression by 42% [Australian].
- **Study 2 (Add-on):** Evaluated natalizumab in combination with interferon beta-1a. While effective, this combination is not the approved use due to the risk profile [Australian].
- **Biosimilar Comparability:** Tyruko demonstrated PK, PD, and clinical efficacy/safety comparability to the reference product (Tysabri) in healthy subjects and RRMS patients (Study PB006-03-01) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store between 2°C and 8°C. Do **not** freeze or shake. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Diluted Solution:** If not used immediately, can be stored at 2°C to 8°C for up to 24 hours. Must be allowed to warm to room temperature before infusion [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Special Requirements:** Administration must be performed by a healthcare professional with access to MRI and facilities to manage hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis [Australian].
