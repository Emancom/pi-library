## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Recommended dose: 480 mg nivolumab plus 160 mg relatlimab administered as an intravenous infusion once every 4 weeks. Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. [Australian]

### Paediatric dosing (where specified)
- Paediatric patients ≥12 years and weighing ≥40 kg: same dose as adults (480 mg nivolumab + 160 mg relatlimab every 4 weeks). [Australian]
- No recommended dose established for paediatric patients ≥12 years who weigh <40 kg.
- Use in children <12 years and/or <40 kg: Not established. [Australian]

### Dose modifications and interruption rules
- Dose escalation or reduction is not recommended.
- In general:
  - Withhold for severe (Grade 3) immune-related adverse reactions.
  - Permanently discontinue for life‑threatening (Grade 4) immune-related adverse reactions, recurrent severe (Grade 3) immune-related adverse reactions requiring systemic immunosuppression, or inability to taper corticosteroid to ≤10 mg prednisone (or equivalent) per day within 12 weeks of initiating steroids.
- Specific recommended modifications (severity grading per NCI‑CTCAE v5.0):
  - Pneumonitis: Grade 2 — withhold; Grade 3–4 — permanently discontinue.
  - Colitis/diarrhoea: Grade 2 or 3 — withhold; Grade 4 — permanently discontinue.
  - Hepatitis:
    - AST/ALT >3–5× ULN or total bilirubin >1.5–3× ULN — withhold.
    - AST or ALT >5× ULN, total bilirubin >3× ULN, or concurrent AST/ALT >3× ULN with total bilirubin >2× ULN — permanently discontinue.
  - Nephritis/renal dysfunction: Grade 2–3 creatinine elevation — withhold; Grade 4 — permanently discontinue.
  - Endocrinopathies: Grade 3–4 — withhold until clinically stable or permanently discontinue depending on severity.
  - Exfoliative skin reactions: suspected SJS/TEN/DRESS — withhold; confirmed SJS/TEN/DRESS — permanently discontinue.
  - Myocarditis: Grade 2–4 — permanently discontinue.
  - Neurological toxicity: Grade 2 — withhold; Grade 3–4 — permanently discontinue.
- Resumption criteria:
  - Resume if complete or partial resolution to Grade 0–1 after corticosteroid taper.
  - Permanently discontinue if no resolution within 12 weeks of last dose, or inability to reduce prednisone (or equivalent) to ≤10 mg/day within 12 weeks of initiating steroids.

### Administration instructions
- Treatment must be initiated and supervised by physicians experienced in cancer treatment.
- Preparation:
  - Use aseptic technique.
  - Inspect solution visually for particulate matter and discoloration; do not shake. Discard if cloudy, discoloured, or contains extraneous particulate matter (except a few translucent-to-white particles).
  - Withdraw required volume and transfer to an appropriate sterile IV container if diluting.
  - OPPORTUNITIES FOR DILUTION: May be administered undiluted or diluted.
  - Acceptable diluents: sodium chloride 9 mg/mL (0.9%) or glucose 50 mg/mL (5%).
  - When diluted:
    - Final concentration should not be below 3 mg/mL nivolumab and 1 mg/mL relatlimab.
    - Total infusion volume must not exceed 160 mL for adults, or 4 mL/kg for patients <40 kg.
  - Gently mix by manual rotation; do not shake.
- Infusion:
  - Administer over 30 minutes via an intravenous line containing a sterile, non‑pyrogenic, low protein‑binding in‑line filter (pore size 0.2 μm to 1.2 μm).
  - Do not administer by IV push or bolus injection.
  - Do not co‑administer other medicinal products through the same infusion line.
  - Flush the line after infusion.
- Compatibility and equipment:
  - Documented compatibility with certain infusion containers and sets and with specific in‑line filter membrane materials in available sources. Follow local institutional policies and product reconstitution guidance.
- Missed dose:
  - Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **nivolumab**, **relatlimab**, or to any of the excipients. [Australian]
- No other absolute contraindications specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources as a boxed/black‑box style warning. (Serious immune‑related adverse reactions described below.)

### General warnings and precautions
- **Immune‑related adverse reactions**:
  - Can be severe or fatal and may affect any organ system; may occur during treatment or after discontinuation (monitor for a minimum of 5 months after last dose).
  - Multiple organ systems may be affected simultaneously.
  - Early identification and prompt management are essential.
- Baseline and periodic monitoring recommended:
  - Hepatic enzymes (AST, ALT), total bilirubin.
  - Renal function (serum creatinine).
  - Serum glucose.
  - Thyroid function tests.
  - Additional testing as clinically indicated to exclude alternative etiologies (including infection).
- Management principles for suspected immune‑related adverse reactions:
  - Withhold or permanently discontinue depending on severity (see Section 4).
  - Initiate systemic corticosteroids (generally 1–2 mg/kg/day prednisone or equivalent) for most severe immune‑related events; tailor dose to organ and severity.
  - Taper corticosteroids over at least 1 month once improved to avoid rebound.
  - Consider other systemic immunosuppressants if worsening or no improvement with corticosteroids.
  - Do not resume treatment while patient is receiving immunosuppressive doses of corticosteroids or other systemic immunosuppressants; consider antibiotic prophylaxis during prolonged immunosuppression.
- Specific organ‑directed guidance (generalised steroid dosing shown; escalate or reduce per clinical response):
  - Pneumonitis:
    - For Grade 2: consider 1 mg/kg/day methylprednisolone equivalents; increase to 2–4 mg/kg/day if worsening.
    - For Grade 3–4: initiate 2–4 mg/kg/day methylprednisolone equivalents.
  - Colitis/diarrhoea:
    - Grade 2: 0.5–1 mg/kg/day methylprednisolone equivalents; increase to 1–2 mg/kg/day if no improvement.
    - Grade 3–4: 1–2 mg/kg/day methylprednisolone equivalents.
    - For steroid‑refractory colitis, evaluate for infectious causes (e.g., CMV) and consider additional immunosuppression.
  - Hepatitis:
    - AST/ALT >5× ULN or total bilirubin >3× ULN, or concurrent AST/ALT >3× ULN with bilirubin >2× ULN: 1–2 mg/kg/day methylprednisolone equivalents.
    - AST/ALT >3–5× ULN or bilirubin >1.5–3× ULN: 0.5–1 mg/kg/day methylprednisolone equivalents; increase to 1–2 mg/kg/day if worsening.
  - Nephritis/renal dysfunction:
    - Grade 2–3: 0.5–1 mg/kg/day methylprednisolone equivalents; increase to 1–2 mg/kg/day if worsening.
    - Grade 4: 1–2 mg/kg/day methylprednisolone equivalents.
  - Endocrinopathies:
    - Monitor and provide hormone replacement as indicated (thyroid hormone, insulin, physiologic corticosteroid replacement for adrenal insufficiency, other pituitary hormone replacement).
    - For symptomatic hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism: withhold and initiate appropriate hormone/antithyroid therapy; consider corticosteroids if acute thyroid inflammation suspected.
    - For symptomatic adrenal insufficiency: withhold and initiate physiologic corticosteroid replacement.
    - Hypophysitis: withhold for symptomatic Grade 2–3; initiate hormone replacement and consider 1–2 mg/kg/day methylprednisolone equivalents if acute inflammation suspected. Permanently discontinue for life‑threatening (Grade 4) hypophysitis.
    - Diabetes mellitus with potential for DKA: withhold and initiate insulin as needed; permanently discontinue for life‑threatening diabetes.
  - Skin reactions:
    - Manage mild–moderate non‑exfoliative rash with topical emollients/topical corticosteroids.
    - Severe rash: systemic high‑dose corticosteroid (1–2 mg/kg/day methylprednisolone equivalents).
    - Suspected SJS/TEN/DRESS: withhold and refer to specialised care; confirmed SJS/TEN/DRESS — permanently discontinue.
  - Myocarditis:
    - High index of suspicion for cardiac or cardio‑pulmonary symptoms.
    - If suspected, promptly initiate high‑dose corticosteroids (1–2 mg/kg/day methylprednisolone equivalents) and urgent cardiology evaluation; permanently discontinue if diagnosis of Grade 2–4 myocarditis is established.
    - If no improvement or worsening, escalate to 2–4 mg/kg/day methylprednisolone equivalents.
- Myocarditis‑myositis‑myasthenia gravis overlap syndrome:
  - Reported with combination therapy, some fatal. Requires early recognition and aggressive management.
- Pre‑existing autoimmune disease:
  - Patients with active autoimmune disease were generally excluded from trials; risk of immune‑mediated adverse reactions and flares may be increased. Use cautiously after individual benefit–risk assessment.
- Infusion reactions:
  - Severe infusion reactions have been reported; discontinue infusion for severe or life‑threatening reactions and provide appropriate medical treatment.
  - Patients with mild–moderate reactions may continue with close monitoring and premedication per local guidelines.
- Allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) complications:
  - Fatal and serious transplant‑related complications have occurred when HSCT is performed before or after PD‑1/PD‑L1 inhibitor therapy (hyperacute GVHD, acute/chronic GVHD, veno‑occlusive disease, steroid‑requiring febrile syndromes). Monitor closely and weigh benefit–risk when sequencing therapies.
- Patient information:
  - Patients should be informed of potential serious immune‑related adverse reactions and provided with a patient card (or equivalent) to carry at all times.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic:
  - AST, ALT, total bilirubin.
  - Serum creatinine.
  - Serum glucose.
  - Thyroid function tests.
- Additional tests as clinically indicated to evaluate suspected immune‑related events (e.g., cardiac enzymes, ECG, troponin for myocarditis; pituitary/adrenal hormone assays for suspected hypophysitis/adrenal insufficiency; amylase/lipase for pancreatitis; CMV testing in steroid‑refractory colitis).

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use caution in populations excluded from registrational trials (active autoimmune disease, need for moderate/high dose systemic corticosteroids or immunosuppressants, uveal melanoma, active/untreated brain or leptomeningeal metastases) — limited data available.
- Safety and efficacy not studied in patients with severe renal or severe hepatic impairment; use with caution and consider lack of data when treating these patients.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- All adverse effects listed below are reported in the available sources for **nivolumab + relatlimab**. Where frequency is not provided in the available sources, frequency is noted as "Not specified in available sources."
- A number of immune‑related adverse reactions can be severe or fatal.
- Some adverse reactions were reported as occurring at an incidence of <1% (unless otherwise noted) in patients who received the combination or have been reported with other anti‑PD‑(L)1 therapies; these are indicated where provided.
