# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory information into a single reference.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local PI approved by your national regulatory authority before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/generic name). It is a carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. 
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in available sources [US].
- **ATC code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage form(s) and strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with an inborn error of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours (either by infusion or intravenous injection).
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended in the range of 50 mg/kg or as required by therapy. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L).

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Initiation:** Therapy may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations below normal (40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L).
- **Titration:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### General Administration Instructions
- **Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL).
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion; the formulation contains no preservative.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of injection. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately.
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In ESRD patients, monitor trough levocarnitine levels regularly. In metabolic disorders, monitor blood chemistries and vital signs.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by underlying pathology, particularly in the ESRD population.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain, accidental injury, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension.
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea, vomiting.
- **Nervous:** Dizziness.
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis.

### Common (1–10%)
- **Body as a Whole:** Allergic reaction, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever.
- **Cardiovascular:** Arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation, palpitations, tachycardia.
- **Digestive:** Anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia, melena, nausea.
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight increase/decrease.
- **Musculo-Skeletal:** Leg cramps, myalgia.
- **Nervous:** Anxiety, depression, hypertonia, insomnia, paresthesia.
- **Skin:** Pruritus, rash.
- **Special Senses:** Ambylopia, eye disorder, taste perversion.
- **Urogenital:** Kidney failure, urinary tract infection.

### Postmarketing and Serious Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (regardless of prior history).
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm.
- **Other:** Body odor, gastritis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed. Monitor INR levels after initiating **levocarnitine** or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned in provided source. 
    - **Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women exist. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentration in milk following administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is addressed in the Dosage and Administration section for metabolic disorders and ESRD. Specific age-related normal concentrations define deficiency in premature infants and newborns [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **ESRD:** Use is indicated for dialysis patients; however, chronic high doses of **oral** levocarnitine (not the injection) in patients with severe renal impairment may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO). 
    - **Dialysis:** **Levocarnitine** is easily removed by dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it addresses low plasma carnitine and high acylcarnitine/carnitine ratios caused by reduced intake, reduced renal synthesis, and dialytic losses.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly after dialysis increased mean trough levocarnitine concentrations from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While **levocarnitine** increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the effects on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) have not been definitively determined in this population [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.
