# Editorial Product Information: Nintedanib

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) sourced from the Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or SmPC required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive prescribing instructions.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Nintedanib (as esilate).
*   **Chemical Class:** Small molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitor (triple angiokinase inhibitor).
*   **Brand Name(s):** **Ofev** [Australian].
*   **ATC Code:** L01EX09.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   100 mg soft capsules (peach-coloured, opaque, oblong).
    *   150 mg soft capsules (brown-coloured, opaque, oblong).
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Excipients of Note:** Contains soya lecithin [Australian].
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Boehringer Ingelheim [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC):** In combination with docetaxel for adult patients with locally advanced, metastatic, or recurrent NSCLC of adenocarcinoma histology after failure of first-line chemotherapy [Australian].
*   **Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF):** Monotherapy for the treatment of IPF [Australian].
*   **Progressive Fibrosing Interstitial Lung Diseases (PF-ILD):** Monotherapy for the treatment of other chronic fibrosing ILDs with a progressive phenotype [Australian].
*   **Systemic Sclerosis-Associated Interstitial Lung Disease (SSc-ILD):** Monotherapy to slow the rate of decline in pulmonary function [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Standard Administration:** Capsules must be swallowed whole with water during food. They should not be chewed, crushed, or opened. If contact with capsule content occurs, wash hands immediately.
*   **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the patient should skip that dose and resume at the next scheduled time. Do not administer an additional dose.

### Indication-Specific Dosing
*   **NSCLC:** 200 mg twice daily (approximately 12 hours apart) on days 2 to 21 of a standard 21-day docetaxel cycle. **Ofev** must not be taken on day 1 (the day of docetaxel administration). Maximum daily dose: 400 mg.
*   **IPF, PF-ILD, and SSc-ILD:** 150 mg twice daily (approximately 12 hours apart). Maximum daily dose: 300 mg.

### Dose Adjustments and Titration
*   **Toxicity Management:** For adverse reactions (e.g., diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, or liver enzyme elevations), treatment should be interrupted until the reaction resolves to Grade 1 or baseline.
*   **Reduction Schedule:**
    *   **NSCLC:** Reduce in 100 mg steps per day (50 mg per dose). If 100 mg twice daily is not tolerated, discontinue permanently.
    *   **ILD Indications:** Reduce from 150 mg twice daily to 100 mg twice daily. If not tolerated, discontinue permanently.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Known hypersensitivity to **nintedanib**, peanut, or soya [Australian].
*   **Pregnancy:** Strictly contraindicated due to embryo-fetal lethality and teratogenicity [Australian].
*   For docetaxel-related contraindications (when used in NSCLC), refer to the specific docetaxel PI.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Gastrointestinal Disorders:** Diarrhea is the most frequent adverse event. Manage at first signs with adequate hydration and anti-diarrheals (e.g., loperamide). Persistent severe diarrhea requires dose reduction or discontinuation.
*   **Hepatic Function:** Drug-induced liver injury (DILI), including fatal cases, has been reported. 
    *   **Monitoring:** Conduct liver function tests (ALT, AST, bilirubin) at baseline, regularly during the first 3 months, and periodically thereafter.
    *   **Discontinuation:** Permanently discontinue if jaundice or specific elevations occur (AST/ALT >3x ULN with Bilirubin ≥2x ULN and ALP <2x ULN).
*   **Haemorrhage:** VEGFR inhibition may increase bleeding risk. Use with caution in patients with known bleeding predispositions or those on full-dose anticoagulation. Avoid in patients with recent pulmonary bleeding (>2.5 mL).
*   **Arterial Thromboembolic Events:** Use caution in patients with high cardiovascular risk. Interrupt treatment if signs of acute myocardial ischemia occur.
*   **GI Perforations:** Use caution in patients with previous abdominal surgery or history of peptic ulcers. Initiate treatment at least 4 weeks after abdominal surgery.
*   **Vascular Risks:** Increased risk of aneurysms and artery dissections (with or without hypertension). 
*   **Other:** Monitor for proteinuria (nephrotic range), wound healing complications, and Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary
*   **Very Common (>10%):** Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, increased ALT/AST/ALP, decreased appetite, electrolyte imbalance (NSCLC), bleeding (NSCLC), neutropenia (NSCLC), mucositis (NSCLC).
*   **Common (1–10%):** Weight decreased, dehydration, headache, hypertension, febrile neutropenia (NSCLC), thrombocytopenia (NSCLC), proteinuria.
*   **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Pancreatitis, GI perforation, drug-induced liver injury, colitis, hyperbilirubinemia, pruritus, alopecia.
*   **Rare/Unknown:** Aneurysms, artery dissections, renal failure, PRES.

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Severe drug-induced liver injury.
*   Gastrointestinal perforation.
*   Arterial thromboembolic events (e.g., myocardial infarction).
*   Fatal sepsis (noted in NSCLC combination therapy).

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **P-glycoprotein (P-gp):** Nintedanib is a P-gp substrate. 
    *   **Inhibitors:** Co-administration with potent P-gp inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, erythromycin) may increase nintedanib exposure (1.6 to 1.8-fold).
    *   **Inducers:** Co-administration with potent P-gp inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, St John's Wort) may decrease nintedanib exposure by ~50%.
*   **Cytochrome P450:** Minimal CYP involvement; low likelihood of CYP-based interactions.
*   **Oral Contraceptives:** While pharmacokinetics are not directly altered, efficacy may be compromised if severe diarrhea or vomiting occurs.
*   **Pirfenidone:** Co-administration was investigated; no evidence of a relevant PK interaction, though GI adverse events may increase.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [Australian] **Category D**. Potential for human fetal harm. Pregnancy testing is required before and during treatment.
*   **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use highly effective contraception during and for at least 3 months after the last dose.
*   **Breastfeeding:** No human data. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment due to potential serious adverse effects in infants.
*   **Fertility:** Preclinical data show no effect on male fertility but reduced female fertility and increased resorptions in animals at clinical exposure levels.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in children.
*   **Elderly (≥65 years):** No general dose adjustment required; however, nintedanib exposure increases linearly with age.
*   **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild to moderate impairment. Not studied in severe renal impairment (CrCL <30 mL/min).
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    *   **Child-Pugh A (Mild):** In IPF/ILD, the recommended dose is 100 mg twice daily.
    *   **Child-Pugh B/C (Moderate/Severe):** Treatment is not recommended.
*   **Body Weight:** Exposure is inversely correlated with weight. Patients <50 kg (NSCLC) or <65 kg (ILD) may be at higher risk for liver enzyme elevations.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Nintedanib blocks the kinase activity of Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor Receptors (VEGFR 1-3), Platelet-Derived Growth Factor Receptors (PDGFR $\alpha$ and $\beta$), and Fibroblast Growth Factor Receptors (FGFR 1-3). This inhibits signaling pathways essential for the proliferation/survival of endothelial and perivascular cells and the pathogenesis of fibrotic tissue remodeling.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Bioavailability:** ~4.7%.
    *   **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels in 2–4 hours (fed). Food increases exposure by ~20%.
    *   **Distribution:** High protein binding (97.8%), primarily to albumin.
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily via hydrolytic cleavage by esterases to the free acid BIBF 1202, then glucuronidation by UGT enzymes (1A1, 1A7, 1A8, 1A10).
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily biliary/fecal excretion (>90%). Terminal half-life is 10–15 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **LUME-Lung 1 (NSCLC):** A Phase III trial (n=1314) showed that adding nintedanib to docetaxel significantly improved PFS (3.4 vs 2.7 months) and OS in the adenocarcinoma population (12.6 vs 10.3 months).
*   **INPULSIS 1 & 2 (IPF):** Two Phase III trials (n=1061) demonstrated a significant reduction in the annual rate of FVC decline (approximately 110 mL/year difference vs. placebo).
*   **INBUILD (PF-ILD):** A Phase III trial (n=663) showed a 57% relative reduction in the annual rate of FVC decline across various progressive fibrosing ILDs.
*   **SENSCIS (SSc-ILD):** A Phase III trial (n=576) demonstrated a significant slowing of FVC decline (41 mL/year difference vs. placebo) over 52 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   Store below 25°C [Australian].
*   Store in the original package to protect from moisture [Australian].
*   Shelf life varies; refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
