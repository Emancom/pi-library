# Editorial Product Information: Naloxegol

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of naloxegol product information for use by healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis of the provided source document and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Naloxegol oxalate; a PEGylated derivative of naloxone.
- **Chemical Class:** Peripherally acting mu-opioid receptor antagonist (PAMORA).
- **Brand Names:** **MOVANTIK** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 12.5 mg and 25 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Valinor Pharma, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Opioid-Induced Constipation (OIC):** Treatment of OIC in adult patients with chronic non-cancer pain, including patients with chronic pain related to prior cancer or its treatment who do not require frequent opioid dosage escalation [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 25 mg once daily in the morning [US].
- **Dosing Adjustment:** If the 25 mg dose is not tolerated, the dosage should be reduced to 12.5 mg once daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Administer on an empty stomach at least 1 hour prior to the first meal of the day or 2 hours after the meal [US].
    - Discontinue all maintenance laxative therapy prior to starting naloxegol; laxatives may be resumed as needed if OIC symptoms persist after 3 days of treatment [US].
    - Discontinue naloxegol if the underlying opioid pain medication is stopped [US].
    - **Alternate Administration:** For patients with dysphagia, tablets may be crushed to a powder, mixed with 120 mL (4 ounces) of water, and consumed immediately. For nasogastric (NG) tube administration, the crushed tablet should be mixed with 60 mL of water, administered via the tube, and the tube flushed with an additional 60 mL of water [US].
- **Drug Interaction Management:** Avoid grapefruit and grapefruit juice. If moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors are unavoidable, reduce naloxegol dosage to 12.5 mg once daily [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known or suspected gastrointestinal (GI) obstruction and patients at risk of recurrent obstruction due to the potential for GI perforation [US].
- Concomitant use with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin) [US].
- Known serious or severe hypersensitivity reaction to naloxegol or its excipients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Opioid Withdrawal:** Clusters of symptoms (e.g., hyperhidrosis, chills, diarrhea, abdominal pain, anxiety, irritability, yawning) have occurred. Patients with blood-brain barrier disruptions may be at increased risk. Monitor methadone-treated patients closely for GI symptoms of withdrawal [US].
- **Severe Abdominal Pain and/or Diarrhea:** Generally occurs within days of initiation. Monitor and discontinue if severe symptoms develop. Consider restarting at 12.5 mg [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Reported with PAMORAs. Use with caution in patients with known or suspected lesions of the GI tract (e.g., diverticulitis, infiltrative malignancies, Crohn's disease, recent GI surgery). Discontinue if severe or persistent abdominal pain occurs [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally dose-dependent and occur most frequently within the first few days of treatment.

| Adverse Reaction | Naloxegol 25 mg (n=446) | Naloxegol 12.5 mg (n=441) | Placebo (n=444) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Abdominal Pain | 21% | 12% | 7% |
| Diarrhea | 9% | 6% | 5% |
| Nausea | 8% | 7% | 5% |
| Flatulence | 6% | 3% | 3% |
| Vomiting | 5% | 3% | 4% |
| Headache | 4% | 4% | 3% |
| Hyperhidrosis | 3% | <1% | <1% |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Gastrointestinal perforation and severe opioid withdrawal [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Hypersensitivity reactions (angioedema, rash, urticaria) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin):** Contraindicated; significantly increases naloxegol exposure [US].
- **Moderate CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., diltiazem, erythromycin, verapamil):** Avoid use; if essential, reduce naloxegol dose to 12.5 mg once daily [US].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine, St. John’s Wort):** Not recommended; significantly decreases naloxegol exposure and efficacy [US].
- **Weak CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** No dosage adjustment required [US].
- **Other Opioid Antagonists:** Avoid concomitant use due to the risk of additive effects and precipitated withdrawal [US].
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Inhibitors (e.g., quinidine):** May increase naloxegol concentrations, but no dosage adjustment is necessary [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/Letter system replaced]. Data are insufficient to inform drug-associated risks. Use may precipitate opioid withdrawal in the mother and fetus. Animal studies at high doses showed no embryo-fetal toxicity [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Naloxegol is present in rat milk; potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, including opioid withdrawal [US].
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility or reproductive performance were observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No general dosage adjustment is required. Note that exposure was higher in elderly healthy Japanese subjects in clinical pharmacology studies [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Moderate, severe, or end-stage renal disease (CLcr <60 mL/min): Start at 12.5 mg once daily. If tolerated and OIC symptoms persist, may increase to 25 mg [US].
    - Mild renal impairment: No dosage adjustment required [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - Mild or moderate (Child-Pugh Class A or B): No dosage adjustment required [US].
    - Severe (Child-Pugh Class C): Avoid use; pharmacokinetics not evaluated [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Naloxegol functions as a peripherally acting mu-opioid receptor antagonist (PAMORA) in tissues such as the GI tract. Its PEGylation and P-gp substrate properties result in high efflux across the blood-brain barrier, minimising CNS penetration and preserving central opioid analgesia [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Antagonism of mu-receptors in the GI tract inhibits opioid-induced delay of transit time. Naloxegol does not prolong the QTc interval at therapeutic or supratherapeutic doses [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations reached in <2 hours. High-fat meals increase AUC by 45% and Cmax by 30% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Low plasma protein binding (~4.2%) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by the CYP3A enzyme system [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily fecal (68%) and urinary (16%). Half-life is 6 to 11 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies:** Two replicate 12-week, randomised, double-blind trials (Study 1 and Study 2) evaluated naloxegol 12.5 mg and 25 mg in patients with OIC and chronic non-cancer pain.
- **Primary Outcomes:** The 25 mg dose showed a statistically significant increase in the proportion of responders (defined by spontaneous bowel movement frequency) compared to placebo in both trials. The 12.5 mg dose reached significance in Study 1 but not Study 2 [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20-25°C (68-77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15-30°C (59-86°F) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only) [US].
