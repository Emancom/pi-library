# Editorial Product Information: Nefazodone Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only the US (FDA) PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nefazodone hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Phenylpiperazine antidepressant.
- **Brand Names:** Nefazodone Hydrochloride Tablets, USP [US]. (Note: Nefazodone is chemically related to trazodone).
- **ATC Code:** N06AX06.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Tablets:** 50 mg (light-pink), 100 mg (white), 150 mg (peach), 200 mg (light-yellow), and 250 mg (white) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Teva Pharmaceuticals USA, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** Treatment of depression in adults. Efficacy was established in 6- to 8-week trials in outpatients and a 6-week trial in inpatients [US].
- **Relapse Prevention:** Maintenance of clinical response for up to 36 weeks following 16 weeks of acute open-label treatment (52 weeks total) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** 
    - **Initial Dose:** 200 mg/day, administered in two divided doses (BID) [US].
    - **Titration:** Increase in increments of 100–200 mg/day (on a BID schedule) at intervals of no less than one week [US].
    - **Therapeutic Range:** Generally 300–600 mg/day. As with all antidepressants, several weeks may be required to observe the full therapeutic effect [US].
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food. Food delays absorption and decreases bioavailability by approximately 20% [US].
- **Switching to/from MAOIs:** 
    - At least 14 days must elapse between discontinuing a Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitor (MAOI) and starting nefazodone [US].
    - At least 7 days should be allowed after stopping nefazodone before starting an MAOI [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Patients should skip the missed dose and resume the regular schedule. Do not double doses [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to nefazodone, other phenylpiperazine antidepressants, or any inactive ingredients [US].
- **Liver Injury:** Patients withdrawn from nefazodone due to evidence of hepatocellular injury [US].
- **Coadministration with Specific Drugs:**
    - **MAOIs:** Risk of serious, potentially fatal reactions [US].
    - **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Terfenadine, astemizole, cisapride, or pimozide (due to risk of QT prolongation and Torsade de Pointes) [US].
    - **Carbamazepine:** Coadministration is contraindicated as it significantly reduces nefazodone plasma levels [US].
    - **Triazolam:** Coadministration should generally be avoided; if used, triazolam dose must be reduced by 75% [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Hepatotoxicity [US]:** 
    - Rare cases of life-threatening hepatic failure (resulting in death or transplant) have been reported. 
    - Reported rate: ~1 case per 250,000 to 300,000 patient-years.
    - Treatment should not be initiated in patients with active liver disease or elevated baseline transaminases.
    - **Action:** Discontinue immediately if ALT/AST levels are ≥ 3x Upper Limit of Normal (ULN). Do not re-challenge.
- **Boxed Warning: Suicidality [US]:** 
    - Antidepressants increase the risk of suicidal thinking and behaviour in children, adolescents, and young adults (ages 18–24).
    - Patients of all ages must be monitored for clinical worsening and emergence of suicidality, particularly during the first few months of therapy or during dose changes.
- **Postural Hypotension:** Observed in 2.8% of patients. Use caution in patients with known cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease [US].
- **Activation of Mania/Hypomania:** Reported in 0.3% of unipolar and 1.6% of bipolar patients [US].
- **Priapism:** Rare reports post-marketing. Immediate medical attention is required for erections lasting > 24 hours [US].
- **Visual Disturbances:** Blurred vision (9%) and "abnormal vision" (scotoma, visual trails) (7%) reported, often dose-dependent [US].
- **Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Pupillary dilation may trigger an attack in patients with anatomically narrow angles without a patent iridectomy [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hepatic Failure:** Life-threatening liver necrosis [US].
- **Hyponatremia/SIADH:** Particularly in the elderly [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Reported, especially with concomitant serotonergic agents [US].
- **Seizures:** Rare occurrences reported post-marketing [US].
- **Rhabdomyolysis:** Rare cases reported in patients taking nefazodone with simvastatin or lovastatin [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibition:** Nefazodone is a potent inhibitor of CYP3A4.
    - **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors:** ~20-fold increase in simvastatin levels; ~3-4 fold increase in atorvastatin levels. Avoid or use with extreme caution (risk of rhabdomyolysis). Pravastatin/fluvastatin are preferred as they are not metabolized by CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Benzodiazepines:** Increases AUC of triazolam (4-fold) and alprazolam (2-fold). Reduce triazolam dose by 75% or alprazolam dose by 50% [US].
    - **Immunosuppressants:** Increases blood concentrations of cyclosporine and tacrolimus to toxic ranges [US].
    - **Buspirone:** Significant increase in buspirone AUC (up to 50-fold). Use low doses (e.g., 2.5 mg QD) [US].
- **Digoxin:** Increases digoxin Cmax and AUC by ~29% and 15%. Monitor plasma levels [US].
- **Carbamazepine:** Decreases nefazodone/metabolite AUC by 95% (contraindicated) [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Concomitant use in depressed patients is not advised [US].
- **General Anesthetics:** Discontinue nefazodone prior to elective surgery as clinically feasible [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C [US]:** No adequate human studies. Animal studies showed increased pup mortality and decreased weight at high doses. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown. Exercise caution [US].
- **Fertility:** Rat studies showed a slight decrease in fertility at 200 mg/kg/day (~3x maximum human dose) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not approved. Safety and efficacy not established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** 
    - Single-dose PK studies show Cmax and AUC are doubled in the elderly. 
    - **Dosing:** Start at 100 mg/day (50 mg BID). Titrate carefully based on response [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No effect on steady-state plasma concentrations in patients with CrCl 7–60 mL/min/1.73m² [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** AUC values for nefazodone and its hydroxy-metabolite were ~25% higher in patients with liver cirrhosis (see Contraindications/Warnings) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Unknown, but inhibits neuronal uptake of serotonin (5-HT) and norepinephrine (NE). It is a potent antagonist at central 5-HT2 receptors and alpha-1 adrenergic receptors [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and complete, but absolute bioavailability is low (~20%) due to extensive first-pass metabolism.
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed; > 99% protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized by n-dealkylation and hydroxylation (CYP3A4). 
    - **Metabolites:** Hydroxynefazodone (active, similar to parent); m-chlorophenylpiperazine (mCPP) (active, 5-HT agonist/antagonist); Triazole-dione (active).
    - **Elimination:** Half-life of nefazodone is 2–4 hours; ~55% excreted in urine and 20–30% in feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Acute Efficacy:** Two 6- to 8-week trials in outpatients showed nefazodone (300–600 mg/day) superior to placebo on the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS). One trial in inpatients also showed efficacy [US].
- **Maintenance:** A 52-week study (16 weeks open-label, 36 weeks double-blind) demonstrated a significantly lower relapse rate for nefazodone compared to placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F).
- **Packaging:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Not a controlled substance [US].
- **Australian:** Not specified in available sources.
