# Editorial Product Information: naltrexone + bupropion

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): naltrexone + bupropion
- Chemical class:
  - Naltrexone — opioid receptor antagonist
  - Bupropion — aminoketone antidepressant; inhibitor of dopamine and norepinephrine reuptake; CYP2D6 inhibitor
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- For the management of weight in adult patients (≥18 years) as an adjunct to a reduced‑calorie diet and increased physical activity in patients with:
  - BMI ≥ 30 kg/m2 (obese), or
  - BMI ≥ 27 kg/m2 to < 30 kg/m2 (overweight) in the presence of one or more weight‑related comorbidities (e.g., type 2 diabetes, dyslipidaemia, controlled hypertension).
- Treatment should be discontinued after 16 weeks if the patient has not lost at least 5% of initial body weight.
- Re-evaluate the need for continued treatment annually.
- Other indications: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- Initiate with a 4‑week titration schedule (daily doses expressed as tablets containing 8 mg naltrexone / 90 mg bupropion per tablet):
  - Week 1: Morning — 1 tablet; Evening — none
  - Week 2: Morning — 1 tablet; Evening — 1 tablet
  - Week 3: Morning — 2 tablets; Evening — 1 tablet
  - Week 4 onward: Morning — 2 tablets; Evening — 2 tablets
- Maximum recommended daily dose: 32 mg naltrexone / 360 mg bupropion (achieved at start of week 4).

### Administration instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed whole with some water.
- Preferably taken with food.
- Do not cut, chew, or crush tablets.
- If a dose is missed, do not take an additional dose to make up; take the next scheduled dose at the usual time.

### Dosing by special situations
- If clinically indicated, reassess treatment at week 16 and discontinue if <5% weight loss.
- For patients who require intermittent opioid therapy (e.g., for surgery), stop naltrexone + bupropion at least 3 days before the opioid administration. If opioid therapy is required after treatment initiation, stop naltrexone + bupropion.
- When converting to/from MAOIs allow at least 14 days free interval (see Interactions/Contraindications).

### Missed dose
- Do not double dose; take next scheduled dose at usual time.

## 5. Contraindications
(Include all contraindications as specified)
- Hypersensitivity to bupropion, naltrexone or any excipients.
- Uncontrolled hypertension.
- Seizure disorder or history of seizures.
- Known central nervous system tumour.
- Undergoing acute alcohol withdrawal or benzodiazepine withdrawal.
- History of bipolar disorder.
- Concurrent treatment containing bupropion or naltrexone.
- Current or previous diagnosis of bulimia or anorexia nervosa.
- Patients currently dependent on opioids, including opioid‑containing medication, or patients treated with opioid agonists used in opioid dependence (e.g., methadone, buprenorphine), or patients in acute opioid withdrawal.
- Concomitant administration of monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs). At least 14 days should elapse between discontinuation of an MAOI and initiation of naltrexone + bupropion. Initiation of naltrexone + bupropion in a patient currently treated with reversible MAOIs is also contraindicated.
- Pregnancy.
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- End‑stage renal failure.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Suicide and suicidal behaviour:
  - Contains bupropion. Antidepressants (including bupropion) have been associated with increased risk of suicidal behaviour in subjects <25 years in pooled analyses of antidepressant trials.
  - Suicidality events (including ideation) have been reported in post‑marketing experience with naltrexone + bupropion.
  - Close supervision recommended, particularly in early treatment and after dose changes. Advise patients/caregivers to monitor for worsening clinical status, suicidal ideation, or unusual behavioural changes and seek immediate medical attention if these occur.
- Seizures:
  - Bupropion is associated with a dose‑related risk of seizures. Estimated seizure incidence with bupropion SR 300 mg is ~0.1%. In clinical trials with naltrexone + bupropion, seizure incidence was approximately 0.06% (2/3,239) vs 0% (0/1,515) on placebo.
  - Discontinue and do not restart naltrexone + bupropion in patients who experience a seizure.
  - Use caution in patients with factors that increase seizure risk (see list below).
- Risk factors that may increase seizure risk:
  - History of head trauma
  - Excessive alcohol use or dependence, recent withdrawal from alcohol or benzodiazepines
  - Addiction to cocaine or stimulants
  - Concomitant medications that lower seizure threshold (e.g., antipsychotics, other antidepressants, antimalarials, tramadol, theophylline, systemic steroids, quinolones, sedating antihistamines)
  - Rapid reduction of blood glucose in diabetic patients (assess insulin/oral antidiabetic doses)
- Alcohol:
  - Consumption of alcohol during treatment should be minimised or avoided.
- Patients receiving opioids:
  - **naltrexone + bupropion** antagonises opioid receptors and may precipitate opioid withdrawal or block opioid analgesia.
  - Do not administer to patients currently dependent on opioids or receiving opioid agonists for dependence.
  - Prior to initiating treatment, consider testing when opioid use is suspected to ensure clearance.
  - If opioid therapy is required after initiation, stop naltrexone + bupropion. Do not compensate opioid receptor blockade by administering large amounts of opioids — this may cause life‑threatening overdose.
  - After discontinuation, patients may have diminished opioid tolerance and may require lower opioid doses.
  - For patients needing intermittent opioids (e.g., surgery), stop naltrexone + bupropion for a minimum of 3 days before the opioid; do not increase opioid dose above standard dosing.
- Allergic reactions and hypersensitivity:
  - Anaphylactoid/anaphylactic reactions (pruritus, urticaria, angioedema, dyspnoea) have been reported.
  - Rare reports of erythema multiforme and anaphylactic shock associated with bupropion.
  - Arthralgia, myalgia and fever with rash suggestive of delayed hypersensitivity/serum‑sickness have been reported; discontinue if suspected.
  - Severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCARs), including Stevens–Johnson syndrome (SJS) and acute generalised exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP), have been reported; withdraw immediately if suspected and do not restart.
- Blood pressure and heart rate:
  - May cause increases in systolic/diastolic blood pressure and increased resting heart rate.
  - Measure blood pressure and pulse prior to initiation and monitor regularly. Discontinue if clinically relevant sustained increases occur.
  - Contraindicated in uncontrolled hypertension. Use with caution in patients with controlled hypertension.
  - Post‑marketing hypertensive crises have been reported during initial titration.
- Cardiovascular disease:
  - No clinical experience in patients with recent myocardial infarction, unstable heart disease, or NYHA Class III–IV heart failure. Use is not recommended in these populations.
- Brugada syndrome:
  - Bupropion may unmask Brugada syndrome; use caution in patients with Brugada syndrome or a family history of sudden cardiac death/cardiac arrest.
- Hepatotoxicity:
  - Naltrexone (at high doses) can cause hepatocellular injury; drug-induced liver injury (DILI) has been reported in clinical studies and post‑marketing with naltrexone-containing products.
  - Discontinue and seek medical evaluation if symptoms of acute hepatitis occur.
- Serotonin syndrome:
  - Cases have been reported with coadministration of bupropion and serotonergic agents (SSRIs, SNRIs) or opioids (e.g., tramadol, methadone). Monitor closely if used with other serotonergic drugs.
  - Serotonin syndrome has been reported in overdose.
- Neuropsychiatric symptoms and mania:
  - Activation of mania/hypomania has been reported with similar agents; use with caution in patients with history of mania. Panic attacks reported, mainly during titration/dose changes.
- Drug abuse and dependence:
  - Systematic abuse potential not established. Post‑marketing reports exist of inhalation/injection of crushed single‑ingredient bupropion products leading to seizures and deaths. Such misuse may lead to rapid release and overdose.
- Lactose content:
  - Contains lactose; patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose–galactose malabsorption should not take this product.
- Laboratory testing:
  - Bupropion can cause false‑positive urine immunoassay tests for amphetamines. Confirmatory testing (e.g., GC/MS) can distinguish bupropion from amphetamines.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic measurement of blood pressure and pulse.
- Assess eGFR prior to initiation in patients at increased risk for renal impairment (e.g., diabetes, elderly).
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of hepatotoxicity; consider liver function testing if clinically indicated.
- Monitor for neuropsychiatric changes and suicidal ideation, especially in younger patients and during early treatment or dose changes.
- Monitor for signs of serotonin syndrome when used with serotonergic agents.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Contraindicated interactions
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs):
  - Concomitant use contraindicated. Allow at least 14 days between stopping an MAOI and starting naltrexone + bupropion, and at least 14 days after stopping naltrexone + bupropion before starting an MAOI.
- Opioids:
  - Concomitant use contraindicated in patients dependent on opioids or on opioid agonists for dependence (e.g., methadone, buprenorphine). Naltrexone antagonises opioid receptors; co‑administration may precipitate withdrawal or block analgesic effects and can be life‑threatening.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- CYP2D6 inhibition:
  - Bupropion and hydroxybupropion are inhibitors of CYP2D6. Co‑administration with CYP2D6 substrates can increase exposure of those drugs.
  - Example: Co‑administration with metoprolol (CYP2D6 substrate) increased metoprolol AUC ~4‑fold and Cmax ~2‑fold.
  - Similar interactions observed with desipramine and venlafaxine when bupropion is given as a single agent.
  - Clinical approach: Use caution with drugs metabolised by CYP2D6 (certain antidepressants [SSRIs, many TCAs], antipsychotics [haloperidol, risperidone, thioridazine], beta‑blockers [metoprolol], Type IC antiarrhythmics [flecainide]). Consider initiating concomitant therapy at the lower range, consider dose reduction of the CYP2D6 substrate, and consider therapeutic drug monitoring when available for narrow therapeutic index drugs.
- CYP2B6 interactions:
  - Bupropion is primarily metabolised to hydroxybupropion by CYP2B6. Drugs that inhibit CYP2B6 can alter the balance of bupropion and active metabolites.
  - Ticlopidine and clopidogrel (CYP2B6 inhibitors) can increase bupropion exposure and decrease hydroxybupropion exposure.
  - During concomitant use with CYP2B6 inhibitors, the daily dose of naltrexone + bupropion should not exceed two tablets daily (one in the morning and one in the evening).

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Serotonergic agents:
  - Post‑marketing data suggest an increased risk of serotonin syndrome when bupropion is combined with SSRIs, SNRIs, and certain opioids (e.g., tramadol, methadone). Monitor closely for signs of serotonin syndrome, particularly during initiation and dose escalation.
- Drugs that lower seizure threshold:
  - Use caution with concomitant medications that may lower seizure threshold (see Warnings section).
- Opioids:
  - Reduced efficacy of opioid analgesics; risk of precipitated withdrawal.

### Other interaction notes
- False‑positive urine amphetamine immunoassays when the patient is taking bupropion; use confirmatory testing where indicated.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Not recommended in children and adolescents <18 years. Safety and efficacy have not been established in this population.
- Elderly use:
  - Clinical studies did not include sufficient subjects ≥65 years to determine differences. Use with caution in patients >65 years; not recommended in patients >75 years.
  - Elderly patients more likely to have decreased renal function which may increase exposure and adverse effects; consider renal function assessment and careful dose selection.
- Renal impairment:
  - Contraindicated in end‑stage renal failure.
  - In moderate or severe renal impairment, maximum recommended daily dose is reduced to two tablets daily (one in morning and one in evening). Dose reduction is not necessary in mild renal impairment.
  - For patients at elevated risk for renal impairment (e.g., diabetes, elderly), assess eGFR prior to initiation.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment.
  - Not recommended in mild or moderate hepatic impairment.
  - In moderate‑to‑severe hepatic impairment, systemic exposure is higher for bupropion and metabolites (approximately 2–3‑fold) and for naltrexone and metabolites (up to ~10‑fold); therefore avoid use.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **Naltrexone**: opioid receptor antagonist — blocks peripheral and central opioid receptors and thereby antagonises opioid effects.
- **Bupropion**: inhibits reuptake of dopamine and norepinephrine; has activating properties consistent with aminoketone antidepressants. Bupropion and hydroxybupropion inhibit CYP2D6.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Tablets should be taken orally and preferably with food (to assist tolerability/administration). Specific parameters (Tmax, bioavailability) are not specified in the available source extract.
- Distribution:
  - Not specified in available sources.
- Metabolism:
  - Bupropion is primarily metabolised to the active metabolite hydroxybupropion predominantly via CYP2B6.
  - Bupropion and hydroxybupropion are inhibitors of CYP2D6.
- Elimination:
  - Naltrexone, bupropion and their metabolites are substantially excreted by the kidney; exposure increases in renal impairment and elderly patients.
  - In moderate‑to‑severe hepatic impairment, systemic exposure to bupropion/metabolites is increased approximately 2–3 fold and to naltrexone/metabolites up to approximately 10‑fold.
- Pharmacodynamics:
  - Naltrexone produces opioid receptor blockade that may last after dosing and affects response to exogenous opioids (reduced analgesia, precipitated withdrawal).
  - Bupropion increases synaptic dopamine and norepinephrine concentrations and can affect mood and seizure threshold; affects metabolic drug‑drug interaction pathways (CYP2D6 inhibition).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Efficacy and duration:
  - Clinical trials evaluating naltrexone + bupropion for obesity included studies up to 56 weeks in duration. The product label indicates to discontinue treatment if patients do not achieve ≥5% weight loss after 16 weeks of therapy.
  - Safety and efficacy beyond 1 year have not been established.
- Safety findings:
  - In pooled clinical trial data, seizure incidence in subjects receiving naltrexone + bupropion was approximately 0.06% (2/3,239) versus 0% (0/1,515) on placebo.
  - No suicides or suicide attempts were reported in clinical trials up to 56 weeks; suicidality events have been reported post‑marketing.
  - In a large cardiovascular outcomes trial (CVOT) referenced in the source, seizure incidence was no higher than that observed with bupropion alone at approved doses.
  - During clinical studies, concomitant opioid use or opioid‑like medicinal products was generally excluded; however, approximately 12% of subjects took an opioid or opioid‑like product while enrolled, and the majority continued treatment without interruption and without untoward consequences.
- Trial design and outcomes:
  - Detailed pivotal trial names, designs, primary efficacy endpoints, statistical outcomes, and complete adverse‑event tables are not specified in the available source extract. Not specified in available sources.
