# Editorial Product Information: Nitroglycerin (Topical Ointment)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States (FDA)**: Nitroglycerin Ointment USP, 2% (Encube Ethicals, Inc.)
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of publication, only the United States PI was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nitroglycerin (1,2,3-propanetriol trinitrate), an organic nitrate vasodilator.
- **Brand Names:** Nitroglycerin Ointment USP, 2% [US].
- **ATC Code:** C01DA02 (Nitroglycerin).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Topical ointment containing 2% nitroglycerin (20 mg per 1 gram of ointment). One inch (2.5 cm) of ointment contains approximately 15 mg of nitroglycerin [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical (not for oral, sublingual, or ophthalmic use).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Distributed by Encube Ethicals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Angina Pectoris Prophylaxis:** Indicated for the prevention of angina pectoris due to coronary artery disease (CAD) [US].
- **Important Limitation:** This formulation is not indicated for the immediate relief of acute anginal attacks due to its slow onset of action [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** 1/2 inch (7.5 mg) of ointment applied twice daily [US].
- **Dosing Schedule:** One dose should be applied upon rising in the morning, and the second dose applied six hours later [US].
- **Titration:** If the initial dose is tolerated but clinical response is inadequate, the dose may be doubled (1 inch) and increased further as needed, provided the patient tolerates the higher dose [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Application Method:** Use the provided ruled, impermeable paper applicators to measure the dose. Place the applicator on a flat surface, squeeze the ointment onto it, and apply (ointment-side down) to the skin. Tape the applicator in place [US].
- **Application Site:** Typically applied to the truncal skin. Do not rub or massage the ointment into the skin [US].
- **Pouch Formulation:** If using unit-dose pouches, one pouch is equivalent to approximately 1 inch of ointment. Use the entire contents of the pouch and discard immediately [US].

### Nitrate-Free Interval
- To prevent the development of pharmacological tolerance, a nitrate-free interval of 10 to 12 hours is required in every 24-hour cycle [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy or hypersensitivity to nitroglycerin or other organic nitrates [US].
- **Drug Interactions:** Concomitant use with sildenafil or other phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5) inhibitors is contraindicated due to the risk of severe hypotension [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **PDE5 Inhibitor Interaction:** Use with sildenafil can result in profound vasodilation and severe hypotension. Management should include extremity elevation and volume expansion [US].
- **Hypotension and Cardiovascular Effects:** Severe hypotension, particularly in the upright position, may occur even with low doses. Use with caution in volume-depleted patients or those with pre-existing hypotension. Paradoxical bradycardia and increased angina may accompany nitrate-induced hypotension [US].
- **Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy:** Nitrate therapy may exacerbate angina caused by hypertrophic cardiomyopathy [US].
- **Tolerance and Physical Dependence:** Continuous 24-hour exposure leads to tolerance, rendering the drug ineffective. Industrial workers with chronic high-dose exposure have demonstrated physical dependence, with reports of chest pain, MI, and sudden death upon temporary withdrawal [US].
- **Acute MI/CHF:** Benefits in acute myocardial infarction or congestive heart failure are not established. If used in these settings, intensive clinical and hemodynamic monitoring is required to avoid tachycardia and hypotension [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typically dose-related and stem from the drug's vasodilatory activity.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache (often severe; may decrease with continued therapy) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Lightheadedness, transient dizziness |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypotension, syncope, crescendo angina, rebound hypertension, contact dermatitis (at application site) |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anaphylactoid reactions, methemoglobinemia |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Severe hypotension, syncope, and rare methemoglobinemia (characterised by chocolate-brown blood and impaired oxygen delivery) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **PDE5 Inhibitors (e.g., Sildenafil):** Potentiates vasodilatory effects leading to severe hypotension [US].
- **Other Vasodilators:** Additive hypotensive effects [US].
- **Alcohol:** May exhibit additive vasodilatory and hypotensive effects [US].
- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Risk of marked symptomatic orthostatic hypotension; dose adjustments may be required [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US] No animal reproduction studies have been conducted. It is unknown if nitroglycerin causes fetal harm. Administer to pregnant women only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** [US] It is unknown if nitroglycerin is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised when administered to a nursing woman.
- **Fertility:** [US] Effects on reproductive capacity are unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical documents do not specify unique geriatric dosing, but general caution regarding hypotension and orthostatic effects in the elderly is advised [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though central volume expansion in renal disease patients (during overdose management) carries risks [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Relaxes vascular smooth muscle. Venous dilation predominates, increasing peripheral pooling and decreasing venous return (preload reduction). Arteriolar relaxation reduces systemic vascular resistance and arterial pressure (afterload reduction). It also dilates coronary arteries [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rate and extent vary by site and area of skin covered. 
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 3 L/kg [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly cleared (1 L/kg/min) by the liver and extrahepatic sites (red blood cells and vascular walls). Metabolised to dinitrates (less potent) and mononitrates (inactive) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Serum half-life is approximately 3 minutes. After ointment removal, levels wane with a half-life of roughly 30 minutes [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset is not rapid enough for acute relief. Anti-anginal activity lasts up to 7 hours after a single application, but total daily efficacy is limited to approximately 12 hours due to tolerance development [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Controlled trials showed reduced exercise-related angina for up to 7 hours following a single application. 
- Doses studied ranged from 0.5 inches (7.5 mg) to 2 inches (30 mg). 
- Continuous 24-hour therapy trials showed the drug became indistinguishable from placebo within 24 hours due to tolerance [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the tube tightly closed. Discard unit-dose pouches immediately after use [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** [US] Prescription only (Rx-only).
