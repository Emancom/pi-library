# Editorial Product Information: Nabilone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory data into a clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing nabilone in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the US-specific PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Nabilone (a synthetic cannabinoid).
- **Chemical Class:** Dibenzopyran derivative; (±)-trans-3-(1,1-dimethylheptyl)-6,6a,7,8,10,10a-hexahydro-1-hydroxy-6-6-dimethyl-9H-dibenzo[b,d]pyran-9-one.
- **Brand Names:** Cesamet [US].
- **ATC Code:** A04AD09.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral capsule, 1 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bausch Health US LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (CINV):** Indicated for the management of nausea and vomiting associated with cancer chemotherapy in patients who have failed to respond adequately to conventional antiemetic treatments [US].
- **Note on Use:** Due to the risk of psychotomimetic reactions, nabilone is restricted to second-line therapy and is not intended for use as a first-line antiemetic or on an as-needed basis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The standard dosage is 1 mg or 2 mg administered twice daily [US].
- **Chemotherapy Coordination:** The initial dose should be administered 1 to 3 hours prior to the start of chemotherapy. To minimise adverse effects, it is recommended to initiate therapy at the lower end of the dosing range and titrate as necessary [US].
- **Extended Use:** Dosing may be continued 2 to 3 times daily throughout the chemotherapy cycle and, if clinically indicated, for up to 48 hours following the final chemotherapeutic dose [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** 6 mg per day, administered in divided doses three times daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** A dose of 1 mg or 2 mg the night before chemotherapy may be beneficial for some patients [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to nabilone or any other cannabinoid [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **CNS Effects:** Nabilone may cause significant central nervous system (CNS) changes, including dizziness, drowsiness, euphoria, ataxia, anxiety, disorientation, depression, hallucinations, and psychosis. These effects may persist for 48 to 72 hours after treatment cessation [US].
- **Supervision:** Patients should remain under the supervision of a responsible adult, particularly during the initiation of therapy and during dose titrations [US].
- **Psychiatric History:** Use with caution in patients with a history of psychiatric disorders (e.g., depression, schizophrenia, mania), as nabilone may unmask or exacerbate these conditions [US].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Nabilone can cause tachycardia and orthostatic hypotension. Caution is advised in patients with hypertension or heart disease, and in elderly patients [US].
- **Impairment of Driving:** Patients must be explicitly warned not to drive, operate heavy machinery, or engage in hazardous activities while taking nabilone [US].
- **Abuse and Dependence:** Nabilone is a highly abusable substance with a potential for psychological and physical dependence. Prescriptions should be limited to the amount necessary for a single chemotherapy cycle [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are common and often dose-related. In clinical trials, virtually all patients experienced at least one adverse reaction [US].

| Frequency | Adverse Event [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Drowsiness (52–66%), Vertigo/Dizziness (52–59%), Dry mouth (22–36%), Euphoria (11–38%), Ataxia (13–14%), Sleep disturbance (11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dysphoria (9%), Headache (6–7%), Concentration difficulties (12%), Hypotension/Orthostatic hypotension (8%), Asthenia (8%), Anorexia (8%), Nausea (4%), Depression (14% in active-controlled trials), Visual disturbance (13% in active-controlled trials) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Psychotic episodes (including hallucinations and anxiety reactions), respiratory depression, coma (associated with large overdose), and potential cardiotoxicity when combined with sympathomimetics [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects occur with alcohol, sedatives, hypnotics, benzodiazepines, barbiturates, opioids, and tricyclic antidepressants. These combinations increase the risk of CNS depression and should be avoided or used with extreme caution [US].
- **Sympathomimetics:** Concomitant use with amphetamines or cocaine may lead to additive hypertension and tachycardia [US].
- **Anticholinergic Agents:** Atropine or scopolamine may produce additive tachycardia and drowsiness [US].
- **Protein Binding:** Nabilone is highly protein-bound and may displace other protein-bound medications. Clinicians should monitor patients for changes in dosage requirements for co-administered highly protein-bound drugs [US].
- **Metabolic Interactions:** While nabilone may inhibit CYP2C8 and CYP2C9 in vitro, clinical interference with P450-mediated metabolism is considered unlikely due to low plasma concentrations [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not specified by US letter category in the current source, but noted that there are no adequate/well-controlled human studies. Animal studies showed increased embryo lethality and decreased fetal weight. Use only if potential benefits justify the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if nabilone is excreted in human milk. Because other cannabinoids are excreted in breast milk, use in nursing mothers is not recommended [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effect on fertility or reproductive performance at doses up to 4 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years of age. Caution is recommended due to psychoactive effects [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Use with caution. Elderly patients are more sensitive to the psychoactive and cardiovascular effects (orthostatic hypotension/tachycardia) of nabilone. Dosing should generally start at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The effects of renal insufficiency on nabilone pharmacokinetics have not been investigated [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** The effects of hepatic dysfunction on nabilone pharmacokinetics have not been investigated [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Synthetic cannabinoid that interacts with the cannabinoid receptor system, specifically the CB1 receptor found in neural tissues, to produce antiemetic effects [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Completely absorbed from the GI tract; peak plasma concentrations reached within 2 hours [US].
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is approximately 12.5 L/kg [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic metabolism via multiple P450 isoforms. Significant metabolites may accumulate with repeated use [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily biliary/fecal (60–67%) and urinary (22–24%). The plasma half-life of nabilone is approximately 2 hours, while the half-life of total radioactivity (metabolites) is about 35 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Nabilone was evaluated in placebo-controlled and active-controlled (prochlorperazine) trials. It demonstrated effectiveness in treating CINV across various chemotherapy regimens, including low-dose cisplatin [US].
- **Safety findings:** Trials highlighted a higher incidence of CNS-related adverse effects compared to prochlorperazine or placebo, though most were of mild to moderate severity [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature, 25°C (77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Formulated as 1 mg capsules in bottles of 50 [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **US:** Classified as a Schedule II (CII) controlled substance due to high abuse potential. Rx-only [US].
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
