# Editorial Product Information: Minoxidil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single, concise reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines. Prescribers should consult the current approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Minoxidil (a piperidinopyrimidine derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Orally active peripheral vasodilator.
- **Brand Name(s):** **LONITEN** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C02DB01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 10 mg scored tablets (white, circular, half-oval, marked "10" and "U/137") [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Refractory Hypertension:** Indicated as adjunctive therapy in adults for hypertension that has failed to respond to extensive multiple-drug regimens [Australian].
- **Reversal of Complications:** When used in conjunction with a diuretic and beta-blocker, it has been shown to reverse encephalopathy and retinopathy in severe cases [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** Therapy should be introduced into a regimen already consisting of a diuretic and a beta-adrenergic blocking agent.
- **Maintenance Range:** Usually 5 mg to 40 mg per day [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** 100 mg per day [Australian].
- **Frequency:** Can be administered as a single daily dose or in divided doses. If a reduction in diastolic pressure >30 mmHg is required, divided dosing is recommended to minimise diurnal fluctuations [Australian].

### Titration and Rapid Reduction
- **Titration:** Adjustments should be made at intervals of 3 days or longer [Australian].
- **Rapid Reduction:** In a monitored setting with continuous blood pressure tracking, increments of 5 mg every 6 hours may be used [Australian].

### Concomitant Medication Requirements
- **Diuretics:** **Must** be co-administered to maintain salt/water balance. If weight gain exceeds 2.0–2.5 kg on thiazides, a more potent agent (e.g., furosemide) is required [Australian].
- **Sympathetic Suppressants:** Usually required to limit reflex tachycardia. Beta-blockers (equivalent to 80–160 mg propranolol) are preferred. If contraindicated, methyldopa or clonidine may be used (started 24 hours prior to minoxidil) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Phaeochromocytoma (may stimulate catecholamine release) [Australian].
- Pulmonary hypertension secondary to mitral stenosis [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to minoxidil or any tablet excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Salt and Water Retention:** If used without adequate diuretics, minoxidil can cause significant fluid retention, leading to peripheral oedema, neck vein distention, and congestive heart failure. Monitoring of body weight and electrolytes is mandatory [Australian].
- **Tachycardia and Angina:** Reflex tachycardia may precipitate angina pectoris in patients with underlying coronary artery disease unless protected by beta-blockers [Australian].
- **Pericarditis/Effusion:** Reported in 3% to 4% of patients. Close monitoring for pericardial effusion is required; echocardiography should be performed if suspicion arises [Australian].
- **ECG Alterations:** Approximately 60% of patients experience T-wave changes (magnitude/direction) shortly after initiation. These are typically asymptomatic and disappear with continued treatment [Australian].
- **Hypertrichosis:** Elongation and thickening of fine body hair occur in most patients within 3–6 weeks. This is reversible within 1–3 months of cessation [Australian].
- **Inappropriate Use:** Not recommended for labile or mild hypertension, or for hypertension ameliorated by surgery (e.g., coarctation of the aorta) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most patients experience a reduction in pre-existing adverse events related to their hypertension, but may develop the following:

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Cardiac; Skin; Investigations | Tachycardia, Pericarditis; Hypertrichosis, hair colour changes; Abnormal ECG |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Metabolism; Cardiac; Gastrointestinal | Fluid retention, Oedema; Pericardial effusion, Cardiac tamponade; Gastrointestinal disorder |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Respiratory; Reproductive | Pleural effusion; Breast tenderness |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Blood; Skin | Leucopenia, Thrombocytopenia; Stevens-Johnson syndrome, Bullous dermatitis, Rash |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Skin | Toxic epidermal necrolysis |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Cardiac | Angina pectoris |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Guanethidine:** Co-administration can result in profound orthostatic hypotension. If possible, guanethidine should be discontinued well before starting minoxidil. If not feasible, minoxidil must be started in a hospital setting [Australian].
- **Other Antihypertensives:** Effects are additive. Interaction with agents causing orthostatic hypotension may result in excessive blood pressure reduction [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. Associated with hypertrichosis in newborns following in utero exposure. Not recommended during pregnancy or in women of childbearing potential not using contraception [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Minoxidil is excreted in human milk. Not recommended for nursing mothers as a risk to the infant cannot be excluded [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited data. For children >12 years: Start 5 mg daily; increase by 5–10 mg every 3 days up to 50 mg, then in increments of 25 mg to a maximum of 100 mg/day [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage requirements may be lower in patients with renal failure or those undergoing chronic dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics are altered (approx. 50% increase in AUC in mild cirrhosis). Consider starting at a reduced dose once daily and titrate to effect [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selectively relaxes arteriolar smooth muscle, reducing peripheral vascular resistance. It does not interfere with vasomotor reflexes or directly stimulate the heart/kidneys [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Elicits reflex-mediated increases in cardiac output and plasma renin activity, alongside salt/water retention [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** ≥95% absorbed via the GI tract; peak serum levels within 1 hour [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Does not bind to plasma proteins or enter the CNS [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Predominantly via glucuronic acid conjugation [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is 4–4.5 hours, but hypotensive action duration exceeds 24 hours due to tissue localisation. Metabolites are excreted in urine and can be removed by haemodialysis [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C. Protect from moisture [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Typically supplied in HDPE bottles of 100 tablets [Australian].
