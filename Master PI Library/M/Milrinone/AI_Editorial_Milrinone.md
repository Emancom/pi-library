# Editorial Product Information: Milrinone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial was developed using official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview of milrinone for healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines governing prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Milrinone (as lactate).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective phosphodiesterase III (PDE III) inhibitor; bipyridine derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Milrinone-Baxter [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C01CE02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrated solution for infusion containing 10 mg milrinone per 10 mL (1 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion (requires dilution).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Congestive Heart Failure:** Short-term (up to 48 hours) intravenous therapy for severe congestive heart failure in intensive care or coronary care settings when patients are unresponsive to conventional maintenance therapy (e.g., diuretics, digitalis, or ACE inhibitors) [Australian].
- **Cardiac Surgery:** Management of low output states following cardiac surgery, including assistance in weaning patients from cardio-pulmonary bypass pumps [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
Milrinone must be administered as a loading dose followed by a continuous maintenance infusion. Dosage should be titrated according to clinical and hemodynamic response (e.g., cardiac output, pulmonary capillary wedge pressure).

- **Loading Dose:** 50 µg/kg administered slowly over 10 minutes [Australian].
- **Maintenance Infusion:**
    - **Minimum:** 0.375 µg/kg/min (Total daily dose: 0.60 mg/kg) [Australian].
    - **Standard:** 0.50 µg/kg/min (Total daily dose: 0.77 mg/kg) [Australian].
    - **Maximum:** 0.75 µg/kg/min (Total daily dose: 1.13 mg/kg) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted prior to use. Recommended diluents include 0.45% sodium chloride, 0.9% sodium chloride, or 5% glucose [Australian].
- **Incompatibility:** Do **not** dilute in sodium bicarbonate intravenous solution [Australian].
- **Preparation:** Use non-PVC (polypropylene) bags for compatibility. Prepared mixtures not used within 24 hours should be discarded [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Infusion site monitoring is required to prevent extravasation [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Valvular Disease:** Severe obstructive aortic or pulmonary valvular disease [Australian].
- **Subaortic Stenosis:** Hypertrophic subaortic stenosis [Australian].
- **Surgical Relief:** Use as a substitute for surgical correction of obstructive lesions is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to milrinone, other bipyridines, or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Myocardial Infarction:** Not recommended during the acute phase of post-myocardial infarction due to the potential for increased myocardial oxygen consumption (MVO2) [Australian].
- **Arrhythmias:** Milrinone may increase ventricular ectopy and has been associated with supraventricular and ventricular arrhythmias. Continuous ECG monitoring is mandatory [Australian].
- **Atrial Flutter/Fibrillation:** Milrinone may increase the ventricular response rate due to slight shortening of A-V node conduction time. Digitalization prior to milrinone should be considered in these patients [Australian].
- **Hypotension:** Due to its vasodilatory effects, milrinone may induce hypotension. Exercise caution in patients with low blood pressure or those who exhibit a significant drop in pressure during therapy. If hypotension occurs, the infusion should be slowed or stopped until stabilized [Australian].
- **Laboratory Monitoring:** Monitor fluid balance, electrolytes (especially potassium), and renal function. Improvement in cardiac output may cause diuresis, necessitating a reduction in diuretic dose and potassium supplementation to prevent arrhythmias [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorized by frequency based on clinical study data [Australian]:

| Frequency | System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Cardiovascular | Ventricular arrhythmias (12.1%), including ventricular ectopic activity (8.5%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Cardiovascular | Supraventricular arrhythmias (3.8%), Hypotension (2.9%), Angina/Chest pain (1.2%), Non-sustained ventricular tachycardia (2.8%) |
| | Nervous System | Headache (2.9%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Cardiovascular | Sustained ventricular tachycardia (1%), Ventricular fibrillation (0.2%) |
| | Hepatobiliary | Abnormal liver function tests |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Renal/Urinary | Renal failure (secondary to hypotension) |
| | Blood/Lymphatic | Thrombocytopenia |
| | Metabolic | Hypokalaemia |
| | Nervous System | Tremor |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Cardiovascular | Torsades de pointes |
| | Skin | Rash |
| | Immune System | Anaphylactic shock, Bronchospasm |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Life-threatening arrhythmias are infrequent but more likely in patients with pre-existing arrhythmias or metabolic abnormalities [Australian]. Cases of patent ductus arteriosus have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Chemical Incompatibilities:** Immediate precipitation occurs if **furosemide** or **bumetanide** is injected into intravenous lines containing milrinone. Do not administer these agents in the same line [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No adverse clinical manifestations have been observed when used concurrently with digitalis glycosides, lidocaine, quinidine, hydralazine, prazosin, nitrates, ACE inhibitors (captopril), beta-blockers, anticoagulants (heparin, warfarin), or insulin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. There are no adequate or well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies showed increased fetal resorption at high IV doses in rabbits. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Use with caution. It is unknown whether milrinone is excreted in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no effect on male or female fertility at oral doses up to 32 mg/kg/day [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No special dosage recommendations are required. Clinical responses and adverse event profiles in the elderly are similar to those in younger patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Terminal elimination half-life is significantly increased. Reductions in the starting infusion rate are necessary based on the following creatinine clearance (CrCl) guidelines [Australian]:
    - CrCl 5 mL/min/1.73m²: 0.20 µg/kg/min
    - CrCl 10 mL/min/1.73m²: 0.23 µg/kg/min
    - CrCl 30 mL/min/1.73m²: 0.33 µg/kg/min
    - CrCl 50 mL/min/1.73m²: 0.43 µg/kg/min
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Milrinone is a positive inotrope and vasodilator with minimal chronotropic activity. It selectively inhibits the Peak III cAMP phosphodiesterase isozyme in cardiac and vascular muscle. This leads to increased intracellular cyclic AMP (cAMP), enhancing intracellular ionized calcium and contractile force in the myocardium, while facilitating relaxation in vascular muscle [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 0.38 to 0.45 L/kg. It is approximately 70% bound to human plasma proteins [Australian].
- **Metabolism:** Primarily excreted via the urine; 12% is recovered as an o-glucuronide metabolite [Australian].
- **Elimination:** Primarily renal. Approximately 83% of the dose is excreted as unchanged drug in the urine. The mean terminal elimination half-life is approximately 2.3 to 2.4 hours in patients with heart failure [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamics
Milrinone produces a dose-related increase in cardiac output and a decrease in pulmonary capillary wedge pressure and systemic vascular resistance. Therapeutic effects are typically observed at plasma concentrations of 100 ng/mL to 300 ng/mL [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Cardiopulmonary Bypass Weaning:** In a double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 100% of patients receiving milrinone were successfully weaned from bypass, compared to 33% in the placebo group [Australian].
- **Hemodynamic Response:** In CHF patients, standard dosing (0.50 µg/kg/min) resulted in significant increases in cardiac index (approx. 38%) and decreases in pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (approx. 23%) and systemic vascular resistance (approx. 21%) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C. Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Handling:** For single use in one patient only. Discard any remaining residue [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
