# Editorial Product Information: Methyl Aminolevulinate

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following region: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesizes clinical data into a single reference point.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing or administering this therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methyl aminolevulinate (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Antineoplastic agent; porphyrin precursor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Metvix** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source [Australian].
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Topical cream containing 160 mg/g of methyl aminolevulinate (as hydrochloride) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical application followed by light activation (Photodynamic Therapy - PDT).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Galderma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Metvix** is indicated for the treatment of the following conditions in adults over 18 years of age [Australian]:
- **Actinic Keratoses (AK):** Treatment of thin or non-hyperkeratotic and non-pigmented lesions on the face and scalp, specifically when other registered therapies are considered unacceptable.
- **Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC):** Primary treatment of superficial and/or nodular BCC in cases where surgical intervention is deemed inappropriate.
- **Squamous Cell Carcinoma in situ (Bowen’s Disease):** Treatment of biopsy-proven lesions where surgery is considered inappropriate.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Elderly)
The treatment involves the topical application of cream followed by light activation, either via red LED light (conventional PDT or c-PDT) or natural daylight (DL-PDT).

- **Actinic Keratoses:** 
    - **c-PDT:** One session.
    - **DL-PDT:** One session (exclusively for face and scalp lesions).
    - Assessment occurs at 3 months; non-complete responders should be retreated [Australian].
- **Basal Cell Carcinoma and Bowen’s Disease:**
    - Two sessions of c-PDT administered with a one-week interval between sessions.
    - Assessment occurs at 3 months. Clinical data suggests approximately 25-30% of BCC patients and 20% of Bowen's disease patients may require retreatment at the 3-month mark [Australian].

### Administration Instructions (c-PDT)
1. **Lesion Preparation:** Scales and crusts must be removed and the surface roughened. For nodular BCC, the epidermal keratin layer should be removed; exposed tumour material should be gently removed without excising beyond margins.
2. **Application:** Apply a 1 mm thick layer of cream to the lesion and a 5-10 mm margin of surrounding healthy skin.
3. **Occlusion:** Cover the area with an occlusive dressing for 3 hours.
4. **Illumination:** Remove dressing and clean with saline. Immediately expose to red light:
    - **Continuous spectrum:** 570-670 nm (total dose 75 J/cm²).
    - **LED source:** ~630 nm (total dose 37 J/cm²).
    - Surface intensity must not exceed 200 mW/cm².
5. **Safety:** Both patient and operator must wear protective goggles corresponding to the lamp spectrum [Australian].

### Administration Instructions (DL-PDT for AK)
1. **Sunscreen:** Apply SPF 30+ chemical-filter sunscreen to all exposed areas 15 minutes prior to lesion preparation. Avoid physical filters (e.g., zinc oxide, titanium dioxide) as they inhibit efficacy.
2. **Application:** Prepare lesions (as above) and apply cream. **No occlusion** is required.
3. **Exposure:** Start daylight exposure within 30 minutes of application and continue for 2 hours outdoors. In the event of rain or unsuitable weather, DL-PDT should not be used.
4. **Post-Exposure:** Remove cream with saline [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients, specifically including **arachis oil (peanut oil)** and **almond oil** [Australian].
- Morpheaform basal cell carcinoma [Australian].
- Invasive squamous cell carcinoma of the skin [Australian].
- Porphyria [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Administration:** Avoid direct eye contact; do not apply to eyelids or mucous membranes [Australian].
- **Sensitization:** Methyl aminolevulinate may cause skin sensitization, potentially leading to angioedema, application site eczema, or allergic contact dermatitis [Australian].
- **Excipient Reactions:** Contains cetostearyl alcohol (local skin reactions), arachis oil, and hydroxybenzoates (potential delayed allergic reactions) [Australian].
- **UV Exposure:** Discontinue any UV therapy prior to treatment. Avoid sun exposure on treated sites and surrounding skin for several days post-treatment [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** Pain during red light illumination may induce increased blood pressure. Monitoring of blood pressure is recommended in patients with a history of hypertension who experience severe pain [Australian].
- **Transient Global Amnesia:** Conventional PDT may, in very rare instances, precipitate transient global amnesia due to the stress and pain of illumination. Discontinue PDT immediately if confusion or disorientation occurs [Australian].
- **Specific Lesion Limitations:** 
    - No experience treating thick (hyperkeratotic), pigmented, or highly infiltrating lesions.
    - No experience in patients with xeroderma pigmentosum, Gorlin’s syndrome, or those on immunosuppressive therapy [Australian].
    - Bowen’s disease: No experience for lesions >40 mm or those located on genitalia [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Approximately 60-80% of patients undergoing c-PDT experience localized phototoxic reactions.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects (Skin and Subcutaneous) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Pain, burning sensation (typically during/after illumination), erythema, oedema, itching, stinging/tingling. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Crusting, ulceration, blisters, suppuration, infection, peeling, bleeding, hypo/hyperpigmentation. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Rash, urticaria, eczema, skin irritation. |

- **Non-Local Effects (Uncommon):** Headache, dizziness, eye pain/irritation, wound hemorrhage, nausea, and fatigue [Australian].
- **Daylight PDT:** Generally associated with significantly lower pain scores compared to c-PDT (e.g., 0.8 vs 5.7 on an 11-point scale) [Australian].
- **Serious/Post-Marketing Reports:** Angioedema, facial oedema, hypertension, and transient global amnesia have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No specific interaction studies have been conducted [Australian].
- **Management Note:** Metvix cream should not be mixed with other drugs or preparations [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. No clinical data on exposed pregnancies are available. Animal reproductive studies have not been performed. Use is not recommended during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion into human milk is unknown. Due to potential risk, breastfeeding should be discontinued for **48 hours** following the application of Metvix [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Studies on reproductive toxicity have not been performed [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established. Use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required for elderly patients [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No information available [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No information available [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Methyl aminolevulinate is a pro-drug. After topical application, porphyrins (including Photoactive Protoporphyrin IX - PpIX) accumulate intracellularly in the treated lesions. Upon light activation in the presence of oxygen, singlet oxygen is formed, leading to mitochondrial damage and cell death of the target cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** In vitro studies show a mean cumulative absorption of 0.26% of the dose through human skin after 24 hours. A skin depot of 4.9% was observed.
    - **Distribution:** Selective accumulation of porphyrins in lesions compared to normal skin.
    - **Elimination:** Following illumination, complete photobleaching occurs, with porphyrin levels returning to pre-treatment values [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Actinic Keratosis:** Pivotal studies showed complete response rates of 79-81% with Metvix-PDT vs. 13-21% with placebo. A study comparing one vs. two sessions (7 days apart) showed a single session was non-inferior (89% vs. 80% patient complete response) [Australian].
- **Basal Cell Carcinoma:** In nodular BCC, Metvix-PDT demonstrated a 76% histological complete response at 6 months. In superficial BCC, Metvix-PDT was comparable to cryotherapy (95% vs 91% response) [Australian].
- **Bowen’s Disease:** Metvix-PDT was superior to placebo (ITT 86% vs 24% response) and non-inferior to cryotherapy and 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) at 3 months [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 8°C. **Refrigerate** [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:**
    - Unopened: 15 months.
    - After first opening: 3 months [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Epoxy-coated aluminum tubes (2 g) [Australian].
