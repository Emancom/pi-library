# Editorial Product Information: mifepristone + misoprostol

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **mifepristone + misoprostol**
- Chemical class:
  - Mifepristone: progesterone receptor antagonist with antiglucocorticoid activity.
  - Misoprostol: prostaglandin (prostaglandin analogue).
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: oral (mifepristone), buccal (misoprostol) as described in dosing instructions.
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form physical descriptions, or schedule listings.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Medical termination of an intrauterine pregnancy up to 63 days gestation (use in females of childbearing age).  
  - Ultrasound confirmation of gestational duration is recommended; if ultrasound is not possible, exercise extra caution.  
  - Ectopic pregnancy should be excluded prior to use.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Mifepristone: **200 mg orally** (single dose).
- Misoprostol: **800 micrograms** administered buccally (total), given **36–48 hours after** mifepristone.
  - Buccal administration details as described: misoprostol kept between the cheek and gum for ~30 minutes; any fragments may then be swallowed with water.

### Dosing by indication
- Indication: medical termination of intrauterine pregnancy up to 63 days gestation — dose regimen as above.

### Administration instructions
- Confirm intrauterine pregnancy (ultrasound preferred) and exclude ectopic pregnancy prior to initiation.
- Remove any intrauterine device (IUD) before administration.
- Take tablets 2 hours before or 2 hours after a meal (no robust data on food effects; this timing is recommended).
- Ensure consent is obtained and the patient has access to 24‑hour emergency care for at least 14 days after initiation.
- Follow-up visit required 14–21 days after mifepristone to confirm complete termination (clinical exam, ultrasound, or beta‑hCG measurement).

### Titration schedules
- Not applicable / Not specified in available sources.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
Do not prescribe **mifepristone + misoprostol** in patients with any of the following:
- Lack of access to emergency medical care within 14 days following start of treatment.
- Suspected or confirmed ectopic pregnancy.
- Uncontrolled asthma.
- Intrauterine device (IUD) in place.
- Uncertainty about gestational age.
- Chronic adrenal failure.
- Concurrent long‑term systemic corticosteroid therapy.
- Suspected or known haemorrhagic disorders or ongoing treatment with anticoagulants.
- Known hypersensitivity to mifepristone, misoprostol (or any prostaglandin), or any excipients in the product.
- Pregnancy not confirmed by an ultrasound or a biological test (urine or serum hCG).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- Not specified in available sources as a formal boxed/black‑box statement. (If local jurisdiction imposes boxed warnings, refer to that jurisdiction's official PI.)

### General warnings and precautions
- Ensure legal and consent requirements are met per local legislation prior to treatment.
- Patients must have reliable access to medical care for emergency management of incomplete abortion or bleeding.
- Follow-up within 14–21 days is mandatory to verify complete expulsion and to exclude ongoing pregnancy.
- Ectopic pregnancy must be excluded before treatment.
- Rhesus status should be assessed and managed according to current clinical guidelines for induced abortion.
- In case of suspected acute adrenal failure, dexamethasone administration is recommended.
- Because of mifepristone’s antiglucocorticoid activity, the efficacy of long‑term corticosteroid therapy (including inhaled corticosteroids in asthmatic patients) may be decreased for approximately 3–4 days after mifepristone dosing; therapy should be adjusted accordingly.
- Prostaglandins (including misoprostol) have been associated with rare serious cardiovascular events (cardiac arrest, myocardial infarction, coronary artery spasm, severe hypotension); treat women with established cardiovascular disease or cardiovascular risk factors with caution.
- Bronchospasm may occur with prostaglandins; exercise caution in patients with a history of asthma or bronchospasm.
- Seizures have been reported with some prostaglandins and prostaglandin analogues; consider this risk in patients with epilepsy.
- Severe cutaneous adverse reactions (including toxic epidermal necrolysis and acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis) have been reported with mifepristone; discontinue immediately if such reactions occur and do not re‑treat.
- Angioedema (face, lips, tongue, larynx) and anaphylaxis have been reported post‑marketing; airway compromise may be life‑threatening — treat promptly.
- There are no data available in patients with inherited porphyria.
- The method requires patient involvement and understanding: adherence to both medications in sequence, follow‑up schedule, and access to emergency care must be emphasized.

### Populations not studied / recommended with caution
- Not recommended (in absence of specific data) in patients with:
  - Cardiovascular disease
  - Hypertensive disease
  - Hepatic disease
  - Respiratory disease
  - Renal disease
  - Diabetes
  - Severe anaemia
  - Malnutrition
  - Heavy smokers
- Women older than 35 years who smoke ≥15 cigarettes/day: treat with caution (these patients were generally excluded from clinical trials).

### Monitoring requirements
- Routine follow-up 14–21 days post‑treatment to confirm completion (clinical exam, ultrasound, or beta‑hCG).
- Monitor for signs of heavy or prolonged vaginal bleeding and counsel patients to seek urgent care if heavy bleeding occurs.
- Be alert for signs of infection or sepsis after treatment — persistent fever (≥38°C), severe abdominal pain, pelvic tenderness, malaise, nausea, vomiting or diarrhea — and obtain full blood count if infection suspected.
- Consider immediate empirical antimicrobial therapy with anaerobic coverage (including Clostridium species) if sepsis is suspected.
- Monitor haemoglobin in cases with significant bleeding or suspected anaemia.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Expulsion may occur prior to misoprostol administration in ≈3% of cases; follow-up is still required.
- Persistent bleeding after follow-up may indicate incomplete abortion or ectopic pregnancy; further evaluation and management are required.
- Heavy bleeding requiring blood transfusion observed in up to 0.2% of treated women (≤63 days gestation); haemostatic curettage required in up to 5% during the medical method.
- In case of ongoing pregnancy after treatment, counsel regarding risks of fetal exposure to these agents and options for management including surgical termination; consider specialist ultrasound follow‑up.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions — mifepristone
- Concomitant CYP3A4 inhibitors:
  - Itraconazole increased mifepristone AUC by ~2.6‑fold; metabolite exposures (22‑hydroxy and N‑demethyl mifepristone) were also altered. Cmax changes noted. No formal dose adjustment recommended, but caution is warranted.
  - Other CYP3A4 inhibitors (examples listed in sources): ketoconazole, erythromycin, grapefruit juice — caution advised.
- Concomitant CYP3A4 inducers:
  - Rifampicin decreased mifepristone AUC by ~6.3‑fold and reduced metabolite exposures markedly; reduced efficacy of mifepristone may be expected with strong/moderate inducers. Examples of inducers: rifampicin, dexamethasone, St. John’s Wort, anticonvulsants (phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine).
  - In patients receiving strong or moderate CYP3A4 inducers, consider alternative termination methods or ensure close follow‑up to confirm pregnancy termination.
- Effect of mifepristone on other drugs:
  - Mifepristone is an inhibitor of CYP3A4 (in vitro and in vivo); due to irreversible CYP binding and slow elimination of mifepristone, inhibition may persist for a prolonged period post‑dosing. Caution when co‑administering drugs that are CYP3A4 substrates with narrow therapeutic ranges (including some agents used during general anaesthesia).

### Pharmacokinetic interactions — misoprostol
- Misoprostol has no known clinically significant drug interactions from the available sources.
- No induction of cytochrome P450 enzymes observed in studies.
- Protein binding of misoprostol acid was generally not altered by many tested drugs; salicylic acid at high concentration lowered protein binding from 84% to 52%, which is not considered clinically significant given misoprostol acid's short half‑life.
- No drug interactions attributed to misoprostol in extensive clinical trials.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Concomitant long‑term corticosteroid therapy: mifepristone's antiglucocorticoid effects may reduce corticosteroid efficacy for ~3–4 days; adjust corticosteroid therapy as needed.

### Clinically significant interactions and management notes
- Avoid or use caution when prescribing with strong CYP3A4 inducers — expect reduced mifepristone efficacy; consider alternative termination methods or ensure strict follow‑up.
- With strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, increased mifepristone exposure may occur; exercise caution.
- Avoid re‑challenge with mifepristone after severe cutaneous adverse reactions.
- Monitor for reduced corticosteroid efficacy; if acute adrenal failure suspected, treat with dexamethasone.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Use for medical termination of pregnancy up to 63 days gestation. If the pregnancy continues after treatment, fetal exposure to **mifepristone + misoprostol** is associated with increased risk of congenital anomalies.
- Reported fetal risks with ongoing pregnancy after exposure:
  - Association of misoprostol exposure (and combined exposures) with Moebius syndrome (congenital facial paralysis), amniotic band syndrome (limb deformities/amputations, clubfoot, oligodactyly), and central nervous system anomalies (anencephaly, hydrocephaly, cerebellar hypoplasia, neural tube defects).
- Human data:
  - A review of 105 pregnancies exposed in the first trimester (46 to mifepristone alone; 59 to both mifepristone and misoprostol) found 94 live births (90.4%) and 10 miscarriages (9.6%); overall major congenital malformation rate was 4.2% (95% CI: 1.2–10.4%).
  - Data remain limited; a teratogenic risk cannot be excluded.
- Animal data:
  - Mifepristone: abortifacient; foetal skull/brain malformations seen in some species at sub‑abortive doses; effects may be secondary to progesterone antagonism.
  - Misoprostol: embryotoxicity and skeletal/cleft palate anomalies observed at high doses in animal studies.
- Pregnancy category/system:
  - Not specified in available sources for jurisdictions in a standardized format (e.g., Australian pregnancy category, US pregnancy category). Use local jurisdiction guidance where applicable.

### Breastfeeding
- Not specified in available sources.

### Fertility
- During clinical trials, pregnancies occurred between embryo expulsion and resumption of menses.
- Animal data: mifepristone inhibited estrous cycling in rats at low doses in a reversible manner; no subsequent effects on reproductive performance observed.
- Misoprostol: increased pre‑implantation loss in animal fertility studies at high doses.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Limited data available for use in women <18 years of age. Administration to adolescents <18 years should be undertaken with caution.
- No relevant use in prepubertal paediatric population for the indication.

### Elderly use
- Not relevant for the indication.

### Renal impairment
- No dosage adjustment necessary with renal insufficiency when administered at recommended doses (as stated in available sources).

### Hepatic impairment
- Pharmacokinetic data: mifepristone and metabolites showed decreased peak and exposure in participants with moderate hepatic impairment compared with healthy matched participants; however, no dose adjustments are recommended for moderate hepatic impairment at the recommended doses.
- Overall: no dose adjustment recommended for hepatic insufficiency at recommended doses per available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Mifepristone: progesterone receptor antagonist (antiprogestin) with antiglucocorticoid activity; antagonism of progesterone leads to changes in the uterine lining facilitating termination of pregnancy.
- Misoprostol: prostaglandin/prostaglandin analogue that induces uterine contractions and cervical softening to complete expulsion.

### Pharmacokinetics (as specified in available sources)
- Mifepristone:
  - Drug–drug interaction data show substantial alteration in exposure with CYP3A4 modulators (e.g., itraconazole increases AUC ~2.6‑fold; rifampicin decreases AUC ~6.3‑fold).
  - Mifepristone is an inhibitor of CYP3A4 and may exert prolonged CYP3A4 inhibition due to irreversible binding and slow elimination.
  - In moderate hepatic impairment, mifepristone exposure and peak concentrations decreased compared to healthy subjects; nonetheless, no dose adjustment is recommended at the studied dose.
- Misoprostol:
  - Misoprostol acid protein binding is low to moderate and generally unaffected by many common drugs; salicylic acid at high concentration decreased protein binding but was not considered clinically significant given the short elimination half‑life.
  - Misoprostol has not demonstrated induction of cytochrome P450 enzymes in laboratory studies.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Mifepristone antagonizes progesterone receptors disrupting pregnancy maintenance; antiglucocorticoid effects can reduce corticosteroid efficacy temporarily.
- Misoprostol causes uterine contractions and cervical ripening to facilitate expulsion.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Efficacy outcomes summarized from available sources:
  - Medical termination up to 63 days gestation with the described regimen results in successful termination in the majority; up to 7% experience failure (including continuing pregnancy and incomplete abortion) prior to 63 days gestation. Approximately 1% have continuing pregnancies; the remainder of failures require surgical intervention (curettage).
  - Expulsion may occur prior to misoprostol in about 3% of cases.
- Safety outcomes:
  - Reviews estimate serious infection rates after medical abortion <1%; very rare cases of fatal septic shock have been reported, often associated with vaginal misoprostol use in post‑marketing reports.
  - A review of 105 pregnancies exposed in the first trimester reported 94 live births and an overall rate of major congenital malformations of 4.2% (95% CI: 1.2–10.4%); data are limited and interpretation cautious.

- Trial design and primary outcomes: Detailed pivotal trial protocols, sample sizes, randomization, and primary endpoint definitions are not specified in available sources.
