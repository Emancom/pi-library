# Editorial Product Information: Methenamine hippurate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating data from the provided regulatory source.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics in a specific jurisdiction when prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active ingredient:** Methenamine hippurate, a urinary tract antibacterial.
- **Brand name(s):** **Hexamine Micro** [Australian].
- **ATC code:** J01XX05.
- **Dosage form(s) and strength(s):** 1 gram tablets (capsule-shaped, biconvex, debossed with ‘H’ and ‘1’) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/sponsor:** Micro Labs Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prophylaxis or suppression of bacteriuria:** Indicated for patients with chronic or recurrent urinary tract infections (UTIs) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 1 gram (one tablet) administered orally twice daily [Australian].
- **Paediatric Dosing (12 years and older):** 1 gram (one tablet) administered orally twice daily [Australian].
- **Administration instructions:** For maximum effectivity, treatment requires an acidic urinary environment and adequate exposure time to the released formaldehyde (see Section 10).
- **Missed dose instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **methenamine hippurate**, formaldehyde, or any identified excipients (colloidal anhydrous silica, povidone, magnesium stearate) [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment, defined as an eGFR < 10 mL/min/1.73m² [Australian].
- Severe hepatic dysfunction [Australian].
- Severe dehydration [Australian].
- Metabolic acidosis [Australian].
- Gout [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed warnings:** None [Australian].
- **Urinary Acidity:** The efficacy of **methenamine hippurate** is strictly dependent on the acidity of the urine. If urine pH increases, the conversion to formaldehyde is reduced.
- **Patient Monitoring:** Clinicians should investigate underlying causes and risk factors for recurrent UTIs (e.g., hygiene, voiding habits) before pharmacological intervention. Bacteriological analysis is recommended to confirm diagnosis [Australian].
- **Catheterisation and Structural Abnormalities:** The drug is not recommended for patients with neurogenic bladder, renal tract abnormalities, or those utilizing long-term catheters [Australian].
- **Concurrent Infection:** If active bacteriuria is present, it should be cleared with appropriate antibiotics until the urine is sterile (<10⁴ counts/mL) before starting **methenamine hippurate** prophylaxis [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** 
    - **Oestriol:** Methenamine/formaldehyde in urine can cause falsely low oestriol results when using acid hydrolysis during pregnancy; enzymatic hydrolysis should be used instead.
    - **Other tests:** May interfere with urinary determinations of steroids, catecholamines, and 5-hydroxyindole acetic acid (5-HIAA), depending on the analytical method [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is derived from well-established use and limited published literature.

| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Uncommon (0.1–1%) | Gastric irritation, nausea, vomiting |
| | Not known | Diarrhoea, abdominal pain |
| **Skin/Subcutaneous** | Uncommon (0.1–1%) | Rash, pruritus |
| **Renal/Urinary** | Uncommon (0.1–1%) | Bladder irritation, dysuria |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** High doses (4 to 8 grams daily for 3 to 4 weeks) have been associated with albuminuria and hematuria [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Sulphonamides:** Concurrent administration must be avoided, as certain sulphonamides may form insoluble crystalline precipitates with formaldehyde in the urine [Australian].
- **Alkalising Agents:** Substances that increase urinary pH (e.g., potassium citrate, acetazolamide) reduce the hydrolysis of methenamine into formaldehyde, thereby diminishing therapeutic efficacy. These agents should be avoided [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category A:** Drugs which have been taken by a large number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age without any proven increase in the frequency of malformations or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the fetus having been observed. 
    - Despite the category, animal studies in rabbits (at 3x clinical dose) showed increased post-implantation loss and limited foetal deformities. As a precautionary measure, it is preferable to avoid use during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Methenamine is excreted in breast milk, but the quantities are considered insignificant to the infant; breastfeeding is considered permissible [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Human data are unavailable. Rat studies showed no effect on female fertility; male fertility has not been adequately assessed [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric use:** Not recommended for children under 12 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly use:** While the elderly have higher UTI risk factors (e.g., oestrogen changes, prostate issues, mobility issues), no significant safety differences compared to younger populations have been observed [Australian].
- **Renal impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild or moderate renal insufficiency. Contraindicated in severe renal failure (eGFR < 10 mL/min/1.73m²) [Australian].
- **Hepatic impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic dysfunction [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Methenamine hippurate is a pro-drug. In acidic urine, it undergoes hydrolysis to release formaldehyde. Formaldehyde is a non-specific antibacterial that denatures bacterial proteins and nucleic acids. 
- **Spectrum of Activity:** Effective against *E. coli*, enterococci, and staphylococci. Urea-splitting organisms (*Proteus*, *Pseudomonas*) are only inhibited if the urine remains sufficiently acidic [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** A formaldehyde concentration > 25 $\mu$g/mL maintained for approximately 2 hours is bactericidal. Activity typically begins within 30 minutes of administration [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma concentration (~30 mg/L) in 1-2 hours. Steady-state peak is ~35 mg/L.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is ~0.56 L/kg (correlates to total body water).
    - **Metabolism:** A small amount is degraded by stomach acid to formaldehyde (then converted to formic acid), but the majority remains unchanged until reaching the kidneys.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted via glomerular filtration and tubular secretion. Approximately 82% of a dose is recovered in urine within 24 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Cochrane Review (2012):** Concluded methenamine hippurate may be effective for UTI prevention in patients without renal tract abnormalities, particularly for short-term use. It was found ineffective in patients with neuropathic bladder.
- **Crossover Study (Cronberg et al., 1987):** A randomised, double-blind study (n=21) comparing 1g BID to placebo over two years. The methenamine regimen resulted in significantly fewer cystitis episodes (11 vs. 41 for placebo; p<0.01) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Al-Al foil blister packs (20 or 100 tablets) or HDPE bottles with PP screw caps (100 tablets) [Australian].
