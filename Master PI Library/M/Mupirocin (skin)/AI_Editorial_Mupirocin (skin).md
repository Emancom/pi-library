# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial is based on official Product Information (PI) source(s) from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single coherent reference. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a specific region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine [USP], a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC plastic bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** No contraindications are known at this time [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, discontinue the drug and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels and monitor weekly/monthly. The target plasma free carnitine concentration is 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Oral Safety in Renal Impairment:** The safety of **oral** levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal failure. High-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Less frequent effects include body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Clinical Trial Data (ESRD Patients):** In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, the following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
    - **General:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), chest pain (6–15%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), fever (5–12%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concurrently. **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but text provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate human studies exist. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against the risks of excess carnitine intake for the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (mg/kg), which includes paediatric applications [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing adjustments are required for patients on hemodialysis (see Section 3). Oral formulations are cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** Efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients has been demonstrated in multiple studies. A 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) showed pre-dialysis trough levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Note:** The clinical impact of supplemental carnitine on specific outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat. Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
