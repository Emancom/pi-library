# Editorial Product Information: Methazolamide

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of methazolamide, synthesised from official product information provided by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current approved PI in their specific region for definitive guidance. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methazolamide (a sulfonamide derivative)
- **Chemical Class:** Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor
- **Brand Names:** Methazolamide Tablets USP (Generic) [US]
- **ATC Code:** S01EC01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 25 mg, 50 mg [US]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ANI Pharmaceuticals, Inc.; Repackaged by Bryant Ranch Prepack [US]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Glaucoma Management:** Treatment of ocular conditions where reducing intraocular pressure (IOP) is therapeutically beneficial, specifically:
    - Chronic open-angle glaucoma [US]
    - Secondary glaucoma [US]
    - Preoperative management of acute angle-closure glaucoma where IOP reduction is required prior to surgery [US]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **General Range:** The effective therapeutic dose is 50 mg to 100 mg, administered two or three times daily [US].
- **Combination Therapy:** Methazolamide may be used concomitantly with miotic or osmotic agents [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets are for oral use.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Electrolyte Depletion:** Depressed serum sodium and/or potassium levels [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Disease:** Cases of marked kidney or liver disease or dysfunction [US].
- **Hepatic Cirrhosis:** Use in patients with cirrhosis may precipitate hepatic encephalopathy [US].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Adrenal gland failure [US].
- **Metabolic Derangement:** Hyperchloremic acidosis [US].
- **Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Contraindicated for long-term administration in patients with angle-closure glaucoma, as organic closure of the angle may occur despite lowered IOP [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Severe Sulfonamide Reactions:** Although rare, fatalities have occurred due to severe reactions to sulfonamides, including:
    - Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS)
    - Toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN)
    - Fulminant hepatic necrosis
    - Agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, and other blood dyscrasias [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Drug must be discontinued immediately if hypersensitivity or serious reactions occur. Hypersensitivity may recur upon re-administration of sulfonamides regardless of the route [US].
- **Acidosis:** Use with caution in patients with pulmonary obstruction or emphysema where alveolar ventilation is impaired, as the drug may precipitate or aggravate acidosis [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Hematology:** A baseline Complete Blood Count (CBC) and platelet count are recommended prior to therapy and at regular intervals during treatment [US].
- **Electrolytes:** Periodic monitoring of serum electrolytes is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions occur most frequently early in the course of therapy.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common/Frequent** | Paresthesias (tingling in extremities), fatigue, malaise, loss of appetite, taste alteration, gastrointestinal disturbances (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea), polyuria [US]. |
| **Occasional** | Drowsiness, confusion, hearing dysfunction or tinnitus, urticaria, melena, hematuria, glycosuria, photosensitivity, convulsions [US]. |
| **Rare/Serious** | Metabolic acidosis, electrolyte imbalance, transient myopia, hepatic insufficiency, flaccid paralysis, crystalluria, renal calculi, and severe sulfonamide-associated dermatological/haematological reactions (SJS, TEN, aplastic anemia) [US]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **High-Dose Aspirin:** **Caution advised.** Concomitant use of high-dose aspirin and carbonic anhydrase inhibitors has resulted in reports of anorexia, tachypnea, lethargy, coma, and death [US].
- **Steroid Therapy:** Use with caution due to the increased risk of developing hypokalemia [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C:** Methazolamide has demonstrated teratogenicity (skeletal anomalies) in rats at approximately 40 times the human dose. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if methazolamide is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the importance of the medication to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies on fertility have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in "marked" kidney disease or dysfunction [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in "marked" liver disease; use in cirrhosis may precipitate hepatic coma [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Methazolamide is a potent inhibitor of carbonic anhydrase. In the eye, this inhibitory action decreases the secretion of aqueous humor, resulting in a reduction of intraocular pressure [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Well absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract. Peak plasma concentrations are achieved within 1 to 2 hours [US].
- **Distribution:** Distributed into plasma, cerebrospinal fluid, aqueous humor, red blood cells (RBCs), bile, and extracellular fluid. Approximately 55% is bound to plasma proteins [US].
- **Metabolism:** Linear relationship between dose and plasma levels. RBC-to-plasma ratio varies by dose (27:1 at 25 mg bid) [US].
- **Elimination:** Mean steady-state plasma elimination half-life is approximately 14 hours. About 25% of the dose is recovered unchanged in urine. Steady-state concentrations are reached within 7 days of bid/tid dosing [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
- **IOP Reduction:** Onset occurs within 2 to 4 hours; peak effect at 6 to 8 hours; total duration of 10 to 18 hours [US].
- **Systemic Effects:** Weak and transient diuretic effect; increases urinary volume and excretion of Na+, K+, and Cl-. Decreases urinary citrate excretion by ~40% and uric acid output by ~36% [US].
