# Editorial Product Information: Meropenem

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- This document serves as a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information when prescribing in a particular region. 
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not available in the provided source material and is therefore not included in this editorial.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Meropenem (as trihydrate)
- **Chemical Class:** Carbapenem antibiotic
- **Brand Name(s):** **Meropenem-AFT** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** J01DH02 (not explicitly stated in source, but standard for meropenem)
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** White to light yellow crystalline powder for injection; 500 mg and 1 g vials [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) bolus injection or infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Meropenem** is indicated for the treatment of the following infections in adults and children (aged 3 months and older) when caused by susceptible organisms or when resistance to common antibiotics is suspected:
- Community-acquired lower respiratory tract infection
- Hospital-acquired lower respiratory tract infection
- Complicated urinary tract infection
- Febrile neutropenia
- Intra-abdominal and gynaecological (poly-microbial) infections
- Complicated skin and skin structure infections
- Meningitis
- Septicaemia

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Usual Dose:** 500 mg to 1 g administered every 8 hours. The dose is dependent on the type and severity of infection, pathogen susceptibility, and patient condition.

### Dosing by Specific Indication (Adult)
- **Febrile episodes in neutropenic patients:** 1 g every 8 hours.
- **Meningitis:** 2 g every 8 hours.

### Paediatric Dosing (3 Months to 12 Years)
- **General Dose:** 10 to 40 mg/kg every 8 hours.
- **Febrile episodes in neutropenic patients:** 20 mg/kg every 8 hours.
- **Meningitis:** 40 mg/kg every 8 hours.
- **Weight > 50 kg:** Use adult dosing.

### Administration Instructions
- **IV Bolus:** Administer over approximately 5 minutes. *Note: Limited safety data for 2 g or 40 mg/kg bolus doses.*
- **IV Infusion:** Administer over approximately 15 to 30 minutes.
- **Reconstitution:** 
    - Bolus: Sterile Water for Injections (10 mL per 500 mg) to reach ~50 mg/mL.
    - Infusion: 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% glucose, then further dilute to a final concentration of 1 to 20 mg/mL (50 to 200 mL).
- **Stability:** Reconstituted solutions should be used immediately. The time from the start of reconstitution to the completion of administration must not exceed one hour. Do not freeze.

## 4. Contraindications
- Demonstrated hypersensitivity to **meropenem** or any components of the product.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious and occasionally fatal reactions have occurred with $\beta$-lactam therapy. Use with caution in patients with a history of sensitivity to carbapenems, penicillins, or other $\beta$-lactams. Cross-reactivity may occur.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** SJS, TEN, DRESS, erythema multiforme, and AGEP have been reported. Discontinue immediately if suspected.
- **CNS Effects:** Neurological sequelae and convulsions have been reported, particularly in the treatment of meningitis.
- **Rhabdomyolysis:** Reported with use; discontinue if signs/symptoms occur.
- **Pseudomembranous Colitis:** Consider this diagnosis in patients developing diarrhoea; severity may range from mild to life-threatening.
- **Valproic Acid Interaction:** Concomitant use is not recommended as **meropenem** may significantly reduce valproic acid levels to subtherapeutic ranges.
- **Pseudomonas aeruginosa:** Resistance may develop during treatment; regular sensitivity testing is recommended.

## 6. Adverse Effects
**Meropenem** is generally well-tolerated, with treatment cessation due to adverse events occurring in <1% of patients.

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Kounis syndrome (acute coronary syndrome associated with allergy) has been reported with other $\beta$-lactams.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Probenecid:** Competes for active tubular secretion, increasing **meropenem** half-life and plasma concentration. Co-administration is not recommended.
- **Valproic Acid/Sodium Valproate:** Carbapenems can reduce valproic acid levels by 60–100% within two days. This interaction is not considered manageable; avoid combination.
- **Laboratory Tests:** A positive direct or indirect Coombs test may develop.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B2. Reproduction studies in rats and monkeys showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, adequate trials in pregnant women are lacking. Use only if potential benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** **Meropenem** is excreted in human breast milk. Use is not recommended unless potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the infant.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Efficacy and safety not established for infants under 3 months of age.
- **Elderly Use:** No adjustment required for patients with normal renal function (CrCl > 50 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment necessary; however, monitor liver function in patients with pre-existing liver disorders.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage must be reduced when CrCl < 51 mL/min.

**Table 1: Renal Dosing Adjustment (Adults) [Australian]**
| Creatinine Clearance (mL/min) | Dose (unit dose = 500mg, 1g, or 2g) | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 26 to 50 | One unit dose | Every 12 hours |
| 10 to 25 | One-half unit dose | Every 12 hours |
| < 10 | One-half unit dose | Every 24 hours |

- **Haemodialysis:** **Meropenem** is cleared by haemodialysis. If treatment is required, administer the dose after the procedure.
- **Peritoneal Dialysis:** No experience reported [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Bactericidal agent that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to Penicillin Binding Proteins (PBPs). It is stable to hydrolysis by most $\beta$-lactamases, including serine $\beta$-lactamases. It is stable against human dehydropeptidase-1 (DHP-1).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Low protein binding (~2%). Penetrates well into tissues and fluids, including CSF in patients with inflamed meninges.
    - **Metabolism:** One microbiologically inactive metabolite is formed.
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 70% is excreted unchanged in urine over 12 hours. Elimination half-life is ~1 hour in adults with normal renal function and ~1.5 hours in children under 2 years.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 30°C.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Do not freeze. Use within one hour of constitution.
