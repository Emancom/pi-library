## 3. Approved Indications
- Active immunization for the prevention of measles, mumps, and rubella in individuals 12 months of age and older.
- Where the vaccine is used as a second dose in older children and adults, it may be given to individuals who previously received a first dose of another measles–mumps–rubella vaccine (i.e., can be used interchangeably as a second dose).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing
- Typical single dose (after reconstitution): approximately 0.5 mL administered subcutaneously.
- Routine schedule (primary recommendations):
  - First dose: 12 through 15 months of age.
  - Second dose: 4 through 6 years of age.
- If the recommended routine schedule is not followed and two doses are required, a minimum interval of 4 weeks (28 days) should be observed between the first and second dose.

### Administration instructions
- Reconstitution:
  - Reconstitute the lyophilized antigen component only with the provided sterile water diluent component.
  - The reconstituted vaccine should appear as a clear suspension with the expected colour (visual inspection recommended). Do not administer if particulate matter or discoloration is present.
  - After reconstitution, withdraw the entire contents into the syringe and change the needle before subcutaneous administration.
- Route: Subcutaneous injection.
- Concomitant administration:
  - Can be administered concomitantly with other live viral vaccines.
  - If not given concomitantly with another live viral vaccine, give this vaccine at least 1 month before or 1 month after other live viral vaccines to avoid potential immune interference.
- Missed dose:
  - If the routine schedule is not adhered to and two doses are indicated, give the second dose with a minimum interval of 4 weeks from the first dose.

### Titration schedules
- Not applicable.

## 5. Contraindications
- History of a severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) to any component of the vaccine or after a previous dose of any measles-, mumps-, and rubella-containing vaccine.
- Severe immunodeficiency or immunosuppression due to disease or medical therapy (do not administer; risk of disseminated vaccine virus infection).
- Pregnancy — do not administer to pregnant individuals. Pregnancy should be avoided for 1 month following vaccination.
- Hypersensitivity to latex: tip caps of the prefilled syringes contain natural rubber latex which may cause allergic reactions (precaution for persons with severe latex allergy).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed or prominent warnings
- **Do not administer to individuals who are pregnant or severely immunocompromised.** These are absolute contraindications because of the live attenuated viral nature of the vaccine.

### General warnings and precautions
- Allergic reactions: Appropriate medical treatment and supervision must be available to manage immediate allergic reactions including anaphylaxis.
- Febrile seizures: There is a risk of febrile seizure following immunization.
- Thrombocytopenia: Thrombocytopenia and thrombocytopenic purpura have been reported following vaccination.
- Syncope: Fainting can occur in association with administration of injectable vaccines; procedures to prevent injury from syncope should be in place.
- Risk of vaccine virus detection/transmission: Live attenuated rubella vaccine virus has been detected in nasopharyngeal specimens 7–28 days after vaccination; no documented confirmed cases of transmission of rubella vaccine virus have been reported in available sources.
- Limitation of effectiveness: Vaccination may not protect all susceptible individuals.
- Tuberculin skin test: Vaccination may temporarily suppress tuberculin reactivity; timing of tuberculin testing should be adjusted to avoid false-negative results (see Drug Interactions).
- Preparation check: Inspect reconstituted vaccine visually prior to administration and do not administer if particulate matter or discoloration is observed.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of anaphylaxis and other immediate adverse reactions for an appropriate period after vaccination per local clinical practice.
- Monitor for fever, seizures, signs of thrombocytopenia (e.g., unexplained bruising, petechiae), and neurologic symptoms following vaccination.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid administration to persons known to be pregnant; counsel individuals of childbearing potential to avoid pregnancy for 1 month after vaccination.
- Avoid use in persons on immunosuppressive therapy (contraindicated).
- Consider postponing tuberculin skin testing until appropriate interval after vaccination (see section 8).

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The adverse effects described below are derived from clinical trial experience and postmarketing reports contained in the available source material. Frequency categories: very common (>10%), common (1–10%), uncommon (0.1–1%), rare (<0.1%), very rare (<0.01%). Postmarketing spontaneous reports are listed as frequency not known due to uncertain denominators.

### Summary — most frequently reported solicited adverse reactions (by age group)
- Age 12 through 15 months (first dose): pain (26%), redness (25%), irritability (63%), loss of appetite (45%), drowsiness (45%), fever (35%).
- Age 4 through 6 years (second dose): pain (41%), redness (22%), swelling (11%), loss of appetite (21%), drowsiness (27%), fever (24%).
- Age 7 years and older (second dose): pain (12%), redness (12%).

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Immunosuppressive drugs:
  - Do not administer to individuals who are immunosuppressed from medical therapy or disease; vaccination in these individuals can lead to disseminated disease due to vaccine viruses.
- Immune globulins and blood products:
  - Concomitant administration of immune globulins or other blood products may contain antibodies that interfere with vaccine virus replication and reduce the expected immune response to the vaccine. Specific recommended intervals between administration of antibody-containing products and live viral vaccines are provided by public health authorities; consult local guidance.
- Tuberculin skin testing:
  - The vaccine may cause temporary suppression of tuberculin reactivity. If a tuberculin skin test is planned, it should be performed either any time before vaccination, simultaneously with vaccination, or at least 4 weeks after vaccination to avoid false-negative results.
- Other live viral vaccines:
  - The vaccine may be administered concomitantly with other live viral vaccines. If not administered concomitantly, separate administration by at least 1 month (before or after) is recommended to avoid potential immune interference.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- The vaccine contains live attenuated measles, mumps, and rubella viruses that stimulate an active immune response, resulting in production of protective antibodies against the three viruses.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Classical pharmacokinetic parameters (absorption, distribution, metabolism, elimination) as applied to small‑molecule drugs are not specified for this live attenuated vaccine in the provided sources.
- Live attenuated rubella vaccine virus has been detected in nasopharyngeal specimens 7–28 days after vaccination in some individuals; shedding has been documented, but no confirmed cases of virus transmission have been reported in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Induction of humoral immunity (antibody responses to measles, mumps, and rubella viruses) is the primary intended pharmacodynamic effect. Detailed immunogenicity titers and seroconversion rates are not fully specified in the provided excerpts.

## 12. Clinical Trials
### Overview of key clinical studies (as described in available sources)
- Multiple randomized, observer‑blind, controlled clinical studies were conducted across several countries evaluating safety and immunogenicity in children and older individuals. Study populations included thousands of participants:
  - Study 1 (NCT01702428): First-dose study in 5 countries in participants 12–15 months of age; ~5,003 participants received the investigational vaccine or a comparator measles–mumps–rubella vaccine; solicited local adverse reactions recorded for 4 days; systemic reactions recorded for up to 43 days for certain events.
  - Study 2 (NCT01681992): First and second doses given 6 weeks apart in participants 12–15 months of age; safety profile after second dose similar to first dose.
  - Study 3 (NCT01621802): Second‑dose study in children 4–6 years of age; solicited adverse reactions recorded and compared with a comparator measles–mumps–rubella vaccine when administered concomitantly with other routine pediatric vaccines.
  - Study 4 (NCT02058563): Second‑dose study in individuals ≥7 years of age (including adults); safety data recorded for 4 and 43 days respectively.
- Safety findings:
  - Solicited local and systemic reactions were recorded; the most commonly reported reactions were injection site pain and redness, fever, irritability (in younger children), drowsiness, and loss of appetite.
  - Febrile convulsions were observed at low rates in some trial cohorts.
  - The overall safety profile following second dose was similar to the safety profile after the first dose.
- Immunogenicity and primary efficacy outcomes:
  - Detailed seroconversion rates, antibody titers and primary immunogenicity outcomes are referred to in clinical study sections but specific numerical immunogenicity results are not specified in the provided excerpts.
