# Editorial Product Information: Magnesium Carbonate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is derived from Product Information (PI) and labeling documents provided by the **Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA)** (Australia) and the **Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** (United States).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating data for magnesium carbonate as used in bowel cleansing kits and antacid formulations.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Healthcare professionals must consult the approved PI in their specific region before prescribing or dispensing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Magnesium Carbonate (as Magnesium Carbonate Hydrate [TGA]).
- **Chemical Class:** Inorganic salt; Antacid/Osmotic Laxative.
- **Brand Names:** 
    - **GLYCOPREP-O KIT** (contains magnesium carbonate as a component of a multi-step kit) [TGA].
    - **Good Neighbor Pharmacy Antacid Extra Strength** [FDA].
- **ATC Code:** A02AA01 (Magnesium carbonate); A06AD (Osmotic laxatives).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Powder for solution:** 7.2 g magnesium carbonate hydrate (reconstituted with 14.3 g citric acid to form magnesium citrate) [TGA].
    - **Chewable Tablet:** 105 mg magnesium carbonate in combination with 160 mg aluminum hydroxide [FDA].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Fresenius Kabi Australia Pty Ltd [TGA].
    - Amerisource Bergen [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Bowel Cleansing:** Indicated for bowel emptying and cleansing as part of total gastrointestinal tract perfusion. This is used in preparation for gastrointestinal examinations (e.g., colonoscopy, barium enema), intravenous pyelograms (IVP), or colorectal surgery [TGA].
- **Antacid Relief:** Indicated for the symptomatic relief of acid indigestion, heartburn, sour stomach, and upset stomach associated with these symptoms [FDA].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Antacid Use [FDA]
- **Adults and Children (consult doctor for age-specific limits):** Chew 2 to 4 tablets completely, up to four times daily.
- **Timing:** Administer after meals and at bedtime, or as needed.
- **Instruction:** Do not swallow tablets whole. Follow with half a glass of water or other liquid for best results.

### 3.2 Bowel Cleansing Regimen [TGA]
Magnesium carbonate is one component of a multi-step kit (including bisacodyl and macrogol).
- **Preparation of Magnesium Citrate:** Dissolve the 21.5 g sachet (containing 7.2 g magnesium carbonate hydrate and 14.3 g citric acid) in 250 mL of warm water. The solution will effervesce and become hot. Stir until effervescence ceases.
- **Dosing Schedule:** 
    - Typically taken as the "Second Dose" at 5:00 PM on the day before the procedure (following bisacodyl).
    - Ingest the reconstituted solution slowly but completely.
- **Hydration:** Patients must maintain adequate hydration by drinking clear fluids (at least 250 mL per hour) throughout the preparation process.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Renal Impairment:** Significant clinical renal impairment [TGA].
- **Acute Abdominal Conditions:** Appendicitis, gastrointestinal obstruction, gastric retention, bowel perforation (frank or suspected), toxic megacolon, toxic colitis, or ileus [TGA].
- **General Safety:** Body weight less than 20 kg [TGA]; severe dehydration [TGA]; hypersensitivity to any ingredients [TGA].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Duration of Use:** For antacid indications, do not use the maximum dosage for more than 2 weeks [FDA].
- **Neurological Risks:** Cases of seizures have been associated with macrogol-containing bowel preparations, often linked to electrolyte abnormalities such as severe hyponatraemia. Use caution in patients with a history of seizures or those at risk of electrolyte disturbances [TGA].
- **Gastrointestinal Risks:** Excessive vomiting during bowel preparation has been linked to rare cases of oesophageal rupture (Boerhaave syndrome), particularly in elderly patients [TGA].
- **Medical Supervision:** Patients on a sodium-restricted diet should consult a doctor before use [FDA]. Caution is required in patients with congestive heart failure, diabetes, or impaired gag reflex [TGA].
- **Monitoring:** Monitor patients with kidney disease or those using medications that affect electrolyte levels (e.g., diuretics, calcium channel blockers) [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common:** Headache, dizziness, nausea, abdominal fullness, and bloating [TGA].
- **Less Common:** Abdominal cramps, vomiting, and anal irritation [TGA].
- **Serious/Rare:** 
    - Hypersensitivity reactions (angioedema, anaphylaxis) [TGA].
    - Seizures (associated with electrolyte shifts during bowel prep) [TGA].
    - Oesophageal rupture (secondary to incoercible vomiting) [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Absorption Interference:** Oral medications, especially those with a narrow therapeutic window or sustained-release formulations, may be flushed from the GI tract and not absorbed if taken within one hour of commencing or completing bowel preparation [TGA].
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Low-dose contraceptive pills may have reduced efficacy due to insufficient absorption time [TGA].
- **Specific Antibiotics:** Possible reduction in the effect of bacitracin and benzylpenicillin when used concomitantly with macrogol-based preparations [TGA].
- **General Antacid Interactions:** Antacids may interact with various prescription drugs; professional consultation is advised before co-administration [FDA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **TGA Category:** None assigned. Use only if the potential benefits clearly outweigh the risks, as it is unknown if the preparation causes fetal harm.
    - **FDA:** Not specifically detailed in provided OTC labeling.
- **Breastfeeding:** No specific lactation studies have been conducted [TGA].
- **Fertility:** No fertility studies have been conducted [TGA].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established for bowel cleansing [TGA].
- **Elderly Use:** Exercise caution due to increased risk of dehydration and electrolyte depletion; ensure adequate fluid intake [TGA].
- **Renal Impairment:** Monitor patients with impaired renal function; contraindicated in significant impairment [TGA].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No data available [TGA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Antacid:** Magnesium carbonate reacts with gastric acid to neutralize pH [FDA].
    - **Osmotic Laxative:** When reconstituted with citric acid, it forms magnesium citrate, which increases intestinal osmotic pressure, drawing water into the small intestine to stimulate peristalsis. Bowel movement typically occurs within 3 to 6 hours [TGA].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Magnesium carbonate and magnesium citrate are not significantly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract [TGA].
    - **Metabolism/Excretion:** Any absorbed magnesium is primarily excreted in the urine [TGA].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [TGA/FDA].
- Store in a dry place [TGA].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** If refrigerated (2-8°C), the solution should be used within 24 hours. If stored at room temperature, it should be used within 6 hours [TGA].
