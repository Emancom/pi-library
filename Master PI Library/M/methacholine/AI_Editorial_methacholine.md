# Editorial Product Information: Methacholine Chloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States**: US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for Provocholine.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer**: This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing and dispensing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Methacholine chloride, a parasympathomimetic (cholinergic) bronchoconstrictor agent.
- **Brand Name**: **Provocholine** [US].
- **ATC Code**: R03DX01 (Note: Not explicitly stated in the source but standard for methacholine).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths** [US]:
    - **Powder for Inhalation Solution**: 100 mg methacholine chloride in amber glass vials (requires reconstitution).
    - **Inhalation Solution Ready-to-use Kit**: Sterile plastic vials (3 mL) containing base solution (0 mg/mL), 0.0625 mg/mL, 0.25 mg/mL, 1 mg/mL, 4 mg/mL, and 16 mg/mL.
    - **Inhalation Solution Single-Dose Vial**: 16 mg/mL (48 mg/3 mL) in a plastic vial.
- **Route of Administration**: Oral inhalation only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Methapharm Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Diagnosis of Bronchial Airway Hyperreactivity**: Indicated for use in the methacholine challenge test for adults and paediatric patients (5 years of age and older) who do not have clinically apparent asthma [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Requirements
- The test must be performed in a pulmonary function laboratory or clinic by trained personnel.
- Emergency equipment and medication (rapid-acting inhaled beta-agonists) must be immediately available to treat severe bronchoconstriction.
- Baseline FEV₁ must be measured prior to testing to ensure patient safety.

### Reconstitution and Dilution (Powder) [US]
1. **Reconstitution**: Add 6.25 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to the 100 mg vial to achieve a 16 mg/mL concentration.
2. **Stepwise Dilution**: Use 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to create doubling or quadrupling strengths (from 0.0625 mg/mL up to 16 mg/mL).
3. **Equilibration**: Refrigerated solutions must reach room temperature (approx. 30 minutes) before use.

### Administration Protocols
Two primary dosing methods are utilised:
- **5-Breath Dosimeter Dosing Method**: The patient takes five deep inhalations (5 seconds each) from the nebulizer, holding each breath for 5 seconds.
- **1-Minute Tidal Breathing Dosing Method**: The patient breathes the aerosol quietly (tidal breathing) for exactly one minute.

### Procedure and Interpretation
- **Baseline**: Administer a diluent (base solution) first to determine the post-diluent FEV₁.
- **Escalation**: Administer increasing concentrations of methacholine chloride every 5 minutes.
- **Positive Result**: A reduction in FEV₁ of ≥ 20% compared to the post-diluent baseline.
- **Negative Result**: Completion of the highest dose (16 mg/mL) without a 20% reduction in FEV₁.
- **Termination**: Stop the test immediately if FEV₁ falls by ≥ 20% or if severe bronchoconstriction occurs.

### Post-Test Management
- Administer an inhaled beta-agonist immediately after the test to expedite recovery. Patients should not leave the facility until FEV₁ returns to within 90% of baseline.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known allergy to methacholine chloride or other parasympathomimetic agents [US].
- **Compromised Lung Function**:
    - Baseline FEV₁ < 60% of predicted value (adults or children) [US].
    - Baseline FEV₁ < 1.5 L (adults) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Severe Bronchoconstriction [US]
- Methacholine chloride can cause severe, life-threatening bronchoconstriction even at the lowest dose. 
- Use is contraindicated in patients with significant pre-existing respiratory impairment (FEV₁ < 60% predicted or < 1.5 L).
- Not recommended for patients with clinically apparent asthma or wheezing.

### Healthcare Provider Safety
- Personnel should minimize inhalation of the powder or nebulized aerosol. 
- Those with asthma or hay fever should avoid handling the powder. 
- Use low-resistance filters on expiratory ports of the dosing apparatus to protect the room air.

### Coexisting Conditions
- Use with caution and only if benefits outweigh risks in patients with:
    - Uncontrolled hypertension or aortic aneurysm.
    - Recent myocardial infarction or stroke.
    - Epilepsy, vagotonia, peptic ulcer disease, thyroid disease, or urinary tract obstruction.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally identified through clinical studies or post-marketing reports. Frequencies are not systematically defined in provided sources.
- **Respiratory**: Bronchospasm (including chest tightness, cough, or wheezing).
- **Neurological**: Headache, light-headedness.
- **Other**: Throat irritation, itching, rash, or swelling (particularly of the face/tongue/throat).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Response Inhibitors (Hold Prior to Testing)
The following medications inhibit the airway response to methacholine and must be withheld [US]:
- **Short-acting beta-agonists (e.g., albuterol)**: Hold for 6 hours.
- **Long-acting beta-agonists (e.g., salmeterol)**: Hold for 36 hours.
- **Short-acting anticholinergics (e.g., ipratropium)**: Hold for 12 hours.
- **Long-acting anticholinergics (e.g., tiotropium)**: Hold for ≥ 168 hours (7 days).
- **Theophylline**: Hold for 12–48 hours.

### Response Modifiers
- **Beta-Adrenergic Blockers**: May impair the ability of beta-agonists to reverse methacholine-induced bronchoconstriction.
- **Corticosteroids and Cromoglycate**: Regular use may acutely decrease bronchial responsiveness; however, the source notes these may be continued during use if necessary, though they may impact test sensitivity.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: Not recommended [US]. There are insufficient data to evaluate drug-associated risks of birth defects or miscarriage. Methacholine-induced bronchoprovocation carries a risk of fetal hypoxia. If airway hyperreactivity is suspected in a pregnant patient, empiric treatment should be considered instead of testing.
- **Breastfeeding**: No data available regarding the presence of methacholine chloride in human milk or its effects on milk production or the infant.
- **Fertility**: No studies have been conducted to evaluate effects on fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy established for children aged 5 years and older. Use in children under 5 years is not recommended as it has not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Clinical studies did not include patients aged 65 years and older. This population is less likely to undergo this diagnostic test [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Methacholine chloride is a cholinergic agonist that binds to muscarinic receptors in bronchial smooth muscle, inducing bronchoconstriction.
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Patients with asthma/airway hyperreactivity exhibit significantly higher sensitivity to methacholine-induced bronchoconstriction than healthy individuals.
- **Pharmacokinetics**: No metabolic or pharmacokinetic data are available for methacholine chloride [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- The pharmacological basis of the test is the documented difference in sensitivity to muscarinic stimulation between asthmatic and non-asthmatic individuals. Specific pivotal trial data were not detailed in the provided regulatory document.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Powder/Kits**: Store at 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solutions**: Must be refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) and are stable for up to 2 weeks.
- **Preparation for Use**: Diluted solutions should be allowed to reach room temperature for approximately 30 minutes before administration to ensure consistent nebulizer output.
