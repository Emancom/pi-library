# Editorial Product Information: Montelukast

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of montelukast product information for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) when prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Montelukast (as sodium). 
- **Chemical Class:** Selective and orally active leukotriene receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** APX-MONTELUKAST [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R03DC03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - 10 mg film-coated tablets (uncoated, round biconvex, light brown).
    - 5 mg chewable tablets (pink, round, uncoated).
    - 4 mg chewable tablets (pink, oval biconvex, uncoated).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Asthma:** Prophylaxis and chronic treatment of asthma in adults and paediatric patients aged 2 years and older [Australian].
- **Seasonal Allergic Rhinitis:** Symptomatic treatment of seasonal allergic rhinitis [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Adults and Adolescents (≥15 years):** One 10 mg tablet daily.
- **Paediatric Patients (6–14 years):** One 5 mg chewable tablet daily.
- **Paediatric Patients (2–5 years):** One 4 mg chewable tablet daily.

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** 
    - For asthma: The dose must be taken in the evening.
    - For seasonal allergic rhinitis: The time of administration may be individualised.
    - For comorbid asthma and seasonal allergic rhinitis: One tablet should be taken daily in the evening.
- **Food:** May be taken with or without food.
- **Method:** Chewable tablets must be chewed and not swallowed whole. 10 mg tablets should not be broken prior to administration.
- **Duration:** Patients should continue therapy during periods of controlled asthma as well as during acute worsening.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to montelukast sodium or any inactive ingredient in the formulation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- **Serious Neuropsychiatric Events [Australian]:** Behavioural changes, depression, and suicidality have been reported across all age groups. Symptoms may be serious and may persist after treatment discontinuation. Montelukast should be discontinued immediately if neuropsychiatric symptoms occur.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Asthma:** Oral montelukast is not indicated for the reversal of bronchospasm in acute asthma attacks, including status asthmaticus. Patients must have appropriate rescue medication (short-acting beta-agonists) available.
- **Corticosteroid Tapering:** Montelukast should not be abruptly substituted for inhaled or oral corticosteroids. Corticosteroids should be tapered gradually under medical supervision.
- **Eosinophilic Conditions:** In rare cases, patients may present with systemic eosinophilia, vasculitic rash, or clinical features of Churg-Strauss syndrome. This is often associated with a reduction in oral corticosteroid therapy.
- **Aspirin Sensitivity:** Patients with known aspirin sensitivity should continue to avoid aspirin or NSAIDs, as montelukast does not modify the bronchoconstrictor response to these agents.
- **Phenylketonuria:** Chewable tablets (4 mg and 5 mg) contain aspartame, a source of phenylalanine.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Montelukast is generally well-tolerated. In clinical trials, the incidence of side effects was comparable to placebo.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, headache, thirst (paediatric 2–5 years), asthenia/fatigue, fever, dyspepsia, infectious gastroenteritis, dental pain, dizziness, insomnia, nasal congestion, cough, influenza, rash, increased ALT/AST. |
| **Post-marketing / Rare** | Increased bleeding tendency, thrombocytopenia, anaphylaxis, dream abnormalities, hallucinations, somnambulism, suicidal thinking, seizures, palpitations, epistaxis, pulmonary eosinophilia, hepatitis (cholestatic/hepatocellular), angioedema, erythema multiforme, arthralgia, enuresis (children). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP450 System:** Montelukast is a substrate of CYP2C8, 2C9, and 3A4. While it inhibits CYP2C8 in vitro, clinical data suggest it does not inhibit this enzyme in vivo.
- **Phenobarbital:** Co-administration results in an approximately 40% reduction in the AUC of montelukast. No dose adjustment is recommended, but clinical monitoring is advised [Australian].
- **Gemfibrozil:** A potent inhibitor of CYP2C8, gemfibrozil can increase the systemic exposure of montelukast by 4.4-fold. While no dose adjustment is formally required, clinicians should be alert for increased adverse reactions.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Theophylline:** No dose adjustment of theophylline is required at standard montelukast doses (10 mg).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B1]. Drugs taken by a limited number of pregnant women without evidence of increased frequency of malformation. Animal studies show no increased occurrence of fetal damage. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown, though studies in rats show excretion into milk. Caution is advised when administering to nursing mothers.
- **Fertility:** Studies in rats showed a slight reduction in fecundity in females at high doses (200 mg/kg/day); no effect was seen at 100 mg/kg/day (20x human exposure).

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - Asthma: Studied from 6 months to 14 years. Safety/efficacy < 6 months not established [Australian].
    - Allergic Rhinitis: Studied from 2 to 14 years. Safety/efficacy < 2 years not established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No age-related differences in safety or efficacy profiles.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment necessary.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment. No clinical data available for severe hepatic insufficiency (Child-Pugh score > 9).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective cysteinyl leukotriene receptor antagonist. It binds with high affinity to the CysLT1 receptor, inhibiting the physiological actions of LTC4, LTD4, and LTE4, which are mediators of airway oedema, smooth muscle contraction, and inflammatory processes.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed. For 10 mg tablets, Tmax is 3 hours; bioavailability is 64%. For chewable tablets, Tmax is 2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** >99% protein bound. Minimal crossing of the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted almost exclusively via the bile. Plasma half-life ranges from 2.7 to 5.5 hours in healthy adults.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Asthma (Adults):** 12-week trials demonstrated significant improvement in FEV1 and "as-needed" beta-agonist use. Response was observed within one day of the first dose.
- **Asthma (Paediatric):** Trials in patients aged 2–14 years showed consistent improvement in asthma control and symptoms. Studies up to 56 weeks indicated no significant effect on growth rate compared to placebo.
- **Seasonal Allergic Rhinitis:** Trials in patients ≥15 years showed significant reduction in the Daily Rhinitis Symptoms score (composite of nasal congestion, rhinorrhoea, sneezing, and itching) compared to placebo.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Store in the original package to protect from light and moisture.
