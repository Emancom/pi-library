# Editorial Product Information: Mafenide Acetate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mafenide acetate (a sulfonamide derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** **SULFAMYLON** [US].
- **ATC Code:** D06BA03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Topical cream containing mafenide acetate equivalent to 85 mg of the base per gram [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Rising Pharma Holdings, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Burn Wound Management:** Indicated for use as an adjunctive topical antimicrobial agent in the therapy of patients with second- and third-degree burns [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric):**
  - **Application:** Apply with a sterile gloved hand to a thickness of approximately 1/16 inch [US].
  - **Frequency:** Once or twice daily. The burned area must be covered with the cream at all times.
  - **Reapplication:** If the cream is removed (e.g., by patient activity), it should be reapplied immediately [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
  - Wounds should be cleansed and debrided prior to application [US].
  - Dressings are usually not required; if necessary, only a thin layer should be used [US].
  - Daily bathing (whirlpool, bed bath, or shower) is recommended to assist in debridement [US].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Continue treatment until healing is progressing well or the site is ready for grafting. The drug should not be withdrawn while a risk of infection persists [US].
- **Management of Acidosis:** If metabolic acidosis occurs and becomes difficult to control (especially in patients with pulmonary dysfunction), therapy may be discontinued for 24 to 48 hours while maintaining fluid therapy to restore acid-base balance [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to mafenide acetate or any components of the formulation [US].
- **Cross-Sensitivity:** It is currently unknown if cross-sensitivity exists between mafenide and other sulfonamides [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hemolytic Anemia:** Fatal cases of hemolytic anemia associated with disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) have been reported, presumably related to glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency [US].
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** The cream contains sodium metabisulfite. This may trigger allergic-type reactions, including anaphylaxis or life-threatening asthmatic episodes, particularly in susceptible individuals (more common in asthmatics) [US].
- **Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibition:** Mafenide and its metabolite (p-carboxybenzenesulfonamide) inhibit carbonic anhydrase. This may lead to metabolic acidosis, usually compensated by hyperventilation [US]. 
- **Acid-Base Monitoring:** Close monitoring of acid-base balance is essential, particularly in patients with:
  - Extensive second-degree or partial-thickness burns.
  - Pulmonary dysfunction.
  - Renal dysfunction [US].
- **Respiratory Alkalosis:** An unexplained syndrome of marked hyperventilation and respiratory alkalosis (low arterial $pCO_2$, decreased total $CO_2$) has been reported in some burn patients [US].
- **Fungal Colonization:** Fungal growth in and below the eschar may occur simultaneously with the reduction of bacterial growth. Fungal dissemination is reported to be rare [US].
- **Delayed Eschar Separation:** It should be noted that delayed eschar separation has occurred in some cases [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions can be difficult to distinguish from the effects of a severe burn.

- **Very Common (>10%):**
  - **Dermatologic:** Pain or a burning sensation upon application [US].
- **Frequency Not Specified:**
  - **Allergic:** Rash, pruritus, facial edema, swelling, hives, blisters, erythema, and eosinophilia [US].
  - **Metabolic/Respiratory:** Metabolic acidosis, increased serum chloride, tachypnea, hyperventilation, and decreased arterial $pCO_2$ [US].
  - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (reported following accidental ingestion) [US].
- **Rare (<0.1%) / Isolated Cases:**
  - **Dermatologic:** Excoriation of new skin and skin bleeding [US].
  - **Hematologic:** Bone marrow depression; fatal hemolytic anemia with DIC (G6PD deficiency-related) [US].
  - **Other:** Acute attack of porphyria [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Clinically Significant Interactions:** While not listed as a drug-drug interaction, the drug's inhibition of carbonic anhydrase may have additive effects with other systemic carbonic anhydrase inhibitors [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category:** Not specified by letter category in the available source [US].
  - **Safety Data:** Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if mafenide can cause fetal harm or affect reproductive capacity.
  - **Recommendations:** Not recommended for women of childbearing potential unless the burn covers >20% of total body surface area, or the clinical benefit outweighs the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if mafenide acetate is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug, considering the drug's importance to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies to evaluate potential impairment of fertility have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing and safety profile are reported as the same as for adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution in patients with acute renal failure or renal dysfunction due to the risk of exaggerated carbonic anhydrase inhibition and metabolic acidosis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Exerts bacteriostatic action against various gram-negative and gram-positive organisms, including *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and certain anaerobes. Its activity is maintained in the presence of pus and serum and is not affected by environmental pH changes [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Diffuses through devascularized (burned) areas and is absorbed systemically [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Rapidly converted to the metabolite p-carboxybenzenesulfonamide [US].
  - **Elimination:** The metabolite is cleared through the kidneys [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Topical application produces a marked reduction in bacterial populations in avascular tissues. This may prevent the conversion of deep partial-thickness burns to full-thickness burns by allowing spontaneous healing [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Avoid exposure to excessive heat (temperatures above 104°F or 40°C) [US].
- **Handling:** For external use only. Keep out of reach of children [US].
