# Editorial Product Information: Motixafortide

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for motixafortide. It is designed as a clinically focused reference for healthcare professionals to support safe and effective prescribing and administration.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial compilation and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) mandated by local regulatory authorities. Clinicians must consult the approved PI within their specific jurisdiction before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Motixafortide (acetate).
*   **Chemical Class:** Synthetic cyclic peptide; hematopoietic stem cell mobiliser; CXCR4 inhibitor.
*   **Brand Name:** **Aphexda** [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for reconstitution (62 mg per single-dose vial) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured for Ayrmid Pharma Ltd; Distributed by Gamida Cell Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Hematopoietic Stem Cell (HSC) Mobilisation:** Indicated in combination with filgrastim (G-CSF) to mobilise HSCs to the peripheral blood for collection and subsequent autologous transplantation in patients with multiple myeloma [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
*   **Preparation Phase:** Administer filgrastim 10 mcg/kg subcutaneously once daily for four days prior to the first dose of motixafortide and on each day prior to apheresis.
*   **Motixafortide Dosage:** 1.25 mg/kg (based on **actual body weight**) administered via slow subcutaneous injection (approximately 2 minutes).
*   **Timing:** Administer 10 to 14 hours prior to the initiation of the first apheresis.
*   **Subsequent Doses:** A second dose may be administered 10 to 14 hours prior to a third apheresis, if necessary.

### Premedication Protocol [US]
To mitigate the risk of hypersensitivity and injection site reactions, all patients must receive the following 30 to 60 minutes prior to every motixafortide dose:
1.  **H1-antihistamine:** Diphenhydramine (12.5 mg IV or 25–50 mg PO) or an equivalent.
2.  **H2-blocker:** e.g., famotidine.
3.  **Leukotriene inhibitor:** e.g., montelukast.
4.  **Analgesic:** Premedication with acetaminophen is recommended.

### Administration Instructions
*   **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with 2 mL of 0.45% Sodium Chloride (resulting in 36.5 mg/mL). Alternatively, 1 mL Sterile Water + 1 mL 0.9% Sodium Chloride may be used.
*   **Injection Volume:** Maximum volume per injection site is 2 mL. Doses requiring >2 mL must be divided into multiple syringes and injected at different sites.
*   **Sites:** Abdomen (avoiding a 5 cm radius around the navel), back, side of upper arms, or thighs. Rotate sites.
*   **Monitoring:** Observe the patient for at least one hour post-injection for signs of hypersensitivity.

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** History of serious hypersensitivity reactions to motixafortide [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Anaphylactic Shock and Hypersensitivity:** Occurred in 0.7% of patients, typically within 5 to 30 minutes of administration. A triple-drug premedication regimen is mandatory. Administer only in settings equipped for emergency resuscitation.
*   **Injection Site Reactions (ISRs):** Very common (73%); 9% were severe. Manifestations include pain, erythema, pruritus, and induration. Analgesic premedication and local post-dose treatments are recommended.
*   **Leukocytosis:** Motixafortide increases circulating white blood cells. Monitor WBC counts during use.
*   **Potential for Tumor Cell Mobilization:**
    *   **Leukemia:** Motixafortide may mobilise leukemic cells and is **not intended** for use in patients with leukemia.
    *   **Other Malignancies:** Tumor cells may be released into the peripheral blood during mobilisation; the clinical effect of reinfusing these cells during transplant is unknown.
*   **Embryo-fetal Toxicity:** Based on mechanism of action, can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status prior to initiation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** In vitro studies suggest low potential for CYP450 inhibition or induction. Transporter-mediated interactions are also unlikely.
*   **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    *   **Negative Chronotropic Drugs:** Patients on beta-blockers may be at higher risk for hypotension during a hypersensitivity reaction. Replace with non-chronotropic agents where appropriate [US].
*   **Clinically Significant Interactions:** None definitively established in available clinical sources beyond the use of filgrastim for the indicated combination therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **Category:** Not formally assigned in the provided source, but noted as having "Embryo-fetal Toxicity" risk [US].
    *   **Recommendations:** Advise females of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during treatment and for **8 days** after the final dose.
*   **Breastfeeding:** No data on presence in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for **8 days** after the final dose [US].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no histopathological toxicity to reproductive organs, but formal fertility studies have not been conducted.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger patients.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Mild-to-moderate impairment does not significantly affect pharmacokinetics. The effect of severe impairment is unstudied but not expected to be significant [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Mildly impaired liver function does not significantly affect pharmacokinetics. Moderate-to-severe impairment has not been evaluated, but risk is considered low due to single-dose administration.

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Motixafortide is a CXCR4 inhibitor. It blocks the binding of stromal-derived factor-1α (SDF-1α)/CXCL12 to the CXCR4 receptor. This disrupts the anchoring of HSCs to the bone marrow matrix, leading to their release into the peripheral circulation.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Tmax is 0.25 to 1.17 hours.
    *   **Distribution:** >99% protein bound; Vd of the central compartment is ~27 L.
    *   **Metabolism:** Non-specific catabolism into small peptides and amino acids via blood and liver microsomes.
    *   **Elimination:** Effective half-life of ~2 hours; clearance ~46.5 L/h. Primary excretion of metabolites is via urine (approx. 80%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **GENESIS Trial (NCT 03246529):** A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study (N=122) in multiple myeloma patients. 
*   **Primary Outcome:** 67.5% of patients in the motixafortide + filgrastim arm achieved the collection goal of ≥ 6 × 10^6 CD34+ cells/kg in up to 2 aphereses, compared to 9.5% in the placebo + filgrastim arm (p < 0.0001). 
*   **Secondary Outcome:** 87.5% achieved the goal within a single apheresis session [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Vial Storage:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) in the original carton to protect from light.
*   **Reconstituted Solution:** Stable for up to 24 hours under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C) or at room temperature (20°C to 25°C), protected from light [US].
