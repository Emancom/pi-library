# Editorial Product Information: Metolazone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Metolazone (a quinazoline diuretic/saluretic).
- **Brand Names:** Zaroxolyn (reference formulation), Mykrox (rapidly available formulation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** C03BA08.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - Oral Tablets: 2.5 mg, 5 mg, and 10 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Innogenix, LLC; Distributed by Amici Pharmaceuticals, LLC; Marketed by GSMS, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Edema:** Treatment of salt and water retention associated with congestive heart failure and renal diseases, including nephrotic syndrome and states of impaired renal function [US].
- **Hypertension:** Treatment of mild to moderate essential hypertension, either as monotherapy or in combination with other antihypertensive classes [US].
- **Pregnancy-related Edema:** Indicated when edema is due to pathologic causes [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Metolazone dosage must be individualised based on patient response and the specific clinical indication. A single daily dose is recommended.
- **Edema (Cardiac Failure or Renal Disease):** 5 mg to 20 mg once daily [US].
- **Mild to Moderate Essential Hypertension:** 2.5 mg to 5 mg once daily [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Formulation Bioequivalence:** **CRITICAL WARNING:** Metolazone tablets (USP), Zaroxolyn, and their bioequivalents share a slow and incomplete bioavailability. They are **not** therapeutically equivalent to Mykrox (a rapidly available form). These formulations must not be interchanged on a mg-for-mg basis [US].
- **Titration:** Therapy should be titrated to the minimum effective dose. If switching from standard metolazone to Mykrox for hypertension, titration should start at 0.5 mg once daily [US].

### Duration of Effect
Diuresis typically begins within one hour and persists for 24 hours or longer. In patients with paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, higher doses may be required to ensure coverage for the full 24-hour period [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Anuria [US].
- Hepatic coma or precoma [US].
- Known hypersensitivity or allergy to metolazone [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Rapid Onset Electrolyte Imbalance
Severe hyponatremia and/or hypokalemia can occur rapidly following initial dosing. Therapy should be discontinued immediately if symptoms of severe electrolyte imbalance appear [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Regular determination of serum electrolytes (sodium, potassium, chloride, magnesium, calcium) is essential.
- Monitor for clinical signs of fluid/electrolyte imbalance: dry mouth, thirst, weakness, lethargy, muscle cramps, hypotension, and gastrointestinal disturbances [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Hypokalemia:** Risk is increased with large doses, rapid diuresis, severe liver disease, or concurrent corticosteroid use. It may sensitise the myocardium to digitalis, potentially precipitating fatal arrhythmias [US].
- **Azotemia:** Prerenal azotemia may occur. If azotemia and oliguria worsen during treatment of severe renal disease, metolazone should be discontinued [US].
- **Glucose Tolerance:** May raise blood glucose levels, potentially manifesting as hyperglycemia or glycosuria in diabetic or pre-diabetic patients [US].
- **Hyperuricemia:** May increase serum uric acid levels and precipitate gouty attacks [US].
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):** Thiazide-like diuretics may exacerbate or activate SLE [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild and transient, often representing extensions of the drug’s pharmacologic activity.

| Body System | Adverse Effects (Frequency not quantified in source) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiovascular** | Orthostatic hypotension, chest pain, palpitations, venous thrombosis, excessive volume depletion. |
| **Central Nervous System** | Dizziness, headache, fatigue, paresthesias, syncope, psychotic depression, drowsiness. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, hepatitis, intrahepatic cholestatic jaundice, pancreatitis. |
| **Hematologic** | Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis, aplastic/hypoplastic anemia. |
| **Dermatologic** | **Serious:** Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS). **Others:** Photosensitivity, urticaria, pruritus. |
| **Metabolic** | Hypokalemia, hyponatremia, hyperuricemia, hypomagnesemia, hypercalcemia, hyperglycemia. |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Muscle cramps, joint pain, acute gouty attacks. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Diuretics (Loop):** Concurrent use with furosemide may produce profound diuresis and electrolyte loss. Monitor closely [US].
- **Lithium:** Diuretics reduce renal clearance of lithium, significantly increasing the risk of lithium toxicity [US].
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** Diuretic-induced hypokalemia increases myocardial sensitivity to digitalis, raising the risk of serious arrhythmias [US].
- **Antihypertensives:** Potentiation of hypotensive effects; dosage adjustments may be required [US].
- **NSAIDs/Salicillates:** May decrease the antihypertensive efficacy of metolazone [US].
- **Curariform Drugs:** Hypokalemia may enhance the neuromuscular blocking effects (e.g., tubocurarine), potentially leading to respiratory depression. Discontinuation 3 days before surgery is advised [US].
- **Anticoagulants:** May affect hypoprothrombinemic response; monitor PT/INR [US].
- **Methenamine:** Efficacy may be reduced due to the urinary alkalising effect of metolazone [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category B [US].** Metolazone crosses the placental barrier and appears in cord blood. 
  - Use only if clearly needed. Routine use for edema in healthy pregnant women is inappropriate. 
  - Risks include fetal/neonatal jaundice and thrombocytopenia [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Metolazone is excreted in breast milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed reduced pregnancy rates and increased resorption sites at high doses (50 mg/kg), though no evidence of altered reproductive capacity in mice was found [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in controlled trials. Limited experience suggests doses of 0.05 to 0.1 mg/kg once daily may be used in specific conditions (e.g., CHF, nephrotic syndrome), but prolonged use is not recommended [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects over 65. Start at the low end of the dosing range due to higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; drug accumulation may occur. Unlike thiazides, metolazone may remain effective in patients with a GFR below 20 mL/min [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; minor alterations in fluid and electrolyte balance may precipitate hepatic coma [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A quinazoline diuretic that inhibits sodium reabsorption at the cortical diluting site and, to a lesser extent, the proximal convoluted tubule. It does not inhibit carbonic anhydrase [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Maximum blood levels achieved approximately 8 hours after dosing (standard tablets) [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Only a small fraction is metabolised [US].
  - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted unchanged in the urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal trial data was not detailed in the provided source document. Clinical experience indicates potency approximately equal to thiazide diuretics at maximum therapeutic doses, with preserved activity in low GFR states [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F) [US].
- **Container:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].
- **Protection:** Protect from light [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx Only.
