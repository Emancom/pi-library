# Editorial Product Information: Magnesium Oxide

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from regulatory data provided by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) regarding combination bowel cleansing preparations and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) regarding magnesium oxide as an oral antacid. This document combines the clinical requirements for both high-dose bowel preparation and low-dose symptomatic relief.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Names:** Magnesium Oxide (Tablet), PICOSALAX (Powder for oral solution)
**Active Ingredients:** 
*   Magnesium Oxide (400 mg per tablet, equivalent to 241.3 mg elemental magnesium) [FDA]
*   Light Magnesium Oxide (3.5 g per sachet, which reacts with citric acid in solution to form magnesium citrate) [TGA]

## 2. Approved Indications
Magnesium oxide is indicated for the following therapeutic uses:
*   **Bowel Cleansing:** Clearance of the colon in preparation for surgical procedures, endoscopies, or radiological examinations [TGA].
*   **Dyspepsia:** Temporary relief of symptoms associated with gastric hyperacidity, including acid indigestion and upset stomach [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
Magnesium oxide is contraindicated in patients with the following conditions:
*   Severe renal impairment or kidney failure (due to the risk of magnesium accumulation) [TGA].
*   Congestive heart failure [TGA].
*   Known or suspected gastrointestinal obstruction, ileus, or perforation [TGA].
*   Acute surgical abdominal conditions, such as appendicitis [TGA].
*   Toxic megacolon, gastric retention, or active inflammatory bowel disease [TGA].
*   Severe dehydration or rhabdomyolysis [TGA].
*   Existing hypermagnesemia [TGA].
*   Hypersensitivity to magnesium oxide or any excipients in the formulation.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
**Electrolyte Disturbance and Dehydration:** High-dose use for bowel preparation carries a risk of life-threatening dehydration and serious electrolyte imbalances. Patients at increased risk—including the elderly, debilitated individuals, or those with low body weight—require close monitoring of hydration and electrolyte levels [TGA].

**Renal Function:** Use with extreme caution in patients with any degree of renal impairment. These patients must be monitored for signs of magnesium toxicity and may require alternative preparations [TGA, FDA].

**Fluid Intake:** When used as a purgative, patients must consume adequate clear liquids (not just water) to prevent hyponatremia and seizures. Rehydration should include balanced electrolyte solutions [TGA].

**Concurrent Medical Conditions:**
*   **Diabetes:** Diabetics may require blood glucose monitoring and medication adjustment during bowel preparation [TGA].
*   **Cardiac Disease:** Use with caution in patients with heart disease or those taking medications that affect electrolyte balance, such as diuretics [TGA].

**Laxative Effect:** Even at antacid doses, magnesium oxide may exert a mild laxative effect [FDA]. It is not intended for use as a routine laxative [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### For Gastric Hyperacidity (Antacid)
*   **Adults:** One to two tablets daily.
*   **Maximum Dose:** Do not exceed two tablets in a 24-hour period unless directed by a physician [FDA].

### For Bowel Clearance (Purgative)
Dosing typically involves a two-sachet regimen (using powder for reconstitution) according to a split-dose or day-before schedule [TGA]:
*   **Split-Dose Regimen:** One sachet the evening before the procedure, followed by the second sachet on the morning of the procedure (5–9 hours prior).
*   **Day-Before Regimen:** One sachet in the afternoon and the second sachet approximately six hours later in the evening.
*   **Reconstitution:** Each sachet should be mixed with 150 mL of water and stirred for 2–3 minutes. The resulting solution may become warm and should be allowed to cool before drinking.
*   **Pediatric Use:** Bowel preparation regimens are generally indicated for children aged 9 years and older [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequently reported adverse reactions include:
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and proctalgia [TGA].
*   **Neurological:** Headache [TGA].

**Serious/Rare Reactions (primarily associated with high doses):**
*   **Metabolic:** Clinically significant hyponatremia, hypokalemia, and hypermagnesemia [TGA].
*   **Neurological:** Seizures (generalized tonic-clonic), confusional states, or loss of consciousness secondary to electrolyte shifts [TGA].
*   **Dermatological:** Rashes, including urticaria and purpura [TGA].
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Isolated reports of ileal ulcers [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
Magnesium oxide may interfere with the absorption and efficacy of other medications:
*   **Chelation:** Magnesium may bind to and reduce the absorption of tetracyclines, fluoroquinolone antibiotics, digoxin, iron supplements, chlorpromazine, and penicillamine. These drugs should be taken at least 2 hours before or 6 hours after magnesium administration [TGA].
*   **Gastrointestinal Transit:** Increased transit speed during bowel prep may reduce the absorption of oral contraceptives, anti-epileptics, and anti-diabetics [TGA].
*   **Electrolyte-Affecting Drugs:** Caution is required when used with diuretics, corticosteroids, or medications known to induce SIADH (e.g., SSRIs, tricyclic antidepressants, and NSAIDs), as these increase the risk of water retention and electrolyte imbalance [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
**Pregnancy:** Clinical data regarding magnesium oxide use in pregnant women is limited. While animal studies have not demonstrated direct fetal harm, use during pregnancy (especially in the first trimester) should only occur under medical supervision when the benefits outweigh the risks [TGA, FDA].

**Breastfeeding:** It is not currently established whether magnesium citrate is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised, and the drug should only be used if clearly necessary [TGA, FDA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
When magnesium oxide is used in combination with citric acid for bowel preparation, it forms magnesium citrate in solution. Magnesium citrate is an osmotic agent that is minimally absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract [TGA]. When used in tablet form as an antacid, the magnesium acts locally in the stomach to neutralize acid.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Magnesium oxide facilitates bowel clearance through its conversion to magnesium citrate, which acts as an osmotic laxative. It increases intestinal osmotic pressure, drawing water into the lumen and stimulating peristalsis [TGA]. As an antacid, magnesium oxide reacts with gastric hydrochloric acid to neutralize stomach acidity [FDA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store in a dry environment at controlled room temperature, typically between 15°C and 30°C [FDA, TGA].
*   Keep the container tightly closed and out of reach of children.
*   Reconstituted solutions for bowel prep must be used immediately and should not be prepared in advance [TGA].
