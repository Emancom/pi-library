# Editorial Product Information: Metoprolol

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) documents from the following regulatory authorities: 
    *   Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of metoprolol tartrate for use by healthcare professionals.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Metoprolol tartrate
*   **Chemical Class:** Beta-adrenoceptor antagonist (cardioselective)
*   **Brand Names:** APO-Metoprolol [Australian]
*   **ATC Code:** C07AB02
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   Oral Film-Coated Tablets: 50 mg and 100 mg [Australian]
*   **Routes of Administration:** Oral (Information regarding intravenous administration is referenced in adverse effect profiles but specific dosing is not provided in available source) [Australian].
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Hypertension:** As monotherapy or in combination with other antihypertensive agents [Australian].
*   **Angina Pectoris:** Long-term prophylaxis; acute attacks require glyceryl trinitrate [Australian].
*   **Myocardial Infarction:** For use in suspected or definite myocardial infarction [Australian].
*   **Migraine Prophylaxis:** [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Maximum Dose:** Should not exceed 400 mg daily [Australian].
*   **Administration:** Tablets should be swallowed whole. While twice-daily dosing is optimal, daily doses of 150 mg or less may be administered as a single dose [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication [Australian]
*   **Hypertension:**
    *   Mild: 50 mg to 100 mg daily for one week.
    *   Moderate to Severe: 50 mg to 100 mg twice daily for one week.
    *   Maintenance: 50 mg to 100 mg once or twice daily. (Note: Increasing dose beyond 200 mg rarely improves response).
*   **Angina Pectoris:** 50 mg to 100 mg, administered two or three times daily.
*   **Myocardial Infarction:**
    *   Initial: 50 mg twice daily for 48 hours.
    *   Maintenance: Generally 100 mg twice daily.
*   **Migraine Prophylaxis:** 100 mg to 150 mg daily, administered in two divided doses (morning and evening).

### Titration and Clinical Notes
*   Dosage must be individualised. In Myocardial Infarction, dosage should be reduced if the patient is haemodynamically unstable [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **Respiratory:** History of bronchospasm, obstructive airway disease, or allergic disorders (e.g., allergic rhinitis) suggesting a predisposition to bronchospasm [Australian].
*   **Cardiac:** 
    *   Sinus bradycardia (<45–50 beats/minute).
    *   Second or third-degree atrioventricular (A-V) block.
    *   Sick-sinus syndrome (in the absence of a functional permanent pacemaker).
    *   Right ventricular failure secondary to pulmonary hypertension or significant right ventricular hypertrophy.
    *   Untreated congestive heart failure.
    *   Shock (cardiogenic and hypovolaemic).
    *   Hypotension.
    *   In Myocardial Infarction: HR <45 bpm, P-R interval ≥0.24 s, SBP <100 mmHg, or moderate to severe heart failure [Australian].
*   **Vascular:** Severe peripheral arterial circulatory disorders [Australian].
*   **Other:**
    *   Untreated phaeochromocytoma.
    *   Known hypersensitivity to metoprolol, related derivatives, or excipients (cross-sensitivity between beta-blockers may occur).
    *   Concurrent continuous or intermittent inotropic therapy (beta-receptor agonism) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
*   **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Do not discontinue abruptly in patients with ischaemic heart disease. Severe exacerbation of angina, myocardial infarction, and ventricular arrhythmias may occur. Taper dosage over 8–14 days [Australian].
*   **Cardiac Failure:** Beta-blockade may precipitate heart failure by depressing contractility. Patients should be stabilised (e.g., digitalised/diuretics) before use.
*   **Prinzmetal Angina:** Risk of exacerbating coronary artery spasms. Treatment should only occur in intensive care settings if essential [Australian].
*   **Phaeochromocytoma:** Always initiate an alpha-blocker prior to metoprolol to prevent hypertensive crisis [Australian].

### Clinical Precautions
*   **Diabetes:** Metoprolol may mask premonitory signs of acute hypoglycaemia (e.g., tachycardia) and may inhibit recovery of glucose levels.
*   **Hyperthyroidism:** May mask clinical signs of thyrotoxicosis.
*   **Anaphylaxis:** Patients at risk of severe anaphylaxis may be resistant to standard doses of adrenaline while on beta-blockers.
*   **Peripheral Vascular Disease:** May exacerbate symptoms of Raynaud’s phenomenon or intermittent claudication [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
*   Monitor heart rate and blood pressure regularly.
*   In patients with suspected thyrotoxicosis, monitor both thyroid and cardiac function closely [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often mild and transient, occurring primarily at the start of therapy.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | General / CNS | Fatigue |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Nervous System | Dizziness, headache |
| | Cardiovascular | Bradycardia, postural disorders (rarely syncope), cold extremities, palpitations |
| | Respiratory | Dyspnoea (including on exertion) |
| | Gastrointestinal | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation, abdominal pain, heartburn |
| | Skin | Pruritus, rash |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Nervous System | Paraesthesia, muscle cramps |

*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** Bronchospasm (even in patients without obstructive lung disease), cardiogenic shock in acute MI, and hepatitis [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Verapamil and diltiazem may cause severe bradycardia, hypotension, and asystole. Intravenous verapamil is contraindicated in patients on metoprolol [Australian].
*   **Clonidine:** Concurrent use increases the risk of rebound hypertension upon withdrawal. Discontinue metoprolol several days before tapering clonidine [Australian].
*   **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., fluoxetine, paroxetine, sertraline, bupropion, quinidine, ritonavir) may significantly increase metoprolol plasma concentrations [Australian].
*   **Antiarrhythmics:** Increased negative inotropic/chronotropic effects with Class IA (disopyramide, quinidine), Class III (amiodarone), and Class IV agents [Australian].
*   **Catecholamine-depleting agents:** (e.g., reserpine, guanethidine) require close monitoring for excessive sympathetic blockade.
*   **Other:** 
    *   **Alcohol:** May raise metoprolol plasma levels and alter alcohol pharmacokinetics.
    *   **Warfarin:** Potential for increased anticoagulant effect.
    *   **Digitalis Glycosides:** Increased risk of bradycardia and prolonged A-V conduction time [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy Category:** **Category C** [Australian].
    *   Metoprolol crosses the placenta. Beta-blockers may reduce placental perfusion, leading to intrauterine growth retardation, foetal death, or premature labour.
    *   **Management:** Discontinue 2–3 days before delivery to avoid neonatal bradycardia or hypoglycaemia [Australian].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Metoprolol is excreted in breast milk at concentrations approximately three times higher than maternal plasma. While the ingested amount is generally negligible at therapeutic doses, infants should be monitored for signs of beta-blockade [Australian].
*   **Fertility:** Reversible effects on spermatogenesis observed in rats; no human data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
*   **Elderly Use:** Caution is recommended due to an increased likelihood of adverse events and potential pharmacodynamic changes in receptor sensitivity [Australian].
*   **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment usually required, but caution is advised in severe impairment. Metabolite accumulation may occur if creatinine clearance is <5 mL/min [Australian].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Significant increase in plasma levels occurs due to high first-pass metabolism. Initiate at low doses and titrate slowly [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive, cardioselective beta-1 adrenoceptor antagonist. It lacks intrinsic sympathomimetic activity (ISA) and has negligible membrane-stabilising activity at therapeutic doses [Australian].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Rapid and near-complete (>95%) from the GI tract. 
    *   **Metabolism:** Subject to significant first-pass metabolism via CYP2D6. Bioavailability is increased by food. 
    *   **Distribution:** Highly lipid-soluble; plasma protein binding is approximately 12%.
    *   **Elimination:** Primarily hepatic; approximately 95% of the dose is excreted in urine (only 3% as unchanged drug). The elimination half-life is 3–5 hours [Australian].
