# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulations and the most current local PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. It is a naturally occurring carrier molecule for fatty acid transport [US].
- **Brand Names:** **Levocarnitine Injection, USP** (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus injection (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In patients with severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and must not be less frequent than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administration should be via slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Trough Concentrations:** Initiation of therapy in ESRD may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels below 40–50 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Adjustments should be guided by trough plasma concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy in dialysis patients [US].
- **Reference Range:** For metabolic disorders, monitoring should target a plasma free carnitine concentration between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are currently no known contraindications for levocarnitine injection [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine (both oral and intravenous) has been associated with reports of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** While the injection is used in ESRD, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in the urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials for ESRD patients (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Chest pain (12%), Headache (22%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%) |
| **Neurologic** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].
- **Other Notable Effects:** Transient nausea/vomiting and body odor have been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels at the initiation of therapy and after any dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned under current labeling, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8x human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have been established for the treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism. Specific dosing for ESRD in the paediatric population is not explicitly detailed outside of weight-based generalisations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, safety for *oral* formulations in this population is not established due to metabolite accumulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, it helps alleviate metabolic abnormalities resulting from the accumulation of toxic acylCoA compounds [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg dose after dialysis), mean trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While the increase in plasma carnitine was significant, effects on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population were not determined in these trials [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Vials are single-dose; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
