# Editorial Product Information: Metreleptin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial harmonises product information for **metreleptin** based on the following source:
    - **United States (FDA)**: MYALEPT (metreleptin) for injection, for subcutaneous use.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a unified, clinically accurate overview of **metreleptin** for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) mandated in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Metreleptin (a recombinant human leptin analog).
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant methionyl-human leptin produced in *E. coli*. It consists of a 147-amino acid, nonglycosylated polypeptide with one disulfide bond.
- **Brand Names:** Myalept [US].
- **ATC Code:** H04AA01 (Leptin analogues).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 11.3 mg of **metreleptin** as a sterile, white, lyophilized cake in a single-dose vial. 
    - Upon reconstitution with 2.2 mL of solvent, the concentration is 5 mg/mL [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Chiesi USA, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Metreleptin** is indicated as an adjunct to diet as replacement therapy for the treatment of complications resulting from leptin deficiency in:
- Patients with congenital or acquired generalized lipodystrophy [US].

**Limitations of Use:**
- Safety and effectiveness for the treatment of complications of partial lipodystrophy have not been established [US].
- Not indicated for liver disease, including nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) [US].
- Not indicated for use in patients with HIV-related lipodystrophy [US].
- Not indicated for metabolic disease without evidence of generalized lipodystrophy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
Dosage is based on baseline body weight and must be individualised based on clinical response [US].

| Baseline Weight | Starting Daily Dose | Dose Adjustments | Maximum Daily Dose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **≤ 40 kg** (males & females) | 0.06 mg/kg | 0.02 mg/kg | 0.13 mg/kg |
| **> 40 kg** (males) | 2.5 mg | 1.25 to 2.5 mg | 10 mg |
| **> 40 kg** (females) | 5 mg | 1.25 to 2.5 mg | 10 mg |

### Administration Instructions
- Administer subcutaneously once daily at the same time each day, without regard to meals [US].
- **Reconstitution:**
    - Use 2.2 mL of Bacteriostatic Water for Injection (BWFI) or preservative-free sterile Water for Injection (WFI).
    - If reconstituted with BWFI, the solution can be used for up to 3 days if refrigerated and protected from light.
    - If reconstituted with WFI, use immediately and discard any unused portion [US].
- **Technique:** Avoid intramuscular injection, particularly in patients with minimal subcutaneous tissue. Rotate injection sites (abdomen, thigh, or upper arm) [US].
- **Do not mix** with other medications, including insulin. If co-administered with insulin, use separate syringes and different injection sites [US].

### Missed Dose
Administer the missed dose as soon as noticed. Resume the normal schedule the following day. Do not double the dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **General Obesity:** Contraindicated in patients with general obesity not associated with congenital leptin deficiency [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a history of prior severe hypersensitivity reactions to **metreleptin** or any product components [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **Risk of Anti-Metreleptin Antibodies with Neutralizing Activity:** Neutralizing antibodies could inhibit endogenous leptin action and lead to a loss of **metreleptin** efficacy. This may result in worsening metabolic control or severe infections. Testing is required for patients with severe infections or loss of efficacy.
- **Risk of Lymphoma:** T-cell lymphoma has been reported in patients with acquired generalized lipodystrophy, both treated and untreated with **metreleptin**.
- **Restricted Distribution:** Available only through the **MYALEPT REMS PROGRAM**.

### General Warnings
- **Hypoglycemia:** Concomitant use with insulin or insulin secretagogues increases hypoglycemia risk. Large dose reductions of concomitant medications may be necessary. Monitor blood glucose closely [US].
- **Autoimmunity:** Progression of autoimmune disorders (e.g., autoimmune hepatitis, membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis) has been observed. Benefits and risks must be weighed in patients with existing autoimmune disease [US].
- **Benzyl Alcohol Toxicity:** BWFI contains benzyl alcohol. Preservative-free WFI is recommended for reconstitution when used in neonates and infants to avoid "gasping syndrome" [US].
- **Pancreatitis Risk:** Discontinuation or non-compliance can cause severe hypertriglyceridemia and pancreatitis. Tapering the dose over one week is recommended when discontinuing [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on an open-label, single-arm study (N=48) [US]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache (13%), Hypoglycemia (13%), Decreased weight (13%), Abdominal pain (10%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Arthralgia (8%), Dizziness (8%), Ear infection (8%), Fatigue (8%), Nausea (8%), Ovarian cyst (8%), Upper respiratory tract infection (8%), Anemia (6%), Back pain (6%), Diarrhea (6%), Paresthesia (6%), Proteinuria (6%), Pyrexia (6%) |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Immunogenicity:** 84% of patients in clinical trials developed anti-drug antibodies. 6% (2/33) of tested patients with generalized lipodystrophy developed antibodies with neutralizing activity [US].
- **Pancreatitis:** Reported in 4% of trial participants, all of whom had a history of pancreatitis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted. Leptin is a cytokine and may potentially alter the formation of Cytochrome P450 (CYP450) enzymes. This may be clinically relevant for CYP450 substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., warfarin, cyclosporine, theophylline). Monitor these agents closely upon initiation or discontinuation of **metreleptin** [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Increased risk of hypoglycemia when used with insulin or insulin secretagogues [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified (current FDA labeling uses Risk Summary).
    - **Risk Summary:** Available human data are insufficient to evaluate for drug-associated risks. Animal studies (mice) showed prolonged gestation and dystocia (maternal death) at doses approximately equivalent to the maximum clinical dose. Use only if needed [US].
    - **Registry:** There is a pregnancy exposure registry to monitor outcomes [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data are available on the presence of **metreleptin** in human milk. Endogenous leptin is present in human milk. Consider the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding along with the mother’s clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** In mouse studies, no adverse effects on mating, fertility, or early embryonic development were observed at doses up to 15-fold the maximum clinical dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Evaluated in 35 pediatric patients (73% of trial population) aged 1 to 17 years. No clinically meaningful differences in safety or efficacy were observed between pediatric and adult patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently. Generally, dose selection should be cautious, starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No formal pharmacokinetic studies. Nonclinical data indicate renal clearance is the major elimination route; therefore, pharmacokinetics may be altered in renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No formal pharmacokinetic studies were conducted [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
**Metreleptin** binds to and activates the human leptin receptor (ObR), which signals through the JAK/STAT transduction pathway. In patients with generalized lipodystrophy, it replaces the missing hormone leptin, thereby reducing food intake and improving insulin sensitivity [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Peak serum concentration ($C_{max}$) occurs approximately 4.0 to 4.3 hours after subcutaneous administration [US].
- **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 4 to 5 times plasma volume [US].
- **Metabolism/Elimination:** No formal metabolism studies. Renal clearance is the primary route of elimination. Half-life is 3.8 to 4.7 hours [US].
- **Antibody Effect:** Clearance is expected to be delayed in the presence of anti-metreleptin antibodies [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study Design:** A single-arm, open-label, NIH-sponsored study of 48 patients (32 with congenital, 16 with acquired generalized lipodystrophy) [US].
- **Primary Outcomes:**
    - **HbA1c Reduction:** Mean baseline 8.5%; mean reduction at 12 months was 2% [US].
    - **Triglyceride Reduction:** Median baseline 348 mg/dL; median percent change at 12 months was -55% [US].
    - Reductions were sustained over the study period (median exposure 2.7 years) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store in the refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F). Keep in the original carton to protect from light [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:**
    - With BWFI: Stable for 3 days when refrigerated.
    - With WFI: Use immediately; do not store.
- **Do not freeze or shake** reconstituted vials [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Restricted Access:** Subject to the **MYALEPT REMS** (Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy) due to risks of neutralizing antibodies and lymphoma. Prescribers and pharmacies must be certified [US].
