# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only United States regulatory documentation was provided; information for other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) is marked as "Not specified in available sources."

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [United States].
- **Monitoring:** Pre-therapy plasma carnitine levels are recommended. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [United States].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations (normal range: 40 to 50 µmol/L) [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Not specified as a formal contraindication in available sources, but serious reactions are warned against.
- **General:** None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [United States].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, discontinue the drug immediately [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with existing epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation):** While this PI covers the injection, it notes that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (frequency ≥5% regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, fever, abdominal pain, flu syndrome, asthenia |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, vascular disorder |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhoea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, dyspepsia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcaemia, hypervolaemia, peripheral oedema, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnoea, rhinitis |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm [United States].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** Seizures and transient nausea/vomiting or body odour have been reported [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Frequent INR monitoring is recommended for patients on anticoagulant therapy after starting levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in provided text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk. The risks of excess intake by the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness for metabolic disorders and ESRD are established. See "Dosage and Administration" for weight-based dosing [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral therapy (but not intravenous) is cautioned against in renal failure due to metabolite accumulation [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and subsequent energy production [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors or ESRD, it clears accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study (ESRD):** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly, trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [United States].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg doses three times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [United States].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [United States].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [United States].
