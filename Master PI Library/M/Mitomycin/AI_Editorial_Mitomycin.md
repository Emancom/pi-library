# Editorial Product Information: Mitomycin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of mitomycin for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosing strategies, and safety profiles.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mitomycin (a trifunctional alkylating antibiotic isolated from *Streptomyces caespitosus*).
- **Brand Names:** Mitocin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01DC03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Powder for injection/infusion or intravesical use: 20 mg single-use vials [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Slow intravenous (IV) infusion or intravesical instillation. 
    - *Note: Mitomycin must NOT be administered orally, intrathecally, intramuscularly, or subcutaneously [Australian].*
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Echo Therapeutics Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Palliative Treatment of Carcinoma:** Approved for use in cancers of the stomach, pancreas, colon, lung (non-small cell), breast, cervix, head and neck, liver, and bladder [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Mitomycin is typically administered at 6 to 8-week intervals. Treatment should only be repeated after full haematological recovery. 

### Dosing Schedules [Australian]
Two primary IV schedules are utilised:
1.  **Single Dose:** 20 mg/m² intravenously every 6–8 weeks.
2.  **Divided Dose:** 2 mg/m²/day for 5 days, followed by a 2-day drug-free interval, then 2 mg/m²/day for an additional 5 days (total initial dose of 20 mg/m² over 10 days).

### Intravesical Administration (Bladder Tumours)
- **Dose:** 20 mg diluted in 50 mL sterile water.
- **Instruction:** Instil directly into the bladder via catheter; retain as long as possible [Australian].

### Dose Adjustments based on Haematological Nadir
No repeat dose should be given until the leucocyte count returns to ≥3,000/mm³ and the platelet count returns to ≥75,000/mm³.
| Leucocytes (/mm³) | Platelets (/mm³) | % of Prior Dose to be Given |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| >4,000 | >100,000 | 100% |
| 3,000–3,999 | 75,000–99,999 | 100% |
| 2,000–2,999 | 25,000–74,999 | 70% |
| <2,000 | <25,000 | 50% |

### Administration Instructions
- **IV:** Reconstitute 20 mg with 20 mL Sterile Water for Injection. Administer via a running IV infusion of 5% Glucose, 0.9% Sodium Chloride, or Sodium Lactate. 
- **Extravasation Risk:** Use extreme caution to avoid extravasation; cellulitis, ulceration, and sloughing may occur. 
- **Handling:** As a cytotoxic agent, use appropriate personal protective equipment (gloves, etc.) and follow hazardous waste disposal protocols.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Absolute Contraindications:** 
    - Hypersensitivity to mitomycin or any excipients.
    - Pancytopenia, isolated leucopenia, or thrombocytopenia.
    - Haemorrhagic diathesis.
    - Acute infections.
    - Coagulation disorders.
    - Breastfeeding.
- **Intravesical Therapy:** Perforation of the bladder wall (absolute contraindication).
- **Relative Contraindications:** Restrictive/obstructive pulmonary disturbances, renal or liver dysfunction, poor general health, or cystitis (for intravesical use) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** Highly common (approx. 64%) and potentially fatal. Thrombocytopenia and leucopenia are cumulative and may occur up to 8 weeks post-therapy [Australian].
- **Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (HUS):** A serious complication involving microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, and irreversible renal failure. Most cases occur at cumulative doses >60 mg. Mortality is high (52%) [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** Dyspnoea and non-productive cough may signal severe pulmonary infiltrates. Risk increases when used with vinca alkaloids or high FiO₂ (>50%) perioperatively [Australian].
- **Renal Toxicity:** Mitomycin should not be administered to patients with a serum creatinine >1.7 mg/dL [Australian].
- **Extravasation:** May cause severe tissue necrosis. Symptoms can be delayed by weeks or months. If pelvic/abdominal pain occurs after intravesical use, consider extravasation/perforation [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain CBC (platelets, WBC, differential) and haemoglobin repeatedly during and for at least 7–8 weeks following therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Hematologic | Bone marrow toxicity (thrombocytopenia, leucopenia) |
| | Gastrointestinal | Nausea, vomiting, anorexia (approx. 14%) |
| | General | Fever |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Integumentary | Cellulitis/necrosis (if extravasation), stomatitis, alopecia |
| | Hepatic | Hepatic dysfunction (approx. 5%) |
| | Renal | Significant rise in creatinine (2%) |
| **Uncommon/Rare** | Respiratory | Pulmonary toxicity, Adult Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) |
| | Cardiac | Congestive Heart Failure (rarely; mostly in patients with prior doxorubicin exposure) |
| | Neurological | Headache, confusion, blurred vision, syncope, fatigue |
| | Genitourinary | (Intravesical only) Cystitis, dysuria, hematuria, bladder fibrosis/contraction |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Irreversible renal failure via HUS, fatal septicaemia due to leucopenia, and severe tissue sloughing/amputation following extravasation [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Vinca Alkaloids:** May cause acute respiratory distress and bronchospasm within minutes to hours of administration in mitomycin-primed patients.
- **Doxorubicin:** Increased risk of congestive heart failure.
- **Oxygen Therapy:** Patients previously treated with mitomycin may develop ARDS if maintained on FiO₂ >50% during surgery.
- **Incompatibilities:** Mitomycin is unstable in highly acidic or alkaline environments; the optimal pH is 7.0 [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category D. Safe use in pregnancy is not established. Animal studies show teratogenicity. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. It is unknown if mitomycin is excreted in human milk; however, serious adverse reactions in infants are possible [Australian].
- **Fertility:** The impact on human fertility is unknown [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data provided in available sources [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage reduction is required; do not use if serum creatinine is >1.7 mg/dL [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosage reduction is usually required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Mitomycin is an inactive prodrug converted to a trifunctional alkylating agent at physiological pH in the presence of NADPH. It inhibits DNA synthesis by cross-linking guanine and cytosine. At high concentrations, it also suppresses RNA and protein synthesis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised in the liver; degradative pathways are saturable at low doses.
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly cleared from serum (half-life of 17 minutes after 30 mg bolus). Does not cross the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 10% is excreted unchanged in the urine. Renal clearance increases with higher doses due to metabolic saturation [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in available sources [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store between 15°C and 25°C. Keep in the outer carton to protect from light [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** 
    - Stable for 1 hour in Water for Injections.
    - Stable for 2 hours in 0.9% Sodium Chloride.
    - Solutions should be stored in a refrigerator (away from light) but warmed to room/body temperature before use [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** S4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
