# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions’ Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data to support safe and effective prescribing and administration.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for analysis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/generic name).
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt; a naturally occurring carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Given as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours).
    - Maximum recorded dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Given as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments: Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before starting therapy.
    - Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting. In ESRD patients, other common events (which may be confounded by underlying pathology) include hypertension (18-21%), headache (12-37%), pharyngitis (15-27%), and injection site reactions (27-38%) [US].
- **Uncommon/Rare:** Body odor, gastritis, and dizziness.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
    - Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress (laryngeal edema/bronchospasm).
    - Neurologic: Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (at 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg (see Section 3).
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. 
    - In ESRD patients on dialysis, chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) which are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing the substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, it helps correct the carnitine deficiency caused by reduced meat/dairy intake, reduced renal synthesis, and dialytic losses.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; mean distribution half-life of 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times per week after dialysis increased mean plasma concentrations from 20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to trough levels between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentration range: 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
