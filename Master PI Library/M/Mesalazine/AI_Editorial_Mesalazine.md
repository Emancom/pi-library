# Editorial Product Information: Mesalazine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of mesalazine for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines. Prescribers should refer to the current official PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mesalazine (also known as 5-aminosalicylic acid or 5-ASA), an intestinal anti-inflammatory agent.
- **Brand Names:** **Asacol** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A07EC02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **400 mg enteric-coated tablets:** Reddish to brownish oblong tablets [Australian].
    - **800 mg enteric-coated tablets:** Reddish to brownish oblong tablets [Australian].
    - **1600 mg film-coated tablets:** Dark orange oblong tablets with a multi-layer coating system [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Chiesi Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Ulcerative Colitis:** Treatment of mild to moderate active ulcerative colitis in adults [Australian].
- **Maintenance of Remission:** Maintenance of remission of ulcerative colitis in adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Acute Disease:** 2.4 g to 4.8 g per day. This may be administered as a single daily dose or in divided doses [Australian].
- **Maintenance Therapy:** 1.6 g to 2.4 g per day, administered once daily or in divided doses [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets must be swallowed whole with liquid.
- **Do not** chew, crush, or break tablets as this compromises the pH-dependent enteric coating.
- May be taken with or without food [Australian].

### Missed Doses
- If a dose is missed, the next dose should be taken as scheduled; do not double the dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to mesalazine or any tablet excipients (e.g., lactose in 400 mg and 800 mg strengths) [Australian].
- History of hypersensitivity to salicylates.
- Severe renal impairment (GFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m²).
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Renal Function:** Mesalazine-induced nephrotoxicity should be suspected if renal function declines. Monitor serum creatinine and urinary status (dipsticks) prior to and during treatment. Recommended monitoring: 14 days after initiation, then 2–3 times at 4-week intervals, followed by 3-monthly checks [Australian].
- **Blood Dyscrasias:** Rare but serious cases of bone marrow depression and blood dyscrasias have occurred. Stop treatment immediately if symptoms such as unexplained bleeding, bruising, fever, or sore throat develop [Australian].
- **Cardiac Hypersensitivity:** Rare reports of mesalazine-induced myocarditis and pericarditis. Re-challenge is contraindicated if cardiac hypersensitivity is suspected.
- **Idiopathic Intracranial Hypertension:** Patients should be monitored for severe headache, visual changes, or tinnitus. Discontinuation should be considered if these symptoms occur [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Including SJS, TEN, and DRESS. Discontinue at the first appearance of skin rash or mucosal lesions [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Disease:** Patients with asthma or existing lung disease require careful monitoring [Australian].
- **Sulfasalazine Intolerance:** Close supervision is required for patients with a history of intolerance to sulfasalazine [Australian].
- **Urine Discoloration:** Contact with sodium hypochlorite bleach (e.g., in toilets) may turn urine red-brown.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Organ-specific reactions can affect the heart, lungs, liver, kidneys, and skin.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (≥1% to <10%) | Dyspepsia, Rash. |
| **Uncommon** (0.1% to 1%) | Eosinophilia, Paresthesia, Urticaria, Pruritus, Pyrexia, Chest pain. |
| **Rare** (<0.1%) | Headache, Dizziness, Myocarditis, Pericarditis, Abdominal pain, Diarrhoea, Flatulence, Nausea, Vomiting, Photosensitivity. |
| **Very Rare** (<0.01%) | Aplastic anaemia, Agranulocytosis, Pancytopenia, Peripheral neuropathy, Allergic/fibrotic lung reactions (dyspnoea, cough, alveolitis), Acute pancreatitis, Hepatitis, Alopecia, Myalgia, Arthralgia, Interstitial nephritis, Renal failure, Reversible oligospermia. |
| **Not Known** | Idiopathic intracranial hypertension, Pleurisy, DRESS, SJS, TEN, Nephrolithiasis, Lupus-like syndrome. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Nephrotoxic Agents:** Concurrent use of NSAIDs or azathioprine increases the risk of renal adverse reactions [Australian].
- **Azathioprine, 6-Mercaptopurine, and Thioguanine:** Mesalazine may increase the myelosuppressive effects of these agents, potentially leading to life-threatening infections. Regular haematological monitoring (weekly) is recommended during initiation [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Weak evidence suggests mesalazine may decrease the anticoagulant effect of warfarin [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** Potential for false-positive results in urinary normetanephrine measurements using liquid chromatography [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Mesalazine crosses the placental barrier. Data in humans are limited but generally do not indicate a high risk of malformations. Use only if benefits clearly outweigh risks. High-dose maternal use (2–4 g) has been associated with one case of neonatal renal failure [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** N-acetyl-5-ASA and mesalazine are excreted in breast milk. Use only if benefits outweigh risks. If the infant develops diarrhoea, breastfeeding must be discontinued [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show no effect on male or female fertility. Reversible oligospermia has been reported in humans [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children aged 6–18 years (400 mg/800 mg tablets) and safety/efficacy not established for the 1600 mg tablet in those under 18 [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Administer with caution. Use is limited to those with normal or non-severe renal/hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment (GFR < 30 mL/min). Monitor renal function regularly [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Caution is recommended as mesalazine may increase liver enzyme levels [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Exerts a topical anti-inflammatory effect on colonic mucosal cells. It inhibits LTB4-stimulated macrophage migration, inhibits pro-inflammatory leukotrienes, and activates PPAR-γ receptors to counteract inflammatory responses. It also acts as an antioxidant [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Enteric coating ensures release starts at pH 7 (terminal ileum and colon). Bioavailability is approximately 25–30% [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** ~43% of mesalazine is protein-bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly acetylated to inactive N-acetyl-5-ASA in both the intestinal mucosa and the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (as N-acetyl-5-ASA) and faecal. The elimination half-life of mesalazine is approximately 20 hours (range 5–77 hours) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **400 mg Tablet:** A Japanese Phase 3 study (n=229) demonstrated non-inferiority of 2.4 g/day and superiority of 3.6 g/day compared to Pentasa 2.25 g/day for inducing remission in mild-moderate UC [Australian].
- **800 mg Tablet:** A multinational study demonstrated that 4.8 g/day was effective in inducing endoscopic remission at week 6 and week 10 compared to placebo [Australian].
- **1600 mg Tablet:** Investigated in an induction trial (n=817) where 3.2 g once daily was non-inferior to twice-daily 400 mg tablets (total 3.2 g/day) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Keep in original packaging to protect from moisture.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
