# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)** via Baxter Healthcare Corporation.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a professional synthesis for informational purposes and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism; chemically designated as (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Errors:** Acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Severe Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dosing:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment if trough levels are adequate.

### Administration Notes
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter or discolouration before use.
- The formulation contains no preservatives; any unused portion must be discarded.

## 4. Contraindications
- **General:** None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and laryngeal edema, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures may occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Renal Impairment Nuance:** While the IV form is used for ESRD, the safety of *oral* levocarnitine in renal insufficiency has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event frequencies are based on placebo-controlled trials in ESRD patients undergoing chronic hemodialysis.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), fever (7-12% [US]), asthenia (9-12% [US]), accidental injury (10-12% [US]).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8-12% [US]).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9-18% [US]).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13-18% [US]), paresthesia (5-12% [US]).

### Common (1–10%)
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia (6%), chest pain (12%), palpitations (5%), arrhythmia (2%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain (9%), anorexia (5%), constipation (3%), dyspepsia (5%).
- **Musculoskeletal:** Back pain (8%), leg cramps (4%).
- **Other:** Body odor (reported post-marketing), gastritis, rash (3%), pruritus (5%), anemia (6%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity reactions.
- Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) has been reported when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin anticoagulation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response, use during pregnancy only if clearly required.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the clinical benefit to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders and ESRD apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis via the intravenous route.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available source.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process delivers substrates for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels typically stabilize or increase after one week of therapy (3 doses).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** Studies demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis significantly increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine levels from ~20 µmol/L (baseline) to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Bioavailability:** In healthy volunteers, absolute bioavailability of oral formulations (tablets and solution) was approximately 15-16% after correcting for endogenous concentrations.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Cautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Schedule:** Not a scheduled controlled substance [US].
