# Editorial Product Information: Mitozantrone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Mitozantrone Ebewe (Sandoz Pty Ltd)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of mitozantrone for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local PI before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Mitozantrone hydrochloride (a synthetic anthracenedione).
- **Brand Names**: **Mitozantrone Ebewe** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: L01DB07.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - Sterile, clear, blue, aqueous, isotonic solution for injection [Australian].
    - 10 mg/5 mL (2 mg/mL) glass vial [Australian].
    - 20 mg/10 mL (2 mg/mL) glass vial [Australian].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous (IV) infusion only. 
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Sandoz Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic carcinoma of the breast** [Australian-approved].
- **Non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma** [Australian-approved].
- **Adult acute non-lymphocytic leukaemia (ANLL)** [Australian-approved].
- **Chronic myelogenous leukaemia (CML) in blast crisis** [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Cumulative Dose Limit**: Total cumulative doses exceeding 140 mg/m² are not recommended due to the risk of fatal overdose from severe leucopenia and infection [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Breast Cancer and Non-Hodgkin’s Lymphoma (Single Agent)**: 
    - Initial dose: 14 mg/m² IV as a single dose every 21 days.
    - Reduced initial dose: 12 mg/m² for patients with prior therapy or poor general condition.
- **Breast Cancer and Non-Hodgkin’s Lymphoma (Combination Therapy)**: 
    - Reduce the initial dose by 2 to 4 mg/m² compared to single-agent dosing (approx. 10–12 mg/m²) [Australian].
- **Leukaemia (ANLL or CML Blast Crisis)**:
    - Induction: 12 mg/m² IV daily for 5 consecutive days (total 60 mg/m² per course).
    - Combination (with Cytosine Arabinoside): 10–12 mg/m² daily for 3 days (mitozantrone) and 100 mg/m² daily for 7 days (cytosine arabinoside continuous infusion).

### Dose Adjustments based on Myelosuppression (Solid Tumours)
Subsequent dosing should be adjusted based on the nadir of white blood cell (WBC) and platelet counts:
- **WBC > 1.5 and Platelets > 50 (x10⁹/L)**: Repeat prior dose; if recovery ≤ 21 days, may increase by 2 mg/m².
- **WBC < 1.5 or Platelets < 50 (x10⁹/L)**: Decrease subsequent dose by 2 mg/m² after recovery.
- **WBC < 1.0 or Platelets < 25 (x10⁹/L)**: Decrease subsequent dose by 4 mg/m² after recovery.

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution**: Dilute to at least 50 mL with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Glucose.
- **Infusion Rate**: Administer into a freely running IV infusion over 3 to 5 minutes.
- **Extravasation**: If extravasation occurs, stop immediately and restart in another vein.
- **Staining**: Mitozantrone can cause blue/green staining of skin and surfaces.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to mitozantrone [Australian].
- Breastfeeding [Australian].
- Patients with abnormal cardiac function who have had substantial prior anthracycline exposure [Australian].
- Severe myelosuppression (where recovery from prior therapy has not occurred) [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- **Intrathecal use** is strictly contraindicated [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings**: Not specified as "boxed" in the source but highlighted as critical [Australian].
- **Cardiac Toxicity**: Cases of congestive heart failure (CHF) and decreased Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF) have been reported. Risk increases with cumulative doses > 140 mg/m², prior anthracycline use, or mediastinal radiotherapy. Regular cardiac monitoring is required.
- **Myelosuppression**: Severe leucopenia is the primary dose-limiting toxicity. Nadir typically occurs at day 10, with recovery by day 21. Serial full blood counts (FBC) are mandatory.
- **Secondary Malignancies**: Risk of secondary Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML), Acute Promyelocytic Leukaemia (APL), or Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS), particularly when used with other Topoisomerase II inhibitors [Australian].
- **Hyperuricaemia**: Rapid tumour lysis may occur; monitor uric acid and consider hypouricaemic therapy before starting leukaemia treatment.
- **Vaccination**: Avoid live virus vaccines during therapy due to the risk of infection in immunocompromised patients [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%)**: Nausea, vomiting, stomatitis/mucositis (more frequent in leukaemia patients), alopecia (usually mild/reversible), leucopenia, and neutropenia.
- **Common (1–10%)**: Thrombocytopenia, anaemia, urinary tract infections, upper respiratory infections, and elevated liver enzymes.
- **Uncommon/Rare (<1%)**:
    - **Gastrointestinal**: Diarrhoea, GI bleeding, abdominal pain, pancreatitis.
    - **Respiratory**: Dyspnoea, interstitial pneumonitis.
    - **Cardiovascular**: Arrhythmias, ECG changes, bradycardia, chest pain, and CHF.
    - **Dermatological**: Rash, nail pigmentation, onycholysis, tissue necrosis (following extravasation).
    - **General**: Fatigue, somnolence, confusion, anxiety, anaphylaxis.
- **Serious Effects**: Fatal myelosuppression/infection, cardiotoxicity, and secondary AML (incidence reported up to 5% in one study combined with methotrexate) [Australian].
- **Visual/Renal**: Reversible blue discolouration of the urine (up to 24h post-dose) and sclera [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Heparin**: Must not be mixed in the same infusion as it may cause precipitation [Australian].
- **BCRP Transporter**: Mitozantrone is a substrate for Breast Cancer Resistance Protein (BCRP). Inhibitors may increase and inducers may decrease bioavailability.
- **Vitamin K Antagonists**: Increased bleeding risk; monitor INR closely.
- **Immunosuppressants**: Increased risk of lymphoproliferative syndromes.
- **Myelosuppressive agents**: Potential for additive haematological toxicity.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: **Category D** [Australian]. Known to be mutagenic and potentially teratogenic. Avoid use unless benefits outweigh risks. 
    - **Contraception**: Females should use effective contraception during and for 4 months after treatment. Males should use contraception during and for at least 6 months after treatment [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: **Contraindicated**. Mitozantrone is excreted in breast milk and has been detected 28 days post-administration [Australian].
- **Fertility**: High risk of transitory or persistent amenorrhoea. Preservation of gametes should be considered before therapy.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Experience in children is limited; safety and efficacy not fully established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No specific data provided in available sources [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment**: Not formally studied. However, since renal excretion is limited, toxicity is unlikely to be removed by dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Use with extreme caution. Clearance may be reduced in severe dysfunction (bilirubin > 60 µmol/L). Dosage adjustment may be necessary [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: DNA-reactive agent and Topoisomerase II inhibitor. It intercalates into DNA and causes cross-links and strand breaks. It is not cell-cycle phase-specific.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Rapid and wide extravascular distribution; 78% protein bound [Australian]. Does not cross the blood-brain barrier.
    - **Metabolism**: Primarily metabolized to inactive mono- and dicarboxylic acid derivatives.
    - **Excretion**: Primarily biliary (major route). Renal excretion is limited (6–11%).
    - **Elimination**: Triphasic clearance with a long terminal half-life of over 12 days (range 5–18 days) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Breast Cancer**: Single-agent response rates range from 20% (previously treated) to 40% (first-line).
- **Non-Hodgkin’s Lymphoma**: A 41% response rate was demonstrated in relapsed/refractory cases using 14 mg/m² every three weeks.
- **ANLL**: A 39% response rate was observed with 12 mg/m² daily for five days [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage**: Store below 25°C. Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Stability**: Diluted solutions are stable for 72 hours at room temperature, but should ideally be used within 24 hours (if stored at 2°C–8°C) to maintain microbiological safety.
- **Handling**: Cytotoxic agent; requires preparation in a laminar flow cabinet by trained personnel using protective equipment. 

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Classification**: Cytotoxic antineoplastic agent.
