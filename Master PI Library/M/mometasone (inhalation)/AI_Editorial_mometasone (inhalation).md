# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinical overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local approved labeling before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/generic).
- **Chemical Class:** A carrier molecule involved in long-chain fatty acid transport; (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered initially, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are generally 50 mg/kg/day, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg/day may be required [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are supra-normal [US].

### Monitoring
- It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to starting therapy. 
- Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended for metabolic disorders, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable and compatible when mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL. Mixed solutions are stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management. Re-challenge should only be considered after weighing risks and benefits, with close monitoring for recurrence [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **General Monitoring:** Safety and efficacy of oral formulations have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency; chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
The following events occurred with a frequency $\ge$ 5% in clinical trials of ESRD patients on hemodialysis (regardless of causality) [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event | Frequency (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain | $\ge$ 5% |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations | $\ge$ 5% |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, anorexia | $\ge$ 5% |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight change | $\ge$ 5% |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression | $\ge$ 5% |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis | $\ge$ 5% |
| **Skin** | Pruritus | $\ge$ 5% |
| **Urogenital** | Kidney failure | $\ge$ 5% |

### Other Reactions
- **Metabolic/Endocrine:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis have been observed less frequently [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned/specified]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate/well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies on levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine following exogenous administration. The risk of excess carnitine intake by the infant must be weighed against the benefit to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See dosage recommendations for metabolic disorders; safety and efficacy are established for inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specially indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. Use of the *oral* formulation is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following intravenous bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical studies in ESRD patients on hemodialysis demonstrated that 20 mg/kg dosing three times weekly significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough concentrations reached steady state by 12–24 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is proven, the effects on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat. Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
