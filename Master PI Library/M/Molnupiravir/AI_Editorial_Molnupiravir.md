# Editorial Product Information: Molnupiravir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory guidance into a single, clinically focused reference.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory requirements for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Molnupiravir (a 5´-isobutyrate prodrug of the cytidine nucleoside analogue N4-hydroxycytidine [NHC]).
- **Brand Name**: Lagevrio [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: Not yet assigned [Australian].
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: 200 mg opaque, ‘Swedish Orange’ capsules, printed with "82" and the corporate logo in white ink.
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Merck Sharp & Dohme (Australia) Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Treatment of COVID-19**: Indicated for the treatment of COVID-19 in adults who do not require immediate initiation of supplemental oxygen and who are at an increased risk of progression to severe disease, hospitalisation, or death [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: 800 mg (four 200 mg capsules) taken orally every 12 hours for a duration of 5 days.
- **Timing of Initiation**: Treatment should commence as soon as possible following a COVID-19 diagnosis, and strictly within 5 days of the onset of symptoms.
- **Administration**: Capsules may be taken with or without food. They should be swallowed whole and not opened, broken, or crushed.
- **Missed Dose**: 
    - If within 10 hours of the scheduled time: The patient should take the dose immediately and resume the normal schedule.
    - If more than 10 hours have elapsed: The dose should be skipped, and the next dose taken at the regular time.
    - Patients must not double the dose to compensate for a missed one.
- **Duration of Therapy**: Safety and efficacy have not been established for treatment periods exceeding 5 days.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity**: Known hypersensitivity to **molnupiravir** or any of the inactive excipients (e.g., croscarmellose sodium, magnesium stearate, microcrystalline cellulose).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis and angioedema, have been reported. If a clinically significant hypersensitivity reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately and appropriate supportive care initiated.
- **Bone and Cartilage Toxicity**: Based on animal data (rats), **molnupiravir** may affect bone and cartilage. While these findings are considered not relevant for adults with mature skeletons, they inform the recommendation against use in paediatric populations.
- **Monitoring**: Healthcare providers should monitor for signs of disease progression and potential adverse reactions.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in the MOVe-OUT trial were generally mild (Grade 1) or moderate (Grade 2).

### Clinical Trial Experience (Common ≥1% to <10%)
| Adverse Reaction | Molnupiravir (N=710) | Placebo (N=701) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Diarrhoea** | 2% | 2% |
| **Nausea** | 1% | 1% |
| **Dizziness** | 1% | 1% |

### Post-marketing Experience (Frequency Not Known)
- **Gastrointestinal**: Vomiting.
- **Immune System**: Hypersensitivity, anaphylaxis, angioedema.
- **Skin and Subcutaneous Tissue**: Erythema, pruritus, rash, urticaria.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**: No clinical drug-drug interaction trials have been conducted. However, based on *in vitro* data, **molnupiravir** and its metabolite NHC are not substrates, inhibitors, or inducers of major drug-metabolising enzymes or transporters.
- **Clinical Significance**: The potential for interaction with concomitant medications is considered low. NHC metabolism follows endogenous pyrimidine pathways.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Classification**: Category D [Australian].
    - **Recommendation**: Not recommended for use during pregnancy. Animal studies have shown embryofetal lethality and teratogenicity (at 8 times human exposure) and reduced fetal growth.
    - **Prescribing Note**: In females of childbearing potential, pregnancy status should be considered/verified before initiating treatment.
- **Contraception**:
    - **Females**: Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for 4 days after the final dose.
    - **Males**: Men who are sexually active with partners of childbearing potential should use an adequate form of contraception during treatment and for at least 3 months after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown if **molnupiravir** is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 4 days after the last dose due to the potential for adverse reactions in the infant.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Not recommended for patients under 18 years of age. Safety and efficacy have not been established, and animal data suggests potential risks to bone and cartilage growth.
- **Elderly Use**: No dose adjustment is required. Clinical trials showed no significant difference in safety or PK profiles between patients >65 years and younger subjects.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose adjustment is required for any degree of renal impairment. NHC is not significantly eliminated via renal clearance.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No dose adjustment is required. Preclinical data indicate hepatic elimination is not a major route for NHC.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: **Molnupiravir** is a prodrug that is converted to NHC and subsequently phosphorylated intracellularly into NHC-triphosphate (NHC-TP). NHC-TP is incorporated into viral RNA by viral RNA polymerase, leading to an accumulation of errors in the viral genome (viral error catastrophe), which inhibits viral replication.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Median $T_{max}$ is 1.5 hours. A high-fat meal decreases $C_{max}$ by 35% but does not significantly impact total exposure (AUC).
    - **Distribution**: NHC does not bind to plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism**: Hydrolyzed by carboxylesterases to NHC; further metabolism follows pyrimidine pathways.
    - **Elimination**: Effective half-life is approximately 3.3 hours. Less than 3% of the dose is excreted in the urine as NHC.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MOVe-OUT Trial**: A Phase 3, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in non-hospitalised, unvaccinated adults with mild-to-moderate COVID-19 and at least one risk factor for progression (e.g., obesity, age >60, diabetes, serious heart conditions).
- **Primary Outcome**: **Molnupiravir** reduced the risk of hospitalisation or death by approximately 30% (6.8% in the treatment group vs. 9.7% in the placebo group; adjusted risk difference -3.0%).
- **Mortality**: At Day 29, 1 death was reported in the **molnupiravir** group compared to 9 deaths in the placebo group.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage**: Store below 30°C.
- **Handling**: Keep in the original primary container (HDPE bottle) to protect from moisture.
- **Shelf Life**: Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
- **Approval Status**: Provisional approval granted based on Phase 3 data; continued approval is subject to the submission of additional confirmatory data [Australian].
