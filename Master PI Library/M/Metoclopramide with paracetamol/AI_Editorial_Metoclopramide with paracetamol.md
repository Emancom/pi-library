# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a structured format for quick reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule essential for long-chain fatty acid transport across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from genetic metabolic disorders [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are high [US].

### Monitoring and Administration Instructions
- **Target Plasma Levels:** For metabolic disorders, plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** None listed as absolute contraindications in available sources, though serious reactions are noted under warnings [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred—sometimes within minutes of administration—particularly in ESRD patients. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit assessment [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may trigger seizures or increase seizure frequency/severity in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity [US].
- **Oral Metabolite Accumulation:** While this PI concerns the injectable form, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients are often confounded by underlying pathology.
- **Very Common (>10%):** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), flu syndrome (25–29%), headache (22%), hypertension (20–21%), diarrhea (16–35%), vomiting (15–21%), pharyngitis (15–27%), and dizziness (10–18%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, chest pain, fever, infection, tachycardia, nausea, dyspepsia, hypercalcemia, and anemia [US].
- **Specific Reactions:**
    - **Body Odor:** Noted as a less frequent but specific side effect [US].
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis and transient nausea/vomiting [US].
    - **Neurological:** Seizures (postmarketing) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified, but animal data provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Animal data (dairy cows) show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were found in standard bacterial and yeast tests. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (mg/kg) and applicable to paediatric patients. See Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; generally, dose selection should be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:** Injectable levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, safety/efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency due to metabolite accumulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Data:** In ESRD patients, a dose of 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine concentrations from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Clinical Outcomes:** While levocarnitine injection effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx only [US].
- **NDC Number:** 36000-368-10 (10 x 5 mL vials) [US].
