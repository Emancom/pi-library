# Editorial Product Information: Methyldopa

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of methyldopa, synthesised from official Product Information (PI) for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI was available for synthesis; information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian) is marked as "Not specified in available sources" where data was absent.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methyldopa (as sesquihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Centrally acting antihypertensive; L-isomer of alpha-methyldopa.
- **Brand Names:** **Aldomet** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablet:** 250 mg (yellow, biconvex, film-coated, marked "250" and "ALDOMET") [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Management of mild, moderate, or severe hypertension [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial Dose:** 250 mg two or three times daily for the first 48 hours [Australian].
- **Titration:** Adjust dosage at intervals of not less than two days until adequate blood pressure control is achieved. To mitigate initial sedation, dose increases should preferably be initiated with the evening dose [Australian].
- **Maintenance/Maximum Dose:** The maximum recommended daily dosage is 3 g [Australian].
- **Transition from other Antihypertensives:** When adding methyldopa to existing therapy, the initial dose should be limited to 500 mg daily. Previous agents may need to be tapered or terminated gradually [Australian].

### Combination Therapy
- Methyldopa may be used concomitantly with hydrochlorothiazide or beta-blockers. A specific combination of methyldopa 500 mg and hydrochlorothiazide 50 mg may be administered once daily [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets are administered orally.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
Methyldopa is contraindicated in patients with:
- **Active Hepatic Disease:** Including acute hepatitis and active cirrhosis [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to any component, including patients who developed hepatic disorders during previous methyldopa therapy [Australian].
- **MAOI Therapy:** Concomitant use with monoamine oxidase inhibitors [Australian].
- **Catecholamine-Secreting Tumours:** Phaeochromocytoma or paraganglioma [Australian].
- **Porphyria:** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None listed in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Acquired Haemolytic Anaemia:** Rarely occurs. If symptoms of anaemia arise, perform haemoglobin/haematocrit tests. Evidence of haemolytic anaemia requires immediate discontinuation; steroids may be required. Fatalities have occurred rarely [Australian].
- **Positive Coombs Test:** Occurs in 10–20% of patients, typically after 6–12 months of therapy. It is dose-related (rare at <1 g/day). A positive test may interfere with blood cross-matching, necessitating an indirect Coombs test [Australian].
- **Liver Function:** Fever, jaundice, or eosinophilia may occur, usually within the first 2–3 months. Fatal hepatic necrosis has been reported. Monitor hepatic function and WBC counts during the first 6–12 weeks of therapy [Australian].
- **CNS Effects:** Transient sedation is common at initiation or dose increases. Monitor for depression, particularly in patients with a history of depressive illness [Australian].
- **Surgery/Anaesthesia:** Reduced doses of anaesthetics may be required. Hypotension during surgery is typically manageable with vasopressors [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Liver function tests (LFTs) and full blood count (WBC/differential) during the first 6–12 weeks or if unexplained fever occurs.
- Periodic Coombs tests [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally infrequent and methyldopa is usually well-tolerated.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Central Nervous System** | **Common/Transient:** Sedation, headache, asthenia. **Infrequent:** Paraesthesia, parkinsonism, Bell's palsy, involuntary movements, nightmares, impaired mental acuity, reversible psychosis/depression, dizziness, light-headedness. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Bradycardia, orthostatic hypotension, aggravation of angina, AV block, oedema/weight gain (may require diuretics; discontinue if heart failure signs appear). |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, constipation, flatus, diarrhoea, colitis, dry mouth, 'black' tongue, pancreatitis, sialadenitis. |
| **Haematological** | Positive Coombs test, haemolytic anaemia, bone marrow depression, leucopenia, granulocytopenia, thrombocytopenia, eosinophilia. |
| **Hepatic** | Hepatitis, jaundice, abnormal LFTs. |
| **Allergic/Dermatological** | Drug-related fever, lupus-like syndrome, myocarditis, pericarditis, angioedema, urticaria, eczema/rash, toxic epidermal necrolysis. |
| **Other** | Nasal stuffiness, gynaecomastia, lactation, hyperprolactinaemia, amenorrhoea, impotence, decreased libido, arthralgia, myalgia, darkened urine when exposed to air. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Lithium:** Increased risk of lithium toxicity; monitor serum lithium levels closely [Australian].
- **Other Antihypertensives:** Potentiation of antihypertensive effects [Australian].
- **Iron:** Bioavailability of methyldopa is significantly reduced when taken with ferrous sulfate or ferrous gluconate, potentially compromising blood pressure control [Australian].
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated; may lead to hypertensive crises [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category A:** Used extensively under medical supervision without evidence of foetal abnormalities. 
    - Methyldopa crosses the placental barrier. While no teratogenic effects are reported, potential risks should be weighed against benefits [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Methyldopa is excreted in breast milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Initial dose is 10 mg/kg/day in 2–4 divided doses. Maximum dose is 65 mg/kg or 3.0 g daily (whichever is less) [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity and risk of syncope, possibly due to advanced arteriosclerotic disease. Lower starting doses are recommended [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Methyldopa is primarily renally excreted. Patients with renal impairment may require lower doses [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in active hepatic disease. Use with caution in patients with a history of liver disease [Australian].
- **Dialysis:** Methyldopa is dialysable; hypertension may recur following the procedure [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Antihypertensive activity is due to the L-isomer. It inhibits dopa decarboxylase and depletes noradrenaline in animal tissues. It reduces blood pressure by lowering peripheral resistance [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces both supine and standing blood pressure. Cardiac output is usually maintained, though heart rate may slow in some patients. It does not typically reduce GFR or renal blood flow [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in detail in the available source; noted to be dialysable and primarily renally excreted [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 3 years [Australian].
