# Editorial Product Information: Melatonin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. As only the Australian PI was provided for this synthesis, regional comparisons are limited to that jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Melatonin (a naturally occurring hormone, not of plant or animal origin).
- **Chemical Class:** Melatonin receptor agonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Circadin** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N05CH01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Prolonged-release tablet, 2 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** RAD Data Australia Pty Ltd; Distributed by Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Primary Insomnia:** Monotherapy for the short-term treatment of primary insomnia, specifically characterised by poor quality of sleep in patients aged 55 years or older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose (≥ 55 years):** 2 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Timing:** Administer 1 to 2 hours before bedtime [Australian].
- **Food Effect:** Tablets should be taken after food to maintain the intended absorption profile [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole. Do not crush or chew, as this destroys the prolonged-release properties [Australian].
- **Duration of Treatment:** May be continued for up to 13 weeks [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to melatonin or any excipients (e.g., lactose) in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Drowsiness:** Melatonin induces sleepiness. Caution is required if the effects of drowsiness pose a safety risk (e.g., operating machinery or driving) [Australian].
- **Autoimmune Diseases:** Use is not recommended due to a lack of clinical data in patients with autoimmune conditions [Australian].
- **Excipients (Lactose):** Contains lactose monohydrate. Not recommended for patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance, LAPP lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** No specific laboratory monitoring requirements identified [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
In clinical trials, approximately 48.8% of patients reported an adverse reaction compared to 37.8% for placebo [Australian].

### Common Adverse Effects (1–10%)
| Body System | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Infections** | Nasopharyngitis (4.0%), Upper respiratory tract infection (2.9%) |
| **Nervous System** | Headache (5.7%) |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Back pain (3.8%), Arthralgia (3.5%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea (3.1%) |

### Uncommon and Rare Effects (<1%)
- **Psychiatric:** Irritability, nervousness, restlessness, insomnia, abnormal dreams, anxiety [Australian].
- **Vascular:** Hypertension (uncommon); Hot flush (rare) [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, dyspepsia, mouth ulceration, dry mouth [Australian].
- **Hepatobiliary:** Hyperbilirubinaemia [Australian].
- **Serious/Rare:** Angioedema, chest pain, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and palpitations [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP1A Enzymes:** Melatonin metabolism is primarily mediated by CYP1A1 and CYP1A2.
    - **Fluvoxamine:** Increases melatonin levels (17-fold higher AUC); this combination should be avoided [Australian].
    - **Quinolones (CYP1A2 inhibitors):** May increase melatonin exposure [Australian].
    - **5- or 8-methoxypsoralen:** Increases melatonin levels by inhibiting metabolism [Australian].
    - **Cimetidine:** Increases melatonin exposure (1.7-fold increase) [Australian].
    - **Oestrogens:** May increase melatonin levels by inhibiting CYP1A1 and CYP1A2 [Australian].
    - **Inducers (Carbamazepine, Rifampicin):** May reduce plasma melatonin concentrations [Australian].
    - **Cigarette Smoking:** May decrease melatonin levels via CYP1A2 induction [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Should be avoided. Alcohol reduces sleep effectiveness and may disrupt the prolonged-release mechanism of the tablet [Australian].
- **Z-drugs and Benzodiazepines:** May enhance sedative properties (e.g., zolpidem, zaleplon, zopiclone). Co-administration with zolpidem led to increased impairment of attention and coordination [Australian].
- **Thioridazine and Imipramine:** Increased feelings of "muzzy-headedness" or difficulty performing tasks when co-administered [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Animal studies show no significant embryofetal development effects at high doses, but clinical data in humans are absent. Use is not recommended in pregnant women or those intended to become pregnant [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Exogenous melatonin is likely excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No significant effects on fertility were observed in animal models at doses >900-fold the human clinical dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for use in children or adolescents under 18 years due to insufficient safety and efficacy data [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Melatonin metabolism declines with age, leading to higher AUC and Cmax levels in older subjects [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics have not been studied across renal insufficiency stages; use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended. Endogenous melatonin levels are markedly elevated in hepatic impairment due to decreased clearance [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Melatonin is a MT1 and MT2 receptor agonist. MT1 receptors are involved in inhibiting neuronal firing, while MT2 receptors relate to the phase-shifting response of the circadian clock [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Associated with the control of circadian rhythms, entrainment to the light-dark cycle, and increased sleep propensity [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is ~15% with significant first-pass metabolism (85%). Tmax is 2.6 hours (fed state) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is ~60% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP1A1, CYP1A2, and potentially CYP2C19. The main inactive metabolite is 6-sulphatoxy-melatonin (6-S-MT) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 3.5–4 hours. Excretion is renal (89% as metabolites) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
Pivotal studies focused on patients $\ge$ 55 years with primary insomnia. 
- **Quality of Sleep (QOS):** Significant improvement seen in 47% of melatonin patients vs 27% for placebo.
- **Sleep Latency:** A polysomnography study showed a reduction in sleep onset latency by 9 minutes compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Withdrawal:** Studies showed no evidence of rebound insomnia or withdrawal symptoms upon discontinuation [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Condition:** Protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
