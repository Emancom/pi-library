# Editorial Product Information: Memantine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of memantine for healthcare professionals. It is designed to synthesise clinical and regulatory data into a concise, structured format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Memantine hydrochloride
- **Chemical Class:** N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptor antagonist; adamantane derivative.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Memantine [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** N06DX01 (Not explicitly stated in provided source, but standard for class).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **10 mg film-coated tablets:** White, peanut-shaped, biconvex, scored. [Australian]
    - **20 mg film-coated tablets:** Pale red, oval, biconvex. [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Alzheimer’s Disease:** Treatment of symptoms associated with moderately severe to severe Alzheimer’s disease. [Australian]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Frequency:** Administer once daily, at the same time each day, with or without food.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 20 mg per day.

### Titration Schedule
To reduce the risk of adverse effects, the dose should be escalated by 5 mg per week over a 3-week period to reach the maintenance dose:
- **Week 1:** 5 mg (half of a 10 mg tablet) daily.
- **Week 2:** 10 mg daily.
- **Week 3:** 15 mg (one and a half 10 mg tablets) daily.
- **Week 4 onwards:** 20 mg daily.

### Clinical Management
- **Monitoring:** Reassess tolerance and dosing regularly, ideally within three months of starting therapy.
- **Continuance:** Treatment should continue only as long as a therapeutic benefit is observed and the patient tolerates the medication. 

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to memantine hydrochloride or any excipients.
- **Seizure Disorders:** Currently active seizure disorder or a history of seizures. [Australian]
- **Epilepsy:** Specifically contraindicated in patients with epilepsy. [Australian]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Risk of Seizures:** Use with caution in patients with a history of convulsions or those with predisposing factors for epilepsy.
- **Ocular Toxicity:** Animal studies (rats, dogs, baboons) have shown evidence of lens opacities and corneal clouding at systemic exposures higher than clinical doses. While human clinical trials did not show significant ocular changes over 6–12 months, clinician awareness is advised.
- **Urinary pH Elevation:** Factors that increase urinary pH (e.g., drastic diet change from carnivore to vegetarian, alkalising gastric buffers, renal tubular acidosis, or Proteus urinary tract infections) may reduce memantine clearance, leading to increased plasma levels.
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Use with caution and close supervision in patients with recent myocardial infarction, uncompensated congestive heart failure (NYHA III-IV), or uncontrolled hypertension, as these populations were largely excluded from clinical trials.
- **Lactose Intolerance:** The 10 mg tablet formulation contains lactose monohydrate; not recommended for patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption.
- **Caregiver Requirement:** Therapy should only be initiated if a caregiver is available to monitor patient compliance.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally mild to moderate. The following frequencies were observed in clinical trials (≥ 2% incidence):

| **Psychiatric** | Agitation | 8.7 | 16.7 |
| | Insomnia | 5.0 | 4.9 |
| | Hallucination | 4.7 | 2.1 |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness | 5.0 | 2.8 |

| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea | 5.4 | 4.9 |

**Serious/Other Adverse Effects:**
- **Common (1–10%):** Drug hypersensitivity.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Venous thrombosis/thromboembolism.
- **Post-marketing Reports:** Depression, suicidal ideation, and suicide have been reported (causal relationship not established).
- **Other:** Seizures, hepatitis, liver function test elevations, and acute renal failure have been reported rarely.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **NMDA Antagonists:** Avoid concomitant use with amantadine, ketamine, or dextromethorphan. There is a specific risk of **pharmacotoxic psychosis** when combined with amantadine.
- **CNS-Active Drugs:** Memantine may potentiate the effects of L-dopa, dopaminergic agonists (e.g., bromocriptine), and anticholinergics.
- **Barbiturates/Neuroleptics:** Effects may be modified; dose adjustments may be necessary.
- **Warfarin:** Isolated cases of increased INR have been reported; close monitoring of prothrombin time or INR is advised.
- **Renal Cationic Transport System:** Drugs like cimetidine, ranitidine, procainamide, quinidine, and nicotine may interact with memantine, potentially increasing plasma levels.
- **Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ):** Memantine may reduce HCTZ bioavailability by approximately 20%, though this is not considered clinically significant.
- **Hepatic Metabolism:** Low potential for CYP450-mediated interactions.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. Animal studies showed no evidence of teratogenicity at clinically relevant doses, but reduced neonatal body weight was noted in rats.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use is **contraindicated** in nursing women. It is unknown if memantine is excreted in human milk, but toxicity potential exists.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were noted in rat studies at systemic exposures 2–4 times the clinical dose.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is not recommended; safety and efficacy data are not available.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data provided, though trials were conducted in an elderly population (mean age 74–76).
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild (CrCl 50–80 mL/min): No dose adjustment required.
    - Moderate (CrCl 30–49 mL/min): 10 mg per day. May increase to 20 mg/day after 7 days if well tolerated.
    - Severe (CrCl 5–29 mL/min): 10 mg per day.
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A & B): No dose adjustment required.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh C): **Not recommended** due to lack of data.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Memantine is a voltage-dependent, moderate-affinity, uncompetitive NMDA receptor antagonist. It blocks the effects of pathologically elevated tonic levels of glutamate that may lead to neuronal dysfunction, while allowing for normal physiological NMDA receptor activation required for learning and memory.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 100% absolute bioavailability; Tmax 3–8 hours. Food does not affect the extent of absorption.
    - **Distribution:** Vd ≈ 10 L/kg; protein binding 42–54%. Crosses the blood-brain barrier (CSF/serum ratio of 0.52).
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal (approx. 10% phase II); not metabolised by major CYP450 isoenzymes.
    - **Elimination:** Predominantly renal (60–80% unchanged); terminal half-life is 60–100 hours. Clearance is reduced in alkaline urine.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **6-Month Study:** A multicenter, double-blind study of 252 patients (MMSE 3–14) showed memantine (10 mg BID) was superior to placebo in cognitive (SIB), functional (ADCS-ADLsev), and global (CIBIC-plus) domains.
- **3-Month Study:** A study of 79 nursing home residents with AD or vascular dementia (MMSE < 10) demonstrated significant improvement in cognitive and functional scores compared to placebo at a dose of 10 mg daily.
