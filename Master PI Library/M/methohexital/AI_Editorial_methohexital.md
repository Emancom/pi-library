# Editorial Product Information: Methohexital Sodium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of methohexital sodium for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory guidance into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines mandated for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for definitive prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methohexital sodium (an ultrashort-acting barbiturate).
- **Chemical Class:** Barbiturate (methylated oxybarbiturate).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Brevital®** Sodium [US].
- **ATC Code:** N01AF03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Lyophilised powder for injection (crystalline) in 500 mg multi-dose vials [US].
    - Contains 6% anhydrous sodium carbonate as a buffer [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** 
    - **Intravenous (IV):** Primarily for adults.
    - **Intramuscular (IM):** For pediatric patients only [US].
    - **Rectal:** For pediatric patients only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Par Health USA, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Adult Indications [US]:**
- Induction of anesthesia prior to the administration of other general anesthetic agents.
- Induction of anesthesia and as an adjunct to subpotent inhalational anesthetics (e.g., nitrous oxide) for short surgical procedures.
- Maintenance of anesthesia in combination with other parenteral agents (typically narcotic analgesics) for longer surgical procedures.
- Intravenous anesthesia for short diagnostic, surgical, or therapeutic procedures involving minimal painful stimuli.
- Induction of a hypnotic state.

**Pediatric Indications (>1 month of age) [US]:**
- Rectal or intramuscular induction of anesthesia prior to other agents.
- Rectal or intramuscular induction/adjunct for short surgical procedures.
- Rectal or intramuscular anesthesia for short procedures with minimal painful stimuli.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Dosage must be strictly individualised. Use must be restricted to clinicians familiar with the drug's potency and pharmacokinetics compared to other barbiturates [US].

### Preparation and Dilution [US]
Only use **bacteriostatic-free** diluents. **Incompatible with Lactated Ringer’s Injection.**
- **1% Solution (10 mg/mL):** Preferred for intermittent IV and rectal use. Reconstitute with Sterile Water, D5W, or 0.9% NaCl.
- **0.2% Solution (2 mg/mL):** Prepared for continuous IV infusion. Use 5% Dextrose or 0.9% NaCl (to avoid extreme hypotonicity of Sterile Water).
- **5% Solution (50 mg/mL):** Prepared for IM use only.

### Adult Dosing (IV) [US]
- **Induction:** 1 to 1.5 mg/kg. Rate of administration is approximately 1 mL (1% solution) every 5 seconds. Duration of anesthesia is typically 5 to 7 minutes.
- **Maintenance:** Intermittent injections of 20 to 40 mg (2–4 mL of 1% solution) every 4 to 7 minutes, or continuous drip at ~3 mL/minute of a 0.2% solution.

### Pediatric Dosing (>1 month) [US]
- **Intramuscular:** 6.6 to 10 mg/kg using a 5% concentration.
- **Rectal:** 25 mg/kg using a 1% concentration.

## 4. Contraindications
- Patients in whom general anesthesia is contraindicated [US].
- Latent or manifest porphyria [US].
- Known hypersensitivity to barbiturates [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
**Brevital** should be used only in settings equipped for continuous respiratory and cardiac monitoring. Immediate availability of resuscitation drugs and age-/size-appropriate equipment for ventilation and intubation must be assured. Personnel must be skilled in airway management.

### General Warnings
- **Respiratory Risk:** Laryngospasm and apnea are common during induction. Apnea duration may be longer than with other barbiturates [US].
- **Cardiovascular Stability:** Use with extreme caution in patients with severe cardiovascular instability, shock, or severe hepatic dysfunction [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** May elicit seizures in patients with a history of convulsive activity or partial seizure disorders [US].
- **Intra-arterial Injection:** Danger of thrombosis, necrosis, and gangrene. If fiery burning is reported at the injection site, stop the injection immediately [US].
- **Pediatric Neurotoxicity:** Evidence from animal studies suggests that exposure longer than 3 hours to NMDA antagonists or GABA-potentiating drugs can lead to widespread neuronal apoptosis and long-term cognitive deficits in the developing brain [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Continuous monitoring of pulse oximetry and cardiac function is mandatory during administration [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally extensions of the drug's pharmacological actions.

| System | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiovascular** | Circulatory depression, hypotension, tachycardia, peripheral vascular collapse, thrombophlebitis. |
| **Respiratory** | Respiratory depression, apnea, laryngospasm, bronchospasm, hiccups, dyspnea. |
| **Neurological** | Skeletal muscle hyperactivity (twitching), seizures, nerve injury near injection site. |
| **Psychiatric** | Emergence delirium, restlessness, anxiety. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, emesis, abdominal pain, abnormal liver function tests. |
| **Allergic** | Erythema, pruritus, urticaria; anaphylaxis (rare). |
| **Other** | Pain at injection site, salivation, headache, rhinitis. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzyme Induction:** Prior chronic use of barbiturates or phenytoin may reduce the effectiveness of **methohexital** [US].
- **Metabolic Interference:** Barbiturates may alter the metabolism of phenytoin, halothane, anticoagulants, corticosteroids, and ethyl alcohol [US].
- **Additive Effects:** CNS-depressant effects are additive with other CNS depressants, including alcohol and propylene glycol [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** No adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal data at 4–7x human doses showed no harm to the fetus, but primate studies indicate neurotoxicity risk during the third trimester [US]. It readily traverses the placenta [US].
- **Labor and Delivery:** Has been used in cesarean sections [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Use with caution; safety in nursing women has not been established [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Pediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in infants under 1 month of age have not been established. Safety for IV use in pediatrics is not established in well-controlled studies [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Start at the low end of the dosing range. Monitor for decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function and concomitant disease [US].
- **Hepatic/Renal Impairment:** Use with extreme caution due to the liver's role in demethylation and oxidation, and the kidney's role in excretion [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** GABA-A receptor potentiator (barbiturate).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Rapid uptake by the brain (within 30 seconds of IV administration). It provides little analgesia; use in the presence of pain may cause excitation [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapid redistribution determines the duration of a single dose. Does not concentrate in fat depots as much as other barbiturates [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via demethylation and oxidation [US].
    - **Elimination:** Renal via glomerular filtration. Absolute bioavailability of rectal administration is 17% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store between 20°C and 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Chemically stable at room temperature for 24 hours [US].
