# Editorial Product Information: Measles, Mumps, Rubella, and Varicella Vaccine (Live, Attenuated)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or administration in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Measles virus (Schwarz strain), mumps virus (RIT 4385 strain), rubella virus (Wistar RA 27/3 strain), and varicella-zoster virus (OKA strain).
- **Chemical Class:** Live attenuated viral vaccine.
- **Brand Name:** **Priorix Tetra** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J07BD54.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for reconstitution with a sterile diluent (Water for Injection). Each 0.5 mL reconstituted dose contains:
    - Measles virus: ≥ 10³·⁰ CCID₅₀
    - Mumps virus: ≥ 10⁴·⁴ CCID₅₀
    - Rubella virus: ≥ 10³·⁰ CCID₅₀
    - Varicella-zoster virus: ≥ 10³·³ PFU
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) or Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Active Immunisation:** Indicated for the prevention of measles, mumps, rubella, and varicella in individuals from 9 months of age [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Primary Schedule:** Infants and children (9 months to 12 years) should receive two 0.5 mL doses [Australian].
- **Dosing Interval:** The preferred interval is between 6 weeks and 3 months. The interval must never be less than 4 weeks [Australian].
- **Alternative Schedules:**
    - A single dose of this combination vaccine may be administered to children who have previously received a single dose of another MMR vaccine (with or without a varicella vaccine).
    - It may also be used as the first dose, followed by a subsequent dose of another MMR vaccine.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Site:** For children <12 months, the anterolateral thigh is recommended. For children ≥12 months, the deltoid region is preferred [Australian].
    - **Reconstitution:** Add the entire contents of the diluent syringe to the vial. The vaccine must be well shaken and used promptly.
    - **Appearance:** The reconstituted vaccine may range from clear peach to fuchsia pink.
    - **Contraindicated Routes:** Under no circumstances should the vaccine be administered intravascularly or intradermally.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Vaccination is strictly contraindicated during pregnancy.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to neomycin or any vaccine component. A history of contact dermatitis to neomycin is not a contraindication.
- **Previous Reaction:** History of hypersensitivity following prior administration of measles, mumps, rubella, or varicella vaccines.
- **Immunodeficiency:** Severe primary or acquired humoral or cellular immunodeficiency (e.g., patients receiving high-dose steroids).
- **Immunosuppression:** Patients currently on or recently receiving immunosuppressive therapy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Illness:** Administration should be delayed in patients with acute severe febrile illness (Temp >38.5°C). Minor infections (e.g., common cold) do not require deferral.
- **Febrile Convulsions:** There is an increased risk of fever and febrile convulsions 5 to 12 days following the first dose of this combination vaccine compared to separate MMR and varicella injections. No increased risk is noted with the second dose. Caution is advised in patients with a personal or family history of convulsions [Australian].
- **Egg Allergy:** The measles and mumps components are produced in chick embryo cell cultures and may contain trace egg protein. Patients with a history of anaphylactic or immediate reactions to egg ingestion should be vaccinated with extreme caution; adequate treatment for anaphylaxis must be available.
- **Transmission:** Transmission of the Oka varicella vaccine virus to susceptible, seronegative contacts can occur at a very low rate, primarily if the vaccinee develops a rash. Vaccinated individuals should avoid contact with immunocompromised persons susceptible to varicella for up to 6 weeks [Australian].
- **Syncope:** Fainting may occur as a psychogenic response to injection; procedures should be in place to prevent injury.
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Risk-benefit should be evaluated in patients who experienced worsening or recurrence of thrombocytopenia after the first dose of an MMR vaccine.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects generally correspond to those observed with monovalent or separate combination vaccines.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Pain and redness at the injection site; fever (rectal ≥38°C–≤39.5°C; axillary/oral ≥37.5°C–≤39°C) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Swelling at the injection site; fever (rectal >39.5°C; axillary/oral >39°C); irritability; rash |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Upper respiratory tract infection; lymphadenopathy; anorexia; abnormal crying; nervousness; insomnia; somnolence; rhinitis; parotid gland enlargement; diarrhoea; vomiting; lethargy; malaise; fatigue |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Otitis media; febrile convulsions; cough; bronchitis |

- **Post-marketing Experience (Rare/Serious):** Meningitis, herpes zoster, measles-like/mumps-like syndrome (including orchitis and parotitis), thrombocytopenic purpura, encephalitis, cerebrovascular accident, Guillain-Barré syndrome, transverse myelitis, erythema multiforme, and arthralgia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Simultaneous Administration:** Can be given with hexavalent (DTPa-HBV-IPV/Hib), DTPa, Hib, IPV, HBV, HAV, MenB, MenC, MenACWY, and PCV10 vaccines, provided separate injection sites are used.
- **Live Vaccines:** If not given simultaneously, a minimum interval of 1 month is required between live attenuated vaccines.
- **Blood Products:** Vaccination should be delayed for at least 3 months following human gammaglobulin or blood transfusion to avoid vaccine failure from passive antibodies.
- **Tuberculin Testing:** Should be performed before or simultaneously with vaccination, as MMR vaccines may temporarily depress tuberculin skin sensitivity for up to 6 weeks.
- **Salicylates:** Avoid use for 6 weeks post-vaccination due to the risk of Reye’s Syndrome, which has been reported during natural varicella infection [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy [Australian Category B2]:** Contraindicated. Pregnancy must be avoided for one month following vaccination. If a woman intends to become pregnant, she should be advised to delay until the one-month post-vaccination window has passed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Human data are limited. Vaccination may be considered where the clinical benefit outweighs the potential risk.
- **Fertility:** Evaluation of potential impairment of fertility has not been conducted [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated from 9 months to 12 years of age.
- **Elderly Use:** No data available [Australian].
- **Adolescents and Adults:** No clinical data available on safety, immunogenicity, or efficacy in these populations [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Induces the production of protective antibodies against measles, mumps, rubella, and varicella viruses.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not applicable to vaccines.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Seroconversion rates 42 days post-dose 2 in children (11–23 months) are reported as:
    - Measles: 99.1%
    - Mumps: 98.8%–99.4%
    - Rubella: 99.9%
    - Varicella: 99.2%–99.8% [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** In an active-controlled trial (N=2279), 2 doses of the combination vaccine demonstrated 94.9% efficacy against confirmed varicella of any severity and 99.5% efficacy against moderate or severe varicella over a 35-month follow-up. 
- **Comparative Data:** Seroconversion rates were comparable to those achieved with separate injections of MMR and varicella vaccines. Long-term data (3 years) showed that >97% of vaccinees remained seropositive for all four antigens.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Must be stored in a refrigerator between 2°C and 8°C.
- **Light:** Store in the original packaging to protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Post-reconstitution:** Should be injected promptly. It may be stored between 2°C and 8°C for up to 8 hours if necessary.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
