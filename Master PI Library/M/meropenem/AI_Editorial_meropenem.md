# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing or dispensing. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for full legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - Acute Crisis: A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Dosing should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less frequent than every 6 hours [US].
    - Maximum Dose: The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Monitoring: Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40 to 50 µmol/L.
    - Dose Adjustments: Should be guided by trough concentrations; downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be performed as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of injection. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Metabolite Accumulation):** While the injection bypasses the gut, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not formally categorised by letter in the current PI, but listed as "should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine milk concentrations. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 (Dosage and Administration). Clinical studies included paediatric patients with inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; however, dose selection should generally be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine over 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** Conducted in healthy volunteers and ESRD patients. In ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg 3x weekly, trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** In ESRD patients, doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg resulted in dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels at 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Efficacy Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion.
