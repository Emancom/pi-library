# Editorial Product Information: Midodrine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of midodrine hydrochloride for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory information. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Midodrine hydrochloride (a peripheral alpha-1 adrenergic agonist).
- **Brand Names:** **Vasodrine** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Tablets: 2.5 mg and 5 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Southern XP IP Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Symptomatic Orthostatic Hypotension:** Treatment of severe symptomatic orthostatic hypotension (OH) due to autonomic dysfunction in adults, specifically when exacerbating factors have been addressed and other treatment modalities remain inadequate [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** 2.5 mg three times daily.
- **Titration:** Increments of 2.5 mg weekly based on supine and standing blood pressure response.
- **Maintenance Dose:** Usually 10 mg three times daily; however, twice-daily dosing may be appropriate for some patients.
- **Maximum Recommended Dose:** 30 mg daily [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Timing:** To prevent supine hypertension, the last daily dose must be taken at least 4 hours before bedtime. Doses should not be spread evenly over the day; rather, they should be timed to coincide with periods when the patient is upright and active.
- **Food:** May be taken with or without food [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a dose is missed, the next dose should be taken at the scheduled time. Patients should not double the dose to compensate for a missed one due to the risk of supine hypertension [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to midodrine or any tablet excipients.
- **Cardiovascular:** Severe organic heart disease (e.g., bradycardia, myocardial infarction, congestive heart failure, conduction disturbances, or aortic aneurysm); hypertension.
- **Vascular:** Obliterative blood vessel disease, cerebrovascular occlusions, and vessel spasms.
- **Renal:** Acute kidney disease; acute nephritis; severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance < 30 mL/min).
- **Urinary/Prostate:** Serious prostate disorders, prostatic hypertrophy with increased residual urine volume, and urinary retention.
- **Other:** Proliferative diabetic retinopathy; phaeochromocytoma; hyperthyroidism or thyrotoxicosis; narrow-angle glaucoma; vasovagal hypotension [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Supine Hypertension:** Midodrine can cause marked elevation of supine blood pressure. It should only be used in patients whose quality of life is considerably impaired despite non-pharmacological treatment. Treatment must be stopped if supine hypertension occurs and is not corrected by dose reduction.
- **Monitoring:** Regular monitoring of supine and standing blood pressure is mandatory. Patients should report symptoms of supine hypertension (e.g., chest pain, palpitations, headache, blurred vision) immediately.
- **Bradycardia:** Midodrine may cause a slowing of the heart rate due to vagal reflex. Caution is required in patients taking other agents that reduce heart rate (e.g., cardiac glycosides).
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** A small but significant increase in QT interval has been observed in some populations. Use with caution in patients with congenital QT prolongation, bradycardia, or those taking other QT-prolonging medications.
- **Autonomic Nervous System Disturbances:** In severe autonomic failure, midodrine may paradoxically lead to a further reduction in standing blood pressure; if this occurs, therapy should be discontinued.
- **Other Precautions:** Caution is advised in patients with atherosclerotic disease (especially intestinal angina or leg claudication), diabetes mellitus (risk of supine hypertension), and prostate disorders (risk of urinary retention) [Australian].

### Serious and Other Notable Adverse Effects
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Tachycardia, palpitations, arrhythmias, chest pain, cerebrovascular accident, erythema multiforme, and raised liver enzymes.
- **Frequency Not Known:** Respiratory failure, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis, and peripheral ischemia [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Vasopressors/Sympathomimetics:** Avoid concomitant use with agents such as decongestants, appetite suppressants, MAO-inhibitors, and tricyclic antidepressants, as they may cause a pronounced increase in blood pressure.
- **Alpha-adrenergic Antagonists:** Effects of midodrine are blocked by prazosin and phentolamine.
- **Ergot Alkaloids:** Combined administration is not recommended.
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** Not recommended; midodrine may potentiate heart rate reduction and lead to heart block.
- **Corticosteroids:** Midodrine may enhance the hypertensive effects and increase the risk of glaucoma/intraocular pressure when used with fludrocortisone or other steroids [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2D6:** Midodrine's active metabolite (desglymidodrine) is primarily metabolised by CYP2D6. Inhibitors like quinidine may enhance and prolong activity.
- **Transporters:** Midodrine absorption is facilitated by PEPT1; substrate competition may occur with $\beta$-lactam antibiotics or antivirals.
- **Renal Tubular Secretion:** There is potential for interaction with base-secreting systems (e.g., metformin, cimetidine, ranitidine) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Animal studies show reproductive toxicity at maternally toxic doses (embryo resorption, reduced foetal weight). Midodrine is not recommended during pregnancy or for women of childbearing potential not using contraception.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if midodrine is excreted in human milk. Use is not recommended during breastfeeding as a risk to the infant cannot be excluded.
- **Fertility:** Animal data are insufficient to assess impact on fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established; no data available.
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data. Cautious dose titration is recommended; starting at 2.5 mg TID and increasing at 2–3 month intervals may reduce adverse reactions.
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in acute kidney disease or severe renal impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min). Regular monitoring of renal function is advised for long-term use.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in this population. Evaluation of hepatic parameters is recommended before and during treatment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Midodrine is a prodrug of desglymidodrine, a selective peripheral alpha-1 adrenergic agonist. It increases peripheral arterial resistance and induces vasoconstriction of the venous system, thereby reducing venous pooling and increasing arterial blood pressure.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The active (-) enantiomer of desglymidodrine is responsible for the pressor effect. It has no beta-adrenergic effects.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (facilitated by PEPT1). Peak plasma concentration of midodrine at 30 minutes; desglymidodrine peaks at 1 hour.
    - **Distribution:** Poor diffusion across the blood-brain barrier; less than 30% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Hydrolysed to desglymidodrine in the intestines and plasma.
    - **Elimination:** >90% excreted in urine within 24 hours. Half-life is ~30 minutes for midodrine and ~3 hours for desglymidodrine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Phase III (Low 1997):** A randomised, double-blind study (n=171) demonstrated that 10 mg TID significantly improved standing systolic blood pressure (mean increase 21.8 mmHg) and global orthostatic symptom scores compared to placebo.
- **Post-marketing Study (Smith 2016):** A tilt-table study (n=20) showed that midodrine significantly increased the time to onset of syncopal or near-syncopal symptoms compared to placebo (1626.6 s vs 1105.6 s, p=0.0131) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** 27 months [Australian].
