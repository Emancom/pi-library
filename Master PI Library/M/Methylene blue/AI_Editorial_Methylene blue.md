# Editorial Product Information: Methylene Blue

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical data into a structured format for ease of reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methylene blue (also known as methylthioninium chloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Thiazide dye.
- **Brand Name(s):** Methylene Blue Injection (Phebra) [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** V03AB17.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection or oral use; 50 mg/5 mL (1% w/v) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (preferred for acute settings), Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Phebra Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Treatment of drug-induced methaemoglobinaemia [Australian].
- Treatment of idiopathic methaemoglobinaemia (where structural haemoglobin abnormalities are absent) [Australian].
- Use as a bacteriological stain [Australian].
- Diagnostic dye for procedures such as fistula detection [Australian].
- Delineation of specific body tissues during surgical procedures [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Children):** 1 mg to 2 mg per kg of lean body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer intravenously as a 1% solution over several minutes. 
- **Repeat Dosing:** A second dose may be administered after one hour if clinical response is inadequate.
- **Maximum Dose:** A cumulative maximum of 7 mg/kg is recommended [Australian].
- **Tissue Delineation (e.g., Parathyroid):** 5 mg/kg diluted in 500 mL of Glucose 5%, infused over one hour [Australian].
- **Oral Administration:** 5–10 mL of the 1% solution diluted in 100–200 mL of water for injections (high volume used to minimize gastric irritation and dysuria) [Australian].
- **Important Preparation Note:** Do **not** dilute with Sodium Chloride 0.9% (saline), as chloride ions reduce solubility and may cause precipitation [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to methylene blue or other thiazide dyes [Australian].
- Patients with severe renal impairment [Australian].
- Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency (risk of severe haemolysis) [Australian].
- Methaemoglobinaemia caused by chlorate poisoning [Australian].
- Methaemoglobinaemia occurring during the treatment of cyanide poisoning [Australian].
- **Route Contraindications:** Intrathecal and subcutaneous administration are strictly contraindicated due to risks of neural damage and necrotic abscesses, respectively [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Methylene blue is a potent monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI). It can cause fatal serotonin toxicity when combined with serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, etc.). Avoid concomitant use unless essential; if required, use the lowest dose and monitor for cognitive dysfunction, hyperpyrexia, and hyperreflexia [Australian].
- **Photosensitivity:** Patients may experience cutaneous photosensitivity when exposed to strong light sources (e.g., operating theatre lights, phototherapy, pulse oximeters). Protective measures are advised [Australian].
- **Haematological Monitoring:** Long-term use may cause marked anaemia due to erythrocyte destruction. Monitor haemoglobin and reticulocyte counts [Australian].
- **Skin/Fluid Discolouration:** Imparts a blue-green colour to urine and faeces, and a blue tint to the skin. This may interfere with the clinical diagnosis of cyanosis [Australian].
- **Extravasation:** Must be injected slowly to prevent high local concentrations; extravasation can lead to necrotic abscesses [Australian].
- **Dapsone-induced Methaemoglobinaemia:** Use caution; do not exceed a cumulative dose of 4 mg/kg as it may exacerbate haemolytic anaemia [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following reactions have been identified following intravenous administration:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, blue-coloured saliva/faeces. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension (potentially fatal), arrhythmia, tachycardia, chest pain. |
| **Nervous System** | Headache, dizziness, paraesthesia, tremor, anxiety, confusion, agitation, serotonin syndrome. |
| **Dermal** | Skin discolouration (blue), rash (blue macules), urticaria, photosensitivity, profuse sweating. |
| **Blood/Lymphatic** | Haemolysis (especially in G6PD deficiency), hyperbilirubinaemia (reported in infants), methaemoglobinaemia (with high doses). |
| **Respiratory** | Dyspnoea, tachypnoea, hypoxia. |
| **Injection Site** | Pain, thrombophlebitis, necrosis (if extravasated). |
| **Renal** | Blue-coloured urine, dysuria (primarily with oral use). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Serotonergic Agents:** SSRIs (e.g., fluoxetine, sertraline), SNRIs (e.g., venlafaxine), and certain opioids (tramadol, pethidine) increase the risk of Serotonin Syndrome.
- **CYP450 Enzymes:** In vitro data suggests methylene blue is a potential inhibitor of CYP1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, and 3A4/5. Clinical significance is likely minimal for single emergency doses [Australian].
- **Transporters:** Inhibits OCT2, MATE1, and MATE2-K. May increase exposure to drugs like metformin, cimetidine, and acyclovir [Australian].
- **Laboratory Interference:** 
    - **Pulse Oximetry:** May cause false low oxygen saturation readings. 
    - **Bispectral Index (BIS):** May interfere with readings.
    - **Phenolsulfonphthalein test:** May cause false positives [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Associated with fetal intestinal atresia. It should not be administered to pregnant women [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if methylene blue is excreted in human milk. Safety in newborns is not established; breastfeeding should be discontinued prior to administration [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In vitro studies show reduced human sperm motility and inhibition of mouse embryo growth. In vivo human data is unavailable [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended in infants under 4 months of age. Extreme caution is required for infants under 3 months due to lower levels of NADPH-methaemoglobin reductase, making them highly susceptible to drug-induced methaemoglobinaemia [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in provided sources [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. Use with caution in mild-to-moderate renal impairment as the drug is primarily excreted renally [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** In methaemoglobinaemia, methylene blue activates a dormant reductase system. It is reduced to leucomethylene blue, which then reduces methaemoglobin back to functional haemoglobin. At high doses, methylene blue can paradoxically induce methaemoglobinaemia by oxidising haemoglobin [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Possesses weak antiseptic properties and inhibits amine oxidase in tissues [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly reduced in tissues to leucomethylene blue.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 75% of an oral dose is excreted via urine (mostly as leucomethylene blue), with a small portion excreted via bile [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Protect from light [Australian].
- Use immediately following dilution [Australian].
