# Editorial Product Information: Mosunetuzumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, incorporating regulatory-approved data into a single clinically relevant resource.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only United States (FDA) documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mosunetuzumab-axgb
- **Chemical Class:** Bispecific CD20-directed CD3 T-cell engager; humanised monoclonal IgG1-isotype antibody.
- **Brand Name(s):** **LUNSUMIO VELO** [US]
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Subcutaneous injection: 5 mg/0.5 mL solution in a single-dose vial [US].
    - Subcutaneous injection: 45 mg/mL solution in a single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Genentech, Inc. (Member of the Roche Group) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Follicular Lymphoma (FL):** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma who have received two or more prior lines of systemic therapy. [US-approved under accelerated approval based on response rate; continued approval may be contingent upon verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment is administered in 21-day cycles. **LUNSUMIO VELO** utilizes a step-up dosing schedule during Cycle 1 to mitigate the risk of Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS).

### Step-Up Dosing Schedule (Cycle 1)
- **Cycle 1, Day 1:** 5 mg
- **Cycle 1, Day 8:** 45 mg
- **Cycle 1, Day 15:** 45 mg

### Maintenance Dosing (Cycle 2 and onwards)
- **Day 1 of each cycle:** 45 mg

### Duration of Treatment
- Patients achieving a **complete response (CR)** should complete 8 cycles and then cease treatment.
- Patients with a **partial response (PR)** or **stable disease (SD)** after 8 cycles should continue for an additional 9 cycles (total of 17 cycles), unless disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs.

### Administration Instructions
- **Route:** Subcutaneous injection into the abdomen (preferred) or thigh.
- **Formulation Safety:** **LUNSUMIO VELO** (subcutaneous) and **LUNSUMIO** (intravenous) have different dosing and administration instructions. Do not substitute one for the other [US].
- **Preparation:** Vials are ready-to-use and do not require dilution. Use aseptic technique.
- **Premedication:** 
    - **Cycle 1 (All patients):** Administer a corticosteroid (Dexamethasone 20mg or Methylprednisolone 80mg), antihistamine (Diphenhydramine 50-100mg), and antipyretic (Acetaminophen 500-1000mg) prior to each dose.
    - **Cycle 2+:** Required only for patients who experienced CRS with the previous dose.

### Missed Dose/Dose Delay
Restarting therapy depends on the duration of the delay and the last dose administered. If a dose is delayed by more than 2 to 6 weeks (depending on the cycle), the step-up schedule (starting at 5 mg) may need to be repeated [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS) [US]
Severe or life-threatening CRS can occur. In clinical trials, CRS occurred in 30% of patients. Clinical signs include fever, hypoxia, hypotension, and chills. 
- **Management:** Ensure patients are well-hydrated. Withhold treatment for Grade 1–3 CRS until resolution. Permanently discontinue for Grade 4 or recurrent Grade 3 CRS.

### Neurologic Toxicity
Immune Effector Cell-Associated Neurotoxicity Syndrome (ICANS) and other neurologic toxicities (e.g., headache, dizziness, insomnia, confusional state) have been reported. 
- **Monitoring:** Monitor for signs of impaired consciousness or cognitive changes. 
- **Advice:** Patients should not drive or operate heavy machinery if experiencing symptoms that impair consciousness.

### Infections
Serious or fatal infections (e.g., pneumonia, sepsis, COVID-19) occurred in 17% of patients. Do not initiate treatment in the presence of an active infection. Prophylactic antimicrobials should be considered according to institutional guidelines.

### Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis (HLH)
HLH, a life-threatening hyperinflammatory syndrome, has been reported (0.5% of the broader trial population). Six of seven reported cases were fatal [US]. Monitor for fever, cytopenias, and hepatosplenomegaly.

### Cytopenias
Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia (26%), lymphopenia (69%), and thrombocytopenia (6%) are common. Monitor complete blood counts (CBC) regularly throughout treatment.

### Tumor Flare
Worsening of tumor-related symptoms (e.g., localized pain, swelling, pleural effusion) may occur. Patients with bulky tumors near vital organs (e.g., airways) require close monitoring for compression effects.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions (≥20%)
- Injection site reactions (69%)
- Fatigue (39%)
- Rash (35%)
- Cytokine Release Syndrome (30%)
- COVID-19 infection (27%)
- Musculoskeletal pain (20%)
- Diarrhea (20%)

### Laboratory Abnormalities (Grade 3 or 4, ≥15%)
- Decreased lymphocyte count (69%)
- Decreased neutrophil count (26%)
- Increased uric acid (28%)

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Infections:** 17% of patients experienced serious infections.
- **Fatalities:** Fatal adverse reactions occurred in 4.3% of patients, primarily due to COVID-19 and HLH [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Substrates:** The release of cytokines during treatment can suppress CYP450 enzyme activity, potentially increasing the exposure of concomitant CYP450 substrates. This risk is highest during Cycle 1 (specifically after the 45 mg doses) and during CRS episodes. Monitor for toxicity in patients taking drugs with a narrow therapeutic index that are CYP450 substrates [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in the US PI, but identified as having a risk of fetal harm based on mechanism of action. 
    - **Risks:** May cause B-cell lymphocytopenia in the fetus.
    - **Requirements:** Verify pregnancy status before initiation. Effective contraception is required during treatment and for **3 months** after the final dose [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Advise women not to breastfeed during treatment and for **3 months** after the final dose, as human IgG is known to be excreted in breast milk and may cause B-cell depletion in the infant.
- **Fertility:** No dedicated human studies; no adverse effects on reproductive organs were observed in a 26-week repeat-dose study in monkeys [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** In clinical trials, 51% of patients were ≥65 years. No significant differences in safety or effectiveness were observed compared to younger patients, though data for those ≥75 are limited [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically significant PK differences in mild to moderate impairment (CrCL 30–89 mL/min). Data are unavailable for severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No clinically significant PK differences in mild hepatic impairment. Data are unavailable for moderate to severe hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Mosunetuzumab-axgb is a bispecific antibody that cross-links CD20 (on B-cells) and CD3 (on T-cells). This interaction leads to T-cell activation, the release of proinflammatory cytokines, and the subsequent lysis of CD20-expressing B-cells.

### Pharmacodynamics
- **B-cell Depletion:** Peripheral B-cell counts decreased to undetectable levels in 94% of patients by Cycle 2 and remained depleted through Cycle 8.
- **Hypogammaglobulinemia:** 39% of patients with normal baseline IgG experienced a decrease to <500 mg/dL [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Bioavailability is approximately 89.8% following subcutaneous administration.
- **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is approximately 5.5 L.
- **Elimination:** Geometric mean half-life is 17 days. Clearance decreases from 1.1 L/day at baseline to 0.58 L/day at steady state [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
### Study GO29781 [US]
- **Design:** Open-label, multicenter, multi-cohort study in patients with relapsed or refractory FL after 2+ lines of therapy (N=94).
- **Key Outcomes:**
    - **Objective Response Rate (ORR):** 75% (95% CI: 64, 83).
    - **Complete Response (CR) Rate:** 59% (95% CI: 48, 69).
    - **Median Duration of Response (DOR):** 22.4 months.
    - **Median Time to First Response:** 2.8 months.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vial Storage:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) in the original carton to protect from light. Do not freeze or shake.
- **Prepared Syringe:** 
    - Use immediately if possible.
    - If not used immediately, store refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 32 hours (protected from light) or at room temperature (9°C to 25°C) for up to 8 hours (ambient light).
