# Editorial Product Information: Methylnaltrexone Bromide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of methylnaltrexone bromide for use by healthcare professionals. It aims to consolidate clinical data, dosing protocols, and safety information into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methylnaltrexone bromide.
- **Chemical Class:** Peripherally acting mu-opioid receptor antagonist (PAMORA); quaternary ammonium derivative of naltrexone.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Relistor** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Solution for injection:** 12 mg methylnaltrexone bromide in 0.6 mL (20 mg/mL concentration) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Opioid-Induced Constipation (OIC):** Treatment of OIC in patients with advanced illness receiving palliative care when the response to conventional laxative therapy has been insufficient [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Methylnaltrexone is administered as a single subcutaneous dose, typically on alternate days. Dosing intervals may be extended based on clinical need. If no bowel movement occurs within 24 hours of a dose, an additional dose may be administered.

### Weight-Based Dosing [Australian]
The recommended dose is determined by patient weight as follows:
- **38 kg to <62 kg:** 8 mg (0.4 mL).
- **62 kg to 114 kg:** 12 mg (0.6 mL).
- **Outside these ranges (<38 kg or >114 kg):** 0.15 mg/kg (calculated volume = weight in kg × 0.0075, rounded to the nearest 0.1 mL).

### Administration Instructions
- **Sites:** Inject into the upper arm, abdomen, or thigh.
- **Rotation:** Rotate injection sites; avoid areas that are tender, bruised, scarred, or hard.
- **Setting:** Patients should remain near toileting facilities due to the potential for rapid onset of action (often within 30 minutes).
- **Concomitant Therapy:** In clinical trials, the drug was used alongside existing laxative regimens.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Mechanical Obstruction:** Known or suspected mechanical gastrointestinal (GI) obstruction or acute surgical abdomen [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to methylnaltrexone bromide or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal Perforation:** Cases of GI perforation have been reported, particularly in patients with advanced illness and conditions that compromise GI wall integrity (e.g., cancer, peptic ulcer, or use of bevacizumab, NSAIDs, and steroids). Use with extreme caution in patients with known or suspected GI lesions. 
- **Severe Diarrhea:** Advise patients to discontinue therapy and consult a physician if severe or persistent diarrhea develops.
- **Post-operative Ileus:** Not recommended for use in patients with post-operative ileus or following GI resection.
- **Unstudied Conditions:** Use with caution in patients with colostomies, peritoneal catheters, active diverticular disease, or faecal impaction, as these populations have not been studied.
- **Duration of Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established for use beyond 4 months in palliative care settings [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily gastrointestinal, consistent with the mechanism of action.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Abdominal pain (28.5%), Flatulence (13.3%), Nausea (11.5%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness (7.3%), Diarrhea (5.5%), Hyperhidrosis [Post-marketing] |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- **GI Perforation:** Reported in post-marketing surveillance in patients with compromised structural integrity of the GI tract.
- **Severe Abdominal Pain:** If pain is persistent and not relieved by laxation, patients should be evaluated for potential obstruction.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450:** Methylnaltrexone is minimally metabolised by CYP enzymes. It does not inhibit CYP1A2, 2A6, 2C9, 2C19, or 3A4. It is a weak inhibitor of CYP2D6, but clinical studies with dextromethorphan showed no significant interaction [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** As a peripheral mu-opioid antagonist, it does not interfere with the central analgesic effects of opioids.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **Australia:** Category B1. (Drugs taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the human fetus having been observed). 
    - Use only if clearly needed and the benefit outweighs potential risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Caution is advised. Animal studies (rats) show excretion in milk.
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in rat studies at doses resulting in 36 times the human exposure.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients under 18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required based on age. Safety profiles were similar between elderly and younger patients in clinical trials.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min):** Reduce the dose by one-half.
    - **ESRD on Dialysis:** Not recommended due to lack of data [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate (Child-Pugh A and B):** No dose adjustment required.
    - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended due to lack of data [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A selective, competitive antagonist of mu-opioid receptors. It is a quaternary amine with restricted ability to cross the blood-brain barrier, allowing it to reverse opioid-induced GI effects without affecting central analgesia.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** May induce rapid laxation. In clinical studies, approximately half of responders had a bowel movement within 30 minutes of dosing. It does not significantly affect QT/QTc intervals.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak concentration (Cmax) reached in ~0.5 hours. Bioavailability is ~82% following subcutaneous injection.
    - **Distribution:** Moderate tissue distribution (Vss ~1.1 L/kg); low plasma protein binding (~11%).
    - **Metabolism:** Minimally metabolised in humans. Primary metabolites include methyl-6-naltrexol isomers and methylnaltrexone sulfate.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted unchanged. Approximately 50% is recovered in urine and slightly less in faeces. Terminal half-life (t1/2) is ~8 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies (Study 301 and 302):** Two randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in palliative care patients with OIC.
- **Efficacy Results:** In Study 301, laxation within 4 hours occurred in 62% (0.15 mg/kg dose) and 58% (0.3 mg/kg dose) of patients compared to 14% for placebo (p < 0.0001). 
- **Durability:** Response rates remained consistent over 2 weeks of every-other-day dosing. Median time between doses in open-label extensions was 3.2 days.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Light:** Protect from light.
- **Handling:** Once drawn into a syringe, use as soon as possible. If not used immediately, it may be stored at room temperature for up to 6 hours.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
