# Editorial Product Information: Mirabegron

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinically focused reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical guide; it is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mirabegron (a selective beta-3 adrenoceptor agonist).
- **Brand Name:** **Betmiga** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G04BD12.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Prolonged-release film-coated tablets: 25 mg (oval, brown, debossed with "325") and 50 mg (oval, yellow, debossed with "355").
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Astellas Pharma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Overactive Bladder (OAB) Syndrome:** Symptomatic management of urinary urgency, increased micturition frequency, and/or urgency incontinence [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Adult Dosing (including Elderly):** The recommended starting dose is 25 mg once daily. Depending on individual patient response and tolerability, the dose may be titrated to a maximum of 50 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole with liquid. They must not be crushed, divided, or chewed, as this compromises the prolonged-release mechanism. Administration may occur with or without food [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specifically detailed in the source, but standard clinical practice suggests taking the dose as soon as remembered unless it is nearly time for the next dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to mirabegron or any tablet excipients.
- **Severe Uncontrolled Hypertension:** Defined as systolic blood pressure ≥180 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥110 mmHg [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Mirabegron can increase blood pressure (BP) and heart rate. BP should be measured at baseline and monitored periodically, particularly in patients with pre-existing hypertension. 
- **Moderate Hypertension:** Caution is advised in patients with Grade 2 hypertension (SBP 160–169 mmHg or DBP 100–109 mmHg) [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** While therapeutic doses do not show clinically relevant QT prolongation, caution is required in patients with a history of QT prolongation or those taking medications known to prolong the QT interval [Australian].
- **Urinary Retention:** Reported in patients with bladder outlet obstruction (BOO) and those taking concomitant antimuscarinic therapy for OAB. Use with caution in these populations.
- **Angioedema:** Cases involving the face, lips, tongue, or larynx have occurred, sometimes after the first dose. If the airway is threatened, discontinue immediately and initiate emergency care [Australian].
- **ANCA-associated Vasculitis:** Rare reports exist; discontinuation should be considered if diagnosed [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse reactions are mild to moderate in severity. 

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypertension, nasopharyngitis, urinary tract infection (UTI), headache, constipation, upper respiratory tract infection, tachycardia, arthralgia, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, fatigue. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Palpitations, increased BP, dyspepsia, gastritis, abdominal distension, sinusitis, rhinitis, pruritus, rash, urticaria, nephrolithiasis, bladder pain, vaginal infection. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Glaucoma, leukocytoclastic vasculitis, purpura, lip oedema. |
| **Post-marketing** | Atrial fibrillation, nausea, angioedema, urinary retention. |

*Note: Hypertension was the most common reason for discontinuation in clinical trials [Australian].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP2D6 Substrates:** Mirabegron is a moderate inhibitor of CYP2D6. It increases systemic exposure to drugs like metoprolol and desipramine. Appropriate monitoring and dose adjustment are required for narrow therapeutic index drugs such as thioridazine, flecainide, and propafenone [Australian].
- **Digoxin:** Mirabegron increases digoxin $C_{max}$ (29%) and AUC (27%). When initiating the combination, prescribe the lowest digoxin dose and monitor serum concentrations for titration [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Mirabegron slightly increases warfarin exposure (approx. 9%), though single-dose studies showed no effect on INR. Multi-dose effects have not been fully investigated [Australian].
- **Transporters:** Mirabegron inhibits P-gp at high concentrations; clinically relevant inhibition of OCT is not expected.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal studies showed some reproductive toxicity (fetal death, dilated aorta) at high exposures (22-fold human dose). Use during pregnancy is not recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mirabegron is excreted in the milk of rodents and is predicted to be present in human milk. It should not be administered during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No treatment-related effects were observed in animal studies at non-lethal doses. Human data is unavailable [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is required based on age [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - Mild/Moderate (eGFR 30–89 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$): No adjustment required.
    - Severe (eGFR 15–29 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$): Maximum dose 25 mg once daily.
    - End-Stage Renal Disease (eGFR <15 or dialysis): Not recommended [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
    - Mild (Child-Pugh A): No adjustment required.
    - Moderate (Child-Pugh B): Maximum dose 25 mg once daily.
    - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Mirabegron is a selective beta-3 adrenoceptor agonist. It stimulates beta-3 receptors in the bladder, leading to detrusor muscle relaxation during the storage phase, which increases bladder capacity without affecting voiding pressure [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Reaches peak plasma concentration ($C_{max}$) in 3–4 hours. Absolute bioavailability is 29% (at 25 mg) to 35% (at 50 mg). High-fat meals significantly reduce $C_{max}$ and AUC.
    - **Distribution:** Extensively distributed ($V_{ss} \approx 1670$ L); approximately 71% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised via multiple pathways including dealkylation, oxidation, glucuronidation, and amide hydrolysis. CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 play a role.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is approximately 50 hours. Excretion is via urine (55%) and faeces (34%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies (046, 047, 074):** Three 12-week, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 studies demonstrated that mirabegron (25 mg and 50 mg) significantly reduced the mean number of incontinence episodes and micturitions per 24 hours compared to placebo.
- **Long-term Efficacy:** A 1-year study (Study 049) confirmed that efficacy was sustained over a 52-week period [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be returned to a pharmacy for safe disposal.
