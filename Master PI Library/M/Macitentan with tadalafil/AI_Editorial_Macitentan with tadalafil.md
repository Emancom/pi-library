# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: **United States (FDA)**.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, dosage requirements, and safety information found in the official product labeling.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg or as clinically required.
    - **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are adequate.

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) levels (target: 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None listed in provided source [US].

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including **anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm**, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately and initiate medical treatment [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Precaution):** While this document concerns the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Monitor **INR** in patients concurrently receiving **warfarin** [US].
- Monitor plasma carnitine concentrations regularly to guide dosing.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on clinical trials in ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% regardless of causality) [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), chest pain (12%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), anorexia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), rhinitis (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).

### Serious and Postmarketing Adverse Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and related respiratory distress [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and gastritis. Body odor has also been reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a lettered category in the provided source, but noted that reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration.
    - **Recommendation:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy for metabolic disorders are established. Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See **Dosage and Administration** for ESRD-specific protocols. Note the precaution regarding the accumulation of metabolites (TMA/TMAO) if switching to oral formulations in renal failure [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg given three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from ~20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases plasma concentrations in ESRD, the effect on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentrations) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Integrity:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
