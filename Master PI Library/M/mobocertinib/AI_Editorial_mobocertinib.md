# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial Product Information (PI) was developed using documentation from the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference only. It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information approved for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP). It is the (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism which result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration should be via slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Subsequent daily maintenance doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine between 35 and 60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Initiation of therapy may be prompted by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occurred within minutes of injection. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].

### General Precautions
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While the injection bypasses some metabolic issues of the oral route, chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** In ESRD patients, the effects of supplemental carnitine on clinical outcomes (rather than just plasma levels) have not been fully determined [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequency was determined in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials of ESRD patients on chronic hemodialysis.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Chest pain (12%), Asthenia (9%–12% depending on dose) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%) [US].

### Common (1–10%)
- **Digestive:** Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%), Anorexia (5%), Constipation (3%) [US].
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%), Peripheral edema (5%), Weight decrease (5%) [US].
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%) [US].
- **Respiratory/Other:** Cough increase (9%), Rhinitis (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Fever (7%), Pruritus (5%), Rash (3%) [US].

### Serious/Other Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis (less frequent) [US].
- **Other:** A distinct "fishy" body odor has been reported [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in post-marketing experience [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when co-administered with levocarnitine. Monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No other specific drug-drug interactions were specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a lettered category in the provided source; however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources; however, dose selection should be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [General Principle].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For the injection, ESRD patients are a primary target population; see Section 3 for specific dialysis dosing [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, delivering substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD 9-Week Study:** 12 patients on hemodialysis received 20 mg/kg three times weekly. Pre-therapy mean plasma levocarnitine was 20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis). After one week (3 doses), trough concentrations ranged between 54 and 180 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels. At 24 weeks, the 10 mg/kg group averaged 148 ± 50 µmol/L, while the 40 mg/kg group averaged 456 ± 162 µmol/L [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Stability in Solutions:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
