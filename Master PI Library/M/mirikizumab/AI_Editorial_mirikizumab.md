# Editorial Product Information: Mirikizumab

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA). 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of mirikizumab for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians should refer to the local approved labelling for prescribing purposes in their specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mirikizumab (mirikizumab-mrkz [US])
- **Chemical Class:** Humanized immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4) variant monoclonal antibody; interleukin-23 (IL-23) antagonist (p19 subunit).
- **Brand Name:** **OMVOH** [US]
- **ATC Code:** L04AC21
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Intravenous Infusion:** 300 mg/15 mL (20 mg/mL) solution in a single-dose vial [US].
    - **Subcutaneous Injection:**
        - 100 mg/mL solution in single-dose prefilled pen or syringe [US].
        - 200 mg/2 mL (100 mg/mL) solution in single-dose prefilled pen or syringe [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (induction); Subcutaneous (maintenance).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Eli Lilly and Company [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Ulcerative Colitis (UC):** Treatment of moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis in adults [US-approved].
- **Crohn’s Disease (CD):** Treatment of moderately to severely active Crohn’s disease in adults [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration

### Pre-treatment Requirements
Prior to initiation, clinicians must:
- Screen for tuberculosis (TB) infection.
- Evaluate baseline liver enzymes (ALT, AST) and bilirubin.
- Ensure all age-appropriate vaccinations are completed according to current guidelines.

### Adult Dosing: Ulcerative Colitis
- **Induction Phase:** 300 mg administered via intravenous infusion over at least 30 minutes at Weeks 0, 4, and 8.
- **Maintenance Phase:** 200 mg administered via subcutaneous injection every 4 weeks, starting at Week 12. This may be given as a single 200 mg injection or two 100 mg injections.

### Adult Dosing: Crohn’s Disease
- **Induction Phase:** 900 mg administered via intravenous infusion over at least 90 minutes at Weeks 0, 4, and 8.
- **Maintenance Phase:** 300 mg administered via subcutaneous injection every 4 weeks, starting at Week 12. This is given as two consecutive injections (100 mg and 200 mg in any order).

### Administration and Missed Doses
- **Intravenous:** Must be diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Dextrose (50 mL to 250 mL for UC; 100 mL or 250 mL for CD).
- **Subcutaneous:** Administer into the abdomen, thigh, or back of the upper arm. 
- **Missed Dose:** Administer as soon as possible, then resume the regular 4-week schedule.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of serious hypersensitivity reactions to mirikizumab or any of the inactive excipients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis and infusion-related reactions (e.g., pruritus, erythema), have occurred. If a severe reaction occurs, therapy must be discontinued immediately and appropriate treatment initiated.
- **Infections:** Mirikizumab may increase infection risk. Do not initiate in patients with clinically important active infections until the infection resolves or is appropriately treated.
- **Tuberculosis:** Do not administer to patients with active TB. Latent TB must be treated prior to starting mirikizumab. Monitor for signs of active TB during and after treatment.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Drug-induced liver injury (DILI) has been reported. Monitor LFTs at baseline and for at least the first 24 weeks of treatment. If DILI is suspected, interrupt treatment until the diagnosis is ruled out.
- **Immunizations:** Avoid the use of live vaccines during treatment. No data are available on the efficacy of live or non-live vaccines in patients receiving mirikizumab.

## 6. Adverse Effects

### Ulcerative Colitis
- **Induction Phase (Very Common/Common):** Upper respiratory tract infections (8%), arthralgia (2%).
- **Maintenance Phase (Common):** Upper respiratory tract infections (14%), injection site reactions (9%), arthralgia (7%), rash (4%), headache (4%), and herpes viral infections (2%).

### Crohn’s Disease
- **Common (>5%):** Upper respiratory tract infections (28%), injection site reactions (10%), headache (6%), arthralgia (6%), and elevated liver tests (5%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Infections:** Serious infections (e.g., intestinal sepsis, pneumonia, listeria sepsis) occurred in <1% of subjects in clinical trials.
- **Liver Injury:** Significant elevations (≥5x ULN) in ALT and AST have been observed.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Substrates:** Chronic inflammation suppresses CYP450 enzymes via increased cytokines. Mirikizumab, by reducing pro-inflammatory cytokines, may normalize CYP450 levels, potentially decreasing the exposure of concomitant CYP450 substrates. Monitor drug concentrations for CYP substrates with narrow therapeutic indices (e.g., warfarin) upon initiation or discontinuation of mirikizumab.
- **Common IBD Medications:** Pharmacokinetic analysis suggests no impact on mirikizumab clearance by aminosalicylates, corticosteroids, or oral immunomodulators (e.g., azathioprine, methotrexate).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (current US labelling uses risk summary). Available human data are insufficient to determine drug-associated risks of birth defects or miscarriage. 
    - **Clinical Consideration:** Monoclonal antibodies are actively transported across the placenta during the third trimester; therefore, mirikizumab may affect the immune response of the in utero-exposed infant. Monitoring for infection in infants for the first 2 months of life is recommended.
- **Breastfeeding:** Presence in human milk is unknown. Endogenous IgG is known to be excreted in human milk. The benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need and potential infant exposure.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in monkeys showed no adverse effects on male or female reproductive tracts.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger patients, though data for those ≥75 years are limited. 
- **Renal Impairment:** No clinically significant impact on pharmacokinetics was observed in patients with mild to moderate renal impairment. 
- **Hepatic Impairment:** The effect of hepatic impairment on the pharmacokinetics of mirikizumab has not been studied.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody that selectively binds to the p19 subunit of the human IL-23 cytokine. This inhibits the interaction of IL-23 with its receptor, blocking the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines involved in mucosal inflammation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is approximately 44% in UC and 36% in CD. Tmax is approximately 5 days.
    - **Distribution:** Total volume of distribution is approximately 4.4 L to 4.8 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Expected to be degraded into small peptides and amino acids via catabolic pathways.
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is approximately 9.3 days. Clearance is independent of dose.
- **Immunogenicity:** Anti-drug antibodies (ADA) developed in 23% of UC patients and 13% of CD patients over 52 weeks. In a subset of UC patients with high ADA titers, reduced serum trough concentrations and reduced efficacy were noted.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Ulcerative Colitis (LUCENT-1 & LUCENT-2):** Mirikizumab demonstrated significantly higher rates of clinical remission at Week 12 (24% vs 15% placebo) and Week 52 (51% vs 27% placebo). Benefits were also seen in bowel urgency and endoscopic improvement.
- **Crohn’s Disease (VIVID-1):** At Week 52, clinical remission was achieved by 53% of patients (vs 36% placebo), and endoscopic response was achieved by 46% (vs 23% placebo).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F).
- **Room Temperature:** If necessary, prefilled pens/syringes may be kept at room temperature (up to 30°C/86°F) for up to 2 weeks in the original carton. Do not return to the refrigerator once warmed.
- **Special Handling:** Do not freeze, shake, or expose to direct heat or light. The product is preservative-free.
