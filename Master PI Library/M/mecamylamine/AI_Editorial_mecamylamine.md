# Editorial Product Information: Mecamylamine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized, clinically focused overview of mecamylamine for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mecamylamine hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Secondary amine; ganglionic blocking agent.
- **Brand Names:** **Vecamyl** [US].
- **ATC Code:** C02BC01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 2.5 mg oral tablets [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Tilde Sciences LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Essential Hypertension:** Management of moderately severe to severe essential hypertension [US].
- **Malignant Hypertension:** Management of uncomplicated cases of malignant hypertension [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** 2.5 mg twice daily [US].
- **Titration:** Increase by 2.5 mg increments at intervals of no less than two days. The goal is to reach the dosage just below that which causes signs of mild postural hypotension [US].
- **Maintenance Dose:** The average total daily dose is 25 mg, typically administered in three divided doses. Some patients may require as little as 2.5 mg daily, while severe cases may require four or more doses per day [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Consistency with Meals:** Administration after meals may result in more gradual absorption and smoother blood pressure control. Dosing timing relative to meals should be kept consistent [US].
- **Diurnal Adjustment:** Because the blood pressure response is higher in the early morning, the larger portion of the daily dose is typically given at noon and in the evening. The morning dose is generally the smallest and may be omitted in some cases [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

### Combination Therapy
- When used with other antihypertensives, the dosage of those agents and mecamylamine should be reduced. Thiazides may be continued at their usual dose, but the mecamylamine dose should be reduced by at least 50% [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Mild, moderate, or labile hypertension [US].
- Coronary insufficiency or recent myocardial infarction [US].
- Uremia [US].
- Glaucoma [US].
- Organic pyloric stenosis [US].
- Concurrent use of antibiotics and sulfonamides [US].
- Uncooperative patients [US].
- Hypersensitivity to mecamylamine or any excipients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **CNS Penetration:** As a secondary amine, mecamylamine readily crosses the blood-brain barrier. It may cause tremors, choreiform movements, mental aberrations, and convulsions, particularly at high doses or in patients with renal or cerebral insufficiency [US].
- **Withdrawal Syndrome:** Abrupt discontinuation can lead to a rapid return of hypertension. In patients with malignant hypertension or heart failure, sudden withdrawal may cause fatal cerebrovascular accidents or acute congestive heart failure. Withdrawal must be gradual while substituting other therapy [US].
- **Orthostatic Hypotension:** Significant blood pressure reduction is predominantly orthostatic. Patients should be cautioned against sudden changes in posture and warned that heat, alcohol, and exercise may potentiate this effect [US].
- **Renal Function:** Use with extreme caution, if at all, if renal insufficiency is present (e.g., rising or elevated BUN) [US].
- **Paralytic Ileus:** Frequent loose stools with abdominal distension and decreased bowel sounds may be early signs of paralytic ileus. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if these symptoms occur [US].
- **Urinary Retention:** Caution is required in patients with prostatic hypertrophy, bladder neck obstruction, or urethral stricture [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies are not specified in the available source.*

- **Gastrointestinal:** Ileus, constipation (sometimes preceded by frequent liquid stools), nausea, vomiting, anorexia, glossitis, xerostomia (dry mouth).
- **Cardiovascular:** Orthostatic dizziness, syncope, postural hypotension.
- **Nervous System/Psychiatric:** Convulsions, choreiform movements, mental aberrations, tremor, paresthesias, sedation, fatigue.
- **Respiratory:** Interstitial pulmonary edema and fibrosis.
- **Urogenital:** Urinary retention, impotence, decreased libido.
- **Special Senses:** Blurred vision, mydriasis (dilated pupils).
- **Miscellaneous:** Weakness.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antihypertensives:** Potentiation of effect; requires dose reduction of all agents [US].
- **Alcohol:** Potentiates the hypotensive effect of mecamylamine [US].
- **Anesthesia & Surgery:** Potentiates hypotensive effects [US].
- **Antibiotics/Sulfonamides:** Generally contraindicated for concurrent use with ganglion blockers [US].
- **Thiazide Diuretics:** Potentiate effect; mecamylamine dose should be halved [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category C [US]:** Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if mecamylamine causes fetal harm or affects reproductive capacity. The drug crosses the placental barrier and should only be used if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Due to the risk of serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug, considering the drug's importance to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies to evaluate impacts on fertility have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in pediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed; however, caution is advised regarding cardiovascular and renal insufficiency, which are more prevalent in this population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in uremia. Use with extreme discretion if BUN is rising or elevated [US]. Renal elimination is influenced by urinary pH; alkalinization reduces excretion, and acidification promotes it [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A potent oral ganglion blocker that reduces blood pressure in both normotensive and hypertensive individuals.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of action is gradual (0.5 to 2 hours) with a long-lasting effect (typically 6 to 12 hours). It affects both orthostatic and supine blood pressure [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Almost completely absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract [US].
  - **Distribution:** Crosses both the blood-brain and placental barriers [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Not specified in available sources.
  - **Elimination:** Excreted slowly in the urine in an unchanged form. The rate of renal elimination is markedly influenced by urinary pH [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Container:** Dispense in a tight container [US].
