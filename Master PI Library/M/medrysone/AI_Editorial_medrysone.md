# Editorial Product Information: Medrysone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. As only the United States PI was available for this review, information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) is noted as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Medrysone (a synthetic corticosteroid).
- **Chemical Class:** Glucocorticoid; 11β-hydroxy-6α-methylpregn-4-ene-3,20-dione.
- **Brand Name(s):** HMS [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01BA08.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Ophthalmic suspension, 1% (10 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical Ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Allergan, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Treatment of allergic conjunctivitis [US].
- Treatment of vernal conjunctivitis [US].
- Treatment of episcleritis [US].
- Treatment of epinephrine sensitivity [US].
- **Important Note:** Medrysone is specifically **not recommended** for the treatment of iritis or uveitis, as its efficacy has not been demonstrated for these conditions [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Instill one drop into the conjunctival sac up to every four hours [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - The suspension must be shaken well before use [US].
    - To prevent contamination, avoid touching the dropper tip to the eyelids or any other surface. 
    - The product should be used by a single patient only to prevent the spread of infection [US].
- **Monitoring/Renewal:** Initial prescriptions and renewals exceeding 20 mL should only be issued after a physician has performed an examination using magnification (e.g., slit-lamp biomicroscopy) and fluorescein staining where appropriate [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Most viral diseases of the cornea and conjunctiva, including epithelial herpes simplex keratitis (dendritic keratitis), vaccinia, and varicella [US].
- Mycobacterial infections of the eye [US].
- Fungal diseases of ocular structures [US].
- Known or suspected hypersensitivity to medrysone, any ingredient in the formulation (e.g., benzalkonium chloride), or other corticosteroids [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Intraocular Pressure (IOP):** Prolonged use may result in ocular hypertension or glaucoma, potentially damaging the optic nerve and impairing visual acuity/fields. If used for 10 days or longer, IOP must be routinely monitored, even in children or uncooperative patients [US].
- **Cataract Formation:** Long-term use may lead to posterior subcapsular cataract formation [US].
- **Infection Risk:** Corticosteroids may suppress the host immune response, increasing the hazard of secondary fungal, bacterial, or viral ocular infections. They may also mask or enhance acute purulent infections [US].
- **Delayed Healing:** Ocular steroids may delay the healing process following cataract surgery and increase the incidence of bleb formation [US].
- **Corneal/Scleral Thinning:** In conditions causing thinning of the cornea or sclera, topical steroids may lead to perforation [US].
- **Herpes Simplex:** Use in patients with a history of herpes simplex requires extreme caution and frequent slit-lamp microscopy [US].
- **Non-Responsive Conditions:** Medrysone is ineffective in Sjören's keratoconjunctivitis and mustard gas keratitis [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are listed in decreasing order of frequency:
- **Ocular (Common/Frequent):** Elevation of intraocular pressure (IOP), which may lead to glaucoma and infrequent optic nerve damage [US].
- **Ocular (Infrequent/Rare):** Posterior subcapsular cataract formation and delayed wound healing [US].
- **Irritation:** Transient burning and stinging upon instillation, allergic reactions, foreign body sensation, and blurred vision [US].
- **Other Reported Ocular Effects:** Keratitis, conjunctivitis, corneal ulcers, mydriasis, conjunctival hyperemia, loss of accommodation, ptosis, and perforation of the globe [US].
- **Systemic Effects:** Rare occurrences of systemic hypercorticoidism (Cushing's syndrome symptoms) have been reported following topical steroid use [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in the available US source.
- **Specific Clinical Notes:** While not explicitly listed as a drug interaction, medrysone is indicated for "epinephrine sensitivity," suggesting its use in managing inflammatory responses related to other ocular medications [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C.** 
    - Animal data: Medrysone is embryocidal in rabbits when administered at 10 to 30 times the human ocular dose, resulting in increased early resorptions. 
    - Human data: No adequate or well-controlled studies exist for pregnant women. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if topical ophthalmic administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to produce detectable levels in human milk. Because systemically absorbed steroids can suppress growth or interfere with endogenous corticosteroid production in infants, a decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug, considering the mother's clinical need [US].
- **Fertility:** No animal or human studies have been conducted to evaluate the potential for impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children under the age of 3 years have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between elderly and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Medrysone is a synthetic glucocorticoid. It inhibits the inflammatory response, including oedema, fibrin deposition, capillary dilation, and phagocytic migration. It also limits capillary proliferation, collagen deposition, and scar formation [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Medrysone has less anti-inflammatory potency than 0.1% dexamethasone [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not detailed in the available ophthalmic PI, though topical corticosteroids are known to be absorbed into the aqueous humor and can reach systemic circulation in small amounts.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Specific pivotal trial data was not detailed in the provided regulatory documentation [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at temperatures up to 25°C (77°F) [US].
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing [US].
- **Integrity:** Keep the bottle tightly closed when not in use.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
- **Other Jurisdictions:** Not specified in available sources.
