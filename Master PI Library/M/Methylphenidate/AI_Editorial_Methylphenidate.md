# Editorial Product Information: Methylphenidate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the local PI for definitive legal and prescribing requirements in their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methylphenidate hydrochloride (a central nervous system stimulant).
- **Chemical Class:** Phenethylamine derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Artige [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06BA04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Immediate-release tablets: 10 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD):** Indicated as part of a comprehensive treatment program (including psychological, educational, and social measures) for children (6 years and over), adolescents, and adults [Australian].
- **Narcolepsy:** Treatment of symptoms of daytime sleepiness and inappropriate sleep episodes [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- Dosage must be individualised based on clinical need and patient response.
- Treatment should begin at low doses with weekly increments.

### ADHD (Children and Adolescents 6 years and over)
- **Initial Dose:** 5 mg once or twice daily (e.g., at breakfast and lunch) [Australian].
- **Titration:** Increase by 5–10 mg weekly. Total daily dose should be divided.
- **Timing:** Administer to coincide with periods of maximum academic or social difficulty.
- **Evening Doses:** A small additional dose at ~8:00 p.m. may be considered if symptoms recur as the drug wears off, provided it does not cause insomnia [Australian].

### Narcolepsy (Adults)
- **Average Dose:** 20–30 mg daily in 2–3 divided doses [Australian].
- **Range:** Some patients require 10–15 mg, while others may require 40–60 mg.
- **Administration Timing:** To avoid insomnia, the final dose should generally be taken before 6:00 p.m.

### Maximum Daily Doses
- **ADHD and Narcolepsy:** 60 mg/day [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Onset of action is faster when taken with food; therefore, administration should be standardised in relation to meals to ensure consistent effects [Australian].
- Doses should ideally be taken 1–2 hours before the maximum effect is required.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to methylphenidate or formulation excipients.
- Anxiety, tension, or agitation states.
- Glaucoma (including acute angle-closure).
- Diagnosis or family history of Tourette’s syndrome.
- Hyperthyroidism.
- **Cardiovascular Disorders:** Severe hypertension, heart failure, myocardial infarction, angina pectoris, arterial occlusive disease, hemodynamically significant congenital heart disease, cardiomyopathies, cardiac arrhythmia, or channelopathies [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Conditions:** Severe depression, anorexia nervosa, psychotic symptoms, or suicidal tendency.
- Phaeochromocytoma.
- History of drug dependence or alcohol abuse.
- Concurrent use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs), or use within 14 days of discontinuing an MAOI [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Drug Dependence):** Give cautiously to patients with a history of drug dependence or alcoholism. Chronic abuse can lead to marked tolerance and psychological dependence. Withdrawal requires careful supervision to avoid severe depression [Australian].
- **Sudden Death and Cardiovascular Events:** Reported in association with CNS stimulants at usual doses, particularly in patients with structural cardiac abnormalities. Adults have a higher baseline risk for serious cardiac conditions; stimulants should generally be avoided in those with known abnormalities [Australian].
- **Psychiatric Effects:** May exacerbate symptoms in psychotic patients. New onset or worsening of aggressive behavior, mania, or hallucinations has been reported. Monitor for suicidal ideation and worsening behavior [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Reported when co-administered with serotonergic agents (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs). Concomitant use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Growth Retardation:** Long-term use in children may cause temporary slowing in growth rate and weight gain. Monitor height and weight; treatment interruption may be necessary [Australian].
- **Priapism:** Prolonged/painful erections requiring surgical intervention have been reported, often following dose increases or during drug withdrawal [Australian].
- **Seizures:** May lower the convulsion threshold in patients with or without a history of seizures; discontinue if seizures occur [Australian].
- **Peripheral Vasculopathy:** Associated with Raynaud’s phenomenon; monitor for digital changes [Australian].
- **Haematological Monitoring:** Periodic CBC, differential, and platelet counts are advised during prolonged therapy [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are most common at the start of treatment.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nasopharyngitis, decreased appetite, nervousness, insomnia, irritability, nausea, dry mouth. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Anxiety, restlessness, sleep disorder, agitation, depression, aggression, bruxism, dyskinesia, tremor, headache, drowsiness, dizziness, tachycardia, palpitation, arrhythmias, hypertension, cough, abdominal pain, vomiting, dyspepsia, rash, pruritus, urticaria, fever, scalp hair loss, hyperhidrosis, arthralgia, feeling jittery, weight decreased, Raynaud’s phenomenon. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Trismus. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Moderately reduced weight gain/growth retardation (prolonged use), visual accommodation difficulties, blurred vision, angina pectoris. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, hypersensitivity (including angioedema/anaphylaxis), psychosis, hallucinations, convulsions, choreoathetoid movements, tics/Tourette's exacerbation, cerebrovascular disorders (vasculitis, haemorrhage, CVA), NMS, abnormal liver function (to hepatic coma), exfoliative dermatitis, erythema multiforme, muscle cramps. |

**Post-marketing/Spontaneous Reports (Frequency Not Known):** Pancytopenia, suicidal ideation/attempt, mood swings, mania, drug abuse/dependence, reversible ischemic neurological deficit, migraine, diplopia, mydriasis, cardiac arrest, myocardial infarction, epistaxis, gynaecomastia, priapism, and sudden cardiac death [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Risk of hypertensive crisis (Contraindicated).
- **Antihypertensives:** Methylphenidate may decrease the efficacy of antihypertensive drugs.
- **Drugs that Elevate Blood Pressure:** Use with caution.
- **Anaesthetics:** Risk of sudden blood pressure/heart rate increase during surgery; omit methylphenidate on the day of surgery [Australian].
- **Centrally Acting Alpha-2 Agonists (e.g., Clonidine):** Serious adverse events, including sudden death, reported; causality not established.
- **Dopaminergic Drugs:** Potential for interactions with dopamine agonists (including L-DOPA/TCAs) and antagonists (antipsychotics). Concomitant use with antipsychotics may increase the risk of extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS) [Australian].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Risk of serotonin syndrome.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not significantly metabolised by CYP450. However, may inhibit the metabolism of coumarin anticoagulants, certain anticonvulsants (phenobarbitone, phenytoin, primidone), and tricyclic antidepressants (imipramine, desipramine) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Safety not established. Animal studies showed teratogenic effects in rabbits (at 3x human exposure). Not recommended unless the benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Distributed into breast milk (milk-to-plasma ratio ~2.5). Mothers should refrain from breastfeeding during treatment due to potential risks to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data available. Animal studies (mice) showed no impairment of fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children under 6 years are not established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed; however, pharmacokinetic behavior is reportedly similar between hyperactive children and healthy adult volunteers [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Renal excretion of unchanged drug is negligible, but renal excretion of the metabolite ritalinic acid may be reduced in impairment [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** CNS stimulant. Blocks the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine into presynaptic neurons, increasing these neurotransmitters in the extraneuronal space [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and almost complete; absolute bioavailability is low (~22% for d-enantiomer) due to extensive first-pass metabolism.
    - **Distribution:** Low plasma protein binding (~15%).
    - **Metabolism:** Rapidly and extensively biotransformed by carboxylesterase CES1A1 into the inactive metabolite ritalinic acid.
    - **Excretion:** Eliminated primarily in the urine (78–97%); main metabolite is ritalinic acid. Half-life is ~2–3 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Growth Study:** A 14-month randomised study and 36-month naturalistic follow-up in children (7–10 years) suggested that consistent daily treatment results in a temporary slowing of growth (average 2 cm less in height and 2.7 kg less in weight over 3 years) without evidence of growth rebound during the treatment period [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Protect from moisture [Australian].
- Shelf life is specified on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 8 (S8) - Controlled Drug [Australian]**.
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription only.
