# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI used for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should refer to the current local PI for legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered in severe metabolic crisis, followed by equivalent doses over the next 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and not less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Post-Dialysis Dosing:** For ESRD patients, the dose should be administered into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels prior to treatment. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is advised [US].

### Dose Adjustments
- **ESRD:** Dose adjustments in ESRD patients should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug and initiate medical treatment [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral formulation context):** While the injection is used in ESRD, the safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects observed in clinical trials of patients on chronic hemodialysis (occurring at ≥5%) include:
- **General:** Abdominal pain, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, and pain [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, and palpitations [US].
- **Digestive:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, dyspepsia, and anorexia [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, and peripheral edema [US].
- **Neurologic/Psychiatric:** Dizziness, insomnia, depression, and paresthesia [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnea, and rhinitis [US].
- **Other:** Body odor and gastritis have been reported less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specifically assigned a letter category in the provided source, but noted that reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations. The benefits of treatment to the mother must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3 for dosing). Oral formulations should be used with caution due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- In ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg) administered three times weekly after dialysis resulted in significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy. The clinical effect on signs/symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) at room temperature [US].
