# Editorial Product Information: Metyrapone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a structured format.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource; it is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Metyrapone (a diagnostic agent for pituitary function).
- **Brand Name(s):** Metopirone [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** V04CD01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Soft gelatin capsules containing 250 mg of metyrapone. The capsules are white to yellowish-white, oblong, and marked "M01" in red ink [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Asteri Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Metyrapone is indicated for diagnostic use in the following contexts [Australian]:
- **Diagnosis of latent ACTH deficiency:** Specifically in cases of suspected or known pituitary dysfunction, suspected pituitary tumours, assessment before and after pituitary surgery, and assessment of ACTH suppression following glucocorticoid therapy.
- **Differential diagnosis of Cushing's Syndrome:** To identify states of adrenocortical hyperfunction.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Capsules should be taken with milk, yoghurt, or after meals to reduce nausea and vomiting, which may impair drug absorption [Australian].

### Diagnostic Test Regimens (Adults)
1.  **Short Single-Dose Test (Ambulatory or Inpatient):** 
    - **Dose:** 30 mg/kg (maximum 3 g) administered at approximately midnight.
    - **Procedure:** Blood samples for 11-desoxycortisol (Compound S) and/or ACTH are taken early the following morning (07:30–08:00). 
    - **Follow-up:** A prophylactic dose of 50 mg cortisone acetate is administered after the test [Australian].
2.  **Multiple-Dose Test (Hospital Setting Only):**
    - **Dose:** 500 mg to 750 mg every 4 hours for 24 hours (Total: 3.0 g to 4.5 g).
    - **Procedure:** Baseline 24-hour urinary steroid excretion is measured on Day 1. The drug is administered on Day 2. Evaluation is based on two consecutive 24-hour urinary samples [Australian].

### Assessment Criteria [Australian]
- **Normal ACTH Reserve:** A rise in plasma ACTH to at least 44 pmol/L (200 ng/L) or 11-desoxycortisol to >0.2 µmol/L (70 µg/L).
- **Secondary Adrenocortical Insufficiency:** Indicated by the absence of a pronounced increase in urinary 11-desoxycortisol.
- **Cushing’s Disease:** An increase in 11-desoxycorticosteroids suggests ACTH-led adrenocortical hyperplasia; a lack of increase may suggest an autonomous cortisol-producing tumour.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to metyrapone or any excipients (including sodium ethyl hydroxybenzoate and sodium propyl hydroxybenzoate) [Australian].
- Primary adrenocortical insufficiency [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Adrenal Insufficiency:** Metyrapone may induce acute adrenal crisis in patients with significantly reduced adrenal secretory capacity or global pituitary insufficiency. Adrenal response to exogenous ACTH should be confirmed prior to testing [Australian].
- **Setting:** The multiple-dose test must be performed in a hospital setting with close monitoring [Australian].
- **Hypothyroidism:** Patients with thyroid hypofunction may exhibit a delayed or absent rise in urinary steroids, potentially masking results [Australian].
- **Opportunistic Infections:** Patients with severe Cushing's syndrome are at increased risk for infections such as *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia during treatment; prophylaxis should be considered [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** Long-term use (off-label/extended) may cause or exacerbate hypertension due to the accumulation of desoxycorticosterone [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Contains parahydroxybenzoates which may cause delayed allergic reactions [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are ranked by frequency according to [Australian] data:

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common** (≥1/100 to <1/10) | Dizziness, sedation, headache, hypotension, nausea, vomiting |
| **Rare** (≥1/10,000 to <1/1,000) | Adrenal insufficiency, abdominal pain, allergic skin reactions |
| **Not Known** (Frequency cannot be estimated) | Leukopenia, anaemia, thrombocytopenia, light-headedness, hypertension |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Acute adrenocortical insufficiency is a critical risk, particularly if the test is administered to patients with low adrenal reserve without proper supervision [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Interference with Diagnostic Accuracy:** The following may influence test results and should ideally be discontinued prior to the test [Australian]:
    - Anticonvulsants (e.g., phenytoin, barbiturates).
    - Psychoactive drugs (e.g., amitriptyline, chlorpromazine, alprazolam).
    - Hormone preparations affecting the HPA axis (e.g., corticosteroids).
    - Anti-thyroid agents.
    - Cyproheptadine.
- **Paracetamol (Acetaminophen):** Metyrapone may potentially increase paracetamol toxicity in humans [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B3** [Australian]. 
    - Not recommended for diagnostic use during pregnancy unless the benefits clearly outweigh the risks. 
    - Metyrapone crosses the placenta; fetal pituitary response to the enzymatic block has been observed, and low cortisol levels have been reported in neonates following chronic in utero exposure [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** There is insufficient data regarding excretion into breast milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during metyrapone treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate adverse effects on spermatogenesis (dogs) and ovarian follicular development (mice). No human data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - **Short Single-Dose Test:** Same weight-based dose as adults (30 mg/kg) [Australian].
    - **Multiple-Dose Test:** Limited data; recommended dose is 15 mg/kg (minimum 250 mg) every 4 hours for 6 doses [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical evidence suggests no special dosage regimen is required, but caution is advised due to the higher frequency of hepatic, renal, or cardiac comorbidities [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Patients with liver cirrhosis may exhibit a delayed response due to the prolonged plasma half-life of cortisol [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments are not provided in available sources, though caution in the elderly with reduced renal function is noted [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Metyrapone reversibly inhibits 11-beta-hydroxylation in the adrenal cortex. This blocks the synthesis of cortisol, corticosterone, and aldosterone. In individuals with intact pituitary function, this triggers a compensatory increase in ACTH, leading to the accumulation of precursor steroids (11-desoxycortisol and 11-desoxycorticosterone) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma concentration reached in approximately 1 hour [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Following a 750 mg dose, mean peak concentration is 3.7 µg/mL, falling to 0.5 µg/mL after 4 hours [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primary active metabolite is metyrapol (reduced metyrapone). The plasma elimination half-life of metyrapone is approximately 2 hours [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal. Within 72 hours of a 4.5 g dose, ~5.3% is excreted as metyrapone and ~38.5% as metyrapol (mostly conjugated with glucuronic acid) [Australian].
