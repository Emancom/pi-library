# Editorial Product Information: Methscopolamine Bromide

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction:
    - **United States** (Food and Drug Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinically accurate synthesis of available regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, full Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methscopolamine bromide (a quaternary ammonium derivative of scopolamine).
- **Chemical Class:** Anticholinergic/Antimuscarinic agent.
- **Brand Names:** Noted generically as **Methscopolamine Bromide**; distributed by Bryant Ranch Prepack and Unichem Pharmaceuticals [US].
- **ATC Code:** A03BB03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 2.5 mg and 5 mg [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Unichem Pharmaceuticals (USA), Inc.; Repackaged by Bryant Ranch Prepack [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Peptic Ulcer:** Indicated as adjunctive therapy for the treatment of peptic ulcer [US].
- *Editorial Note:* Current evidence has not demonstrated that methscopolamine bromide is effective in promoting the healing of peptic ulcers, decreasing recurrence rates, or preventing associated complications [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The average regimen is 2.5 mg taken 30 minutes before meals and 2.5 mg to 5 mg at bedtime [US].
- **Initial Dosing:** A total daily starting dose of 12.5 mg is typically effective without significant side effects [US].
- **Acute Symptom Management:** For severe symptoms (e.g., abdominal pain or cramping) requiring prompt relief, the dose may be initiated at 20 mg daily, divided into 5 mg doses 30 minutes before meals and at bedtime [US].
- **Titration:** 
    - If side effects occur early, the dose should be reduced.
    - If symptoms persist without side effects, the daily dose may be increased.
    - Doses up to 30 mg daily have been tolerated by some patients [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be administered on an empty stomach (30 minutes before meals) to optimize pharmacodynamic effects on gastric secretion and motility [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Glaucoma** [US].
- **Obstructive Uropathy:** Including bladder neck obstruction due to prostatic hypertrophy [US].
- **Obstructive GI Disease:** Such as pyloroduodenal stenosis [US].
- **Paralytic ileus** and **intestinal atony** (particularly in elderly or debilitated patients) [US].
- **Severe Ulcerative Colitis** or **Toxic Megacolon** complicating ulcerative colitis [US].
- **Unstable Cardiovascular Status** in acute hemorrhage [US].
- **Myasthenia Gravis** [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to methscopolamine bromide or related anticholinergic drugs [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Environmental Temperature:** Use in high temperatures can lead to heat prostration (fever and heat stroke) due to suppressed sweating [US].
- **Intestinal Obstruction Warning:** Diarrhea may be an early sign of incomplete intestinal obstruction, especially in patients with an ileostomy or colostomy; treatment in these cases is inappropriate and potentially hazardous [US].
- **CNS and Visual Effects:** May cause drowsiness or blurred vision. Patients should be advised against operating motor vehicles or machinery where mental alertness is required [US].
- **Neuromuscular Blockade:** Overdosage may result in a curare-like action, leading to muscular weakness and potential paralysis [US].
- **Specific Clinical Precautions:**
    - **Autonomic Neuropathy:** Use with caution [US].
    - **Cardiovascular Disease:** Caution in patients with hyperthyroidism, coronary heart disease, congestive heart failure, hypertension, or tachyarrhythmias, as the drug may increase heart rate [US].
    - **Prostatic Hypertrophy:** May aggravate urinary retention [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are not established in available sources, but the following reactions have been reported:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cardiovascular** | Tachycardia, palpitations |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Xerostomia (dry mouth), nausea, vomiting, constipation, bloated feeling |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, drowsiness, headache, nervousness, mental confusion, insomnia |
| **Special Senses** | Blurred vision, mydriasis (dilation of pupil), cycloplegia, increased ocular tension, loss of taste |
| **Renal/Urinary** | Urinary hesitancy, urinary retention |
| **Dermatologic** | Decreased sweating, urticaria, other dermal manifestations |
| **Immunological** | Anaphylaxis, severe allergic reactions |
| **Reproductive/Other** | Impotence, suppression of lactation, weakness |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Anticholinergics:** Additive effects occur when used concomitantly with antipsychotics, tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs), and other agents possessing anticholinergic properties [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Antacids:** Concurrent administration may interfere with the absorption of methscopolamine bromide [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C:** Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if it causes fetal harm or affects reproductive capacity. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if the drug is excreted in human milk. Because many drugs are excreted in milk, caution is advised. Additionally, anticholinergics may suppress lactation [US].
- **Fertility:** No long-term animal studies have been performed to evaluate carcinogenic or fertility-impairing potential [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Use with caution due to increased risk of intestinal atony and potential for aggravated urinary retention or CNS effects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution [US]. No specific dosing adjustments are provided in the source document.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution [US]. No specific dosing adjustments are provided in the source document.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Methscopolamine bromide is a muscarinic antagonist. It reduces the volume and total acid content of gastric secretions, inhibits gastrointestinal motility, suppresses salivary excretion, and dilates the pupils [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Poor and unreliable (10% to 25% absorbed). Quaternary ammonium salts have limited absorption from intact skin and poor conjunctival penetration [US].
    - **Distribution:** Limited ability to cross the blood-brain barrier [US].
    - **Onset/Duration:** Effects appear within one hour of oral administration and persist for 4 to 6 hours [US].
    - **Excretion:** Primarily eliminated in the urine and bile, or as unabsorbed drug in the feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal trial data were not detailed in the provided source document. The document notes that efficacy in healing peptic ulcers has not been proven [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
