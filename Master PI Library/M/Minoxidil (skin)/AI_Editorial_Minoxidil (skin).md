# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial information is derived from the official product labeling provided by the United States (FDA) for **Levocarnitine Injection, USP**.
- **Purpose:** This document serves as a harmonized clinical synthesis for healthcare professionals, providing an editorial overview of the drug’s profile, administration, and safety.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial tool and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the United States [US] source was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic), marketed by Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus (over 2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion.
- **Loading Doses:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses divided every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h). Doses should not be spaced further than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg/day, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg/day have been utilized based on clinical requirements [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels should guide adjustments. If levels are below the normal range (40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L), therapy may be initiated or adjusted. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be considered as early as the third or fourth week of treatment based on plasma concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** While no specific contraindications are listed as "known" in the source document, caution is advised in patients with previous reactions to levocarnitine components [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, particularly in ESRD patients. These can occur within minutes of IV administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Gastritis has also been reported.
- **Body Odor:** A distinct body odor may occur during therapy [US].

### Clinical Trial Data (ESRD Patients)
In placebo-controlled trials of ESRD patients, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% regardless of causality [US]:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain (15%), Headache (37%), Injection site reaction (27-38%), Pain (32%), Fever (5-12%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (18-21%), Hypotension (15-19%), Tachycardia (5-9%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea (9-35%), Vomiting (9-21%), Nausea (5-12%), Abdominal pain (5-21%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (15-27%), Cough increase (10-18%), Rhinitis (6-11%) |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness (10-18%), Paresthesia (3-12%) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustment in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a lettered category in the current document version]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response, use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after administration. Consider the benefits of maternal treatment against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant; discontinuation of nursing may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg, suggesting application across age groups, though specific pediatric trial data is not detailed beyond general dosing recommendations [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** IV levocarnitine is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. However, the use of **oral** levocarnitine is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This provides substrates for oxidation and subsequent energy production in tissues (except the brain), notably skeletal and cardiac muscle [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12) receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis, mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations rose from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While IV levocarnitine effectively increases plasma carnitine concentrations, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** When diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription only). Available as a 1 g/5 mL single-dose vial [US].
