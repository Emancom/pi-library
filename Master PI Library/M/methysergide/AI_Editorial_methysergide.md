# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data to support safe and effective prescribing.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes. It is not a substitute for the official PI and should not be used as the sole basis for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; frequency should never be less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Titration:** Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (target 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **Plasma Levels:** Monitor free carnitine levels (target 35–60 µmol/L) and overall clinical condition [US].
    - **Coagulation:** Monitor International Normalised Ratio (INR) in patients concurrently taking warfarin [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **Body as a Whole:** Headache (up to 37%), Pain (up to 35%), Injection site reaction (up to 38%), Flu syndrome [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (up to 21%), Hypotension (up to 19%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (up to 35%), Vomiting (up to 21%), Nausea (up to 12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (up to 27%), Dyspnea (up to 11%), Cough increase [US].

### Common (1–10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, abdominal pain, melena, anorexia [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, peripheral edema, weight changes [US].
- **Nervous:** Dizziness, paresthesia, insomnia, depression [US].
- **Other:** Body odor (transient) [US].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis** and other severe hypersensitivity reactions.
- **Seizures** [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increased INR when used concurrently with levocarnitine. Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended following initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not formally assigned a letter grade in the source, but the text states: "Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed."
    - **Animal Data:** No evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose based on surface area [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Bovine studies show increased carnitine concentrations in milk. Consider the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant versus the benefits of treatment to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders). See Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **ESRD on Dialysis:** Specific dosing provided (see Section 3). 
    - **General Renal Insufficiency:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in this group. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities related to the accumulation of toxic organic acids and restores plasma carnitine concentrations [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in ESRD patients undergoing chronic hemodialysis demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Scheduled as a Human Prescription Drug [US].
