# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for use by healthcare professionals.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of preparation, only the United States PI was available for review; comparisons to other regions (e.g., Australian, European) are omitted unless specified in the source text.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP), a naturally occurring carrier molecule.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (not explicitly stated in source, but standard for levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Dialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
Levocarnitine injection must be administered intravenously. 

### Dosing by Indication
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
  - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
  - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
  - Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
  - Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
  - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
  - Administration: Slow bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
  - Dose adjustments: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Use only when solution and container permit.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [United States].
- **General Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) before and during therapy (weekly/monthly), alongside blood chemistries and vital signs.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting. In dialysis patients, frequently reported events regardless of causality include:
  - Injection site reactions (33–38%)
  - Pain (30–35%)
  - Hypertension (18–21%)
  - Diarrhea (9–35%)
  - Headache (3–37%)
  - Pharyngitis (15–27%)
- **Less Frequent:** Gastritis, body odor.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and laryngeal edema [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitor INR levels at initiation and after dose adjustments [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not explicitly assigned by letter in this version of PI, but notes standard precautionary language]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess intake by the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment in animal studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for metabolic disorders and ESRD; see Section 3 for dosing [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally excreted in urine [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Corrects carnitine deficiency, which is biochemically defined as plasma concentrations <20 µmol/L.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include TMAO (in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (in feces).
  - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Levocarnitine is readily removed by hemodialysis [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses 3 times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks. 
- **Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is demonstrated, the impact on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) at room temperature [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only.
