# Editorial Product Information: Methoxsalen (Extracorporeal)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official PI. Clinicians must consult the approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methoxsalen (a naturally occurring photoactive substance of the furocoumarin/psoralen class).
- **Brand Name:** **UVADEX** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** LO3AX.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile concentrated injection for extracorporeal use; 200 micrograms per 10 mL (20 mcg/mL) in single-use amber glass vials [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Extracorporeal administration (administered directly into the photoactivation bag of the **THERAKOS CELLEX** Photopheresis System; **not for direct injection into patients**).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Therakos Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Graft Versus Host Disease (cGVHD):** Treatment of steroid-refractory and steroid-intolerant cGVHD in adults following allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell (HSC) transplantation [Australian-approved].
- **Cutaneous T-cell Lymphoma (CTCL):** Palliative treatment of skin manifestations in patients unresponsive to other forms of treatment [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
The dosage is calculated based on the treatment volume displayed by the photopheresis instrument.
- **Formula:** Treatment volume (mL) x 0.017 mL of **UVADEX**.
- **Example:** For a treatment volume of 240 mL, the dose is 4.1 mL of **UVADEX**.

### Indication-Specific Schedules [Australian]
- **cGVHD:** Three treatments in the first week, followed by two treatments per week for at least 12 weeks, or as clinically indicated.
- **CTCL:** Treatment on two successive days every month for six months. If skin scores increase after eight sessions, frequency may be increased to two successive days every two weeks for three months.

### Administration Instructions
- **Ex Vivo Use Only:** The calculated dose must be injected into the photoactivation bag before the photoactivation phase.
- **Timing:** Use immediately after drawing into a syringe; discard if held in a syringe for more than one hour.
- **Photoactivation:** Leukocyte-enriched blood is exposed to UVA light for a maximum of 90 minutes.
- **Reinfusion:** Photoactivated cells should be reinfused over 15 to 20 minutes.
- **Safety:** Procedures must occur in facilities with standard emergency equipment and available volume replacement fluids.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Drug-Related:**
    - Hypersensitivity to methoxsalen, psoralen compounds, or any excipients.
    - Co-existing melanoma, basal cell, or squamous cell skin carcinoma.
    - Aphakia (due to significantly increased risk of retinal damage).
    - Lactation [Australian].
- **Procedure-Related (Photopheresis):**
    - Photosensitive diseases (e.g., porphyria, systemic lupus erythematosus).
    - Inability to tolerate extracorporeal volume loss (e.g., severe cardiac disease, severe anaemia).
    - White blood cell count > 25,000/mm³.
    - Previous splenectomy.
    - Coagulation disorders.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Special Competence:** Should only be used by physicians with specific training in cGVHD/CTCL and the **THERAKOS CELLEX** system [Australian].
- **Ocular Protection:** Methoxsalen enters the lens and binds irreversibly to proteins/DNA when exposed to UVA. Patients **must** wear wrap-around, UVA-opaque sunglasses during the treatment cycle and for 24 hours post-treatment.
- **Skin Protection:** Patients must avoid sunlight exposure for 24 hours following treatment to prevent serious burns and photosensitivity reactions.
- **Thromboembolism:** Both arterial and venous thromboembolic events (DVT, PE) have been reported, particularly in cGVHD patients [Australian].
- **Alcohol Content:** Contains 4.1% w/v ethanol (40.55 mg/mL). Use with caution in patients with liver disease, alcoholism, or epilepsy.
- **Haemolysis:** Inspect blood visually; do not reinfuse if haemolysis is suspected following equipment alarms.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are generally mild and transient, often related to the underlying condition or the extracorporeal procedure.

| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea, Nausea | Very Common (>10%) |
| **Blood/Lymphatic** | Anaemia | Very Common (>10%) |

### Post-Marketing/Serious Effects
- **Serious:** Anaphylactic reactions, sepsis, endocarditis, exacerbation of congestive heart failure.
- **Procedural:** Vascular access complications, vasovagal spasms, and hypercoagulability.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Metabolism:** Methoxsalen is a potent inhibitor of hepatic microsomal enzymes (CYP1A2, 2A6, 2B1). 
    - **Caffeine:** Clearance is markedly reduced.
    - **Paracetamol:** Metabolic activation is decreased.
- **Protein Binding:** Methoxsalen is moderately bound to albumin.
    - **Tolbutamide:** Can displace methoxsalen, potentially enhancing photosensitivity.
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Phenytoin may increase methoxsalen metabolism, potentially leading to treatment failure.
- **Photosensitising Agents:** Exercise extreme caution with concomitant use of other topically or systemically administered photosensitisers.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Methoxsalen may cause fetal harm. Animal studies show significant fetal toxicity (mortality and skeletal malformations) at high doses. Women of childbearing potential should avoid pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** **Contraindicated [Australian]**. Excretion in human milk is unknown, but safety cannot be assured.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate adverse effects on both male and female reproductive organs at high doses, including reduced sperm counts and follicular damage.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No evidence suggests a need for dose or frequency adjustment in the elderly.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not clinically evaluated. Use with caution; however, reports in renal transplant recipients suggest no extra precautions were required [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not clinically evaluated. Since methoxsalen is hepatically biotransformed, impairment may lead to prolonged photosensitivity (beyond 24 hours).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Methoxsalen intercalates into double-stranded DNA. Upon UVA activation, it forms covalent cross-links with pyrimidine bases (thymine, cytosine, uracil), leading to proliferative arrest and apoptosis of lymphocytes. It may also induce an autoregulatory host immune response.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** High albumin binding (80-90%). Distributes to most organs; concentrations in the lens are proportional to serum levels.
    - **Metabolism:** Nearly complete hepatic biotransformation. 
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 74% is excreted in the urine as metabolites within 48 hours.
    - **Plasma Levels:** 30 minutes post-reinfusion, plasma levels are typically <10 ng/mL.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **cGVHD (Study GVHD-SK1):** A study of 99 patients comparing photopheresis + standard therapy (ST) vs. ST alone. While the photopheresis group showed greater improvement in skin scores, the difference did not reach statistical significance in the primary analysis.
- **CTCL (Study CTCL 3):** An uncontrolled study of 51 patients. 33% achieved a $\geq$ 25% reduction in skin score within 180 days. Overall response rates in literature are cited at approximately 60% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Stability:** The product is for single use only. Discard any unused portion.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
- **Additional Monitoring:** This product is subject to additional monitoring in Australia to allow quick identification of new safety information [Australian].
