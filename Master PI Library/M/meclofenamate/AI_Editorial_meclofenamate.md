# Editorial Product Information: Meclofenamate Sodium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals to provide a concise, clinically accurate summary of the product's profile.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Meclofenamate sodium (a fenamate derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Nonsteroidal Anti-inflammatory Drug (NSAID).
- **Brand Name(s):** Meclofenamate Sodium Capsules [US].
- **ATC Code:** M01AG04.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Hard-shell gelatin capsules containing 50 mg or 100 mg of meclofenamic acid (as the sodium salt) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mylan Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Meclofenamate sodium** is indicated for the following [US-approved]:
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis and Osteoarthritis:** Relief of signs and symptoms of acute and chronic disease.
- **Pain Management:** Relief of mild to moderate pain in adults.
- **Dysmenorrhea:** Treatment of primary dysmenorrhea.
- **Heavy Menstrual Blood Loss:** Treatment of idiopathic heavy menstrual blood loss.
- **Juvenile Arthritis:** Relief of signs and symptoms.
- **Fever:** Reduction of fever in adults.
- **Ankylosing Spondylitis:** Acute or long-term relief of signs and symptoms.
- **Acute Gouty Arthritis:** Relief of signs and symptoms.
- **Acute Painful Shoulder:** Treatment of acute subacromial bursitis or supraspinatus tendinitis.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
The lowest effective dose should be utilised for the shortest duration necessary to achieve patient treatment goals.

### Adult Dosing by Indication [US]
- **Mild to Moderate Pain:** 50 mg every 4 to 6 hours. Some patients may require 100 mg for optimal relief. The total daily dose must not exceed 400 mg.
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis and Osteoarthritis:** 200 mg to 400 mg per day, administered in 3 or 4 equal doses. Therapy should be initiated at a lower dose and titrated based on clinical response.
- **Primary Dysmenorrhea and Heavy Menstrual Blood Loss:** 100 mg three times daily (TID) for up to 6 days, beginning at the onset of menstrual flow.

### Administration Instructions
- If gastrointestinal (GI) intolerance occurs, **meclofenamate sodium** may be administered with meals or milk. However, clinicians should note that administration with food may decrease both the rate and extent of absorption [US].

## 4. Contraindications
**Meclofenamate sodium** is contraindicated in the following [US]:
- Known hypersensitivity to meclofenamate sodium or any component of the formulation.
- History of asthma, urticaria, or allergic-type reactions following the use of aspirin or other NSAIDs.
- In the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery.
- Patients with previous serious skin reactions to NSAIDs (e.g., SJS, TEN, DRESS).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Cardiovascular Thrombotic Events:** NSAIDs cause an increased risk of serious cardiovascular (CV) thrombotic events, including myocardial infarction and stroke, which can be fatal. Risk increases with duration of use and in patients with pre-existing CV disease or risk factors.
- **Gastrointestinal Risk:** NSAIDs cause an increased risk of serious GI adverse events, including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation of the stomach or intestines, which can be fatal. Elderly patients and those with a history of peptic ulcer disease/GI bleeding are at significantly higher risk.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Borderline elevations in liver tests occur in up to 15% of patients. Severe hepatic reactions (jaundice, fatal hepatitis) have been reported (approx. 1%). Discontinue if liver disease develops or systemic manifestations occur [US].
- **Hypertension:** NSAIDs can lead to the onset of new hypertension or worsening of pre-existing hypertension, contributing to CV risk.
- **Heart Failure and Edema:** Fluid retention and edema have been observed; use with caution in patients with heart failure.
- **Renal Effects:** Long-term use may cause renal papillary necrosis. Use is not recommended in patients with advanced renal disease [US].
- **Anaphylactoid Reactions:** May occur in patients without prior exposure; contraindicated in patients with the "aspirin triad."
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) can occur without warning.

### Monitoring Requirements
- Periodic Check: Complete Blood Count (CBC) and chemistry profile for patients on long-term therapy.
- Blood Pressure: Monitor closely during initiation and throughout therapy.
- Renal/Hepatic Function: Monitor in patients with pre-existing impairment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (10%–33%), Nausea with or without vomiting (11%), other general GI disturbances (10%) [US].

### Common (1–10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, pyrosis, flatulence, anorexia, constipation, stomatitis, peptic ulcer.
- **Central Nervous System:** Headache, dizziness.
- **Dermatologic:** Rash, urticaria, pruritus.
- **Special Senses:** Tinnitus.
- **Cardiovascular:** Edema.

### Uncommon (0.1–1%) / Rare (<0.1%) [Causal Relationship Probable]
- **Gastrointestinal:** GI bleeding, perforation, colitis, cholestatic jaundice.
- **Renal:** Renal failure.
- **Hematologic:** Neutropenia, agranulocytosis, hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenic purpura, leukopenia, eosinophilia.
- **Dermatologic:** Erythema multiforme, SJS, TEN, exfoliative dermatitis.
- **Allergic:** Lupus-like symptoms.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **ACE Inhibitors/ARBs:** NSAIDs may diminish the antihypertensive effect of these agents.
- **Aspirin:** Concomitant use increases GI risk. **Meclofenamate sodium** protein binding is reduced by aspirin, though clearance of free drug is unaffected.
- **Diuretics (Furosemide/Thiazides):** Reduced natriuretic effect due to inhibition of renal prostaglandin synthesis.
- **Lithium:** NSAIDs increase plasma lithium levels (approx. 15% increase) and reduce renal clearance (approx. 20% decrease).
- **Methotrexate:** NSAIDs may enhance methotrexate toxicity by inhibiting renal accumulation.
- **Warfarin:** Synergistic effect on GI bleeding; risk is significantly higher than with either drug alone.
- **Antacids:** Concomitant aluminum and magnesium hydroxides do not interfere with absorption [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **General:** Avoid use in pregnant women starting at 30 weeks gestation due to the risk of premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus [US].
    - **20–30 Weeks Gestation:** Limit use to the lowest dose/shortest duration; consider ultrasound monitoring for oligohydramnios if treatment exceeds 48 hours [US].
    - **Labour and Delivery:** NSAIDs may delay parturition and increase the incidence of dystocia (based on animal studies) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Trace amounts are excreted in human milk. A decision should be made based on the importance of the drug to the mother versus the potential risk to the infant [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children under 14 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Caution is required; elderly patients (65+ years) are at higher risk for GI bleeding, renal decompensation, and fatal GI events.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to lack of experience in these populations; close monitoring of renal function is required if therapy is initiated in patients with advanced renal disease [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits prostaglandin synthesis and competes for binding at prostaglandin receptor sites. In vitro, it also inhibits human leukocyte 5-lipoxygenase activity [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma levels in 0.5 to 2 hours. Bioavailability decreases by 26% and Cmax decreases 4-fold if taken 30 minutes after a meal.
    - **Distribution:** >99% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised to an active 3-hydroxymethyl metabolite (Metabolite I) and six minor metabolites. Metabolite I has 20% of the activity of the parent drug.
    - **Elimination:** Approx. 70% renal (primarily as conjugates); 30% faecal. Elimination half-life is 0.8 to 5.3 hours (parent) and approx. 15 hours (Metabolite I) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Trials demonstrated comparable efficacy to aspirin, with fewer reports of tinnitus but a higher incidence of diarrhea.
- **Pain Management:** Significant relief was noted at 50 mg doses in dental and episiotomy pain models, with 100 mg providing additional benefit for some. Onset of action is generally within 1 hour, lasting 4 to 6 hours [US].
- **Heavy Menstrual Blood Loss:** Crossover trials vs. placebo showed reduction in flow in 90% of subjects. Associated with a 1-day reduction in menses duration and decreased sanitary product usage.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from light and moisture. Dispense in tight, light-resistant, child-resistant containers [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx-only). Schedule: Not specified in available sources.
