# Editorial Product Information: Mecobalamin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA)**, Australia.
- **Note on Jurisdictional Sources:** While the user provided two documents, Document 2 (FDA) pertained to **Levocarnitine Injection**, a distinct chemical entity with different indications and pharmacology. To ensure clinical accuracy and avoid hazardous misinformation, this editorial focuses exclusively on **Mecobalamin** as requested in the prompt, using the Australian [TGA] source. Information for Mecobalamin from the US FDA was not available in the provided context.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs). It is a synthesis for reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mecobalamin (a vitamin B12 variant).
- **Chemical Class:** Cobalamins.
- **Brand Name(s):** Biological Therapies Methylcobalamin [TGA].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 10 mg in 2 mL solution for injection [TGA].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) [TGA].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Biological Therapies (Australia) [TGA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cobalamin Deficiency:** Treatment of deficiency that cannot be corrected by oral intake, including cases involving pernicious anaemia (where mecobalamin is disproportionately reduced), malabsorption disorders, gastrectomy, and gastrointestinal pathologies [TGA].
- **Neuropathy:** As an adjunct in the treatment of peripheral neuropathies and diabetic polyneuropathy [TGA].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Administered by slow intramuscular (deep intragluteal) injection. Frequency is determined by the physician based on clinical response and laboratory findings [TGA].
- **Pernicious Anaemia:** Requires long-term therapy, typically monthly injections for life to prevent neurological damage [TGA].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Obtain a sensitivity history prior to use.
    - An intradermal test dose is recommended for patients with suspected cobalamin sensitivity.
    - Administer as soon as possible after removal from the box due to light sensitivity.
    - Single-use only; discard any remaining residue [TGA].
- **Monitoring for Dose Response:** Initial treatment of pernicious anaemia requires monitoring of serum potassium and hematological parameters (see Section 5).

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to Vitamin B12, mecobalamin, or cobalt [TGA].
- Pre-existing hypervitaminosis [TGA].
- Should not be used to treat megaloblastic anaemia of pregnancy without confirming a B12 deficiency (as it is more commonly caused by folate deficiency) [TGA].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis Warning:** Anaphylactic shock and death have been reported following parenteral Vitamin B12. If shock occurs, stop administration immediately and treat with intramuscular adrenaline (1:1000), corticosteroids, and antihistamines [TGA].
- **Diagnostic Masking:** Do not institute therapy until a diagnosis is fully established. Vitamin B12 can mask the symptoms of subacute degeneration of the spinal cord or the true diagnosis of pernicious anaemia [TGA].
- **Hypokalaemia:** Intensive treatment of megaloblastic anaemia can lead to hypokalaemia and cardiac arrest; monitor serum potassium closely during the first 48 hours [TGA].
- **Spinal Cord Lesions:** Deficiency progressing beyond 3 months may result in permanent degenerative spinal cord lesions [TGA].
- **Polycythaemia Vera:** B12 deficiency may suppress symptoms of polycythaemia vera; treatment may unmask this condition [TGA].
- **Laboratory Monitoring:** Obtain baseline hematocrit, reticulocyte count, vitamin B12, folate, and iron levels. Repeat hematocrit and reticulocyte counts daily from day 5–7 of therapy [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Frequency data not explicitly categorized in source documents.*
- **General:** Anaphylactic shock, death, and rare allergic hypersensitivity reactions [TGA].
- **Cardiovascular:** Pulmonary oedema, congestive heart failure (early in treatment), peripheral vascular thrombosis, cardiac arrest, and hypotension [TGA].
- **Nervous System:** Severe dizziness, drowsiness, muscular paralysis, vision problems, and loss of consciousness [TGA].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Mild transient diarrhoea, prolonged abdominal pain, nausea, or vomiting [TGA].
- **Dermatological:** Itching, transitory exanthema [TGA].
- **Hematological:** Polycythaemia vera, hypokalaemia [TGA].
- **Miscellaneous:** Feeling of body swelling, bleeding [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Chloramphenicol:** May impair the therapeutic response to vitamin B12 by suppressing bone marrow [TGA].
- **Folic Acid:** Doses >0.1 mg/day may result in hematologic remission in B12-deficient patients but allow irreversible neurological damage to progress. Large doses of folic acid can also reduce serum B12 concentrations [TGA].
- **Malabsorption Inducers:** Colchicine, para-aminosalicylic acid, and heavy alcohol intake (>2 weeks) may produce B12 malabsorption [TGA].
- **Oral Contraceptives:** May lower serum concentrations of cobalamins [TGA].
- **Laboratory Interference:** Antibiotics, methotrexate, and pyrimethamine invalidate B12 and folate blood assays [TGA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Exempt from the Australian categorisation system. Not recommended unless benefits outweigh potential risks; there are no specific safety studies in pregnant women, though B12 crosses the placenta [TGA].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mecobalamin is distributed into breast milk. Not recommended for breastfeeding mothers unless essential. Deficiency has been noted in infants of vegetarian mothers who breastfeed [TGA].
- **Fertility:** No data available [TGA].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available [TGA].
- **Elderly Use:** No data available [TGA].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments not specified in available sources. Note: Uraemia may impair therapeutic response [TGA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Mecobalamin is an active form of Vitamin B12. It acts in transmethylation processes, specifically converting homocysteine to methionine. It is essential for nucleic acid synthesis, cell reproduction, and the maintenance of myelin throughout the nervous system [TGA].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Transported via transcobalamin I and II binding proteins. The liver is the primary storage organ [TGA].
    - **Metabolism:** Transformed into at least two active co-enzymes (mecobalamin and adenosylcobalamin) [TGA].
    - **Elimination:** 50–98% of an injected dose (100–1000 mcg) is excreted in the urine within 48 hours, mostly within the first 8 hours [TGA].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data provided in the source documents [TGA].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze) [TGA].
- **Light Sensitivity:** Mecobalamin is highly sensitive to light. Protect from light and seal the box immediately after removing a vial [TGA].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [TGA].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Not a scheduled medicine (Poisons Standard) [TGA].
- **United States:** Levocarnitine (provided in Document 2) is Rx-only; however, the regulatory status for Mecobalamin in the US was not available in the provided documents.
