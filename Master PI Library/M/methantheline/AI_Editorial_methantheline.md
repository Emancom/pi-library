# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals, synthesised from official product information.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic), marketed by Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; intervals should never be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Initiation is prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40 to 50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Preparation:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizures:** Reports exist of seizures occurring in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** The safety of oral levocarnitine in patients with renal impairment or ESRD has not been established. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted renally [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Clinical Trial Experience
In clinical trials involving ESRD patients on hemodialysis, the following adverse events were reported at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality):
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), infection (15%), injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), fever (7%), and flu syndrome (25%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), and chest pain (12%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), nausea (8%), and vomiting (15%) [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hypervolemia (5%) [US].
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), and paresthesia (5%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), and dyspnea (7%) [US].

### Less Common/Post-marketing
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and gastritis. Body odor has also been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concurrently with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not explicitly assigned, but descriptive]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women; use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Bovine studies show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing for ESRD patients is specific to post-dialysis administration. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8. While it effectively increases carnitine concentrations, the impact on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
