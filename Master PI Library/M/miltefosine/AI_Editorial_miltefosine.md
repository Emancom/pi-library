# Editorial Product Information: Miltefosine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of miltefosine for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data to facilitate safe and effective prescribing.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Miltefosine (an antileishmanial agent).
- **Chemical Class:** Alkylphosphocholine.
- **Brand Names:** **Impavido** [US].
- **ATC Code:** P05AX05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral capsule: 50 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Profounda, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Miltefosine is indicated for the treatment of the following forms of leishmaniasis in adults and adolescents aged 12 years and older weighing at least 30 kg [US]:
- **Visceral Leishmaniasis:** Caused by *Leishmania donovani*.
- **Cutaneous Leishmaniasis:** Caused by *Leishmania braziliensis*, *Leishmania guyanensis*, and *Leishmania panamensis*.
- **Mucosal Leishmaniasis:** Caused by *Leishmania braziliensis*.

**Limitations of Use:** Efficacy against species other than those listed has not been evaluated. Geographic variation in clinical response may occur within the same *Leishmania* species [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents ≥12 years)
The standard treatment duration is 28 consecutive days. Dosage is determined by body weight:
- **30 kg to 44 kg:** 50 mg (one capsule) twice daily (with breakfast and dinner).
- **45 kg or greater:** 50 mg (one capsule) three times daily (with breakfast, lunch, and dinner).

### Administration Instructions
- Administer with food to improve gastrointestinal tolerability.
- Capsules must be swallowed whole; do not break, crush, or chew.
- Complete the full 28-day course to prevent relapse and resistance.

### Missed Dose
Not specifically addressed in available source; standard clinical practice suggests taking the missed dose as soon as remembered unless it is nearly time for the next dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Miltefosine may cause fetal harm and is strictly contraindicated in pregnant women [US].
- **Sjögren-Larsson Syndrome:** Contraindicated due to a defect in fatty aldehyde dehydrogenase required for miltefosine metabolism [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to miltefosine or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Embryo-Fetal Toxicity [US]
- **Miltefosine is teratogenic.** Animal studies demonstrated fetal death and malformations at doses lower than the human equivalent.
- **Pregnancy Testing:** Obtain a serum or urine pregnancy test prior to initiation.
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during therapy and for **5 months** after completion.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Reproductive Effects:** Animal data show testicular atrophy and impaired fertility. Human male fertility has not been adequately studied; scrotal pain and impaired ejaculation have been reported post-marketing [US].
- **Renal Effects:** Elevations in serum creatinine are common. Monitor renal function weekly during therapy and for 4 weeks post-therapy [US].
- **Hepatic Effects:** Elevations in transaminases (ALT, AST) and bilirubin may occur. Monitor liver function tests during treatment [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Effects:** Vomiting and diarrhea are very common and may lead to volume depletion. Maintain adequate fluid intake.
- **Thrombocytopenia:** Monitor platelet counts, particularly in patients treated for visceral leishmaniasis [US].
- **Absorption of Oral Contraceptives:** Severe vomiting or diarrhea may reduce the efficacy of oral contraceptives. Advise an alternative non-hormonal method of contraception if GI upset occurs.
- **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS):** Discontinue miltefosine immediately if an exfoliative or bullous rash develops.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450 Enzymes:** In vitro and animal studies indicate miltefosine does not markedly induce or inhibit major human CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, 3A4/5) [US].
- **Drug Transporters:** The interaction between miltefosine and drug transporters has not been evaluated [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic:** Potential additive nephrotoxicity if used with other nephrotoxic agents.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **US Category D.** Animal studies show significant teratogenicity (undeveloped cerebrum, cleft palate, skeletal malformations). Use is contraindicated. If a patient becomes pregnant, they should be encouraged to enroll in the pregnancy registry [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if miltefosine is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be avoided during therapy and for **5 months** after the final dose [US].
- **Fertility:** Evidence of impaired fertility in both male and female rats. Reversibility in humans is not established.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <12 years of age have not been established. Juvenile rats showed increased sensitivity to retinal and kidney toxicity [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Insufficient data from clinical trials to determine if patients ≥65 years respond differently from younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Patients with Cr/BUN ≥1.5x ULN were excluded from trials. No specific dosing adjustments are provided; caution and monitoring are required [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Patients with AST/ALT ≥3x ULN or Bilirubin ≥2x ULN were excluded from trials. Pharmacokinetics in hepatic impairment have not been studied [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Not fully elucidated. Likely involves interaction with Leishmania cell lipids (phospholipids/sterols), inhibition of mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase, and induction of apoptosis-like cell death.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is unknown. Slow absorption; Tmax is approximately 4–12 hours.
    - **Distribution:** High plasma protein binding (98%). 
    - **Metabolism:** Slow oxidative metabolism is not observed via CYP450. Metabolic breakdown likely involves phospholipase D-like cleavage to choline and fatty acids.
    - **Elimination:** Extremely long terminal half-life (>6 days). Urinary excretion is negligible (<0.2%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Visceral Leishmaniasis (India):** A Phase 3 trial (N=299) showed a final cure rate of 94% for miltefosine compared to 97% for amphotericin B deoxycholate [US].
- **Cutaneous Leishmaniasis (Colombia/Guatemala):** A placebo-controlled trial showed cure rates of 66% for miltefosine vs. 30% for placebo [US].
- **Mucosal Leishmaniasis (Bolivia):** A single-arm study (N=79) showed a 62% resolution of mucosal lesions at 12 months [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture. Dispense only in the original carton (blister packs) [US].
