# Editorial Product Information: Metoclopramide and Paracetamol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to local regulatory guidelines for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Metoclopramide hydrochloride and Paracetamol.
- **Chemical Class:** Metoclopramide (Dopamine antagonist/Antiemetic); Paracetamol (Aniline-derivative analgesic).
- **Brand Name(s):** Anagraine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N02BE51 (Paracetamol, combinations excluding psycholeptics).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral tablet containing 5 mg metoclopramide hydrochloride and 500 mg paracetamol [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Migraine Management:** Symptomatic relief of headache, nausea, and vomiting associated with migraine attacks [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** One or two tablets administered at the first sign of a migraine attack.
- **Dosing Frequency:** If symptoms persist, doses may be repeated every four hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** Total dosage must not exceed six tablets (30 mg metoclopramide / 3000 mg paracetamol) within a 24-hour period [Australian].
- **Weight-Based Considerations:** To mitigate the risk of dystonic reactions, the total daily dose of metoclopramide should generally not exceed 0.5 mg/kg body weight, particularly in young adults and adolescents [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known intolerance to paracetamol, metoclopramide, or any tablet excipients.
- **Gastrointestinal Risk:** Conditions where increased gastric motility is hazardous, including mechanical obstruction, perforation, or gastrointestinal haemorrhage.
- **Neurological Conditions:** Epilepsy (metoclopramide may increase the frequency and severity of seizures).
- **Endocrine Disorders:** Phaeochromocytoma (risk of hypertensive crisis due to potential catecholamine release).
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Patients with porphyria [Australian].
- **Drug Interactions:** Concurrent use with other medications known to induce extrapyramidal reactions.
- **Age Restriction:** Contraindicated in children and adolescents under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Pregnancy/Lactation:** Use is contraindicated due to insufficient safety data for the combination [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Tardive Dyskinesia:** May occur during or after discontinuing metoclopramide, especially in elderly females on high-dose therapy. This condition may be irreversible and has no known effective treatment. Therapy should be discontinued if symptoms (e.g., involuntary tongue or facial movements) appear.
- **Extrapyramidal Symptoms:** Dystonic reactions occur in approximately 1% of patients, often after a single dose. Risk is higher in younger populations.
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** Rare but potentially fatal. Characterised by hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, and autonomic instability. Discontinue treatment immediately if NMS is suspected.
- **High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (HAGMA):** Reported with paracetamol use in patients with risk factors for glutathione deficiency (e.g., sepsis, malnutrition, chronic alcoholism) or concomitant use of flucloxacillin.
- **Hyperprolactinaemia:** Metoclopramide elevates prolactin levels; caution is advised in patients with a history of breast cancer.
- **Surgical Precaution:** Withhold treatment for 3–4 days following procedures such as pyloroplasty or gut anastomosis, as vigorous contractions may impede healing.
- **Parkinson’s Disease:** Metoclopramide may exacerbate symptoms and should be used with extreme caution.
- **Masking of Underlying Disease:** Symptomatic relief may delay the diagnosis of serious conditions (e.g., cerebral irritation).

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (>10%)** | Restlessness, drowsiness, fatigue, lassitude. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Dystonic reactions (extrapyramidal), insomnia, headache, dizziness, nausea, bowel disturbances. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Acute depression, anxiety, agitation, parkinsonian symptoms (tremor, rigidity), hepatotoxicity (when used with other hepatotoxic drugs). |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome, AV block. |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Tardive dyskinesia, methaemoglobinaemia, sulfhaemoglobinaemia, HAGMA, hypersensitivity (urticaria, rash), haematological reactions (neutropenia, agranulocytosis), galactorrhoea, gynaecomastia, sexual dysfunction. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anticholinergics and Opioids:** Antagonise the prokinetic effects of metoclopramide on gastrointestinal motility.
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive sedative effects when combined with alcohol, sedatives, or hypnotics.
- **Gastrointestinal Motility Effects:** Metoclopramide may increase the absorption rate of small-bowel absorbed drugs (e.g., paracetamol, tetracycline, levodopa) and decrease the absorption of stomach-absorbed drugs (e.g., digoxin).
- **Anticoagulants:** Prolonged paracetamol use may require a reduction in anticoagulant dosage.
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Alcohol and anticonvulsants may increase the risk of paracetamol-induced hepatotoxicity.
- **Flucloxacillin:** Increased risk of HAGMA when used concurrently with paracetamol [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category A. While both individual components are Category A, there is insufficient safety data for the specific combination product; therefore, its use is not supported [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Both paracetamol and metoclopramide are excreted in breast milk. Paracetamol is generally considered safe in standard doses, but metoclopramide may increase the risk of adverse reactions in the infant. Use of the combination is not recommended during lactation [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children and adolescents under 18 years due to increased risk of extrapyramidal effects [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of tardive dyskinesia. Specific dosing data for the combination in the elderly is not available in the source document.
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance of metoclopramide is reduced; paracetamol conjugates may accumulate in moderate-to-severe impairment. Use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to reduced metoclopramide clearance and potential paracetamol toxicity [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Metoclopramide:** Stimulates upper GI motility and increases the rate of gastric emptying; acts as a dopamine antagonist with antiemetic properties.
    - **Paracetamol:** Provides analgesic relief.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Metoclopramide enhances the speed of paracetamol absorption by accelerating gastric emptying [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available source.
