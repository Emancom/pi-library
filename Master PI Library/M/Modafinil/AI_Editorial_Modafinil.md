# Editorial Product Information: Modafinil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **modafinil** for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements from the provided source.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing in a particular region. Clinicians should consult local regulations and current official labeling.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Modafinil (a wakefulness-promoting agent).
- **Chemical Class:** 2-[(diphenylmethyl)sulfinyl] acetamide.
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-MODAFINIL [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 100 mg capsule-shaped, biconvex, uncoated tablets [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Modafinil** is indicated for the following:
- Improvement of wakefulness in patients experiencing excessive daytime sleepiness associated with **narcolepsy**.
- Treatment of excessive sleepiness associated with moderate to severe chronic **shift work sleep disorder (SWSD)**, specifically where non-pharmacological interventions have failed or are unsuitable.
- Adjunctive treatment to continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) in **obstructive sleep apnoea/hypopnoea syndrome (OSAHS)** to improve wakefulness.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Range:** 200 mg to 400 mg daily.
- **Administration:** Tablets should be swallowed whole. 

### Dosing by Indication
- **Narcolepsy and OSAHS:** 200 mg to 400 mg per day. This may be taken as a single morning dose or split into two doses (morning and noon). While 400 mg is well-tolerated, there is no consistent evidence of additional benefit over 200 mg.
- **Shift Work Sleep Disorder:** 200 mg taken as a single dose approximately one hour before the start of the work shift.

### Titration and Instructions
- Dose increases should occur in 100 mg increments.
- **OSAHS Note:** CPAP must be the primary treatment; **modafinil** is only an adjunct. Compliance with CPAP should be periodically assessed.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **modafinil** or any of its excipients (including lactose).
- **Pregnancy:** **Modafinil** is strictly contraindicated during pregnancy [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Rare but life-threatening rashes, including **Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS)**, **Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN)**, and **Drug Rash with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS)**, have been reported. Discontinue at the first sign of rash.
- **Psychiatric Symptoms:** Monitor for *de novo* or worsening psychiatric disorders (e.g., psychosis, mania, aggression, or suicidal ideation). Discontinue if such symptoms develop.
- **Multi-organ Hypersensitivity:** Rare, potentially life-threatening reactions may involve fever, rash, and internal organ involvement (e.g., myocarditis, hepatitis). Discontinue immediately if suspected.
- **Cardiovascular Risk:** Use with caution in patients with a history of left ventricular hypertrophy, ischaemic ECG changes, chest pain, or arrhythmias. ECG monitoring is recommended prior to initiation.
- **Contraceptive Interference:** **Modafinil** reduces the effectiveness of steroidal contraceptives. Patients must use alternative/concomitant contraception during treatment and for **two months** following discontinuation.
- **Abuse and Dependence:** Caution in patients with a history of substance abuse. **Modafinil** has potential for psychoactive/euphoric effects and is considered a stimulant-like agent.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Cardiovascular:** Rare reports of ischaemic heart disease and arrhythmias (e.g., atrial fibrillation).
- **Psychiatric:** Rare reports of suicidal ideation and psychosis.
- **Dermatologic:** Very rare cases of TEN, SJS, and DRESS.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP2C19 Inhibition:** **Modafinil** may increase plasma levels of drugs metabolised by this pathway, including **diazepam**, **phenytoin**, **propranolol**, and certain **SSRIs/TCAs**.
- **CYP3A4 Induction:** **Modafinil** modestly induces CYP3A4, reducing levels of **cyclosporin**, **theophylline**, and **steroidal contraceptives**.
- **CYP2C9 Suppression:** *In vitro* data suggests a risk for **warfarin** and **phenytoin**; monitoring of prothrombin time is recommended for **warfarin** co-administration.
- **Methylphenidate:** May delay **modafinil** absorption by approximately one hour.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **MAO Inhibitors:** Caution is advised; specific interaction studies have not been performed.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Contraindicated. Post-marketing data suggests an increased risk of major congenital malformations (estimated at 17.3% compared to 3% in the general population) and spontaneous abortion. A negative pregnancy test is required within one week of starting treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. While confirmed in animal milk, it is unknown if **modafinil** is excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility:** No observed effects in animal studies at investigated doses, though study limitations were noted.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not approved or recommended. Increased incidence of skin and neuropsychiatric reactions observed in clinical trials.
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** Lower doses should be considered due to reduced drug clearance.
- **Renal Impairment:** No significant pharmacokinetic change in severe chronic renal failure (CrCl ≤ 20 mL/min), but metabolite (modafinil acid) exposure increases 9-fold. Safety data in this population is limited.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In severe hepatic impairment, the dose **must be reduced by 50%**.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Precise mechanism is unknown. It binds to the dopamine reuptake site with low affinity, increasing extracellular dopamine without triggering dopamine release. It is distinct from sympathomimetic amines.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Improves wakefulness and alertness; opposes cognitive and memory impairment induced by sleep deprivation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Slow; Tmax reached in ~3 hours. Food may delay absorption by ~1 hour but does not affect overall bioavailability.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution (~0.9 L/kg); 62% protein bound (primarily to albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via hydrolytic deamination and S-oxidation. The chief metabolite is inactive modafinil acid.
    - **Elimination:** Chiefly renal (80% as metabolites, <10% unchanged). Elimination half-life is 10–12 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Narcolepsy:** Two 9-week trials demonstrated significant improvement in sleep latency (MWT) and overall disease status (CGI-C) at doses of 200 mg and 400 mg.
- **OSAHS:** Trials confirmed efficacy in improving wakefulness (MWT and ESS) when used as an adjunct to CPAP over 12 weeks.
- **SWSD:** A 12-week study (n=209) showed significant improvement in sleep latency (MSLT) and CGI-C when 200 mg was taken before night shifts.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Store in the original container to protect from moisture.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
