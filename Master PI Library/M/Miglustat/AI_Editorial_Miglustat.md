# Editorial Product Information: Miglustat

## Editorial Note
- **Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial:** Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **miglustat** (specifically in the context of combination therapy with cipaglucosidase alfa) for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis of available regulatory documents and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Miglustat (an N-alkylated iminosugar; a synthetic analog of D-glucose).
- **Brand Name(s):** **OPFOLDA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AX06.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Hard gelatin capsule containing 65 mg of miglustat. The capsule features a grey opaque cap and a white opaque body, printed with “AT2221” in black ink.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amicus Therapeutics Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Late-Onset Pompe Disease (LOPD):** Indicated as an enzyme stabiliser for use in combination with cipaglucosidase alfa for long-term enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) in adults with acid $\alpha$-glucosidase (GAA) deficiency [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
**Miglustat** must be used exclusively in combination with cipaglucosidase alfa. The recommended dose is administered orally every other week based on patient weight:
- **$\ge$ 50 kg:** 260 mg (4 capsules) every other week.
- **$\ge$ 40 kg to < 50 kg:** 195 mg (3 capsules) every other week.

### Administration Instructions
- **Fasting Requirement:** Patients must fast for at least 2 hours before and 2 hours after taking the dose. Only water should be consumed during this period.
- **Administration:** Capsules must be swallowed whole with water only.
- **Co-administration Timeline:** Cipaglucosidase alfa infusion should ideally start 1 hour after **miglustat** ingestion. The infusion must start no later than 3 hours after the oral dose.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If the cipaglucosidase alfa infusion is delayed by more than 3 hours after taking **miglustat**, the entire treatment must be rescheduled for at least 24 hours later.
- If the **miglustat** dose is missed, the cipaglucosidase alfa infusion should not be started. Both medicines should be administered at the next available opportunity following the protocol timeline.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **miglustat** or any of the excipients (including sucralose and sulfites).
- Any contraindication applicable to cipaglucosidase alfa.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **General Warnings:** Adverse drug reactions may occur when used in combination with cipaglucosidase alfa (referred to as POMBILITI in some jurisdictions).
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Patient response should be routinely evaluated based on clinical manifestations. If response is insufficient or safety risks are intolerable, discontinuation of the combination therapy should be considered.
- **Intestinal Enzyme Inhibition:** **Miglustat** inhibits intestinal disaccharidases (sucrase, maltase, isomaltase). This can lead to maldigestion of carbohydrates, osmotic water influx, and fermentation, resulting in gastrointestinal distress if the fasting requirements are not strictly followed.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported during combination therapy with cipaglucosidase alfa (based on the PROPEL trial and extension studies):

| Frequency | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Diarrhoea, nausea, abdominal pain, vomiting, flatulence, abdominal distension, constipation, fatigue, pyrexia, chills, dizziness, tremor, somnolence, dysgeusia, paraesthesia, tachycardia, flushing, hypotension, dyspnoea, cough, urticaria, rash, pruritus, hyperhidrosis, muscle spasms, myalgia, arthralgia, muscular weakness, peripheral swelling, increased blood pressure. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypersensitivity, migraine, presyncope, balance disorder, asthma, pharyngeal oedema, dyspepsia, oesophageal pain, dysphagia, skin discolouration, flank pain, malaise. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** The most frequent serious reactions include urticaria (2.0%), anaphylaxis (1.3%), and respiratory symptoms such as wheezing and dyspnoea (0.7%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450:** In vitro data indicate no relevant inhibitory effects on CYP1A2, 2A6, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, or 3A4.
- **Transporters:** **Miglustat** does not inhibit major transporters (OAT, OCT, P-gp, BCRP). It is a substrate for OCT1 (liver), OCT2 (kidney), and a weak substrate for P-gp.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** OCT2 inhibition may increase **miglustat** and cipaglucosidase alfa exposure (up to 20%), but this is not considered clinically meaningful.
- **Food Interaction:** Consumption of sucrose, maltose, or isomaltose shortly after dosing may lead to gastrointestinal symptoms due to enzyme inhibition.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D** [Australian]: **Miglustat** crosses the placenta. Animal studies show reproductive toxicity, including cardiovascular malformations in rabbits.
    - **Recommendation:** Not recommended during pregnancy. Women of childbearing potential must use reliable contraception during treatment and for 4 weeks after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Secretion in human milk is unknown, though animal studies show excretion in rat milk. Due to potential risks to the infant, breastfeeding should be discontinued during therapy.
- **Fertility:** No effect on sperm parameters was observed in human males (100 mg BID for 6 weeks). Animal studies showed reversible decreased spermatogenesis in rats at high doses.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients under 18 years of age.
- **Elderly Use:** Limited experience in patients $\ge$ 65 years. No dose adjustment is currently required.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not clinically evaluated in this population. However, pharmacokinetic modelling suggests that 260 mg every two weeks will not result in significant accumulation; thus, no dose adjustment is recommended even in severe renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Safety and efficacy have not been evaluated; no specific dose recommendations are available.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Miglustat** acts as a pharmacokinetic enzyme stabiliser (chaperone). It binds selectively to cipaglucosidase alfa in the blood during infusion, stabilising its conformation and minimising activity loss while in circulation. Binding is transient; the complex dissociates upon reaching the acidic environment of the lysosome.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ is 2–3 hours. Food decreases $C_{max}$ by 36% and delays absorption, necessitating the fasting window.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is ~90 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Largely unmetabolised (<5% as glucuronides).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (82.8% recovered in urine). Terminal half-life is approximately 6 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trial (PROPEL):** A 52-week, Phase 3, randomised, double-blind study (n=122) compared **miglustat** + cipaglucosidase alfa against alglucosidase alfa + placebo.
- **Primary Outcome:** The combination group showed a mean improvement of 20.8 metres in the 6-Minute Walk Distance (6MWD) compared to 7.2 metres in the control group. While the overall population did not reach statistical superiority (p=0.071), ERT-experienced subjects showed a statistically significant benefit (p=0.047).
- **Secondary Outcome:** Forced Vital Capacity (FVC) showed a mean change of -0.9% in the combination group vs. -4.0% in the control group, representing a treatment effect of 3.0%.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Store in the original container. Do not use if the inner seal is broken.
- **Shelf Life:** Once the bottle is opened, the capsules should be used within 6 months.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
