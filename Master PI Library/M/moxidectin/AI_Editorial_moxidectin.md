# Editorial Product Information: Lotilaner, Moxidectin, Praziquantel, and Pyrantel

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Note on Australian context**: While a full Australian PI was not provided, the US source document references data from an Australian field study regarding lotilaner. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, specifically veterinarians and veterinary pharmacists.
- This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing or dispensing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients**: 
    - **Lotilaner**: An ectoparasiticide of the isoxazoline class.
    - **Moxidectin**: A semisynthetic macrocyclic lactone endectocide.
    - **Praziquantel**: An isoquinolone anthelmintic.
    - **Pyrantel (as pamoate salt)**: A tetrahydropyrimidine nematocide.
- **Brand Names**: **Credelio Quattro**; **Credelio Quattro-CA1** (conditionally approved for specific indications) [US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: Flavored chewable tablets available in five strengths [US]:
    - 56.25 mg lotilaner / 0.056 mg moxidectin / 14.25 mg praziquantel / 14.25 mg pyrantel
    - 112.5 mg lotilaner / 0.113 mg moxidectin / 28.5 mg praziquantel / 28.5 mg pyrantel
    - 225 mg lotilaner / 0.225 mg moxidectin / 57 mg praziquantel / 57 mg pyrantel
    - 450 mg lotilaner / 0.45 mg moxidectin / 114 mg praziquantel / 114 mg pyrantel
    - 900 mg lotilaner / 0.9 mg moxidectin / 228 mg praziquantel / 228 mg pyrantel
- **Route of Administration**: Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Elanco US Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Heartworm Disease**: Prevention of disease caused by *Dirofilaria immitis* [US].
- **Ectoparasites**: 
    - Treatment and prevention of flea infestations (*Ctenocephalides felis*) [US].
    - Treatment and control of tick infestations: *Amblyomma americanum* (lone star tick), *Dermacentor variabilis* (American dog tick), *Ixodes scapularis* (black-legged tick), *Rhipicephalus sanguineus* (brown dog tick), and *Haemaphysalis longicornis* (longhorned tick) [US].
    - Prevention of *Borrelia burgdorferi* (Lyme disease) infections via killing of *Ixodes scapularis* vector ticks [US].
- **Endoparasites (Intestinal)**: 
    - Roundworm: Treatment and control of *Toxocara canis* (adult and immature adult) and *Toxascaris leonina* (adult) [US].
    - Hookworm: Treatment and control of *Ancylostoma caninum* (L4 larvae, immature adult, and adult) and *Uncinaria stenocephala* (adult) [US].
    - Tapeworm: Treatment and control of *Dipylidium caninum*, *Taenia pisiformis*, and *Echinococcus granulosus* [US].
- **New World Screwworm (NWS)**: Treatment of infestations caused by *Cochliomyia hominivorax* larvae (myiasis) [US: Conditional approval pending full demonstration of effectiveness].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing**: Administered orally once monthly at the following minimum dose levels [US]:
    - Lotilaner: 20 mg/kg (9 mg/lb)
    - Moxidectin: 0.02 mg/kg (0.009 mg/lb)
    - Praziquantel: 5 mg/kg (2.28 mg/lb)
    - Pyrantel: 5 mg/kg (2.28 mg/lb)
- **Administration Instructions**: **Must be administered with food** or within 30 minutes of feeding to ensure adequate lotilaner bioavailability [US].
- **Vomiting**: If vomiting occurs within one hour of administration, redosing is required [US].
- **Missed Dose**: Administer immediately and resume the regular monthly schedule [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- There are no known contraindications for the use of this product [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Human Safety**: Not for human use. Keep out of reach of children. Wash hands after handling. Seek medical attention immediately if accidentally ingested [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions**: Lotilaner is an isoxazoline. This class is associated with neurologic adverse events including tremors, ataxia, and seizures. Use with caution in dogs with a history of seizures or neurologic disorders [US].
- **Heartworm Testing**: Dogs should be tested for existing heartworm infection prior to administration. This product is not effective against adult *D. immitis* [US].
- **NWS Myiasis Treatment**: Use in conjunction with mechanical removal of larvae and appropriate wound care [US].
- **Animal Safety**: Store securely to prevent accidental ingestion by dogs, cats, or other animals [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Concurrent Medication**: Field studies demonstrated safe concurrent use with vaccines, anthelmintics, antimicrobials, antiemetics, steroids, NSAIDs, anesthetics, and analgesics. No adverse interactions were identified [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy/Breeding**: Safety has not been evaluated in breeding, pregnant, or lactating dogs [US].
- **Fertility**: No data available in source documents.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Indicated for dogs and puppies 8 weeks of age and older, weighing at least 3.3 lbs (1.5 kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Clinical signs (vomiting, ataxia, nystagmus) were noted in one geriatric dog in field trials, which resolved within 24 hours [US].
- **Avermectin Sensitive (Collie) Safety**: Safety was demonstrated in healthy avermectin-sensitive Collies at up to 5X the maximum dose, though dose-dependent salivation and vomiting were observed [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**:
    - **Lotilaner**: Inhibits insect/acarine GABA-gated chloride channels, causing uncontrolled neuromuscular activity and death [US].
    - **Moxidectin**: Interferes with chloride channel-mediated neurotransmission in parasites, leading to paralysis and death [US].
    - **Praziquantel**: Induces rapid influx of calcium ions and tegument disruption in tapeworms, causing muscular paralysis [US].
    - **Pyrantel**: Depolarizing neuromuscular-blocking agent causing paralysis and expulsion of nematodes [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Absorption**: Lotilaner requires food for absorption. Bioavailability: Lotilaner (93%), Praziquantel (41%), Pyrantel (31%) in fed state [US].
    - **Peak Concentration (Tmax)**: Lotilaner (10h), Moxidectin (4.5h), Praziquantel (0.75h), Pyrantel (2.5h) [US].
    - **Elimination Half-life**: Lotilaner (806h/~33.6 days), Moxidectin (634h/~26.4 days), Praziquantel (3.64h), Pyrantel (4.87h) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Heartworm Prevention**: 100% effective in laboratory studies and 0% positive cases in a US field study (n=156) over 11 months [US].
- **Intestinal Parasites**: ≥97% effective against roundworms and hookworms; 100% effective against *E. granulosus* [US].
- **Flea/Tick Efficacy**: 100% flea kill within 24 hours for 36 days. ≥97.1% tick kill at 48 hours for 30–32 days [US].
- **NWS Myiasis (Brazil Study)**: Lotilaner alone demonstrated 100% overall effectiveness at 24 hours via larval expulsion and larvicidal activity [US-referenced study].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 15–25°C (59–77°F).
- **Excursions**: Permitted between 5°C and 40°C (41–104°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life**: Not specified in available sources.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States**: Classified as a Prescription Animal Drug (Rx-only). Approved under NADA # 141-581. The NWS indication (Credelio Quattro-CA1) is under conditional approval [US].
