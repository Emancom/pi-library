# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing. Clinicians should consult local regulations and the most current manufacturer documentation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Titration:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Adjustments:** Dose reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry and vital signs. Plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with a history of seizures, there may be an increase in frequency or severity [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine Limitation:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High doses of oral levocarnitine in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials (ESRD patients on hemodialysis) with a frequency ≥5%, regardless of causality, include:

| Body System | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia, palpitations. |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia. |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, depression, paresthesia. |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis. |
| **Other** | Body odor (transient), anemia, hypercalcemia, hypervolemia. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels frequently after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C equivalent]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, no adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While carnitine is a normal component of human milk, the risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). See Section 3 for specific instructions [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specified in available sources, though clinical studies in ESRD often include older populations.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. Use of oral formulations is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic disorders, carnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, intravenous levocarnitine (20 mg/kg) administered three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to trough levels between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week [US].
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 12 and 24 weeks showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial, as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Legal Classification:** Human Prescription Drug [US].
