# Editorial Product Information: Mefenamic Acid

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **mefenamic acid** for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not serve as a substitute for the official, full Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Mefenamic acid**
- **Chemical Class:** Fenamate series of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory agents (NSAIDs)
- **Brand Names:** Femin [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 250 mg hard gelatin capsules [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Primary Dysmenorrhoea:** Treatment of pain associated with menstruation [Australian-approved].
- **Primary Menorrhagia:** Treatment of heavy menstrual bleeding [Australian-approved].
- **Mild to Moderate Pain:** Short-term relief of pain, specifically dental and soft tissue pain [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children >14 years)
- The standard dose is 500 mg (2 capsules) administered three times daily.
- **Administration:** Should be taken with meals to improve tolerability.
- **Duration:** The lowest effective dose should be used for the shortest possible duration to control symptoms.

### Indication-Specific Instructions [Australian]
- **Dysmenorrhoea:** Initiate 500 mg three times daily at the onset of pain and continue for the usual duration of symptoms.
- **Menorrhagia:** Initiate 500 mg three times daily from the onset of menses. Treatment should not exceed 7 days unless advised by a physician.

### Clinical Monitoring
- Patients on long-term therapy should undergo regular clinical review regarding efficacy, risk factors, and continued necessity of treatment.

## 4. Contraindications
**Mefenamic acid** is contraindicated in the following patient populations [Australian]:
- Hypersensitivity to **mefenamic acid** or any capsule excipients (e.g., lactose).
- History of aspirin- or NSAID-induced bronchospasm, allergic rhinitis, or urticaria (cross-sensitivity).
- Active ulceration or chronic inflammation of the upper or lower gastrointestinal (GI) tract.
- Pre-existing renal disease or impaired renal function.
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- Severe heart failure.
- History of diarrhoea associated with **mefenamic acid** use.
- Peri-operative pain management in the setting of Coronary Artery Bypass Graft (CABG) surgery.
- Children under 14 years of age.
- Pregnancy (third trimester) and breastfeeding.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Cardiovascular Effects
- **Thrombotic Events:** NSAIDs may increase the risk of serious cardiovascular thrombotic events, including fatal myocardial infarction and stroke. Risk may increase with dose and duration of use.
- **Hypertension:** May lead to new-onset hypertension or worsening of pre-existing disease. Blood pressure should be monitored closely during initiation and throughout therapy.
- **Heart Failure:** Fluid retention and oedema have been observed; caution is required in patients with compromised cardiac function.

### Gastrointestinal (GI) Effects
- **GI Toxicity:** Serious and potentially fatal GI inflammation, bleeding, ulceration, and perforation can occur at any time without warning.
- **Risk Factors:** Risk is higher in the elderly, those with a history of GI disease, smokers, those consuming alcohol, and those on concomitant corticosteroids, anticoagulants, or SSRIs.
- **Action:** If GI bleeding or ulceration occurs, the drug must be withdrawn immediately.

### Severe Skin Reactions
- **NSAIDs** can rarely cause fatal reactions including Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) syndrome.
- Therapy should be discontinued at the first appearance of skin rash or mucosal lesions.

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Diarrhoea:** If diarrhoea occurs, the medication should be discontinued immediately, as it may be severe and may recur upon re-exposure.
- **Asthma:** Use with caution in patients with known asthma.
- **Contraception:** Physicians should be alert to a potential reduction in the efficacy of intrauterine contraceptive devices (IUCDs).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Gastrointestinal (Most Common)
- **Diarrhoea:** Occurs in approximately 5% of patients; often dose-related and requires discontinuation if severe [Australian].
- **Common:** Nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain.
- **Less Frequent:** Anorexia, flatulence, constipation, enterocolitis, and pyrosis.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Haematologic:** Auto-immune haemolytic anaemia (with continuous use ≥12 months; reversible), leukopenia, eosinophilia, thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis, and pancytopenia.
- **Hepatic:** Borderline elevations of LFTs (up to 15% of patients); rare cases of fatal hepatitis or cholestatic jaundice.
- **Renal:** Acute interstitial nephritis, papillary necrosis, and nephrotic syndrome.
- **Neurological:** Aseptic meningitis, convulsions, and blurred vision.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and angioedema.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Aspirin:** **Mefenamic acid** interferes with the anti-platelet effect of low-dose aspirin.
- **Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** Increases risk of GI bleeding. **Mefenamic acid** may displace warfarin from protein binding sites, enhancing the anticoagulant effect. Frequent prothrombin time/INR monitoring is required.
- **Anti-hypertensives:** May reduce the efficacy of ACE inhibitors, AIIAs, diuretics, and beta-blockers. Co-administration in patients with compromised renal function (elderly/dehydrated) increases the risk of acute renal failure.
- **Lithium:** Reduces renal clearance of lithium, increasing the risk of toxicity.
- **Methotrexate:** May increase plasma levels of methotrexate, increasing toxicity risk.
- **Ciclosporin/Tacrolimus:** Increases the risk of nephrotoxicity.
- **Corticosteroids:** Increased risk of GI ulceration or bleeding.
- **Hypoglycaemic Agents:** Potential changes in the effects of insulin or oral hypoglycaemics.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** Category C [Australian].
- **First/Second Trimester:** Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk; use lowest dose for shortest duration. Data suggests increased risk of miscarriage and malformations.
- **Third Trimester:** **Contraindicated.** Risks include premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus, fetal renal impairment (oligohydramnios), and inhibition of platelet aggregation.
- **Breastfeeding:** **Contraindicated.** Trace amounts are excreted in breast milk and may affect the infant's cardiovascular system.
- **Fertility:** May delay or prevent ovulation; withdrawal should be considered in women having difficulty conceiving.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Safety and efficacy in children under 14 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Increased risk of GI complications and renal failure. Monitor renal function closely.
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in pre-existing renal disease or significant impairment. Elimination is primarily renal.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment; use with caution in mild-to-moderate cases.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Dual action involving the inhibition of prostaglandin synthetase (COX enzymes) and antagonism of prostaglandin action at receptor sites.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels reached in 2 to 4 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Highly bound to plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Predominantly mediated by CYP2C9 in the liver. Two main metabolites: hydroxymethyl and carboxy derivatives.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 67% excreted in urine (as drug or metabolites); 20-25% excreted in faeces. Half-life is approximately 2 hours.
