# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document was developed using official Product Information from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This is a harmonised editorial synthesis intended for use by healthcare professionals. It provides a structured overview of the clinical properties, dosing, and safety profile of intravenous levocarnitine.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 [Not explicitly specified in source; inferred from standard classification].
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects (e.g., glutaric aciduria II, methyl malonic aciduria, propionic acidemia, medium-chain fatty acylCoA dehydrogenase deficiency) that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Standard Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [United States].
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours. Doses should not be spaced further than every 6 hours [United States].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response [United States].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [United States].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [United States].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma carnitine levels. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [United States].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [United States].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Not specified as an absolute contraindication in the provided source, but the document notes that "None known" are established for this product [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, typically within minutes of intravenous administration. These are reported most frequently in ESRD patients on dialysis [United States].
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in the frequency or severity of events has been observed [United States].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Regular monitoring (weekly and monthly) should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>5% in ESRD trials):**
    - **General:** Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain [United States].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia [United States].
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea, abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting [United States].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnea, rhinitis [United States].
    - **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Dizziness, insomnia, depression [United States].
- **Less Frequent/Post-marketing:**
    - Body odor and gastritis [United States].
    - Seizures [United States].
    - Anaphylaxis and other severe hypersensitivity reactions [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not assigned in text]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Animal studies (dairy cows) show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is established for metabolic disorders and ESRD; specific dosing is based on mg/kg weight [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for ESRD patients. However, the safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency, as chronic high doses may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating oxidation and energy production [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed that trough carnitine concentrations (baseline ~21 µmol/L) increased to steady-state levels between 54 and 180 µmol/L after 3 doses. However, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in this population has not been definitively determined [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [United States].
- **General Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing. The product contains no preservatives; discard unused portions [United States].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [United States].
