# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Note:** At the time of this writing, only the United States Product Information was provided for review. Consequently, jurisdictional comparisons (e.g., with Australian, European, or Canadian sources) are not included. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acids (amino acid derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic/Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from metabolic errors [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Max Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, primarily in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon a severe reaction.
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.
- **General Precautions:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide).

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L).
- **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels (normal range 40 to 50 µmol/L).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology (e.g., ESRD, metabolic errors).

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), flu syndrome (15–29%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), abdominal pain (5–21%), fever (5–12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), rhinitis (6–11%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).

### Common (1–10%)
- **Body as a Whole:** Chest pain, asthenia, back pain, accidental injury.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, palpitations, arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation.
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes.
- **Other:** Gastritis, body odor (reported as less frequent but noted).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Seizures (neurologic).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned letter, but data provided]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the maternal benefits. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered.
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) for metabolic disorders; safety and efficacy are integrated into general recommendations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific dosing provided for ESRD patients on hemodialysis (see Section 3). Oral levocarnitine should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production.

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
- **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours.
- **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased pre-dialysis carnitine levels from 20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcome Note:** While IV levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in this population has not been definitively determined.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) when stored in PVC bags at room temperature.
