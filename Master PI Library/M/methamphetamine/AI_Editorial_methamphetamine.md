# Editorial Product Information: Methamphetamine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of methamphetamine hydrochloride based on official Product Information from the United States. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a clinically accurate, structured reference.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methamphetamine hydrochloride (a central nervous system stimulant of the amphetamine class).
- **Brand Name(s):** Desoxyn (Referenced generically in provided source as Methamphetamine Hydrochloride Tablets, USP) [US].
- **ATC Code:** N06BA03
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Tablets:** 5 mg [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD):** Indicated as an integral part of a total treatment program for ADHD in pediatric patients aged 6 years and older [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Pediatric 6 years and older):**
    - **Initial Dose:** 5 mg once or twice daily [US].
    - **Titration:** Dosage may be increased in 5 mg increments at weekly intervals until optimum clinical response is achieved [US].
    - **Maintenance Range:** The usual effective dosage range is 20 mg to 25 mg daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Administer orally once daily or in two divided doses daily [US].
    - Avoid administration late in the evening to minimise the risk of insomnia [US].
- **Dosage Modifications:** 
    - Adjustments may be required when co-administered with agents that alter urinary pH [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to amphetamine or any component of the formulation (angioedema and anaphylaxis have occurred with other amphetamines) [US].
- Concomitant use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use of an MAOI within the preceding 14 days, due to the risk of hypertensive crisis [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Abuse, Misuse, and Addiction [US]:** 
    - Methamphetamine has a high potential for abuse and misuse, which can lead to substance use disorders including addiction. 
    - Misuse and abuse can result in overdose and death; risks are increased with unapproved routes (snorting/injection). 
    - Clinicians must assess risk prior to prescribing and monitor patients throughout treatment [US].
- **Serious Cardiac Disease:** Use should be avoided in patients with known structural cardiac abnormalities, cardiomyopathy, serious arrhythmias, or coronary artery disease. Sudden death has been reported in these populations at recommended dosages [US].
- **Blood Pressure and Heart Rate:** Monitor for increases in heart rate (mean 3–6 bpm) and blood pressure (mean 2–4 mmHg) [US].
- **Psychiatric Adverse Reactions:** May exacerbate pre-existing psychosis or induce manic/mixed episodes in patients with bipolar disorder. New-onset psychotic or manic symptoms (e.g., hallucinations, mania) can occur in patients without a prior history at recommended doses [US].
- **Suppression of Growth:** Monitor height and weight in pediatric patients. Treatment interruption may be necessary if growth velocity is not maintained [US].
- **Peripheral Vasculopathy:** Includes Raynaud’s phenomenon. Digital changes should be monitored; very rare sequelae include digital ulceration or soft tissue breakdown [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** A potentially life-threatening risk, especially when combined with other serotonergic agents (SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans) or CYP2D6 inhibitors [US].
- **Seizures:** May lower the convulsive threshold. Discontinue if seizures occur [US].
- **Tics and Tourette’s Syndrome:** Assess for tics or family history before initiation; monitor for emergence or worsening of symptoms [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Adverse Effects:** Dizziness, insomnia, tremor, headache, palpitations, diarrhea, and xerostomia (dry mouth) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, elevation of blood pressure, cardiomyopathy (with chronic use), and rare fatal cardiorespiratory arrest [US].
- **Central Nervous System:** Restlessness, euphoria, dysphoria, overstimulation, dyskinesia, fatigue, and aggression [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Constipation, unpleasant taste, intestinal ischemia, and abdominal pain [US].
- **Endocrine/Metabolic:** Impotence, changes in libido, frequent/prolonged erections, and weight loss [US].
- **Dermatologic/Allergic:** Urticaria, rash, alopecia; serious reactions such as Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis have been reported [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Sudden death, myocardial infarction, stroke, and rhabdomyolysis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **MAOIs:** Contraindicated; risk of hypertensive crisis [US].
    - **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome; monitor closely [US].
    - **Guanethidine:** Methamphetamine may decrease the hypotensive effect of guanethidine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Alkalinizing Agents (e.g., Sodium Bicarbonate):** Increase blood levels and potentiate amphetamine action [US].
    - **Acidifying Agents (e.g., Ascorbic Acid):** Decrease blood levels and efficacy [US].
    - **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** May increase exposure and risk of serotonin syndrome [US].
    - **Tricyclic Antidepressants:** May increase d-amphetamine brain concentration and potentiate cardiovascular effects [US].
    - **Gastric pH Modulators:** May decrease the time to maximum concentration (Tmax) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified by letter, but source indicates risk of fetal harm [US].
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Infants born to mothers taking amphetamines have increased risk of premature delivery and low birth weight. Neonates may experience withdrawal symptoms (e.g., agitation, excessive drowsiness) [US].
    - **Pregnancy Registry:** National Pregnancy Registry for ADHD Medications is available for monitoring outcomes [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Methamphetamine and its metabolite (amphetamine) are present in human milk. Due to potential serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, breastfeeding is not recommended [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate irregular estrous cycles and decreased sperm density/motility; however, clinical significance in humans is unclear [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in patients aged 6 to 17 years for ADHD. Not established in children under 6 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Non-catecholamine sympathomimetic amines with CNS stimulant activity. The exact mechanism in ADHD is not fully elucidated [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Amphetamines block the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine into the presynaptic neuron and increase their release into the extraneuronal space [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primary site is the liver via aromatic hydroxylation, N-dealkylation, and deamination. At least seven metabolites identified [US].
    - **Elimination:** Biological half-life is approximately 4 to 5 hours. Excretion is primarily urinary and heavily dependent on pH; alkaline urine significantly increases the drug’s half-life [US].
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 62% of an oral dose is eliminated in urine within 24 hours (one-third as intact drug) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant, child-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Schedule II Controlled Substance (CII); Rx-only [US].
