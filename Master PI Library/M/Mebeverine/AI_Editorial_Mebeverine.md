# Editorial Product Information: Mebeverine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian** (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of mebeverine hydrochloride for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mebeverine hydrochloride (a derivative of phenyl-ethylamine).
- **Chemical Class:** Antispasmodic; smooth muscle relaxant.
- **Brand Names:** APO-MEBEVERINE [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 135 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS):** Management of IBS and related conditions, including irritable colon, spastic colon, functional bowel disorders, spastic constipation, and nervous diarrhoea [Australian-approved].
- **Symptomatic Relief:** Treatment of associated symptoms such as abdominal pain and cramps, persistent non-specific diarrhoea (with or without alternating constipation), and flatulence [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 135 mg (one tablet) administered three times daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should preferably be taken before or with food [Australian].
- **Dose Titration:** After several weeks, once the desired clinical effect is achieved, the dosage may be gradually reduced [Australian].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** Patients should continue with the next scheduled dose. Missed doses must not be taken in addition to the regular dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to mebeverine hydrochloride or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None reported in available sources.
- **Cardiac Precautions:** Use with caution in patients with cardiac dysrhythmia, particularly those with partial or complete atrioventricular (AV) heart block, angina, or severe ischaemic heart disease [Australian].
- **Lactose Intolerance:** Formulations containing lactose monohydrate should be avoided in patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption. Lactose intolerance may also simulate IBS symptoms [Australian].
- **Hepatic Monitoring:** Liver function tests (LFTs) may be indicated if a patient develops jaundice or gastrointestinal symptoms suggestive of hepatic sensitivity, especially in cases of advanced liver disease or cirrhosis [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Due to the low reported incidence of adverse effects, frequency estimates are not robustly established in available sources.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources; no data available [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B2:** Safe use in pregnancy has not been established. Mebeverine is not recommended during the first trimester. Clinical use requires a careful risk-benefit assessment [Australian].
    - Animal studies in rats and rabbits did not demonstrate teratogenicity [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mebeverine is secreted into breast milk (concentrations <10 mcg/mL following a 100 mg dose). It is generally recommended that mebeverine not be used by breastfeeding women unless clinical benefits outweigh potential risks [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data not available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No data available [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No data available [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Caution is advised in patients with advanced renal disease due to the primary excretory pathway of metabolites [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Caution is advised in patients with advanced liver disease (e.g., cirrhosis) due to the metabolic pathway of the drug [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Mebeverine is a polyvalent spasmolytic with a direct, non-specific relaxant effect on vascular, cardiac, and other smooth muscle. It operates via three primary mechanisms:
    1. Direct musculotropic action involving calcium ion exchange and stabilisation of excitable membranes.
    2. Competitive antimuscarinic activity (weak; approximately 0.05–0.1 times the potency of atropine).
    3. Local anaesthetic activity and blockade of noradrenaline uptake into sympathetic nerve endings [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** It exhibits spasmolytic activity throughout the gastrointestinal tract, particularly in the colon, without significantly affecting normal intestinal peristalsis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Following oral administration, the drug is absorbed and appears in plasma as metabolites (veratric acid and mebeverinic acid).
    - **Distribution:** Peak radioactivity occurs 1–3 hours post-dose. Plasma protein binding (to albumin) is approximately 75%.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised via hydrolysis of the ester function.
    - **Elimination:** 95% of metabolites are excreted via urine, with peak excretion occurring within two hours. Approximately 98% is recovered within 24 hours. No unchanged mebeverine is excreted in the urine [Australian].
