# Editorial Product Information: Micafungin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) documentation from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local guidelines and the specific approved PI for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Micafungin (as sodium).
- **Chemical Class:** Echinocandin lipopeptide.
- **Brand Names:** Micafungin-AFT [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J02AX05.
- **Dosage Form and Strengths:** Powder for injection (lyophilised solid) in 50 mg and 100 mg vials [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AFT Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Micafungin is indicated for:
- **Invasive Candidiasis:** Treatment in both adult and paediatric patients [Australian].
- **Oesophageal Candidiasis:** Treatment in adults, adolescents (≥ 16 years of age), and elderly patients where intravenous therapy is appropriate [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis of *Candida* Infection:** For adult and paediatric patients undergoing allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) or those expected to have significant neutropenia (ANC < 500 cells/μL) for 10 days or longer [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults, Adolescents ≥ 16 years, and Elderly)
- **Invasive Candidiasis:** 100 mg/day (or 2 mg/kg/day for weight ≤ 40 kg). Dose may be increased to 200 mg/day (or 4 mg/kg/day) if clinical response is inadequate [Australian].
- **Oesophageal Candidiasis:** 150 mg/day (or 3 mg/kg/day for weight ≤ 40 kg) [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis:** 50 mg/day (or 1 mg/kg/day for weight ≤ 40 kg) [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Children (4 months to < 16 years):** 
    - Invasive Candidiasis: 100 mg/day (weight > 40 kg) or 2 mg/kg/day (weight ≤ 40 kg).
    - Prophylaxis: 50 mg/day (weight > 40 kg) or 1 mg/kg/day (weight ≤ 40 kg).
- **Infants and Neonates (< 4 months):**
    - Invasive Candidiasis: 4–10 mg/kg/day. If CNS infection is suspected, 10 mg/kg/day is recommended due to dose-dependent CNS penetration [Australian].
    - Prophylaxis: 2 mg/kg/day [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Each vial must be reconstituted with 5 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Glucose. Rotate gently; do not shake [Australian].
- **Dilution:** Reconstituted solution must be further diluted in 100 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Glucose.
- **Infusion:** Administer via IV infusion over approximately one hour.
- **Light Protection:** The infusion bag must be protected from light using a closable opaque bag [Australian].
- **Incompatibilities:** Do not mix or co-infuse with other medications as precipitation may occur. Flush existing lines with 0.9% Sodium Chloride prior to infusion [Australian].

### Treatment Duration
- **Invasive Candidiasis:** Minimum 14 days. Continue for at least one week after two sequential negative blood cultures and resolution of clinical symptoms.
- **Oesophageal Candidiasis:** Continue for at least one week after resolution of symptoms.
- **Prophylaxis:** Continue for at least one week after neutrophil recovery [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to micafungin sodium, any excipients (including lactose monohydrate), or other echinocandins [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, including shock, may occur. Discontinue immediately if reactions are observed.
- **Haemolysis:** Isolated cases of acute intravascular haemolysis and haemolytic anaemia have been reported. Monitor closely for worsening clinical or laboratory evidence of haemolysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Effects:** Liver function (ALT/AST) should be monitored. Discontinue early if significant and persistent elevations occur. Use with caution in patients with severe hepatic impairment or chronic liver diseases (e.g., cirrhosis, fibrosis, neonatal liver disease) due to potential pre-neoplastic risks [Australian].
- **Skin Reactions:** Exfoliative cutaneous reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis) have been reported. Discontinue if lesions progress [Australian].
- **Renal Monitoring:** Monitor for signs of renal impairment or acute renal failure.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data [Australian]
- **Very Common (> 10%):** Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, pyrexia, hypokalaemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, headache, phlebitis (injection site).
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, dyspepsia, mucosal inflammation, rigors, peripheral oedema, fatigue, hypomagnesaemia, hypocalcaemia, anorexia, hyperglycaemia, fluid overload, bacteraemia, sepsis, cough, dyspnoea, epistaxis, anaemia, febrile neutropenia, increased transaminases (AST/ALT), increased alkaline phosphatase, rash, pruritus, insomnia, anxiety, hypotension, hypertension, tachycardia, back pain.
- **Post-Marketing/Serious:** Anaphylactic shock, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC), hepatic failure, acute renal failure, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis.

**Paediatric Nuance:** The incidence of certain reactions (thrombocytopenia, tachycardia, hypertension, and hyperbilirubinaemia) is higher in paediatric patients compared to adults [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
Micafungin has a low potential for CYP3A-mediated interactions.
- **Sirolimus:** AUC increased by 21%; monitor for toxicity and reduce sirolimus dose if necessary [Australian].
- **Nifedipine:** AUC and Cmax increased by 18% and 42% respectively; monitor for toxicity and reduce dose if necessary [Australian].
- **Itraconazole:** AUC increased by 22%; monitor for toxicity [Australian].
- **No significant interaction:** Noted with mycophenolate mofetil, cyclosporin, tacrolimus, prednisolone, fluconazole, ritonavir, rifampicin, voriconazole, or amphotericin B [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal studies show micafungin crosses the placenta; visceral abnormalities and increased abortion were noted in rabbits at 2-fold clinical exposure. Use only if benefits outweigh risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if micafungin is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies suggested potential testicular toxicity (seminiferous tubular atrophy, reduced sperm count); clinical relevance in humans is unknown [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based. Infants < 4 months require higher mg/kg doses to match adult exposure.
- **Elderly:** No dosage adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required, even in severe impairment (CrCl < 30 mL/min). Micafungin is not dialysable [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for mild to severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh 7–12). However, monitor liver function closely [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Non-competitive inhibition of 1,3-β-D-glucan synthesis, an essential component of fungal cell walls (absent in mammalian cells).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Fungicidal against most *Candida* species and inhibits active growth of *Aspergillus* hyphae [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (> 99%), mainly to albumin. Rapidly distributed into tissues.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised in the liver to M1 (catechol), M2 (methoxy), and M5 (hydroxylation) forms. CYP3A is not a major pathway *in vivo*.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 10–17 hours. Primarily faecal excretion (71%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Candidiaemia/Invasive Candidiasis:** A non-inferiority study showed micafungin (100 mg/day or 2 mg/kg/day) was as effective as and better tolerated (lower renal toxicity and infusion reactions) than liposomal amphotericin B (3 mg/kg) [Australian].
- **Oesophageal Candidiasis:** Micafungin (150 mg/day) demonstrated non-inferiority to fluconazole (200 mg/day) with similar endoscopic cure rates (~87.7%) [Australian].
- **Prophylaxis:** Micafungin (50 mg/day) was significantly more effective than fluconazole (400 mg/day) in preventing invasive fungal infections in HSCT recipients [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store below 30 °C and protect from light [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Concentrate (Vial):** May be stored at 25 °C for up to 6 hours if protected from light.
- **Diluted Infusion Solution:** Stable for up to 24 hours at 2 °C to 8 °C if protected from light.
- **Preservatives:** None; for single use only.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
