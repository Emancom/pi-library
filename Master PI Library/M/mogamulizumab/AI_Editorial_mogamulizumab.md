# Editorial Product Information: Mogamulizumab

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by synthesizing official regulatory documents from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It represents a comprehensive overview intended for healthcare professionals, incorporating the most conservative safety warnings and detailed clinical guidance from both agencies. Where specific terminology or recommendations differ between agencies, the source is noted in brackets (e.g., [FDA] or [TGA]).

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** POTELIGEO  
**Active Ingredient:** Mogamulizumab (as mogamulizumab-kpkc [FDA])  
**Formulation:** 20 mg/5 mL (4 mg/mL) concentrated solution for intravenous infusion.  
**Description:** A recombinant, defucosylated, humanized IgG1 kappa monoclonal antibody produced in Chinese hamster ovary cells.

## 2. Approved Indications
POTELIGEO is indicated for the treatment of adult patients (≥ 18 years of age) with relapsed or refractory [FDA] mycosis fungoides (MF) or Sézary syndrome (SS) who have received at least one prior systemic therapy.

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of severe hypersensitivity to mogamulizumab or any of its excipients [TGA]. 
*   **Note:** While the FDA lists "None," clinical standard of care dictates that known life-threatening reactions to the drug components preclude use.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Dermatologic Toxicity
Fatal and life-threatening skin reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), have occurred. The most common reaction is drug eruption (rash), occurring in approximately 24-25% of patients. 
*   **Monitoring:** Patients must be monitored throughout the course of treatment. 
*   **Management:** For Grade 2 or 3 (moderate to severe) rash, interrupt treatment and administer topical corticosteroids. For SJS, TEN, or any Grade 4 (life-threatening) skin reaction, permanently discontinue the drug.

### Infusion-Related Reactions
Infusion reactions are very common (approx. 33-35%), with the majority (90%) occurring during or shortly after the first infusion. 
*   **Signs:** Chills, nausea, pyrexia, tachycardia, rigors, headache, and vomiting.
*   **Management:** Temporarily interrupt the infusion for any reaction and treat symptoms. Reduce the infusion rate by at least 50% upon restarting. Permanently discontinue for Grade 4 reactions.

### Infections
Mogamulizumab increases the risk of serious, fatal, or life-threatening infections, including sepsis and pneumonia. 
*   **Hepatitis B Virus (HBV):** Patients should be screened for HBV before starting treatment. If positive, consult a specialist regarding reactivation risks [TGA/FDA].
*   **General:** Monitor for signs of infection and treat promptly.

### Autoimmune Complications
Fatal and life-threatening immune-mediated reactions have been reported. These include, but are not limited to, pneumonitis, hepatitis, myocarditis, myositis, polymyositis, and Guillain-Barré syndrome [FDA]. New-onset hypothyroidism and immune-mediated colitis have also been observed.

### Complications of Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (HSCT)
Increased risks of severe (Grade 3 or 4) acute graft-versus-host disease (GVHD), steroid-refractory GVHD, and transplant-related mortality have been reported in patients receiving allogeneic HSCT after mogamulizumab therapy. The risk is higher if the drug is administered within 50 days of the transplant.

### Additional Metabolic and Cardiac Risks [TGA]
*   **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Most common during the first month of treatment. Monitor electrolytes and hydration in patients with high tumour burden.
*   **Cardiac Disorders:** Rare cases of acute myocardial infarction and stress cardiomyopathy have been reported. Monitor patients with existing cardiac risk factors.

## 5. Dosage and Administration

### Recommended Dosage
The standard dose is **1 mg/kg** administered as an intravenous infusion over at least 60 minutes.
*   **Cycle 1 (28-day cycle):** Weekly administration on Days 1, 8, 15, and 22.
*   **Subsequent Cycles:** Administration every two weeks on Days 1 and 15.
*   **Duration:** Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

### Missed Doses
Administer the dose within 2 days of the scheduled date. If delayed by more than 2 days, administer the dose as soon as possible and reset the dosing schedule based on the new date.

### Premedication
Administer premedication with an anti-pyretic (e.g., paracetamol/acetaminophen) and an anti-histamine (e.g., diphenhydramine) for the **first infusion**. If an infusion reaction occurs, premedication is required for all subsequent doses.

### Preparation and Handling
*   Calculate the volume based on patient weight and dilute into an infusion bag containing 0.9% Sodium Chloride to a final concentration between **0.1 mg/mL and 3.0 mg/mL**.
*   Mix by gentle inversion; **do not shake**.
*   Use a sterile, low protein-binding 0.22 micron in-line filter for administration.

## 6. Adverse Effects
**Very Common (≥10%):** 
*   Infusion-related reactions, rash (drug eruption), fatigue, diarrhea, upper respiratory tract infection, musculoskeletal pain, pyrexia, nausea, edema, headache, and stomatitis/mucositis.

**Common (1% to 10%):** 
*   Anemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, hypothyroidism, constipation, vomiting, and increased hepatic enzymes (ALT/AST).

**Serious Reactions:** 
*   Pneumonia, sepsis, SJS/TEN, and Grade 4 respiratory failure.

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal drug-drug interaction studies have been conducted. Avoid co-administering other medicinal products through the same intravenous line.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
POTELIGEO is not recommended during pregnancy (TGA Category C). Animal studies show that mogamulizumab crosses the placental barrier and can cause fetal depletion of CCR4-expressing lymphocytes. 
*   **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for **3 months** after the final dose.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Because human IgG is known to be excreted in milk, a risk to the infant cannot be excluded. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** The central volume of distribution is approximately 3.6 L.
*   **Elimination:** The terminal half-life is approximately **17 days**, with a clearance of 12 mL/h.
*   **Steady State:** Reached after approximately 12 weeks of the recommended dosing regimen.
*   **Special Populations:** No clinically significant PK differences were observed based on age, sex, renal impairment, or mild-to-moderate hepatic impairment. It has not been studied in patients with severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Mogamulizumab is a defucosylated, humanized IgG1 kappa monoclonal antibody that selectively binds to **CCR4** (CC chemokine receptor type 4). CCR4 is expressed on the surface of malignant T-cells in MF and SS. Binding induces antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), leading to the targeted depletion of malignant cells.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened Vials:** Store in a refrigerator at **2°C to 8°C**. Keep the vial in the outer carton to protect from light. Do not freeze or shake.
*   **Diluted Solution:** 
    *   Ideally, use immediately. 
    *   Can be stored under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C) for no more than **24 hours** from the time of preparation. Do not freeze the diluted solution.
