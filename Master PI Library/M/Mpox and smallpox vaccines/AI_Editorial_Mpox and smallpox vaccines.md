# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The jurisdictions contributing to this editorial include: **US (FDA)**. 
- Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not available in the provided source materials.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial reference and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, but clinically standard).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer via the intravenous route only [US].
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations <40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments should be guided by trough levels; downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with pre-existing conditions, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy and monitor weekly and monthly. Monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma free carnitine levels (target range: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Renal Function:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), Pain (21–35%), Flu syndrome (15–29%), Headache (3–37%), Infection (10–24%), Abdominal pain (5–21%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%) [US].
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%), Paresthesia (3–12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Rhinitis (6–11%), Dyspnea (3–11%) [US].

### Common (1–10%)
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia (5–9%), Palpitations (3–8%), Chest pain (6–15%), Arrhythmia (3%) [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (3–12%) [US].
- **Nervous:** Insomnia (3–6%), Depression (5–6%), Anxiety (2–5%) [US].

### Postmarketing and Serious Effects
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with and without history [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and bronchospasm [US].
- **Other:** Less frequent reactions include body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned in OCR, but clinical text applies.] Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. Risks to the child of excess intake should be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing information is provided for metabolic disorders (50 mg/kg), which includes paediatric applications, but specific age-group safety profiles were not detailed [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically for ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). Use of **oral** formulations in severe renal impairment is cautioned due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine, which is quickly excreted [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis 3x/week) increased mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While carnitine concentrations increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx Only) [US].
