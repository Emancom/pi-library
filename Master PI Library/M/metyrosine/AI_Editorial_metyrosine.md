# Editorial Product Information: Metyrosine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Note:** At the time of this publication, only the United States PI was available for review. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) is noted as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, locally approved PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Metyrosine (INN); (–)-α-methyl-L-tyrosine (α-MPT).
- **Chemical Class:** Tyrosine hydroxylase inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** Metyrosine [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01XX05 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for class).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Oral capsule, 250 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amneal Pharmaceuticals NY LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Metyrosine is indicated for the treatment of patients with **pheochromocytoma** in the following clinical scenarios:
- Preoperative preparation of patients for surgical intervention.
- Long-term management of patients for whom surgery is contraindicated.
- Chronic treatment of patients with malignant pheochromocytoma.

**Note:** Metyrosine is explicitly not recommended for the management of essential hypertension [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children $\ge$ 12 years)
- **Initial Dosage:** 250 mg orally four times daily.
- **Titration:** The dose may be increased by 250 mg to 500 mg daily. 
- **Maximum Dosage:** 4 g/day in divided doses.
- **Preoperative Preparation:** To achieve optimal effectiveness, the drug should be administered for at least five to seven days prior to surgery.

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Hypertensive Patients:** Titrate to achieve blood pressure normalization and control of clinical symptoms.
- **Normotensive Patients:** Titrate to a dosage that reduces urinary metanephrines and/or vanillylmandelic acid (VMA) by 50% or more.
- **Adjunctive Therapy:** If symptoms are not adequately controlled, an alpha-adrenergic blocking agent (e.g., phenoxybenzamine) should be added to the regimen.

### Administration Instructions
- Patients should maintain a high fluid intake to ensure a daily urine volume of 2000 mL or greater to minimize the risk of crystalluria [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to metyrosine or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Surgical Precautions
- **Fluid Volume Maintenance:** Adequate intravascular volume must be maintained intraoperatively and postoperatively to prevent hypotension and reduced organ perfusion following tumor removal.
- **Arrhythmias:** Life-threatening arrhythmias may occur during anesthesia or surgery; continuous ECG and blood pressure monitoring are required. Treatment with beta-blockers or lidocaine may be necessary.
- **Hypertensive Crisis:** Metyrosine does not eliminate the risk of hypertensive crisis during tumor manipulation; phentolamine should be available for immediate use.

### General Precautions
- **Metyrosine Crystalluria:** Metyrosine may form needle- or rod-shaped crystals in the urine. Risk increases with doses >2 g/day. If crystalluria occurs, increase fluid intake; if it persists, reduce the dose or discontinue the drug.
- **Sedation:** Occurs in almost all patients. Patients should be cautioned against operating heavy machinery or driving until their response to the drug is established.
- **Long-term Use:** Limited data exists for chronic use. Periodic laboratory testing is recommended for patients on prolonged therapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Sedation:** Occurs in nearly all patients. Maximal effect is seen within 2–3 days of therapy; typically wanes after one week but may persist at doses >2 g/day.
- **Extrapyramidal Signs:** Reported in approximately 10% of patients; includes drooling, speech difficulty, tremor, trismus, and frank parkinsonism.
- **Diarrhea:** Reported in approximately 10% of patients; may be severe enough to require anti-diarrheal therapy.

### Common to Rare (Frequencies not always specified)
- **Psychiatric:** Anxiety, depression, hallucinations, disorientation, and confusion (dose-dependent).
- **Withdrawal Effects:** Insomnia and feelings of "psychic stimulation" (increased alertness/ambition) may occur for 2–3 days after discontinuation.
- **Endocrine/Reproductive:** Galactorrhea, breast swelling, impotence, or failure of ejaculation.
- **Gastrointestinal/General:** Dry mouth (decreased salivation), nasal stuffiness, headache, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain.
- **Hematologic:** Eosinophilia, anemia, thrombocytopenia, and thrombocytosis (rare).
- **Renal:** Transient dysuria and hematuria associated with crystalluria.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Urticaria and pharyngeal edema (rare).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Metyrosine has additive sedative effects with alcohol, hypnotics, sedatives, and tranquilizers [US].
- **Antipsychotics (Phenothiazines/Haloperidol):** Inhibition of catecholamine synthesis by metyrosine may potentiate the extrapyramidal effects of these agents.
- **Laboratory Interference:** Metyrosine metabolites may cause spurious increases in urinary catecholamine measurements.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned]. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. It is unknown if metyrosine causes fetal harm or affects reproductive capacity. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if metyrosine is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Data not available; long-term studies on impairment of fertility have not been performed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children under 12 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects $\ge$65 years to determine if they respond differently. Use caution, generally starting at the low end of the dosing range due to higher frequency of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Observe caution; specific eGFR-based dosing adjustments are not provided in available sources, but the risk of crystalluria may be increased.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Observe caution; periodic laboratory tests are recommended during prolonged use.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Metyrosine inhibits **tyrosine hydroxylase**, the rate-limiting enzyme in catecholamine biosynthesis (converting tyrosine to DOPA). This leads to a reduction in endogenous norepinephrine and epinephrine levels.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Well absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract.
- **Distribution:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Metabolism:** Minimal; less than 1% of the dose is recovered as catechol metabolites.
- **Elimination:** Primarily renal; 53% to 88% of an oral dose is recovered unchanged in the urine within 24 hours.
- **Half-life:** Plasma half-life ranges from 3.0 to 3.7 hours.

### Pharmacodynamics
- **Biochemical Effect:** Reductions in catecholamine biosynthesis range from 35% to 80% at doses of 1–4 g/day.
- **Onset/Duration:** Maximum biochemical effect occurs within 2–3 days; levels return to baseline 3–4 days after discontinuation.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal clinical data for metyrosine was established in the mid-1960s. A key reference cited in the PI is the 1966 NIH Combined Clinical Staff Conference (*Ann Intern Med. 1966;65:1302-1326*), which characterized the drug's role in preoperative and chronic management of pheochromocytoma.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Packaging:** Dispense in a tight, child-resistant container.
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.
