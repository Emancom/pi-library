# Editorial Product Information: Minocycline

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was provided for synthesis; therefore, jurisdictional variations from other regions (e.g., US, Europe) are not reflected in this version.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Minocycline hydrochloride dihydrate (a semisynthetic tetracycline derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Akamin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (J01AA08).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 50 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd trading as Viatris [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Minocycline is indicated for the treatment of infections caused by susceptible strains of the following organisms:
- **Gram-negative:** *Escherichia coli*, *Enterobacter aerogenes*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Klebsiella* species, and *Proteus* species.
- **Gram-positive:** *Streptococcus pyogenes* (group A $\beta$-haemolytic) and *Streptococcus faecalis*. Use should be restricted to cases where sensitivity is confirmed due to high resistance rates [Australian].
- **Staphylococcal Infections:** Not the first-line agent; use only if the organism is sensitive and other agents are unavailable [Australian].
- **Acne:** Specifically for the treatment of tetracycline-resistant acne [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Infections:** Initial loading dose of 200 mg, followed by 100 mg every 12 hours.
- **Duration:** Continue therapy for 24 to 48 hours after fever and symptoms have resolved.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Tetracycline-resistant Acne:** 100 mg daily, preferably administered as 50 mg twice daily. Clinical resolution typically occurs within 3 months [Australian].
- **Streptococcal Infections:** Must be treated for at least 10 days to prevent complications [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed with adequate fluids.
- Absorption is not significantly influenced by food or dairy products [Australian].

### Special Adjustments
- **Renal Impairment:** Total dosage should be reduced by lowering individual doses or extending the dosing interval [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** Should not exceed 400 mg/day [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to minocycline or any member of the tetracycline class.
- Severe renal insufficiency [Australian].
- Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) [Australian].
- Concomitant use with **Vitamin A** or **retinoids** (e.g., isotretinoin, etretinate) due to the risk of pseudotumour cerebri [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Tooth Discolouration:** Use during tooth development (last half of pregnancy, infancy, and childhood up to 8 years) may cause permanent yellow-grey-brown discolouration and enamel hypoplasia.
- **Hyperpigmentation:** Blue-black cutaneous pigmentation may occur in the skin, nails, mucous membranes, and bone. This is more common with prolonged high-dose use and may take months or years to resolve upon discontinuation [Australian].
- **Photosensitivity:** Exaggerated sunburn reactions can occur. Patients should avoid direct sunlight and UV light; treatment should stop at the first sign of skin erythema.
- **Clostridioides difficile-Associated Disease:** Pseudomembranous colitis has been reported, ranging from mild to life-threatening. Avoid drugs that inhibit peristalsis [Australian].
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects:** High incidence of vestibular symptoms (dizziness, vertigo, ataxia) compared to other tetracyclines. Patients should be cautioned regarding driving or operating machinery.
- **Pseudotumour Cerebri:** Benign intracranial hypertension (headache, blurred vision) has been reported in adults and bulging fontanelles in infants.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Use with caution in patients with hepatic dysfunction or those taking other hepatotoxic medications [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The adverse effect profile is similar to other tetracyclines, but with a notably higher incidence of vestibular issues.

| System | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Vestibular/CNS** | Dizziness, vertigo, ataxia (very common/common), headache, convulsions, hypesthesia, paresthesia, sedation. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, anorexia, glossitis, dysphagia, enterocolitis, pancreatitis, oesophageal ulceration (rare). |
| **Dermatological** | Maculopapular/erythematous rashes, photosensitivity, exfoliative dermatitis (uncommon), fixed drug eruptions, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome. |
| **Hepatic** | Elevated liver enzymes, hepatitis, acute liver failure (rare), autoimmune hepatitis. |
| **Renal** | Increased serum urea (dose-related), acute renal failure. |
| **Haematological** | Agranulocytosis, haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, eosinophilia. |
| **Hypersensitivity** | Urticaria, angioedema, anaphylaxis, polyarthralgia, pulmonary infiltrates, reversible lupus-like syndrome. |
| **Other** | Brown/black microscopic discolouration of the thyroid (long-term use). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Polyvalent Cations:** Antacids (Al, Ca, Mg) and iron preparations impair absorption. Do not administer concurrently.
- **Anticoagulants:** Tetracyclines depress prothrombin activity; anticoagulant dosage may require downward adjustment.
- **Retinoids:** Concomitant use with isotretinoin or etretinate increases the risk of pseudotumour cerebri; avoid use before, during, or shortly after minocycline therapy [Australian].
- **Methoxyflurane:** Concurrent use has been reported to cause fatal renal toxicity [Australian].
- **Penicillins:** Bacteriostatic drugs may interfere with the bactericidal action of penicillins.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Potential for reduced efficacy and breakthrough bleeding. Use of additional mechanical contraception is recommended [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Tetracyclines cross the placenta and cause permanent tooth discolouration and skeletal growth retardation if used after the 18th week of gestation. Use should be avoided during the second and third trimesters.
- **Breastfeeding:** Tetracyclines are excreted in human milk. Use is generally not recommended due to potential effects on the infant.
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children under 8 years of age due to risks of tooth discolouration and enamel hypoplasia. Reversible reduction in fibula growth rate has been seen in premature infants [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data provided in source [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Avoid in patients with renal failure. Usual doses may lead to systemic accumulation and hepatotoxicity. Monitor for increased serum urea, azotaemia, and acidosis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; monitor for hepatotoxicity [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primarily bacteriostatic. Inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 30S ribosomal subunit.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Active against many Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms, including some *S. aureus* strains resistant to other tetracyclines.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma levels in 1–4 hours. 200 mg dose yields ~4 mcg/mL.
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed; approximately 75% protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Significant metabolism occurs (unlike other tetracyclines), with metabolites recovered in urine.
    - **Elimination:** Plasma half-life is approximately 13 hours. Excreted in bile (undergoes enterohepatic circulation) and faeces (~35%). Less than 10% is excreted in urine [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in the original container (e.g., HDPE bottle with PP screw cap) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
