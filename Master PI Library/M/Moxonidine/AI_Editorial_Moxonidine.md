# Editorial Product Information: Moxonidine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- Information from other major regulatory regions (e.g., US, Europe, Canada, Japan) was not available at the time of this review.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Moxonidine (a centrally acting antihypertensive; I1-imidazoline receptor agonist).
- **Brand Names:** APO-MOXONIDINE [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 0.2 mg and 0.4 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Southern Cross Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** For the treatment of high blood pressure [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Initial treatment begins with 0.2 mg once daily, administered in the morning [Australian]. 
- **Titration Schedule:** 
    - If the response is insufficient after two weeks, the dose may be increased to 0.4 mg daily, given as a single dose or in divided doses (morning and evening). 
    - After a further two weeks, the dose may be increased to a maximum of 0.6 mg daily, administered in divided doses [Australian].
- **Dose Limits:**
    - **Maximum single dose:** 0.4 mg [Australian].
    - **Maximum daily dose:** 0.6 mg [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** May be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to moxonidine or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- Heart failure, encompassing NYHA Classes I through IV [Australian].
- Patients aged 75 years or older [Australian].
- Bradycardia (Heart Rate < 50 beats/minute) or severe bradyarrhythmia [Australian].
- Sick sinus syndrome [Australian].
- Second or third-degree atrioventricular (AV) block [Australian].
- Malignant arrhythmias [Australian].
- Severe renal impairment (GFR < 30 mL/min; serum creatinine > 160 µmol/L) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cessation of Beta-Blocker Combination:** If moxonidine is used with a beta-blocker and therapy must be stopped, the beta-blocker should be discontinued several days before moxonidine to prevent rebound hypertension [Australian].
- **Atrioventricular Block:** Post-marketing reports indicate moxonidine may delay AV conduction. Use with extreme caution in patients with 1st-degree AV block to avoid bradycardia [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Caution is required in patients with unstable angina or severe coronary artery disease due to limited clinical experience [Australian].
- **Psychiatric and Neurological Conditions:** Due to lack of data, use is not recommended in patients with depression, Parkinson's disease, or epileptic disorders [Australian].
- **Peripheral Vascular Disease:** Avoid use in Raynaud’s disease or intermittent claudication [Australian].
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** While no rebound effect has been confirmed in limited studies, it is recommended to withdraw moxonidine gradually over several days [Australian].
- **Lactose Intolerance:** Tablets contain lactose; unsuitable for patients with galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Close monitoring of hypotensive effects is required when initiating therapy in patients with moderate renal impairment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse effects are mild to moderate and typically do not require discontinuation.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dry mouth [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Headache, dizziness, nausea, asthenia, vasodilatation, anxiety, sleep disturbances, somnolence, diarrhoea, back pain [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Sedation, insomnia, skin rash, urticaria, pruritus, peripheral oedema, vertigo [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypotension, postural hypotension [Australian]. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Angioedema [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Other Antihypertensives:** Concurrent use enhances the hypotensive effect [Australian].
    - **CNS Depressants:** Moxonidine potentiates the sedative effects of alcohol, tranquilizers, sedatives, hypnotics, and benzodiazepines (e.g., lorazepam) [Australian].
    - **Tricyclic Antidepressants (TCAs):** May reduce the effectiveness of centrally acting antihypertensives; co-administration is not recommended [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - No significant interactions were observed with glibenclamide or digoxin [Australian].
    - As moxonidine is eliminated via tubular excretion, interactions with other drugs using the same pathway cannot be excluded [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** Not specified. (Note: The provided source classifies it as **Category B3**). 
    - Animal studies showed embryofoetal toxicity at maternally toxic high doses. There is a lack of adequate human data; use only if the benefit clearly outweighs the risk to the foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Moxonidine is excreted into human breast milk. Use by nursing mothers is not recommended unless the benefits justify the risks to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no effect on fertility at doses significantly higher than clinical exposures [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 16 years due to insufficient data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Exposure (AUC) increases by approximately 50% in the elderly. Use is contraindicated in patients $\ge$ 75 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Moderate (GFR 30–60 mL/min):** Maximum single dose 0.2 mg; maximum daily dose 0.4 mg [Australian].
    - **Severe (GFR < 30 mL/min):** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Moxonidine is a selective agonist at I1-imidazoline receptors in the rostral ventrolateral medulla. It reduces sympathetic activity, thereby decreasing systemic vascular resistance and blood pressure. It has a significantly lower affinity for $\alpha_2$-adrenoreceptors compared to older centrally acting agents, reducing the incidence of sedation and dry mouth [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** ~90% absorbed; 88% bioavailability (no first-pass metabolism). Food does not interfere [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Low protein binding (~10%) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal (10–20%); primarily to 4,5-dehydromoxonidine (activity is 1/10th of parent) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** >90% renal elimination within 24 hours. Plasma half-life is 2.2–2.3 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trials:** Three double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (n=464) compared moxonidine (0.2 mg to 0.6 mg) to enalapril. 
    - **0.2 mg dose:** Statistically significant reduction in diastolic blood pressure (DBP) vs. placebo; comparable to 5 mg enalapril [Australian].
    - **0.4 mg and 0.6 mg doses:** Highly significant reductions in both systolic (SBP) and DBP vs. placebo; equivalent to 10 mg and 20 mg enalapril respectively [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** A study of moxonidine (0.4 mg) combined with hydrochlorothiazide (25 mg) demonstrated a significant additive effect in blood pressure reduction compared to monotherapy [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Store in the original container (PVC/PVDC/Al blister strips) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
