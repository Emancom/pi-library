# Editorial Product Information: Midostaurin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for full prescribing details and legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Midostaurin (a multikinase inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** Actarin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XE39.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Soft gelatin capsules containing 25 mg of midostaurin [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Physical Description:** Pale orange to orange oblong capsules containing a pale yellow to yellow liquid, with a red imprint “657” [Australian].
- **Excipients of Note:** May contain traces of sulfites in gelatin and 13% v/v alcohol [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Actor Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Midostaurin is indicated for the treatment of adult patients in the following conditions:
- **Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML):** In combination with standard anthracycline and cytarabine induction and cytarabine consolidation chemotherapy, followed by single-agent maintenance therapy in patients achieving complete response, for newly diagnosed AML that is FLT3 mutation-positive [Australian].
- **Advanced Systemic Mastocytosis:** Including aggressive systemic mastocytosis (ASM), systemic mastocytosis with associated haematological neoplasms (SM-AHN), or mast cell leukaemia (MCL) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Treatment should be initiated by a physician experienced in anticancer therapies.
- **Administration:** Capsules must be swallowed whole with a glass of water and should not be opened, crushed, or chewed.
- **Food Requirement:** Must be taken with food to mitigate nausea [Australian].
- **Schedule:** Twice daily at approximately 12-hour intervals.
- **Prophylaxis:** Prophylactic anti-emetics should be administered according to local practice and patient tolerance.

### Dosing by Indication [Australian]
- **AML:** 50 mg (two 25 mg capsules) twice daily. Dosing occurs on days 8–21 of induction and consolidation cycles. Maintenance therapy is 50 mg twice daily until relapse or for up to 12 cycles (28 days each).
- **Advanced SM:** 100 mg (four 25 mg capsules) twice daily. Treatment continues as long as clinical benefit is observed or until unacceptable toxicity.

### Dose Modifications
- **AML:** For Grade 3/4 pulmonary infiltrates, interrupt treatment for the remainder of the cycle and resume at the same dose once resolved to Grade ≤1. For Grade 4 neutropenia during maintenance, interrupt until ANC ≥1.0 x 10⁹/L, then resume at 50 mg BID; discontinue if neutropenia persists >2 weeks and is related to midostaurin.
- **Advanced SM:** For ANC <1.0 x 10⁹/L (not related to baseline), interrupt until ANC ≥1.5 x 10⁹/L, resume at 50 mg BID, and if tolerated, increase to 100 mg BID. Discontinue if ANC remains low and is suspected to be drug-related. Similar interruptions apply for Grade 3/4 non-haematological toxicities, nausea/vomiting, or haemoglobin <8 g/dL.

### Missed Dose and Vomiting
- If a dose is missed, it should not be made up; the patient should take the next dose at the scheduled time.
- If vomiting occurs, an additional dose should not be taken; the patient should wait for the next scheduled dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to midostaurin or any excipients.
- Concomitant use of potent CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampicin, St. John’s Wort, carbamazepine, enzalutamide, phenytoin) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neutropenia and Infection:** Midostaurin can cause severe neutropenia. White blood cell counts should be monitored regularly, particularly at the start of treatment. Active serious infections must be controlled before starting monotherapy.
- **Cardiac Dysfunction:** Congestive heart failure (CHF) and transient decreases in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) have been reported in advanced SM trials. Caution is advised in patients at risk; monitor LVEF at baseline and during treatment.
- **Pulmonary Toxicity:** Interstitial lung disease (ILD) and pneumonitis, including fatal cases, have occurred. Monitor for pulmonary symptoms; discontinue treatment for Grade ≥3 symptoms without an infectious etiology.
- **QT Prolongation:** While dedicated studies did not show clinically significant prolongation at 75 mg BID, consider interval ECG assessments if midostaurin is used with other QT-prolonging medications.
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Midostaurin can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status within 7 days of initiation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (Combination Therapy)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Febrile neutropenia (83.4%), nausea (83.4%), exfoliative dermatitis (61.6%), vomiting (60.7%), headache (45.9%), petechiae (35.8%), pyrexia (34.5%), epistaxis (27.5%), device-related infection (24.0%), stomatitis (21.8%), back pain (21.8%), and hyperglycaemia (20.1%) [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Febrile neutropenia was the most frequent serious reaction (16.2%). Sepsis was reported in 1.2% of patients.

### Advanced Systemic Mastocytosis (Monotherapy)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Nausea (82%), vomiting (68%), diarrhoea (51%), peripheral oedema (35%), fatigue (31%), constipation (29%), pyrexia (27%), and urinary tract infection (13%) [Australian].
- **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Very common elevations in glucose, bilirubin, lipase, AST, and ALT. Absolute lymphocyte and neutrophil decreases are also very common.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (Victim):** Strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, ritonavir, clarithromycin) significantly increase midostaurin exposure. Use with caution and monitor for toxicity.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (Victim):** Potent inducers significantly decrease midostaurin exposure (e.g., rifampicin reduces AUC by 96%) and are contraindicated.
- **CYP Substrates (Perpetrator):** Midostaurin is a mild inducer of CYP2B6 (e.g., bupropion) and may inhibit CYP1A2 and CYP2E1. Use caution with narrow therapeutic index substrates of these enzymes.
- **Transporters:** Midostaurin may inhibit BCRP (e.g., rosuvastatin) and P-gp (e.g., digoxin). Caution is required with narrow therapeutic index substrates of BCRP.
- **Contraceptives:** No clinically significant interaction with oral ethinyl estradiol/levonorgestrel is anticipated.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Midostaurin is not recommended during pregnancy or for women of childbearing potential not using contraception. Animal studies show reproductive toxicity, including resorptions and skeletal ossification delays.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if midostaurin is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment and for at least 4 months after the final dose [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during and for 4 months after treatment. Sexually active males should use condoms during intercourse with females of reproductive potential or pregnant women for 4 months after treatment.
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggest midostaurin may impair male and female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended in combination with intensive paediatric AML regimens due to risks of prolonged haematological recovery (severe neutropenia and thrombocytopenia) [Australian].
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** No dose adjustment is required. Limited experience in AML patients >70 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild or moderate impairment. Data are lacking for severe or end-stage renal disease [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild or moderate (Child-Pugh A or B) impairment. Efficacy data in severe hepatic impairment are insufficient to make a recommendation, though exposure is lower in these patients [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Midostaurin inhibits multiple receptor tyrosine kinases, including FLT3 and KIT (both wild type and D816V mutant). Signaling inhibition leads to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis in leukemic and mast cells. It also inhibits PDGFR, VEGFR2, and PKC isoforms.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed (Tmax 1–3 hours). High-fat meals increase AUC by 59%.
    - **Distribution:** Highly tissue-distributed; >99.8% protein bound (mainly to plasma proteins).
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by CYP3A4 via oxidative pathways. Two major active metabolites: CGP62221 and CGP52421.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (78%); terminal half-lives are approximately 20 hours (parent), 33 hours (CGP62221), and 495 hours (CGP52421) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **AML (RATIFY Trial):** A Phase III study of 717 patients showed midostaurin plus chemotherapy significantly improved overall survival (HR 0.774; p=0.0078) and event-free survival (median 8.2 months vs 3.0 months) compared to placebo plus chemotherapy [Australian].
- **Advanced SM:** A Phase II single-arm study of 116 patients demonstrated an overall response rate of 59.6% (by modified Valent/Cheson criteria), with activity observed regardless of KIT D816V mutation status [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture; store in the original package.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the packaging for the expiry date (information also available via the ARTG for Australia).
