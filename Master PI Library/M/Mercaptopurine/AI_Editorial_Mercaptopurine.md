# Editorial Product Information: Mercaptopurine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview of mercaptopurine for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information used for prescribing in specific jurisdictions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mercaptopurine monohydrate (a purine analogue).
- **Brand Names:** ALLMERCAP [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (L01BB02).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral liquid suspension, 20 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia (ALL):** Indicated for the treatment of ALL in paediatric patients [Australian]. 

*Note: The specific formulation (ALLMERCAP) provided in the source is only indicated for use in children [Australian].*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Paediatric Dosing:** The standard starting dose is 2.5 mg/kg bodyweight daily. The final dose and duration depend on the specific combination therapy regimen and the dosage of other concomitant cytotoxic agents [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - May be taken with or without food, but the method should be standardized daily.
    - **Milk/Dairy Restriction:** To avoid interaction with xanthine oxidase found in milk, the dose should be taken at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after consuming milk or dairy products [Australian].
- **Dose Adjustments for Interactions:** If co-administered with allopurinol, the mercaptopurine dose must be reduced to **one-quarter (25%)** of the standard dose [Australian].
- **Genetic Considerations:** Substantial dose reductions are required for patients with inherited Thiopurine Methyl Transferase (TPMT) deficiency or NUDT15 variants [Australian].
- **Formulation Switching:** The oral suspension and tablet formulations are not bioequivalent regarding peak plasma concentrations; intensified haematological monitoring is required when switching between forms [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to mercaptopurine or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].
- Concurrent administration of the **yellow fever vaccine** [Australian].
- *Note on Cross-Sensitivity:* Patients with a history of hypersensitivity to azathioprine (a pro-drug) must be assessed for mercaptopurine hypersensitivity prior to treatment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [Australian]:** Mercaptopurine is an active cytotoxic agent and must only be used under the direction of physicians experienced in such therapy.
- **Myelosuppression:** Mercaptopurine is strongly myelosuppressive, causing leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, and anaemia. Full blood counts (FBC) must be monitored daily during remission induction and at least weekly during maintenance [Australian]. 
- **Hepatotoxicity:** The drug is hepatotoxic; liver function tests (LFTs) must be monitored weekly. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if jaundice appears [Australian].
- **Genetic Testing:**
    - **TPMT:** Patients should be tested for TPMT activity prior to treatment. Homozygous deficiency carries a high risk of life-threatening bone marrow suppression [Australian].
    - **NUDT15:** Patients with inherited NUDT15 mutations (particularly those of Asian ethnicity) are at increased risk of severe toxicity and require dose reduction [Australian].
- **Immunosuppression and Infection:** Increased susceptibility to viral, fungal, and bacterial infections. Live organism vaccines are generally not recommended and must be avoided for at least 3 months after chemotherapy completion [Australian].
- **Carcinogenesis:** Potential risk of lymphoproliferative disorders, skin cancers, and cervical cancer. Risk increases with the degree and duration of immunosuppression [Australian].
- **Macrophage Activation Syndrome (MAS):** A life-threatening disorder that may develop in patients with autoimmune conditions (e.g., IBD, an unlicensed indication) [Australian].
- **Pellagra:** Purine analogues may interfere with the niacin pathway, potentially leading to nicotinic acid deficiency [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorized by frequency below [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Bone marrow suppression, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Anaemia, anorexia, nausea, vomiting, stomatitis, diarrhoea, biliary stasis, hepatotoxicity. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Bacterial/viral infections, arthralgia, skin rash, drug fever, pancreatitis (in licensed indications), hepatic necrosis. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Neoplasms (lymphoproliferative, skin cancers), facial oedema, intestinal ulceration, alopecia, transient oligospermia. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Secondary leukaemia, myelodysplasia. |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (primarily in IBD), hypoglycaemia, pellagra, portal hypertension, cholestasis of pregnancy, photosensitivity. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Xanthine Oxidase Inhibitors (Allopurinol, Oxipurinol, Thiopurinol):** Reduce mercaptopurine catabolism. The mercaptopurine dose **must** be reduced to 25% of the usual dose. Use with **febuxostat** is not recommended [Australian].
- **Ribavirin:** Not advised; may increase toxicity and reduce efficacy [Australian].
- **Aminosalicylates (e.g., Mesalazine, Olsalazine):** May inhibit TPMT, increasing the risk of myelosuppression [Australian].
- **Methotrexate:** Increases mercaptopurine AUC; dose adjustments may be needed [Australian].
- **Infliximab:** May increase levels of active 6-TGN metabolites; close haematological monitoring is required [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants (Warfarin):** Mercaptopurine may inhibit the anticoagulant effect; increased monitoring of PT/INR is required [Australian].
- **Anti-epileptic Medicines:** Cytotoxic agents may reduce the absorption of phenytoin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category D** [Australian].
- **Maternal/Paternal Exposure:** Mercaptopurine is embryotoxic and teratogenic. It should be avoided, especially during the first trimester. Both men and women should use effective contraception during and for a period after treatment (3 months for men, 6 months for women) [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mercaptopurine has been detected in breast milk. Mothers receiving mercaptopurine should **not** breastfeed [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May cause transient profound oligospermia [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** The provided formulation (ALLMERCAP) is specifically indicated for children. Caution is needed due to the risk of symptomatic hypoglycaemia, particularly in children under six or those with a low BMI [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** The provided source specifies use only in children for this formulation [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Consideration should be given to dose reduction in patients with impaired renal or hepatic function [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** An adenine and hypoxanthine analogue that acts as an antimetabolite, interfering with nucleic acid biosynthesis in proliferating cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Variable and incomplete (average 50%).
    - **Distribution:** Crosses the blood-brain barrier in negligible amounts.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolized by TPMT and xanthine oxidase.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted via the kidneys. Plasma half-life is approximately 90 minutes [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific clinical trial data provided in the available Australian source for the ALLMERCAP formulation [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** 56 days after first opening the bottle when stored below 25°C [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
