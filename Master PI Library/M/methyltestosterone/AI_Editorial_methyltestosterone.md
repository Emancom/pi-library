# Editorial Product Information: Esterified Estrogens and Methyltestosterone

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination product containing esterified estrogens and methyltestosterone for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis based on provided regulatory documentation and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Note that the source document for the United States indicates this specific combination has not been found by the FDA to be safe and effective, and its labeling has not been approved by the FDA (DESI drug status) [US].

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** 
    - **Esterified Estrogens:** A mixture of sodium salts of sulfate esters of estrogenic substances (principally estrone) derived from pregnant mares.
    - **Methyltestosterone:** A synthetic 17-alpha-alkylandrogen (derivative of testosterone).
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in available sources (Distributed by Bryant Ranch Prepack) [US].
- **ATC Code:** G03EK (Androgens and estrogens in combination).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Full Strength Tablet:** 1.25 mg Esterified Estrogens / 2.5 mg Methyltestosterone [US].
    - **Half Strength (H.S.) Tablet:** 0.625 mg Esterified Estrogens / 1.25 mg Methyltestosterone [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ECI Pharmaceuticals (Manufacturer); Bryant Ranch Prepack (Repackager) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Vasomotor Symptoms:** Treatment of moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms associated with menopause in patients who have not achieved adequate improvement with estrogens alone [US].
- **Note:** This combination has not been shown to be effective for nervous symptoms or depression and should not be used for these purposes [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** The lowest effective dose should be used for the shortest duration consistent with treatment goals.
- **Usual Regimen:** 
    - 1 tablet (Full Strength) or 1 to 2 tablets (Half Strength) daily [US].
- **Administration Schedule:** 
    - **Cyclic Administration:** Typically administered for three weeks followed by one week off.
    - **Tapering:** Attempts to discontinue or taper the medication should be made at 3- to 6-month intervals [US].
- **Clinical Requirement:** In women with an intact uterus, a progestin should be initiated to reduce the risk of endometrial cancer [US].

## 4. Contraindications
Use is contraindicated in patients with [US]:
- Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding.
- Known, suspected, or history of breast cancer.
- Known or suspected estrogen-dependent neoplasia.
- Active or history of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE).
- Active or recent (within the past year) arterial thromboembolic disease (e.g., stroke, myocardial infarction).
- Liver dysfunction or disease (specifically severe liver damage for the methyltestosterone component).
- Known hypersensitivity to any ingredients.
- Known or suspected pregnancy.
- Breastfeeding mothers.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Endometrial Cancer:** Unopposed estrogen use in women with a uterus increases the risk of endometrial cancer. Close surveillance is mandatory.
- **Cardiovascular Risks:** Estrogens should not be used to prevent cardiovascular disease. Increased risks of myocardial infarction, stroke, DVT, and PE have been reported.
- **Dementia:** Increased risk of probable dementia in postmenopausal women ≥65 years of age.

### General Warnings
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Prolonged use of high-dose androgens is associated with peliosis hepatis and hepatic neoplasms, including hepatocellular carcinoma. Cholestatic hepatitis and jaundice may occur with 17-alpha-alkylandrogens even at low doses [US].
- **Virilization:** Women should be monitored for signs of virilization (deepening voice, hirsutism, acne, clitoromegaly). Therapy should be discontinued at the first sign of mild virilism to prevent irreversibility [US].
- **Visual Abnormalities:** Retinal vascular thrombosis has been reported. Discontinue immediately if there is sudden loss of vision or proptosis [US].
- **Hypercalcemia:** Androgens and estrogens may induce severe hypercalcemia in patients with breast cancer and bone metastases [US].
- **Fluid Retention:** Caution is required in patients with cardiac, renal, or hepatic dysfunction [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects

| System | Adverse Events |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Genitourinary** | Changes in bleeding patterns, breakthrough bleeding, dysmenorrhea, vaginitis, cystitis-like syndrome, clitoral enlargement [US]. |
| **Endocrine** | Amenorrhea, virilization, hirsutism, increased/decreased libido [US]. |
| **Breast** | Tenderness, enlargement, galactorrhea, fibrocystic changes, breast cancer [US]. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Myocardial infarction, stroke, VTE, hypertension, edema [US]. |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, cholestatic jaundice, gallbladder disease, pancreatitis [US]. |
| **Skin** | Acne, alopecia, chloasma/melasma, erythema multiforme, rash [US]. |

| **Metabolic** | Increased serum cholesterol, reduced glucose tolerance, weight changes [US]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Metabolism:** Estrogens are partially metabolized by CYP3A4. 
    - **Inducers** (e.g., St. John’s Wort, phenobarbital, rifampin) may decrease estrogen plasma levels.
    - **Inhibitors** (e.g., erythromycin, ketoconazole, ritonavir, grapefruit juice) may increase levels and risk of side effects [US].
- **Oral Anticoagulants:** Methyltestosterone may decrease the required dose of anticoagulants; close monitoring of INR/prothrombin time is required [US].
- **Insulin/Antidiabetics:** Androgens may decrease blood glucose and insulin requirements in diabetic patients [US].
- **Oxyphenbutazone:** Concurrent use may result in elevated serum levels of oxyphenbutazone [US].
- **Laboratory Interferences:** Androgens may decrease thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG) and protein-bound iodine (PBI). Estrogens may increase TBG, leading to increased total T4 and T3 but unchanged free levels [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X [US].** Methyltestosterone can cause masculinization of the female fetus. The drug is contraindicated in women who are or may become pregnant.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Estrogen can decrease the quantity and quality of breast milk. Androgens may pass into milk and pose a risk of serious adverse reactions in the infant [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for use in children [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Clinical experience has not identified differences in response compared to younger patients, but dose selection should be cautious (usually starting at the low end) due to higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Estrogens and androgens should be used with caution due to the risk of fluid retention and potential for poor metabolism in impaired liver function [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Estrogens:** Bind to nuclear receptors in estrogen-responsive tissues to modulate pituitary secretion of gonadotropins (LH, FSH) via negative feedback [US].
    - **Androgens:** Responsible for the growth and development of male sex organs and secondary characteristics. They promote nitrogen, sodium, and potassium retention and decrease calcium excretion [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption/Metabolism:** Methyltestosterone is a synthetic androgen designed for oral use as it is less extensively metabolized by the liver in the first pass compared to testosterone, resulting in a longer half-life [US].
    - **Distribution:** Estrogens and testosterone circulate largely bound to sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) and albumin [US].
    - **Excretion:** Primarily renal (urine) as glucuronide and sulfate conjugates [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Women’s Health Initiative (WHI):** This study highlighted increased risks of MI, stroke, invasive breast cancer, and VTE with conjugated estrogens and medroxyprogesterone. 
- **WHI Memory Study (WHIMS):** Demonstrated a twofold increase in the risk of developing probable dementia in women >65 years [US].
- **Note:** The specific combination of esterified estrogens and methyltestosterone was not the subject of these large-scale trials; risks are inferred from the class of hormones [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15° to 30°C (59° to 86°F) [US].
- **Container:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant, child-resistant container [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a **Schedule III (CIII)** controlled substance under the DEA due to the methyltestosterone component [US].
- **Legal Status:** Rx-only. Note the FDA disclaimer regarding "unapproved drug" status [US].
