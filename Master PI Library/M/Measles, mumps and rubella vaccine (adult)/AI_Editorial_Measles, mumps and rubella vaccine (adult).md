# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory information into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current official PI from their local regulatory authority for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN), a carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Acute Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no known contraindications listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks versus benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in both patients with and without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history, there may be an increase in seizure frequency or severity [US].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** 
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before starting parenteral therapy.
    - Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** High doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), Pain (21–35%), Headache (3–37%), Flu syndrome (15–29%), Infection (10–24%), Chest pain (6–15%), Asthenia (8–12%), Accidental injury (8–12%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%), Rhinitis (6–11%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Abdominal pain, Fever, Allergic reaction, Tachycardia, Palpitations, Arrhythmia, Anorexia, Constipation, Dyspepsia, Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Peripheral edema, Weight changes, Dizziness, Insomnia, Paresthesia, Pruritus, Rash, Eye disorder, Urinary tract infection [US].
- **Other Reported Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting, gastritis, and body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concurrently with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiation of levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins or albumin, suggesting a low potential for protein-binding displacement interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified in available sources [US].
    - **Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal constituent of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, Saccharomyces, Schizosaccharomyces) were negative. Long-term animal studies for carcinogenic potential have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing and administration are as specified in the general dosing section. Safety and efficacy for the approved indications are established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].
    - In ESRD, intravenous levocarnitine is indicated, but patients are at risk for hypersensitivity reactions and seizure activity [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients on hemodialysis results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study (Healthy Volunteers):** A study in 15 healthy males demonstrated that levocarnitine tablets and oral solution are bioequivalent. Absolute bioavailability (corrected for endogenous levels) is approximately 15% [US].
- **ESRD Studies:** 
    - A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg post-dialysis three times weekly showed that trough concentrations reached 54–180 µmol/L after one week (baseline ~20 µmol/L) [US].
    - Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels at 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
