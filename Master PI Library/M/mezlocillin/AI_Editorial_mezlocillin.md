# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- At the time of writing, official PI documents from other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinical synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection for intravenous use: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or infusion.
    - Acute Crisis: A loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Administer every 3 to 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours.
    - Maximum Dose: Up to 300 mg/kg has been administered in clinical practice [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose Adjustments: Should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (normal range: 40–50 µmol/L). Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation does not contain preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These may occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with established seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **General:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
    - **Warfarin Therapy:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). The safety of the oral formulation in renal impairment has not been fully evaluated [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trials Experience (ESRD Population)
Adverse events reported at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), headache (2–37%), chest pain (6–15%), fever (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%), and flu syndrome (15–29%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), and palpitations (3–8%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%), and anorexia (3–6%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), and dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Other:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%), and hypercalcemia (6–15%).

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Transient Reactions:** Nausea and vomiting; gastritis.
- **Body Odor:** A "fishy" body odor has been observed [US].
- **Serious Effects:** Seizures and anaphylaxis (see Warnings and Precautions).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Pharmacodynamic interaction resulting in increased INR. Close monitoring of coagulation parameters is required [US].
- **Other Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in provided text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal constituent of human milk. Supplementation has not been specifically studied in nursing mothers. Decisions should weigh the benefits of treatment against the potential for excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders follows the mg/kg guidelines provided for adults (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not provided in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **ESRD:** Levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. It is easily removed by dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation. It also clears toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitines, which are then excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute oral bioavailability is low (approximately 15–16%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly, mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed a dose-proportional increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
