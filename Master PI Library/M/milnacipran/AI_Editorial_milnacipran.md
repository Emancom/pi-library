# Editorial Product Information: Milnacipran Hydrochloride

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Where clinical guidelines or safety restrictions differ between these two agencies, the more comprehensive or conservative information has been included and attributed accordingly.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
*   **Active Ingredient:** Milnacipran hydrochloride
*   **Brand Names:** Joncia [TGA], Savella [FDA]
*   **Formulations:** 12.5 mg, 25 mg, 50 mg, and 100 mg oral capsules or tablets.

## 2. Approved Indications
Milnacipran is indicated for the management of fibromyalgia in adults. 

**Note:** Milnacipran is not approved for use in pediatric patients. While it belongs to the selective serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (SNRI) class, it is not approved for the treatment of major depressive disorder (MDD) in the regions covered by this document, although it is an antidepressant in other jurisdictions.

## 3. Contraindications
Milnacipran is contraindicated in the following cases:
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to milnacipran hydrochloride or any excipients.
*   **MAOIs:** Concomitant use with Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 14 days of discontinuing an MAOI. Conversely, at least 5 days should elapse after stopping milnacipran before starting an MAOI. This includes non-selective MAOIs, selective MAO-A or MAO-B inhibitors, and agents with MAOI activity such as linezolid or intravenous methylene blue [FDA].
*   **Severe Cardiovascular Disease:** Patients with severe cardiac impairment, unstable coronary heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension, or high risk of serious ventricular arrhythmia [TGA].
*   **Glaucoma:** Patients with uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma.
*   **Specific Drug Combinations:** Co-administration with adrenaline (epinephrine), noradrenaline (norepinephrine), or other serotonergic agents (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, tramadol, St John’s Wort) [TGA].
*   **Lactation:** Breastfeeding is strictly contraindicated [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Suicidality and Antidepressant Drugs [FDA Boxed Warning]
In short-term studies, antidepressants increased the risk of suicidal thinking and behavior (suicidality) in children, adolescents, and young adults (up to age 24) with MDD and other psychiatric disorders. Healthcare professionals should monitor all patients starting milnacipran for clinical worsening and emergence of suicidal thoughts, particularly during the first few months of therapy or during dose adjustments.

### Cardiovascular Effects
Milnacipran can increase blood pressure and heart rate. Pre-existing hypertension and cardiovascular disease should be treated before initiating therapy. Blood pressure and heart rate must be monitored at baseline and periodically throughout treatment. For patients experiencing a sustained, clinically significant increase in blood pressure or heart rate, dose reduction or discontinuation should be considered.

### Hepatotoxicity
Elevations in ALT and AST (1–3 times the upper limit of normal) have been observed. Post-marketing reports include rare cases of severe liver injury and fulminant hepatitis. Milnacipran should be avoided in patients with substantial alcohol use or evidence of chronic liver disease. Treatment should be discontinued if jaundice or other evidence of liver dysfunction occurs.

### Serotonin Syndrome
A potentially life-threatening condition may occur, particularly with concomitant use of other serotonergic drugs (including triptans, tricyclic antidepressants, lithium, and tryptophan). Symptoms include mental status changes (agitation, hallucinations), autonomic instability (tachycardia, labile blood pressure), and neuromuscular aberrations.

### Genitourinary Effects
Due to its noradrenergic effect, milnacipran may affect urethral resistance and micturition. Caution is advised in male patients with a history of obstructive uropathies, such as prostatic hypertrophy or prostatitis, as they may experience higher rates of dysuria or urinary retention.

### Discontinuation Syndrome
Abrupt discontinuation has been associated with withdrawal symptoms, including dizziness, headache, nausea, anxiety, and paresthesia ("electric shock" sensations). Doses should be tapered gradually over a period of no less than two weeks.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Milnacipran should be administered in two divided doses daily. It may be taken with or without food, though administration with food is recommended to improve gastrointestinal tolerability.

### Recommended Titration Schedule
The standard target dose is 100 mg per day (50 mg twice daily). Based on the source agencies, two titration pathways are suggested:

**Pathway A [FDA]:**
*   **Day 1:** 12.5 mg once.
*   **Days 2–3:** 25 mg/day (12.5 mg twice daily).
*   **Days 4–7:** 50 mg/day (25 mg twice daily).
*   **After Day 7:** 100 mg/day (50 mg twice daily).

**Pathway B [TGA]:**
*   **Days 1–2:** 25 mg once daily (evening).
*   **Days 3–7:** 50 mg/day (25 mg twice daily).
*   **After Day 7:** 100 mg/day (50 mg twice daily).

The dose may be increased to 200 mg/day (100 mg twice daily) based on individual patient response. Doses above 200 mg/day have not been studied.

### Renal Insufficiency
*   **Mild Impairment:** No adjustment required.
*   **Moderate Impairment:** Use with caution.
*   **Severe Impairment (GFR 5–29 mL/min):** Maintenance dose should be reduced by 50% (e.g., 50 mg/day in two divided doses).
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (CKD Stage 5):** Not recommended [FDA]; contraindicated [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequently reported adverse reactions (occurring in ≥5% of patients and significantly more than placebo) include:
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (most common, ~37%), constipation, vomiting, dry mouth, abdominal pain.
*   **Nervous System:** Headache, dizziness, insomnia, migraine, paresthesia, tremors.
*   **Vascular/Cardiac:** Hot flushes, palpitations, increased heart rate, hypertension, tachycardia.
*   **Skin:** Hyperhidrosis (increased sweating), rash, pruritus.
*   **Systemic:** Fatigue, chest pain, chills.

Male-specific adverse effects include dysuria, ejaculation disorder, and erectile dysfunction.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **MAOIs:** Use is contraindicated (see Section 3).
*   **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of Serotonin Syndrome when combined with SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, fentanyl, lithium, or St John's Wort.
*   **Catecholamines:** Potential for paroxysmal hypertension and arrhythmia when used with adrenaline or noradrenaline.
*   **Digoxin:** Potential for potentiation of hemodynamic effects, including postural hypotension and tachycardia.
*   **Clonidine:** Milnacipran may inhibit the antihypertensive effect of clonidine.
*   **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets:** Increased risk of bleeding when co-administered with warfarin, NSAIDs, or aspirin due to effects on platelet serotonin reuptake.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
*   **Category B3 [TGA]**: Limited data exist for use in pregnant women. Animal studies indicate potential embryofetal lethality at doses near the maximum human dose.
*   **Clinical Considerations:** Use late in the third trimester may increase the risk of neonatal complications (respiratory distress, feeding difficulties, tremors) and may be associated with an increased risk of postpartum hemorrhage [FDA]. Milnacipran should only be used if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus.

### Breastfeeding
Milnacipran is excreted in human milk. The TGA considers breastfeeding **contraindicated** during treatment. The FDA recommends weighing the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding against the mother's clinical need for the drug and potential adverse effects on the breastfed child.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Well-absorbed with an absolute bioavailability of 85–90%. Peak plasma concentrations ($C_{max}$) are reached within 2 to 4 hours.
*   **Distribution:** Low plasma protein binding (13%). The volume of distribution is approximately 5 L/kg [TGA] to 400 L [FDA].
*   **Metabolism:** Minimal hepatic metabolism via CYP450 isoenzymes. It is not an inducer or inhibitor of CYP1A2, 2D6, 2C19, or 3A4.
*   **Excretion:** Primarily renal (55% excreted unchanged in urine). The terminal elimination half-life is approximately 6 to 8 hours.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Milnacipran is a potent and selective dual reuptake inhibitor of norepinephrine and serotonin. In vitro studies demonstrate that milnacipran inhibits norepinephrine uptake with approximately 3-fold higher potency than serotonin. It has no significant affinity for dopaminergic, muscarinic, histaminergic, or GABA receptors, nor does it inhibit monoamine oxidase activity.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **TGA:** Store below 30°C.
*   **FDA:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F–86°F).
*   Keep out of reach of children.
