# Originality Audit Report: milnacipran

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 72/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 4
- **Overall Assessment:** Pass (with minor concern)

The editorial document demonstrates a high level of effort in synthesizing two distinct regulatory documents (TGA and FDA). However, several technical sections—specifically clinical titration schedules, the mechanism of action, and specific safety warnings—rely on near-verbatim or verbatim lifts from the source material. While some degree of verbatim carryover is expected in regulatory writing for medical accuracy, the "Breastfeeding" and "Dosage" sections are direct reproductions.

## Verbatim Copied Passages

**1. Recommended Titration Schedule (Pathway A)**
- **Editorial Text:** "Day 1: 12.5 mg once. Days 2–3: 25 mg/day (12.5 mg twice daily). Days 4–7: 50 mg/day (25 mg twice daily). After Day 7: 100 mg/day (50 mg twice daily)."
- **Source Match:** Document 2 (FDA), Section 2.1 (p. 1/4)
- **Location:** Editorial Section 5: Dosage and Administration.

**2. Breastfeeding Recommendation (FDA view)**
- **Editorial Text:** "weighing the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding against the mother's clinical need for the drug and potential adverse effects on the breastfed child."
- **Source Match:** Document 2 (FDA), Section 8.2 (p. 20)
- **Location:** Editorial Section 8: Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding.

**3. Hepatotoxicity Discontinuation**
- **Editorial Text:** "Milnacipran should be avoided in patients with substantial alcohol use or evidence of chronic liver disease. Treatment should be discontinued if jaundice or other evidence of liver dysfunction occurs."
- **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 4.4 (p. 7)
- **Location:** Editorial Section 4: Warnings and Precautions.

**4. Mechanism of Action (Norepinephrine potency)**
- **Editorial Text:** "milnacipran inhibits norepinephrine uptake with approximately 3-fold higher potency than serotonin. It has no significant affinity for dopaminergic, muscarinic, histaminergic, or GABA receptors..."
- **Source Match:** Document 2 (FDA), Section 12.1 (p. 23)
- **Location:** Editorial Section 10: Mechanism of Action.

## Close Paraphrases

**1. Cardiovascular Contraindications**
- **Editorial:** "Patients with severe cardiac impairment, unstable coronary heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension, or high risk of serious ventricular arrhythmia."
- **Source:** TGA Section 4.3 (p. 3). The editorial condenses the source's longer list into a single sentence but retains the exact list of conditions in the same order.

**2. Genitourinary Effects**
- **Editorial:** "Due to its noradrenergic effect, milnacipran may affect urethral resistance and micturition. Caution is advised in male patients with a history of obstructive uropathies, such as prostatic hypertrophy or prostatitis..."
- **Source:** TGA Section 4.4 (p. 7). The phrasing is almost identical, changing only "Because of their" to "Due to its" and "notably in male patients" to "Caution is advised in male patients."

## Originality Strengths
- **Section 2 (Approved Indications):** The author successfully synthesizes the status of milnacipran across different jurisdictions, noting its off-label/non-approved status for MDD in the US/Australia vs. other countries.
- **Section 5 (Renal Insufficiency):** This section expertly merges two different sets of guidelines (FDA vs. TGA) for GFR-based dosing, which required active comparison and restructuring of data.
- **Section 8 (Pregnancy):** The writer does a good job of separating the TGA's "Category B3" classification from the FDA's narrative risk assessment.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Harmonization:** The editorial creates a "Pathway A" and "Pathway B" for titration, which does not exist in the source documents. This is a unique structural choice to present conflicting regulatory data side-by-side.
- **Simplification of Adverse Events:** Instead of reproducing the massive tables from the PIs, the editorial synthesizes the data into "most frequently reported" categories, combining data points (e.g., merging "Vascular" and "Cardiac" descriptions).
- **Unit/Terminology Conversion:** The editorial correctly attributes specific brand names (Joncia vs. Savella) to their respective agencies, providing clarity not found in the individual source documents.

## Recommendation
**PASS**

The editorial is a successful synthesis. While some verbatim phrases remain, they are largely confined to medical facts (the 3-fold potency, the specific mg titration) where altering the wording too significantly could lead to clinical inaccuracy. To improve the "Originality Score," the author could further rephrase the "Lactation" section and the "Hepatotoxicity" warning, which are currently direct copies. No revision is strictly required for compliance, but it is recommended for a "strict" originality standard.
