# Editorial Product Information: Mecasermin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a concise reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information when prescribing or administering this medication in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mecasermin (recombinant DNA-derived human insulin-like growth factor-1 [IGF-1]).
- **Chemical Class:** Recombinant human insulin-like growth factor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Increlex** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** H01AC03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Solution for injection. Each multidose vial contains 40 mg of mecasermin in 4 mL (10 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Asteri Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Primary Insulin-like Growth Factor-1 Deficiency (Primary IGFD):** For the long-term management of growth failure in children and adolescents aged 2 to 18 years [Australian].
- **Diagnostic Criteria for Severe Primary IGFD [Australian]:**
    - Height standard deviation score (SDS) $\le -3.0$.
    - Baseline height velocity $< 25$th percentile for bone age (measured over 12 months).
    - Basal IGF-1 levels $< 2.5$th percentile for age and gender.
    - Growth hormone (GH) sufficiency.
    - Exclusion of secondary IGF-1 deficiency (e.g., malnutrition, hypopituitarism, hypothyroidism, or chronic high-dose steroid use).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Paediatric):** Treatment must be supervised by a paediatric endocrinologist. The dose is highly individualised based on growth velocity and tolerance.
- **Starting Dose:** 0.04 mg/kg body weight administered twice daily via subcutaneous injection [Australian].
- **Titration:** If tolerated for at least one week, the dose may be increased in increments of 0.04 mg/kg to a maximum of 0.12 mg/kg twice daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer shortly before or after a meal or snack to mitigate hypoglycaemic risk.
    - Rotate injection sites (upper arm, thigh, buttock, or abdomen) with every dose to prevent lipohypertrophy.
    - **Do not** administer intravenously.
    - The solution should be clear; do not use if cloudy or contains particulate matter.
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, it should be withheld. Do not increase the subsequent dose to compensate for a missed dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to mecasermin or any excipients (including benzyl alcohol) [Australian].
- **Neoplasia:** Active or suspected neoplasia, or any medical history/condition that increases the risk of benign or malignant neoplasia. Therapy must be discontinued if neoplasia develops [Australian].
- **Neonatal Use:** Must not be given to premature babies or neonates due to the presence of benzyl alcohol [Australian].
- **Epiphyseal Closure:** Not for use for growth promotion in patients with closed epiphyses [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neoplasia Risk:** There is a known risk of benign and malignant neoplasms. IGF-1 is a potent mitogen. Physicians should perform baseline and regular skin checks for naevi and remain vigilant for symptoms of malignancy [Australian].
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Mecasermin possesses insulin-like glucose-lowering effects. Monitoring is critical during initiation and titration. Patients should avoid high-risk activities (e.g., driving or operating machinery) for 2–3 hours post-dose, particularly at the start of therapy [Australian].
- **Lymphoid Tissue Hypertrophy:** Hypertrophy of tonsils and adenoids is common and may lead to complications such as snoring, sleep apnoea, or middle-ear effusions. Periodic examinations are required [Australian].
- **Intracranial Hypertension (IH):** Reported cases involve papilloedema, visual changes, headache, and vomiting. Funduscopic exams are recommended at initiation and during treatment [Australian].
- **Musculoskeletal Effects:** Rapid growth may lead to slipped capital femoral epiphysis or progression of scoliosis. Patients exhibiting a limp or hip/knee pain should be evaluated immediately [Australian].
- **Benzyl Alcohol Toxicity:** The preservative benzyl alcohol is associated with "gasping syndrome" in neonates. Use is restricted to patients $\ge 2$ years old [Australian].
- **Antibody Formation:** Allergic reactions or failure to respond to treatment may indicate the development of anti-IGF-1 antibodies [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (44%), hypoglycaemia (28%), vomiting (26%), injection site hypertrophy (17%), otitis media (17%), arthralgia, and upper abdominal pain [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Thymus hypertrophy, hypoglycaemic seizure, hyperglycaemia, convulsions, dizziness, papilloedema, cardiac murmur, tachycardia, sleep apnoea, tonsillar/adenoidal hypertrophy, snoring, scoliosis, gynaecomastia, and injection site reactions (bruising, pain, erythema) [Australian].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Depression, benign intracranial hypertension, cardiomegaly, ventricular hypertrophy, mitral/tricuspid valve incompetence, and lipohypertrophy [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - **Anaphylaxis:** Systemic hypersensitivity reactions requiring hospitalisation have been reported post-marketing.
    - **Neoplasia:** Both benign and malignant tumours reported.
    - **Hypoglycaemic Seizure:** Risk is highest in young children with inconsistent food intake.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Insulin and Antidiabetics:** Doses of insulin or other hypoglycaemic agents may require reduction due to the additive glucose-lowering effects of mecasermin [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal interaction studies have been performed [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. There are no adequate data in pregnant women. Animal studies showed embryofetal lethality at high doses. A negative pregnancy test is recommended for women of childbearing potential before starting treatment, and adequate contraception should be used during therapy. Mecasermin is not recommended during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Endogenous IGF-1 is present in human milk. Due to insufficient data on mecasermin excretion and potential risks to the infant, breastfeeding while taking this medication is not recommended [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility were observed in animal models (rats) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children 2 to 18 years. Safety and efficacy in children under 2 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No data available for patients over 65 years [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Data are limited in these populations. Dosing should be individualised based on clinical response and tolerance [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Mecasermin is a recombinant human IGF-1. IGF-1 is the primary mediator of statural growth, acting via the Type 1 IGF-1 receptor to stimulate intracellular signalling, leading to skeletal and organ growth. It also increases bone density and stimulates the uptake of glucose and amino acids into tissues [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Subcutaneous bioavailability is approximately 100% in healthy subjects [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 80% is bound to IGF binding protein-3 (IGFBP-3) and an acid-labile subunit. Volume of distribution is approximately 0.257 L/kg [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised by both the liver and kidneys [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life is approximately 5.8 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Clinical efficacy was established in five studies (4 open-label, 1 double-blind) involving 92 paediatric subjects with severe Primary IGFD.
- **Results:** Treatment-naive subjects showed a significant increase in height velocity. Mean height velocity increased from a baseline of 2.6 cm/year to 8.0 cm/year in the first year. Growth velocity remained statistically greater than baseline through 8 years of treatment. The average height gain over 11 years of treatment was approximately 13 cm greater than expected without treatment [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Protection:** Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life/Stability:** Once opened, the multidose vial is stable for 30 days when stored at 2°C to 8°C. Any remaining product after 30 days must be discarded [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (S4)** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription only.
