# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP), also known as L-carnitine.
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring amino acid derivative and carrier molecule.
- **Brand Names:** Noted in the source as **Levocarnitine Injection, USP** (distributed by Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from metabolic disorders [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing chronic hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose may be followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing intervals should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Hemodialysis:** Administered as a slow bolus (2-3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL. Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Subsequent daily doses should be titrated based on clinical response and plasma carnitine levels (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L). The highest reported dose used is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD:** Dose adjustments may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, potentially down to 5 mg/kg, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur in ESRD patients and may manifest within minutes of administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with established epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been noted [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with renal insufficiency or ESRD has not been evaluated. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trials Experience (ESRD Patients)
In placebo-controlled trials of hemodialysis patients, the following effects were reported (≥5% frequency):
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), injection site reaction (33%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), arrhythmia (2%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), insomnia (4%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough (9%), dyspnea (7%).

### Serious and Postmarketing Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis:** Reported following intravenous administration [US].
- **Neurological:** Seizures (both new-onset and exacerbation of existing disorders) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended following initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C equivalent]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosage is consistent with adult weight-based dosing (e.g., 50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not detailed in source; generally requires caution reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosage adjustments for ESRD patients are provided in Section 3. The drug is easily removed by hemodialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates the effects of carnitine loss caused by dialysis and reduced renal synthesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** Bioavailability of oral forms (tablets and solution) is approximately 15-16% after correction for endogenous levels.
- **Dialysis Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L by week 8 [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
