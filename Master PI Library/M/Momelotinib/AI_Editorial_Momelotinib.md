# Editorial Product Information: Momelotinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **momelotinib** intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, safety profiles, and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved by the relevant regulatory authority in a specific jurisdiction for the purpose of prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Momelotinib (as dihydrochloride monohydrate). It is a Janus Kinase (JAK) 1 and 2 inhibitor and an activin A receptor type 1 (ACVR1) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Omjjara** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - 100 mg film-coated tablets (brown, round, debossed "M" and "100").
  - 150 mg film-coated tablets (brown, triangle, debossed "M" and "150").
  - 200 mg film-coated tablets (brown, capsule-shaped, debossed "M" and "200").
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** GlaxoSmithKline Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Momelotinib** is indicated for the treatment of disease-related splenomegaly or symptoms in adult patients with moderate to severe anaemia who have [Australian]:
- Primary myelofibrosis;
- Post polycythaemia vera myelofibrosis; or 
- Post essential thrombocythaemia myelofibrosis.

Eligible patients include those who are Janus Kinase (JAK) inhibitor naïve or those previously treated with ruxolitinib.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dose:** The recommended starting dose is 200 mg taken orally once daily. 
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food. Tablets should be swallowed whole and not crushed or chewed.
- **Dose Modifications for Toxicity:**
  - **Thrombocytopenia:** 
    - If baseline $\ge100 \times 10^9/L$ and count drops to $20 - <50 \times 10^9/L$: Reduce daily dose by 50 mg.
    - If count drops below $20 \times 10^9/L$: Interrupt treatment until recovery to $\ge50 \times 10^9/L$, then restart at 50 mg below the last dose.
  - **Neutropenia:** If Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) $<0.5 \times 10^9/L$: Interrupt treatment until ANC recovers to $\ge0.75 \times 10^9/L$, then restart at 50 mg below the last dose.
  - **Hepatotoxicity:** If ALT/AST $>5 \times$ ULN or Bilirubin $>2 \times$ ULN: Interrupt treatment. If recovery occurs, restart at 50 mg lower. Permanently discontinue if ALT/AST elevations $>5 \times$ ULN recur.
  - **Non-haematologic Toxicity:** For Grade 3 or higher (or Grade 2+ bleeding): Interrupt until resolved to Grade 1 or baseline, then restart 50 mg lower.
- **Minimum Dose:** Treatment should be discontinued if a patient cannot tolerate 100 mg once daily.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, take the next scheduled dose the following day. Do not double the dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Momelotinib is contraindicated during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients (including lactose).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serious Infections:** Bacterial and viral infections, including fatal cases (e.g., COVID-19), have occurred. Do not initiate in patients with active infections.
- **Hepatitis B Reactivation:** JAK inhibitors have been associated with increased HBV viral load. Screen and monitor patients with chronic HBV according to clinical guidelines.
- **Thrombocytopenia and Neutropenia:** Significant myelosuppression (Grade $\ge3$) can occur. Monitor Complete Blood Count (CBC) before initiation and periodically during treatment.
- **Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events (MACE):** Although not causally established for **momelotinib**, MACE (cardiovascular death, MI, stroke) has been observed in the JAK inhibitor class. Cautious use is advised in patients $>65$ years, smokers, or those with existing CV risk factors.
- **Thrombosis:** Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE) have been reported. Evaluate symptoms of thrombosis promptly.
- **Second Primary Malignancies:** There is a potential class risk for malignancies (e.g., lung cancer, lymphoma, non-melanoma skin cancer) based on studies of other JAK inhibitors.
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Potential for reduced effectiveness of hormonal contraceptives. Use a barrier method during treatment and for 1 week after the final dose.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency based on clinical trials (MOMENTUM, SIMPLIFY-1, SIMPLIFY-2).

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Infections (39.5%), Thrombocytopenia (21%), Diarrhoea (22.8%), Nausea (16.7%), Headache (13.4%), Dizziness (12.9%), Fatigue (12.3%), Asthenia (10.9%), Abdominal pain (10.7%), Cough (10%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Neutropenia (5.8%), Peripheral neuropathy (8.7%), Rash (5.6%), Arthralgia (7.6%), Pyrexia (9.2%), Vomiting (8%), Constipation (9.6%), Hypertension (6%), ALT/AST increased (4.7%/3.6%), Vitamin B1 deficiency (3.1%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Grade $\ge3$ thrombocytopenia (12%) was the most common severe reaction. Reversible drug-induced liver injury and serious infections (10%) have also been reported.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Co-administration with strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., carbamazepine, phenytoin, St John’s wort) may reduce **momelotinib** exposure and efficacy. Monitor closely.
- **Transporters:** **Momelotinib** is a substrate of P-gp, BCRP, OATP1B1, and OATP1B3.
- **Effect on Other Drugs:**
  - **BCRP and MATE1 Substrates:** **Momelotinib** inhibits these transporters and may increase plasma concentrations of their substrates (e.g., rosuvastatin, sulfasalazine).
  - **UGT1A1 and UGT1A9:** Inhibited by **momelotinib** *in vitro*.
  - **CYP2B6:** Inhibited by **momelotinib** *in vitro*.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Animal studies demonstrate embryofetal toxicity, including malformations and death at exposures exceeding human therapeutic levels. Highly effective contraception is required during treatment and for 1 week post-therapy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use is prohibited. Animal data suggests presence in milk; a risk to the infant cannot be excluded. Discontinue breastfeeding during and for 1 week after treatment.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed reduced male and female fertility at high doses. Human data is not available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric:** Safety and efficacy in children/adolescents under 18 years are not established.
- **Elderly:** No dose adjustment is required for patients $\ge65$ years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild, moderate, or severe impairment. No data is available for patients on dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
  - Mild or Moderate (Child-Pugh A or B): No dose adjustment required.
  - Severe (Child-Pugh C): Recommended starting dose is **150 mg once daily**.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits JAK1 and JAK2 (reducing inflammatory signalling) and ACVR1/ALK2. Inhibition of ACVR1 down-regulates liver hepcidin expression, increasing iron availability and promoting erythropoiesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Rapid; $T_{max}$ within 3 hours. Food slightly increases exposure but is not clinically significant.
  - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (91%). Large volume of distribution (984 L) suggests extensive tissue distribution.
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 (36%), with contributions from CYP2C8, 2C19, 2C9, and 1A2. The major active metabolite, M21, has 40% of the parent's activity.
  - **Elimination:** $t_{1/2}$ is 4 to 8 hours. Excreted mainly in faeces (69%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MOMENTUM:** Double-blind, active-controlled (vs danazol) study in symptomatic, anaemic myelofibrosis patients previously treated with a JAK inhibitor. **Momelotinib** was superior in achieving a $\ge50\%$ reduction in Total Symptom Score (TSS) and demonstrated non-inferiority in transfusion independence.
- **SIMPLIFY-1:** Double-blind, active-controlled (vs ruxolitinib) study in JAK-inhibitor-naïve patients. **Momelotinib** was non-inferior for spleen volume reduction but did not meet the non-inferiority margin for TSS reduction compared to ruxolitinib.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C. 
- **Handling:** Store in the original bottle to protect from moisture. Do not remove the desiccant.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring by the TGA to identify new safety information [Australian].
