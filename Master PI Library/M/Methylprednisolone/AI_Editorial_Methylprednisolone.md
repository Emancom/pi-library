# Editorial Product Information: Methylprednisolone Aceponate

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of methylprednisolone aceponate for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a structured reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI in a specific jurisdiction when prescribing. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for the most current prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methylprednisolone aceponate (a synthetic glucocorticoid).
- **Brand Name(s):** Advantan [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** D07AC14.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Cream: 1 mg/g (0.1%) [Australian].
    - Lotion: 1 mg/g (0.1%) [Australian].
    - Ointment: 1 mg/g (0.1%) [Australian].
    - Fatty Ointment: 1 mg/g (0.1%) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** External topical use only; not for ophthalmic use.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** LEO Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Eczema:** Topical treatment of various forms of eczema in adults and children [Australian-approved].
- **Psoriasis:** Topical treatment of psoriasis in adults and children [Australian-approved for Cream, Ointment, and Fatty Ointment formulations; Lotion not indicated for psoriasis].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Apply a thin coating to the affected skin once daily. For the treatment of psoriasis, twice-daily application may be required [Australian].
- **Dosing by Formulation:**
    - **Lotion:** Apply sparingly once daily and rub in gently. Ideal for acute eczema, large areas, and hairy regions due to cooling properties and low occlusion [Australian].
    - **Cream:** Suitable for acute/subacute weeping eczema and greasy skin [Australian].
    - **Ointment:** For skin that is neither weeping nor very dry; provides balanced fat/water proportions [Australian].
    - **Fatty Ointment:** For very dry/chronic conditions; provides high occlusion for thick skin areas (e.g., elbows, palms) [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** If skin dries excessively with cream or lotion, transition to a formulation with higher fat content (ointment or fatty ointment) [Australian].
- **Treatment Duration:** 
    - Adults: Duration should not exceed 12 weeks [Australian].
    - Children: Duration should not exceed 4 weeks [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Infections:** Presence of viral diseases (e.g., herpes zoster, varicella, vaccinia), tuberculosis, or syphilitic processes in the treatment area [Australian].
- **Skin Reactions:** Post-vaccination skin reactions in the area to be treated [Australian].
- **Facial Conditions:** Rosacea, acne vulgaris, perioral dermatitis, or atrophic skin diseases when applied to the face [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to methylprednisolone aceponate or any excipient (e.g., cetostearyl alcohol or butylated hydroxytoluene in the cream formulation) [Australian].
- **Age:** Children under four months of age [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Precautions:** For external use only. Avoid contact with the eyes, deep open wounds, or mucous membranes [Australian].
- **Secondary Infections:** Skin conditions infected with bacteria or fungi require specific additional therapy. If infection spreads, topical corticosteroid therapy must be withdrawn [Australian].
- **Systemic Absorption:** Potent corticosteroids can be absorbed percutaneously, potentially leading to hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression and Cushing’s syndrome. Risk increases with large surface area treatment, prolonged use, or occlusive dressings [Australian].
- **Visual Disturbance:** Both systemic and topical corticosteroid use may lead to visual disturbances. Reported cases include cataract, glaucoma, and central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR). Referral to an ophthalmologist is recommended if blurred vision occurs [Australian].
- **Local Effects:** Prolonged treatment may cause skin atrophy, striae, or telangiectasia. Discontinue use if cutaneous atrophy occurs [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Some excipients may reduce the integrity of latex products (e.g., condoms, diaphragms) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by formulation and frequency according to MedDRA conventions.

### Common (1–10%)
- **Cream/Ointment:** Application site burning, application site pruritus [Australian].
- **Fatty Ointment:** Application site folliculitis, application site burning [Australian].

### Uncommon (0.1–1%)
- **Cream:** Application site dryness, erythema, vesicles, folliculitis, rash, paraesthesia; drug hypersensitivity [Australian].
- **Ointment:** Application site erythema, dryness, vesicles, irritation, eczema, papules; peripheral oedema; skin atrophy, ecchymosis, impetigo, greasy skin [Australian].
- **Fatty Ointment:** Application site pustules, vesicles, pruritus, pain, erythema, papules; skin fissures, telangiectasia [Australian].

### Serious/Rare Adverse Effects
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Application site cellulitis, oedema, irritation. Skin disorders including pyoderma, skin fissures, telangiectasia, skin atrophy, and fungal infections [Australian].
- **Post-marketing:** Blurred vision (frequency not known) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Specific Interactions:** No specific information on interactions with other medications is available in provided sources [Australian].
- **Latex Products:** Excipients in all formulations may reduce the effectiveness of latex condoms and diaphragms [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Animal studies show embryolethal and teratogenic effects (cleft palate, ventricular septal defects) at systemic or high dermal doses. Epidemiological data suggest a possible risk of oral clefts in infants following 1st-trimester glucocorticoid use. Use should be avoided during the first trimester; clinical benefits must be weighed against risks thereafter. Avoid large area or prolonged treatment (>4 weeks) [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if methylprednisolone aceponate is secreted in human milk. Dermal absorption at therapeutic doses is minimal, but nursing mothers must not apply the product to the chest area to prevent infant ingestion. Avoid large areas or occlusive dressings during lactation [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Children are more susceptible to HPA axis suppression and Cushing’s syndrome due to a higher skin surface area to bodyweight ratio. Napkins/plastic pants may act as occlusive dressings. Chronic therapy may interfere with growth. Limit use to the minimum amount required for efficacy [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific data provided in available sources for topical application [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Methylprednisolone aceponate is a corticosteroid with anti-inflammatory, anti-pruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. It binds to the intracellular glucocorticoid receptor.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Specifically binds to DNA regions, inducing lipomodulin synthesis. This inhibits arachidonic acid release and subsequent inflammatory mediators (prostaglandins and leukotrienes). It also has immunosuppressive and anti-mitotic effects [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioactivation:** Hydrolysed in the epidermis and dermis to the more active metabolite, 6α-methylprednisolone-17-propionate [Australian].
    - **Absorption:** Percutaneous absorption is low (typically <2.5% in inflamed skin). Absorption increases significantly (up to 15%) if the stratum corneum is removed [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Once systemic, the 17-propionate metabolite is inactivated via glucuronide conjugation. Elimination occurs primarily via the kidneys with a half-life of approximately 16 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Eczema (Lotion):** A 14-day, double-blind, vehicle-controlled study (n=147) demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in eczema sum scores (erythema, oedema, vesicles, papules, weeping, itching) favouring methylprednisolone aceponate lotion over vehicle (p=0.001) [Australian].
- **Comparative Efficacy:** In separate 14-day trials, methylprednisolone aceponate 0.1% lotion was found to be therapeutically equivalent to amcinonide 0.1% lotion and betamethasone 17-valerate 0.1% lotion [Australian].
- **Paediatric Eczema:** A 14-day trial in children showed methylprednisolone aceponate 0.1% lotion once daily was equivalent to hydrocortisone 17-butyrate 0.1% lotion applied twice daily [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Ointment or Cream:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Fatty Ointment or Lotion:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Do not use beyond the expiry date; do not use if packaging is tampered with [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
