# Editorial Product Information: methotrexate

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): methotrexate
- Chemical class: folate analogue; metabolic inhibitor of folate-dependent enzymes
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: oral (other routes are not described in the available sources for this formulation; alternate formulations should be used for non-oral routes)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 1953 (as stated in available sources) [US]
- Date(s) of last revision/update of available product information: May 2026 [US]
- Recent major labeling changes noted in available sources: boxed warning revised May 2026; other warnings/precautions updated May 2026 [US]
- Withdrawals, suspensions, restrictions: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of pediatric patients with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) as part of a multi‑phase, combination chemotherapy maintenance regimen. [US]
- Management of pediatric patients with active polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis (pJIA) who are intolerant of or had an inadequate response to first‑line therapy (including full‑dose NSAIDs). [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General principles
- Methotrexate dosing in the available sources is presented for oral administration. For patients requiring non-oral routes of administration, use an alternate methotrexate formulation appropriate to that route.
- Instruct patients and caregivers that the prescribed dose is given once weekly unless otherwise directed. Mistaken daily administration of the weekly dose has resulted in fatal toxicity; emphasize weekly dosing and the risk of medication error.
- Methotrexate oral solution must be measured with an accurate milliliter measuring device. A household teaspoon is not accurate; pharmacists should provide an appropriate device and instructions.

### Dosing by indication (starting/recommended doses reported in available sources)
- Acute lymphoblastic leukemia (pediatric, maintenance): recommended starting dose 20 mg/m2 given once weekly. Monitor hematologic parameters (ANC and platelet counts) and adjust to maintain desired ANC and avoid excessive hematologic toxicity.
- Polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis (pediatric): recommended starting dose 10 mg/m2 given once weekly. Dosage should be individualized and adjusted gradually. Experience exists with doses up to 30 mg/m2/week in pediatric patients, but doses >20 mg/m2/week increase the incidence and severity of serious toxic reactions, especially bone marrow suppression. Doses between 20 and 30 mg/m2/week may have better absorption and fewer GI side effects if administered by a non-oral route using an alternative formulation.
- Formulation strength noted in available sources: 2.5 mg/mL oral solution.

### Administration instructions
- Administer the prescribed dose once weekly as directed.
- Use an accurate milliliter measuring device; advise patients not to use household teaspoons.
- Counsel on avoidance of concomitant live virus vaccines during therapy (see Warnings and Precautions).

### Titration and response
- For pJIA: therapeutic response typically begins within 3 to 6 weeks; improvement may continue for up to 12 additional weeks or longer.
- Adjust doses gradually to optimize therapeutic response and minimize toxicity; monitor hematologic, hepatic and renal parameters during titration and maintenance.

### Missed dose
- Specific instructions for missed doses are Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List includes all contraindications stated in available sources:
- Pregnancy in patients treated for non‑malignant diseases (e.g., use in pJIA is contraindicated in pregnancy due to risk of embryo‑fetal toxicity and fetal death). [US]
- Known severe hypersensitivity to methotrexate. [US]

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning
- **Severe toxic reactions, including embryo‑fetal toxicity, and risk of medication errors.** Methotrexate can cause severe or fatal toxicities including but not limited to bone marrow suppression, serious infections, renal failure, severe gastrointestinal toxicity, hepatic failure, pulmonary toxicity, and severe dermatologic reactions. Mistaken daily administration of a weekly regimen has resulted in fatal outcomes. [US]

### General warnings and precautions (comprehensive)
- Bone marrow suppression: may cause severe, life‑threatening pancytopenia, anemia, leukopenia, neutropenia and thrombocytopenia. Obtain baseline and periodic blood counts; adjust dose or discontinue as needed.
- Serious infections and opportunistic infections: increased risk of bacterial, fungal and viral infections including Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia, invasive fungal infections, hepatitis B reactivation, tuberculosis (primary infection or reactivation), disseminated herpes zoster, and cytomegalovirus infections. Monitor closely and treat promptly; consider dose modification or discontinuation for serious infection.
- Renal toxicity and increased toxicity with renal impairment: methotrexate can cause renal damage including acute renal failure. Monitor renal function; increased systemic toxicity occurs with renal impairment. Consider administration of glucarpidase for patients with toxic methotrexate plasma concentrations (>1 micromole/L) and delayed clearance due to impaired renal function.
- Gastrointestinal toxicity: can cause diarrhea, vomiting, stomatitis, hemorrhagic enteritis and intestinal perforation. Patients with peptic ulcer disease or ulcerative colitis are at higher risk. Concomitant NSAIDs may increase the risk of severe GI toxicity, particularly at high methotrexate doses.
- Hepatic toxicity: can cause severe, potentially irreversible hepatotoxicity including fibrosis, cirrhosis and liver failure. Avoid use in patients with chronic liver disease. Assess liver function before initiation and monitor periodically. Risk factors include alcoholism, obesity, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, prior exposure to hepatotoxins, family history of inheritable liver disease, persistent abnormal LFTs, longer duration of therapy and advanced age.
- Pulmonary toxicity: methotrexate‑induced interstitial pneumonitis and other pulmonary toxicities, some irreversible or fatal, have occurred at all dose levels. Monitor for respiratory symptoms; discontinue if suspected.
- Hypersensitivity and severe dermatologic reactions: severe skin reactions (including toxic epidermal necrolysis and Stevens–Johnson syndrome), anaphylaxis and other serious hypersensitivity reactions have occurred. Discontinue and treat appropriately for severe reactions. Radiation dermatitis and sunburn may be "recalled" by methotrexate.
- Secondary malignancies: cases of lymphoproliferative disorders have been reported with low‑dose methotrexate and in some instances regressed upon withdrawal of methotrexate; discontinue methotrexate if lymphoma is suspected and manage as clinically indicated.
- Embryo‑fetal toxicity: can cause fetal malformations and fetal death. Methotrexate is contraindicated in pregnant women treated for non‑malignant indications. Counsel females and males of reproductive potential about effective contraception during treatment and for specified intervals after treatment (see below).
- Ineffective immunization and risks with live vaccines: immunization may be ineffective during methotrexate therapy. Live viral vaccines are not recommended; disseminated vaccinia infections have been reported with smallpox immunization in patients receiving methotrexate.
- Effects on reproduction: may impair fertility, cause oligospermia and menstrual dysfunction; reversibility is not established.
- Third‑space accumulations: methotrexate may sequester in third‑space fluid (e.g., pleural effusions, ascites) prolonging half‑life and toxicity; evacuate significant third‑space fluid prior to administration when feasible.
- Radiation therapy interactions: concomitant or sequential radiation therapy increases the risk of soft tissue necrosis and osteonecrosis.
- Medication errors: emphasize weekly dosing schedule and use of accurate measuring device. Household spoons are not accurate and have led to overdoses. Pharmacists should counsel and provide appropriate measuring devices.

### Monitoring requirements
- Hematology: baseline and periodic complete blood counts (at least monthly during maintenance therapy in general practice); increase frequency during initial dosing, dose changes, or periods of increased risk of elevated methotrexate levels (e.g., dehydration).
- Renal function: assess baseline and monitor periodically (every 1–2 months is suggested for routine therapy in available sources); increase monitoring when renal risk is present.
- Liver function tests: assess baseline and monitor every 1–2 months; persistent abnormalities or decreased serum albumin warrant further evaluation.
- Pulmonary function tests: may be useful if methotrexate‑induced lung disease is suspected, especially if baseline measurements are available.

### Handling
- Methotrexate is a cytotoxic agent; follow applicable special handling and disposal procedures.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- The most frequently reported adverse reactions in clinical experience include ulcerative stomatitis, leukopenia, nausea, abdominal distress, and elevated liver function tests. Other frequently reported reactions include malaise, fatigue, chills, fever, dizziness and decreased resistance to infection.
- Postmarketing experience has identified additional adverse reactions across multiple organ systems (listed below).

### Frequency definitions used in this document
- Very common: >10%

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic interactions and clinical management
- Penicillins: may reduce renal clearance of methotrexate and increase serum concentrations, leading to hematologic and GI toxicity. Monitor patients closely when co‑administered.
- Trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole (co‑trimoxazole): has been reported to increase bone marrow suppression in patients receiving methotrexate. Monitor blood counts if used concurrently.
- Oral antibiotics (general): may increase hematologic and gastrointestinal toxicity with methotrexate. Monitor patients accordingly.
- Nonsteroidal anti‑inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), aspirin, and systemic corticosteroids: may elevate and prolong serum methotrexate concentrations and increase gastrointestinal toxicity; monitor patients when used concomitantly and consider dose adjustments or additional monitoring.
- Nitrous oxide: may increase the risk of methotrexate toxicity. Avoid or use with caution; monitor closely.
- Hepatotoxins (e.g., azathioprine, retinoids, sulfasalazine and other agents listed as potential hepatotoxins in available sources): coadministration may increase risk of hepatotoxicity; monitor liver function tests closely.
- Vaccines: immunizations may be less effective during methotrexate therapy; live viral vaccines are not recommended due to risk of disseminated infection.
- Glucarpidase: recommended consideration for patients with toxic methotrexate plasma concentrations (>1 micromole/L) and delayed methotrexate clearance due to impaired renal function.

### Jurisdiction-specific interactions
- No jurisdiction-specific drug interaction differences are specified in the available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Embryo‑fetal toxicity:** Methotrexate can cause fetal malformations and fetal death when administered to a pregnant woman. Methotrexate is contraindicated in pregnant patients treated for non‑malignant diseases (e.g., pJIA). In patients treated for malignancy, the risks to the fetus should be considered against maternal benefit.
- Contraception:
  - Females of reproductive potential: advise effective contraception during therapy and for 6 months after the final methotrexate dose. [US]
  - Males of reproductive potential: advise use of effective contraception during therapy and for at least 3 months after the final methotrexate dose. [US]
- Pregnancy category/classification systems (e.g., Australian category, US lettered categories): Not specified in available sources.

### Breastfeeding
- Advise women not to breastfeed during methotrexate therapy. [US]

### Fertility
- Methotrexate may impair fertility, cause oligospermia and menstrual dysfunction. It is not established whether infertility is reversible in affected patients.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Methotrexate is approved for pediatric use in the indications noted (pediatric ALL as part of maintenance combination chemotherapy; pediatric pJIA). Dosage recommendations for these pediatric indications are provided above.
- Pediatric pharmacokinetic adjustments beyond weight or body surface area–based dosing are Not specified in available sources.

### Elderly
- Advanced age is identified as a risk factor for hepatotoxicity. Specific dose reductions or formal dosing guidance for elderly patients are Not specified in available sources.

### Renal impairment
- Methotrexate toxicity is increased in renal impairment. Monitor renal function closely. Consider glucarpidase in patients with toxic plasma methotrexate concentrations (>1 micromole/L) and delayed clearance due to renal impairment. Specific dose adjustments by eGFR or creatinine clearance are Not specified in available sources.

### Hepatic impairment
- Avoid use in patients with chronic liver disease. Monitor LFTs and evaluate persistent abnormalities; specific dosing adjustments by severity of hepatic impairment are Not specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Methotrexate is a folate analogue that inhibits folate‑dependent enzymes, disrupting DNA synthesis and cell replication (described as a folate‑antagonist metabolic inhibitor in available sources).

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption, distribution, metabolism and elimination: detailed numeric pharmacokinetic parameters (e.g., bioavailability, half‑life, clearance, protein binding) are Not specified in available sources.
- Third‑space accumulation: methotrexate can be sequestered in third‑space fluid collections (e.g., ascites, pleural effusions), resulting in prolonged terminal plasma half‑life and increased toxicity; evacuation of significant third‑space fluid is recommended prior to administration when clinically feasible.
- Toxic plasma concentration threshold prompting consideration of glucarpidase: >1 micromole/L in the setting of delayed clearance due to impaired renal function.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Specific pharmacodynamic parameters are Not specified in available sources beyond the mechanism of action and clinical toxicities.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Clinical-trial data in available sources are described primarily in relation to adverse‑reaction incidence in pJIA patients treated with weekly oral methotrexate (5–20 mg/m2/week or 0.1–0.65 mg/kg/week). In that clinical experience, approximate incidences included:
  - Elevated liver function tests: 14%
  - Gastrointestinal reactions (e.g., nausea, vomiting, diarrhea): 11%
  - Stomatitis: 2%
  - Leukopenia: 2%
  - Headache: 1.2%
  - Alopecia: 0.5%
  - Dizziness: 0.2%
  - Rash: 0.2%
- Details of pivotal trials for the pediatric ALL indication (design, endpoints, outcomes) and additional trial summaries are Not specified in the available sources.
