# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, legally binding Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the United States PI was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection, solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - **Crisis Management:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours (administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours).
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Timing:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustments:** Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Monitoring
- Pre-treatment plasma carnitine levels should be obtained.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported. These may occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Note):** While this document concerns the injection, clinicians should note that the safety/efficacy of high-dose *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal failure and may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD)
In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, the following adverse events occurred at a frequency $\ge$ 5% regardless of causality:
- **General:** Abdominal pain, chest pain, headache, injection site reaction (38% at 10 mg/kg), fever, flu syndrome.
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%).
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia, insomnia.
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase, dyspnea.

### Postmarketing & Other Experience
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
- **Uncommon/Rare:** Body odor, gastritis.
- **Serious:** Seizures, anaphylaxis [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored upon initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **United States:** Category not explicitly assigned in new labeling format, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following exogenous administration. Clinicians must weigh the benefits to the mother against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for inborn errors of metabolism are inclusive of paediatric patients; however, specific age-range safety profiles for ESRD were not detailed [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis; however, monitoring for metabolite accumulation is required if using oral forms in patients with severe renal impairment [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** In ESRD patients, a dose of 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) levocarnitine levels from 21.3 $\pm$ 7.7 µmol/L to 190 $\pm$ 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** In a study of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients, trough concentrations at 24 weeks were 148, 240, and 456 µmol/L, respectively [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags. 
- **Preservative:** None; discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
