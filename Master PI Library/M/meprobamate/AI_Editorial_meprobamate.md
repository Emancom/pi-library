# Editorial Product Information: Meprobamate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Meprobamate
- **Chemical Class:** Carbamate derivative
- **Brand Name(s):** Meprobamate (Generic) [United States]
- **ATC Code:** N05BA05 (Note: Not explicitly listed in the source PI but standard clinical classification).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Oral tablets: 200 mg and 400 mg [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Cipla USA Inc. (InvaGen Pharmaceuticals, Inc.) [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anxiety Management:** Indicated for the management of anxiety disorders or the short-term relief of symptoms of anxiety [United States].
- **Note on Use:** Anxiety or tension associated with the stress of everyday life typically does not require anxiolytic treatment. The effectiveness of long-term use (defined as >4 months) has not been systematically assessed; clinicians should periodically re-evaluate the drug's utility for individual patients [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Standard Dosage:** 1200 mg to 1600 mg daily, administered in three or four divided doses [United States].
- **Maximum Dose:** Dosages exceeding 2400 mg daily are not recommended [United States].

### Paediatric Dosing (Ages 6–12)
- **Standard Dosage:** 200 mg to 600 mg daily, administered in two or three divided doses [United States].

### Special Instructions
- **Debilitated/Elderly Patients:** Use the lowest effective dose to prevent over-sedation [United States].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Porphyria:** Acute intermittent porphyria [United States].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergic or idiosyncratic reactions to meprobamate or related compounds, including carisoprodol, mebutamate, tybamate, or carbromal [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None listed in provided sources.

### General Warnings
- **Drug Dependence:** Meprobamate carries a risk of physical and psychological dependence and abuse. Chronic intoxication (often involving doses higher than recommended) manifests as ataxia, slurred speech, and vertigo [United States].
- **Withdrawal Syndrome:** Abrupt cessation after prolonged/excessive use can precipitate anxiety, anorexia, insomnia, vomiting, tremors, muscle twitching, confusional states, and convulsive seizures. Seizures are more likely in patients with CNS damage or pre-existing seizure disorders [United States].
- **Potentially Hazardous Tasks:** Patients must be warned that the drug may impair mental and physical abilities required for driving or operating machinery [United States].
- **Additive Effects:** Effects are additive with alcohol and other CNS depressants or psychotropic medications [United States].

### Specific Precautions
- **Suicide Risk:** Consider the possibility of suicide attempts; prescribe the minimum feasible quantity [United States].
- **Epilepsy:** Meprobamate may occasionally precipitate seizures in patients with epilepsy [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Central Nervous System
- Drowsiness, ataxia, dizziness, slurred speech, headache, vertigo, weakness, and paresthesias.
- Paradoxical excitement, euphoria, overstimulation, and fast EEG activity [United States].

### Cardiovascular
- Palpitation, tachycardia, arrhythmias, transient ECG changes, and syncope.
- Hypotensive crisis (one fatal case reported) [United States].

### Gastrointestinal
- Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea [United States].

### Hematologic
- Agranulocytosis and aplastic anaemia (rarely fatal).
- Thrombocytopenic purpura and leukopenia [United States].

### Allergic or Idiosyncratic Reactions
- **Mild:** Itchy, urticarial, or erythematous maculopapular rash (may be generalised or confined to the groin).
- **Severe (Rare):** Hyperpyrexia, chills, angioneurotic oedema, bronchospasm, oliguria, anuria, anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, and exfoliative dermatitis [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Alcohol and CNS Depressants:** Additive pharmacodynamic effects; caution is required when used concomitantly [United States].
- **Cross-Sensitivity:** Potential for cross-reaction/sensitivity with carisoprodol, mebutamate, and carbromal [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Risk Category:** Not specified by letter category, but several studies suggest an increased risk of congenital malformations when used during the **first trimester**. Use during this period should almost always be avoided [United States].
- **Placental Transfer:** Meprobamate passes the placental barrier; umbilical cord levels are near maternal plasma levels [United States].

### Breastfeeding
- **Safety Information:** Meprobamate is excreted in human breast milk at concentrations **two to four times** that of maternal plasma. Caution should be exercised when considering use in lactating patients [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children under **six years of age** have not been established [United States].
- **Geriatric Use:** Clinical studies were insufficient to determine if those >65 respond differently. Start dosing at the low end of the range due to higher frequencies of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [United States].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Exercise caution; the drug is metabolised in the liver and excreted by the kidney. Impairment may lead to drug accumulation [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A carbamate derivative that affects multiple sites in the CNS, specifically the thalamus and limbic system [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Acts as an anxiolytic with sedative properties.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Metabolism:** Hepatic.
    - **Elimination:** Renal excretion [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Systematic clinical studies for meprobamate use exceeding four months have not been conducted [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F).
- **Container:** Dispense in a well-closed, child-resistant container [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a Schedule IV (CIV) controlled substance. Rx-only [United States].
