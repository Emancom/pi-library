# Editorial Product Information: Medroxyprogesterone Acetate

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) clinical data for healthcare professionals. 
- **Prescribing Note:** This is an editorial overview and must not be used as a substitute for the official, current Product Information mandated in a specific jurisdiction for prescribing purposes.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA).
- **Chemical Class:** Progestogen; derivative of progesterone (6α-methyl-3,20-dioxopregn-4-en-17α-yl acetate).
- **Brand Names:** **DEPO-PROVERA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** G03AC06 (progestogens, contraceptives), L02EB01 (progestogens, endocrine therapy).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Injection, suspension (150 mg/mL). Available in 1 mL vials and 1 mL pre-filled syringes [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Deep intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Contraception:** Long-term prevention of pregnancy when administered at 3-month intervals [Australian].
- **Endometriosis:** Treatment of visually proven (via laparoscopy) endometriosis, specifically when pregnancy is the desired endpoint or when surgical intervention is contraindicated or unsuccessful [Australian].
- **Oncology (Palliative Treatment):**
    - Recurrent and/or metastatic endometrial carcinoma (inoperable).
    - Recurrent and/or metastatic renal cell cancer.
    - Recurrent and/or metastatic breast cancer [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Administration:** Must be administered by deep IM injection into the gluteal muscle. The suspension must be shaken well before use to ensure a uniform dose. If the volume exceeds 2.5 mL, divided administration into multiple sites is recommended [Australian].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Contraception:** 150 mg every 3 months. The initial dose should be given within the first 5 days of a normal menstrual cycle, within 5 days post-partum (if not breastfeeding), or at 6 weeks post-partum (if breastfeeding) [Australian].
- **Endometriosis:** 50 mg weekly or 100 mg every 2 weeks for at least 6 months [Australian].
- **Endometrial and Renal Carcinoma:** Initially 600 mg to 1200 mg weekly. Maintenance dosing ranges from 450 mg to 600 mg every 1 to 4 weeks [Australian].
- **Breast Carcinoma:** 500 mg daily for 4 weeks, followed by maintenance doses of 500 mg to 1000 mg at weekly intervals [Australian].

### Missed Dose and Timing
- **Contraception:** If the interval between injections exceeds 14 weeks, pregnancy must be excluded before the next dose is administered [Australian].
- **Switching Methods:** When switching from oral contraceptives, the first MPA injection should ideally occur within 7 days of taking the last active pill [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
MPA is contraindicated in patients with:
- Known or suspected pregnancy.
- Undiagnosed vaginal or urinary tract bleeding.
- Marked impairment of liver function.
- Known or suspected malignancy of the breast (when used for contraception or endometriosis).
- Active or history of thrombophlebitis, thromboembolic disorders, or cerebral apoplexy.
- Severe uncontrolled hypertension.
- Missed abortion.
- Undiagnosed breast pathology.
- Hypersensitivity to MPA or any excipients (e.g., methyl/propyl hydroxybenzoate) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bone Mineral Density (BMD) Loss:** Long-term use (>2 years) is associated with significant BMD reduction due to lowered serum estrogen. This is particularly critical in adolescents (ages 12–18) who have not reached peak bone mass. BMD should be evaluated if use extends beyond 2 years. Patients with osteoporotic risk factors (chronic alcohol/tobacco use, low BMI, metabolic bone disease) should consider alternative treatments [Australian].
- **Thromboembolic Disorders:** Discontinue immediately if thrombophlebitis, pulmonary embolism, cerebrovascular disorders, or retinal thrombosis occur or are suspected [Australian].
- **Meningioma:** Cases have been reported following long-term progestin use. MPA should be discontinued if a meningioma is diagnosed [Australian].
- **Cancer Risk:** While overall breast cancer risk is not significantly increased, a higher relative risk (RR 2.19) has been observed in women whose first exposure was under age 35 and within the previous 4 years [Australian].
- **Fluid Retention:** Use with caution in patients with conditions sensitive to fluid shifts, such as epilepsy, migraine, asthma, or cardiac/renal dysfunction [Australian].
- **Ocular Disorders:** Discontinue if sudden loss of vision, proptosis, diplopia, or migraine occurs pending examination for papilledema or retinal vascular lesions [Australian].
- **Mental Depression:** Monitor patients with a history of depression; discontinue if the condition recurs to a serious degree [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Analysis [Australian]
- **Very Common (>10%):** Amenorrhoea (57% at 12 months, 68% at 24 months), weight fluctuation.
- **Common (1–10%):** Menstrual irregularities (bleeding/spotting), abdominal pain, dizziness, nervousness, headache, asthenia, nausea, insomnia, rash, acne, backache, pelvic pain, breast pain, and decreased libido.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Blood dyscrasia, anaemia, tachycardia, chest pain, jaundice, hypersensitivity reactions, changes in appetite, and genitourinary infections.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Osteoporosis and osteoporotic fractures (post-marketing reports).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and anaphylactoid reactions.
- Thromboembolic events (Cerebral infarction, pulmonary embolism).
- Injection site reactions: Persistent atrophy, dimpling, or abscess formation (particularly with high volumes) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Aminoglutethimide:** May significantly decrease MPA serum concentrations, potentially compromising contraceptive or therapeutic efficacy [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Metabolism:** MPA is primary metabolised by CYP3A4. While specific studies are limited, CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin) may decrease MPA levels, while inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) may increase them [Australian].
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** MPA may decrease levels of gonadotrophins, progesterone, estrogen (in females), testosterone (in males), and cortisol. It may also affect glucose tolerance tests and increase coagulation values for Factors II, VII, VIII, IX, and X [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. MPA is not to be used during pregnancy or as a pregnancy test. Exposure in the first trimester is associated with an approximately doubled risk of hypospadias in male fetuses and mild virilisation in female fetuses [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** MPA and its metabolites are detectable in breast milk. Studies show no adverse effects on milk composition or the developmental/behavioural health of the infant through puberty [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Return of fertility may be delayed. The median time to conception after discontinuation is 10 months, with a range of 4 to 31 months, and is unrelated to the duration of use [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated before menarche. Safety/efficacy in post-menarcheal adolescents (12–18 years) is expected to be similar to adults, except for the heightened risk of BMD loss during this critical growth period [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in the provided sources [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** MPA is almost exclusively eliminated via hepatic metabolism. It is contraindicated in cases of marked liver dysfunction [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specifically studied; however, fluid retention precautions apply [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** MPA is a potent progestational agent. In contraception, it inhibits the production of gonadotrophins, preventing follicular maturation and ovulation. In oncology, it transforms proliferative endometrium into secretory endometrium and possesses adrenocorticoid-like activity at high doses [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Slow absorption from the IM site. Following 150 mg IM, peak plasma concentrations (1 to 7 ng/mL) are reached in approximately 3 weeks.
    - **Metabolism:** Hepatic metabolism via hydroxylation and conjugation.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal excretion as conjugated and free forms. Serum levels become undetectable (<100 pg/mL) between 120 and 200 days post-injection [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Contraceptive Efficacy:** In five clinical trials, the 3-month failure rate was zero (Life-Table method range up to 0.7) [Australian].
- **BMD Studies (Adults):** A 5-year study showed a 5-6% decrease in BMD in the spine and hip, compared to no change in the control group. Partial recovery was noted 2 years post-discontinuation [Australian].
- **BMD Studies (Adolescents):** An open-label study (12–18 years) showed mean BMD decreases at the total hip (-6.4%) and femoral neck (-5.4%) after 240 weeks. Recovery to baseline levels took approximately 1.2 years for the spine and 4.6 years for the hip/femoral neck [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Do not refrigerate or freeze [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (S4)**; Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
