# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Jurisdictions' PI documents contributing to this editorial: United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the provided official Product Information for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial serves as a clinical reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing decisions in any particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered first, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (and never less frequently than every 6 hours).
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [United States].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Titration:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [United States].

### Monitoring
- It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to starting therapy. 
- Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations.
- **Target Range:** The target plasma free carnitine concentration is generally between 35 and 60 µmol/L [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- There are no known contraindications listed for levocarnitine injection [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [United States].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without a history of seizure activity. In patients with pre-existing epilepsy, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures may occur [United States].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulations):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in the urine [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Reactions:** Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed. Less frequent reactions include body odor, abdominal pain, and gastritis.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Warnings and Precautions).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not explicitly lettered, but follows historical Category C logic]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Animal data (dairy cows) indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients; however, specific age-range data for ESRD was not further detailed beyond general dosing [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in the available source [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** See "Dosage and Administration" for ESRD patients. The drug is removed by hemodialysis [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine helps clear accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients showed that 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) plasma carnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [United States].
- **Dose-Ranging Trials:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses three times weekly demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [United States].
- **Poison/Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
