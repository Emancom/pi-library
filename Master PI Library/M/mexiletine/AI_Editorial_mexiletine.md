# Editorial Product Information: Mexiletine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mexiletine hydrochloride (a Class 1B antiarrhythmic).
- **Brand Names:** Mexiletine Hydrochloride Capsules, USP (Macleods Pharmaceuticals) [US].
- **ATC Code:** C01BB02 (Antiarrhythmics, class IB).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Capsules:** 150 mg, 200 mg, and 250 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Macleods Pharmaceuticals Limited [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Ventricular Arrhythmias:** Indicated for the treatment of documented life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias, such as sustained ventricular tachycardia [US].
- **Usage Restrictions:** 
    - Not recommended for patients with lesser arrhythmias due to proarrhythmic potential [US].
    - Treatment of asymptomatic ventricular premature contractions (VPCs) should be avoided [US].
    - Initiation of therapy should generally occur in a hospital setting [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** 200 mg every 8 hours. Dosage must be individualised based on therapeutic response and tolerance [US].
- **Titration:** Adjustments of 50 mg or 100 mg (up or down) should occur no more frequently than every 2 to 3 days [US].
- **Maintenance Dose:** Most patients are well-controlled at 200 mg to 300 mg every 8 hours, administered with food or antacids [US].
- **Maximum Daily Dose:** 1200 mg/day [US].

### Specific Dosing Schedules
- **Rapid Control (Loading Dose):** Where rapid suppression is essential, an initial loading dose of 400 mg may be followed by a 200 mg dose 8 hours later. Onset is typically seen within 30 minutes to 2 hours [US].
- **Twice-Daily (q12h) Regimen:** If adequate suppression is achieved on ≤300 mg every 8 hours, the patient may be transferred to a 12-hour schedule (total daily dose divided). The maximum q12h dose is 450 mg [US].

### Transferring from Other Antiarrhythmics
- **From Class I Oral Agents:** Initiate 200 mg of mexiletine 6–12 hours after the last dose of quinidine or disopyramide, 3–6 hours after procainamide, or 8–12 hours after tocainide [US].
- **From Lidocaine:** Stop lidocaine infusion upon the first oral dose of mexiletine; maintain the IV line until the arrhythmia is satisfactorily suppressed [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Cardiogenic shock** [US].
- **Pre-existing second- or third-degree AV block** in the absence of a functioning artificial pacemaker [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning
- **Mortality Risk:** In the Cardiac Arrhythmia Suppression Trial (CAST), an excessive mortality or non-fatal cardiac arrest rate (7.7%) was seen with Class 1C antiarrhythmics (encainide, flecainide) compared to placebo (3.0%). While mexiletine's specific risk in this population is uncertain, its use should be reserved for life-threatening arrhythmias [US].

### General Warnings
- **Proarrhythmic Effects:** Mexiletine can cause new or worsened arrhythmias. In patients with sustained VT, exacerbation rates are 10–15% [US].
- **Acute Liver Injury:** Abnormal liver function tests and rare instances of hepatic necrosis have been reported, typically within the first few weeks of therapy [US].
- **DRESS (Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms):** Severe, potentially fatal reactions including fever, rash, and organ involvement (hepatitis, nephritis, myocarditis) have been reported. Discontinue immediately if DRESS is suspected [US].
- **Blood Dyscrasias:** Leukopenia, agranulocytosis, and thrombocytopenia have occurred rarely, often in patients with serious underlying illness or on concomitant myelosuppressive therapy [US].

### Precautions and Monitoring
- **Cardiac Conduction:** Use with caution in patients with first-degree AV block, sinus node dysfunction, or intraventricular conduction defects [US].
- **Seizures:** Use with caution in patients with known seizure disorders; convulsions have been reported in post-marketing use [US].
- **Urinary pH:** Marked changes in urinary pH (via diet or drugs) can significantly alter excretion (acidification accelerates; alkalinization retards) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequent but generally reversible and dose-related. Approximately 40% of patients in clinical trials required discontinuation or dose reduction [US].

| System Organ Class | Common (>10%) | Common (1-10%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Digestive** | Nausea, vomiting, heartburn (39%) | Diarrhea (5.2%), Constipation (4%) |
| **CNS** | Dizziness/Lightheadedness (18.9-26%), Tremor (13.2%) | Coordination difficulties (9.7%), Nervousness (5-11%), Changes in sleep habits (7%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | - | Palpitations (4.3-7.5%), Chest pain (2.6-7.5%) |
| **Other** | - | Blurred vision (5.7-7.5%), Headache (5.7-7.5%), Rash (3.8-4.2%) |

**Serious/Rare Adverse Effects (<1%):** 
- **Cardiovascular:** Syncope, hypotension, bradycardia, AV block, cardiogenic shock [US].
- **Dermatologic:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, exfoliative dermatitis [US].
- **Hepatic:** Severe hepatitis, hepatic necrosis [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** GI bleeding, peptic ulcer, esophageal ulceration [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP1A2 Inhibitors (e.g., Fluvoxamine):** Significantly decrease mexiletine clearance (38% reduction in study) [US].
- **CYP2D6 Inhibitors/Metabolizers:** Clearance is reduced in poor CYP2D6 metabolizers or when co-administered with inhibitors like propafenone [US].
- **Hepatic Enzyme Inducers (e.g., Phenytoin, Rifampin, Phenobarbital):** May lower mexiletine plasma levels; monitoring is recommended [US].
- **Caffeine:** Mexiletine decreased caffeine clearance by 50% [US].
- **Theophylline:** Concurrent use can lead to a ~72% increase in plasma theophylline levels. Monitor theophylline levels closely [US].
- **Cimetidine:** May increase, decrease, or have no effect on mexiletine levels; monitor carefully [US].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Lidocaine:** Potential for additive adverse effects; monitor closely [US].
- **Antacids/Magnesium-Aluminum Hydroxide:** May slow absorption but also reported to lower serum digoxin levels when used for GI symptoms caused by mexiletine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered in PI, but animal studies (rats, mice, rabbits) at 4x human doses showed no teratogenicity, though increased fetal resorption occurred. Use in pregnancy only if potential benefit outweighs risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mexiletine is excreted in human milk at concentrations similar to plasma. Consider alternative feeding methods if mexiletine is essential [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility observed in rat studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Patients with renal failure typically require usual doses of mexiletine [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is significantly restricted. Moderate to severe liver disease can double the elimination half-life (~25 hours vs 10-12 hours). Use lower doses and monitor closely [US].
- **Congestive Heart Failure:** Severe right-sided heart failure can reduce hepatic metabolism; dose reduction may be necessary [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Local anaesthetic and Class 1B antiarrhythmic. It inhibits the inward sodium current (Phase 0), reducing the rate of rise of the action potential. It decreases the effective refractory period (ERP) in Purkinje fibres [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Minimal effect on cardiac impulse generation/propagation in normal conduction systems. Does not typically prolong QRS or QT intervals [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** ~90% absorbed from the GI tract. Peak blood levels in 2–3 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 50–60% protein bound; volume of distribution is 5–7 L/kg.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP2D6 and CYP1A2.
    - **Elimination:** ~10% excreted unchanged by the kidneys. Half-life is 10–12 hours in healthy subjects [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Comparative Efficacy:** Controlled trials against placebo, quinidine, procainamide, and disopyramide established effectiveness. Doses of 200–400 mg q8h significantly reduced PVCs, paired beats, and non-sustained VT episodes [US].
- **Note:** Approximately 40% of trial participants failed to complete 3-month studies due to adverse effects (predominantly GI and CNS) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
