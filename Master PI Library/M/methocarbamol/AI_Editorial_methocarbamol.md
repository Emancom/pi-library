# Editorial Product Information: Methocarbamol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of methocarbamol for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methocarbamol (a carbamate derivative of guaifenesin).
- **Chemical Class:** Central Nervous System (CNS) depressant; skeletal muscle relaxant.
- **Brand Names:** Noted in provided source as Methocarbamol Tablets, USP; distributed by RemedyRepack Inc. [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - **Oral Tablets:** 500 mg and 750 mg [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Granules India Ltd (Manufacturer); RemedyRepack Inc. (Repackager) [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Musculoskeletal Conditions:** Indicated as an adjunct to rest, physical therapy, and other therapeutic measures for the relief of discomfort associated with acute, painful musculoskeletal conditions [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Initial Therapy:** 6 grams per day is recommended for the first 48 to 72 hours. For severe conditions, up to 8 grams per day may be administered [United States].
- **Maintenance Therapy:** 4 grams per day is typically sufficient following the initial period [United States].

### Specific Regimens by Tablet Strength [United States]
- **500 mg Tablets:**
  - **Initial:** 3 tablets (1500 mg) four times daily.
  - **Maintenance:** 2 tablets (1000 mg) four times daily.
- **750 mg Tablets:**
  - **Initial:** 2 tablets (1500 mg) four times daily.
  - **Maintenance:** 1 tablet every 4 hours OR 2 tablets three times daily.

### Administration Instructions
- Administer orally with or without food.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with known hypersensitivity to methocarbamol or any excipients in the formulation [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **CNS Depression:** Methocarbamol may cause general CNS depression. Concurrent use with alcohol or other CNS depressants may result in additive effects [United States].
- **Impairment of Mental Alertness:** May impair the mental and physical abilities required for hazardous tasks (e.g., operating machinery or driving). Patients should be advised not to engage in such activities until they are certain the therapy does not adversely affect them [United States].
- **Myasthenia Gravis:** Use with caution in patients receiving anticholinesterase agents, as methocarbamol may inhibit the effects of pyridostigmine bromide [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies of adverse effects are not provided in the source document; the following have been reported coincident with administration.*

- **Body as a Whole:** Anaphylactic reaction, angioneurotic edema, fever, headache.
- **Cardiovascular:** Bradycardia, flushing, hypotension, syncope, thrombophlebitis.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Dyspepsia, nausea, vomiting, jaundice (including cholestatic jaundice).
- **Nervous System:** Drowsiness, dizziness, lightheadedness, amnesia, confusion, diplopia, insomnia, mild muscular incoordination, nystagmus, sedation, seizures (including grand mal), vertigo.
- **Hematologic:** Leukopenia.
- **Dermatologic/Senses:** Blurred vision, conjunctivitis, nasal congestion, metallic taste, pruritus, rash, urticaria.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants and Alcohol:** Potential for additive sedative effects [United States].
- **Pyridostigmine Bromide:** Methocarbamol may inhibit the therapeutic effect of pyridostigmine. Use with extreme caution in patients with myasthenia gravis [United States].
- **Laboratory Test Interferences:**
  - **5-HIAA:** May cause color interference in screening tests for 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid using nitrosonaphthol reagent.
  - **VMA:** May cause color interference in screening tests for urinary vanillylmandelic acid using the Gitlow method [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category C [United States]:** Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. Fetal and congenital abnormalities have been reported following in utero exposure.
  - **Recommendation:** Should not be used in women who are or may become pregnant, particularly during early pregnancy, unless the potential benefits outweigh the possible hazards to the fetus [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Methocarbamol/metabolites are excreted in dog milk; it is unknown if they are excreted in human milk. Exercise caution when administering to nursing women [United States].
- **Fertility:** Studies assessing the effect on fertility have not been conducted [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 16 years of age have not been established [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** The elimination half-life is slightly prolonged in the elderly (approx. 1.5 hours vs 1.1 hours in younger populations). Protein binding is slightly decreased (41–43% vs 46–50%) [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** In patients on maintenance hemodialysis, clearance was reduced by approximately 40%, though the elimination half-life remained similar to healthy subjects [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In patients with cirrhosis secondary to alcohol abuse, mean total clearance was reduced by approximately 70%, and the elimination half-life was significantly prolonged to approximately 3.38 hours [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The exact mechanism is not established in humans. It is believed to act via general CNS depression. It does not have a direct effect on the contractile mechanism of striated muscle, the motor end plate, or the nerve fiber [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Metabolism:** Via dealkylation and hydroxylation; conjugation is likely.
  - **Elimination:** Almost all metabolites are excreted in the urine, with small amounts of unchanged drug also appearing in urine.
  - **Half-life:** Approximately 1–2 hours in healthy volunteers [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal clinical trial data was not detailed in the provided source document. Information is limited to pharmacokinetic studies in healthy volunteers and specific populations (elderly, renally impaired, and hepatically impaired).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 20°C and 25°C (68°F and 77°F) [United States].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight container as defined by USP [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Only) [United States].
