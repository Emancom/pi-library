# Editorial Product Information: Moxifloxacin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority:
    - Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **moxifloxacin** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved labeling for definitive prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Moxifloxacin (as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** 8-methoxyfluoroquinolone synthetic antibiotic.
- **Brand Name(s):** Avelox [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Oral Tablet:** 400 mg film-coated tablets (containing 436.8 mg moxifloxacin hydrochloride).
    - **Intravenous (IV) Solution:** 400 mg/250 mL (1.6 mg/mL) sterile solution for infusion in 0.8% sodium chloride.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral, Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bayer Australia Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Moxifloxacin** is indicated for the treatment of adults with infections caused by susceptible organisms in the following conditions:

- **Acute bacterial sinusitis** [Australian-approved].
- **Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP)** [Australian-approved].
- **Acute exacerbations of chronic bronchitis (AECB)** [Australian-approved].
    - *Note for IV use:* Indicated when caused by organisms bacteriologically proven to be resistant to other antibiotic classes or when there is intolerance to other agents [Australian].
- **Complicated skin and skin structure infections (cSSSI)**, including major abscesses, wound infections (post-surgery/trauma), and diabetic foot infections [Australian-approved for IV/sequential therapy].
    - *Note for IV use:* Indicated for patients requiring initial parenteral therapy who have intolerance to alternative agents, specifically penicillin allergy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The standard dose is **400 mg once every 24 hours** via oral or intravenous route.

### Dosing by Indication
| Infection | Daily Dose | Duration |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Acute bacterial sinusitis | 400 mg | 10 days (Oral) |
| Acute exacerbations of chronic bronchitis | 400 mg | 5 days (Oral or Sequential IV/Oral) |
| Community-acquired pneumonia | 400 mg | 7 – 14 days (Sequential IV/Oral) or 10 days (Oral) |
| Major abscess, wound infection, or diabetic foot infection | 400 mg | 7 – 21 days (Sequential IV/Oral) |

### Administration Instructions
- **Oral:** Tablets should be swallowed whole with liquid and may be taken independently of food. 
- **Cation Interaction Spacing:** Administer oral doses at least 2 hours before or 4 hours after antacids (containing magnesium or aluminium), iron-containing products, multivitamins with zinc, sucralfate, or didanosine chewable/buffered tablets.
- **Intravenous:** Administer by **intravenous infusion only** over a 60-minute period. Do NOT administer via bolus, rapid infusion, intra-arterial, intramuscular, intrathecal, or subcutaneous routes.
- **Sequential Therapy:** Patients may be switched from IV to oral 400 mg tablets when clinically indicated; no dosage adjustment is necessary when switching routes.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- If a dose is missed, take it as soon as remembered, provided it is at least 8 hours before the next scheduled dose.
- If less than 8 hours remain before the next dose, skip the missed dose.
- Do not double the dose to compensate for a missed dose.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **moxifloxacin**, other quinolones, or any excipients.
- Patients with known prolongation of the QTc interval.
- Patients with uncorrected hypokalaemia.
- Concomitant use of Class IA (e.g., quinidine, procainamide) or Class III (e.g., amiodarone, sotalol) antiarrhythmic agents due to additive QTc effects.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- **Serious Disabling and Potentially Irreversible Adverse Reactions:** Fluoroquinolones are associated with disabling and potentially irreversible serious reactions involving the nervous system (seizures, peripheral neuropathy), musculoskeletal system (tendonitis, tendon rupture), and psychiatric effects. These may occur together in the same patient regardless of age or pre-existing risk factors [Australian].

### General Warnings
- **Cardiac Effects:** Moxifloxacin prolongs the QTc interval. Avoid exceeding the recommended dose or infusion rate. Use with caution in patients with proarrhythmic conditions (e.g., bradycardia, acute myocardial ischaemia). Women and elderly patients may be more susceptible to QTc effects.
- **Tendonitis and Tendon Rupture:** May occur within 48 hours of treatment or several months after cessation, predominantly affecting the Achilles tendon. Risk is increased in elderly patients, those on corticosteroids, those with renal impairment, or solid organ transplant recipients. Discontinue at the first sign of tendon pain or inflammation.
- **Psychiatric Reactions:** May occur after the first dose, including toxic psychosis, suicidal ideation/attempts, hallucinations, depression, and anxiety.
- **Central Nervous System Disorders:** Use with caution in patients with known or suspected CNS disorders (e.g., stroke, epilepsy) that may lower the seizure threshold.
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Sensory or sensorimotor polyneuropathy (e.g., paraesthesia, numbness, weakness) has been reported. Discontinue immediately if symptoms occur.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Including SJS, TEN, AGEP, and DRESS. Discontinue at the first appearance of skin rash or mucosal lesions.
- **Aortic Aneurysm and Dissection:** Epidemiologic studies show an increased risk, particularly in the elderly. Use only after careful benefit-risk assessment in patients with positive family history or pre-existing aneurysm.
- **Dysglycaemia:** Disturbances in blood glucose (hypoglycaemia and hyperglycaemia) may occur, primarily in elderly diabetic patients on insulin or oral hypoglycaemics.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylactic reactions can occur even after the first dose. Discontinue at the first sign of an allergic reaction.

### Monitoring Requirements
- Blood glucose monitoring is recommended for diabetic patients.
- INR monitoring is required for patients on concomitant oral anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin).
- Liver function tests should be performed if signs of hepatic dysfunction occur.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency of Adverse Reactions
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, gastrointestinal/abdominal pain, headache, dizziness, QTc prolongation (in hypokalaemic patients), injection/infusion site reactions. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Mycotic superinfections, anaemia, leucopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, allergic reactions, pruritus, rash, hyperlipidaemia, anxiety, psychomotor hyperactivity, palpitations, tachycardia, dyspnoea, hepatic impairment (increased bilirubin/alkaline phosphatase). |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, angioedema, emotional lability, depression, hallucinations, hypoaesthesia, seizures, tinnitus, hearing impairment (reversible), ventricular tachyarrhythmias, syncope, hypotension, hypertension, antibiotic-associated colitis, jaundice, tendonitis. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Anaphylactic shock, depersonalisation, psychotic reactions, transient loss of vision, cardiac arrest, torsade de pointes, fulminant hepatitis, tendon rupture, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN). |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Kounis Syndrome:** Acute myocardial ischaemia as part of a hypersensitivity reaction [Australian].
- **Fulminant Hepatitis:** Potentially leading to life-threatening liver failure.
- **Clostridium difficile-associated disease:** Ranging from mild diarrhoea to fatal colitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Multivalent Cations:** Antacids (Mg, Al), iron, and zinc significantly reduce the bioavailability of oral moxifloxacin. Spaced dosing is mandatory.
- **Activated Charcoal:** Reduces systemic availability of oral moxifloxacin by >80% if administered in the early absorption phase.
- **Ranitidine and Digoxin:** No clinically significant interactions; however, moxifloxacin may increase Cmax of digoxin by ~30% at steady state without affecting AUC.
- **Probenecid:** Does not significantly affect the clearance of moxifloxacin.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **QTc Prolonging Drugs:** Avoid concomitant use with Class IA and III antiarrhythmics. Use with caution with cisapride, erythromycin, antipsychotics, and tricyclic antidepressants.
- **Oral Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** Potential for increased anticoagulant activity. Monitor INR and adjust doses as necessary.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. Moxifloxacin has been shown to cause embryonic deaths and abortions in monkeys and skeletal malformations in rabbits at sub-therapeutic doses. Use in pregnancy is not recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Preclinical data indicate moxifloxacin is secreted in milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering its importance to the mother.
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in male or female rats at oral doses up to 500 mg/kg/day, though slight effects on sperm morphology and oestrus cycling were noted [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in children and adolescents (<18 years) is not recommended due to the risk of cartilage lesions in weight-bearing joints (observed in immature animals).
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment is necessary. However, clinicians should exercise caution regarding the higher risk of tendon disorders and QTc prolongation.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required, including for patients on haemodialysis or continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild to moderate impairment (Child-Pugh A or B). Use is **not recommended** in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) due to limited clinical data.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Moxifloxacin is a bactericidal agent that inhibits **topoisomerase II (DNA gyrase)** and **topoisomerase IV**. These enzymes are essential for bacterial DNA replication, transcription, repair, and recombination.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapidly and almost completely absorbed (90% absolute bioavailability). Steady state is achieved after 3 days.
- **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is ~2 L/kg. Protein binding is 40–42% (primarily to albumin). High concentrations are achieved in alveolar macrophages, bronchial mucosa, and sinus mucosa.
- **Metabolism:** Metabolised via glucuronide (M2) and sulphate (M1) conjugation. The **Cytochrome P450 system is not involved**. 
- **Elimination:** Approximately 45% is excreted as unchanged drug (~20% in urine, ~25% in faeces). Total recovery of dose (unchanged + metabolites) is ~96%. Plasma half-life is approximately 12 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **AECB:** In two large randomised trials, moxifloxacin 400 mg (5 days) demonstrated clinical response rates (82–89%) equivalent to clarithromycin (80–89%).
- **CAP:** In three trials, oral moxifloxacin (10 days) showed response rates of 93–95%. Sequential IV/Oral therapy for 7–14 days achieved success rates of 86–93%.
- **cSSSI:** Two Phase III studies demonstrated that sequential moxifloxacin therapy (79–81% success) was non-inferior to comparator regimens (piperacillin/tazobactam or amoxicillin/clavulanate).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Tablets:** Store below 25°C.
- **IV Solution:** Store below 30°C. Do not store below 15°C; precipitation may occur if refrigerated, though it will redissolve at room temperature (15°C – 25°C). Do not freeze.
