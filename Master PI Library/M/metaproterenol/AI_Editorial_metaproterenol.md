# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It consolidates clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a specific region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for intravenous use; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours).
    - Maximum reported dose: 300 mg/kg.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments: May be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels.

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Plasma Levels:** Target free carnitine concentrations should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
- **Initiation in ESRD:** Therapy may be prompted by trough concentrations below 40–50 µmol/L.
- **Administration Instructions:** Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration. Use only clear, colorless solutions. Discard unused portions as the product contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks before considering re-administration.
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Frequency $\ge$ 5% (regardless of causality) in Dialysis Patients [US]:
| Body System | Adverse Events |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |

### Other Notable Adverse Effects:
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
- **Uncommon/Rare:** Body odor, gastritis.
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, and seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not lettered in provided source]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies on levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers have not been conducted. While carnitine is a normal component of human milk, the risks of excess intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3.
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]), which are normally excreted in urine.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic errors involving acyl-CoA accumulation, levocarnitine promotes the formation of acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted, thereby alleviating metabolic acidosis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is approximately 0.6 hours.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine effectively increases plasma carnitine levels, the specific impact on clinical outcomes (signs and symptoms of deficiency) in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
