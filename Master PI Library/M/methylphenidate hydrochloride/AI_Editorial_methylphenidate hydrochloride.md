# Editorial Product Information: methylphenidate hydrochloride

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **methylphenidate hydrochloride**
- Chemical class: Central nervous system (CNS) stimulant (piperidine-derivative psychostimulant)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI if stated: Last revision noted in the available source: 04/2026 [US]
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) in patients for whom methylphenidate is appropriate.
- Limitation of use: Not recommended in pediatric patients younger than 6 years because of higher plasma exposure and a higher incidence of adverse reactions at equivalent doses [US].

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult and pediatric ≥6 years)
- Recommended starting dose for patients 6 years and older: 20 mg orally once daily in the morning.
- Dose titration: increase or decrease weekly in increments of 10 mg, 15 mg, or 20 mg as clinically indicated.
  - Note: 10 mg and 15 mg dose increments may be achieved by breaking functionally scored lower-strength tablets in the available source product; avoid assuming exact milligram equivalence when switching products due to differing formulations and pharmacokinetics.
- Maximum recommended daily dose: Daily doses above 60 mg have not been studied and are not recommended.

### Dosing by indication
- ADHD: as above. No additional indication-specific dosing information provided in the available sources.

### Administration instructions
- Administer orally once daily in the morning.
- May be taken with or without food.
- When switching from another methylphenidate product: discontinue the prior product and titrate methylphenidate hydrochloride according to the dosing/titration schedule above. Do not substitute on a strict milligram-for-milligram basis because formulations differ in methylphenidate base composition and pharmacokinetic profile.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to methylphenidate or any component of the product (reports of angioedema and anaphylaxis have occurred).
- Concomitant use with monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs), or use of an MAOI within the preceding 14 days — risk of hypertensive crisis.
- Any additional jurisdiction‑specific contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warning
- **Abuse, misuse, and addiction**: [US]  
  - **methylphenidate hydrochloride** has a high potential for abuse and misuse which can lead to substance use disorder, including addiction. Misuse and abuse can result in overdose and death; risk is increased with higher doses and with unapproved routes of administration (e.g., snorting, injection). Prior to initiation, assess each patient’s risk for abuse/misuse/addiction; educate patients and families on secure storage and proper disposal. Reassess risk and monitor frequently throughout treatment.

### General warnings and precautions
- Cardiovascular risks:
  - Avoid use in patients with known structural cardiac abnormalities, cardiomyopathy, serious cardiac arrhythmias, coronary artery disease, or other serious cardiac disease. Sudden death has been reported in stimulant-treated patients with serious cardiac conditions.
  - CNS stimulants can increase blood pressure (mean increase approximately 2–4 mmHg) and heart rate (mean increase approximately 3–6 bpm); some patients may have larger increases.
- Psychiatric adverse reactions:
  - May exacerbate pre-existing psychosis and may induce manic or mixed episodes in patients with bipolar disorder. Screen patients for risk factors for mania prior to initiation. New psychotic or manic symptoms (hallucinations, delusional thinking, mania) have been reported in patients without prior history—consider discontinuation if these occur.
- Priapism:
  - Reports of prolonged, painful erections (occasionally requiring surgical intervention) have occurred; may occur after initiation, after dose increases, or during withdrawal. Seek immediate medical attention for sustained painful erections.
- Peripheral vasculopathy, including Raynaud’s phenomenon:
  - Cases reported across age groups; signs usually intermittent but can lead to digital ulceration or soft tissue breakdown. Observe for digital changes; consider dose reduction or discontinuation if signs develop. Further evaluation (e.g., rheumatology) may be appropriate.
- Growth suppression in pediatric patients:
  - CNS stimulants have been associated with weight loss and slowing of growth. In longitudinal data, continuous methylphenidate treatment was associated with small average reductions in expected height and weight gain (example: approximately 2 cm less in height and 2.7 kg less in weight over 3 years in one dataset). Closely monitor height and weight; consider treatment interruption if growth is inadequate.
- Phenylketonuria:
  - Certain chewable formulations contain phenylalanine (as part of aspartame). Consider total daily phenylalanine intake in patients with PKU before prescribing.
- Ophthalmologic:
  - Reports of angle-closure glaucoma and increased intraocular pressure (IOP). Patients at risk for acute angle-closure glaucoma should be evaluated by an ophthalmologist. Prescribe to patients with open-angle glaucoma or increased IOP only if benefits outweigh risks and monitor closely.
- Motor and verbal tics / Tourette’s syndrome:
  - May precipitate onset or worsening of tics; assess family history and evaluate for tics prior to initiation and monitor regularly. Discontinue if clinically appropriate.
- Abuse/diversion risk:
  - Potential for illicit distribution and non-medical use. Counsel on safe storage and not sharing medication.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic assessment of blood pressure and heart rate.
- Baseline cardiac history and family history (including sudden death or ventricular arrhythmia) and physical exam; consider further cardiac evaluation if indicated.
- Monitor for psychiatric symptoms (new or worsening psychosis, mania), tics, peripheral vascular symptoms, and visual complaints.
- In pediatric patients: monitor growth (height and weight) periodically.
- Monitor for signs of misuse, abuse, diversion, and dependence.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Clinically significant interactions
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs): Concomitant administration or use within 14 days of discontinuing an MAOI is contraindicated because of the risk of hypertensive crisis.
- Antihypertensive agents: CNS stimulant-induced increases in blood pressure and heart rate may reduce the efficacy of antihypertensive medications. Monitor blood pressure; adjust antihypertensive dosage as needed.
- Serotonergic drugs: Concomitant use with serotonergic agents has been associated with serotonin syndrome in postmarketing reports. Monitor for signs of serotonin syndrome and manage appropriately.
- Other stimulants/adrenergic agents: Potential for additive sympathomimetic effects (blood pressure and heart rate increases); use caution and monitor.
- Switching between methylphenidate products: Do not substitute on a milligram-for-milligram basis due to differing methylphenidate base compositions and pharmacokinetic profiles; titrate when initiating a different methylphenidate product.

### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Specific metabolic pathway interactions (e.g., CYP enzymes) and quantitative interaction data: Not specified in available sources.

### Management notes
- Assess concomitant medications for potential additive cardiovascular or psychiatric effects.
- Monitor blood pressure and heart rate when starting or changing dose and when co-administered with interacting drugs.
- Counsel patients about signs of serotonin syndrome, hypertensive crises, and other serious interactions.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **methylphenidate hydrochloride** is a central nervous system stimulant. The precise mechanism by which it exerts therapeutic effects in ADHD is not fully described in the available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- CNS stimulants cause modest average increases in blood pressure (mean approximately 2–4 mmHg) and heart rate (mean approximately 3–6 bpm). Some patients may experience larger changes.
- Effects on growth (weight and height) have been observed in pediatric populations with long-term use.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Specific absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination parameters are not fully provided in the available sources.
- Note: Pediatric patients under 6 years demonstrated higher plasma exposure compared with older pediatric patients at equivalent doses in the available data, leading to higher adverse reaction rates.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Key trial (pediatric laboratory classroom study):
  - Population: Children aged 6–12 years with ADHD (approximately 90 subjects).
  - Design: Six-week dose-optimization period followed by a randomized, double-blind, parallel-group treatment period comparing individually optimized methylphenidate hydrochloride dose to placebo.
  - Findings: Safety data from the double-blind randomized phase (optimized doses 20–60 mg/day) showed common adverse reactions (≥2% and greater than placebo) including decreased appetite, aggression, emotional poverty, nausea, headache, and weight decreased (each reported in 2.4% of methylphenidate-treated subjects in the randomized phase).
  - Efficacy outcomes and detailed efficacy metrics: Not specified in available sources.

- Additional clinical-trial safety data summarized from pooled trials of methylphenidate products indicate common adverse reactions (≥2% and greater than placebo) including appetite decreased, weight decreased, nausea, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, dry mouth, vomiting, insomnia, anxiety, nervousness, restlessness, affect lability, agitation, irritability, dizziness, vertigo, tremor, blurred vision, blood pressure increased, heart rate increased, tachycardia, palpitations, hyperhidrosis, and pyrexia.

- Longitudinal growth data:
  - In pediatric comparisons over 14–36 months, continuous methylphenidate treatment was associated on average with a temporary slowing in growth rate; example aggregate figures noted include about 2 cm less height and 2.7 kg less weight gain over 3 years in treated children compared with controls in one dataset.

- Further pivotal efficacy trial details, endpoints, and statistical results: Not specified in available sources.
