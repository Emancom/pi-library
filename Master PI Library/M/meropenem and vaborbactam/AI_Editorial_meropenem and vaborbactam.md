# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory information into a single clinical reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection or by infusion.
    - Severe metabolic crisis: A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less frequently than every 6 hours.
    - Maximum recorded dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustment: Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels, typically as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of intravenous administration, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels.
    - Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Precaution):** While this document focuses on the injection, clinicians should note that high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions in ESRD patients are often confounded by the underlying pathology.
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, body odor, nausea, gastritis, vomiting, dizziness, and parathyroid disorder [US].
- **Clinical Trial Data (ESRD patients at doses of 10–40 mg/kg):**
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%).
    - **Neurological:** Headache (3–37%), dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
    - **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified in source text]. Animal reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Consider weighing the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) for metabolic disorders [US]. See Section 3.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical documents do not specify unique dose adjustments for the elderly, though general caution regarding renal function is standard.
- **Renal Impairment:** IV levocarnitine is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. The drug is easily removed via dialysis [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also aids in the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis), trough levels rose from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks. While concentrations increase, the specific impact on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison Schedule/Classification not applicable [US].
