# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulations and the most current manufacturer labeling for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); chemical class: Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus or by infusion. In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours. Daily maintenance doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though up to 300 mg/kg has been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **ESRD:** Initiation may be prompted by pre-dialysis plasma levels <40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These can occur within minutes of IV administration. Discontinue immediately if reactions occur [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with reports of seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with established epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Restriction:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, regardless of causality, include:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, abdominal pain, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, fever |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, hyperkalemia, weight changes |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, rhinitis, cough, dyspnea |

### Less Frequent/Postmarketing Reactions
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; gastritis; body odor [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Warnings and Precautions) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine levels in milk following exogenous administration. Clinicians must weigh the benefits to the mother against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is established; see Section 3.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This process delivers substrates for oxidation and subsequent energy production in all tissues except the brain [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are quickly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical data demonstrate that 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis increases mean plasma levocarnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to trough levels of ~190–243 µmol/L after 8 to 24 weeks of therapy. While the increase in carnitine levels is proven, the definitive effect on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been fully determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
