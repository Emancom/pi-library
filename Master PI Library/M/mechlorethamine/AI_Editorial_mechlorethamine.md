# Editorial Product Information: Mechlorethamine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a concise reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing or dispensing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mechlorethamine hydrochloride (also known as nitrogen mustard).
- **Chemical Class:** Alkylating agent.
- **Brand Names:** **Valchlor** [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01AA05.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Topical gel, 0.016% w/w mechlorethamine (equivalent to 0.02% mechlorethamine HCl) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical (dermatological use only).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Helsinn Therapeutics (U.S.), Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Mycosis Fungoides-type Cutaneous T-cell Lymphoma (MF-CTCL):** Indicated for the topical treatment of Stage IA and IB MF-CTCL in patients who have previously received skin-directed therapy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Application:** Apply a thin film once daily to the affected skin areas [US].
- **Modification for Toxicity:** Treatment must be suspended for any grade of skin ulceration or blistering, or for moderately-severe to severe dermatitis. 
- **Reintroduction:** Once the skin reaction improves, treatment may be restarted at a reduced frequency (e.g., once every 3 days). If tolerated for one week, the frequency may be increased to every other day for one week, then returned to once-daily application as tolerated [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Skin Preparation:** Apply to completely dry skin. Application should occur at least 4 hours before or 30 minutes after showering/washing [US].
- **Drying Time:** Allow treated areas to dry for 5 to 10 minutes before dressing.
- **Emollients:** Moisturizers may be applied 2 hours before or 2 hours after **mechlorethamine** application.
- **Occlusion:** Do not use occlusive (air or water-tight) dressings on treated areas.
- **Safety:** **Mechlorethamine** is a cytotoxic drug. Caregivers must wear disposable nitrile gloves during application and wash hands thoroughly with soap and water afterward [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known severe hypersensitivity to **mechlorethamine**. Observed reactions include anaphylaxis with topical formulations [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Mucosal and Eye Injury:** Exposure to mucous membranes (oral, nasal) or eyes can cause severe injury, including pain, burns, and potential blindness. If exposure occurs, irrigate immediately with copious water or saline for at least 15 minutes and seek urgent medical consultation [US].
- **Secondary Exposure:** Direct skin contact with the gel by individuals other than the patient must be avoided due to risks of dermatitis, mucosal injury, and secondary cancers [US].
- **Dermatitis:** Dermatitis is the most frequent adverse reaction (56%). Patients should be monitored for redness, swelling, inflammation, or blisters, particularly on the face, genitalia, and intertriginous areas [US].
- **Non-Melanoma Skin Cancer (NMSC):** Cases of NMSC (e.g., in 4% of trial patients) have occurred on both treated and untreated skin areas. Periodic skin monitoring is required during and after treatment [US].
- **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity:** Based on its mechanism of action and animal studies, **mechlorethamine** can cause fetal harm [US].
- **Flammability:** The gel is alcohol-based and flammable. Patients must avoid fire, flames, and smoking until the gel has completely dried [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following cutaneous adverse reactions were reported in clinical trials [US]:

| Adverse Reaction | Any Grade (%) | Moderately-Severe to Severe (%) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Dermatitis | 56% | 23% |
| Pruritus | 20% | 4% |
| Bacterial skin infection | 11% | 2% |
| Skin ulceration/blistering | 6% | 3% |
| Skin hyperpigmentation | 5% | 0% |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Severe dermatitis and skin ulceration.
- Potential for systemic hematologic effects (e.g., reductions in hemoglobin, neutrophils, or platelets were observed in 13% of patients, though systemic absorption was not detected) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted. Systemic exposure is undetectable following topical application; therefore, systemic interactions are considered unlikely [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Pregnancy category not specified; however, the PI notes potential for fetal harm]. Case reports of systemic **mechlorethamine** in humans and animal subcutaneous studies indicate teratogenicity and embryo-lethality. Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data on the presence of the drug in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding is not advised during treatment [US].
- **Fertility:** Based on animal data and its mechanism as an alkylating agent, **mechlorethamine** may impair fertility in both males and females. Reversibility is unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** In clinical trials, 31% of patients were ≥65 years. Response rates were lower (44%) compared to younger patients (66%). Elderly patients experienced more cutaneous adverse reactions (70% vs 58%) and higher discontinuation rates (38% vs 14%) [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, lack of systemic absorption suggests no dosage adjustment is required.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Mechlorethamine** is a nitrogen mustard that acts as a bifunctional alkylating agent. It inhibits rapidly proliferating cells by cross-linking DNA.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Systemic exposure is undetectable following topical application at the clinical dose [US].
    - **Metabolism/Elimination:** Not characterized for topical application due to lack of systemic absorption.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Specifically targets cutaneous lesions in MF-CTCL.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trial:** A randomized, multicenter, observer-blind, non-inferiority trial compared **mechlorethamine** 0.016% gel to a 0.02% ointment comparator in 260 patients with Stage IA–IIA MF-CTCL [US].
- **Outcomes:** 60% of patients in the gel arm achieved a Composite Assessment of Index Lesion Severity (CAILS) response (≥50% reduction in score) compared to 48% in the ointment arm. The onset of response ranged from 1 to 11 months [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Dispensing Storage:** Prior to dispensing, store frozen at -25°C to -15°C [US].
- **Patient Storage:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. The product must be discarded 90 days after being removed from the freezer for use [US].
- **Handling:** Return the tube to the refrigerator immediately after each use. If left at room temperature for more than one hour per day, consult a pharmacist [US].
