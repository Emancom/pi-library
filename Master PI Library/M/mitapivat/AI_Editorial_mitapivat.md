# Editorial Product Information: Mitapivat

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (Food and Drug Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **mitapivat** for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data from the provided source.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Mitapivat** (as **mitapivat** sulfate), a pyruvate kinase activator.
- **Brand Names:** **Pyrukynd** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 5 mg, 20 mg, and 50 mg [US].
    - Available in 28-day packs and specific Taper Packs for dose reduction/discontinuation [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Agios Pharmaceuticals, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hemolytic Anemia:** Treatment of hemolytic anemia in adults with pyruvate kinase (PK) deficiency [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The starting dose is 5 mg twice daily.
- **Titration Schedule:** Dosage should be escalated every 4 weeks to achieve and maintain a target hemoglobin (Hb) level.
    - **Initial:** 5 mg twice daily for 4 weeks.
    - **Step 1:** Increase to 20 mg twice daily for 4 weeks if Hb is below normal or if a transfusion was required in the last 8 weeks.
    - **Step 2:** Increase to the maximum dose of 50 mg twice daily.
- **Maintenance and Discontinuation:** If no clinical benefit is observed by 24 weeks (based on Hb response, hemolysis markers, and transfusion requirements), **mitapivat** should be discontinued [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole and should not be crushed, split, or chewed. They may be taken with or without food.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed by ≤4 hours, it should be taken immediately. If >4 hours have passed, the dose should be skipped, and the patient should resume the next scheduled dose. Double doses should not be taken [US].
- **Discontinuation Taper:** To minimize the risk of acute hemolysis, avoid abrupt cessation. A gradual taper is required (e.g., tapering from 50 mg BID to 20 mg BID, then 5 mg QD before stopping) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None identified in current available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Hemolysis with Abrupt Discontinuation:** Sudden interruption of treatment has been associated with acute hemolysis and subsequent anemia. Healthcare providers must ensure patients do not abruptly stop therapy. If discontinuation is necessary, the recommended taper schedule must be followed unless the risk of a severe adverse reaction outweighs the risk of hemolysis [US].
- **Hepatocellular Injury:** Liver injury has been observed in patients treated with **mitapivat** for other conditions at higher-than-recommended doses. 
    - **Monitoring:** Obtain liver tests (ALT, AST, bilirubin) at baseline, then monthly for the first 6 months, and as clinically indicated thereafter.
    - **Management:** Interrupt treatment if ALT exceeds 5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN) or if clinically significant increases occur. Discontinue permanently if **mitapivat**-induced hepatic injury is confirmed [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - Decreased estrone and estradiol levels (in males)
    - Increased urate (hyperuricemia)
    - Back pain
    - Arthralgia
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Hypertriglyceridemia (8%)
    - Gastroenteritis (8%)
    - Hot flush (8%)
    - Oropharyngeal pain (8%)
    - Hypertension (5%)
    - Arrhythmia, including tachycardia and atrial fibrillation (5%)
    - Paresthesia (5%)
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Reported in 10% of patients in pivotal trials, including atrial fibrillation, gastroenteritis, rib fracture, and musculoskeletal pain [US].
- **Hormonal Variations:** In male patients, **mitapivat** may increase serum testosterone and decrease estrone/estradiol. These changes typically return to baseline within 28 days of treatment cessation [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A Inhibitors and Inducers:** Co-administration should be avoided as it significantly alters **mitapivat** exposure [US].
- **Moderate CYP3A Inhibitors:** Do not titrate **mitapivat** beyond 20 mg twice daily due to increased plasma concentrations [US].
- **Moderate CYP3A Inducers:** Consider alternative non-inducing therapies. If co-administration is necessary, monitor Hb; the **mitapivat** dose may be increased up to 100 mg twice daily [US].
- **Effect of Mitapivat on Other Drugs:**
    - **CYP3A, CYP2B6, CYP2C Substrates:** **Mitapivat** induces these enzymes. Avoid use with narrow therapeutic index substrates.
    - **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Induction of CYP3A may reduce contraceptive efficacy. Patients should use non-hormonal or barrier methods [US].
    - **UGT1A1 Substrates:** Induction may decrease concentrations of substrates.
    - **P-gp Substrates:** **Mitapivat** inhibits P-gp; monitor for adverse reactions when used with narrow therapeutic index P-gp substrates [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** Insufficient human data are available to evaluate drug-associated risks. Animal studies showed maternal toxicity and fetal malformations only at doses significantly higher than the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) [US].
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Untreated PK deficiency in pregnancy carries risks of acute hemolysis, preterm labour, and pre-eclampsia [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** There are no data regarding the presence of **mitapivat** in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother’s clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no adverse effects on male or female fertility at exposures approximately 45–48 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients <18 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended for patients with eGFR ≥60 mL/min/1.73 m². Data are limited for eGFR 30 to <60 mL/min/1.73 m², and no data are available for eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m² [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Avoid use in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class B or C) due to the risk of increased systemic exposure and potential for hepatocellular injury [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Mitapivat** is an allosteric activator of the pyruvate kinase tetramer. It increases the activity of the mutated PK-R enzyme, thereby increasing adenosine triphosphate (ATP) levels and decreasing 2,3-diphosphoglycerate (2,3-DPG) levels, which helps normalize red blood cell lifespan and reduce hemolysis [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~73%. Median Tmax is 0.5 to 1.0 hour. A high-fat meal reduces Cmax by 42% but does not change the overall AUC [US].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (97.7%).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized via CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Effective half-life is 3–5 hours. Total recovery of radiolabeled dose was 89.2% (49.6% in urine, 39.6% in feces) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ACTIVATE Trial:** A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in 80 adults with PK deficiency who were not regularly transfused. 40% of the **mitapivat** group achieved a hemoglobin response (≥1.5 g/dL increase) compared to 0% in the placebo group (p <0.0001).
- **ACTIVATE-T Trial:** A single-arm study in 27 regularly transfused adults. 33% of patients achieved a transfusion reduction response (≥33% reduction in RBC units transfused), and 22% became transfusion-free during the 24-week fixed-dose period [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Store blister wallets in the original carton until use to protect from light [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
