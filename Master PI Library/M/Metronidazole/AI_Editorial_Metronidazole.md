# Editorial Product Information: Metronidazole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines. Prescribers should consult the official PI approved in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Metronidazole (Chemical class: Nitroimidazole)
- **Brand Names:** Flagyl [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** J01X D01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Oral Tablets:** 400 mg [Australian]
    - **Suppositories:** 500 mg [Australian]
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral, Rectal
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Vitalion Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anaerobic Infections:** Treatment of confirmed or suspected infections involving *Bacteroides fragilis*, *Bacteroides* spp., *Fusobacteria*, *Eubacteria*, *Clostridia*, and anaerobic streptococci. Specific conditions include septicaemia, bacteraemia, brain abscess, necrotising pneumonia, osteomyelitis, puerperal sepsis, pelvic abscess, pelvic cellulitis, and postoperative wound infections.
- **Surgical Prophylaxis:** Prevention of surgical site infections (e.g., appendicectomy, colonic surgery, vaginal hysterectomy) potentially contaminated by anaerobic organisms [Australian].
- **Urogenital Trichomoniasis:** Treatment of trichomonal vaginitis in females and urethritis in males.
- **Bacterial Vaginosis.**
- **Amoebiasis:** All forms including intestinal disease, extraintestinal disease, and symptomless cyst passers.
- **Giardiasis.**
- **Acute Ulcerative Gingivitis.**

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Maximum Dose:** 4 g per 24-hour period should not be exceeded.
- **Administration:** Tablets should be swallowed whole with water during or after meals.

### Dosing by Indication [Australian]
- **Anaerobic Infections:** 
    - **Oral:** 400 mg three times daily for 7 days.
    - **Suppository:** 1 g (two 500 mg suppositories) every 8 hours for 3 days, then every 12 hours if continued beyond 3 days.
- **Urogenital Trichomoniasis:** 2 g as a single dose OR 200 mg three times daily for 7 days.
- **Bacterial Vaginosis:** 2 g as a single dose OR 400 mg twice daily for 7 days.
- **Amoebiasis (Invasive Intestinal):** 800 mg three times daily for 5 days.
- **Giardiasis:** 2 g once daily for 3 days.
- **Acute Ulcerative Gingivitis:** 200 mg three times daily for 3 days.

### Surgical Prophylaxis
- **Oral:** 400 mg 1–2 hours before surgery, then every 8 hours for 24 hours (if oral intake is feasible).
- **Suppository:** 1 g at diagnosis (pre-surgery), then every 8 hours for 48 hours post-surgery.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to metronidazole or other imidazole derivatives [Australian].
- **Blood Dyscrasias:** Patients with evidence of or a history of blood dyscrasias (due to risk of leucopenia).
- **Central Nervous System (CNS):** Patients with active organic disease of the CNS.
- **Cockayne Syndrome:** Contraindicated due to risk of severe, irreversible hepatotoxicity and fatal liver failure [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Alcohol Interaction:** Patients must avoid alcohol during therapy and for at least 24 hours afterward to prevent a **disulfiram-like reaction** (nausea, tachycardia, flushing).
- **Neurological Effects:** Discontinue treatment immediately if abnormal neurological signs occur (e.g., ataxia, dizziness, convulsive seizures, peripheral neuropathy). **Posterior Reversible Encephalopathy Syndrome (PRES)** has been reported; monitor for headache, seizures, or visual disturbances.
- **Long-term Therapy:** If used for >10 days, regular monitoring of total and differential leucocyte counts and clinical monitoring for peripheral or central neuropathy is required [Australian].
- **Severe Bullous Skin Reactions:** Discontinue immediately if signs of SJS, TEN, AGEP, or DRESS appear.
- **Suicidal Ideation:** Cases with or without depression have been reported; advise patients to seek immediate medical help if psychiatric symptoms emerge.
- **Latex Products:** Flagyl suppositories may increase the risk of rupture in condoms or diaphragms [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| System Organ Class | Frequency/Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | **Very Common/Common:** Nausea, metallic taste, anorexia, epigastric distress. **Rare:** Pancreatitis (reversible), oral mucositis, furry tongue. |
| **Psychiatric/CNS** | Headache, dizziness, vertigo, confusion, hallucinations. **Serious:** Convulsive seizures, peripheral neuropathy, encephalopathy, aseptic meningitis. |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Increased liver enzymes (AST, ALT, ALP), hepatitis, jaundice. **Fatal:** Acute liver failure in Cockayne Syndrome. |
| **Haematology** | Leucopenia (transient). **Rare:** Agranulocytosis, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, bone marrow depression. |
| **Skin** | Rash, pruritus, flushing. **Serious:** SJS, TEN, AGEP, DRESS. |
| **Other** | Darkened urine (metabolite-related, no clinical significance), hearing impairment, tinnitus, QT prolongation (especially with concurrent QT-prolonging drugs). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Metronidazole enhances anticoagulant activity; prothrombin time should be monitored and anticoagulant doses recalibrated.
- **Lithium:** May increase serum lithium levels, leading to toxicity; monitor lithium, creatinine, and electrolytes.
- **Liver Enzyme Inducers (e.g., Phenytoin, Phenobarbital):** May accelerate metronidazole elimination, reducing plasma levels.
- **Liver Enzyme Inhibitors (e.g., Cimetidine):** May prolong metronidazole half-life.
- **Disulfiram:** Risk of psychotic reactions; do not use metronidazole in patients who have taken disulfiram within the last two weeks.
- **Busulfan/Ciclosporin/5-Fluorouracil:** Metronidazole may increase plasma levels or reduce clearance of these agents, increasing toxicity risk.
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Potential for additive effects on the QT interval.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B2.** 
    - **First Trimester:** Should not be used as it rapidly enters foetal circulation and effects on organogenesis are unknown.
    - **Second/Third Trimester:** Use for trichomoniasis should be restricted to cases where local palliative treatment is ineffective [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Metronidazole is secreted in breast milk at concentrations similar to serum levels. Because of potential tumorigenic and mutagenic risks, **breastfeeding is not recommended** [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight or age-dependent (refer to Section 3). For prophylaxis in children <12 years, doses range from 100 mg to 400 mg depending on age.
- **Elderly Use:** Pharmacokinetics may be altered; monitoring of serum levels may be necessary to adjust dosage [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Half-life of metronidazole is unchanged, but metabolites may accumulate. In patients on haemodialysis, a supplemental dose is required post-dialysis as the drug is rapidly removed.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Caution is required in patients with severe hepatic disease or hepatic encephalopathy; dosages should be decreased and plasma levels monitored [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Metronidazole exerts bactericidal activity against obligate anaerobes by interfering with DNA synthesis. It is inactive against aerobic and facultative anaerobic bacteria.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and almost completely absorbed after oral administration (peak: 1–2 hours). Rectal absorption is slower and less complete (approx. 67–82% bioavailability; peak: 8 hours).
    - **Distribution:** Widely distributed in tissues; crosses the blood-brain barrier and placenta. Minimal protein binding.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted mostly in urine as metronidazole and metabolites. Half-life is approximately 6–7 hours (oral/IV).
