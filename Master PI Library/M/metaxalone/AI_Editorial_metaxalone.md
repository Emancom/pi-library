# Editorial Product Information: Metaxalone

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating available regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a supplement to, not a substitute for, the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Metaxalone, a skeletal muscle relaxant belonging to the oxazolidinone chemical class.
- **Brand Name(s):** Metaxalone (repackaged by RemedyRepack Inc.; originally manufactured for Lannett Company, Inc.) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Oral Tablet:** 800 mg (pink, capsule-shaped, scored, debossed with "LCI" and "14" bisect "35") [US].
    - **Note:** 640 mg tablets may exist in the market but are not the subject of this specific source document [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lannett Company, Inc. (repackaged by RemedyRepack Inc.) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Musculoskeletal Conditions:** Indicated as an adjunct to rest, physical therapy, and other conservative measures for the relief of discomfort associated with acute, painful musculoskeletal conditions [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosage:** 800 mg orally, three to four times daily [US].
- **Pediatric Dosage (≥ 13 years):** 800 mg orally, three to four times daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - May be taken with or without food.
    - **Food Effect:** Administration with a high-fat meal significantly increases metaxalone exposure (Cmax and AUC) and decreases the elimination half-life [US].
- **Switching Instructions:**
    - **800 mg vs. 640 mg:** These strengths are NOT substitutable on a mg-to-mg basis due to pharmacokinetic differences [US].
    - **Protocol:** If switching a patient from 640 mg (taken on an empty stomach) to 800 mg, the 800 mg dose should also be taken on an empty stomach. Do not switch strengths if the patient is currently taking the medication with food [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to metaxalone or any excipients in the formulation [US].
- Known history of or tendency toward drug-induced, hemolytic, or other anemias [US].
- Severe renal impairment [US].
- Severe hepatic impairment [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening cases have been reported, particularly when metaxalone is used concomitantly with other serotonergic drugs (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, triptans, MAOIs) or in overdose scenarios. Monitor for mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular aberrations. Discontinue immediately if suspected [US].
- **CNS Depression:** Metaxalone may significantly impair mental and physical abilities required for hazardous tasks (e.g., driving, operating machinery). This effect is potentiated by alcohol and other CNS depressants [US].
- **Hepatic and Renal Effects:** Use with caution in patients with mild-to-moderate impairment. Metaxalone is contraindicated in severe impairment due to its extensive hepatic metabolism and renal excretion of metabolites [US].
- **Monitoring:** Follow patients closely for signs of respiratory depression and sedation, especially during treatment initiation or dose escalation [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are reported from clinical studies and post-marketing surveillance; frequencies are not always definitively established.

| **Common (>5%)** | Gastrointestinal | Nausea, vomiting |
| **Frequency Not Specified** | Central Nervous System | Drowsiness, dizziness, headache, nervousness, irritability, serotonin syndrome |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Serotonin syndrome, anaphylaxis, and severe hematologic abnormalities (leukopenia/anemia) require immediate clinical intervention [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome when combined with SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, triptans, opioids (fentanyl, meperidine), and MAOIs (including linezolid).
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive sedative effects and increased risk of respiratory depression when used with alcohol, benzodiazepines, opioids, or tricyclic antidepressants [US].
- **Laboratory Test Interference:** Metaxalone may cause false-positive results in **Benedict's tests** for urinary glucose due to an unidentified reducing substance. Glucose-specific tests (e.g., Clinistix) should be used instead [US].
- **CYP Enzymes:** *In vitro* data suggests metaxalone does not inhibit or induce major CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2A6, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, 3A4) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (Current US labeling uses a Risk Summary format). 
    - There are no available human data to evaluate drug-associated risks of birth defects or miscarriage. Animal reproduction studies in rats did not reveal foetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if metaxalone or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. Consider the developmental benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother's clinical need and any potential adverse effects on the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No effects on fertility were observed in rat studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for patients **13 years of age and older**. Not established for children 12 years and younger [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Patients ≥ 65 years may be more susceptible to CNS depression. While the recommended dose is the same as for younger adults, higher plasma concentrations are observed in the fasted state in elderly patients [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. Use with caution in mild-to-moderate cases [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. Use with caution in mild-to-moderate cases [US].
- **Gender Differences:** Exposure (Cmax and AUC) is significantly higher in females than in males; the mean half-life is also longer in females (11.1 hours vs. 7.6 hours) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Not fully characterised. The effect is likely related to general CNS depression (sedative properties). It has no direct effect on the contractile mechanism of striated muscle, the motor end plate, or the nerve fibre [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** No direct exposure-response relationship has been established for safety or efficacy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Mean Tmax is 3.3 hours (fasted). High-fat food increases Cmax by nearly 94% and AUC by 42-46% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is ~800 L. Protein binding is unknown [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP1A2, CYP2D6, CYP2E1, and CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life is 9 ± 4.8 hours. Excreted in urine as unidentified metabolites [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Specific pivotal trial data (trial names or patient counts) are not detailed in the provided source document, although the efficacy of the 800 mg dose was evaluated in healthy volunteer groups for pharmacokinetic comparison [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children. Use USP-controlled room temperature conditions [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only (Rx Only). Scheduled as a non-controlled substance under federal law [US].
