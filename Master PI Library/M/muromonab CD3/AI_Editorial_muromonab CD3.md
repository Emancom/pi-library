# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Clear, colourless sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer intravenously as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Timing:** Administer as a slow bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** [US Boxed Warning: None]. Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of IV administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate the risks/benefits before considering re-administration [US].

### General Precautions
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While this concerns oral administration more specifically, chronic high doses in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Monitor carnitine levels and clinical response regularly. In patients on warfarin, monitor International Normalized Ratio (INR) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions vary by population; those in ESRD patients are often confounded by underlying pathology.

### Common Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Gastritis and abdominal pain have been observed.
- **Body Odor:** A distinct body odor may occur with therapy.
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness, headache, and paresthesia.

### Frequency Data (ESRD Clinical Trials)
| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, hyperkalemia, peripheral edema |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Seizures (frequency not specified) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increased INR when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Perform frequent INR monitoring in patients receiving warfarin upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specifically lettered, but indicates use only if clearly needed]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies exist in pregnant women [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration. Benefits to the mother should be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (see Section 3).
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources; generally, dose selection should be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function in the elderly.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. IV levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3 for dosing) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine helps clear accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. 
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose, approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean plasma concentrations from 20 µmol/L to trough levels between 54 and 180 µmol/L after one week (3 doses). 
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature when mixed in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL).
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
