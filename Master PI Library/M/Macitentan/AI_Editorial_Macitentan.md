# Editorial Product Information: Macitentan

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of macitentan for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Macitentan, a dual endothelin receptor antagonist (ERA).
- **Brand Name:** OPONOVA [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C02KX04 (Endothelin receptor antagonists).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - **Film-coated tablet:** 10 mg macitentan [Australian]. 
    - Physical description: White to off-white, round, debossed with "T" on one side [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Nova Pharmaceuticals Australasia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Macitentan is indicated for the treatment of Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH) to reduce morbidity and mortality. It may be used as monotherapy or in combination with phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE5) inhibitors or inhaled prostanoids [Australian]. Specifically approved for:
- Idiopathic PAH.
- Heritable PAH.
- PAH associated with connective tissue disease.
- PAH associated with congenital heart disease with repaired shunts.
- Patients with WHO Functional Class II, III, or IV symptoms [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 10 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Administration:** To be swallowed whole with water; tablets must not be broken. May be taken with or without food [Australian].
- **Titration:** Doses higher than 10 mg daily are not recommended and have not been studied [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources (refer to general clinical practice or local patient leaflets).
- **Initiation:** Treatment should only be started and monitored by a physician experienced in PAH management [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated in women who are or may become pregnant [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Women of child-bearing potential not using reliable contraception [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to macitentan or any excipients (including lactose and soya bean products) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe hepatic impairment (with or without cirrhosis) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Enzymes:** Baseline aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and/or alanine aminotransferase (ALT) > 3 × the Upper Limit of Normal (ULN) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [Australian]:** Macitentan may cause birth defects and is strictly contraindicated in pregnancy.
- **Liver Function:** Hepatic enzyme elevations and serious hepatic events are a class effect of ERAs. Liver enzyme tests are required prior to initiation. Monthly monitoring of aminotransferases is recommended during treatment. Discontinuation is required if ALT/AST > 3 × ULN with bilirubin > 2 × ULN or clinical symptoms of injury [Australian].
- **Haematological Changes:** Decreases in haemoglobin and haematocrit typically occur within the first 4–12 weeks and then stabilise. Initiation is not recommended in patients with clinically significant anaemia. Haemoglobin measurement is required prior to initiation and periodically during treatment [Australian].
- **Fluid Retention:** Edema/fluid retention is associated with ERAs and PAH. If clinically significant fluid retention develops, evaluate for underlying causes (e.g., heart failure) [Australian].
- **Pulmonary Veno-occlusive Disease:** If signs of pulmonary edema occur, consider the possibility of associated veno-occlusive disease; discontinuation may be required [Australian].
- **Hypotension:** Caution is advised in patients with pre-existing hypotension [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate. Frequencies from the SERAPHIN study (10 mg dose) are provided below.

| System Organ Class | Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infections** | Very Common (>10%) | Nasopharyngitis (14%), Bronchitis (11.6%) |
| | Common (1–10%) | Pharyngitis, Influenza, Urinary tract infection |
| **Nervous System** | Very Common (>10%) | Headache (13.6%) |
| **Blood/Lymphatic**| Very Common (>10%) | Anaemia (13.2%) |
| | Common (1–10%) | Thrombocytopenia |
| **Vascular** | Common (1–10%) | Hypotension, Flushing |
| **Gastrointestinal**| Common (1–10%) | Diarrhoea, Abdominal pain |
| **General** | Very Common (>10%) | Oedema/fluid retention (up to 21.9% in long-term data) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Serious hepatic injury and severe anaemia requiring transfusion have been reported with the ERA class [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, clarithromycin). These can increase macitentan exposure approximately 2- to 3-fold. Caution is advised [Australian].
- **Moderate Dual CYP3A4/CYP2C9 Inhibitors:** (e.g., fluconazole, amiodarone). May significantly increase macitentan exposure (predicted 3.8-fold with fluconazole). Use with caution [Australian].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, St. John’s Wort, carbamazepine, phenytoin). These significantly reduce macitentan exposure (rifampicin reduced exposure by 79%) and may lead to reduced efficacy. Combination should be avoided [Australian].
- **Sildenafil:** Concomitant use resulted in a 15% increase in sildenafil exposure; no dose adjustment is required [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** No clinically relevant effect on INR or warfarin pharmacokinetics [Australian].
- **Other:** No clinically relevant interactions with ciclosporin A, hormonal contraceptives, riociguat, or rosuvastatin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X [Australian]**. Macitentan was teratogenic in animal studies (cardiovascular and mandibular arch abnormalities). Pregnancy must be excluded before initiation. Monthly pregnancy tests are recommended [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use two reliable methods of contraception during treatment and for **3 months** after discontinuation [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Animal data shows excretion into milk; risk to newborns cannot be excluded [Australian].
- **Fertility:** ERAs may have an adverse effect on spermatogenesis. In animals, macitentan caused testicular tubular atrophy and abnormal sperm morphology at high exposures [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited clinical experience in patients aged 12 and above. Recommended dose for patients ≥ 12 years and > 40 kg is 10 mg once daily. Safety and efficacy in children < 12 years not established; Oponova brand is not suitable for patients < 12 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients > 65 years. Limited data available for patients > 75 years; caution is recommended [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required based on PK data. However, there is no clinical experience in patients with severe renal impairment (caution advised) and use is **not recommended** in patients undergoing dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. **Contraindicated** in severe hepatic impairment or baseline AST/ALT > 3 × ULN [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Macitentan is an orally active, dual endothelin receptor (ETA and ETB) antagonist. It prevents the binding of ET-1 (a potent vasoconstrictor and mediator of fibrosis/hypertrophy) to its receptors. It features high affinity and sustained receptor occupancy [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Does not affect QTc interval at 3x the therapeutic dose [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is approximately 8 hours. Bioavailability is unknown. Exposure is not affected by food [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (> 99% to albumin). Vss is approximately 50 L [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by **CYP3A4** (99%) to an active metabolite, **aprocitentan**, which is approximately 5-fold less potent than the parent drug [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is ~16 hours (parent) and ~48 hours (active metabolite). Primarily excreted via urine (50%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SERAPHIN Study:** A Phase 3, multicentre, double-blind, event-driven study (N=742). Patients received placebo, 3 mg, or 10 mg macitentan once daily.
- **Primary Outcomes:** Macitentan 10 mg resulted in a **45% reduction** in the risk of morbidity or mortality events (death, atrial septostomy, lung transplant, IV/SC prostanoid initiation, or PAH worsening) compared to placebo (p<0.0001).
- **Secondary Outcomes:** Significant increase in 6-minute walk distance (mean 22 metres) and improvement in WHO Functional Class at month 6 [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
