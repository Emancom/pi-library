# Editorial Product Information: Melphalan

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction:
    - **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**
- Information regarding other major jurisdictions (e.g., US FDA, European EMA, Health Canada) was not provided in the source documents and is therefore listed as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing and dispensing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Melphalan hydrochloride (L-phenylalanine mustard)
- **Chemical Class:** Bifunctional alkylating agent (nitrogen mustard derivative)
- **Brand Names:** **Alkeran** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01AA03
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Powder for Injection:** Composite pack containing one single-use vial of 50 mg lyophilized melphalan (as hydrochloride) and one 10 mL vial of sterile diluent [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only. 
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Multiple Myeloma:** Indicated for the treatment of patients with multiple myeloma for whom oral therapy is considered inappropriate [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard IV Dose:** 16 mg/m² administered as a single infusion over 15 to 20 minutes [Australian].
- **Frequency:** Administered at 2-week intervals for a total of four doses. Following adequate recovery from toxicity, the interval is increased to every 4 weeks [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Must be performed at room temperature. Rapidly inject 10 mL of the provided diluent into the 50 mg vial and shake vigorously immediately until clear. This yields a 5 mg/mL solution [Australian].
- **Dilution:** Immediately dilute the reconstituted solution in 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to a concentration no greater than 0.45 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Stability:** The solution is highly unstable. Administration must be completed within 60 minutes of reconstitution. Do **not** refrigerate the reconstituted product, as a precipitate will form [Australian].
- **Incompatibility:** **Alkeran** is incompatible with dextrose-containing solutions; only 0.9% Sodium Chloride should be used [Australian].

### Monitoring and Titration
- Dose adjustment should be based on the blood cell count nadir and the counts on the day of treatment.
- Treatment should be temporarily interrupted if there is an abnormally large fall in leucocyte or platelet counts [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Prior Resistance:** Patients whose disease has demonstrated prior resistance to melphalan [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to melphalan or any excipients [Australian].
- **Lactation:** Breastfeeding is contraindicated during treatment [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Bone Marrow Suppression:** **Alkeran** is a potent myelosuppressive agent. Severe suppression leading to infection or bleeding may occur. Frequent monitoring of CBC (hemoglobin, WBC with differential, and platelets) is mandatory [Australian].
- **Thromboembolic Events:** Increased risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE) when used in combination with immunomodulatory agents (lenalidomide or thalidomide) plus corticosteroids. Prophylactic measures should be assessed based on individual risk factors [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity/Anaphylaxis:** Occurs in approximately 2% of patients, typically after multiple courses. If anaphylaxis occurs, the infusion must be stopped immediately and not restarted [Australian].
- **Carcinogenicity:** Linked to secondary primary malignancies (SPM), particularly acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS). Risk increases with cumulative dosing and chronicity [Australian].
- **Extravasation:** **Alkeran** may cause local tissue damage; it should not be administered via direct injection into a peripheral vein [Australian].
- **Acute Kidney Injury:** High-dose melphalan may cause acute renal failure, particularly in those with pre-existing impairment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies are based on limited modern clinical documentation and may vary by dose and combination therapy.*

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Bone marrow suppression (leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, anemia), nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, stomatitis (high dose), alopecia (high dose), muscle atrophy/fibrosis/myalgia (following limb perfusion), subjective feeling of warmth/tingling at injection site [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Alopecia (conventional dose), blood urea increased, compartment syndrome (following limb perfusion) [Australian]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hemolytic anemia, interstitial lung disease, pulmonary fibrosis (including fatal reports), stomatitis (conventional dose), hepatic disorders (hepatitis, jaundice), veno-occlusive disease, maculopapular rash, pruritus [Australian]. |
| **Not Known** | Secondary AML/MDS, acute kidney injury/failure, muscle necrosis/rhabdomyolysis, azoospermia, amenorrhea, deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Ciclosporin:** Increased risk of impaired renal function in patients conditioned with high-dose melphalan prior to bone marrow transplant [Australian].
- **Cisplatin:** May induce renal dysfunction, subsequently altering melphalan clearance [Australian].
- **Carmustine:** Melphalan may reduce the threshold for carmustine-induced lung toxicity [Australian].
- **Nalidixic Acid:** Concurrent use in pediatric patients may increase the risk of severe hemorrhagic necrotic enterocolitis [Australian].
- **Busulfan:** In pediatric populations, melphalan administration within 24 hours of oral busulfan may influence toxicity development [Australian].
- **Vaccines:** Immunization with live organism vaccines is not recommended in immunocompromised patients [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D** [Australian]. 
    - Melphalan is mutagenic and potentially teratogenic. It may cause fetal harm, including brain and eye malformations and intrauterine death. Use should be avoided during pregnancy, especially in the first trimester [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated; it is unknown if melphalan is excreted in human milk [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Both male and female patients (and/or their partners) must use effective contraception during treatment and for 6 months following cessation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Causes suppression of ovarian function (amenorrhea) and testicular suppression (potentially irreversible sterility) [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical experience has not identified specific differences in response compared to younger patients, but cautious dose selection is advised due to potential decreases in hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance is decreased. Consider a dose reduction of up to 50% in patients with moderate to severe renal impairment (e.g., BUN ≥30 mg/dL). Monitor closely for severe bone marrow suppression [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though general caution is advised in the elderly [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A bifunctional alkylating agent. It forms carbonium intermediates that covalently bind to the 7-nitrogen of guanine on DNA, creating cross-links that prevent cell replication in both resting and rapidly dividing cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is 0.5 L/kg. Plasma protein binding is 60–90% (primarily albumin). CSF penetration is low [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily eliminated via spontaneous chemical hydrolysis to monohydroxymelphalan and dihydroxymelphalan [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Biexponential decline with a terminal elimination half-life of approximately 75 minutes. Total body clearance is approximately 7 to 9 mL/min/kg [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **IV vs. Oral Comparison:** A randomized trial in multiple myeloma compared IV melphalan (16 mg/m²) to oral melphalan. Response rates at 22 weeks were comparable (38% IV vs 44% oral, p>0.2). However, severe myelotoxicity (WBC ≤1000 and/or platelets ≤25,000) was significantly more common with the IV formulation (28%) than the oral formulation (11%) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 30°C. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Do **not** refrigerate; store at room temperature. Must be used within 60 minutes [Australian].
- **Handling:** Cytotoxic agent. Use appropriate protective equipment (gloves, goggles, gown) during preparation. Dispose of as hazardous waste (e.g., high-temperature incineration) [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
