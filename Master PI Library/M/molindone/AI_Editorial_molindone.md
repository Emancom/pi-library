# Editorial Product Information: Molindone Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the product information for molindone hydrochloride. It is intended as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Molindone hydrochloride
- **Chemical Class:** Dihydroindolone (structurally distinct from phenothiazines, butyrophenones, and thioxanthenes)
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources; currently available as generic tablets [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 5 mg, 10 mg, and 25 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Epic Pharma, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Schizophrenia:** Indicated for the management of schizophrenia. Efficacy was established in clinical settings involving acutely ill patients, both newly and chronically hospitalized [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dosage:** The standard starting dose is 50 mg to 75 mg per day [US].
- **Titration:** Increase to 100 mg per day within 3 to 4 days. Dosage should be individualized based on symptom severity and clinical response [US].
- **Maximum Dosage:** Up to 225 mg per day may be required for patients with severe symptomatology [US].

### Maintenance Dosage
Maintenance therapy should be adjusted according to the severity of the condition [US]:
- **Mild:** 5 mg to 15 mg, three or four times daily.
- **Moderate:** 10 mg to 25 mg, three or four times daily.
- **Severe:** Up to 225 mg per day.

### Special Administration Notes
- **Elderly and Debilitated Patients:** Initiation should occur at a lower dosage range [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Severe Central Nervous System (CNS) Depression:** Including depression induced by alcohol, barbiturates, or narcotics [US].
- **Comatose States:** [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to molindone hydrochloride [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **Increased Mortality in Elderly Patients with Dementia-Related Psychosis:** Antipsychotic drugs are associated with an increased risk of death in elderly patients treated for dementia-related psychosis. Most deaths in clinical trials appeared cardiovascular or infectious in nature. Molindone is **not approved** for the treatment of dementia-related psychosis.

### General Warnings
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS):** A potentially fatal complex (hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, autonomic instability). Requires immediate discontinuation of the drug and intensive symptomatic monitoring [US].
- **Tardive Dyskinesia (TD):** Potentially irreversible, involuntary movements. Risk increases with treatment duration and cumulative dose, though it can occur at low doses. Discontinuation should be considered if symptoms appear [US].
- **Falls:** Somnolence, postural hypotension, and motor instability may lead to falls and subsequent injuries. Conduct fall risk assessments for at-risk patients [US].
- **Seizure Threshold:** While molindone does not lower the seizure threshold in animal models to the same extent as sedating antipsychotics, convulsive seizures have been reported in humans [US].
- **Leukopenia, Neutropenia, and Agranulocytosis:** Class effect for antipsychotics. Monitor Complete Blood Count (CBC) frequently during the first few months in patients with pre-existing low White Blood Cell (WBC) counts or history of drug-induced leukopenia [US].

### Precautions
- **Mental Alertness:** Patients may experience initial drowsiness and should avoid activities requiring alertness (e.g., driving) until their response is established [US].
- **Antiemetic Effect:** May obscure signs of intestinal obstruction or brain tumors [US].
- **Prolactin Elevation:** Chronic administration elevates prolactin levels. Caution is advised in patients with previously detected breast cancer, as a fraction of human breast cancers may be prolactin-dependent [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Category: Very Common (>10%)
- **CNS:** Drowsiness (initial, usually subsides with continued use or dose reduction) [US].

### Frequency Category: Common (1–10%) to Uncommon (0.1–1%)
- **Neurological:** Extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS) including akathisia (motor restlessness) and Parkinsonian syndrome (rigidity, tremor, akinesia) [US].
- **Autonomic:** Blurring of vision, tachycardia, nausea, dry mouth, and salivation [US].
- **Metabolic:** Amenorrhea, galactorrhea, gynecomastia, and changes in libido [US].

### Frequency Category: Rare (<0.1%)
- **Neurological:** Dystonia (prolonged muscle contractions), particularly in younger males [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Transient, non-specific T-wave changes on EKG; significant hypotension [US].
- **Hematologic:** Leucopenia and leucocytosis [US].
- **Hepatic:** Clinically significant alterations in liver function [US].
- **Urogenital:** Priapism (may require surgical intervention) [US].
- **Dermatologic:** Early non-specific skin rash [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Phenytoin and Tetracyclines:** The calcium sulfate excipient in molindone tablets may interfere with the absorption of these medications [US].
- **CNS Depressants:** Caution is advised, though specific potentiation with barbiturates or chloral hydrate was not reported in animal studies [US].
- **Antiparkinson Drugs:** No increased toxicity reported when co-administered in animal studies [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified by letter [US]. 
    - **Animal Data:** No teratogenic potential in rats, mice, or rabbits. Slight increase in resorptions noted in mice at 20–40 mg/kg/day [US].
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Neonates exposed in the third trimester are at risk for EPS and withdrawal symptoms (agitation, hypertonia, respiratory distress). Use only if potential benefits justify the risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Data on the presence of molindone in human milk are unavailable [US].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for patients under 12 years of age as safety and efficacy have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher risk of death in dementia-related psychosis (see Section 5). Start treatment at the lower end of the dosing range [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dose adjustments are not provided in source documents, though rare hepatic effects are noted [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Exerts effects on the ascending reticular activating system. Its profile resembles other antipsychotics by reducing locomotion and aggressiveness and antagonizing amphetamine-induced stereotypy [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed following oral administration [US].
    - **Peak Plasma Time:** Approximately 1.5 hours [US].
    - **Duration of Effect:** Single oral dose pharmacological effect persists for 24 to 36 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism/Excretion:** Metabolized into 36 recognized metabolites. Less than 2% to 3% of the drug is excreted unmetabolized in urine and feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Schizophrenia Trials:** Efficacy was established in clinical studies involving both acutely ill and chronically hospitalized patients with schizophrenia. Specific trial identifiers or statistical outcomes were not detailed in the source document [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F).
- **Packaging:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers with child-resistant closures [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only (Rx Only). Scheduled as a non-controlled substance [US].
