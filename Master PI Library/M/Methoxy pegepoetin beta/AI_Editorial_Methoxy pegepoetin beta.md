# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** US (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis of available regulatory information and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for definitive guidance. Note: Only the United States PI was provided for this synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg via slow IV bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or infusion.
    - Acute Crisis: A loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours).
    - Maintenance: Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg/day, though doses up to 300 mg/kg/day have been utilized.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow IV bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Adjustment: Dose may be titrated downward (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels (target: 40–50 µmol/L).

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard unused portions as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and evaluate risks/benefits before considering re-administration.
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed.
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **Metabolic Disorders:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Perform weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L).
    - **ESRD:** Monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels.
- **Oral vs. IV in Renal Impairment:** Safety of high-dose **oral** levocarnitine in renal impairment is not established; chronic oral use in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by underlying pathology (e.g., ESRD, metabolic errors).

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (≥5%)** | Transient nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhea, body odor, headache, hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, chest pain, fever, infection, injection site reaction, pharyngitis, and cough. |
| **Neurologic** | Seizures, dizziness, paresthesia, and depression. |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, and hypervolemia. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and other severe hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category B (equivalent)]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women exist. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. While carnitine is a normal component of human milk, the risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit of maternal supplementation. 
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg) and applicable to pediatric patients.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects. Dose selection should be cautious, starting at the low end of the range.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. High-dose chronic **oral** administration should be avoided in patients with severely compromised renal function due to metabolite accumulation [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations and may alleviate metabolic abnormalities caused by carnitine loss during dialysis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal half-life is 17.4 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough levels from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations. However, the impact of this supplementation on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentration) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags.
