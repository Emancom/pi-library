# Editorial Product Information: Moexipril Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of moexipril hydrochloride, synthesised from official product information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive regional requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Moexipril hydrochloride (a non-sulfhydryl precursor to the active ACE inhibitor moexiprilat).
- **Chemical Class:** Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) Inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** Moexipril Hydrochloride Tablets USP (Glenmark) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** C09AA13.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 7.5 mg and 15 mg [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Glenmark Pharmaceuticals Inc. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** For the treatment of hypertension. It may be utilised as monotherapy or in combination with other antihypertensive agents, specifically thiazide diuretics [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:**
    - **Initial Dose (Non-diuretic patients):** 7.5 mg once daily, administered one hour before meals [United States].
    - **Maintenance Dose:** 7.5 mg to 30 mg daily, administered in one or two divided doses [United States].
- **Dosing in Patients on Diuretics:**
    - If possible, discontinue the diuretic 2 to 3 days prior to initiating moexipril.
    - If the diuretic cannot be stopped, initiate moexipril at a reduced dose of 3.75 mg under medical supervision until blood pressure stabilises [United States].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Must be taken on an empty stomach (one hour before meals) as food significantly reduces bioavailability [United States].
- **Titration:** Dosage should be adjusted based on the trough blood pressure response (measured just before the next dose).
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to moexipril hydrochloride or any component of the formulation [United States].
- History of angioedema related to previous treatment with an ACE inhibitor [United States].
- **Dual Blockade:** Concomitant use with aliskiren in patients with diabetes mellitus [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning: Fetal Toxicity [United States]**
    - Drugs that act directly on the renin-angiotensin system (RAS) can cause injury and death to the developing fetus.
    - Discontinue moexipril immediately upon detection of pregnancy.
- **Angioedema:**
    - **Head and Neck:** May involve the face, extremities, lips, tongue, glottis, and/or larynx. Laryngeal edema can be fatal. Immediate treatment with epinephrine 1:1000 may be required.
    - **Intestinal:** May present as abdominal pain (with or without nausea/vomiting); should be considered in the differential diagnosis of patients on ACE inhibitors with gastrointestinal symptoms [United States].
- **Anaphylactoid Reactions:**
    - High-risk during desensitisation treatment (e.g., hymenoptera venom) and during dialysis with high-flux membranes [United States].
- **Hypotension:** Most likely in volume- or salt-depleted patients (e.g., those on vigorous diuretics, dialysis, or with severe diarrhea/vomiting).
- **Neutropenia/Agranulocytosis:** Monitoring of white blood cell counts should be considered, particularly in patients with renal impairment or collagen-vascular disease [United States].
- **Hepatic Failure:** Rarely associated with a syndrome starting with cholestatic jaundice progressing to fulminant hepatic necrosis; discontinue if jaundice or marked liver enzyme elevations occur [United States].
- **Hyperkalemia:** Risk factors include renal insufficiency, diabetes, and use of potassium-sparing agents [United States].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic assessment of renal function and serum electrolytes is recommended.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild and transient. Discontinuation due to adverse effects occurred in approximately 3.4% of patients in clinical trials [United States].

| **Dizziness** | 4.3% | 2.2% |
| **Diarrhea** | 3.1% | 2.2% |
| **Flu Syndrome** | 3.1% | 0% |

| **Flushing** | 1.6% | 0% |

- **Other effects (<1%):** Symptomatic hypotension (0.5%), syncope, chest pain, palpitations, proteinuria, and taste disturbances [United States].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Angioedema, renal insufficiency, neutropenia, and anaphylactoid reactions.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Diuretics:** May lead to excessive hypotension; reduce initial moexipril dose or pause diuretic therapy [United States].
- **Potassium-Sparing Diuretics/Supplements:** Increased risk of hyperkalemia. Monitor serum potassium closely [United States].
- **Lithium:** ACE inhibitors may increase serum lithium levels and toxicity; frequent monitoring is required [United States].
- **Gold (Sodium Aurothiomalate):** Nitritoid reactions (flushing, nausea, hypotension) have been reported with concomitant ACE inhibitor therapy [United States].
- **NSAIDS/COX-2 Inhibitors:** May attenuate the antihypertensive effect and increase the risk of renal function deterioration, especially in elderly or volume-depleted patients [United States].
- **Dual Blockade (ARB, Aliskiren):** Increased risk of hypotension, hyperkalemia, and renal impairment. Do not use aliskiren with moexipril in patients with diabetes or renal impairment (GFR <60 mL/min) [United States].
- **Warfarin/Digoxin/Cimetidine:** No clinically significant pharmacokinetic interactions identified [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [United States].** Use during the second and third trimesters is associated with fetal and neonatal injury, including skull hypoplasia, anuria, hypotension, renal failure, and death. If pregnancy is detected, moexipril must be discontinued as soon as possible [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if moexipril is excreted in human milk. Exercise caution and consider alternative therapy [United States].
- **Fertility:** No indication of impaired fertility was observed in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Moexiprilat plasma levels are approximately 30% higher in the elderly. Dose selection should be cautious, typically starting at the lower end of the range [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** In patients with CrCl ≤40 mL/min/1.73 m², initiate at 3.75 mg once daily. Maximum recommended daily dose is 15 mg [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In patients with mild to moderate cirrhosis, the AUC of moexiprilat increased by approximately 300% while Cmax decreased [United States]. Use with caution.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Moexipril is a prodrug for moexiprilat, which inhibits ACE. This prevents the conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II (a potent vasoconstrictor) and inhibits the degradation of bradykinin (a vasodilator). This results in decreased systemic vascular resistance and reduced aldosterone secretion [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability as moexiprilat is approximately 13%. Absorption is significantly reduced by food (Cmax reduced by 70–80%) [United States].
    - **Distribution:** Moexiprilat is ~50% protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Converted to moexiprilat (1000 times more potent than parent) and diketopiperazine derivatives [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life of moexiprilat is 2 to 9 hours. Elimination is via both urine and feces [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Moexipril lowers sitting diastolic and systolic blood pressure at trough by 3–6 mmHg and 4–11 mmHg more than placebo, respectively, at doses of 7.5–30 mg daily [United States].
- Antihypertensive effects are detectable within 1 hour, with peak effects between 3 and 6 hours.
- Chronic therapy shows maximal reduction after 4 weeks, with effects maintained for up to 24 months [United States].
- Like other ACE inhibitors, the effect is smaller in Black patients (predominantly a low-renin population) than in non-Black patients [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15° and 30°C (59° to 86°F) [United States].
- **Handling:** Keep container tightly closed and protect from excessive moisture [United States].
