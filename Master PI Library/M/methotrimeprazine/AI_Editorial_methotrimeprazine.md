# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the available data to facilitate clinical decision-making.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) Product Information was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mitochondrial fatty acid transport. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not specified in source, provided for reference).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic management of patients with genetic metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients requiring maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The standard dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Loading Doses:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses divided every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Daily maintenance doses typically remain near 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without a rigorous risk-benefit assessment and close monitoring [US].
- **Monitoring:** For patients with metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended. This includes blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Caution):** While this editorial concerns the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine in patients with severe renal impairment may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are often confounded by the underlying pathology (e.g., ESRD or metabolic crisis).
- **Common (>10% in ESRD trials):** Hypertension, headache, tachycardia, chest pain, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, pharyngitis, and fever [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Other Reported Effects:** Gastritis and a transient "fishy" body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a specific letter category in provided text]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation has not been specifically studied in nursing mothers. Decisions should weigh the benefits of maternal therapy against potential (though unquantified) risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg) and applicable to pediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in the source, though caution is generally advised due to the higher prevalence of decreased renal function in this population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** See "ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis" in Section 3 for specific dosing [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While levocarnitine effectively increases plasma concentrations, its impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in ESRD has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation is preservative-free [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only [US].
