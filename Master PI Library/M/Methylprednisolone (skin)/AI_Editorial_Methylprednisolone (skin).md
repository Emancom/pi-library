# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from congenital metabolic defects [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours [US].
    - Subsequent daily maintenance doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** Clinical response should be guided by plasma carnitine concentrations. For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD Adjustments:** Dosage adjustments in ESRD patients may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. If levels are below normal (40 to 50 µmol/L), initiation or upward titration may be required; downward adjustment to 5 mg/kg may occur if levels are supra-therapeutic [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been established in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) normally excreted in the urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>5%):**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (38%), pain (30%), headache (22%), fever (7%), abdominal pain (21%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (15%), tachycardia (6%) [US].
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (24–27%), cough (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
- **Less Frequent/Post-marketing:**
    - Body odor, gastritis, and dizziness [US].
    - Serious neurologic reactions (seizures) [US].
    - Serious hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis, bronchospasm) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a letter category]. Animal reproductive studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, adequate human studies are lacking. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. A risk-benefit analysis should consider the mother's clinical need versus potential infant exposure [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by body weight (50 mg/kg), similar to adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source; general caution regarding age-related organ dysfunction is standard clinical practice.
- **Renal Impairment:** See "ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis" under Section 3. Oral formulations are cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule essential for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria for beta-oxidation. It also aids in the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV dose, approximately 76% is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) significantly increased plasma concentrations from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L to trough levels of 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in ESRD patients demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL. Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion [US].
