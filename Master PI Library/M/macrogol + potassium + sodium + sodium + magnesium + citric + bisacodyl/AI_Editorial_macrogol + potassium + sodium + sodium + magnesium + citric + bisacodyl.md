# Editorial Product Information: macrogol + potassium + sodium + sodium + magnesium + citric + bisacodyl

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **macrogol + potassium + sodium + sodium + magnesium + citric + bisacodyl**
- Chemical class:
  - Macrogol (polyethylene glycol) — osmotic laxative
  - Electrolytes: potassium chloride, sodium chloride, sodium sulfate, magnesium carbonate/citrate components
  - Bisacodyl — stimulant laxative (diphenylmethane derivative)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration:
  - Oral administration of reconstituted solutions and oral administration of bisacodyl tablets.
  - Administration via nasogastric tube described for clinical use.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval: 24 June 2021 [Australian].
- Date of last revision/update of source PI: 29 May 2025 [Australian].
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.
- If additional jurisdictional registration dates are required: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Bowel emptying and cleansing by means of total gastrointestinal tract perfusion in preparation for gastrointestinal examination (for example colonoscopy, barium enema x‑ray examinations).
- Bowel preparation prior to intravenous pyelogram (IVP).
- Bowel preparation prior to colorectal surgery.
- Indication scope by region: Not specified as differing among jurisdictions in the available source. If jurisdictional differences exist, they are not specified in the available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General adult dosing
- The full treatment course as described in available sources comprises:
  - Oral bisacodyl tablets (three tablets of 5 mg each) taken as the initial dose on the day before the procedure (timed at 14:00 in the provided regimen).
  - One magnesium citrate sachet reconstituted to ~250 mL and ingested as a second dose (timed at ~17:00 in the provided regimen).
  - Three sachets (each reconstituted to 1 L) of macrogol + electrolytes solution: total 3 L required for the full course. Administration timing depends on single-day vs split-dose regimen (details below).

### Recommended regimens (examples from available source)
- Split-dose regimen (preferred when procedure scheduled in the afternoon; described as providing optimal bowel cleansing compared with single-day day-before regimen):
  - Day before procedure:
    - First dose (approx. 14:00): Bisacodyl tablets — take whole with water.
    - Second dose (approx. 17:00): Magnesium citrate solution — ingest slowly and completely.
    - Third dose (approx. 19:00): Two litres of macrogol + electrolytes solution — ingest 1–2 glasses every 15–20 minutes until 2 L consumed.
  - Day of procedure:
    - Fourth dose: Final 1 litre of macrogol + electrolytes solution — ingest approximately 2–5 hours prior to the procedure in the same manner as prior doses.
  - Patients are advised to stop taking any fluids 2 hours prior to the procedure.

- Single-day (day-before) regimen:
  - Day before procedure:
    - First dose (approx. 14:00): Bisacodyl tablets (three tablets).
    - Second dose (approx. 17:00): Magnesium citrate solution.
    - Third dose (approx. 19:00): Entire 3 litres of reconstituted macrogol + electrolytes solution — ingest 1–2 glasses every 15–20 minutes until completed.
  - Patients are advised to stop taking any fluids 2 hours prior to the procedure.

### Preparation and administration instructions
- Bisacodyl tablets: swallow whole; do not crush or chew.
- Magnesium citrate sachet: dissolve contents in ~250 mL warm (not boiling) water in a suitable container; effervescence and heat may occur; stir until effervescence ceases; refrigerate if desired; ingest within 24 hours after reconstitution.
- Macrogol + electrolytes sachet: dissolve each sachet in 1 L water at ambient temperature to produce a cloudy solution; refrigerate if desired; ingest each prepared solution within 24 hours of reconstitution.
- Rate of oral ingestion: for macrogol + electrolytes solution, ingest 1–2 glasses (approx. 250–500 mL) every 15–20 minutes until the designated volume is consumed. If nausea occurs, slow the rate of intake.
- Nasogastric administration: when oral route not possible, administer by nasogastric tube under careful observation to ensure hydration and tolerance. Infuse at a rate of 20–30 mL/min (equivalent to 1.2–1.8 L/hour) consistent with oral dosing volume and timing.
- Hydration and diet:
  - Follow a modified diet (e.g., low-fibre) up to ingestion of the preparation. Once preparation commences, only recommended clear fluids should be consumed.
  - During the day before the procedure, consume at least one glass (approx. 250 mL) of recommended clear fluids each hour in addition to the water used to take the kit to maintain hydration.
  - Recommended clear fluids include water, clear broths, pulp‑free fruit juices (non-red/purple), black tea or coffee (no milk), electrolyte drinks, clear carbonated beverages, plain jelly, sorbet, boiled sweets; avoid red or purple colouring.
- Contraceptives/other medications: see Section 8 (Interactions).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed warnings specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Seizures:
  - Cases of seizures have been reported in association with use of macrogol 3350 with electrolytes for bowel preparation, in patients with and without prior seizure history. Many reported cases were associated with electrolyte abnormalities such as severe hyponatraemia.
  - Use caution when prescribing to patients with a history of seizures, at increased risk of seizure, or at risk of electrolyte disturbance. If neurological symptoms occur, assess and correct fluid and electrolyte abnormalities promptly.
- Oesophageal rupture (Boerhaave syndrome):
  - Post‑marketing reports of oesophageal rupture associated with excessive vomiting after intake have been received, predominantly in elderly patients.
  - Advise patients to discontinue administration and seek immediate medical attention if they experience intractable vomiting followed by chest, neck, or abdominal pain, dysphagia, hematemesis, or dyspnoea.
- Volume status and electrolyte disturbances:
  - Administration is likely to cause transient hypovolaemia; ensure adequate fluid intake or replacement during preparation.
  - Monitor patients at increased risk of dehydration or electrolyte imbalance (for example elderly, debilitated patients).
- Cardiac, renal and electrolyte risk:
  - Use with caution in patients with congestive heart failure, pre‑existing electrolyte disturbances, renal impairment or diseases that predispose to fluid and electrolyte shifts.
  - Patients taking medications that can affect electrolyte balance (e.g., diuretics, calcium channel blockers) should be closely monitored.
- Gastrointestinal disease and structural issues:
  - Use with caution in patients with inflammatory bowel disease (including severe ulcerative colitis), those with a stoma, or other conditions where bowel cleansing may pose increased risk.
- Aspiration risk:
  - Administer under careful observation to patients with impaired gag reflexes, who are semi‑conscious, or who are prone to regurgitation/aspiration.
- Special populations:
  - Elderly: exercise caution due to higher risk of dehydration and electrolyte depletion; ensure adequate fluid intake and monitoring.
  - Paediatric use: safety and efficacy have not been established.
- Sleep disturbance:
  - Sleep disturbance may occur with either single-day or 2-day split-dose regimens; consider adjusting timing/regimen when clinically appropriate.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - No data available to guide use in hepatic impairment.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor fluid status and electrolyte concentrations (sodium, potassium, chloride, calcium) during and after administration in at‑risk patients.
- Monitor renal function in patients with known kidney disease.
- Monitor for signs of neurological change (seizures), severe vomiting, chest pain or respiratory compromise (possible oesophageal rupture).
- Monitor the effect of concomitant medications that may be under‑absorbed due to accelerated gastrointestinal transit (see Section 8).

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Concomitant oral medications with a sustained-release formulation, short half-life, or narrow therapeutic index may be flushed from the gastrointestinal tract and not absorbed if taken within 1 hour prior to commencement of the bowel preparation through 1 hour after completion of administration. Consider withholding such medications or adjusting timing.
- The low‑dose oral contraceptive pill may not be effective when taken during administration of the bowel preparation because absorption may be reduced due to accelerated transit.

### Pharmacodynamic and other interactions
- Macrogol content may reduce the effect of certain antibiotics (possible reduction in effect of bacitracin and benzylpenicillin reported).
- Concomitant use with medications that affect electrolyte balance or volume status (for example diuretics, calcium channel blockers, other agents that affect serum electrolytes) may increase the risk of clinically significant electrolyte disturbances or hypovolaemia. These combinations should prompt closer monitoring and consideration of temporary medication adjustment as clinically appropriate.
- Diabetic patients: the recommended liquid diet and fasting required for bowel preparation can alter blood glucose control. Diabetic medications may require adjustment during preparation and fasting periods; monitor glycaemic control and adjust therapy per clinical judgment.

### Management notes
- Advise patients regarding timing of critical medications (e.g., contraceptives, anti‑epileptics, narrow therapeutic index agents) relative to bowel preparation dosing.
- Monitor electrolytes and renal function when combining with diuretics, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or other renally-active medications.
- Consider alternative strategies or inpatient monitoring for high‑risk patients (heart failure, significant renal impairment, elderly frail patients).

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Bisacodyl:
  - A stimulant laxative acting primarily in the large intestine. It stimulates sensory nerve endings in the colonic mucosa and triggers parasympathetic reflexes that increase colonic peristalsis and promote evacuation. Effects onset typically within 6–8 hours when taken on an empty stomach; onset may be delayed to 10–12 hours with food.
- Magnesium citrate:
  - An osmotic laxative that increases intestinal osmotic pressure, drawing fluid into the small intestine and stimulating peristalsis; bowel movement usually occurs within 3–6 hours.
- Macrogol (polyethylene glycol) with electrolytes:
  - Osmotic laxative that induces a watery diarrhoea through osmotic action. The solution is formulated to be iso‑osmotic with normal intestinal contents to minimise net electrolyte loss while promoting fluid retention in the bowel to evacuate contents.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption:
  - Bisacodyl: minimal systemic absorption from enteric-coated tablets or suppositories.
  - Magnesium citrate: not significantly absorbed.
  - Macrogol 3350: not significantly absorbed.
- Metabolism:
  - Bisacodyl is converted in the intestine by intestinal and bacterial enzymes to the active desacetyl metabolite (bis(p‑hydroxyphenyl)pyridyl‑2‑methane).
- Excretion:
  - Bisacodyl: the small absorbed fraction is excreted in urine as glucuronide; the majority is excreted in faeces.
  - Macrogol and magnesium citrate are not significantly systemically absorbed; elimination is primarily via the gastrointestinal tract.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Macrogol + electrolytes produce a rapid osmotic effect in the bowel, often inducing watery diarrhoea within about 1 hour and removing bowel contents typically within 4 hours of commencement (times are approximate and based on reported clinical onset).
