# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information is derived from the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - Loading Dose: Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
    - Maximum Dose: The highest administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administration: Slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Adjustments: Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations.

### Monitoring
- **Baseline:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating therapy [US].
- **Ongoing:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L).

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (both oral and IV) has been associated with reports of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure activity, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **General (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of **oral** levocarnitine has not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in these patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD Trials)
Reported regardless of causality in clinical trials of dialysis patients [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%).

### Other Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis (less frequent) [US].
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis, seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** It is recommended that INR levels be monitored after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specifically letter-graded in source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied.
    - Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after exogenous administration.
    - Risks to the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit; consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). See Section 3 for details [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Dosing is adjusted based on pre-dialysis trough concentrations.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations and corrects the elevated acylcarnitine/carnitine ratio.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).
    - **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough concentrations reached steady state (54–180 µmol/L) within one week.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Comparisons of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 12 and 24 weeks demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels. Clinical outcome improvements in the ESRD population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].
