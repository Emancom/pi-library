# Editorial Product Information: Moclobemide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **moclobemide** for healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI in any specific jurisdiction. Prescribers should refer to their local regulatory guidance for legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Moclobemide (4-chloro-N-(2-morpholinoethyl)benzamide).
- **Chemical Class:** Reversible Inhibitor of Monoamine Oxidase Type A (RIMA).
- **Brand Names:** Amira 150, Amira 300 [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06AG02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 150 mg and 300 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depression:** Treatment of major depression [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):**
    - **Initial Dose:** 300 mg to 450 mg per day, administered in two divided doses [Australian].
    - **Maintenance Range:** 300 mg to 600 mg per day [Australian].
    - **Titration:** Do not increase the dose until after the first week of therapy, as bioavailability increases during this period [Australian].
    - **Duration:** Treatment should continue for at least 4–6 weeks to properly assess efficacy [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets should be taken after meals [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known hypersensitivity** to moclobemide [Australian].
- **Acute confusional states** [Australian].
- **Concomitant drug therapy:** Co-administration with the following is strictly contraindicated [Australian]:
    - Selegiline (L-deprenyl).
    - Bupropion.
    - Triptans (e.g., sumatriptan, rizatriptan).
    - Pethidine.
    - Tramadol.
    - Dextromethorphan.
    - Linezolid.
- **Clomipramine:** Co-administration should be avoided due to the risk of severe adverse reactions [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Clinical Worsening and Suicide Risk:** Antidepressants increase the risk of suicidality in children, adolescents, and young adults (18–24 years) during initial treatment. Patients of all ages should be monitored for clinical worsening, suicidal ideation, or unusual changes in behaviour, particularly during the first few months of therapy or at times of dose adjustments [Australian].
- **Bipolar Disorder/Mania:** Treatment may precipitate a manic or mixed episode in patients at risk for Bipolar Disorder. Screen for family history of bipolar disorder and suicide prior to initiation [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially life-threatening; risk is increased when combined with other serotonergic agents (e.g., SSRIs, TCAs, buprenorphine, St. John’s Wort). Symptoms include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular abnormalities [Australian].
- **Dietary Tyramine:** Extensive restrictions are generally unnecessary for those with normal dietary habits. However, hypertensive patients should avoid large quantities of tyramine-rich foods (e.g., ripe cheese, yeast extracts) [Australian].
- **Schizophrenic Symptoms:** Exacerbation of psychosis may occur in patients with schizophrenic or schizoaffective disorders; neuroleptic therapy should be maintained if possible [Australian].
- **Hypertensive Reactions:** Use with caution in patients with thyrotoxicosis or pheochromocytoma due to theoretical risk of hypertensive crisis [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Tablets contain lactose; not recommended for patients with galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Moclobemide is generally well-tolerated. Most adverse effects are transient and occur at the start of treatment.

| Organ System | Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Psychiatric** | Very Common | Sleep disorder |
| | Common | Agitation, anxiety, restlessness, irritability |
| | Uncommon | Suicidal ideation, confusional state |
| | Rare | Suicidal behaviour, delusion |
| **Nervous System** | Very Common | Dizziness, headache |
| | Common | Paraesthesia |
| | Uncommon | Dysgeusia |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Very Common | Dry mouth, nausea |
| | Common | Diarrhoea, constipation, vomiting |
| **Vascular** | Common | Hypotension |
| | Uncommon | Flushing |
| **Skin** | Common | Rash |
| | Uncommon | Oedema, pruritus, urticaria |
| **Investigations** | Rare | Increased hepatic enzymes, Serotonin syndrome (with co-medication) |

*Serious Adverse Effects:* Hyponatraemia (rare), Serotonin syndrome (rare), and suicidal behaviour [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Cimetidine:** Reduces moclobemide clearance. If initiated in patients on cimetidine, use the lowest moclobemide dose. If cimetidine is added later, reduce moclobemide dose by 50–66% [Australian].
    - **CYP2C19 Inhibitors (e.g., Omeprazole):** Moclobemide inhibits CYP2C19; exposure to drugs metabolised by this enzyme (like omeprazole) may double [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Serotonergic Agents (SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs):** High risk of Serotonin Syndrome. A washout period is required when switching (e.g., 14 days after irreversible MAOIs) [Australian].
    - **Sympathomimetic Amines:** Slight potentiation of pressor effects. Nasal decongestants are unlikely to cause relevant blood pressure increases at standard doses, but caution is advised [Australian].
    - **Triptans:** Contraindicated due to increased plasma concentrations. If zolmitriptan is essential, limit dose to 5 mg and avoid high-dose moclobemide (>300 mg/day) [Australian].
    - **Opiates:** Moclobemide potentiates opioids; dose adjustments for morphine, fentanyl, or codeine may be necessary [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** No additional impairment of performance observed at therapeutic doses [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3.** Animal studies show no teratogenic activity, but data in humans is lacking. Benefit must clearly outweigh risks to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts (~1/30th of the maternal dose) are excreted in breast milk. Weigh benefits of therapy against possible risks to the infant [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in animal (rat) studies at doses slightly above the maximum human dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dosage adjustments are required [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is typically required, but moclobemide should be used with caution as multiple-dose data is limited [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In patients with severely impaired hepatic metabolism, the daily dose should be reduced to one-half or one-third of the standard dose [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reversible inhibition of monoamine oxidase, primarily type A (RIMA). This reduces the metabolism of serotonin, noradrenaline, and dopamine, increasing their extracellular concentrations [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Inhibition of MAO-A is approximately 80% and is short-lasting (~24 hours). It does not impair alertness or reaction time [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Completely absorbed; bioavailability increases from ~60% (single dose) to >80% (multiple doses) due to first-pass saturation.
    - **Distribution:** Apparent volume of distribution is ~1.2 L/kg; protein binding is ~50%.
    - **Metabolism:** Oxidative reactions via CYP2C19 and CYP2D6.
    - **Elimination:** Rapidly eliminated; <1% excreted unchanged renally. Average half-life is ~2 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Moclobemide vs. Placebo:** Clinical efficacy demonstrated in trials (n=475) using 200–600 mg/day. HAMD score improvement of ≥50% occurred in 58% of moclobemide patients vs. 32% for placebo [Australian].
- **Moclobemide vs. TCAs:** Studies in 1,070 patients showed similar efficacy between moclobemide and tricyclic antidepressants [Australian].
- **Moclobemide vs. Irreversible MAOIs:** Similar efficacy to tranylcypromine, but moclobemide was better tolerated with fewer premature terminations [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
- **Handling:** Unused medicine should be returned to a pharmacy for disposal [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
