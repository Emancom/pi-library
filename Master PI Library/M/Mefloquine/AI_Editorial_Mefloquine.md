# Editorial Product Information: Mefloquine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the available product information for healthcare professionals. 
- **Prescribing Note:** This is an editorial overview and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a clinician's specific jurisdiction. Information provided reflects the source data from the Australian (TGA) Lariam PI.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mefloquine hydrochloride (a quinoline methanol derivative).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Lariam** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 250 mg tablets (equivalent to 274.09 mg mefloquine hydrochloride). The tablets are cross-scored with break bars on both faces and specific quadrant markings ("LA", "RI", "AM", "CP") [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmaco (Australia) Ltd under license from CHEPLAPHARM Arzneimittel GmbH [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Treatment of Malaria:** Management of acute malaria caused by *Plasmodium falciparum* strains resistant to standard antimalarial agents [Australian].
- **Malaria Chemoprophylaxis:** Prevention of malaria for high-risk travellers (up to 3 months duration) visiting rural areas between dusk and dawn in regions with documented *P. falciparum* resistance to chloroquine or antifolate combinations (e.g., sulfadoxine/pyrimethamine) [Australian].
- **Alternative Prophylaxis:** Indicated for high-risk travellers with hypersensitivity to sulfonamides or sulfones in areas with high-level chloroquine-resistant *P. falciparum* [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
Tablets must be swallowed whole with a significant volume of liquid.

### Malaria Treatment (Adults and Children > 45 kg)
- **Total Dose:** 1,250 mg administered in split doses.
- **Loading Dose:** 750 mg (3 tablets).
- **Follow-up Dose:** 500 mg (2 tablets) administered 6 to 8 hours after the initial dose [Australian].
- **Vomiting Management:** If the patient vomits within 30 minutes of a dose, a full repeat dose is required. If vomiting occurs between 30 and 60 minutes after a dose, an additional half-dose should be administered [Australian].

### Malaria Chemoprophylaxis
- **Dosing:** 250 mg (1 tablet) once weekly.
- **Schedule:** Therapy should begin one week prior to entering the endemic area. Weekly doses continue during the stay and for two weeks after departing the area [Australian].
- **Duration:** Current guidance limits use to 3 months [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to mefloquine, related compounds (e.g., quinine, quinidine), or any product excipients [Australian].
- **Psychiatric History (Prophylactic Use Only):** Mefloquine must not be prescribed for prophylaxis in patients with a history of active or recent depression, generalised anxiety disorder, psychosis, schizophrenia, suicidal ideation, or other major psychiatric disorders [Australian].
- **Neurological History:** History of convulsions (prophylactic use contraindicated) [Australian].
- **Organ Impairment:** Severe hepatic impairment or renal insufficiency [Australian].
- **Specific Complications:** History of Blackwater fever (a malaria-related complication involving massive haemolysis and haemoglobinuria) [Australian].
- **Co-administration:** Contraindicated with halofantrine due to fatal QTc prolongation risks [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiac Effects:** Structurally related to quinine; caution is advised in cardiac disease. While clinical trials showed no significant cardiovascular action, animal studies indicate potential myocardial suppressant effects. ECG alterations (bradycardia, AV-block, QTc prolongation) have been reported [Australian].
- **Neuropsychiatric Effects:** May cause severe symptoms including anxiety, paranoia, depression, hallucinations, and psychosis. These may be prodromal to serious events and require immediate discontinuation. Symptoms can persist for months after the drug is stopped due to the long half-life [Australian].
- **Ocular Effects:** Retinal disorders and optic neuropathy have been reported. Referral to an ophthalmologist is required for any visual disturbances [Australian].
- **Drug Resistance:** Geographical resistance patterns for *P. falciparum* vary; resistance is most prevalent in Southeast Asia [Australian].
- **Halofantrine Risk:** Must not be used during mefloquine therapy or within 15 weeks of the last dose due to the risk of fatal QTc prolongation [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects may be indistinguishable from malaria symptoms during acute treatment. 

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Abnormal dreams, insomnia [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Anxiety, depression, dizziness, headache, visual impairment, vertigo, nausea, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, vomiting, pruritus [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Agitation, restlessness, panic attacks, confusion, hallucinations, aggression, somnolence, syncope, convulsions, memory impairment, peripheral neuropathy (paraesthesia, tremor, ataxia), vestibular disorders (tinnitus, hearing impairment) [Australian]. |
| **Not Known** | Agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia, anaphylaxis, tachycardia, palpitations, bradycardia, AV block, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, hepatic failure, suicidal ideation [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **QTc Prolonging Agents:** Halofantrine is strictly contraindicated. Caution and monitoring are required with beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, antihistamines, tricyclic antidepressants, and phenothiazines [Australian].
- **Quinine/Quinidine/Chloroquine:** Increased risk of ECG abnormalities and convulsions [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., Ketoconazole):** Increase mefloquine plasma levels and QTc prolongation risk [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., Rifampicin):** Decrease mefloquine plasma levels and half-life [Australian].
- **Anticonvulsants:** Mefloquine may lower plasma levels of valproic acid, carbamazepine, or phenytoin, potentially reducing seizure control [Australian].
- **Vaccines:** May attenuate oral live typhoid vaccines; vaccination should be completed at least 3 days before the first mefloquine dose [Australian].
- **P-glycoprotein:** Mefloquine is a P-gp inhibitor; it has been shown to significantly decrease ritonavir concentrations [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Mefloquine crosses the placenta. It is teratogenic in mice/rats and embryotoxic in rabbits at high doses. Clinical experience has not confirmed these effects in humans. It should only be used in the first trimester if the benefit justifies the risk [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception during therapy and for 3 months after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in breast milk in small amounts. While evidence suggests it is likely safe for the infant, current clinical guidelines should be consulted [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children under the age of 14 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in provided sources [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No pharmacokinetic studies available; use is contraindicated due to lack of clinical experience [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Mefloquine is extensively metabolised by the liver. Impairment may prolong elimination and increase the risk of adverse reactions. Contraindicated in severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Destroys asexual intraerythrocytic forms of human malarial parasites (*P. falciparum, P. vivax, P. malariae, P. ovale*). It forms complexes with haemin but does not intercalate with DNA [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability >85%; food increases bioavailability by approximately 40%. Peak plasma levels reached in 12–23 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Extensive tissue distribution; protein binding is 98.2% [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver, primarily by CYP3A4. The main metabolite (2,8-bis-trifluoromethyl-4-quinoline carboxylic acid) is inactive [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Average half-life is 21 days in Caucasians. Excreted primarily via bile and faeces [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Comparative Study:** A double-blind, randomised study compared mefloquine (n=483) to atovaquone-proguanil (n=493) for prophylaxis. 
- **Efficacy:** Both treatments showed 100% efficacy in preventing confirmed malaria in non-immune travellers.
- **Safety:** Mefloquine was associated with a significantly higher rate of neuropsychiatric adverse events (28.8%) compared to atovaquone-proguanil (14%). Common events included strange dreams (13.7%) and insomnia (13.5%) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Protection:** Store in the original container to protect from moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
