# Editorial Product Information: Mesna

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of mesna for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and administration guidelines into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mesna (sodium 2-mercapto-ethane sulphonate).
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic sulphydryl compound (thiol).
- **Brand Names:** **Uromitexan** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** V03AF01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile preservative-free solution for injection: 100 mg/mL.
    - Available in 400 mg (4 mL) and 1 g (10 mL) single-dose glass ampoules [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reduction and prevention of urinary tract toxicity (haemorrhagic cystitis):** Specifically indicated for patients receiving oxazaphosphorine therapy (e.g., ifosfamide or cyclophosphamide) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Sufficient mesna must be administered to protect the urothelium. The dose is calculated based on the dose of the oxazaphosphorine agent.
- **Standard IV Regimen:** Mesna is typically administered at 20% of the oxazaphosphorine dose at three specific time points:
    1. Time 0 (concurrent with the cytotoxic agent).
    2. 4 hours post-administration.
    3. 8 hours post-administration.
- **Total Dose:** The total daily dose of mesna is 60% of the total oxazaphosphorine dose [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Administer via IV injection over 15–30 minutes.
    - Round the calculated dose up to the nearest whole ampoule.
    - Inspect visually for particulates or discolouration before use.
- **Dilution:** Mesna may be diluted to a final concentration of 1.5 to 3 mg/mL using:
    - 5% Glucose.
    - 0.9% Sodium Chloride.
    - Lactated Ringer’s Injection.
- **Stability:** Once diluted, infusion should ideally commence as soon as possible due to the lack of preservatives. Infusions should start within 6–8 hours and must be completed within 24 hours of preparation [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to mesna or any component of the formulation.
- Known hypersensitivity to other thiol compounds [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Urothelial Limitation:** Mesna protects only against urothelial toxicity; it does not prevent renal toxicity or other systemic toxicities associated with oxazaphosphorines.
- **Severe Allergic Reactions:** Anaphylactic and systemic hypersensitivity reactions have been reported. These occur more frequently in patients with underlying autoimmune diseases. Emergency medical equipment must be available during administration [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Daily morning urine specimens should be examined for haematuria and proteinuria prior to oxazaphosphorine therapy.
    - Maintain a urinary output of at least 100 mL/hr.
- **Laboratory Test Interferences:** 
    - **Urinary Ketones:** May produce false-positive results in nitroprusside sodium-based tests (a red-violet colour develops).
    - **Urinary Erythrocytes:** May cause false-positive or false-negative dipstick results; microscopy is recommended for confirmation.
    - **Ascorbic Acid:** May cause false-positive results in Tillman’s reagent-based tests.
    - **Serum CPK:** May interfere with thiol-dependent enzymatic creatine phosphokinase tests, leading to lower-than-actual values [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
As mesna is used in combination with highly toxic chemotherapeutic agents, it is often difficult to distinguish its specific adverse effects.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects [Australian] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Bad taste in mouth (100% in some bolus studies), soft stools, nausea, diarrhoea, headache, fatigue, limb pain. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Pharyngitis, anorexia, dizziness, somnolence, flushing, coughing, vomiting, constipation, abdominal pain, flatulence, arthralgia, back pain, fever, rigors, influenza-like symptoms. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Injection site reactions. |
| **Very Rare (<0.1%)** | Thrombocytopenia, anaphylactoid reactions, conjunctivitis, ST-segment elevation, tachycardia, hypotension/hypertension, tachypnoea, severe skin/mucosal reactions (SJS, TEN, Lyell Syndrome), myalgia. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), anaphylaxis, and drug rash with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antitumour Efficacy:** Data suggests mesna does not diminish the antitumour activity of concomitantly administered cytotoxic agents.
- **Incompatibilities:** 
    - **In vitro Incompatibility:** Mesna is incompatible with cisplatin, carboplatin, and nitrogen mustard.
    - **Inactivation:** Mixing mesna with epirubicin leads to the inactivation of epirubicin and must be avoided [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** Animal studies (rats/rabbits) have shown no foetal harm. However, use in pregnant women should only occur if the benefits clearly outweigh potential risks. It is unknown if mesna can cause foetal harm or affect reproductive capacity [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if mesna or its metabolite dimesna are excreted in human milk. A decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the mother's clinical need [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 16 years of age have not been established in formal clinical trials; however, use is documented in medical literature [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Dose selection should be cautious, typically starting at the lower end of the range, reflecting the higher frequency of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction. The ratio of mesna to oxazaphosphorine should remain consistent [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically detailed in available sources, though caution is advised in the elderly regarding organ function [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Mesna is a regional detoxifying agent. It reacts chemically with the urotoxic metabolites of oxazaphosphorines, specifically acrolein and 4-hydroxy-metabolites, in the urine. This forms stable, non-toxic thioethers, thereby preventing haemorrhagic cystitis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Metabolism:** Following IV administration, mesna is rapidly oxidised in the blood to its inactive disulphide metabolite, **dimesna**. 
    - **Distribution:** Dimesna remains in the intravascular space and is transported to the kidneys.
    - **Elimination/Activation:** In the renal tubular epithelium, dimesna is reduced back to the active thiol form (mesna) by glutathione reductase. This active form is then excreted in the urine to exert its protective effect [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Phase 1 Studies:** Bolus doses (0.8–1.6 g/m²) showed high incidences of gastrointestinal symptoms and taste disturbances.
- **Controlled Studies:** Clinical efficacy was established in comparison with standard prophylaxis in patients receiving ifosfamide [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Ampoules:** Store below 30°C.
- **Diluted Solution:** Recommended to use within 6 to 8 hours to ensure microbial safety [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Medicine) [Australian].
