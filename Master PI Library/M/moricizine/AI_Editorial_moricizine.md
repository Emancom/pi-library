# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information has been harmonised from official Product Information (PI) sourced from the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a concise, structured format.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule involved in mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or infusion.
    - Acute Management: A loading dose is often given in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses should range from 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose Adjustments: Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial.

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. 
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue treatment immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].

### General Precautions
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulations):** Safety of oral **levocarnitine** has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Dialysis Patients:** While **levocarnitine** increases carnitine levels in ESRD, the specific effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, arrhythmias) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema.
- **Digestive:** Transient nausea/vomiting, gastritis, and body odor (less frequent) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concurrently with **levocarnitine**. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels at therapy initiation and after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C]. Reproductive studies in rats/rabbits (3.8x human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Weigh the benefits of maternal treatment against the potential risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No long-term animal studies exist to evaluate carcinogenic potential. Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, etc.) were negative [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 (Dosage) for metabolic disorder dosing. In premature infants/newborns, secondary deficiency is defined as plasma levels below age-related normals [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in source documents; dosing generally follows adult protocols based on body weight [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 5. **Levocarnitine** is removed by hemodialysis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Levocarnitine** is required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic/fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine over 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study (ESRD):** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine levels increased from ~20 µmol/L (baseline) to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Concentrations remained stable or increased over the study duration [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** **Levocarnitine** is compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
