# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic; Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The standard dose is 50 mg/kg. It may be administered as a slow bolus (over 2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US]. 
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered in severe cases, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and never less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Hemodialysis Administration:** The dose should be administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Daily doses may be adjusted based on clinical response, with the highest reported dose being 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Monitoring:** Initiation of therapy may be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma concentrations. If concentrations are below 40–50 µmol/L, therapy is indicated. Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) can be made as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These can occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately and appropriate treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **TMA/TMAO Accumulation:** While not specified for the injection, high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

- **Other Reactions:** Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed. Less frequent reactions include body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures are the most significant serious risks identified [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concurrently with warfarin. It is recommended that INR levels be monitored after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No other specific drug-drug interactions are detailed in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Levocarnitine should be used in pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels after administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is approved; see "Dosage and Administration" for metabolic disorders.
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source; general caution regarding organ function decline is standard.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. For the *injection*, dose adjustments are required post-dialysis for ESRD patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While levocarnitine levels were successfully increased, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Once mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s, the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation lacks preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources [US].
