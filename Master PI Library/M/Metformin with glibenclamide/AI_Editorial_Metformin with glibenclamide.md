# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (Food and Drug Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine Injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data from the provided source.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (US) documentation was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for intravenous injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Baxter Healthcare Corporation (Deerfield, IL, USA) [US].
    - Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd (Ahmedabad, India) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with an inborn error of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### Dosing in ESRD (Hemodialysis)
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels.
- Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of IV administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Oral Formulation in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD or severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring:** Visual inspection for particulate matter and discoloration is required before administration [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (ESRD Patients on Dialysis)
In clinical trials, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% regardless of causality [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), chest pain (6–15%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Cough increase (10–18%), pharyngitis (15–27%), rhinitis (6–11%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea, vomiting, body odor, and gastritis [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified (Narrative description provided).
    - Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits to the mother against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** IV levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Note precautions regarding oral formulations in this population (see Section 5) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), ~76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, levocarnitine clears accumulated toxic acylCoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis showed mean pre-dialysis trough levels increased from ~20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients (N≈30 per group) demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion as the formulation is preservative-free [US].
- **Stability (Admixtures):** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
