# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesized from official Product Information (PI) sourced from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements in their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Generic, Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
    - **Administration:** Delivered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours). Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow 2-to-3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustment:** Dose reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** It is recommended to obtain a baseline level before starting parenteral therapy.
- **Ongoing Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical care [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed following levocarnitine administration [US].
- **Uremic Metabolite Accumulation:** The safety of high-dose **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD has not been established, as it may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) usually excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and Hypersensitivity:** Bronchospasm and laryngeal edema [US].
- **Neurological:** Seizures [US].

### Common Adverse Effects (ESRD Clinical Trial Data)
In placebo-controlled trials of ESRD patients, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Chest pain (12%).
- **General/Infection:** Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Fever (7%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Body Odor:** Transient body odor has been observed with levocarnitine therapy [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. While studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine milk concentrations after administration, the risks to the human infant must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg). Specific safety/efficacy profiles for children beyond those described in the general population are not detailed [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for use in ESRD patients undergoing dialysis. Use of the **intravenous** form is preferred in this population to avoid metabolite accumulation associated with high-dose oral administration [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are quickly excreted. This clears toxic acyl-CoA compounds that cause life-threatening acidosis [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While levocarnitine concentrations increased, the specific effect on clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
