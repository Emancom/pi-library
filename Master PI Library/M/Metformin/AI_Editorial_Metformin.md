# Editorial Product Information: Metformin Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview of **metformin** hydrochloride (modified-release) for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the available regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local regulatory documentation before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Metformin hydrochloride, a biguanide oral anti-diabetic agent.
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Metformin XR [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Modified-release tablets: 500 mg and 1000 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus in adults [Australian].
- Particularly indicated for overweight patients when dietary management and exercise alone fail to achieve adequate glycaemic control [Australian].
- May be used as monotherapy or in combination with other oral hypoglycaemic agents or insulin [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Metformin** modified-release tablets must be taken once daily with the evening meal [Australian].
- Tablets should be swallowed whole and not divided, crushed, or chewed [Australian].
- **Initiation:** The usual starting dose is 500 mg once daily [Australian].
- **Titration:** Dose should be adjusted after 10 to 15 days based on blood glucose measurements. Increases should occur in 500 mg increments every 10 to 15 days [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** 2000 mg once daily with the evening meal [Australian].

### Specific Administration Instructions
- If glycaemic control is not achieved at 2000 mg of **metformin** modified-release, a switch to immediate-release (IR) tablets may be considered, up to a maximum IR dose of 3000 mg daily [Australian].
- When switching from IR to modified-release, the starting dose should be equivalent to the previous daily IR dose [Australian].
- For patients on IR doses exceeding 2000 mg daily, switching to the modified-release formulation is not recommended [Australian].

### Combination Therapy
- **With Insulin:** Initiate with 500 mg **metformin** once daily, while insulin dosage is adjusted based on glucose readings [Australian].
- **Transfer from other agents:** Discontinue the previous antidiabetic agent and initiate **metformin** at 500 mg daily with the evening meal [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **metformin** or any excipients [Australian].
- Any form of metabolic acidosis (e.g., lactic acidosis, diabetic ketoacidosis) [Australian].
- Diabetic pre-coma [Australian].
- Renal failure or renal dysfunction (creatinine clearance < 60 mL/min) [Australian].
- Acute conditions that may alter renal function (dehydration, severe infection, shock) [Australian].
- Intravascular administration of iodinated contrast agents [Australian].
- Acute or chronic diseases causing tissue hypoxia (cardiac or respiratory failure, recent myocardial infarction, pulmonary embolism, sepsis, gangrene, pancreatitis) [Australian].
- Major surgery [Australian].
- Severe hepatic insufficiency, acute alcohol intoxication, or alcoholism [Australian].
- Lactation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Lactic Acidosis
**Lactic acidosis is a rare but life-threatening metabolic complication that can occur due to metformin accumulation. The primary risk factor is renal impairment. Other risk factors include advanced age, high doses (≥ 2 g/day), poorly controlled diabetes, ketosis, fasting, excessive alcohol intake, and conditions associated with hypoxia [Australian].**

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Diagnosis of Lactic Acidosis:** Characterised by acidotic dyspnea, abdominal pain, muscle cramps, asthenia, and hypothermia, potentially followed by coma. Laboratory findings include decreased pH, plasma lactate > 5 mmol/L, and an increased anion gap. **Metformin** must be discontinued immediately if acidosis is suspected [Australian].
- **Hypothyroidism:** **Metformin** may reduce thyrotropin (TSH) levels in hypothyroid patients. Regular monitoring of TSH is recommended [Australian].
- **Mitochondrial Diseases:** Not recommended for patients with MELAS (Mitochondrial Encephalopathy, Lactic Acidosis, and Stroke-like episodes) or MIDD (Maternal Inherited Diabetes and Deafness) due to the risk of triggering lactic acidosis [Australian].
- **Surgery:** Discontinue **metformin** 48 hours before elective surgery; restart no earlier than 48 hours post-surgery once renal function is confirmed as normal [Australian].
- **Iodinated Contrast:** Discontinue **metformin** 48 hours before (if renal function is impaired) or at the time of the test (if renal function is normal). Restart after 48 hours if renal function remains stable [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Assess renal function (CrCl or serum creatinine) before initiation and at least annually thereafter (2–4 times annually in elderly or those with borderline renal function) [Australian].
- Annual monitoring of Vitamin B12 levels is recommended [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event reporting for modified-release **metformin** is similar in nature and severity to immediate-release formulations.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Gastrointestinal disorders (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, loss of appetite) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Taste disturbance; Vitamin B12 deficiency (consider in megaloblastic anaemia) |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Lactic acidosis; Skin reactions (erythema, pruritus, urticaria) |
| **Not Known** | Liver function test abnormalities or hepatitis (resolving upon discontinuation); TSH reduction in hypothyroid patients |

- *Note:* Gastrointestinal effects occur most frequently during initiation and usually resolve spontaneously. Slow dose titration improves tolerability [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Increases the risk of lactic acidosis during acute intoxication, especially if fasting or hepatic insufficiency is present [Australian].
- **Hypoglycaemic Potential:** Caution is required when combined with insulin, sulfonylureas, or glinides; ACE inhibitors and beta-blockers may also decrease blood glucose levels [Australian].
- **Hyperglycaemic Potential:** Glucocorticoids, beta-2 agonists, diuretics, and danazol may increase blood glucose; more frequent monitoring is required [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Organic Cation Transporters (OCT):** **Metformin** is a substrate for OCT1 and OCT2.
    - Inhibitors of OCT1 (e.g., verapamil) may reduce efficacy [Australian].
    - Inducers of OCT1 (e.g., rifampicin) may increase absorption and efficacy [Australian].
    - Inhibitors of OCT2 (e.g., cimetidine, dolutegravir, crizotinib, olaparib, daclatasvir) may decrease renal elimination and increase plasma concentration [Australian].
- **Vitamin K Antagonists:** **Metformin** increases the elimination rate of these anticoagulants; monitor prothrombin time closely [Australian].
- **Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors:** (e.g., topiramate, zonisamide) may increase the risk of lactic acidosis [Australian].
- **NSAIDs and Diuretics:** May increase the risk of lactic acidosis by adversely affecting renal function [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category C. Hyperglycaemia during pregnancy is associated with increased risks of congenital malformations and pregnancy loss. Data from registries and studies indicate no increased risk of congenital abnormalities with **metformin**. It may be used if clinically needed as an alternative or addition to insulin [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. **Metformin** is excreted into the milk of lactating rats; human data are unavailable. A decision should be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or discontinue the drug [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on the fertility of male or female rats at doses up to twice the maximum recommended human daily dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for use in children due to a lack of available data [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of renal impairment; dose must be adjusted based on renal function with regular monitoring [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with CrCl < 60 mL/min [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic insufficiency due to increased risk of lactic acidosis [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A biguanide that lowers basal and postprandial plasma glucose without stimulating insulin secretion. It reduces hepatic glucose production, facilitates peripheral glucose uptake by increasing insulin action, and alters glucose turnover in the gut [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Activates adenosine monophosphate-protein-kinase (AMPK) and increases GLUT transporter capacity. It is associated with stable body weight or modest weight loss and may improve lipid profiles [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Significantly delayed in modified-release compared to IR. Tmax is approximately 7 hours (IR Tmax is 2.5 hours). Food does not alter absorption composition but the AUC is decreased by 30-49% in fasting conditions for the modified-release form [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Negligible plasma protein binding; partitions into erythrocytes. Vd ranges from 63 to 276 L [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Excreted unchanged in the urine; no metabolites identified in humans [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Renal clearance is > 400 mL/min via glomerular filtration and tubular secretion. Elimination half-life is approximately 6.5 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dose-ranging studies:** 16-week and 12-week placebo-controlled trials showed significant, dose-related reductions in HbA1c and fasting plasma glucose. Benefits were maintained in 52-week open-label extensions [Australian].
- **Switching studies:** Patients sub-optimally controlled on IR **metformin** maintained glycaemic indices when switched to equivalent daily doses of the modified-release formulation [Australian].
- **UKPDS:** Established the long-term benefit of intensive blood glucose control with IR **metformin** in overweight patients, showing significant reductions in diabetes-related complications, mortality, and myocardial infarction [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian].
- Keep in the original container [Australian].
- Tablet shells may occasionally be present in the faeces; patients should be advised that this is normal [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
