# Editorial Product Information: Morphine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Note on Jurisdictional Scope:** At the time of this editorial's preparation, only the Australian PI was available for review. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) is currently noted as "Not specified in available sources." 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinical reference and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Morphine sulfate pentahydrate; a phenanthrene-derivative opiate agonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** Anamorph [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - **Immediate-release tablets:** 30 mg morphine sulfate pentahydrate [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Pain Management:** Indicated for the short-term relief of severe pain in cases where alternative treatments (non-opioid analgesics) are insufficient, poorly tolerated, or contraindicated [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** Usually 15 mg to 30 mg (half to one 30 mg tablet) initially. Doses may be repeated every 4 to 6 hours [Australian].
- **Chronic Carcinoma Pain:** Dosages must be individualised and titrated to achieve adequate analgesia [Australian].
- **Geriatric Dosing:** Due to prolonged plasma half-life in elderly patients, lower doses or extended dosing intervals are frequently required [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets are scored to allow for 15 mg dosing. 
- **Tapering/Cessation:** Do not discontinue abruptly in physically dependent patients. Tapering should be individualised; a general guideline is a reduction of 10% to 25% every 2 to 4 weeks [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
Morphine is contraindicated in the following clinical scenarios [Australian]:
- Hypersensitivity to morphine or tablet excipients.
- **Respiratory Compromise:** Severe or acute respiratory disease/depression, or depleted respiratory reserve (e.g., severe emphysema, chronic bronchitis, kyphoscoliosis).
- **Neurological/Intracranial Issues:** Raised intracranial or cerebrospinal pressure, head injury, or brain tumour.
- **Cardiac Conditions:** Cardiac arrhythmias or heart failure secondary to chronic pulmonary disease.
- **CNS and Metabolic:** Severe CNS depression, acute alcoholism, delirium tremens, and incipient hepatic encephalopathy.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Biliary tract surgery (immediate post-op), gastrointestinal obstruction, or obstructive bowel disorders.
- **Convulsive States:** Status epilepticus, tetanus, or strychnine poisoning.
- **Concomitant Medication:** Concurrent use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 14 days of discontinuing MAOIs.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [Australian]:**
  - **Limitations of Use:** Reserve for severe pain when other options are inadequate.
  - **Hazardous/Harmful Use:** Potential for abuse, misuse, and addiction, leading to overdose and death.
  - **Life-threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur; monitor closely during initiation and dose escalation.
  - **Concomitant CNS Depressants:** Use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or alcohol may result in profound sedation, coma, and death.
- **Hyperalgesia:** Especially at high doses, patients may experience an unexplained increase in pain. This requires dose reduction rather than escalation [Australian].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with long-term opioid use; monitor for nausea, fatigue, and hypotension [Australian].
- **Endocrine Effects:** May cause androgen deficiency (low libido, erectile dysfunction, infertility) and increased prolactin [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal Toxicity:** Long-term use is associated with esophageal dysfunction and may obscure acute abdominal conditions [Australian].
- **Sleep-Related Breathing Disorders:** Can cause or worsen central sleep apnea (CSA) and hypoxemia in a dose-dependent manner [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data [Australian]
| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Sedation, nausea, vomiting, constipation, sweating (hyperhidrosis). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, headache, somnolence, confusion, insomnia, abdominal pain, anorexia, dry mouth, pruritus, fatigue. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypotension, facial flushing, vertigo, visual impairment, dyspepsia, ileus, peripheral oedema, convulsions, syncope, hallucinations, agitation. |
| **Not Known** | Bradycardia, palpitations, adrenal insufficiency, miosis, anaphylaxis, biliary spasm, urinary retention, respiratory depression, AGEP. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory arrest, circulatory collapse, cardiac arrest, and toxic leukoencephalopathy (observed in overdose) [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Acute generalised exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP) has been reported, usually within the first 10 days of treatment [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Enhanced risk of respiratory depression and death with alcohol, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, tricyclic antidepressants, and antipsychotics [Australian].
- **MAOIs:** Potential for fatal reactions; contraindicated within 14 days of MAOI use [Australian].
- **Cimetidine:** Reported lethal interaction causing apnea, reduced respiratory rate, and seizures [Australian].
- **Propranolol:** Potentially lethal; propranolol increases the acute CNS toxicity of morphine [Australian].
- **Acidifying/Alkalising Agents:** Morphine effects are antagonised by acidifying agents and potentiated by alkalising agents. Antacids may cause rapid release of morphine; separate dosing by at least 2 hours [Australian].
- **Ritonavir/Rifampicin:** May reduce plasma morphine concentrations via metabolic induction [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** Potentiate risk of urinary retention and severe constipation [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category C [Australian]:** Morphine crosses the placenta. Prolonged use may lead to **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome**, which can be life-threatening.
  - Use during labour may cause neonatal respiratory depression.
- **Breastfeeding:** Morphine is excreted in breast milk; use is not recommended for nursing mothers [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Long-term use may impair reproductive function and reduce fertility based on animal data [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under three years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity to adverse effects; lower initial doses and careful titration are mandatory [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Morphine-6-glucuronide (M6G) may accumulate, increasing the risk of CNS and respiratory depression. Use with caution and reduced dosages [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Prolonged duration and cumulative effects possible; use with caution and reduced dosages [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Opioid agonist that binds primarily to mu (µ) opioid receptors in the CNS, altering pain perception and emotional response to pain [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces analgesia, drowsiness, cough suppression (medullary effect), and respiratory depression (reduced CO2 responsiveness). Causes miosis and decreases GI motility [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** Well absorbed but low oral bioavailability due to significant first-pass metabolism in the liver and gut [Australian].
  - **Metabolism:** Conjugated with glucuronic acid to produce morphine-3-glucuronide (M3G) and morphine-6-glucuronide (M6G). M6G is pharmacologically active and contributes to analgesia [Australian].
  - **Elimination:** Approximately 90% excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean plasma half-life is 1.7 hours [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C in a dry place. Protect from light (morphine darkens upon prolonged exposure) [Australian].
- **Disposal:** Unused medicine should be returned to a pharmacy for proper disposal [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 8 (Controlled Drug) [Australian].
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine.
