# Editorial Product Information: Metaraminol

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of metaraminol product information, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current official PI from their local regulatory authority before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Metaraminol (as tartrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Sympathomimetic amine; vasopressor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Aramine** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection solution: 10 mg/mL metaraminol (as tartrate) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (injection or infusion).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Phebra Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypotension associated with spinal anaesthesia:** For the prevention and treatment of acute hypotensive states [Australian-approved].
- **Adjunctive treatment of hypotension:** Due to haemorrhage, drug reactions, surgical complications, and shock resulting from brain damage (trauma or tumour) [Australian-approved].
- **Adjunctive treatment of shock:** May be used in cardiogenic shock or septicaemia [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Metaraminol is intended for intravenous use only. Because the maximum clinical effect is not immediate, at least **10 minutes** must elapse before increasing the dose [Australian].

### Intravenous Infusion (Adjunctive Treatment)
- **Standard Dose:** 15 mg to 100 mg added to 500 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Glucose.
- **Titration:** Adjust the infusion rate to maintain blood pressure at the desired level.
- **High Concentration:** Concentrations of 150 mg to 500 mg per 500 mL have been utilised in clinical practice [Australian].
- **Fluid Restriction:** If a smaller volume is necessary, the required dose may be added to less than 500 mL of diluent.

### Direct Intravenous Injection
- **Indication:** Severe shock where immediate action is imperative to save life.
- **Suggested Dose:** 0.5 mg to 5 mg, followed by an infusion of 15 mg to 100 mg [Australian].
- **Strength Recommendation:** A lower strength of 0.5 mg/mL is recommended for direct injection (achieved by diluting 1 mL of the 10 mg/mL solution to 20 mL with 0.9% Sodium Chloride) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Compatibility:** Physically and chemically compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride, 5% Glucose, Ringer's Injection, and Lactated Ringer's Injection [Australian].
- **Single Use:** Contains no antimicrobial preservatives. Use immediately after preparation; if storage is required, hold at 2-8°C for no more than 24 hours [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to metaraminol or any excipients, including **sulfites** [Australian].
- **Anaesthesia:** Use with cyclopropane or halothane anaesthesia should be avoided unless clinical circumstances necessitate use [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Sulfite Sensitivity:** Contains sodium metabisulfite, which may cause allergic-type reactions (including anaphylaxis or life-threatening asthma) in susceptible individuals. Prevalence is higher in asthmatic patients [Australian].
- **Excessive Blood Pressure Response:** Rapidly induced hypertension may lead to acute pulmonary oedema, cardiac arrhythmias, and cardiac arrest [Australian].
- **Cumulative Effect:** Due to its prolonged action, a cumulative effect may occur, leading to sustained hypertension even after discontinuation [Australian].
- **Extravasation Risk:** Avoid injection into unsuitable areas (e.g., ankle or dorsum of the hand). Use larger veins (e.g., antecubital fossa or thigh) to prevent necrosis or thrombosis, particularly in patients with peripheral vascular disease or diabetes [Australian].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Blood Pressure:** Close monitoring is required to prevent an excessive pressor response.
- **Central Venous Pressure (CVP):** Useful for assessing circulating blood volume. If hypotension is due to decreased volume, blood or plasma expanders should be used first [Australian].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Underlying Conditions:** Use with caution in patients with heart disease, thyroid disease, hypertension, or diabetes [Australian].
- **Cirrhosis:** Use with caution; ensure adequate electrolyte restoration if diuresis occurs [Australian].
- **Malaria:** Sympathomimetic amines may trigger a relapse in patients with a history of malaria [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies of adverse effects are not formally categorised in the available source.
- **Serious/Systemic:** Sinus or ventricular tachycardia, other arrhythmias (especially in myocardial infarction), hypertension, acute pulmonary oedema, and cardiac arrest.
- **Local:** Abscess formation, tissue necrosis, or sloughing at the injection site (rare) [Australian].
- **Overdose-related:** Headache, chest constriction, nausea, vomiting, euphoria, diaphoresis, bradycardia, or convulsions [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Digitalis:** Use with caution; the combination can cause ectopic arrhythmic activity [Australian].
- **MAOIs and Tricyclic Antidepressants:** May potentiate the pressor action of sympathomimetic amines [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. May cause fetal hypoxia by constricting uterine vessels and limiting placental perfusion. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Secretion into human milk is unknown. Exercise caution when administering to breastfeeding women [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data provided in available sources [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Patients with cirrhosis require caution and electrolyte monitoring [Australian]. Specific dosing adjustments for renal or hepatic impairment are not specified.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A potent sympathomimetic amine that increases systolic and diastolic blood pressure. It exerts a positive inotropic effect on the heart and acts as a peripheral vasoconstrictor [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - **Onset of action:** 1–2 minutes (IV); ~10 minutes (IM); 5–20 minutes (SC).
    - **Duration:** 20 minutes to 1 hour [Australian].
    - **Effect:** Alpha-adrenergic blocking agents may decrease but do not reverse the pressor effect.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, and Excretion: Not specified in available sources [Australian].
