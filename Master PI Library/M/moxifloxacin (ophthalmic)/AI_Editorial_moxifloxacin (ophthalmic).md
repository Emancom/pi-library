# Editorial Product Information: Moxifloxacin (Ophthalmic)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of moxifloxacin ophthalmic solution for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial consolidation of available data and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Moxifloxacin hydrochloride (equivalent to 5 mg moxifloxacin base per mL).
- **Chemical Class:** 8-methoxy fluoroquinolone anti-infective.
- **Brand Name(s):** Not specified in provided source (generic labeling provided).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 0.5% (5 mg/mL) sterile ophthalmic solution [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lifestar Pharma LLC [US]; Redpharm Drug (Repacker) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Moxifloxacin ophthalmic solution is indicated for the treatment of bacterial conjunctivitis caused by susceptible strains of the following microorganisms [US]:
- **Gram-Positive Bacteria:** *Corynebacterium* species\*, *Micrococcus luteus*\*, *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Staphylococcus epidermidis*, *Staphylococcus haemolyticus*, *Staphylococcus hominis*, *Staphylococcus warneri*\*, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Streptococcus viridans* group.
- **Gram-Negative Bacteria:** *Acinetobacter lwoffii*\*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Haemophilus parainfluenzae*\*.
- **Other Organisms:** *Chlamydia trachomatis*.

*\*Efficacy for these specific organisms was studied in fewer than 10 infections.*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Pediatrics):** Instill one drop into the affected eye(s) three times daily for a total of 7 days [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - For topical ophthalmic use only.
    - To prevent contamination, patients should be advised not to touch the dropper tip to any surface, including the eye or surrounding structures.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with a known history of hypersensitivity to moxifloxacin, other quinolones, or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious and occasionally fatal hypersensitivity (anaphylactic) reactions have been reported in patients receiving systemic quinolones. Some reactions followed the first dose and were accompanied by cardiovascular collapse, loss of consciousness, angioedema, and airway obstruction. Therapy should be discontinued immediately at the first sign of a rash or allergic reaction [US].
- **Growth of Resistant Organisms:** Prolonged use may result in the overgrowth of non-susceptible organisms, including fungi. If superinfection occurs, moxifloxacin should be discontinued and alternative therapy initiated [US].
- **Contact Lens Wear:** Patients should be advised not to wear contact lenses if they exhibit signs or symptoms of bacterial conjunctivitis [US].
- **Monitoring:** Clinical judgment, including magnification (slit-lamp biomicroscopy) and fluorescein staining, should be employed if symptoms do not improve [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects reported in clinical trials for moxifloxacin ophthalmic solution include:

| Frequency | Ocular Adverse Effects | Non-Ocular Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Conjunctivitis, decreased visual acuity, dry eye, keratitis, ocular discomfort, ocular hyperemia, ocular pain, ocular pruritus, subconjunctival hemorrhage, tearing. | Fever, increased cough, infection, otitis media, pharyngitis, rash, rhinitis (reported at 1–4%). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Specific drug-drug interaction studies have not been conducted with the ophthalmic solution. However, *in vitro* studies indicate that moxifloxacin does not inhibit major CYP450 isozymes (3A4, 2D6, 2C9, 2C19, or 1A2), making it unlikely to alter the pharmacokinetics of drugs metabolised by these enzymes [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified by letter category in current source.
    - **Risk Summary:** There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies involving high systemic doses (oral/IV) showed decreased fetal weight and delayed skeletal development at levels significantly higher than the recommended human ophthalmic dose (RHOD) [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Moxifloxacin has been found in the milk of lactating rats following oral administration. It is unknown if moxifloxacin is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised, considering the developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother’s clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Oral doses up to 500 mg/kg/day (approximately 3224 times the RHOD) did not affect fertility in male or female rats, though slight effects on sperm morphology and estrous cycles were noted at these extreme doses [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have been established across all age groups, including neonates. Evidence is derived from adequate and well-controlled studies in adults, children, and neonates [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between geriatric and younger patients [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources for the ophthalmic route.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Moxifloxacin inhibits topoisomerase II (DNA gyrase) and topoisomerase IV, essential enzymes required for bacterial DNA replication, transcription, repair, and recombination [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Following bilateral topical dosing (3 times daily), mean steady-state Cmax was 2.7 ng/mL, which is significantly lower (approx. 1600 times) than those seen after therapeutic oral doses.
    - **Elimination:** The estimated plasma half-life of moxifloxacin is 13 hours [US].
- **Microbiology:** Moxifloxacin maintains activity against many organisms resistant to other antibiotic classes, as its mechanism differs from macrolides, aminoglycosides, and tetracyclines. Cross-resistance between moxifloxacin and other quinolones has been observed [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Bacterial Conjunctivitis (General):** In two randomised, double-masked trials (dosed 3 times daily for 4 days), moxifloxacin achieved clinical cure in 66% to 69% of patients by Day 5-6. Microbiological eradication success ranged from 84% to 94% [US].
- **Neonatal Study:** In a trial of infants from birth to 31 days of age, moxifloxacin achieved an 80% clinical cure rate and a 92% microbiological eradication rate by Day 9 [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 25°C (36°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Container:** Supplied in a low-density polyethylene (LDPE) bottle with a 3 mL fill in a 5 mL bottle [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only).
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available source (US jurisdiction).
