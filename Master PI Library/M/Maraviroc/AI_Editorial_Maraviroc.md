# Editorial Product Information: Maraviroc

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official, full Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Maraviroc (Chemical Class: CCR5 Antagonist).
- **Brand Name(s):** **CELSENTRI** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J05AX09.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 150 mg and 300 mg [Australian].
    - Physical description: Blue, biconvex, oval tablets debossed with “MVC” and the strength [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ViiV Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated for use in combination with other antiretroviral medicinal products for adult patients infected with only CCR5-tropic HIV-1 [Australian].
- **Clinical Prerequisite:** Use must be guided by tropism testing (using a validated, highly sensitive assay), resistance testing, and treatment history [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
The recommended dose varies (150 mg, 300 mg, or 600 mg twice daily) based on interactions with concomitant medications, particularly those affecting the CYP3A4 pathway.

| Concomitant Medications | Recommended Maraviroc Dose |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Potent CYP3A inhibitors** (e.g., protease inhibitors [except tipranavir/ritonavir], delavirdine, ketoconazole, clarithromycin, telaprevir) | 150 mg twice daily |
| **Other medications** (Not potent CYP3A inhibitors/inducers, including tipranavir/ritonavir, nevirapine, raltegravir, all NRTIs, and enfuvirtide) | 300 mg twice daily |
| **Potent CYP3A inducers** (without a potent inhibitor) (e.g., efavirenz, rifampicin, etravirine, carbamazepine, phenytoin) | 600 mg twice daily |

### Administration Instructions
- May be taken with or without food [Australian].
- Should be initiated by a physician experienced in HIV management.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to maraviroc or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Severe and potentially life-threatening hepatotoxicity has been reported. Often accompanied by a severe rash or systemic allergic reaction (e.g., DRESS). Maraviroc must be discontinued immediately if signs of hepatitis or hypersensitivity develop [Australian].
- **Severe Skin Reactions:** Cases of Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS) and toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) have been reported [Australian].
- **Tropism Requirements:** Not recommended for patients with dual/mixed or CXCR4-tropic HIV-1. Outgrowth of pre-existing low-level CXCR4-using virus can lead to virologic failure [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Safety:** Use with caution in patients at high risk for cardiovascular events. Myocardial ischemia and infarction have been observed in clinical trials [Australian].
- **Postural Hypotension:** Increased risk in patients with severe renal insufficiency or those on antihypertensive medications. Dose adjustments are required in renal impairment if symptomatic hypotension occurs [Australian].
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome:** Inflammatory reactions to residual opportunistic infections (e.g., CMV, mycobacteria) or autoimmune disorders (e.g., Graves' disease) may occur during the initiation of therapy [Australian].
- **Infection/Malignancy Risk:** While clinical trials showed no significant increase, the antagonism of the CCR5 receptor could theoretically affect immune surveillance and increase the risk of certain infections or malignancies [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Serious Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency from treatment-experienced and treatment-naïve studies.

- **Very Common (>10%):** Upper respiratory tract infections, cough, pyrexia, rash, nausea (in treatment-naïve cohorts), and flatulence/bloating [Australian].
- **Common (1–10%):** Dizziness, insomnia, constipation, joint-related signs/symptoms, and disturbances in sleep [Australian].
- **Serious/Less Common (<2%):** 
    - **Cardiac:** Unstable angina, acute cardiac failure, myocardial infarction.
    - **Hepatobiliary:** Hepatic cirrhosis, jaundice, portal vein thrombosis.
    - **Nervous System:** Convulsions, cerebrovascular accident.
    - **Other:** Osteonecrosis, rhabdomyolysis, marrow depression [Australian].

### Laboratory Abnormalities
- **Grade 3-4 shifts:** Elevations in ALT, AST, total bilirubin, amylase, and lipase were noted in ≥2% of patients in clinical trials [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Profile
- **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by **CYP3A4**.
- **Transporters:** Substrate for P-glycoprotein (P-gp), OATP1B1, and MRP2 [Australian].

### Significant Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Increase maraviroc exposure; dose reduction to 150 mg BID is required [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Decrease maraviroc exposure; dose increase to 600 mg BID is required [Australian].
- **St John’s Wort:** Co-administration is **not recommended** as it substantially decreases maraviroc levels, risking virologic failure and resistance [Australian].
- **Rifampicin + Efavirenz:** This specific combination of two inducers is not recommended [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category: B1.** (Animal studies showed no evidence of fetal harm, but human data is lacking). Should only be used if benefits outweigh potential risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Recommended that HIV-infected women do not breastfeed to avoid postnatal transmission of HIV-1 [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility observed in rat studies at high doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients <18 years; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Caution is advised due to a higher frequency of decreased hepatic/renal function and comorbid conditions [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Severe/ESRD (CrCl ≤30 mL/min):** Dose adjustments are highly dependent on concomitant CYP3A4 inhibitors/inducers. For patients on potent CYP3A inhibitors, maraviroc is **not recommended** if renal function is severe/ESRD [Australian].
    - If symptoms of postural hypotension occur in renal impairment, the dose should be reduced to 150 mg BID [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. While mild-to-moderate impairment shows small increases in Cmax, limited data exists for severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A CCR5 antagonist that selectively binds to the human CCR5 chemokine receptor. This prevents the interaction between the receptor and the HIV-1 gp120 envelope glycoprotein, thereby blocking the entry of CCR5-tropic HIV-1 into the cell [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Maraviroc has no activity against CXCR4-using viruses.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** ~23% at 100 mg; predicted ~33% at 300 mg.
    - **Protein Binding:** ~76%.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (76%) and urinary (20%). Terminal half-life is approximately 13.2 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MOTIVATE 1 & 2:** Phase 3 studies in treatment-experienced patients. Maraviroc + Optimised Background Therapy (OBT) was superior to OBT alone in reducing viral load and increasing CD4+ counts at 48 weeks [Australian].
- **MERIT:** Study in treatment-naïve patients comparing maraviroc vs. efavirenz. While maraviroc demonstrated non-inferiority using a highly sensitive tropism assay, more subjects on maraviroc experienced virologic failure compared to the efavirenz group in the original analysis [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Container:** PVC blisters.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
