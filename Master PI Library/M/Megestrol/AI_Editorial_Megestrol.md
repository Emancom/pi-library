# Editorial Product Information: Megestrol Acetate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Note:** While the user context mentioned an Australian source, the provided regulatory document is specifically the US (FDA) approved labeling for Megestrol Acetate Oral Suspension (USP) from Atlantic Biologicals Corp.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Megestrol acetate; a synthetic derivative of the steroid hormone, progesterone.
- **Chemical Class:** Progestogen.
- **Brand Names:** Megestrol Acetate Oral Suspension [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Oral suspension: 40 mg/mL [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Atlantic Biologicals Corp. [US]; Strides Pharma Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Anorexia, Cachexia, or Weight Loss:** Indicated for the treatment of anorexia, cachexia, or an unexplained, significant weight loss in patients with a diagnosis of acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) [US].
- **Note:** Megestrol acetate is not intended for prophylactic use to avoid weight loss [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The recommended initial adult dosage is 800 mg/day (20 mL/day) [US].
- **Clinical Trial Data:** Daily doses of 400 mg and 800 mg were found to be clinically effective in trials [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Shake the container well before use [US].
- **Titration and Missed Doses:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** History of hypersensitivity to megestrol acetate or any component of the formulation [US].
- **Pregnancy:** Known or suspected pregnancy [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Fetal Harm:** May cause fetal harm when administered to pregnant women. Women of childbearing potential should be advised to avoid pregnancy [US].
- **Glucocorticoid Activity:**
    - Clinical cases of new-onset diabetes mellitus and exacerbation of pre-existing diabetes have been reported [US].
    - Overt Cushing’s syndrome has been reported with chronic use [US].
    - **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Observed in patients during therapy or following withdrawal. ACTH stimulation testing has revealed frequent asymptomatic pituitary-adrenal suppression. Replacement with a rapidly acting glucocorticoid should be considered for patients showing signs of hypoadrenalism (e.g., hypotension, nausea, dizziness) or under stress (e.g., surgery, infection) [US].
- **Thromboembolic Disease:** Use with caution in patients with a history of thromboembolic disease [US].
- **Weight Loss Etiology:** Therapy should only be started after treatable causes of weight loss (e.g., malignancies, infections, endocrine disease, renal disease) are addressed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Monitor for signs of adrenal insufficiency [US].
    - Monitor International Normalized Ratio (INR) in patients concurrently taking warfarin [US].

**Other Adverse Effects (<5%):**
- **Cardiovascular:** Cardiomyopathy, palpitation [US].
- **Digestive:** Constipation, dry mouth, hepatomegaly, oral moniliasis [US].
- **Hemic/Lymphatic:** Leukopenia [US].
- **Metabolic:** Peripheral edema, LDH increased [US].
- **Postmarketing Reports:** Thromboembolic phenomena (thrombophlebitis, pulmonary embolism) and glucose intolerance [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Megestrol may interact with warfarin and increase the International Normalized Ratio (INR). Close monitoring of INR is required [US].
- **Zidovudine and Rifabutin:** Pharmacokinetic studies indicate no significant alterations in the parameters of these drugs requiring dosage adjustment when co-administered with megestrol [US].
- **Note:** The effects of zidovudine or rifabutin on the pharmacokinetics of megestrol have not been studied [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered in this source, but contraindicated due to potential fetal harm. 
    - Animal data show reduced fetal weight, increased number of live births, and feminization of male fetuses in rats [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Nursing should be discontinued during therapy due to the potential for adverse effects on the newborn [US].
- **Fertility:** In animal studies, the reproductive capability of male offspring was impaired when the mother was treated during the perinatal/postnatal period [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65 and older to determine if they respond differently. Dosing should be cautious, typically starting at the low end of the range, due to higher frequencies of hepatic, renal, or cardiac impairment [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Megestrol is substantially excreted by the kidney. Risk of toxic reactions may be greater in patients with impaired renal function; monitoring renal function in elderly patients is recommended [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources, though general caution in the elderly is advised [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The precise mechanism for effects in anorexia and cachexia is unknown [US]. 
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Progestational effects; possesses significant glucocorticoid activity [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations (Cmax) of ~753 ng/mL reached at a median Tmax of five hours (800 mg dose) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Partially inactivated by intestinal and hepatic routes; metabolites constitute 5% to 8% of the dose in urine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Major route is urinary excretion. In humans, mean urinary excretion within 10 days was 66.4%; fecal excretion was 19.8% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **AIDS Cachexia Trials:** Two multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials were conducted. 
- **Trial 1:** Compared 100 mg, 400 mg, and 800 mg/day vs. placebo. The 800 mg group showed a statistically significant increase in weight (mean 7.8 lbs) and appetite compared to placebo [US].
- **Trial 2:** Compared 800 mg/day vs. placebo. The megestrol group had a mean weight increase of 11.2 lbs compared to a 2.1 lb loss in the placebo group [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Dispense in a tight container. Protect from heat [US].
- **Special Handling:** Health hazard data indicates no threshold limit value (TLV). Women at risk of pregnancy should avoid exposure to the suspension [US].
