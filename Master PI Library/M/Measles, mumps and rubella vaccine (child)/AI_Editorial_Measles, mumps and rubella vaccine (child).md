# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the US (FDA) source was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Monitoring:** Pre-therapy plasma carnitine levels should be obtained. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a known history, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been reported [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency ≥5% (Regardless of Causality)

| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, Headache, Fever, Infection, Injection site reaction, Pain | Common | Common |

### Serious or Notable Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of gastritis and "fishy" body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not assigned a specific letter category in provided source]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; studies in dairy cows suggest concentrations increase with exogenous administration. Consider the benefits of maternal treatment against the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), which applies to paediatric populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source; general caution regarding dose selection in the elderly is standard clinical practice.
- **Renal Impairment:** IV levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, the use of high-dose **oral** levocarnitine is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria for oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates carnitine deficiency caused by reduced intake, reduced renal synthesis, and dialytic losses [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Excretion:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8. Efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations was demonstrated, though effects on specific clinical outcomes in this population were not determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at 25°C in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified for other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian S4) in the provided source.
