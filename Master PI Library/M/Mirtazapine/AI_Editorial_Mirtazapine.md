# Editorial Product Information: Mirtazapine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements from the provided source.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official PI or local prescribing guidelines in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mirtazapine (present as mirtazapine hemihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Tetracyclic antidepressant; a piperazinoazepine analogue of mianserin.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Mirtazapine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06AX11.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 15 mg (pale yellow, scored), 30 mg (light pink, scored), and 45 mg (white, unscored) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depressive Disorder:** Treatment of major depression, including the prevention of relapse [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):**
    - **Initial dose:** 15 mg daily.
    - **Maintenance dose:** Typically 30 mg to 45 mg daily. 
    - **Maximum dose:** Clinical responses have been observed at doses up to 60 mg per day [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets should be swallowed without chewing, with fluid if necessary.
    - **Timing:** Preferably taken as a single night-time dose before bed. Alternatively, the total daily dose may be divided into two sub-doses (morning and night).
- **Titration and Duration:** 
    - Treatment should continue for 4–6 months until the patient is symptom-free. 
    - Mirtazapine generally begins to exert effects after 1–2 weeks. If no response is seen within 2–4 weeks at the maximum dose, treatment should be stopped.
- **Discontinuation:** Gradual tapering is recommended to avoid withdrawal symptoms (e.g., dizziness, agitation, anxiety, headache, nausea).
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to mirtazapine or any tablet excipients (e.g., lactose).
- **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Concomitant use with MAOIs is contraindicated. A minimum 14-day interval is required between discontinuing an MAOI and starting mirtazapine, or vice versa [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidality and Clinical Worsening:** Antidepressants increase the risk of suicidal ideation and behaviour in children, adolescents, and young adults (aged 18–24 years). Close monitoring is required, especially during the first few months of therapy or at times of dose adjustment. 
- **Neutropenia and Agranulocytosis:** Bone marrow depression, typically presenting as granulocytopenia or agranulocytosis, has been reported (most often after 2–6 weeks of treatment). Clinicians should be alert for fever, sore throat, or stomatitis; if these occur, treatment must be stopped and blood counts monitored [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Risk is increased when mirtazapine is combined with other serotonergic agents (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, lithium, St John's Wort).
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Cases of Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) have been reported. Discontinue immediately if signs appear; do not restart.
- **QT Prolongation:** While clinical trials showed no significant effect at therapeutic doses, post-marketing cases of QT prolongation and Torsades de Pointes have occurred, primarily in overdose or with other risk factors.
- **Bipolar Disorder:** Treatment may precipitate a mixed/manic episode in patients at risk for bipolar disorder.
- **Conditions Requiring Supervision:** Epilepsy, hepatic/renal impairment, cardiac disease (conduction disturbances, recent MI), hypotension, diabetes mellitus, and narrow-angle glaucoma [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency:

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Weight gain, increased appetite, somnolence/sedation, headache, dry mouth. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Drowsiness, amnesia, dizziness, generalized or local oedema, fatigue. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Elevations in serum transaminase activities. |
| **Rare (0.1–0.01%)** | Mania, nightmares/vivid dreams, seizures, tremor, myoclonus, paraesthesia, restless legs, orthostatic hypotension, exanthema, arthralgia, myalgia, agranulocytosis. |
| **Frequency Unknown** | Serotonin syndrome, hyponatremia, SCARs (SJS/TEN/DRESS), rhabdomyolysis, priapism, pancreatitis. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Agranulocytosis, serotonin syndrome, SCARs, and suicidal ideation/behaviour [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, HIV protease inhibitors, azole antifungals) can increase mirtazapine peak plasma levels by ~40-50%.
    - **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Carbamazepine and phenytoin increase mirtazapine clearance two-fold, potentially requiring a dose increase.
    - **Cimetidine:** May increase mirtazapine plasma concentration by more than 50% [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **MAOIs:** Risk of severe serotonergic reactions (contraindicated).
    - **CNS Depressants:** Mirtazapine may potentiate the sedative effects of benzodiazepines, opioids, and alcohol.
    - **Warfarin:** Mirtazapine may cause a small but significant increase in INR; monitor prothrombin time [Australian].
    - **QTc Prolonging Medicines:** Increased risk of arrhythmias.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3:** Drugs which have been taken by only a limited number of pregnant women and women of childbearing age, without an observed increase in the frequency of malformation or other direct or indirect harmful effects on the human fetus. 
    - Animal studies show increased postnatal mortality at high doses but no teratogenicity. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excreted in animal milk in very small amounts. Due to a lack of human data, use in breastfeeding is **not recommended** [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed disrupted oestrus cycling at doses ≥3 times the maximum human dose [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use (<18 years):** Not established and not recommended. Increased risk of suicidal behaviour and hostility compared to placebo [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance may be decreased. Dosing increases should be performed under close supervision [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance is decreased (75% of the drug is excreted via kidneys). Use with caution in patients with impaired renal function [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance may be decreased. Use with caution [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** An antagonist of central $\alpha_2$-auto and hetero-adrenoceptors, leading to increased noradrenaline and serotonin release. It specifically blocks 5-HT$_2$ and 5-HT$_3$ receptors, directing serotonin activity toward 5-HT$_1$ receptors.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Exhibits potent histamine H$_1$ antagonism (causing sedation) but possesses negligible anticholinergic activity at therapeutic doses.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid; peak plasma levels in ~2 hours. Bioavailability is ~50%. Food does not affect levels.
    - **Distribution:** 85% protein bound. 
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via CYP2D6, CYP3A4, and CYP1A2.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is 20–40 hours (up to 65 hours in some cases). Steady state reached in 3–6 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Placebo-controlled trials confirmed mirtazapine is more effective than placebo in short-term treatment of major depression.
- **Comparators:** Studies showed comparable efficacy to amitriptyline, doxepin, clomipramine, and various SSRIs (fluoxetine, paroxetine, sertraline, fluvoxamine, citalopram). 
- **Relapse Prevention:** A 40-week study showed mirtazapine reduced relapse risk significantly compared to placebo (19.7% vs 43.8%) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C in the original package [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
