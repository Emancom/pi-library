# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (a naturally occurring carrier molecule/amino acid derivative).
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses every 3 or 4 hours; doses should never be spaced more than 6 hours apart. 
    - Subsequent daily maintenance typically ranges around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). 
    - Administration should occur as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for discoloration or particulate matter before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s solution in concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL, stored in PVC bags [US].

### Titration and Monitoring
- In ESRD, initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L.
- Dose adjustments should be guided by trough plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These reactions may occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor the patient closely if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before parenteral therapy.
    - Monitor blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) on a weekly and monthly basis.
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While primarily a concern with high-dose oral administration, patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD on dialysis may experience accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally cleared renally [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% frequency) include:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), and abdominal pain (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), and palpitations (5%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), and anorexia (5%).
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (13%), paresthesia (5%), and insomnia (4%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), and dyspnea (7%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), and peripheral edema (5%).
- **Other:** Body odor and gastritis have been reported less frequently.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is strongly recommended upon initiation or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified by letter, but labeled as follows]: Animal reproductive studies (rats and rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly required, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The benefits to the mother should be weighed against potential risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg), covering pediatric populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not provided in available sources, though general caution is advised regarding renal function.
- **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients on dialysis, dosing is specifically adjusted (10–20 mg/kg post-dialysis). High-dose oral therapy is cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This allows for beta-oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normalization of the acylcarnitine/carnitine ratio (typically >0.4 in deficiency) may alleviate metabolic abnormalities.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg thrice weekly after dialysis showed mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations increased from 21.3 µmol/L at baseline to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Comparisons of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 24 weeks in ESRD patients demonstrated a dose-proportional increase in trough levocarnitine concentrations. However, clinical outcomes related to supplemental carnitine in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial, as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only (Rx only).
