# Editorial Product Information: Meperidine Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Meperidine Hydrochloride (INN); a phenylpiperidine-class opioid agonist.
- **Brand Names:** Meperidine Hydrochloride (Generic; distributed by Hikma Pharmaceuticals) [US].
- **ATC Code:** N02AB02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Oral Tablet:** 50 mg, 100 mg [US].
    - **Oral Solution:** 50 mg/5 mL (10 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Hikma Pharmaceuticals USA Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Pain Management:** Indicated for the management of acute pain severe enough to require an opioid analgesic and for which alternative treatment options (e.g., non-opioid analgesics or opioid combination products) are inadequate or not tolerated [US-approved].
- **Limitations of Use:** Due to risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, it should be reserved for cases where alternative treatments are insufficient. Use for extended periods is limited by the risk of accumulation of the metabolite **normeperidine**, which can cause neurotoxicity (e.g., seizures) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Principles:** Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration. Dosing must be individualised based on pain severity, prior analgesic response, and risk factors for addiction [US].
- **Adult Patients:** 50 mg to 150 mg orally every 3 to 4 hours as needed for pain [US].
- **Pediatric Patients:** 1.1 mg/kg to 1.8 mg/kg orally, up to the adult dose, every 3 or 4 hours as needed [US].
- **Titration and Maintenance:** 
    - Titrate to a dose that provides adequate analgesia while minimising adverse effects. 
    - If pain cannot be managed with a total daily dose of 600 mg or less, the manufacturer recommends discontinuing meperidine and tapering the patient onto an alternate analgesic [US].
- **Dose Modification (Concomitant Phenothiazines):** Reduce the meperidine dose by 25% to 50% when administered with phenothiazines or other tranquilizers [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - **Oral Solution:** Must be measured with a calibrated device (not household spoons). Dilute each dose in 1/2 glass of water to avoid a topical anesthetic effect on mucous membranes [US].
- **Discontinuation:** Do not abruptly discontinue in physically dependent patients; follow a gradual tapering schedule (e.g., 10% to 25% reduction every 2 to 4 weeks) to avoid withdrawal and suicidal ideation [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Respiratory Depression:** Significant respiratory depression in any setting [US].
- **Asthma:** Acute or severe bronchial asthma in unmonitored settings or without resuscitative equipment [US].
- **MAOI Interaction:** Concomitant use of monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 14 days of stopping an MAOI [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Obstruction:** Known or suspected obstruction, including paralytic ileus [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to meperidine or any product excipients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [US]:**
    - **Addiction, Abuse, and Misuse:** Potential for opioid use disorder and fatal overdose.
    - **Life-Threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal depression may occur, especially during initiation or after dose increases.
    - **Accidental Ingestion:** Fatal overdose risk, particularly in children.
    - **Neonatal Opioid Withdrawal Syndrome (NOWS):** Prolonged use during pregnancy can lead to life-threatening withdrawal in the neonate.
    - **Cytochrome P450 3A4 Interaction:** Concomitant use with CYP3A4 inhibitors (or stopping inducers) can increase meperidine levels to fatal concentrations.
    - **Medication Errors:** Risks of dosing errors between mg and mL with oral solutions.
    - **CNS Depressant Risks:** Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or other CNS depressants may result in profound sedation, coma, and death.
    - **MAOI Risks:** Concomitant use can cause coma, severe respiratory depression, cyanosis, and hypotension.
- **Seizures:** Meperidine may increase seizure risk. Prolonged use leads to accumulation of **normeperidine**, which has significant CNS excitatory properties [US].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Potentially fatal condition when used with other serotonergic drugs. Discontinue if suspected [US].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Reported with opioid use, typically after >1 month of therapy [US].
- **Severe Hypotension:** Including orthostatic hypotension and syncope. Avoid use in patients in circulatory shock [US].
- **Elevated Intracranial Pressure (ICP):** Use with extreme caution in patients with head injuries or brain tumors; respiratory depression-induced CO2 retention can further increase ICP [US].
- **Biliary Disease:** May cause spasm of the sphincter of Oddi and increase serum amylase [US].
- **Cardiac Effects:** Use with caution in patients with atrial flutter or other supraventricular tachycardias due to vagolytic activity [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (Frequency not specified but noted as "most frequently observed"):** Lightheadedness, dizziness, sedation, nausea, vomiting, and sweating. These are more prominent in ambulatory patients [US].
- **Nervous System:** Mood changes (euphoria, dysphoria), weakness, headache, agitation, tremor, involuntary muscle movements (myoclonus), seizures, visual disturbances, and confusion [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Constipation, dry mouth, biliary tract spasm [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, bradycardia, palpitations, flushing, hypotension, syncope [US].
- **Allergic/Dermatologic:** Pruritus, urticaria, and anaphylaxis [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory depression, respiratory arrest, circulatory depression, shock, and cardiac arrest [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated. May cause unpredictable, severe, and fatal reactions (e.g., hyperpyrexia, coma, convulsions). Do not use within 14 days of an MAOI [US].
- **CYP3A4 and CYP2B6 Inhibitors:** May increase meperidine levels (e.g., macrolides, azole antifungals, protease inhibitors like ritonavir). Monitor closely [US].
- **CYP3A4 and CYP2B6 Inducers:** May decrease meperidine efficacy or precipitate withdrawal (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine, phenytoin). Stopping an inducer can lead to fatal meperidine concentrations [US].
- **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with benzodiazepines, alcohol, and other sedatives [US].
- **Serotonergic Drugs:** Increased risk of serotonin syndrome (e.g., SSRIs, SNRIs, TCAs, triptans) [US].
- **Mixed Agonist/Antagonist and Partial Agonists:** (e.g., buprenorphine, pentazocine). May reduce analgesic effect and precipitate withdrawal [US].
- **Acyclovir and Cimetidine:** May increase plasma concentrations of meperidine or its metabolite normeperidine [US].
- **Anticholinergics:** Increased risk of urinary retention and severe constipation [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US System:** No lettered category provided, but warnings exist for NOWS and potential fetal harm [US].
    - **Labor/Delivery:** Not recommended immediately prior to labor. Opioids cross the placenta and may cause respiratory depression in the neonate [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Meperidine is excreted in human milk. Monitor infants for excess sedation and respiratory depression. Withdrawal can occur in the infant when breastfeeding is stopped or the mother ceases opioid use [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term opioid use may cause reduced fertility in both sexes; reversibility is unknown [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Pediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established. Neonates have a slower elimination rate and higher susceptibility to respiratory depression [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Start at the low end of the dosing range. Elderly patients have a slower elimination rate and increased risk of respiratory depression and renal-related adverse effects [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Accumulation of meperidine and normeperidine can occur. Use with caution; titrate slowly and monitor for CNS excitatory effects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Significant risk of **normeperidine** accumulation. Use with caution and monitor for seizures and respiratory depression [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Opioid agonist with actions qualitatively similar to morphine, primarily affecting the CNS and smooth muscle [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces analgesia, sedation, and respiratory depression (by reducing brain stem responsiveness to CO2). Causes miosis, though mydriasis may occur in hypoxia or overdose [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability is approximately 50% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Free fraction may be higher in patients >45 years [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Biotransformed in the liver (mainly via CYP3A4 and CYP2B6) to **normeperidine**, an active metabolite with a much longer half-life [US].
    - **Elimination:** Meperidine half-life is 3–8 hours. Normeperidine half-life is ~20.6 hours. Excreted by the kidneys [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Specific pivotal trial data for initial approval (1942) are not detailed in current labeling. 
- **Observational Studies:** Referenced for addiction risk (1%–6% incidence of OUD over 12 months in long-term users) and overdose risk (1.5%–4% cumulative incidence over 5 years) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container. Secure storage is required to prevent theft and misuse [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a **Schedule II (CII)** controlled substance under the Controlled Substances Act [US].
