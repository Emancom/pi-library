# Editorial Product Information: Macimorelin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: United States (FDA). 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Macimorelin acetate (a synthetic growth hormone secretagogue receptor agonist).
- **Brand Name:** **Macrilen** [US].
- **ATC Code:** V04CD06.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** For oral solution; white to off-white granules supplied in aluminum pouches containing 60 mg of macimorelin (equivalent to 68 mg of macimorelin acetate) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral (after reconstitution).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aeterna Zentaris GmbH [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Diagnosis of Adult Growth Hormone Deficiency (AGHD):** Indicated for use as a diagnostic agent to identify growth hormone deficiency in adults [US].
- **Limitations of Use:** Safety and diagnostic performance have not been established in patients with a Body Mass Index (BMI) > 40 kg/m² [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Dosing
- **Recommended Dose:** A single oral dose of 0.5 mg/kg of macimorelin [US].
- **Fasting Requirement:** Patients must fast for at least 8 hours prior to administration [US].

### 3.2 Preparation and Administration Instructions
Preparation must be performed by a healthcare professional:
1.  **Calculate Pouches:** Use 1 pouch for patients weighing up to 120 kg; use 2 pouches for patients > 120 kg.
2.  **Reconstitution:** Dissolve the contents of 1 pouch in 120 mL of water (or 2 pouches in 240 mL) to achieve a final concentration of 0.5 mg/mL.
3.  **Stirring:** Stir gently for 2 to 3 minutes; a small amount of undissolved particles is normal.
4.  **Volume Measurement:** Calculate the required volume (Patient weight in kg × 0.5 mg/kg ÷ 0.5 mg/mL). Use a graduated syringe to measure the exact dose and transfer it to a drinking glass.
5.  **Ingestion:** The patient must drink the entire dose within 30 seconds [US].
6.  **Stability:** The solution must be used within 30 minutes of preparation. Discard any unused portion [US].

### 3.3 Test Procedure and Interpretation
- **Blood Sampling:** Draw venous blood samples for Growth Hormone (GH) determination at 30, 45, 60, and 90 minutes post-administration [US].
- **Interpretation:** AGHD is confirmed if the maximally stimulated serum GH level is **less than 2.8 ng/mL** at all time points [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **QT Prolongation:** Macimorelin causes a mean increase in the corrected QT (QTc) interval of approximately 11 msec. QT prolongation can lead to Torsade de Pointes. Avoid concomitant use with drugs known to prolong the QT interval [US].
- **False Positive Risk (CYP3A4 Inducers):** Strong CYP3A4 inducers can significantly reduce macimorelin plasma levels, potentially leading to a false positive result (incorrectly indicating GHD). Discontinue and allow for an adequate washout of strong inducers before testing [US].
- **False Negative Risk (Hypothalamic Disease):** In patients with recent-onset hypothalamic disease, macimorelin may stimulate the release of stored GH from the pituitary, potentially masking a deficiency. Consider repeat testing if clinically indicated [US].
- **Hormone Replacement:** Deficiencies in sex hormones, thyroid hormone, and/or glucocorticoids should be adequately replaced before performing the test [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions were generally mild to moderate. The following frequencies are based on a study of 154 subjects [US]:

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Not specified in available sources |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dysgeusia (4.5%), Dizziness (3.9%), Headache (3.9%), Fatigue (3.9%), Nausea (3.2%), Hunger (3.2%), Diarrhea (1.9%), Upper respiratory tract infection (1.9%), Feeling hot (1.3%), Hyperhidrosis (1.3%), Nasopharyngitis (1.3%), Sinus bradycardia (1.3%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** While not frequent in trials, the potential for QT-related ventricular arrhythmias is a serious clinical consideration [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **QT Prolonging Drugs:** (e.g., antipsychotics like ziprasidone, antibiotics like moxifloxacin, Class IA/III antiarrhythmics). Avoid co-administration and ensure sufficient washout [US].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., carbamazepine, rifampin, St. John’s Wort). These may decrease macimorelin concentrations. Discontinue before testing [US].
- **Drugs Affecting GH Release:**
    - **Avoid:** Somatostatin, insulin, glucocorticoids, and COX inhibitors (e.g., aspirin, indomethacin) as they may impact diagnostic accuracy.
    - **Transient Elevation:** Clonidine, levodopa, and insulin may transiently raise GH.
    - **Blunting Response:** Muscarinic antagonists (atropine), anti-thyroid meds (propylthiouracil), and GH products.
- **GH Therapy Washout:** Discontinue growth hormone therapy at least **one week** before the test [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** No available data in pregnant women. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. As a single-dose diagnostic, the risk of adverse developmental outcomes is considered limited [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** No data exist regarding the presence of macimorelin in human milk or its effects on the infant/milk production. Clinical need and potential infant exposure should be weighed [US].
- **Fertility:** No studies have been conducted to assess effects on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established. Clinical trials in patients aged 3 to 17 failed to establish a consistent diagnostic cut-off point [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** GH secretion decreases with age; elderly subjects might require a lower diagnostic cut-off. Available data are insufficient to determine if the elderly respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Obesity:** Safety and performance not established for BMI > 40 kg/m² [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Macimorelin is a GH secretagogue receptor agonist that activates receptors in the pituitary and hypothalamus to stimulate the release of endogenous growth hormone [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Maximum GH levels are typically observed between 30 and 90 minutes post-dose [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** $C_{max}$ is reached between 0.5 to 1.5 hours. A liquid meal decreases $C_{max}$ and AUC by ~50% [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life ($T_{1/2}$) is approximately 4.1 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Diagnostic Accuracy Study:** An open-label, randomized, cross-over study compared macimorelin (0.5 mg/kg) to the insulin tolerance test (ITT) in 154 subjects. 
- **Outcomes:** The overall agreement between the macimorelin test and ITT was 84%. In subjects with a high likelihood of AGHD, positive agreement was 89%, and negative agreement was 100% [US].
- **Repeatability:** In a subset of 34 subjects, the agreement between two consecutive macimorelin tests was 91.2% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Pouches:** Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Must be used within 30 minutes. Do not store for later use [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Prescription-only medicine (Rx only) [US].
