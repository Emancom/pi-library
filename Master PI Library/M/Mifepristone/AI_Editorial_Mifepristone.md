# Editorial Product Information: Mifepristone and Misoprostol

## Editorial Note
- The jurisdictions contributing to this editorial synthesis include: **Australia (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combined use of mifepristone and misoprostol for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines and the specific approved PI for their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Mifepristone (synthetic steroid with antiprogestational and antiglucocorticoid activity) and Misoprostol (synthetic analogue of prostaglandin E1).
- **Brand Names:** **ARZESTRA** [Australian].
- **ATC Codes:** Mifepristone (G03XB01); Misoprostol (G02AD06).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
  - **Mifepristone:** 200 mg light yellow, round, biconvex tablet (debossed "CC12").
  - **Misoprostol:** 200 microgram white to off-white, round, flat-faced tablet (debossed "C" above "15").
- **Route of Administration:** Sequential oral (mifepristone) followed by buccal (misoprostol) administration.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Anthia Life Sciences Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Medical Termination of Pregnancy:** Indicated in females of childbearing age for the medical termination of an intrauterine pregnancy up to 63 days of gestation [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The regimen requires the sequential administration of two different medications:
1.  **Mifepristone:** A single 200 mg tablet taken orally.
2.  **Misoprostol:** Following a 36 to 48-hour interval, 800 micrograms (4 x 200 microgram tablets) are administered buccally.

### Administration Instructions
- **Buccal Route:** Misoprostol tablets should be kept between the cheek and gum for 30 minutes. Any remaining fragments may then be swallowed with water [Australian].
- **Food Intake:** There are no specific data regarding food effects on absorption; however, it is recommended to take the tablets 2 hours before or 2 hours after a meal [Australian].
- **Failed Termination:** If the regimen fails to terminate the pregnancy, surgical intervention may be required [Australian].

### Missed Dose and Titration
- Not applicable due to the single-dose nature of the sequential regimen.
- If expulsion occurs before misoprostol administration (approx. 3% of cases), follow-up is still mandatory to ensure complete evacuation [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
ARZESTRA is contraindicated in the following clinical scenarios [Australian]:
- Pregnancy not confirmed by ultrasound or biological testing (hCG).
- Suspected or confirmed ectopic pregnancy.
- Gestational age exceeding 63 days or uncertainty regarding gestational age.
- Uncontrolled asthma.
- Chronic adrenal failure.
- Concurrent long-term corticosteroid therapy.
- Known or suspected haemorrhagic disorders or concurrent anticoagulant therapy.
- Presence of an intrauterine device (IUD) (must be removed before administration).
- Hypersensitivity to mifepristone, misoprostol, or any prostaglandins.
- Lack of access to emergency medical care within 14 days following treatment initiation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning (Follow-up):** Mandatory follow-up is required 14 to 21 days after taking mifepristone to confirm complete expulsion via clinical examination, ultrasound, or beta-hCG measurement [Australian].
- **Infection and Sepsis:** Rare but serious (and sometimes fatal) bacterial infections, including septic shock (e.g., *Clostridium sordellii*, *Streptococcus*), have been reported. Clinicians should maintain a high index of suspicion for patients presenting with tachycardia, leukocytosis, or general malaise, even in the absence of fever [Australian].
- **Haemorrhage:** Prolonged vaginal bleeding (average 10–16 days) is expected and occurs in almost all cases. Heavy bleeding requiring transfusion occurs in approximately 0.2% of cases [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Events:** Rare serious events (myocardial infarction, arrhythmia, severe hypotension) have been reported with prostaglandins; caution is advised in patients with cardiovascular risk factors [Australian].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Cases of toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN) and acute generalised exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP) have been reported with mifepristone. Discontinue immediately if SCARs are suspected [Australian].
- **Rhesus Alloimmunisation:** Assessment and prevention should be performed according to clinical guidelines for induced abortion [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Mifepristone
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, erythromycin, grapefruit juice) may increase mifepristone plasma levels. Caution is warranted [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, dexamethasone, St. John’s Wort, phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine) may decrease mifepristone levels and reduce efficacy [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Mifepristone may increase serum levels of drugs metabolised by CYP3A4, particularly those with a narrow therapeutic index (including some anaesthetic agents) [Australian].

### Misoprostol
- No known clinically significant drug interactions. It does not affect the cytochrome P450 system [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** If the medical termination fails and the pregnancy continues to term, there is a risk of foetal malformations. These include Moebius syndrome (congenital facial paralysis), amniotic band syndrome, and CNS anomalies (hydrocephaly, neural tube defects). Ultrasound monitoring is mandatory for continuing pregnancies [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mifepristone is lipophilic and may be excreted in breast milk. Misoprostol acid is excreted in milk and may cause diarrhoea in nursing infants. Use should be avoided during breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Return to fertility is rapid; pregnancies can occur before the resumption of the first menses. Contraception should start immediately after the procedure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Limited data exist for patients under 18 years. Use in adolescents should be undertaken with caution. It is not relevant for prepubertal children [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No relevant indication for this population [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Mifepristone exposure is reduced in moderate hepatic impairment, but no specific dose adjustments are recommended. It is not recommended in patients with hepatic disease [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended for the approved indication, though it is not recommended in patients with renal disease [Australian].
- **Other:** Not recommended in patients with severe anaemia, malnutrition, or heavy smokers (15+ cigarettes/day) over age 35, as these groups were generally excluded from clinical trials [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
  - **Mifepristone:** Blocks progesterone receptors, leading to endometrial degeneration, uterine contractions, and sensitisation of the myometrium to prostaglandins. It also exhibits antiglucocorticoid and anti-androgenic activity [Australian].
  - **Misoprostol:** A PGE1 analogue that induces myometrial contractions and relaxes the cervix, facilitating the expulsion of the conceptus [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics (Mifepristone):** Rapidly absorbed (Tmax 0.75h); 69% bioavailability; 98% protein bound (to albumin and alpha-1-acid glycoprotein); half-life 36.5 to 38.3 hours; metabolised via CYP3A4 (demethylation and hydroxylation); 90% eliminated in faeces [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics (Misoprostol):** Rapidly absorbed and de-esterified to active misoprostol acid (Tmax 15–30 mins); buccal administration has lower Cmax but more sustained levels compared to oral/sublingual; half-life 35 minutes; 1–4% excreted in urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Australian Cohort Study:** In 15,565 women, the method success (complete abortion without surgery) was 95.8%. Method failure occurred in 4.2% of cases. Heavy bleeding was reported in 3.2% [Australian].
- **Gynuity Trial:** Efficacy was reported at 98.0% for gestation ≤49 days, 96.8% for 50–56 days, and 95.9% for 57–63 days [Australian].
- **Comparison of Routes:** Literature indicates buccal misoprostol (96.2% efficacy) is superior to oral misoprostol (91.3% efficacy) for pregnancies up to 63 days [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in the original container (green carton for mifepristone, pink carton for misoprostol) and protect from light [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
