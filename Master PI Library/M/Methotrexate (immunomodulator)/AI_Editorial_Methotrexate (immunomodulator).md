# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial is derived from the official product labeling for **Levocarnitine** Injection as approved by the following regulatory authority:
    - **United States (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation)
- **Purpose**: This document serves as a harmonized editorial synthesis of provided product information (PI) for use by healthcare professionals. It aims to consolidate clinical data, dosing, and safety information into a single reference.
- **Note**: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class**: Carrier molecule / Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names**: Not specified (Generic/USP designation).
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths**: 
    - **Injection**: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: 
    - **United States**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation (Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors)**: For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders**:
    - **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose**: In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency**: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; frequency should not be less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maximum Dose**: The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis**:
    - **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration**: Administer as a slow 2–3 minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Metabolic Disorders**: Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD**: Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility**: Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None [US].

### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration [US].
- **Seizures**: Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### Specific Precautions
- **Renal Impairment**: The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring**: INR monitoring is required for patients on concurrent warfarin therapy [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased INR have been observed when co-administered with levocarnitine. **Management**: Monitor INR after treatment initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: [US: Category not assigned/narrative style]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (BSA) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Dairy cow studies show increased milk concentration following administration. Risks of excess intake to the infant must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing for metabolic disorders is established for pediatric patients (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use**: Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment**: Used as a treatment for carnitine deficiency in ESRD. Use caution with oral formulations in this population due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination**: Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study**: A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed that trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8 [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study**: Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses in ESRD patients (n≈30 per group) demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions**: Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Handling**: Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial; contains no preservative [US].
