# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information was derived from the official Product Information (PI) for the **US (FDA)** as provided by Baxter Healthcare Corporation.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary for educational and clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative/carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer via slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
- **Loading Dose:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Timing:** Administer as a slow bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are high.

### Monitoring
- **Target Levels:** Free plasma carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Frequency:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels is recommended.

## 4. Contraindications
- No known contraindications are specified in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration.
- **Management:** Discontinue treatment immediately upon signs of a severe reaction. Re-administration should only occur after a careful risk-benefit analysis and under close monitoring.

### General Precautions
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase seizure frequency or severity in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history.
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Dosing):** While this PI focuses on the injection, it notes that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
*Events occurring at ≥5% frequency:*
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), palpitations (5%), arrhythmia (2%), atrial fibrillation (2%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%), anorexia (5%).
- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Headache (22%), dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), depression (5%), anxiety (1%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), flu syndrome (25%), infection (15%), chest pain (12%), asthenia (9%), fever (7%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Body Odor:** Less frequent than gastrointestinal symptoms.
- **Seizures:** Reported in postmarketing surveillance.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Pregnancy Category not assigned under current labeling, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm.] Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine should be weighed against the benefits of maternal treatment [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight (see Section 3).
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document; however, clinical monitoring is advised as for all patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically for ESRD on dialysis. Trough level monitoring is essential to avoid over-accumulation.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription only medication) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
