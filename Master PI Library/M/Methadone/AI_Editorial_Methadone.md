# Editorial Product Information: Methadone Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of methadone hydrochloride, synthesized from official product information provided by the regulatory authority listed above. It is intended as a clinically focused reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult the approved PI of their own region for legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methadone hydrochloride (synthetic opioid).
- **Chemical Class:** Opioid agonist; diphenylheptane derivative.
- **Brand Names:** **Aspen Methadone Syrup** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral liquid: 5 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Opioid Dependence:** Treatment and management of dependence on opioid drugs [Australian].
- **Severe Pain:** Management of severe pain where the pain is opioid-responsive and requires daily, continuous, long-term treatment. It is indicated only when other treatment options (non-opioids or immediate-release opioids) have failed, are contraindicated, or are otherwise inadequate [Australian]. 
    - *Note:* Not indicated for "as-needed" (PRN) analgesia.
    - *Note:* In chronic non-cancer pain, use is restricted to exceptional circumstances [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Dosing must be individualised. Due to methadone’s long half-life and risk of accumulation, steady-state concentrations may take up to 12 days to achieve. It is recommended that only prescribers familiar with methadone’s complex pharmacokinetics initiate treatment [Australian].

### Treatment of Opioid Dependence
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg to 20 mg orally.
- **Titration:** Increases of 5 mg to 10 mg daily are permissible. 
- **Maximum Titration Rate:** Do not exceed 5–10 mg daily increases and no more than 30 mg total increase within any 7-day period [Australian].
- **Maintenance:** Stability is typically reached at 30 mg to 50 mg daily (maximum 80 mg daily).
- **Withdrawal:** Doses should be tapered gradually until total cessation.

### Management of Severe Pain
- **Adult Initial Dose:** 5 mg to 10 mg orally every 6 to 8 hours.
- **Chronic Use:** Due to accumulation risks, administration should generally not exceed twice daily in long-term therapy [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Route:** Oral use only. 
- **Parenteral Warning:** **Aspen Methadone Syrup** must not be used parenterally. Abuse of oral forms via injection can result in fatal adverse events [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to methadone or any excipients.
- **Respiratory:** Severe/acute respiratory disease, respiratory depression (especially with cyanosis or excessive secretions), and during an attack of bronchial asthma [Australian].
- **Neurological:** Head injury and raised intracranial pressure.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Ulcerative colitis (risk of toxic dilation or colonic spasm); biliary or renal tract spasm [Australian].
- **Hepatic:** Severe hepatic impairment (risk of hepatic encephalopathy).
- **Cardiac:** Existing QT prolongation, including congenital long QT syndrome [Australian].
- **Interactions:** Concurrent use of Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or use within 14 days of stopping MAOIs [Australian].
- **Other:** Acute alcoholism.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Severe Warnings [Australian]
- **Hazardous and Harmful Use:** Methadone poses risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, which can lead to overdose and death.
- **Life-threatening Respiratory Depression:** Serious or fatal respiratory depression may occur, with peak effects persisting longer than peak analgesic effects. Monitoring is critical during initiation and dose titration.
- **Concomitant CNS Depressant Use:** Use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, alcohol, or other CNS depressants may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death.

### General Precautions
- **QT Prolongation:** Methadone inhibits cardiac potassium channels. Torsade de Pointes has been observed, particularly at high doses. Caution in patients with electrolyte imbalances (hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia) or cardiac hypertrophy [Australian].
- **Hyperalgesia:** High doses may cause an unexplained increase in pain or diffuse sensitivity. In such cases, dose reduction or opioid rotation is required.
- **Phaeochromocytoma:** Extreme caution is required as aggravated hypertension has been reported with other opioids [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal/Hepatobiliary:** May cause spasm of the sphincter of Oddi, increasing intrabiliary pressure. Caution in pancreatitis or biliary disease [Australian].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Opioids may cause reversible adrenal insufficiency; monitor for symptoms such as nausea, fatigue, and hypotension.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies are often not specified in source documents.*

- **Respiratory:** Respiratory depression (Major), central sleep apnoea syndrome (Frequency not known) [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, constipation, dry mouth, loss of appetite. Less common: Stomach cramps, pancreatitis (Frequency not known) [Australian].
- **Neurological:** Dizziness, drowsiness, light-headedness, sweating, confusion, weakness, fatigue, blurred vision. Euphoria at high doses [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension, bradycardia, tachycardia, palpitations, oedema. Very rarely: QT prolongation and Torsade de Pointes [Australian].
- **Endocrine:** Decreased libido, impotence, amenorrhea, gynaecomastia (men), adrenal insufficiency, androgen deficiency [Australian].
- **Metabolic:** Hypoglycaemia (observed during dose escalation or overdose) [Australian].
- **Renal:** Urinary retention or hesitancy; antidiuretic effects.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4/2D6 Inducers:** Rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St John’s Wort, and certain antiretrovirals (nevirapine, efavirenz) may decrease methadone levels, potentially precipitating withdrawal [Australian].
- **CYP3A4/2D6 Inhibitors:** Fluconazole and SSRIs (especially fluvoxamine) may increase methadone levels, increasing toxicity risks [Australian].
- **Effect on other drugs:** Methadone may increase concentrations of nelfinavir, zidovudine, and desipramine, while decreasing concentrations of abacavir and amprenavir [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **CNS Depressants:** Enhanced depressant effects with alcohol, benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, and antipsychotics [Australian].
- **QT Prolonging Agents:** Increased risk of arrhythmia with Class I and III antiarrhythmics, tricyclic antidepressants, and neuroleptics.
- **MAOIs:** Potential for fatal hypotension and coma [Australian].
- **GI Motility Agents:** Methadone may antagonize the effects of metoclopramide or domperidone.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Methadone crosses the placenta. Chronic use near term may result in Neonatal Withdrawal Syndrome (hypertonia, tremors, convulsions, apnoea). Use only if benefits outweigh risks. Not recommended during labour due to prolonged action and risk of neonatal respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Distributed into breast milk (mean milk-to-plasma ratio 0.44). Breastfeeding is permissible for mothers on maintenance therapy, but infants must be monitored closely for sedation and respiratory depression. Use the lowest effective dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** In men, methadone reduces testosterone and depresses ejaculate volume and sperm motility. No evidence of impaired female fertility in humans [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children <18 years due to insufficient data and high sensitivity to respiratory depression [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Caution required due to long half-life and risk of accumulation and confusion. Monitor renal function closely [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Increase dosing interval to minimum 8-hourly (GFR 10–50 mL/min) or 12-hourly (GFR <10 mL/min) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Metabolism is slowed. Use lower initial doses and titrate based on response. Contraindicated in severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Mu-opioid receptor agonist and N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) receptor antagonist. This combination provides analgesia while potentially limiting opioid tolerance [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** High oral bioavailability; peak plasma levels 1–5 hours post-dose.
    - **Distribution:** Highly tissue-bound; 60–90% plasma protein binding (primarily $\alpha_1$-acid glycoprotein).
    - **Metabolism:** Hepatic via N-demethylation (primarily CYP3A4, also CYP2D6) to inactive metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life varies significantly (15–60 hours). Excreted via urine and faeces.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from light.
- **Container:** Amber glass or brown HDPE bottles with child-resistant closures [Australian].
