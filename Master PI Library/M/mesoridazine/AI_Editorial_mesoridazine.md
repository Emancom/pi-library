# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information documents contributed to this editorial: US (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/generic).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source, but standard classification).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance haemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus injection (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Initial Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses administered every 3 to 4 hours (not less than every 6 hours) over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically range from 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring and Instructions
- **Target Concentrations:** Aim for plasma free carnitine concentrations between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately and monitor the patient [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with established seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal impairment. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally cleared renally [US].
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and Severe Hypersensitivity:** Reported during intravenous administration [US].
- **Seizures:** New-onset or exacerbated pre-existing activity [US].

### Common Adverse Effects (ESRD Clinical Trial Data)
Data from double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic haemodialysis patients (frequency ≥5%):
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), abdominal pain (9%), body odour [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhoea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough (9%), dyspnoea (7%) [US].
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitor INR levels following the initiation of treatment or any dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in the provided source, but noted that reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Clinicians must weigh the benefits to the mother against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 for specific instructions [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources; general caution regarding organ function in the elderly is standard clinical practice.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. However, caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral formulations are used in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In 9-week and 24-week clinical studies, ESRD patients on haemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg levocarnitine thrice weekly post-dialysis showed significant increases in plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels typically stabilised between 54 µmol/L and 243 µmol/L (normal range 40–50 µmol/L). While carnitine levels increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in this population remains undetermined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
