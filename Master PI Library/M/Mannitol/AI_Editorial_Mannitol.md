# Editorial Product Information: Mannitol (Inhalation)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data to support safe and effective use.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing or administering the medication in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mannitol (D-mannitol); a hexahydric alcohol.
- **Brand Name(s):** Aridol [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R05CB16 (Mannitol, mucolytics); also used as a diagnostic agent (V04CX).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Powder for inhalation contained in hard gelatin capsules. Available in a composite pack (kit) containing capsules of 0 mg, 5 mg, 10 mg, 20 mg, and 40 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Inhalation (via the **Osmohaler** device).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** BTC Pharma Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Asthma Diagnosis:** Indicated for identifying bronchial hyperresponsiveness to assist in the diagnosis of asthma [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration Instructions
- **Setting:** Must be performed under the supervision of a physician or trained personnel experienced in bronchial provocation tests and acute bronchospasm management [Australian].
- **Patient Positioning:** Patients should be seated comfortably with good posture.
- **Equipment:** A nose clip must be used. A new **Osmohaler** device is required for each test. Capsules must be punctured only once to prevent fragmentation.

### Aridol Challenge Protocol [Australian]
The test consists of a cumulative dose challenge. $FEV_1$ is measured in duplicate 60 seconds after each dose. The test continues until a positive response is achieved or a cumulative dose of 635 mg is reached.

**Table 1: Dose Escalation Schedule**
| Dose Step | Capsule Dose (mg) | Cumulative Dose (mg) | Number of Capsules |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| 2 | 5 | 5 | 1 |
| 3 | 10 | 15 | 1 |
| 4 | 20 | 35 | 1 |
| 5 | 40 | 75 | 1 |
| 6 | 80 | 155 | 2 x 40 mg |
| 7 | 160 | 315 | 4 x 40 mg |
| 8 | 160 | 475 | 4 x 40 mg |
| 9 | 160 | 635 | 4 x 40 mg |

### Defining a Positive Response
A test is considered positive if the patient experiences:
- A $\ge 15\%$ fall in $FEV_1$ from the baseline (established after the 0 mg dose); OR
- A $\ge 10\%$ incremental fall in $FEV_1$ between two consecutive dose steps [Australian].

### Recovery
Following a positive result or significant respiratory symptoms, a standard dose of a $\beta_2$-agonist should be administered to expedite recovery (typically 10–20 minutes). Patients must be monitored until $FEV_1$ returns to within 5% of baseline [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to mannitol or the gelatin capsule excipients [Australian].
- **Compromised Physiological Reserve:** Conditions where induced bronchospasm or repeated forced expiration manoeuvres may be hazardous, including:
    - Aortic or cerebral aneurysm.
    - Uncontrolled hypertension.
    - Myocardial infarction or cerebral vascular accident (CVA) within the previous six months [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bronchospasm Risk:** Mannitol is a bronchoconstrictor. Resuscitation equipment, oxygen, and medications to treat severe bronchospasm (e.g., rapid-acting bronchodilators) must be immediately available in the testing area [Australian].
- **Baseline Lung Function:** Exercise caution in patients with ventilatory impairment (resting $FEV_1 < 70\%$ of predicted or absolute value $< 1.5$ L in adults). 
- **Acute Conditions:** Caution is required in cases of haemoptysis of unknown origin, pneumothorax, or upper/lower respiratory tract infections within the previous 2 weeks.
- **Recent Surgery:** Caution in patients following recent abdominal, thoracic, or eye surgery.
- **Baseline Instability:** If the $FEV_1$ fall following the 0 mg (placebo) capsule is $> 10\%$, the challenge should be discontinued [Australian].
- **Pre-test Restrictions:**
    - **Exercise:** Avoid vigorous exercise on the day of the test.
    - **Smoking:** Refrain for at least 6 hours prior to testing.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse events are mild and transient.

### Serious and Other Effects
- **Infrequent (<1%):** Chest pain, decreased oxygen saturation, retching, migraine, rhinitis, and hyperhidrosis [Australian].
- **Local irritation:** Oropharyngeal pain and irritation are common; rinsing the mouth after the test may mitigate these symptoms.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
The sensitivity of the airway to mannitol is significantly reduced by medications that inhibit bronchoconstriction.

**Recommended Withholding Periods Prior to Testing [Australian]:**
- **6–8 hours:** Inhaled NSAIDs (e.g., sodium cromoglycate).
- **8 hours:** Short-acting $\beta_2$-agonists (SABA) (e.g., salbutamol).
- **12 hours:** Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) (e.g., budesonide), Anticholinergic bronchodilators (e.g., ipratropium).
- **24 hours:** Long-acting $\beta_2$-agonists (LABA) (e.g., salmeterol), Xanthines (e.g., theophylline), Combination ICS/LABA.
- **72 hours (3 days):** Long-acting anticholinergics (LAMA) (e.g., tiotropium), Antihistamines (e.g., cetirizine).
- **4 days:** Leukotriene-receptor antagonists (LTRA) (e.g., montelukast).
- **Day of test:** Caffeine-containing foods (coffee, tea, cola, chocolate) should be avoided as they may decrease responsiveness.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B2.** No animal reproduction studies have been conducted with inhaled mannitol. Orally administered mannitol is not teratogenic in mice/rats. Aridol should not be administered to pregnant women due to unknown effects of hyperresponsiveness reactions on the mother or foetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Use with caution. It is unknown if inhaled mannitol is excreted in human milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** The impact of inhaled mannitol on fertility has not been investigated.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 6 years of age due to the inability to perform reliable spirometry. Safety and efficacy in children $\ge 6$ years appear comparable to adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical experience is limited in the elderly [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No formal studies conducted. As the kidney is the primary route of elimination, systemic exposure may increase in renal failure; however, the dose used for inhalation is low [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No formal studies conducted [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhaled mannitol increases airway osmolarity. This triggers the release of bronchoconstrictor mediators (e.g., histamine, leukotrienes) from inflammatory cells (mast cells, eosinophils). In hyperresponsive individuals, these mediators cause smooth muscle contraction and airway narrowing [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Sensitivity is measured as the dose required to cause a 15% fall in $FEV_1$. Sensitivity is reduced by corticosteroids and specific mediator antagonists (e.g., fexofenadine) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absorption half-life is approximately 0.2–1 hour.
    - **Elimination:** Following systemic absorption, it is eliminated largely unchanged via glomerular filtration. 80% is excreted in urine within 3 hours. The elimination half-life in adults is 1–2 hours (extended in renal failure) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Study:** A randomised, crossover study of 646 subjects (aged 6–83 years) compared mannitol to hypertonic (4.5%) saline.
- **Performance:** In identifying bronchial hyperresponsiveness, the mannitol challenge demonstrated a sensitivity of 81% and specificity of 87% compared to hypertonic saline. 
- **Clinical Correlation:** Against a physician’s diagnosis of asthma, the mannitol challenge had a sensitivity of 60% and a specificity of 95% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Protect from sunlight.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
