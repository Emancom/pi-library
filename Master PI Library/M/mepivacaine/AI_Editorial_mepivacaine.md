# Editorial Product Information: Mepivacaine Hydrochloride

**Editorial Note:** This document represents a unified synthesis of Product Information (PI) sourced from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Please note that these sources cover different clinical applications: the TGA information pertains to human dental use (3% concentration), while the FDA information pertains to equine veterinary use (2% concentration). Practitioners must adhere to the specific concentrations and indications approved for their respective species and jurisdictions.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Mepivacaine Hydrochloride Injection  
**Active Ingredient:** Mepivacaine hydrochloride

Mepivacaine hydrochloride is a local anesthetic of the amide type, chemically designated as (1-Methyl-2-piperidyl)formo-2´,6´-xylidide hydrochloride. It is a white, racemic crystalline powder that is readily soluble in water.

*   **Available Concentrations:** 
    *   3% (30 mg/mL) in 2.2 mL cartridges [TGA]
    *   2% (20 mg/mL) in 50 mL multiple-dose vials [FDA]

## 2. Approved Indications
Mepivacaine is indicated for the production of local anesthesia through various techniques:

*   **Human Dental Use:** Routine dental procedures and oral surgery via infiltration or nerve block techniques [TGA].
*   **Equine Veterinary Use:** Infiltration, nerve blocks (for lameness diagnosis or pain relief), intra-articular anesthesia, and epidural anesthesia. It may also be used topically for the laryngeal mucosa prior to ventriculectomy [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
The use of Mepivacaine is contraindicated in the following circumstances:
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to mepivacaine, other amide-type local anesthetics, or any excipients (such as methylparaben in multi-dose vials). A history of cross-sensitivity to procaine-type anesthetics is also a contraindication [TGA].
*   **Administration Route:** Absolute contraindication against intravenous injection [TGA]. For epidural use, care must be taken to avoid subarachnoid injection [FDA].
*   **Infection:** Presence of inflammation or sepsis at the intended injection site [TGA].
*   **Pediatrics:** Should not be used in children under three years of age [TGA].
*   **Veterinary Specific:** Not for use in horses intended for human consumption [FDA].
*   **Drug Interactions:** Patients currently taking or who have taken Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) or tricyclic antidepressants within the last 14 days [TGA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
**General Safety:**
Local anesthetics should only be administered by clinicians who are proficient in the diagnosis and management of dose-related toxicity. Resuscitative equipment, including oxygen and emergency drugs, must be immediately available to treat potential cardiovascular or respiratory emergencies [TGA].

**Administration Precautions:**
*   **Aspiration:** Clinicians must perform frequent aspirations to avoid accidental intravascular injection, which can cause severe systemic toxicity even at low doses [TGA, FDA].
*   **Site Preparation:** For equine procedures, the skin should be shaved and sterilized, and sharp, appropriately sized needles must be used [FDA].
*   **Monitoring:** Continuous observation of cardiovascular and respiratory vital signs is required following administration [TGA].

**Special Patient Populations:**
*   **Hepatic/Renal Impairment:** Mepivacaine is metabolized by the liver and excreted by the kidneys. Patients with severe impairment are at a higher risk of toxic plasma concentrations [TGA].
*   **Cardiovascular Disease:** Use with caution in patients with bradycardia, heart block, or impaired cardiovascular function, as they may not compensate for the drug's effect on AV conduction [TGA].
*   **Neurological Conditions:** Caution is advised in patients with epilepsy [TGA].
*   **Malignant Hyperthermia:** As amide-type anesthetics may potentially trigger familial malignant hyperthermia, a management protocol should be in place [TGA].

**Patient Advice:**
Patients should be warned to avoid eating, drinking hot liquids, or biting the tongue/lips until normal sensation returns to prevent inadvertent trauma to anesthetized tissues [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
The lowest dose required for effective anesthesia should always be utilized. Dosage depends on the procedure, the area to be anesthetized, and the vascularity of the tissue.

**Human Dental Dosage (3% solution):**
*   **Adults:** 2.2 mL (one cartridge) is typically sufficient; do not exceed 6.6 mL (three cartridges) [TGA].
*   **Adolescents (14-17 years):** Usual dose is 2.2 mL; maximum 4.4 mL [TGA].
*   **Children (6-14 years):** Usual dose is 1.35 mL; maximum 2.7 mL [TGA].
*   **Children (3-6 years):** Maximum dose of 1.8 mL [TGA].

**Equine Dosage (2% solution):**
*   **Nerve Block:** 3 to 15 mL [FDA].
*   **Epidural:** 5 to 20 mL [FDA].
*   **Intra-articular:** 10 to 15 mL [FDA].
*   **Infiltration:** 20 to 50 mL [FDA].
*   **Laryngeal mucosa:** 25 to 40 mL by spray or infiltration [FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
**Local Reactions:**
Common reactions include injection site pain, edema, gingivitis, or infection. In horses, moderate transient edema at the site of injection occurs rarely [TGA, FDA].

**Systemic Toxicity:**
Systemic effects are usually dose-related and involve the Central Nervous System (CNS) and Cardiovascular System (CVS):
*   **CNS:** May include light-headedness, nervousness, tremors, blurred vision, and drowsiness. In severe cases, convulsions, unconsciousness, and respiratory arrest may occur [TGA].
*   **CVS:** Characterized by depressant effects such as bradycardia, hypotension, and potential cardiac arrest [TGA].
*   **Allergic:** Rare cutaneous lesions, urticaria, or anaphylactoid reactions [TGA].

**Laboratory Interference:** Intramuscular injection may increase creatine phosphokinase levels, potentially complicating the diagnosis of acute myocardial infarction [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
Caution is advised when co-administering mepivacaine with:
*   **Anti-arrhythmics and Beta-blockers:** (e.g., amiodarone, propranolol) may reduce mepivacaine clearance or produce additive cardiac depressant effects [TGA].
*   **Cimetidine:** May increase serum levels of mepivacaine [TGA].
*   **Suxamethonium:** Combination may lead to prolonged neuromuscular blockade [TGA].
*   **Epinephrine:** The addition of epinephrine (1:100,000) may enhance the vasoconstrictive activity and prolong the effect of mepivacaine [FDA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
**Pregnancy:**
Classified as Pregnancy Category A. While safe use during pregnancy has not been formally established, extensive clinical use in dental procedures has shown no increase in fetal malformations or harmful effects [TGA].

**Lactation:**
It is not confirmed whether mepivacaine is excreted in human breast milk. Use is generally not recommended during lactation [TGA].

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Completely absorbed following parenteral administration. Rate of absorption is influenced by the site and the presence of vasoconstrictors [TGA].
*   **Distribution:** Highly bound to plasma proteins. It crosses the blood-brain and placental barriers. The plasma half-life in adults is approximately 2 to 3 hours [TGA].
*   **Metabolism:** Rapidly metabolized by the liver [TGA].
*   **Excretion:** Primarily excreted via the bile (over 50%) and kidneys (less than 10% unchanged in urine) [TGA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Mepivacaine is a potent local anesthetic that stabilizes the neuronal membrane. It acts by decreasing the membrane's permeability to sodium ions, which reversibly inhibits the initiation and conduction of nerve impulses [TGA]. Its anesthetic potency is approximately two to two and a half times that of procaine and is considered equal to or greater than that of lidocaine [FDA].

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   Store at controlled room temperature between 20°C to 25°C [FDA, TGA].
*   Protect from light [TGA].
*   **Multi-dose Vials:** For veterinary use, contents should be used within 90 days after the first dose is withdrawn [FDA].
*   **Single-use Cartridges:** Discard any unused portion of a cartridge immediately after use [TGA].
