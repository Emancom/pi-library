# Editorial Product Information: Mitotane

## Editorial Note
- This document has been prepared based on the Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This is a harmonised editorial overview designed for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource. It is not a substitute for the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mitotane (an adrenal cytotoxic agent).
- **Brand Name:** **LYSODREN** [US].
- **ATC Code:** L01XX03 (Pharmacotherapeutic group: Other antineoplastic agents).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 500 mg scored tablets (white, round, biconvex; "BL" over "L1" imprint) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Esteve Pharmaceuticals, S.A. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Treatment of inoperable, functional or nonfunctional adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Initial Dose:** 2000 mg to 6000 mg orally per day, administered in three or four divided doses [US].
- **Titration:** Dose should be incrementally increased based on patient tolerance and clinical response to achieve a target plasma mitotane level of 14 to 20 mg/L [US].
- **Monitoring:** Consider monitoring plasma levels every 2 weeks initially, then monthly once the target is reached (typically within 3 to 5 months) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Food Effect:** Administer with food; timing relative to meals must be consistent. Administration with high-fat food enhances absorption [US].
- **Integrity:** Tablets must be swallowed whole. Do not crush, chew, or split [US].
- **Hazardous Handling:** Mitotane is a hazardous drug. Caregivers should wear disposable gloves and avoid contact with crushed or broken tablets [US].

### Dose Adjustments for Toxicity
- **General Reduction:** Decrease daily dose by 500 mg to 1000 mg for adverse reactions [US].
- **Specific Adjustments:**
    - **CNS/GI Toxicity (Grade 3/4):** Withhold until recovery to Grade ≤1. If plasma levels are >20 mg/L, withhold until levels drop. Resume at a reduced dose [US].
    - **Plasma Levels >20 mg/L:** If asymptomatic, consider a dose reduction of 50 to 75% [US].

### Missed Dose
- Instruct patients to skip the missed dose and take the next dose as scheduled. Do not double the dose. If vomiting occurs after a dose, skip that dose and take the next scheduled dose [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [US]
- **Adrenal Crisis in the Setting of Shock, Severe Trauma, or Infection:** Patients are at increased risk of developing life-threatening adrenal crisis during physiological stress. Mitotane should be temporarily discontinued in these settings, and exogenous steroids administered.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** Mitotane causes adrenal suppression. Monitor for glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid insufficiency. Due to increased steroid clearance, high-dose replacement therapy may be required [US].
- **CNS Toxicity:** Can cause sedation, lethargy, vertigo, and cognitive dysfunction. Monitor mitotane levels; toxicity is more frequent when levels exceed 20 mg/L. Advise patients not to drive or operate machinery if CNS symptoms occur [US].
- **Ovarian Macrocysts:** Premenopausal women may develop multiple, large, bilateral non-malignant cysts. Monitor with pelvic ultrasound at baseline and regular intervals [US].
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Liver injury/failure has occurred. Monitor LFTs prior to start, during titration, and periodically [US].
- **Hematologic Toxicity:** Risk of leukopenia, anemia, and thrombocytopenia. Monitor CBC [US].
- **Prolonged Bleeding Time:** Mitotane can impair platelet aggregation. This must be considered if surgery is required; evaluate bleeding risk via ADP-induced platelet aggregometry [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are common and often dose-related due to adipose tissue accumulation.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Anorexia, epigastric discomfort, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, mucosal inflammation [US]. |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, vertigo, confusion, headache, ataxia, mental impairment, dysarthria, paresthesia, movement disorders [US]. |
| **Endocrine/Metabolic** | Hypothyroidism, hypercholesterolemia, hypertriglyceridemia, growth retardation [US]. |
| **Reproductive** | Gynecomastia, hypogonadism (males), decreased testosterone (females/males), decreased androstenedione [US]. |
| **Dermatologic** | Rash, pruritus, hypersensitivity [US]. |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Hepatitis, elevated liver enzymes [US]. |
| **Blood/Lymphatic** | Leukopenia, anemia, thrombocytopenia, prolonged bleeding time [US]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Spironolactone:** May block the action of mitotane; avoid concomitant use [US].
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Mitotane is a **strong inducer** of CYP3A4. It may significantly decrease the plasma concentration of CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., midazolam), leading to therapeutic failure. Avoid use with substrates where minimal level changes cause serious failure [US].
- **Warfarin:** Mitotane induces warfarin metabolism, reducing its efficacy. Monitor INR closely and adjust warfarin dose as needed [US].
- **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Mitotane may render hormonal contraceptives ineffective; use effective non-hormonal contraception [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Mitotane can cause fetal harm (preterm births, early pregnancy loss).
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a letter grade, but "Embryo-Fetal Toxicity" is warned [US].
    - **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use effective non-hormonal contraception during treatment and after discontinuation for as long as mitotane levels are detectable [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mitotane is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and after discontinuation while plasma levels are detectable [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Effectiveness not established. Reports suggest negative impacts on neuro-psychological development, growth delay, and estrogenic-like effects (e.g., uterine bleeding, gynecomastia) [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Start at the low end of the dosing range; reflect the higher frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not recommended for severe impairment. Monitor levels frequently in mild-to-moderate impairment [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not recommended for severe impairment. Monitor levels frequently in mild-to-moderate impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Adrenal cytotoxic agent. It modifies the peripheral metabolism of steroids and directly suppresses the adrenal cortex. It also increases the formation of 6-β-hydroxycortisol [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 40% of an oral dose is absorbed [US].
    - **Distribution:** Highly lipophilic; accumulates primarily in adipose tissue [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Hepatic and extrahepatic; metabolized to 1,1-(o,p'-dichlorodiphenyl) acetic acid (o,p'-DDA) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life ranges from 18 to 159 days (median 53 days). Significant levels may persist for weeks/months after cessation [US].
    - **Excretion:** 10% in urine as metabolites; 1%-17% in bile [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F-86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Hazardous drug. Dispose of according to applicable special handling procedures for antineoplastics [US].
