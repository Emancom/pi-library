# Editorial Product Information: Mercaptamine (Cysteamine) Hydrochloride

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinically relevant resource.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mercaptamine (cysteamine) hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Cystine-depleting agent.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Cystadrops** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** S01XA21.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile eye drop solution containing 3.8 mg/mL of mercaptamine (as hydrochloride) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Ocular (topical).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Recordati Rare Diseases Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Treatment of corneal cystine deposits in adults and children with cystinosis [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Children ≥ 2 years):** 
    - The standard dose is one drop in each affected eye, four times daily during waking hours [Australian].
    - A recommended interval of approximately four hours should be maintained between instillations [Australian].
- **Titration:**
    - The dose may be progressively reduced based on ophthalmic findings (e.g., cystine crystal density, photophobia) to a minimum of one drop per eye daily [Australian].
    - Maximum dose: 4 drops per eye per day [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - To prevent "sticky eyes" in the morning, the final dose of the day should be applied at least 30 minutes before sleep [Australian].
    - Patients should bring the solution to room temperature before the first use to facilitate administration [Australian].
    - If multiple topical ocular medications are used, allow a 10-minute interval between applications; eye ointments should be applied last [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, treatment should continue with the next scheduled instillation; doses should not be doubled [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to mercaptamine (cysteamine) or any excipients (including benzalkonium chloride) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Benzalkonium Chloride (Preservative):** This product contains benzalkonium chloride, which may cause eye irritation. It is known to cause punctate and/or toxic ulcerative keratopathy; clinical monitoring is required [Australian].
- **Contact Lenses:** Benzalkonium chloride can discolour soft contact lenses. Lenses must be removed before instillation and may be re-inserted 15 minutes after administration [Australian].
- **Contamination:** To avoid contamination, patients must not touch the dropper tip to the eye, eyelids, or any other surfaces [Australian].
- **Visual Impairment:** Temporary blurred vision (typically lasting less than 1 minute) may occur immediately after instillation. Patients should not drive or operate machinery until vision is clear [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The majority of adverse reactions are transient and mild-to-moderate in severity.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Eye pain, blurred vision, eye irritation, ocular hyperaemia, eye pruritus, increased lacrimation, ocular deposits, instillation site discomfort (primarily sticky eyes/eyelashes) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abnormal sensation in the eye, dry eye, foreign body sensation, eyelid oedema, eyelid irritation, visual impairment, hordeolum, instillation site pain |

[Australian]

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal interaction studies have been performed.
- **Systemic Interactions:** Clinically significant interactions with oral medications are not anticipated, as the total daily ocular dose is approximately 0.4% of the highest recommended oral dose of cysteamine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - There are no adequate data on use in pregnant women. Animal studies using high oral doses (up to 800 times the human ocular dose) showed embryofetotoxicity and teratogenicity (including cleft palate, kyphosis, and heart defects) [Australian].
    - Treatment should be reconsidered if pregnancy is planned or confirmed. Ocular mercaptamine should only be used if the potential benefit justifies the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if mercaptamine is excreted in human milk. Due to animal data showing reduced offspring survival following high-dose oral administration, women using **Cystadrops** concurrently with oral cysteamine should not breastfeed [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - Approved for use in children from 2 years of age at the same dose as adults [Australian].
    - Safety and efficacy in children under 2 years of age have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed; no age-specific dose adjustments provided in available sources.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, systemic exposure is negligible [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Mercaptamine acts as a cystine-depleting agent. It reacts with cystine to form cysteine and a cysteine-mercaptamine mixed disulfide, both of which can exit the lysosomes of corneal cells, thereby reducing crystal accumulation [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Human PK assessments following ocular administration have not been performed. While systemic absorption may occur, the exposure is estimated to be less than 0.4% of that following oral administration [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **OCT-1 Study:** A 5-year single-arm study (n=8) showed a 30% average decrease in corneal cystine crystals (IVCM score) within 30 days, which was maintained with a median dosing of 3 drops/eye/day [Australian].
- **CHOC Study:** A 90-day randomised, controlled trial (n=32) compared **Cystadrops** (4 drops/day) to a 0.10% cysteamine formulation. **Cystadrops** demonstrated a 40% mean decrease in crystal density (p<0.0001) and superior improvement in photophobia [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Before First Opening:** Store in a refrigerator (2°C to 8°C). Keep the vial in the outer carton to protect from light [Australian].
- **After Opening:** Store below 25°C. Do not refrigerate. Discard the dropper bottle 7 days after the first opening [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
