# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the provided regulatory information into a clinically relevant reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects (e.g., glutaric aciduria II, methyl malonic aciduria, propionic acidemia, medium-chain fatty acylCoA dehydrogenase deficiency) that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Dialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    *   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    *   **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    *   **Maintenance:** Frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    *   **Maximum Dose:** Daily doses generally range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised.
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    *   **Administration Timing:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
*   **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L).
*   **Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before use.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags.

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If severe, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without careful risk-benefit assessment [US].
*   **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
*   **Renal Impairment (Oral formulation note):** While this document concerns the IV formulation, it is noted that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event profiles differ significantly between the primary metabolic disorder population and the ESRD population due to the confounding effects of underlying disease.

### General Reactions
*   **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting, stomach cramps, and gastritis.
*   **Specific:** A "fishy" body odor has been reported [US].

### ESRD Dialysis Population (Events ≥5%)
| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as Whole** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), flu syndrome (25–29%), headache (22%), infection (15–24%), abdominal pain (5–21%), fever (5–12%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), chest pain (6–15%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) |
| **Metabolic/Nutr.** | Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when co-administered with levocarnitine.
*   **Management:** Monitor INR levels at therapy initiation and following any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C]. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of fetal harm. No adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women exist. Use only if clearly needed.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine should be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug based on clinical priority [US].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for the management of metabolic disorders causing secondary carnitine deficiency. Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Dose adjustments in ESRD are based on pre-dialysis trough levels to prevent excessive accumulation.
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
*   **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
*   **Dialysis:** Levocarnitine is readily removed from plasma by hemodialysis.

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. 
*   **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C.
*   **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
*   **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.
