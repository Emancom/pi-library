# Editorial Product Information: Miglitol

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial is derived from the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI): United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of miglitol for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Miglitol (an alpha-glucosidase inhibitor; desoxynojirimycin derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Miglitol Tablets [US].
- **ATC Code:** A10BF02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Tablets:** 25 mg, 50 mg, and 100 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured by Orient Pharma Co., Ltd. (Taiwan); Distributed by Westminster Pharmaceuticals, LLC; Repackaged/Relabeled by Proficient Rx LP [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus:** Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults with non-insulin-dependent (Type 2) diabetes mellitus [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** There is no fixed dosage; the regimen must be individualized based on effectiveness and patient tolerance [US].
- **Initial Dosage:** 25 mg taken orally three times daily at the start (first bite) of each main meal. To minimize gastrointestinal side effects, some patients may begin with 25 mg once daily, gradually increasing to three times daily [US].
- **Maintenance and Titration:**
    - After 4–8 weeks at 25 mg TID, the dose should be increased to 50 mg TID for approximately three months. 
    - If HbA1c remains unsatisfactory, the dose may be further increased to a maximum of 100 mg TID [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** 100 mg three times daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be taken at the start of each main meal [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Diabetic ketoacidosis** [US].
- **Inflammatory bowel disease**, colonic ulceration, or partial intestinal obstruction [US].
- **Predisposition to intestinal obstruction** [US].
- **Chronic intestinal diseases** associated with marked disorders of digestion or absorption, or conditions that may worsen due to increased gas formation in the intestine [US].
- **Hypersensitivity** to the drug or any of its components [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypoglycemia Management:** Miglitol monotherapy does not cause hypoglycemia. However, when used with sulfonylureas or insulin, the risk of hypoglycemia increases. **Important:** Because miglitol delays the absorption of sucrose (cane sugar), oral **glucose (dextrose)** must be used to treat mild-to-moderate hypoglycemia. Sucrose is ineffective for rapid reversal [US].
- **Loss of Control of Blood Glucose:** Temporary insulin therapy may be required during periods of physiological stress (e.g., fever, trauma, infection, or surgery) [US].
- **Macrovascular Outcomes:** No clinical studies have established conclusive evidence of macrovascular risk reduction with miglitol [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Miglitol is not recommended for patients with significant renal dysfunction (serum creatinine >2.0 mg/dL) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal (Very Common >10%):** Flatulence (41.5%), diarrhea (28.7%), and abdominal pain (11.7%). These symptoms are dose-related, typically occur during the first few weeks of therapy, and tend to diminish over time [US].
- **Dermatologic (Common 1–10%):** Skin rash (4.3%) [US].
- **Abnormal Laboratory Findings:** Low serum iron (9.2% of patients), though typically not associated with anemia or persistent decreases in hemoglobin [US].
- **Postmarketing Reports (Serious/Rare):**
    - Ileus (including paralytic ileus) and subileus [US].
    - **Pneumatosis Cystoides Intestinalis:** Rare reports; may present with diarrhea, mucus discharge, or rectal bleeding. Complications may include intestinal obstruction or perforation. If suspected, miglitol should be discontinued immediately [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Digoxin:** May reduce average plasma concentrations of digoxin by 19–28% in healthy volunteers, though no significant change was noted in patients with diabetes [US].
- **Ranitidine and Propranolol:** Miglitol significantly reduces the bioavailability of ranitidine (by 60%) and propranolol (by 40%) [US].
- **Glyburide:** A trend toward lower AUC and peak levels was observed, though not statistically significant in all trials [US].
- **Intestinal Adsorbents/Digestive Enzymes:** Agents such as charcoal or enzyme preparations (amylase, pancreatin) may reduce the efficacy of miglitol and should not be taken concomitantly [US].
- **Metformin:** Minor (non-significant) reduction in metformin levels when co-administered [US].
- **No Interaction:** No clinically significant interactions observed with antacids, warfarin, or nifedipine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B [US]:** No evidence of fetal malformations in animal studies at doses up to 10–12 times the human exposure. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Miglitol is excreted in human milk to a very small degree (approx. 0.02% of a 100 mg maternal dose). While infant exposure is low (approx. 0.4%), it is recommended that miglitol not be administered to nursing women [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no untoward effects on reproductive performance or fertility at doses up to 8 times the maximum human exposure [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness observed between patients over 65 and younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Plasma levels increase proportionally with the degree of renal dysfunction. Treatment is **not recommended** for patients with a creatinine clearance <25 mL/min [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No influence on miglitol kinetics is expected as the drug is not metabolized; no adjustment required in cirrhotic patients [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Reversible inhibition of membrane-bound intestinal $\alpha$-glucoside hydrolase enzymes. This delays the digestion of carbohydrates and the absorption of glucose, resulting in a reduction of postprandial hyperglycemia [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Saturable; 25 mg is completely absorbed, while 100 mg is 50–70% absorbed. Peak concentrations reached in 2–3 hours. Systemic absorption is not required for therapeutic effect [US].
    - **Distribution:** Negligible protein binding (<4.0%); volume of distribution is 0.18 L/kg [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Not metabolized in humans; no metabolites detected [US].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted primarily unchanged by the kidneys. Elimination half-life is approximately 2 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy:** Clinical trials (U.S. and non-U.S.) demonstrated that miglitol (50–100 mg TID) significantly reduced HbA1c and postprandial glucose compared to placebo. One-year study showed a treatment effect on HbA1c of -0.58% at 50 mg TID [US].
- **Combination Therapy:** When added to sulfonylureas (e.g., glyburide), miglitol produced an additional reduction in HbA1c (ranging from -0.30% to -0.82%) compared to sulfonylurea monotherapy. The effect is additive because miglitol does not enhance insulin secretion [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C (59°F–86°F) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified in available sources.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison/Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
