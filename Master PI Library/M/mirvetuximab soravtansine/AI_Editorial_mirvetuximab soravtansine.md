# Editorial Product Information: Mirvetuximab Soravtansine

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by integrating the official regulatory guidance from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). This editorial synthesis aims to provide a comprehensive reference for healthcare professionals by combining the most conservative and detailed safety and administration data from both agencies. Where information is specific to a single jurisdiction, it is noted as [TGA] or [FDA].

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** ELAHERE  
**Active Ingredient:** Mirvetuximab soravtansine (referred to as *mirvetuximab soravtansine-gynx* in the United States [FDA]).

ELAHERE is a folate receptor alpha (FRα)-directed antibody-drug conjugate (ADC). It consists of a chimeric IgG1 monoclonal antibody covalently linked via a cleavable sulfo-SPDB linker to DM4, a potent antimitotic maytansinoid and microtubule inhibitor. The drug-to-antibody ratio (DAR) is approximately 3.4:1.

---

## 2. Approved Indications
ELAHERE is indicated as monotherapy for the treatment of adult patients with folate receptor-alpha (FRα) positive, platinum-resistant high-grade serous epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer. 

**Patient Selection:**
*   Eligible patients must have received one to three prior systemic treatment regimens.
*   FRα positivity must be confirmed using a validated, regulatory-approved test. 
*   [TGA]: Definition of positivity is ≥75% of viable tumor cells demonstrating moderate (2+) or strong (3+) membrane staining by immunohistochemistry (IHC).

---

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known severe hypersensitivity to mirvetuximab soravtansine or any of the inactive ingredients (including glacial acetic acid, polysorbate 20, sodium acetate, and sucrose) [TGA].
*   [FDA]: The US Prescribing Information lists no specific contraindications.

---

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Ocular Toxicity [Boxed Warning - FDA]
Severe ocular adverse reactions are common, including visual impairment, keratopathy (corneal disorders), dry eye, photophobia, eye pain, and uveitis. 
*   **Monitoring:** Conduct an ophthalmic examination (visual acuity and slit-lamp) prior to initiation. Follow-up exams should occur every other cycle for the first eight cycles [FDA] and thereafter as clinically indicated.
*   **Prophylaxis:** Patients must use prophylactic artificial tears and ophthalmic topical steroids as directed (see *Dosage and Administration*).
*   **Contact Lenses:** Patients should avoid wearing contact lenses during treatment unless specifically cleared by an eye care professional.

### Pneumonitis
Severe, life-threatening, or fatal interstitial lung disease (ILD) or pneumonitis can occur. Monitor for new or worsening pulmonary symptoms (e.g., dyspnea, cough, hypoxia). Withhold treatment for Grade 2 pneumonitis until resolution; permanently discontinue for Grade 3 or 4.

### Peripheral Neuropathy
Patients should be monitored for symptoms of neuropathy, such as paresthesia, tingling, burning sensations, or muscle weakness. Dose modification or discontinuation may be required based on severity.

### Embryo-Fetal Toxicity
Based on its mechanism of action and the genotoxic nature of the DM4 payload, ELAHERE can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before initiation.

## 5. Dosage and Administration

### Recommended Dosage
The standard dose is **6 mg/kg** based on **Adjusted Ideal Body Weight (AIBW)**, administered intravenously every 3 weeks (21-day cycle). Treatment continues until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

**AIBW Calculation:**
1.  **Female IBW (kg)** = 0.9 × height [cm] − 92
2.  **AIBW (kg)** = IBW + 0.4 × (Actual weight − IBW)

### Required Premedication
Administer at least 30 minutes prior to infusion:
*   **Intravenous Corticosteroid:** (e.g., dexamethasone 10 mg)
*   **Antihistamine:** (e.g., diphenhydramine 25-50 mg)
*   **Antipyretic:** (e.g., paracetamol/acetaminophen 325-650 mg)
*   **Antiemetic:** (e.g., 5-HT3 receptor antagonist)

### Mandatory Eye Care Regimen
*   **Topical Steroids:** Administer one drop in each eye 6 times daily starting the day before infusion until Day 4, then 4 times daily for Days 5-8 of each cycle [FDA/TGA].
*   **Lubricating Eye Drops:** Use at least 4 times daily throughout treatment. Wait 10–15 minutes after steroid drops before applying lubricants.

### Administration Instructions
*   **Dilution:** Must be diluted in **5% Glucose (Dextrose)** to a final concentration of 1 mg/mL to 2 mg/mL. **Incompatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride (Normal Saline).**
*   **Filter:** Use a 0.2 or 0.22 µm polyethersulfone (PES) in-line filter.
*   **Infusion Rate:** Start at 1 mg/min. If well-tolerated after 30 minutes, increase to 3 mg/min; after another 30 minutes, increase to a maximum of 5 mg/min.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is characterized by gastrointestinal, ocular, and constitutional symptoms.

*   **Most Common (≥20%):** Blurred vision, fatigue, nausea, diarrhea, abdominal pain, keratopathy, peripheral neuropathy, musculoskeletal pain, and dry eye.
*   **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Increased AST, ALT, and alkaline phosphatase; decreased lymphocytes, platelets, hemoglobin, magnesium, and albumin.
*   **Serious Reactions:** Pneumonitis, small intestinal obstruction, and pleural effusion.

---

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** DM4 is a substrate of CYP3A4. Concomitant use with strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin, ritonavir) may increase exposure to unconjugated DM4, raising the risk of toxicity. Monitor patients closely if inhibitors cannot be avoided.
*   **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** May decrease exposure to unconjugated DM4.

---

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Category D [TGA]. Advise pregnant women of the potential risk to the fetus.
*   **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for **7 months** after the final dose.
*   **Lactation:** It is unknown if mirvetuximab soravtansine passes into breast milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for **1 month** following the last dose.

---

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** The steady-state volume of distribution is approximately 2.6 L. Plasma protein binding of DM4 is >99%.
*   **Metabolism:** The antibody component is catabolized into small peptides. The payload (DM4) is metabolized primarily by CYP3A4.
*   **Elimination:** Total plasma clearance is approximately 19 mL/hour. The terminal half-life of the ADC is approximately 4.8 to 4.9 days.
*   **Excretion:** DM4 and its metabolites are primarily eliminated via biliary excretion in the feces.

---

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Mirvetuximab soravtansine targets cells expressing Folate Receptor Alpha (FRα). Upon binding to the extracellular domain of FRα, the ADC-receptor complex is internalized via endocytosis. Once inside the cell, the DM4 payload is released through proteolytic cleavage of the linker. DM4 then binds to tubulin, disrupting the microtubule network, which leads to cell cycle arrest in the G2/M phase and subsequent apoptotic cell death.

---

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Vials:** Store in the original carton to protect from light. Keep refrigerated at **2°C to 8°C**. Do not freeze or shake.
*   **Diluted Solution:** If not used immediately, store for no more than 8 hours at room temperature (18°C to 25°C) including infusion time, or up to 24 hours under refrigeration (2°C to 8°C). Allow refrigerated bags to reach room temperature before administration.
