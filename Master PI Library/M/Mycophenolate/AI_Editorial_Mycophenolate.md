# Editorial Product Information: Mycophenolate Mofetil

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of mycophenolate mofetil for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory requirements, and pharmacological profiles into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing and dispensing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved documentation for specific legal and regulatory compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF), the 2-morpholinoethyl ester of mycophenolic acid (MPA).
- **Chemical Class:** Selective immunosuppressive agent; Inosine Monophosphate Dehydrogenase (IMPDH) inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Alcept** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AA06.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Powder for oral suspension: 1 g/5 mL (after reconstitution) [Australian].
    - *Note: Other formulations (capsules, tablets, and IV) are available but were not the primary focus of the provided source document.*
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral (after reconstitution).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pharmacor Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Adults:** Prophylaxis of solid organ rejection in patients receiving allogeneic organ transplants [Australian].
- **Paediatrics (2 to 18 years):** Prophylaxis of organ rejection in patients receiving allogeneic renal transplants [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Renal Transplantation:** 1 g administered twice daily (2 g total daily dose).
- **Cardiac Transplantation:** 1.5 g administered twice daily (3 g total daily dose).
- **Hepatic Transplantation:** Initially 1 g intravenously twice daily (2 g/day), followed by 1.5 g orally twice daily (3 g/day) as soon as tolerated.

### Paediatric Dosing (2 to 18 years)
- **Renal Transplantation:** 600 mg/m² administered orally twice daily, up to a maximum daily dose of 2 g.

### Administration and Preparation
- **Timing:** The initial dose should be given as soon as possible following transplantation.
- **Reconstitution (Oral Suspension):** 
    - Reconstitution should be performed by a pharmacist prior to dispensing.
    - Tap the bottle to loosen the powder. Add 94 mL of purified water in two stages, shaking for approximately 1 minute after each addition.
    - Final deliverable volume is 165 mL.
- **Handling:** It is recommended to wear disposable gloves during reconstitution and while wiping the bottle. Avoid inhalation or direct contact of the powder or suspension with skin/mucous membranes.
- **Nasogastic Administration:** May be administered via a nasogastric tube (minimum interior diameter 1.7 mm).

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to mycophenolate mofetil, mycophenolic acid, or any excipient (e.g., aspartame) [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated due to high mutagenic and teratogenic potential [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Contraindicated in women of childbearing potential not using highly effective contraceptive methods [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated as MPA may be excreted in human milk [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neoplasms:** Increased risk of lymphomas and other malignancies, particularly skin cancers. Patients should limit UV exposure.
- **Infections:** Increased susceptibility to opportunistic infections, fatal infections, and sepsis. This includes latent viral reactivation (Hepatitis B/C) and Progressive Multifocal Leukoencephalopathy (PML) associated with the JC virus.
- **Blood and Immune System:**
    - **Neutropenia:** Monitor for neutropenia. If ANC < 1.3 x 10⁹/L, dose should be interrupted or reduced.
    - **Pure Red Cell Aplasia (PRCA):** Cases have been reported; dose reduction or cessation may be required.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Use with caution in patients with active, serious digestive system disease due to risks of ulceration, haemorrhage, and perforation.
- **HGPRT Deficiency:** Avoid use in patients with Lesch-Nyhan or Kelley-Seegmiller syndrome.
- **Vaccinations:** Live attenuated vaccines must be avoided; other vaccinations may be less effective.
- **Donation:** Patients should not donate blood during therapy or for 6 weeks post-discontinuation. Men should not donate semen during or for 90 days post-discontinuation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often complicated by underlying disease and concurrent immunosuppressants (e.g., ciclosporin, steroids).

### Frequency Data (Renal Transplant)
| Adverse Effect | Frequency (MMF 2g/day) | Frequency (MMF 3g/day) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Diarrhoea** | 17.9% | 23.3% |
| **Leucopenia** | 19.0% | 31.2% |
| **Sepsis** | 12.5% | 12.7% |
| **Urinary Tract Infection** | 13.4% | 11.5% |
| **Abdominal Pain** | 13.4% | 12.1% |
| **Nausea** | 9.5% | 12.1% |
| **Anemia** | 6.0% | 4.8% |

### Serious/Other Notable Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Vomiting, constipation, dyspepsia, oral moniliasis.
- **Common (1-10%):** Gastritis, flatulence, cough, dizziness, insomnia, tremor, acne.
- **Post-Marketing:** Bronchiectasis, interstitial lung disease, and de novo purine synthesis inhibitor-associated acute inflammatory syndrome (fever, arthralgia, elevated inflammatory markers).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Acyclovir/Ganciclovir:** Potential for competition for renal tubular secretion, increasing concentrations of both drugs, especially in renal impairment.
- **Antacids (Mg/Al Hydroxide) & PPIs:** Reduced absorption and systemic exposure (AUC) of MPA.
- **Colestyramine:** Reduces MPA AUC by approximately 40% due to interference with enterohepatic recirculation.
- **Ciclosporin:** Decreases MPA exposure by 30-50%. Switching from ciclosporin to tacrolimus/sirolimus may significantly increase MPA levels.
- **Tacrolimus:** No significant effect on MPA AUC in hepatic patients, but MMF may increase tacrolimus AUC by ~20%.
- **Azathioprine:** Concomitant administration is not recommended due to potential for additive bone marrow suppression.
- **Antibiotics:** Those eliminating $\beta$-glucuronidase-producing bacteria (e.g., ciprofloxacin, amoxicillin/clavulanic acid) can reduce MPA trough levels by up to 54%.
- **Sevelamer:** May decrease MPA $C_{max}$ and AUC; should be administered 2 hours after MMF.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category D** [Australian].
- **Teratogenicity:** MMF is a major human teratogen. Spontaneous abortion rates are 45-49% (vs 12-33% for other immunosuppressants). Congenital malformations occur in 23-27% of live births.
- **Malformations:** Includes abnormalities of the ear, eye (coloboma), face (cleft lip/palate), fingers, heart, and nervous system (spina bifida).
- **Contraception:** Females of childbearing potential require two forms of reliable contraception simultaneously before, during, and for 6 weeks after therapy. Males require condom use during and for 90 days after therapy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated; MPA is excreted in milk, and serious adverse reactions in infants are possible.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for renal transplants in patients aged 2-18 years. Safety/efficacy in children < 2 years is not established.
- **Elderly (>65 years):** Increased risk of infections, GI haemorrhage, and pulmonary oedema. No specific dose adjustment is required, but caution is advised.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Severe Chronic Impairment (GFR < 25 mL/min/1.73m²):** Avoid doses > 1 g twice daily outside the immediate post-transplant period.
    - **Delayed Graft Function:** No dose adjustment needed, but monitor closely due to increased MPAG levels.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment needed for renal transplant patients with hepatic parenchymal disease. Data are lacking for cardiac transplant patients with hepatic disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** MPA is a selective, reversible, uncompetitive inhibitor of IMPDH. It inhibits the *de novo* pathway of guanosine nucleotide synthesis. Because T- and B-lymphocytes are critically dependent on this pathway for proliferation (unlike other cells which use salvage pathways), MMF has potent cytostatic effects on lymphocytes.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and extensive; bioavailability ~94%. Food reduces $C_{max}$ by 40% but does not change AUC.
    - **Distribution:** 97% protein bound to albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by glucuronyl transferase (UGT1A9) to inactive MPAG. MPAG undergoes enterohepatic recirculation, where it is converted back to MPA.
    - **Elimination:** ~93% excreted in urine, mostly as MPAG. Half-life is approximately 17.9 hours (oral).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Renal Rejection:** Three multicentre trials confirmed MMF (2 g or 3 g daily) was superior to azathioprine or placebo in reducing biopsy-proven rejection or treatment failure within the first 6 months.
- **Refractory Renal Rejection:** MMF (3 g/day) demonstrated a 45% reduction in graft loss or death compared to high-dose IV steroids.
- **Cardiac/Hepatic Rejection:** Trials showed equivalence or superiority in preventing rejection compared to azathioprine-based regimens.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Powder for Suspension:** Store below 25°C.
- **Reconstituted Suspension:** Store below 25°C or refrigerate (2-8°C). Do not freeze.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused suspension 60 days after reconstitution.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
