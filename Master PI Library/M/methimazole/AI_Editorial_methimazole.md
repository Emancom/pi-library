# Editorial Product Information: Methimazole

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**.
- At the time of compilation, PI documents from other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) were not provided for comparison.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing and dispensing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methimazole (1-methylimidazole-2-thiol).
- **Chemical Class:** Antithyroid agent (imidazole derivative).
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources; listed as generic Methimazole [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Oral tablets: 5 mg and 10 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** RemedyRepack Inc. (Repackager); Avet Pharmaceuticals Inc. (Manufacturer) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
Methimazole is indicated for the following [US]:
- **Hyperthyroidism in Graves’ Disease:** For patients with Graves' disease or toxic multinodular goiter where surgery or radioactive iodine therapy is not appropriate or is contraindicated.
- **Pre-treatment/Preparation:** To ameliorate symptoms of hyperthyroidism in preparation for surgical thyroidectomy or radioactive iodine therapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The total daily dose is typically administered in three equal portions at approximately 8-hour intervals [US].
- **Initial Dose (based on severity):**
    - Mild hyperthyroidism: 15 mg daily.
    - Moderately severe hyperthyroidism: 30 to 40 mg daily.
    - Severe hyperthyroidism: 60 mg daily.
- **Maintenance Dose:** 5 to 15 mg daily [US].

### Pediatric Dosing
- **Initial Dose:** 0.4 mg/kg of body weight daily, divided into three doses given at 8-hour intervals.
- **Maintenance Dose:** Approximately half (50%) of the initial dose [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Clinical resolution of hyperthyroidism may allow for a reduction to maintenance dosing. 
- A rising serum TSH level is an indicator that a lower maintenance dose should be employed [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to methimazole or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Critical Warnings
- **Congenital Malformations:** Methimazole crosses the placenta and can cause fetal harm when used in the first trimester. Documented defects include aplasia cutis, craniofacial malformations (choanal atresia, facial dysmorphism), and gastrointestinal malformations (esophageal atresia, omphalocele). If used during the first trimester, the lowest possible dose should be utilized [US].
- **Agranulocytosis:** A potentially life-threatening reaction. Patients must be instructed to report fever or sore throat immediately. Treatment must be discontinued if agranulocytosis or aplastic anemia (pancytopenia) occurs [US].
- **Liver Toxicity:** Hepatotoxicity, including acute liver failure, has been reported. Discontinue immediately if there is clinically significant evidence of liver abnormality, including transaminases exceeding three times the upper limit of normal (3x ULN) [US].

### General Precautions
- **Vasculitis:** Cases of ANCA-positive vasculitis (including acute kidney injury, pulmonary hemorrhage, and CNS vasculitis) have been reported. Discontinue drug and initiate appropriate therapy if suspected [US].
- **Hypothyroidism:** Routine monitoring of TSH and free T4 is required; dose adjustments are necessary to maintain a euthyroid state [US].
- **Hypoprothrombinemia:** Methimazole may cause vitamin K inhibition and bleeding. Monitor prothrombin time (PT/INR), particularly before surgical procedures [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorized as "major" (less frequent) or "minor" [US].

| Category | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Major (Serious)** | Inhibition of myelopoiesis (agranulocytosis, granulocytopenia, thrombocytopenia, aplastic anemia), drug fever, lupus-like syndrome, insulin autoimmune syndrome (hypoglycemic coma), hepatitis, periarteritis, hypoprothrombinemia, and ANCA-positive vasculitis. |
| **Minor** | Skin rash, urticaria, nausea, vomiting, epigastric distress, arthralgia, paresthesia, loss of taste, alopecia, myalgia, headache, pruritus, drowsiness, neuritis, edema, vertigo, skin pigmentation, jaundice, and lymphadenopathy. |

*Note: Acute pancreatitis has been reported in postmarketing surveillance [US].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Oral Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** Methimazole may increase anticoagulant activity via potential inhibition of vitamin K. Monitor PT/INR closely, especially before surgery [US].
- **Beta-Adrenergic Blocking Agents:** The clearance of beta-blockers may increase in hyperthyroidism. A dose reduction of beta-blockers may be necessary when the patient becomes euthyroid [US].
- **Digitalis Glycosides:** Serum levels may increase as the patient becomes euthyroid; a dose reduction of digitalis may be required [US].
- **Theophylline:** Clearance of theophylline may decrease as the patient becomes euthyroid; dose reductions may be necessary [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Methimazole is associated with rare congenital malformations in the first trimester. Because propylthiouracil (PTU) is associated with higher liver toxicity risks, methimazole may be the preferred agent for the second and third trimesters [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Methimazole is excreted in breast milk. While studies have shown no significant effect on the clinical status of the nursing infant, the infant's thyroid function should be monitored at frequent intervals (weekly or biweekly) [US].
- **Fertility:** Rat studies showed thyroid hyperplasia and carcinoma at high doses, but the clinical significance for human fertility is unclear [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Methimazole is the preferred antithyroid drug in children due to the risk of severe liver injury associated with propylthiouracil (PTU) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution due to the risk of drug-induced hepatotoxicity; discontinue if significant liver abnormalities occur [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Methimazole inhibits the synthesis of thyroid hormones. It does not inactivate existing thyroxine (T4) or triiodothyronine (T3) stored in the thyroid or circulating in the blood [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Readily absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolized in the liver.
    - **Elimination:** Excreted in the urine [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F and 86°F).
- **Handling:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers [US].
