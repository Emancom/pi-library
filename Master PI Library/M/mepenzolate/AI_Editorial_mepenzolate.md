# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial product information was developed using the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused editorial overview for healthcare professionals.
- This document is a synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI when prescribing in a specific region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (distributed by Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Sterile solution for intravenous use; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses over 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - Adjust subsequent daily doses based on clinical response and plasma carnitine levels. The highest recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - Administration should occur as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Therapy may be initiated if trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels are below the normal range of 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L.
    - Dose adjustments should be guided by trough levels; downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be considered as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- For infusion, levocarnitine is compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature (25°C) when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical management. Caution is advised when considering re-administration to these patients [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients receiving levocarnitine, including those without a prior history of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral formulation caution):** While this document concerns the intravenous form, clinicians should note that the safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events occurring at a frequency ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials include:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), asthenia (8–12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), arrhythmia (3%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).

### Post-marketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis have been observed less frequently [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified in available sources]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits using doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake to the infant must be weighed against the benefit to the mother; discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, S. cerevisiae, S. pombe) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 for dosing. Secondary deficiency in premature infants and newborns is defined as plasma levels below age-related normal concentrations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Dosing is adjusted based on pre-dialysis trough levels (See Section 3).

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis), mean trough levocarnitine levels increased from ~20 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes (e.g., malaise, muscle weakness) in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat and protect from freezing. Vials do not contain preservatives; discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

- **United States:** Rx-only.
