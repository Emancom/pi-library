# Editorial Product Information: Migalastat

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of migalastat for healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinical reference and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 
- **Prescriber Notice:** This is an editorial synthesis. Clinicians should consult the current, local PI for their specific region to ensure compliance with local regulatory requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Migalastat (present as migalastat hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Iminosugar; pharmacological chaperone.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Galafold** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AX14.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Size 2 hard capsules containing 123 mg of migalastat (equivalent to 150 mg migalastat hydrochloride) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amicus Therapeutics Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Fabry Disease:** Indicated for the long-term treatment of adults and adolescents (aged 12 years and older) with a confirmed diagnosis of Fabry disease (α-galactosidase A deficiency) who possess an **amenable mutation** [Australian].
- **Weight Requirement:** In the adolescent population (12 to <18 years), the indication is restricted to those weighing ≥45 kg [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Dosing:** The recommended dose is 123 mg (one capsule) taken orally once every other day at the same time of day [Australian].
- **Dosing Frequency:** Migalastat must not be taken on two consecutive days [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole; they must not be crushed, cut, or chewed.
    - **Fasting Requirement:** To ensure adequate absorption, patients must fast for at least 2 hours before and 2 hours after taking the dose (creating a minimum 4-hour fasting window). During this window, only plain/flavoured/sweetened water, fruit juices without pulp, and caffeine-free carbonated beverages are permitted [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken only if it is within 12 hours of the normal administration time. If more than 12 hours have passed, the missed dose should be skipped, and the patient should resume the every-other-day schedule at the next planned dosing day and time [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to migalastat or any of the inactive ingredients (including gelatin, titanium dioxide, and indigo carmine) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Amenability Requirement:** Migalastat is only effective in patients with mutations confirmed as "amenable." It is not indicated for non-amenable mutations [Australian].
- **Concomitant Therapy:** Migalastat is not intended for use in combination with enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to monitor renal function, echocardiographic parameters (e.g., LVMi), and biochemical markers every 6 months [Australian].
- **Clinical Deterioration:** Treatment should be re-evaluated if there is a meaningful decline in clinical status.
- **Proteinuria:** Migalastat has not been shown to reduce proteinuria [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally mild to moderate. The frequency data provided is based on pooled clinical trial results [Australian].

### Very Common (≥10%)
- **Nervous System:** Headache (10.4%).

### Common (1% to 10%)
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness, paraesthesia, hypoaesthesia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea, nausea, abdominal pain, constipation, dry mouth, dyspepsia.
- **Musculoskeletal:** Muscle spasms, myalgia, pain in extremity, torticollis.
- **Psychiatric:** Depression.
- **General:** Fatigue, pain, peripheral oedema.
- **Respiratory:** Dyspnoea, epistaxis.
- **Renal:** Proteinuria.
- **Investigations:** Increased blood creatine phosphokinase, weight gain.

### Post-marketing Experience
- **Skin and Subcutaneous Tissue:** Angioedema (frequency not known) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Caffeine:** Co-administration with caffeine significantly decreases migalastat exposure (AUC reduced by ~55%; Cmax by ~60%), potentially reducing efficacy. Caffeine intake must be avoided for 2 hours before and after dosing [Australian].
- **Enzyme Replacement Therapy (ERT):** Co-administration may increase agalsidase exposure up to 5-fold. Concurrent use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Transporters and Enzymes:** Based on *in vitro* data, migalastat is not an inducer or inhibitor of major CYP450 enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 3A4, etc.) and is not a substrate/inhibitor of major efflux transporters (MDR1, BCRP) or uptake transporters (OATP, OAT, OCT, MATE) [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3** [Australian]. Data in pregnant women are limited. Animal studies (rabbits) showed embryo-foetal toxicity (death, reduced weight) only at maternally toxic doses (≥240 times clinical exposure). Migalastat is not recommended during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Contraception:** Use is not recommended in women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Migalastat is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. A risk to the infant cannot be excluded. A decision should be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or the drug, weighing the benefits of nursing against the maternal benefit of therapy [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Transient, fully reversible infertility was observed in male rats at subclinical exposures; no effect was noted in female rats. Human data are absent [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - **Adolescents (12–18 years):** Approved for those weighing ≥45 kg.
    - **Children (<12 years):** Safety and efficacy not established; no data available [Australian].
    - **Weight cutoff:** Not suitable for adolescents weighing <45 kg [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required based on age [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - **Mild to Moderate:** No dose adjustment is specified for GFR ≥30 mL/min/1.73 m².
    - **Severe:** Not recommended for use in patients with severe renal insufficiency (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Migalastat is a pharmacological chaperone that selectively and reversibly binds with high affinity to the active sites of amenable mutant forms of the α-galactosidase A (α-Gal A) enzyme. This binding stabilises the protein in the endoplasmic reticulum, facilitating proper trafficking to lysosomes. Once in the lysosome, migalastat dissociates, restoring enzyme activity and promoting the catabolism of substrates like GL-3 and lyso-Gb3 [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment generally leads to increased α-Gal A activity in white blood cells, skin, and kidney tissues, alongside reductions in substrate levels (GL-3) in urine and renal capillaries [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is approximately 75%. Tmax is approximately 3 hours. Food intake (especially high-fat) reduces AUC by 37–42% [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 77–133 L. No detectable plasma protein binding [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Minor pathway via UGT; 99% of plasma radioactivity is unchanged drug.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (77% of dose recovered in urine; 55% as unchanged drug). Elimination half-life is 3 to 5 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **FACETS (ERT-naïve):** A 6-month placebo-controlled trial followed by an 18-month open-label period. Migalastat showed significant reductions in kidney interstitial capillary GL-3 inclusions compared to placebo at 6 months [Australian].
- **ATTRACT (ERT-experienced):** An 18-month active-comparator trial where patients switched from ERT (agalsidase alfa or beta) to migalastat. Results indicated stability in renal function (eGFR) and a statistically significant reduction in Left Ventricular Mass Index (LVMi) [Australian].
- **Long-term Extension:** Follow-up data (up to 8.6 years) suggest stabilisation of renal function and sustained reduction in cardiac hypertrophy [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C in the original package to protect from moisture [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
