- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous (IV) infusion

## 3. Approved Indications
- Control and prevention of haemorrhagic episodes in patients with haemophilia A, including control and prevention of bleeding in surgical settings.
- Does not contain von Willebrand factor and should not be used to treat von Willebrand’s disease.
- Any jurisdictional differences in indications: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General principles
- Treatment should be initiated under the supervision of a physician experienced in the management of haemophilia A.
- Dosage and duration depend on severity of factor VIII deficiency, site and extent of bleeding, and patient clinical condition. Individual response (incremental recovery and half‑life) varies between patients; doses should be titrated to clinical response.
- The IU potency relates to the WHO international standard for factor VIII activity. Empirically, 1 IU of factor VIII per kg body weight raises plasma factor VIII activity by approximately 2 IU/dL (2% of normal).
- Required units (IU) = body weight (kg) × desired factor VIII rise (%) × 0.5

### Dosing by indication
- On‑demand treatment of bleeding:
  - Dose selection per clinical situation; use formula above and titrate to clinical response.
  - In the presence of a factor VIII inhibitor, higher doses or alternative haemostatic therapies may be required.

- Target plasma factor VIII levels and suggested frequency/duration for bleeding and surgery:
  - Minor (early haemarthrosis, superficial muscle/soft tissue, oral bleeds): target 20–40% (IU/dL); repeat every 12–24 hours as necessary until resolved; at least 1 day depending on severity.
  - Moderate (muscle bleeds, mild head trauma, minor operations including tooth extraction): target 30–60%; repeat every 12–24 hours for 3–4 days or until adequate wound healing. For tooth extraction, a single infusion plus oral antifibrinolytic therapy within 1 hour may be sufficient.
  - Major (gastrointestinal, intracranial, intra‑abdominal or intrathoracic haemorrhages, fractures, major operations): target 60–100%; repeat infusion every 8–24 hours until threat resolved or until adequate wound healing; continue therapy for at least another 7 days post-operatively as indicated.

- Prophylaxis:
  - Routine prophylaxis to prevent or reduce spontaneous musculoskeletal haemorrhage: 10–50 IU/kg given at least twice weekly.
  - In a pivotal trial in previously treated adolescents and adults, a prophylactic regimen of 30 ± 5 IU/kg given three times weekly was used.
  - In paediatric patients, shorter intervals or higher doses may be necessary.

### Monitoring-guided dosing and assays
- Clinical monitoring of plasma factor VIII activity is recommended, particularly for surgical procedures.
- The labelled potency is based on a chromogenic substrate assay; however, chromogenic assays may give results up to ~100% higher than one‑stage clotting assays for this product.
- Clinical monitoring during treatment with moroctocog alfa should use the one‑stage clotting assay. Adhere to manufacturers’ assay conditions (incubation/activation times).
- When switching between factor VIII products, individually titrate the dose and monitor plasma factor VIII activity.

### Reconstitution and administration (practical points)
- Reconstitution should follow aseptic technique and the product-specific instructions provided with the medicinal pack.
- The reconstituted product is intended for intravenous infusion and should be given over several minutes; infusion rate should be determined by patient comfort.
- Use the infusion set supplied with the product where provided. If multiple vials/syringes are required, reconstitute each separately as per instructions and combine using appropriate sterile technique.
- The reconstituted solution contains polysorbate 80; this increases DEHP extraction from PVC and should be considered when using PVC containers or infusion sets.

### Dose titration and missed doses
- Titrate dosing to clinical response and laboratory monitoring (plasma factor VIII activity).
- If expected plasma factor VIII activity levels are not attained, evaluate for inhibitor development.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to moroctocog alfa or to any excipient in the formulation.
- Known hypersensitivity to hamster proteins (product contains trace amounts of hamster protein; maximum stated in source data).
- Any jurisdiction‑specific additional contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed warnings
- No jurisdictional boxed warnings stated in available sources. If present in other regions, not specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Inhibitor development: Patients treated with factor VIII products may develop neutralising antibodies (inhibitors) to factor VIII. The risk is highest within the first ~20 exposure days but inhibitors may rarely develop after 100 exposure days. Monitor patients for clinical signs of reduced efficacy and perform laboratory testing if expected factor VIII levels are not reached.
- Less than expected therapeutic effect: Reports exist of less than expected clinical effect without demonstrable inhibitor; monitor clinical response and plasma factor VIII activity and titrate dose accordingly.
- Hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis: Allergic reactions, including severe anaphylaxis and shock, have been reported. Patients should be informed about early signs (e.g., urticaria, hives, chest tightness, wheeze, hypotension). If allergic reaction occurs, stop infusion immediately and institute appropriate emergency measures.
- Trace non‑human protein: The product contains trace hamster protein; there is a remote risk of hypersensitivity to this protein.
- Interchangeability: Potency assignment methods differ between manufacturers; switching products may change protein content per IU. Monitor factor activity and clinical response when switching and individually titrate dose.
- Documentation: When possible, document product name and batch number each time the product is administered (e.g., adhesive vial/syringe labels).

### Monitoring requirements
- Plasma factor VIII activity monitoring using one‑stage clotting assay is recommended.
- Test for inhibitors using Bethesda assay when expected factor VIII activity is not attained or bleeding is not controlled.
- Monitor for signs of hypersensitivity during and after infusion.
- Laboratory monitoring (e.g., liver function tests, antibody assays) as clinically indicated.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use in patients with inhibitors: In patients with high titre inhibitors (>5 Bethesda Units), factor VIII replacement may not be effective; consider bypassing agents or specialist haemophilia centre management.
- Renal and hepatic impairment: Dosage adjustment has not been studied.
- Travel: Patients should carry adequate supply consistent with their regimen and consult healthcare professional before travel.

## 7. Adverse Effects

- Safety data in the available source are derived from a pooled dataset that included clinical studies of moroctocog alfa and experience with its predecessor product; the pooled dataset comprised 765 patients (124 previously untreated patients [PUPs], 641 previously treated patients [PTPs]) in the source material.

- Reported serious adverse reactions include hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis and development of factor VIII inhibitors.

- Specific reported rates and events:
  - Less than expected therapeutic effect: reported at a rate of 0.4% (25/6,404 prophylactic infusions) and 0.5% (1/187 episodes) for on‑demand treatment in clinical trials.
  - Factor VIII inhibitors:
    - Pivotal Phase 3 prophylaxis/on‑demand study in PTPs (subjects with ≥50 exposure days): 2 of 89 subjects developed inhibitors.
    - Supporting pilot experience: 1 de novo and 2 recurrent inhibitor cases in 110 subjects in an earlier supporting study.
    - Predecessor product experience: 1 inhibitor case in 113 subjects (reported for predecessor product).
    - Bayesian analysis of pooled data estimated a 95% upper limit for the true inhibitor rate of 4.2% (deemed acceptable limit 4.4% in the analysis).
    - In a paediatric PTP study (≥20 exposure days; ages 6 months to <16 years), 1 low‑titre, clinically silent inhibitor was observed among 49 patients at risk.
  - Hypersensitivity reactions, including angioedema, urticaria, tightness of chest, wheeze, hypotension and anaphylaxis, have been observed infrequently.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism of action
- **moroctocog alfa** is a recombinant factor VIII replacement product intended to restore factor VIII activity in patients with haemophilia A, thereby supporting formation of a fibrin clot and reducing bleeding.

### Pharmacokinetics / Pharmacodynamics
- Patients show interindividual variability in incremental recovery and half‑life of factor VIII; doses should be individualized.
- The labelled potency is assigned using a chromogenic substrate potency assay calibrated to a manufacturing standard; clinical monitoring using the one‑stage clotting assay is recommended because chromogenic assays can yield higher numerical values for factor VIII activity compared with one‑stage assays for this product.
- Detailed ADME (absorption/distribution/metabolism/excretion) parameters and numerical pharmacokinetic values are not specified in available sources.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal Phase 3 prophylaxis/on‑demand trial in previously treated patients:
  - Design: Pivotal clinical program included previously treated subjects receiving moroctocog alfa for prophylaxis and on‑demand treatment; prophylactic regimen used in a pivotal study was 30 ± 5 IU/kg three times weekly in adolescents and adults.
  - Safety endpoint (inhibitors): Among subjects with ≥50 exposure days in a pivotal study, 2 of 89 subjects developed inhibitors.
  - A Bayesian analysis of pooled data (including prior pilot studies and predecessor product experience) estimated a 95% upper limit for population inhibitor rate of 4.2%.
- Surgical prophylaxis study:
  - In a pivotal surgical prophylaxis study, one low‑titre persistent inhibitor and one transient false‑positive inhibitor were reported.
- Paediatric studies:
  - Previously treated children/adolescents (n=18, age 12–16) were included in a pivotal safety evaluation with adverse events similar in incidence and severity to adults.
  - A paediatric PTP study (≥20 exposure days; ages 6 months to <16 years) reported 1 low‑titre, clinically silent inhibitor among 49 patients at risk.
- Pooled safety dataset:
  - Safety data summarised from multiple studies and pooled datasets comprised 765 patients (124 PUPs and 641 PTPs) in the source materials; adverse events and antibody data were derived from these pooled analyses.
- Trial outcome measures and efficacy data (bleeding control rates, annualised bleeding rates, pharmacokinetic parameter estimates) beyond the dosing regimens and inhibitor incidence summaries are not specified in available sources.
