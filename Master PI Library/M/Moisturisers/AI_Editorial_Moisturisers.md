# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdiction: US (Food and Drug Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (US) regulatory documentation was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Manufacturer: Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - Severe metabolic crisis may require a loading dose followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours). 
    - Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though therapy may require up to 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** 
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administration: Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose Adjustment: Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if concentrations are adequate [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate appropriate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with known seizure history, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before starting therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common Reactions:** Transient nausea and vomiting have been observed. Less frequent reactions include body odor and gastritis [US].
- **Clinical Trial Data (ESRD Patients):** In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (21–35%), chest pain (6–15%), headache (3–37%), and fever (5–12%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), and tachycardia (5–9%).
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), and nausea (5–12%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), and dyspnea (3–11%).
    - **Neurologic:** Dizziness (10–18%) and paresthesia (3–12%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when **levocarnitine** is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of **levocarnitine** or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category B (implied)]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations of **levocarnitine** following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, S. cerevisiae, S. pombe) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg and applies to the paediatric population [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. 
    - In ESRD patients on dialysis, chronic high-dose oral administration may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Levocarnitine** is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic defects or ESRD, **levocarnitine** clears accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism/Excretion:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis, mean trough (pre-dialysis) levels rose from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
