# Editorial Product Information: Magnesium sulfate

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **magnesium sulfate** (as heptahydrate) intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical and regulatory data from the provided source to facilitate rapid clinical reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Magnesium sulfate heptahydrate
- **Chemical Class:** Electrolyte/Anticonvulsant
- **Brand Name(s):** DBL Magnesium Sulfate Concentrated Injection [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** A12CC02
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Concentrated injection: 2.465 g magnesium sulfate heptahydrate in 5 mL (493 mg/mL) [Australian]
    - Ionic concentration: 2 mmol (4 mEq) of magnesium and 2 mmol (4 mEq) of sulfate per mL [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV), Intramuscular (IM)
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Hypomagnesaemia:** Treatment of acute magnesium deficiency [Australian].
- **Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN):** Prevention of magnesium deficiency in patients receiving total parenteral nutrition [Australian].
- **Toxemias of Pregnancy:** Prevention and treatment of life-threatening seizures associated with pre-eclampsia and eclampsia [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Maximum Dose:** Total adult daily dose should not exceed 30 to 40 g of magnesium sulfate heptahydrate [Australian].
- **Dosing Adjustment:** Must be individualised based on patient requirements and clinical response.

### Indication-Specific Dosing (Adults)
- **Mild Hypomagnesaemia:** 1 g IM every 6 hours for 4 doses [Australian].
- **Severe Hypomagnesaemia:** 0.25 g/kg IM over 4 hours; alternatively, 5 g via slow IV infusion over 3 hours [Australian].
- **Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN):** 0.5 to 3.0 g daily [Australian].
- **Toxemia of Pregnancy:** 
    - Initial: 4 g IV, followed by 4 to 5 g IM into each buttock.
    - Maintenance: 4 to 5 g IM into alternate buttocks every 4 hours as needed, OR a continuous IV infusion of 1 to 2 g/hr [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Intravenous:** Must be diluted to a concentration of 20% or less. For DBL 493 mg/mL, dilute 5 mL with at least 7.5 mL of a compatible solution [Australian].
- **Intramuscular:** 
    - Adults: 25% to 50% concentration is typically used.
    - Infants/Children: Use a 20% concentration [Australian].
- **Compatibilities:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9%, Lactated Ringer’s, and Glucose 5% (in water or saline). Diluted solutions should be used within 24 hours [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Heart Block:** Magnesium may exacerbate this condition [Australian].
- **Renal Failure:** Contraindicated where creatinine clearance is <20 mL/min due to the risk of hypermagnesaemia [Australian].
- **Obstetric Timing:** Do not administer in the 2 hours preceding delivery unless it is the only available option to manage eclamptic seizures [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Myasthenic Crisis:** May precipitate acute crisis in patients with Myasthenia Gravis [Australian].
- **Clinical Monitoring:** 
    - **Patellar Reflex:** Must be tested before repeat doses; suppression indicates magnesium toxicity [Australian].
    - **Respiration:** Rate should be at least 16 breaths/minute prior to each dose [Australian].
    - **Urine Output:** Should be at least 100 mL in the 4 hours preceding a dose to ensure adequate excretion [Australian].
- **Equipment:** An IV calcium salt (e.g., calcium gluconate) must be readily available to reverse toxicity during IV administration [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies of adverse effects are not specified in the available source.*

- **Hypermagnesaemia (Signs of Toxicity):** 
    - **Early/Moderate:** Nausea, vomiting, flushing, sweating, hypotension, hypothermia, and loss of reflexes.
    - **Neuromuscular:** Muscle weakness and paralysis, blurred or double vision, CNS depression.
    - **Serious/Life-Threatening:** Respiratory depression, respiratory paralysis, circulatory collapse, coma, cardiac arrhythmias, and cardiac arrest [Australian].
- **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Hypocalcaemia with tetany secondary to hypermagnesaemia [Australian].
- **Local Reactions:** Irritation and pain at the site of IM injection [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cardiac Glycosides:** Use with caution; heart block may occur if calcium salts are required to treat magnesium toxicity [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** Concurrent use may enhance CNS depressant effects [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blocking Agents:** May result in excessive/prolonged neuromuscular blockade [Australian].
- **Nifedipine:** Potential for an exaggerated hypotensive response when used concurrently [Australian].
- **Renal Magnesium Loss:** Increased excretion may be caused by aminoglycosides, ciclosporin, amphotericin B, diuretics, and cisplatin [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not specified in available source.
    - **Risk:** Readily crosses the placenta. Administration within 2 hours of delivery may cause neonatal respiratory depression and hypermagnesaemia.
    - **Prolonged Use:** Continuous use for 5–7 days has been associated with fetal bony abnormalities and congenital rickets [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Magnesium is distributed into breast milk at approximately twice the concentration of maternal serum. Caution is advised, though it clears from milk within 24 hours of stopping the infusion [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available source.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
    - TPN Dosing: 0.25 to 1.25 g daily [Australian].
    - IM Concentration: Should be limited to 20% [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in the source [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with extreme caution; contraindicated in severe renal failure (CrCl <20 mL/min) [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available source.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - Essential intracellular cation involved in >300 enzymatic processes, including glycolysis and protein synthesis.
    - Functions as an anticonvulsant and neurochemical transmitter regulator [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - Closely associated with calcium, potassium, and sodium homeostasis.
    - High levels result in neuromuscular and CNS depression [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** ~50% in bone; ~1% in extracellular fluid (33% protein-bound) [Australian].
    - **Onset:** Immediate (IV); ~1 hour (IM) [Australian].
    - **Duration:** ~30 minutes (IV); 3–4 hours (IM) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal; clearance is proportional to plasma concentration and GFR [Australian].
