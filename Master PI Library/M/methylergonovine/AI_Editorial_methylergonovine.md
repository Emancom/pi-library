# Editorial Product Information: Methylergonovine Maleate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methylergonovine maleate (a semi-synthetic ergot alkaloid).
- **Chemical Class:** Ergot alkaloid; ergoline-8-carboxamide derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Noted as Methylergonovine Maleate Tablets [US].
- **ATC Code:** G02AB01 (Methylergometrine).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Oral Tablet:** 0.2 mg [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral (primary for tablets). Note: Source document mentions intramuscular (IM) and intravenous (IV) administration contexts for the drug class, though the specific product provided is the oral tablet.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Major Pharmaceuticals; Granules Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Routine Management of Postpartum Uterine Atony, Hemorrhage, and Subinvolution:** Indicated for use following the delivery of the placenta [US].
- **Control of Uterine Hemorrhage in the Second Stage of Labor:** Indicated following the delivery of the anterior shoulder [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing (Postpartum):**
    - One 0.2 mg tablet orally, 3 or 4 times daily in the puerperium [US].
    - **Maximum Duration:** 1 week [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - While this document pertains to oral tablets, the source notes that if intravenous (IV) administration of the drug class is deemed a life-saving necessity, it must be given slowly over no less than 60 seconds with rigorous blood pressure monitoring [US].
    - Intra-arterial or periarterial injection must be strictly avoided [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated due to uterotonic effects [US].
- **Hypertension:** Contraindicated in patients with high blood pressure [US].
- **Toxemia:** Contraindicated in pre-eclampsia/eclampsia [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Contraindicated in patients with known sensitivity to methylergonovine or any excipients [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **General Warnings:**
    - **Intravenous Administration:** Should not be used routinely via the IV route due to the risk of sudden hypertensive crises and cerebrovascular accidents [US].
    - **Coronary Artery Disease (CAD):** Patients with CAD or risk factors (obesity, smoking, diabetes, hypercholesterolemia) are at higher risk for myocardial ischemia and infarction resulting from drug-induced vasospasm [US].
    - **Medication Errors:** There is a high risk of accidental neonatal exposure if methylergonovine is confused with routine neonatal medications (e.g., Vitamin K, Hepatitis B vaccine). Inadvertent neonatal administration has resulted in respiratory depression, convulsions, cyanosis, and oliguria [US].
- **Precautions:**
    - **Vascular Disease:** Use with caution in patients with sepsis or obliterative vascular disease [US].
    - **Renal and Hepatic Impairment:** Caution is advised as the drug is primarily metabolised by the liver and excreted by the kidneys [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Careful monitoring of blood pressure is required, especially during parenteral administration [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency where data is available.

- **Common (>1%):**
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (sometimes associated with seizure and/or headache) [US].
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain (due to uterine contractions), nausea, and vomiting [US].
- **Rare (<0.1%):**
    - **Cardiovascular:** Acute myocardial infarction, transient chest pain, vasoconstriction, vasospasm, coronary arterial spasm, bradycardia, tachycardia, palpitation, thrombophlebitis [US].
    - **Neurological:** Dizziness, tinnitus, hallucinations [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Dyspnea, nasal congestion [US].
    - **Other:** Hematuria, water intoxication, leg cramps, diarrhea, diaphoresis, rash, foul taste, anaphylaxis [US].
- **Post-Marketing Experience (Frequency Not Known):**
    - **Nervous System:** Cerebrovascular accident (CVA), paraesthesia [US].
    - **Cardiac:** Ventricular fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia, angina pectoris, atrioventricular block [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors (Strong and Moderate):** Concomitant use with strong inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir, indinavir, ketoconazole, clarithromycin) is associated with rare but serious vasospasm leading to cerebral or extremity ischemia. Co-administration is generally not recommended. Use caution with moderate inhibitors (e.g., fluconazole, fluvoxamine) [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Strong inducers (e.g., nevirapine, rifampin) may decrease the therapeutic efficacy of methylergonovine [US].
- **Beta-blockers:** May enhance the vasoconstrictive effects of ergot alkaloids [US].
- **Anesthetics:** Halothane and methoxyflurane may reduce the oxytocic potency of the drug [US].
- **Antianginal Drugs:** Methylergonovine induces vasoconstriction and may reduce the efficacy of glyceryl trinitrate and other antianginals [US].
- **Other Vasoconstrictors:** Caution should be exercised when used concurrently with other ergot alkaloids, prostaglandins, or vasoconstrictors [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated. Uterotonic activity can lead to fetal harm and pregnancy complications. No adequate animal reproductive studies have been conducted [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mothers should not breastfeed during treatment and for at least 12 hours after the final dose. Milk produced during this window should be discarded. The drug may reduce breast milk yield and cause adverse effects in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Effects on human fertility are unknown; no long-term animal studies have been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects ≥65 years. Dosing should be cautious, typically starting at the low end of the range due to higher frequencies of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with caution; renal excretion contributes to the elimination of the drug and its metabolites [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; the drug is primarily eliminated via hepatic metabolism and excretion [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Directly stimulates uterine smooth muscle, increasing the tone, rate, and amplitude of contractions. This induces a rapid, sustained tetanic effect that shortens the third stage of labor and minimizes blood loss [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability is approximately 60%; IM bioavailability is 78% [US].
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributed to peripheral tissues within 2-3 minutes after IV injection. Vdss/F is approximately 56.1 liters [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic [US].
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic decline; mean elimination half-life is 3.39 hours (range 1.5 to 12.7 hours) [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of action is immediate (IV), 2–5 minutes (IM), and 5–10 minutes (Oral) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Pivotal clinical trial data for the original approval are not detailed in the available source document, though pharmacokinetic studies in healthy female volunteers and postpartum women are described [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Packaging:** Dispense in tight, light-resistant containers [US].
- **Handling:** Store injections (if applicable) separately from neonatal medications to prevent errors [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
