# Editorial Product Information: Meloxicam

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of meloxicam for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Meloxicam (an NSAID of the enolic acid class).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Meloxicam [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Tablets: 7.5 mg (pale yellow, circular, 7 mm, flat bevelled, uncoated, with a central break-line) [Australian].
    - Tablets: 15 mg (pale yellow, circular, 10 mm, flat bevelled, uncoated, with a central break-line) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Apotex Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Symptomatic treatment of Osteoarthritis** [Australian].
- **Symptomatic treatment of Rheumatoid Arthritis** [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration possible. Maximum daily dose is 15 mg; this must not be exceeded [Australian].
- **Osteoarthritis:** Recommended dose is 7.5 mg once daily. May be increased to 15 mg daily based on response and comorbidities.
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Recommended dose is 15 mg once daily. May be reduced to 7.5 mg daily based on therapeutic response.
- **Administration Instructions:** To be swallowed with fluid in conjunction with food.
- **Specific Adjustments:** In patients with increased risk of adverse reactions (e.g., history of GI disease or CV risk factors), treatment should start at 7.5 mg daily [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Peri-operative treatment of pain in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery [Australian].
- Known hypersensitivity to meloxicam or excipients; potential for cross-sensitivity with aspirin and other NSAIDs.
- History of asthma, nasal polyps, angioedema, or urticaria following aspirin/NSAID administration.
- Active gastrointestinal ulceration or perforation.
- Active inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis).
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- Severe renal insufficiency in non-dialysed patients.
- Severe uncontrolled heart failure.
- Recent cerebrovascular bleeding or established systemic bleeding disorders.
- Rare hereditary galactose intolerance (due to lactose content) [Australian].
- **Pregnancy** [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding** [Australian].
- Children and adolescents under 18 years of age [Australian].
- Concomitant use of CYP2C9 inhibitors (e.g., sulfaphenazole, sulfinpyrazone, sulfamethoxazole, fluconazole) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Effects:** Potentially fatal GI bleeding, ulceration, or perforation can occur without warning. Risk is higher in the elderly and those with a history of ulcer disease (10-fold higher risk).
- **Cardiovascular (CV) Effects:** All NSAIDs may increase the risk of serious CV thrombotic events, including myocardial infarction and stroke. Risk may increase with dose and duration. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest possible duration.
- **Skin Reactions:** Serious and sometimes fatal reactions, including exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS). Discontinue at the first sign of rash or mucosal lesions [Australian].
- **Renal Effects:** NSAIDs can precipitate renal decompensation by inhibiting renal prostaglandins. Patients at high risk include the elderly, dehydrated patients, and those with heart failure, liver cirrhosis, or concomitant use of diuretics/ACE inhibitors.
- **Hepatic Effects:** Borderline elevations of liver tests occur in up to 15% of patients; severe reactions (jaundice, fatal hepatitis) are rare.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic review of efficacy, risk factors, and renal function (especially when combined with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and diuretics).

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>1% to 10%):** 
    - GI: Dyspepsia (4.5%), Diarrhoea (3.2–7.8%), Nausea (3.3–3.9%), Abdominal pain (1.9–3.1%), Flatulence (1.2–3.2%).
    - CNS: Headache (5.4–8.3%), Dizziness (0.6–3.8%).
    - General: Influenza-like illness (2.3–5.8%), Upper respiratory tract infection (1.9–4.1%).
    - Musculoskeletal: Arthralgia, Back pain.
- **Uncommon (0.1% to 1%):** Vertigo, Tinnitus, Palpitations, Somnolence, Urticaria, Photosensitivity, Increased serum creatinine/urea.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** 
    - Serious GI: Gastrointestinal haemorrhage, perforation, colitis, oesophagitis.
    - Haematological: Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia (risk increased by methotrexate).
    - Respiratory: Onset of asthma in aspirin-sensitive individuals.
    - Renal/Urinary: Acute renal failure, micturition disorders, acute urinary retention.
    - Other: Hepatitis, Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions, Angioedema, SJS, TEN, DRESS.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP2C9 Inhibitors:** Contraindicated; increases meloxicam levels [Australian].
    - **CYP3A4 Inhibitors/Substrates:** Caution required (e.g., ketoconazole, erythromycin, ciclosporin).
    - **Colestyramine:** Accelerates meloxicam elimination by binding in the GI tract.
    - **Lithium:** NSAIDs increase lithium plasma levels; monitoring required.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Anticoagulants/Antiplatelets/SSRIs:** Increased bleeding risk via platelet inhibition; monitor coagulation.
    - **Methotrexate:** Increased haematologic toxicity; strict blood count monitoring required. Contraindicated if dose >15 mg/week [Australian].
    - **Pemetrexed:** Administration should be paused 5 days before, on the day of, and 2 days after pemetrexed in patients with CrCl 45–79 mL/min.
    - **Diuretics/ACE Inhibitors/ARBs:** Synergistic risk of renal impairment ("triple whammy"). Monitor renal function/creatinine.
    - **NSAIDs/Corticosteroids:** Increased risk of GI ulceration and bleeding; not recommended.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. Contraindicated. Prostaglandin synthesis inhibition may cause miscarriage, malformations (cardiac, gastroschisis), premature closure of the ductus arteriosus, and fetal renal dysfunction (oligohydramnios).
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety in humans not established. Excreted in rat milk at levels higher than plasma. Use is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May impair female fertility by delaying ovulation; not recommended for women attempting to conceive.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children and adolescents under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance is decreased. Caution is required due to higher risk of renal, hepatic, or cardiac impairment. Start at 7.5 mg daily.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Mild/Moderate (CrCl >25 mL/min): No dose reduction required.
    - End-stage renal failure on haemodialysis: Dose should not exceed 7.5 mg daily.
    - Non-dialysed severe renal impairment: Contraindicated [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose reduction required for mild to moderate impairment. Severe impairment is a contraindication.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibition of prostaglandin biosynthesis via inhibition of cyclooxygenase (COX). In vitro studies show selective inhibition of COX-2 relative to COX-1.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 89% absolute bioavailability. Steady state achieved in 3–5 days. Absorption is not altered by food.
    - **Distribution:** >99% protein bound. Penetrates synovial fluid (approx. 50% of plasma concentration).
    - **Metabolism:** Almost entirely metabolised to inactive metabolites, primarily by CYP2C9 (two-thirds) and CYP3A4 (one-third).
    - **Elimination:** Excreted equally in faeces and urine. Mean elimination half-life is approximately 20 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Osteoarthritis:** Pivotal trials (MELISSA and SELECT) involving over 9,000 patients demonstrated that meloxicam 7.5 mg once daily is as effective as diclofenac 100 mg SR or piroxicam 20 mg, but with a significantly lower incidence of GI adverse events.
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** A 12-week trial involving 894 patients showed meloxicam (7.5 mg and 15 mg) was superior to placebo in ACR20 response rates and global assessments of disease activity. Efficacy was shown to reach a plateau at the 15 mg dose.

## 12. Storage Conditions

- Protect from direct sunlight [Australian].
- Shelf life: Refer to expiry date on packaging.
