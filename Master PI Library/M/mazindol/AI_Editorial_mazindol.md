# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information was synthesised from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (Food and Drug Administration)**.
- **Note on Jurisdictions:** While the intent of this document is to harmonise data across multiple regions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian), only the United States (US) source document was available for this synthesis. Consequently, clinical recommendations are primarily based on US-approved labeling. 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. It serves as a clinical synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
    - Administration: Slow intravenous bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
    - Loading doses are often utilised in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency: Every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h).
    - Maximum reported dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.

### Titration and Monitoring
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended.
- **Target Levels:** Plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L.
- **Dose Adjustments (ESRD):** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known seizure disorder, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures may occur [US].
- **Accumulation of Metabolites:** In patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD, chronic high-dose oral administration (not specified for IV) may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting. In dialysis patients, common effects include hypertension (18-21%), diarrhea (9-35%), headache (3-37%), and injection site reactions (27-38%).
- **General Adverse Effects:** Body odor, gastritis, abdominal pain, fever, and pharyngitis.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, seizures, and laryngeal edema.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. 
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not assigned a letter category in available sources [US].
    - **Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. 
    - Supplemental use in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. 
    - **Recommendation:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), which is applicable to paediatric populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. 
    - For ESRD patients on dialysis, intravenous levocarnitine is indicated, but monitoring for metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) is advised [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a carrier molecule that facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This allows for fatty acid oxidation and energy production in tissues, particularly skeletal and cardiac muscle.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to 190 µmol/L (trough) at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources (refer to local jurisdictional standards).
