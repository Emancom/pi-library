# Editorial Product Information: Mianserin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a concise, structured format.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mianserin hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Piperazino-azepine (tetracyclic antidepressant).
- **Brand Name(s):** Lumin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 10 mg and 20 mg [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Viatris (Alphapharm Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depression:** Treatment of major depression [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Initial Dose:** 30 mg daily. This may be administered as three divided doses or as a single dose at bedtime.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Dosage should be adjusted weekly based on clinical response.
- **Effective Maintenance Dose:** Typically ranges between 30 mg and 90 mg daily (average 60 mg).
- **Maximum Dose:** Do not exceed 120 mg per day.
- **Duration:** It is often beneficial to continue treatment for several months following initial clinical improvement.

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed whole without chewing, preferably with fluid, and taken between meals.

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to mianserin hydrochloride or any of the product excipients (including sulfites).
- Mania.
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- Concomitant use with Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidality and Clinical Worsening:** Depression is associated with an inherent risk of suicidal ideation and behaviour. Patients, particularly those under 25 years of age, should be closely monitored for clinical worsening or emergent suicidality, especially during the first two months of therapy and during dose adjustments.
- **Bone Marrow Depression:** Cases of neutropenia, agranulocytosis, and thrombocytopenia have been reported, typically appearing after 4 to 6 weeks of treatment. Treatment must be discontinued if signs of infection (fever, sore throat, stomatitis) occur. 
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Use with caution in patients with cardiac impairment (e.g., heart block, recent MI). Although less cardiotoxic than tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs) in overdose, mianserin has been associated with QT prolongation and Torsade de Pointes (TdP).
- **Seizure Threshold:** Mianserin may lower the convulsive threshold. Use with extreme caution in patients with epilepsy; discontinue if convulsions occur.
- **Bipolar Disorder:** Treatment may precipitate a manic or hypomanic episode in patients with bipolar disorder.
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - **Blood Count:** Elderly patients with a history of white blood cell disorders should have a full blood count (FBC) performed every 4 weeks during the first 3 months of treatment [Australian].
    - **Glycaemic Control:** Regular monitoring of blood glucose is advisable in patients with diabetes mellitus.
    - **Ocular/Prostate:** Monitor patients with narrow-angle glaucoma or symptoms of prostatic hypertrophy (though anticholinergic effects are minimal).

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Adverse Effects by Frequency
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Dry mouth, constipation. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Sedation (primarily at initiation), tremor, headache, dizziness, faintness, weakness. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Weight gain. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypomania, hypotension (postural), oedema, arthralgia/arthritis, exanthema, disturbances of liver function (jaundice, hepatitis). |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Convulsions, restless legs (hyperkinesia), neuroleptic malignant syndrome, bradycardia, cardiac arrest, cardiac failure, gynaecomastia, impotence, blood dyscrasias. |
| **Frequency Not Known** | QT prolongation, Torsade de Pointes, elevated liver enzymes. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Haematological:** Bone marrow depression (agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, aplastic anaemia).
- **Cardiac:** Cardiac arrest or failure (necessitates immediate withdrawal).
- **Psychiatric:** Suicidal ideation and behaviour.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Contraindicated. A two-week washout period is required when switching between mianserin and MAOIs.
- **CNS Depressants/Alcohol:** Mianserin may potentiate the central nervous system depressant effects of alcohol and barbiturates.
- **Antiepileptics:** CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine) may reduce mianserin plasma levels. Phenytoin levels should be monitored.
- **Anticoagulants:** Mianserin may affect the metabolism of coumarin derivatives (e.g., warfarin); coagulation monitoring is required.
- **QTc Prolonging Agents:** Increased risk of arrhythmias when co-administered with other QTc-prolonging medicines (e.g., certain antipsychotics or antibiotics).
- **Antihypertensives:** While mianserin does not typically interfere with drugs like bethanidine or clonidine, regular blood pressure monitoring is advised for patients on concurrent antihypertensive therapy.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B2.** 
    - Limited clinical experience exists. Animal studies show reduced implantation sites at high doses but no evidence of fetal damage. Use only if the benefit clearly outweighs the potential risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if mianserin is excreted in human milk. Use in nursing mothers is not recommended.
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggests a reduction in implantation sites at doses >3 mg/kg/day; no human data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents under 18 years have not been established. Use in this population is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Start with a dose not exceeding 30 mg daily. Increase slowly under close supervision. A single night-time dose is preferred due to longer half-life and slower clearance in the elderly.
- **Renal Impairment:** Requires careful monitoring due to impaired metabolism or excretion; dose reduction may be necessary.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe disease. In mild-to-moderate impairment, monitor liver enzymes; discontinue if jaundice occurs.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Mianserin increases central noradrenergic neurotransmission via **$\alpha_2$-autoreceptor blockade** and **noradrenaline (norepinephrine) reuptake inhibition**. It also interacts with serotonin receptors. Unlike TCAs, it lacks the basic side chain responsible for significant anticholinergic activity.

### Pharmacodynamics
- **Sedation:** Attributed to histamine $H_1$ and $\alpha_1$-antagonistic activity.
- **Cardiovascular:** Minimal effect on the cardiovascular system at therapeutic doses.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; peak plasma levels reached within 3 hours.
- **Bioavailability:** Approximately 20%.
- **Distribution:** 95% protein bound.
- **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised via demethylation and oxidation followed by conjugation.
- **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is 21–61 hours. Excreted via urine and faeces within 7–9 days.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Placebo-controlled trials demonstrate efficacy similar to other standard antidepressants. Mianserin shows specific benefits for anxiety and sleep disturbances associated with depression. Specific pivotal trial data was not detailed in the provided source.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Container:** PVC/PVDC/Al blister packs [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
