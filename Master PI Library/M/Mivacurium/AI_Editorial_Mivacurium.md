# Editorial Product Information: Mivacurium

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a structured format for quick reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Mivacurium chloride (a mixture of three stereoisomers).
- **Chemical Class:** Non-depolarising neuromuscular blocking agent (bis-benzylisoquinolinium).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Mivacron** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M03AC10.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile, non-pyrogenic aqueous solution for injection (2 mg/mL).
- **Available Sizes:** 10 mg/5 mL, 20 mg/10 mL, and 50 mg/25 mL glass ampoules [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous injection or continuous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharmacare Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- Used as an adjunct to general anaesthesia to facilitate skeletal muscle relaxation.
- Facilitation of tracheal intubation.
- Maintenance of muscle relaxation during mechanical ventilation.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The mean dose required to produce 95% suppression of the adductor pollicis twitch response (ED95) is 0.07 mg/kg in adults receiving narcotic anaesthesia. Dosing must be individualised based on patient factors including age, weight, comorbidities, and concurrent anaesthetic agents.

### Dosing by Indication (Tracheal Intubation)
- **Standard Bolus:** 0.2 mg/kg administered over 30 seconds (onset of good-to-excellent conditions in 2 to 2.5 minutes).
- **Divided Dose:** 0.15 mg/kg followed 30 seconds later by 0.1 mg/kg (onset of conditions in 1.5 to 2 minutes after the first portion).

### Maintenance Dosing
- **Bolus:** 0.10 mg/kg provides approximately 15 minutes of additional clinically effective block.
- **Continuous Infusion:** Recommended initial rate of 8 to 10 µg/kg/min (0.5 to 0.6 mg/kg/hr) following early evidence of spontaneous recovery. Maintenance rates typically range from 6 to 7 µg/kg/min to maintain 89% to 99% blockade.

### Administration Instructions
- To avoid patient distress, **Mivacron** must only be administered after the induction of unconsciousness.
- Rates of infusion should be adjusted in increments of 1 µg/kg/min every 3 minutes.
- **Mivacron** may be used undiluted or diluted to 0.5 mg/mL with compatible fluids (e.g., 0.9% NaCl, 5% Glucose, or Lactated Ringer’s).

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity or allergic reaction to mivacurium.
- Patients known to be homozygous for the atypical plasma cholinesterase gene [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Respiratory Paralysis:** Mivacurium paralyses respiratory and skeletal muscles without affecting consciousness. It must only be administered by experienced anaesthetists with adequate facilities for intubation and artificial ventilation.
- **Histamine Release:** Rapid bolus doses (≥0.2 mg/kg) are associated with dose-related histamine release. In patients sensitive to histamine (e.g., those with asthma or cardiovascular disease), doses should be ≤0.15 mg/kg administered over 60 seconds.
- **Plasma Cholinesterase Activity:** Prolonged blockade may occur in patients with reduced plasma cholinesterase activity due to genetic abnormalities, pregnancy, liver/renal disease, or certain medications.
- **Neuromuscular Disease:** Increased sensitivity is expected in patients with myasthenia gravis or other neuromuscular diseases.
- **Burn Patients:** May develop resistance to non-depolarising agents but may also have reduced plasma cholinesterase; a test dose of 0.015–0.020 mg/kg is recommended.
- **Cross-Sensitivity:** High rates of cross-sensitivity (>50%) between neuromuscular blocking agents have been reported; exercise caution in patients with prior reactions.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Potentiation (Increased Blockade)
- **Inhalational Anaesthetics:** Isoflurane and enflurane can reduce mivacurium requirements by up to 25–40%. Halothane has a minimal effect.
- **Antibiotics:** Aminoglycosides, polymyxins, tetracyclines, lincomycin, and clindamycin.
- **Anti-arrhythmics:** Propranolol, calcium channel blockers, lidocaine, procainamide, and quinidine.
- **Others:** Magnesium salts, lithium salts, ketamine, and diuretics (furosemide).

### Reduced Plasma Cholinesterase Activity (Prolonged Action)
- Anti-mitotic drugs, MAOIs, ecothiophate iodide, pancuronium, organophosphates, SSRIs, and bambuterol.

### Pharmacodynamic Antagonism (Decreased Blockade)
- Chronic therapy with phenytoin or carbamazepine may lengthen onset and shorten duration of action.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B2]. There is no information on use in pregnant women. It should only be used if the benefit outweighs the risk. Plasma cholinesterase levels decrease during pregnancy, potentially requiring lower infusion rates.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if mivacurium is excreted in human milk. Use with caution in lactating women.
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics (2–12 years):** Higher ED95 (0.1 mg/kg), faster onset, and shorter duration than adults. Recommended bolus: 0.1 to 0.2 mg/kg. Infusion rates: 10 to 15 µg/kg/min.
- **Paediatrics (<2 years):** No dose recommendations available [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Onset, duration, and recovery may be extended by 20–30%. Dose reductions for bolus and infusion rates may be required.
- **Renal Impairment:** In end-stage renal failure, the duration of block is approximately 1.5 times longer. Doses >0.15 mg/kg have not been studied.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In end-stage hepatic failure, the duration of block is approximately 3 times longer due to reduced plasma cholinesterase. Doses >0.15 mg/kg have not been studied.
- **Obesity:** Dosing should be based on ideal body weight for patients weighing ≥30% over ideal weight.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Competitive antagonist at cholinergic (nicotinic) receptors on the motor end-plate, blocking neuromuscular transmission.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily inactivated by enzymatic hydrolysis by plasma pseudocholinesterase. 
    - **Elimination:** Renal and biliary excretion of unchanged drug are minor pathways. 
    - **Half-life:** Short terminal elimination half-life (approx. 1.74 to 2.63 minutes in healthy adults).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Short-acting; spontaneous recovery is independent of the duration of infusion. Action is reversed by acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (e.g., neostigmine).

## 11. Clinical Trials

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from light; do not freeze.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
- **Stability:** Once diluted to 0.5 mg/mL, the solution is chemically stable for 48 hours at 30°C, but must be used immediately due to the lack of antimicrobial preservatives.
