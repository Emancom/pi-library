# Editorial Product Information: Methsuximide

## Editorial Note
- Jurisdictions contributing to this editorial: United States (FDA). 
- **Note:** Only the United States Product Information was available for this synthesis. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australia, Europe, Canada, Japan) is not included.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of methsuximide for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Methsuximide (a succinimide anticonvulsant).
- **Chemical Name:** N,2-Dimethyl-2-phenylsuccinimide.
- **Brand Name(s):** **Celontin** [US].
- **ATC Code:** N03AB05.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 300 mg oral capsules [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Parke-Davis Div of Pfizer Inc [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Absence (petit mal) seizures:** Indicated specifically for the control of absence seizures that have proven refractory to other therapeutic agents [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Dosage must be individualised based on patient response and tolerance. The goal is to achieve seizure control with minimal adverse effects.
- **Titration Schedule:**
    - **Initial Dose:** 300 mg once daily for the first week [US].
    - **Titration:** If necessary, the daily dose may be increased by 300 mg at weekly intervals for three weeks [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** 1.2 g per day [US].
- **Combination Therapy:** Methsuximide may be administered concurrently with other anticonvulsants if the patient presents with mixed seizure types (e.g., absence seizures coexisting with other forms of epilepsy) [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Adherence to a strict dosage regimen is essential. 
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Discontinuation:** Like other antiepileptics, methsuximide should be withdrawn slowly. Abrupt withdrawal may precipitate absence (petit mal) status [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known history of hypersensitivity to methsuximide or other succinimides (e.g., ethosuximide) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Blood Dyscrasias:** Serious and occasionally fatal blood dyscrasias have been reported with succinimide use. Periodic blood counts are mandatory. Patients should be instructed to report symptoms of infection (fever, sore throat) immediately [US].
- **Hepatic and Renal Effects:** Succinimides have shown morphological and functional changes in animal liver studies. Use with extreme caution in patients with pre-existing liver or renal disease. Periodic urinalysis and liver function tests (LFTs) are recommended for all patients [US].
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):** Cases of SLE have been reported; clinicians should monitor for related signs and symptoms [US].
- **Suicidal Behavior and Ideation:** Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) increase the risk of suicidal thoughts or behavior. Risk emerges as early as one week after starting treatment. Monitor all patients for new or worsening depression and unusual changes in mood or behavior [US].
- **Cognitive and Motor Impairment:** Methsuximide may impair mental and physical abilities. Patients should be cautioned against performing hazardous tasks (e.g., driving) until their response to the medication is established [US].
- **Seizure Exacerbation:** If used alone in patients with mixed epilepsy, methsuximide may increase the frequency of grand mal (tonic-clonic) seizures in some individuals [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by system. Frequencies are not quantified in the source PI but are described qualitatively.

- **Gastrointestinal (Frequent):** Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, diarrhea, weight loss, epigastric and abdominal pain, constipation.
- **Nervous System (Most Frequent):** Drowsiness, ataxia, and dizziness. Other effects include irritability, nervousness, headache, blurred vision, photophobia, hiccups, and insomnia.
- **Psychiatric:** Confusion, mental slowness, depression, aggressiveness, instability, and hypochondriacal behavior. Rare reports of psychosis, suicidal behavior, and auditory hallucinations.
- **Hemopoietic:** Eosinophilia, leukopenia, monocytosis, and pancytopenia (with or without bone marrow suppression).
- **Integumentary:** Pruritic erythematous rashes, urticaria, and Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS).
- **Genitourinary:** Proteinuria and microscopic hematuria.
- **Body as a Whole:** Periorbital edema, hyperemia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Methsuximide may increase the plasma concentrations of concurrently administered phenytoin and phenobarbital [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic serum level determinations of co-administered AEDs may be necessary when starting or stopping methsuximide [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Alcohol and other CNS depressants may potentiate the sedative effects of methsuximide [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** There is an association between anticonvulsant use and an increased incidence of birth defects, though a definitive causal relationship is not established. Most women on AEDs deliver normal infants [US].
    - **Registry:** Patients should be encouraged to enroll in the North American Antiepileptic Drug (NAAED) Pregnancy Registry [US].
    - **Management:** Do not discontinue methsuximide abruptly if used to prevent major seizures, as status epilepticus poses a severe threat to the fetus and mother [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if methsuximide is excreted in human milk. Caution should be exercised, and the decision to breastfeed should be made in consultation with a healthcare provider [US].
- **Fertility:** Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is addressed in the Dosage and Administration section. No specific age-related safety data provided beyond the general AED suicide risk analysis (which included ages 5–100) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in source documents; general caution for renal and hepatic impairment applies [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with "extreme caution" due to potential for drug-induced changes [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with "extreme caution" due to reports of succinimide-induced liver changes in animals [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Methsuximide suppresses the paroxysmal three-cycle per second spike-and-wave activity seen in absence (petit mal) seizures. It elevates the CNS threshold to convulsive stimuli and depresses the motor cortex [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised to an active metabolite, N-desmethylmethsuximide [US].
    - **Elimination:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Overdosage:** 
    - **Symptoms:** Nausea, vomiting, CNS depression, coma, and respiratory depression.
    - **Biphasic Course:** Poisoning may follow a biphasic course (awakening from initial coma followed by a relapse within 24 hours), attributed to the N-desmethylmethsuximide metabolite [US].
    - **Toxicity Threshold:** Metabolite levels >40 µg/mL may cause toxicity; coma has been observed at 150 µg/mL [US].
    - **Treatment:** Emesis, gastric lavage, activated charcoal, and general supportive care. Charcoal hemoperfusion may assist in removing the active metabolite. Forced diuresis and exchange transfusions are ineffective [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Suicide Risk Analysis:** A pooled analysis of 199 placebo-controlled trials of 11 different AEDs showed an adjusted relative risk of 1.8 (95% CI: 1.2, 2.7) for suicidal ideation/behavior. The incidence was 0.43% in AED-treated patients vs 0.24% in placebo [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15–30°C (59–86°F) [US].
- **Special Precautions:** Methsuximide has a low melting temperature (124°F / 51°C). Capsules must be protected from excessive heat (above 40°C / 104°F). Do not dispense or use capsules that appear melted or are not full [US].
- **General:** Protect from light and moisture [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Prescription only (Rx only).
- **Poison Schedule:** Not applicable in the US context as a specific schedule like S4, but classified as a prescription-only drug.
