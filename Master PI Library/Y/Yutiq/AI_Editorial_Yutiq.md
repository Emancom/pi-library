# Editorial Product Information: Fluocinolone Acetonide Intravitreal Implant

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising available regulatory data into a single clinically relevant resource.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Fluocinolone acetonide (a synthetic corticosteroid).
- **Brand Name:** **Yutiq** [US].
- **ATC Code:** S01BA15 (Fluocinolone acetonide).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Non-bioerodible intravitreal implant containing 0.18 mg of fluocinolone acetonide. The implant is approximately 3.5 mm x 0.37 mm [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravitreal injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** ANI Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (Labeler); Alimera Sciences, Inc. (Manufactured for); EyePoint US, Inc. (Manufactured by) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Non-Infectious Uveitis:** Indicated for the treatment of chronic non-infectious uveitis affecting the posterior segment of the eye [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** A single 0.18 mg implant administered via intravitreal injection.
- **Release Profile:** The implant is designed to provide sustained release of fluocinolone acetonide at an initial rate of 0.25 mcg/day, with a duration of action lasting approximately 36 months [US].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Must be performed by an ophthalmologist under aseptic conditions (sterile gloves, drapes, calipers, and eyelid speculum).
    - **Pre-procedure:** Administer adequate local anaesthesia and a broad-spectrum microbicide to the injection site.
    - **Site:** Recommended entry is the inferotemporal quadrant, 4 mm from the limbus.
    - **Procedure:** Use the provided preloaded 25-gauge applicator. Displace the conjunctiva to ensure entry sites do not align post-withdrawal. The plunger must be fully depressed to deliver the implant.
    - **Post-procedure:** Perform indirect ophthalmoscopy immediately to verify retinal artery perfusion and placement. Monitor intraocular pressure (IOP) within 30 minutes and perform biomicroscopy between two and seven days following the procedure [US].
- **Missed Dose:** Not applicable as this is a long-term sustained-release implant.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Ocular or Periocular Infections:** Active or suspected infections, including most viral diseases of the cornea and conjunctiva (e.g., active epithelial herpes simplex keratitis, vaccinia, and varicella), mycobacterial infections, and fungal diseases [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to fluocinolone acetonide or any component of the delivery system [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Injection-Related Effects:** Intravitreal injections are associated with risks of endophthalmitis, intraocular inflammation, retinal detachment, and fluctuations in IOP. Hypotony has been observed within 24 hours of injection, typically resolving within two weeks [US].
- **Steroid-Related Effects:** Prolonged use of corticosteroids may lead to posterior subcapsular cataracts, increased IOP, and glaucoma. They may also mask or enhance secondary infections (bacterial, viral, or fungal). 
- **Herpes Simplex Reactivation:** Not recommended in patients with a history of ocular herpes simplex due to the risk of viral reactivation [US].
- **Implant Migration:** There is a risk of the implant migrating into the anterior chamber, particularly in patients who are aphakic or have a tear/absence of the posterior lens capsule [US].
- **Monitoring:** Patients must be monitored for endophthalmitis and IOP elevation regularly following implantation.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following data is based on controlled clinical trials through 12 months for the 0.18 mg implant [US]:

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency (Yutiq n=226) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cataract (phakic eyes)** | 56% |
| **IOP Elevation ≥ 10 mmHg** | 22% |
| **Reduced Visual Acuity** | 15% |
| **IOP Elevation > 30 mmHg** | 12% |
| **Macular Oedema** | 11% |
| **Uveitis** | 10% |
| **Conjunctival Hemorrhage** | 8% |
| **Eye Pain** | 8% |
| **Hypotony of Eye** | 7% |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Development of glaucoma, optic nerve damage, visual field defects, and perforation of the globe (in cases of corneal/scleral thinning) [US].
- **Non-Ocular Effects:** Nasopharyngitis (5%), hypertension (3%), and arthralgia (2%) were reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Dynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources. Systemic absorption is minimal; therefore, systemic drug-drug interactions are unlikely.
- **Regional Variation:** No specific interactions noted in US labeling.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (PLLR labeling). 
    - **Summary:** There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Systemic corticosteroids are known to be teratogenic in animals at low doses. Use during pregnancy only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if intravitreal administration results in sufficient systemic absorption to be detectable in human milk. Exercise caution and consider the clinical need of the mother alongside potential risks to the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** Long-term animal studies on fertility have not been conducted [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between elderly and younger populations [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; systemic exposure is negligible [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Fluocinolone acetonide is a corticosteroid that inhibits inflammatory responses (oedema, fibrin deposition, capillary dilation, and leucocyte migration) by inducing lipocortins. These proteins inhibit phospholipase A2, thereby blocking the release of arachidonic acid and the subsequent biosynthesis of prostaglandins and leukotrienes [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Corticosteroids reduce inflammation and may prevent scar formation associated with chronic uveitis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** The implant provides sustained local delivery. Specific systemic pharmacokinetic parameters following intravitreal injection were not detailed in the source document, though systemic levels are expected to be low [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study Design:** Efficacy was established in two multicentre, randomised, sham-injection-controlled, double-masked studies (Study 1 and Study 2).
- **Primary Outcome:** The proportion of patients experiencing uveitis recurrence within 6 months.
- **Results:** 
    - **Study 1:** Recurrence occurred in 18% of the treatment group vs 79% of the sham group (p < 0.01).
    - **Study 2:** Recurrence occurred in 22% of the treatment group vs 54% of the sham group (p < 0.01).
    - Benefit was maintained through 12 months of follow-up [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Special Handling:** Do not use if the sterile foil pouch is damaged. Use the applicator immediately after opening the inner pouch.
