# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating available regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the local approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; intervals should never be less than every 6 hours.
- **Titration:** Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. The maximum recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration:** Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### General Instructions
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration before use.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with known epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** Note that the safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in chronic hemodialysis trials at a frequency of ≥5% (regardless of causality) include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%), Anorexia (5%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Chest pain (12%) |
| **Nervous System** | Headache (22%), Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%) |
| **Body as a Whole** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Infection (15%), Asthenia (9%), Fever (7%) |
| **Special Senses** | Taste perversion (3%), Amblyopia (3%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].
- **Other:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis have been observed less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase after administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal therapy against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders and ESRD apply to paediatric patients; however, specific age-range limitations are not detailed in the source [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See "Dosage and Administration" for ESRD patients. Oral formulations should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic/fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD patients, IV administration increases plasma carnitine concentrations. Normal plasma free carnitine is 40–50 µmol/L; deficiency is defined as <20 µmol/L [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Dose-ranging studies (10, 20, 40 mg/kg) showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison schedule not applicable (Legend drug) [US].
