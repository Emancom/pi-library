# Editorial Product Information: Yondelis (Trabectedin)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) documents from the following jurisdictions: **Australian (TGA)** and **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory requirements from multiple regions.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trabectedin (an alkylating agent/antineoplastic).
- **Brand Name:** **Yondelis**.
- **ATC Code:** L01CX01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilized powder for solution for infusion. Each single-dose vial contains 1 mg of trabectedin.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion (via a central venous line).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - **Australian:** Specialised Therapeutics Pharma Pty Ltd.
    - **US:** Janssen Products, LP (under license from Pharma Mar, S.A.).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Liposarcoma and Leiomyosarcoma:** Indicated for the treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic liposarcoma or leiomyosarcoma who have received a prior anthracycline-containing regimen.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 1.5 mg/m² body surface area administered as a 24-hour continuous intravenous infusion every 3 weeks (21-day cycle).
- **Premedication:** All patients must receive dexamethasone 20 mg intravenously 30 minutes prior to each infusion. This serves as both anti-emetic prophylaxis and hepatoprotective support.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Infusion Line:** Must be administered through a central venous line [US]. The use of a central venous line is strongly recommended to avoid severe injection site reactions [Australian].
    - **Filter:** Use an infusion set with a 0.2-micron polyethersulfone (PES) in-line filter.
    - **Incompatibilities:** Do not mix with other medicinal products. Compatible with Type I glass, PVC, PE, and PP containers/tubing.
- **Retreatment Criteria [Australian]:** 
    - ANC ≥ 1.5 x 10⁹/L; Platelets ≥ 100 x 10⁹/L.
    - Bilirubin ≤ ULN; ALP ≤ 2.5 x ULN; AST/ALT ≤ 2.5 x ULN.
    - Albumin ≥ 25 g/L.
    - Creatinine clearance ≥ 30 mL/min.
    - CPK ≤ 2.5 x ULN; Haemoglobin ≥ 90 g/L.
- **Dose Reductions:** 
    - First reduction: 1.2 mg/m².
    - Second reduction: 1.0 mg/m².
    - For patients with moderate hepatic impairment, the starting dose is 0.9 mg/m² [US].
- **Dose Modification for Toxicity:** Withhold dose for up to 3 weeks if haematological or biochemical thresholds are not met. Permanently discontinue for rhabdomyolysis, capillary leak syndrome, severe liver dysfunction, or significant cardiac ejection fraction decrease.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known severe hypersensitivity (e.g., anaphylaxis) to trabectedin or any excipients.
- Concurrent serious or uncontrolled infection [Australian].
- Breastfeeding [Australian; note: US advises discontinuation].
- Concomitant use with yellow fever vaccine [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Neutropenic Sepsis:** Severe and fatal cases have occurred. Monitor full blood counts at baseline and weekly for the first two cycles.
- **Rhabdomyolysis:** Can cause fatal muscle toxicity. Monitor CPK levels prior to each administration. Withhold if CPK > 2.5 x ULN.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Reversible Grade 3 or 4 elevations in AST/ALT are common. Monitor LFTs prior to each dose. Avoid use in patients with elevated bilirubin.
- **Cardiomyopathy:** Severe heart failure and decreased LVEF may occur. Assess LVEF via ECHO or MUGA before initiation and every 2–3 months during treatment. Discontinue if LVEF decreases below the lower limit of normal (LLN).
- **Capillary Leak Syndrome (CLS):** Characterised by hypotension, oedema, and hypoalbuminaemia. Discontinue immediately if CLS is suspected.
- **Extravasation:** May result in tissue necrosis requiring debridement. If it occurs, manage according to local standard practice; there is no specific antidote.
- **Alcohol Consumption:** Must be avoided during treatment due to potential hepatotoxicity [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most patients (approx. 97–98%) experience adverse reactions; approximately 63% experience Grade 3 or 4 toxicities.

| Adverse Reaction | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea (75%), Vomiting (46%), Constipation (37%), Diarrhoea (35%), Abdominal pain |
| **Systemic/General** | Fatigue (69%), Pyrexia, Peripheral oedema (28%), Mucosal inflammation |
| **Haematological** | Anaemia (93–96%), Neutropaenia (31–66%), Thrombocytopaenia (19–59%) |
| **Metabolic** | Decreased appetite (37%), Hypokalaemia, Dehydration |
| **Investigations** | Increased ALT (90%), Increased AST (84%), Increased ALP (70%), Increased CPK (33%), Increased Creatinine |
| **Neurological** | Headache (25%), Dizziness, Peripheral neuropathy |
| **Respiratory** | Dyspnoea (25%), Cough |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Septic shock, pulmonary oedema, hepatic failure, rhabdomyolysis, and capillary leak syndrome (uncommon/rare but potentially fatal).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Potent inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir, clarithromycin) increase trabectedin exposure (AUC increased by 66%) and should be avoided.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Potent inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenobarbital, St. John’s Wort) decrease trabectedin exposure (AUC decreased by 31%) and should be avoided.
- **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Inhibitors:** Caution is advised as trabectedin is a substrate of P-gp (e.g., cyclosporine, verapamil).
- **Phenytoin:** Co-administration may reduce phenytoin absorption, potentially increasing seizure risk [Australian].
- **Live Vaccines:** Specifically the yellow fever vaccine is contraindicated [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Trabectedin is suspected to cause serious birth defects based on its mechanism of action. 
    - **US Recommendation:** Advise of potential fetal risk.
- **Contraception:** 
    - Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during treatment and for **8 months** after the final dose.
    - Males with female partners of reproductive potential should use effective contraception for **5 months** after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Discontinue breastfeeding during treatment and for **3 months** after the last dose.
- **Fertility:** Trabectedin may cause irreversible infertility; genetic counselling and sperm/ovule conservation should be discussed prior to treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children <18 years have not been established. Studies in paediatric sarcomas did not demonstrate sufficient efficacy.
- **Elderly:** No routine dose adjustments are recommended based on age alone, though patients >65 years may be at higher risk for cardiac dysfunction.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required for mild to moderate impairment (CrCl 30–89 mL/min). Use is not recommended/not studied in patients with CrCl < 30 mL/min.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild:** No adjustment needed.
    - **Moderate [US]:** Reduce starting dose to 0.9 mg/m².
    - **Moderate/Severe [Australian]:** Do not treat if bilirubin is elevated at baseline.
    - **Severe [US]:** Do not administer.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Trabectedin binds to the minor groove of DNA, bending the helix toward the major groove. This adduct formation interferes with transcription factors, DNA binding proteins, and repair pathways (TC-NER), leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (approx. 97%). Very large volume of distribution (>5000 L).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatically metabolised by CYP3A4.
    - **Excretion:** Primarily faecal (58%); minimal renal excretion (<7%). The terminal elimination half-life is long (approx. 175–180 hours).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trial (ET743-SAR-3007):** A randomised, open-label trial in 518 patients with metastatic liposarcoma or leiomyosarcoma comparing Yondelis (1.5 mg/m² 24h infusion) to dacarbazine (1000 mg/m²). 
- **Outcomes:** Yondelis significantly improved median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) (4.2 months vs 1.5 months; HR 0.55). There was no statistically significant difference in Overall Survival (OS) (13.7 months vs 13.1 months).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Shelf Life:** 60 months [Australian].
- **Stability after Reconstitution/Dilution:** 
    - Chemical and physical stability is maintained for 30 hours at room temperature (up to 25°C). 
    - From a microbiological perspective, it should be used immediately or within 24 hours if stored at 2°C to 8°C.
    - Total time from reconstitution to the end of infusion should not exceed 30 hours.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Classification:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4).
- **US Classification:** Rx-only; Cytotoxic agent.
