# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: US (Food and Drug Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative/carrier molecule.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration should occur as a slow 2–3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. These may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect parenteral products visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended.
    - For ESRD, monitor trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of the patient populations (ESRD or metabolic errors).

- **Common (>10%):** Transient nausea and vomiting. 
- **Less Frequent:** Body odor, gastritis.
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis, seizures [US].

### Clinical Trial Data (ESRD Patients)
The following events occurred at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, regardless of causality [US]:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, anorexia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, depression, insomnia, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Cough increase, dyspnea, pharyngitis, rhinitis |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/available sources note: "use only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy for metabolic disorders and ESRD are established (see Section 3 for dosing) [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis), mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. 
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the effect on clinical outcomes in this specific population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Formulations do not contain preservatives; discard any unused portion [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
