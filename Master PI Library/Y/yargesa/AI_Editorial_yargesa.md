# Editorial Product Information: Miglustat (Yargesa)

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial synthesis: United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of miglustat (Yargesa) for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in a particular region. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labeling for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Miglustat; a glucosylceramide synthase inhibitor and N-alkylated imino sugar (synthetic analog of D-glucose).
- **Brand Name(s):** Yargesa [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Capsules:** 100 mg hard gelatin capsules (white, opaque, imprinted with "709" in black ink) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Edenbridge Pharmaceuticals, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 1 Gaucher Disease:** Indicated as monotherapy for the treatment of adult patients with mild to moderate type 1 Gaucher disease for whom enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) is not a suitable therapeutic option (e.g., due to hypersensitivity, allergy, or poor venous access) [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** The standard recommended dose is 100 mg administered orally three times daily at regular intervals [US].
- **Dose Adjustments for Tolerability:** Dosage may be reduced to 100 mg once or twice daily in patients experiencing dose-limiting adverse effects, specifically tremor or diarrhea [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Therapy should be supervised by physicians experienced in managing Gaucher disease. Capsules should be taken at the same time each day [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, the patient should skip that dose and take the next capsule at the regular scheduled time [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None:** There are no listed contraindications in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Reported in approximately 3% of patients in clinical trials. All patients should undergo baseline and 6-monthly neurological evaluations. If symptoms (pain, weakness, numbness, tingling) develop, treatment cessation should be considered following a risk-benefit reassessment [US].
- **Tremor:** Affects approximately 30% of patients, typically manifesting as an exaggerated physiological tremor of the hands within the first month of therapy. Most cases resolve within 1–3 months of continued treatment. Dose reduction or discontinuation is required if the tremor does not resolve within days of a dose reduction [US].
- **Diarrhea and Weight Loss:** Highly prevalent (diarrhea ~85%; weight loss ~65%). Diarrhea results from the inhibition of intestinal disaccharidases, leading to osmotic diarrhea. Management includes diet modification (reducing sucrose, lactose, and other carbohydrates), taking capsules between meals, or using anti-diarrheal agents (e.g., loperamide). If patients do not respond to these measures, evaluate for underlying gastrointestinal disease [US].
- **Reductions in Platelet Count:** Mild reductions in platelet counts have been observed without associated bleeding. Periodic monitoring of platelet counts is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are classified by frequency based on clinical trial data.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhea (89–100%), weight decrease (39–67%), abdominal pain (18–67%), flatulence (29–50%), nausea (8–22%), headache (21–22%), tremor (11–17%), generalized weakness (17%), dizziness (8–11%), leg cramps (4–11%), cramps (11%), visual disturbance (17%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Vomiting, bloating, anorexia, dyspepsia, epigastric pain, paresthesia, migraine, thrombocytopenia, menstrual disorder, constipation, dry mouth, back pain, heaviness in limbs, memory loss, unsteady gait |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Peripheral neuropathy is the most common serious adverse reaction reported [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Imiglucerase:** Co-administration may increase the clearance of imiglucerase by approximately 70%. While pharmacokinetic data suggests a 22% reduction in miglustat $C_{max}$ and 14% reduction in AUC when combined, the clinical significance of the effect on imiglucerase is not fully established due to small study sizes [US].
- **Loperamide:** Concomitant use does not appear to significantly alter the pharmacokinetics of miglustat [US].
- **Cytochrome P450 Enzymes:** In vitro studies indicate miglustat does not inhibit CYP1A2, 2A6, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, 3A4, or 4A11; significant metabolic interactions via these pathways are unlikely [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned; narrative provided]. Animal reproduction studies indicate potential fetal harm, including increased post-implantation loss and decreased embryo-fetal survival in rats at twice the human dose. Symptomatic Gaucher disease itself carries risks, including hepatosplenomegaly and thrombocytopenia, which may exacerbate during pregnancy. Use is not recommended unless the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Miglustat is likely present in human milk based on its physical properties. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, breastfeeding is not recommended during therapy [US].
- **Fertility:** In male rats, miglustat decreased fertility (reversible after 6 weeks of withdrawal). No effects on male fertility were seen in a small 6-week human study ($n=7$) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years of age have not been established. Reduced weight and height gain percentiles were noted in pediatric patients treated for other indications [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65 and over. Dosing should be cautious, typically starting at the lower end of the range, due to higher frequencies of renal, hepatic, or cardiac impairment [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Miglustat is substantially excreted by the kidneys.
    - **Mild (CrCl 50–70 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$):** Start dose at 100 mg twice daily [US].
    - **Moderate (CrCl 30–50 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$):** Start dose at 100 mg once daily [US].
    - **Severe (CrCl <30 mL/min/1.73 $m^2$):** Use is not recommended [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics have not been assessed in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Miglustat is a "substrate reduction therapy" (SRT). It competitively and reversibly inhibits glucosylceramide synthase, the enzyme responsible for the first step in glycosphingolipid synthesis. This reduces the rate of glucosylceramide formation to a level that can be cleared by the patient's residual glucocerebrosidase activity [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is ~97%. $T_{max}$ is 2 to 2.5 hours. Food decreases $C_{max}$ by 36% and delays $T_{max}$ by 2 hours, but has no significant effect on AUC [US].
    - **Distribution:** Does not bind to plasma proteins. Apparent volume of distribution is 83–105 L. Crosses the blood-brain barrier (Cerebrospinal fluid concentrations are 31–67% of plasma levels) [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily excreted unchanged; miglustat glucuronide accounts for ~5% of the dose in urine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Predominantly renal (83% recovered in urine, 12% in feces). The elimination half-life is approximately 6 to 7 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy Trials (Studies 1 & 2):** Open-label studies in patients unable or unwilling to receive ERT showed significant reductions in liver and spleen volumes. In Study 1 (100 mg TID), liver volume decreased by 12% and spleen volume by 19% at 12 months. Extensions up to 24 months showed continued organ volume reduction and improvements in hemoglobin and platelet counts [US].
- **Active-Controlled Trial (Study 3):** Patients stabilised on ERT were randomised to miglustat monotherapy, ERT alone, or combination therapy. At 6 months, miglustat monotherapy maintained organ volumes but showed a significant decrease in platelet counts compared to the ERT group [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children.
