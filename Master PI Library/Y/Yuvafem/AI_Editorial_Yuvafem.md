# Editorial Product Information: Estradiol (Vaginal Insert)

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of the clinical and regulatory profile of Yuvafem (estradiol vaginal inserts) for healthcare professionals.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should refer to the local approved labeling for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Estradiol (as estradiol hemihydrate).
*   **Chemical Class:** Estrogen.
*   **Brand Names:** **Yuvafem** [US].
*   **ATC Code:** G03CA03 (Estradiol).
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   Vaginal insert: 10 mcg (containing 10.3 mcg estradiol hemihydrate equivalent to 10 mcg estradiol) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravaginal.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AvKARE, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Atrophic Vaginitis:** Treatment of atrophic vaginitis due to menopause [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Initiation:** One 10 mcg insert administered intravaginally once daily for 2 weeks [US].
*   **Maintenance:** Following the initial 2-week course, the dose is reduced to one 10 mcg insert twice weekly (e.g., Tuesday and Friday) [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   Inserts should be administered using the provided disposable applicator.
*   The applicator should be inserted as far as comfortably possible into the vagina (or until half the applicator is inside) before depressing the plunger to eject the insert [US].
*   Therapy should be prescribed at the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary to achieve treatment goals [US].

### Missed Dose
*   Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
*   Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding [US].
*   Known, suspected, or history of breast cancer [US].
*   Known or suspected estrogen-dependent neoplasia [US].
*   Active deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), or a history of these conditions [US].
*   Active arterial thromboembolic disease (e.g., stroke, myocardial infarction) or a history of these conditions [US].
*   Known anaphylactic reaction or angioedema to the product or its components [US].
*   Known liver impairment or disease [US].
*   Known protein C, protein S, or antithrombin deficiency, or other known thrombophilic disorders [US].
*   Known or suspected pregnancy [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
*   **Endometrial Cancer:** There is an increased risk of endometrial cancer in women with a uterus who use unopposed estrogens.
*   **Cardiovascular Disorders:** Estrogens should not be used to prevent cardiovascular disease. The Women’s Health Initiative (WHI) reported increased risks of stroke and DVT (estrogen-alone) and stroke, DVT, PE, and MI (estrogen plus progestin).
*   **Breast Cancer:** Estrogen plus progestin therapy is associated with an increased risk of invasive breast cancer.
*   **Probable Dementia:** Estrogens should not be used to prevent dementia. The WHI Memory Study (WHIMS) reported an increased risk of probable dementia in postmenopausal women ≥65 years.

### General Warnings and Precautions
*   **Systemic Absorption:** Although administered locally, systemic absorption occurs. Warnings associated with systemic estrogen therapy should be considered [US].
*   **Gallbladder Disease:** Estrogens increase the risk of gallbladder disease requiring surgery [US].
*   **Visual Abnormalities:** Retinal vascular thrombosis has been reported. Therapy should be discontinued pending examination if sudden partial or complete loss of vision occurs [US].
*   **Hypercalcemia:** Estrogens may lead to severe hypercalcemia in patients with breast cancer and bone metastases [US].
*   **Vaginal Trauma:** The applicator may cause local abrasion, particularly in women with severe vaginal atrophy [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
*   **Endometrial Surveillance:** Adequate diagnostic measures (sampling) are required to rule out malignancy in women with undiagnosed persistent or recurring abnormal genital bleeding [US].
*   **Thyroid Function:** Monitor thyroid function in women on thyroid replacement therapy, as estrogens may increase thyroid-binding globulin (TBG) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Data from Clinical Trials
The following reactions occurred at a frequency ≥5% in a 12-month 10 mcg trial or a 12-week 25 mcg trial [US]:

| Adverse Reaction | Yuvafem 10 mcg (n=205) | Placebo (n=103) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Back Pain** | 7% | 2% |
| **Vulvovaginal Mycotic Infection** | 8% | 3% |
| **Vulvovaginal Pruritus** | 8% | 2% |
| **Diarrhea** | 5% | 0% |
| **Headache** (25 mcg study) | 9% | 6% |
| **Abdominal Pain** (25 mcg study) | 7% | 4% |
| **Upper Respiratory Tract Infection** (25 mcg study) | 5% | 4% |

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Endometrial cancer, breast cancer, stroke, DVT, and myocardial infarction [US].

### Postmarketing Experience
*   **Genitourinary:** Endometrial hyperplasia, vaginal irritation, vaginismus, vaginal ulceration.
*   **CNS:** Aggravated migraine, depression, insomnia.
*   **Skin:** Urticaria, rash.
*   **Other:** Fluid retention, weight increase, hypersensitivity [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
*   **CYP3A4 Metabolism:** Estrogens are partially metabolized by CYP3A4. 
    *   **Inducers** (e.g., St. John’s wort, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, rifampin) may decrease plasma estrogen concentrations, potentially reducing efficacy or altering the bleeding profile [US].
    *   **Inhibitors** (e.g., erythromycin, clarithromycin, ketoconazole, ritonavir, grapefruit juice) may increase plasma estrogen concentrations, potentially leading to side effects [US].

### Drug-Laboratory Test Interactions
*   Estrogens may increase TBG, leading to increased total thyroid hormone; however, free T4 and T3 remain unaltered [US].
*   Accelerated prothrombin time and increased levels of various clotting factors (II, VII, VIII, IX, X) [US].
*   Reduced response to metyrapone test [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated. Yuvafem should not be used during pregnancy [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Estrogen administration to nursing women has been shown to decrease the quantity and quality of breast milk. Estrogens are detectable in breast milk [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated; safety and efficacy have not been established in children [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include enough subjects ≥65 years to determine if they respond differently. There is an increased risk of probable dementia reported in the WHIMS study for women in this age group [US].
*   **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** The effect of renal or hepatic impairment on Yuvafem pharmacokinetics has not been studied [US]. However, estrogens may be poorly metabolized in patients with impaired liver function [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Estradiol is the principal intracellular human estrogen and is more potent than its metabolites (estrone, estriol) at the receptor level. It acts by binding to nuclear receptors in estrogen-responsive tissues (e.g., the female reproductive tract) to maintain secondary sexual characteristics [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Vaginal delivery circumvents first-pass metabolism. In a multiple-dose study (10 mcg), mean estradiol (E2) Cave at Day 83 was 5.5 pg/mL [US].
*   **Distribution:** Exogenous estrogens are distributed similarly to endogenous estrogens, largely bound to sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) and albumin [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Metabolized mainly in the liver; estradiol is converted reversibly to estrone and can be converted to estriol (major urinary metabolite). Estrogens undergo enterohepatic recirculation [US].
*   **Elimination:** Excreted in the urine as glucuronide and sulfate conjugates [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **10 mcg Efficacy:** A 12-month, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study (n=309) in postmenopausal women (mean age 57.6) showed Yuvafem 10 mcg was statistically superior to placebo in reducing the severity of most bothersome symptoms (MBS) of atrophic vaginitis. It also significantly improved vaginal cytology (increased superficial cells, decreased parabasal cells) and reduced vaginal pH [US].
*   **Endometrial Safety:** Biopsies from the 10 mcg trials showed that the majority of subjects had atrophic or inactive endometrium at the end of the study; one case of adenocarcinoma and two cases of complex hyperplasia without atypia were reported across various 10 mcg and 25 mcg studies [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
*   **Handling:** Do not refrigerate. Keep out of reach of children [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **US:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
