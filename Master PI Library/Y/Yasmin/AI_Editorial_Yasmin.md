# Editorial Product Information: Yasmin (Drospirenone and Ethinylestradiol)

## Editorial Note
- This document is a clinical synthesis of Product Information (PI) sourced from the **Australian (TGA)** and **US (FDA)** regulatory authorities.
- The purpose of this editorial is to provide a harmonised, clinically accurate overview of **Yasmin** for use by healthcare professionals.
- This synthesis is intended for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI or local clinical guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Drospirenone (a progestin) and Ethinylestradiol (an estrogen).
- **Chemical Class:** Drospirenone is a spironolactone analogue with antimineralocorticoid and antiandrogenic activity. Ethinylestradiol is a synthetic estrogen.
- **Brand Name:** **Yasmin**
- **ATC Code:** G03AA12
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - Combined film-coated tablets in a 28-day calendar pack.
    - Active tablets: 3 mg drospirenone and 0.03 mg ethinylestradiol [Australian, US].
    - Placebo/Inert tablets: Containing lactose but no active hormones.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bayer HealthCare Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US]; Bayer Australia Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Oral Contraception:** Indicated for use by females of reproductive potential to prevent pregnancy [Australian, US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One tablet is taken daily for 28 consecutive days.
- **Cycle Structure:** 21 yellow active tablets followed by 7 white inactive/placebo tablets.
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be taken at approximately the same time every day, preferably after the evening meal or at bedtime, with liquid as needed. Efficacy depends on strict adherence to the regimen.
- **Initiation (First Cycle):**
    - **Day 1 Start:** Tablet-taking begins on the first day of menstrual bleeding. 
    - **Sunday Start [US]:** Start on the first Sunday after menstruation begins. If menstruation starts on a Sunday, start that day. A back-up non-hormonal contraceptive (e.g., condoms) is required for the first 7 days of the first cycle if not starting on Day 1 [Australian, US].
- **Switching from Other Methods:** 
    - **From other COCs:** Start **Yasmin** preferably on the day after the last active tablet of the previous pack, or at the latest, the day after the usual tablet-free or placebo interval.
    - **From Progestogen-only methods:** May switch any day (from minipill) or day of removal (implant/IUS). Back-up contraception is required for the first 7 days.
- **Missed Dose Instructions:**
    - **Late < 12 hours [Australian]:** Take the missed tablet immediately; contraceptive protection is not reduced.
    - **Late > 12 hours [Australian] or missed 1 yellow tablet [US]:** Take as soon as remembered (may result in taking two tablets in one day). 
    - **Missed 2+ yellow tablets:** Requires back-up contraception for 7 days. If missed in Week 3, the placebo phase should be skipped, and the next pack started immediately [Australian, US].
- **Gastrointestinal Disturbances:** If vomiting or severe diarrhoea occurs within 3–4 hours of taking an active tablet, absorption may be incomplete. This should be managed as a missed dose.

## 4. Contraindications
**Yasmin** is contraindicated in women with:
- **Renal Impairment:** [US, Australian].
- **Adrenal Insufficiency:** [US-specified].
- **Hepatic Disease:** Current or history of severe hepatic disease (until liver function tests normalise) or liver tumours (benign or malignant) [Australian, US].
- **Thromboembolic Disorders:** High risk of, or current, venous thromboembolism (VTE) or arterial thromboembolism (ATE), including Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT), Pulmonary Embolism (PE), myocardial infarction, or stroke.
- **Vascular Risk Factors:** Including heavy smoking (>15 cigarettes/day) and age >35 years; severe hypertension; diabetes mellitus with vascular involvement; or severe dyslipoproteinaemia.
- **Migraine:** History of migraine with focal neurological symptoms [Australian] or migraine with aura in women >35 years [US].
- **Hormone-sensitive Malignancy:** Known or suspected breast cancer or other sex steroid-influenced malignancies.
- **Abnormal Uterine Bleeding:** Undiagnosed abnormal bleeding.
- **HCV Therapy:** Concomitant use with Hepatitis C drug combinations containing ombitasvir, paritaprevir, and/or dasabuvir [Australian, US] and pibrentasvir/glecaprevir [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Known or suspected pregnancy.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]:** **Cigarette smoking increases the risk of serious cardiovascular events from combination oral contraceptive (COC) use. This risk increases with age (particularly >35 years) and with the number of cigarettes smoked. COCs should not be used by women over 35 who smoke.**
- **Vascular Risks:** **Yasmin** is associated with an increased risk of VTE compared to non-users. Some epidemiological studies suggest drospirenone-containing COCs carry a higher risk of VTE than those containing levonorgestrel [Australian, US].
- **Hyperkalemia:** Drospirenone has antimineralocorticoid activity. Serum potassium should be monitored during the first cycle in patients on long-term treatment with medications that may increase potassium (e.g., ACE inhibitors, ARBs, potassium-sparing diuretics, NSAIDs) [US].
- **Blood Pressure:** Monitor blood pressure regularly; discontinue if significant elevations occur.
- **Malignant Neoplasms:** Long-term COC use may slightly increase the risk of cervical and breast cancer, though data are inconsistent.
- **Liver Disease:** Discontinue if jaundice or acute liver function disturbances develop.
- **Glucose/Lipid Metabolism:** Monitor prediabetic and diabetic women; COCs may decrease glucose tolerance.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (1–10%)
- Headache/Migraine (10.7–20%)
- Menstrual irregularities/intermenstrual bleeding (13–17%)
- Breast pain/tenderness (8.3–12%)
- Nausea/Vomiting (4.5–9%)
- Abdominal pain (2.3%)
- Mood changes/Depression (2.3–4%)

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Vascular:** DVT, pulmonary embolism, myocardial infarction, stroke, mesenteric/retinal thrombosis.
- **Hepatobiliary:** Liver tumours, gallbladder disease, cholestasis.
- **Metabolic:** Hyperkalemia, increased blood pressure.
- **Immune:** Hypersensitivity (angioedema, rash, urticaria).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Substances that induce CYP3A4 (e.g., phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampin, St. John's Wort) increase the clearance of sex hormones, potentially leading to breakthrough bleeding and contraceptive failure. Use back-up contraception during and for 28 days after cessation [Australian, US].
- **Enzyme Inhibitors:** Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin) may increase plasma concentrations of drospirenone.
- **HCV Combinations:** Co-administration with certain Hepatitis C antivirals (e.g., ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir) may cause significant ALT elevations [Australian, US].
- **Potassium-Increasing Drugs:** Potential for pharmacodynamic interaction resulting in hyperkalemia when used with ACE inhibitors, angiotensin-II receptor antagonists, potassium-sparing diuretics, or NSAIDs.
- **Lamotrigine:** COCs may significantly decrease lamotrigine plasma levels, potentially reducing seizure control.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category: B3.** 
    - **US Recommendation:** Not indicated for use during pregnancy. If pregnancy occurs, **Yasmin** should be discontinued immediately. Epidemiological studies do not show an increased risk of birth defects following accidental use in early pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** COCs may reduce the quantity and change the composition of breast milk. Small amounts of steroids are excreted in milk (infant dose ~0.1% of maternal dose [US]). Generally not recommended until the infant is completely weaned [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in females of reproductive age. Not indicated before menarche [Australian, US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not indicated after menopause [Australian, US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated due to the risk of hyperkalemia. Exposure to drospirenone is increased by approximately 37% in those with moderate renal impairment (CLcr 30-49 mL/min) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic disease. Exposure to drospirenone is approximately three times higher in women with moderate liver impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primarily suppresses ovulation. Secondary mechanisms include changes in cervical secretion (increasing difficulty for sperm penetration) and endometrial changes.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Drospirenone is a spironolactone analogue with antimineralocorticoid and antiandrogenic properties, counteracting estrogen-related sodium retention. It is devoid of androgenic, estrogenic, or glucocorticoid activity.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Drospirenone is rapidly absorbed (bioavailability 76–85%). Ethinylestradiol undergoes significant first-pass metabolism (bioavailability ~40-60%).
    - **Distribution:** Drospirenone binds to serum albumin but not SHBG. Ethinylestradiol is highly bound to albumin (98.5%) and induces SHBG.
    - **Metabolism:** Drospirenone is metabolised by CYP3A4. Ethinylestradiol is metabolised via aromatic hydroxylation.
    - **Excretion:** Drospirenone metabolites are excreted in faeces and urine (ratio 1.2 to 1.4). Ethinylestradiol metabolites are excreted in urine and bile (ratio 4:6).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Contraceptive Efficacy:** In clinical studies, the corrected Pearl Index (discounting user failure) was approximately 0.09 [Australian]. US clinical trials reported pregnancy rates of less than one per 100 woman-years.
- **VTE Risk Studies:** Large prospective cohort studies (EURAS, Ingenix) found the risk of VTE in **Yasmin** users to be comparable to other COCs, including those containing levonorgestrel. However, other database studies (e.g., FDA-funded study 2011) estimated a hazard ratio of 1.5 to 1.8 compared to levonorgestrel-containing COCs [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C [Australian, US].
- Keep out of reach of children.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Poison Schedule:** S4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **US Legal Classification:** Rx-only.
