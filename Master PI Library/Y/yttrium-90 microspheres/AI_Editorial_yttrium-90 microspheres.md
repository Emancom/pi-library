# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising available product information into a single reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP.
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended in the range of 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required. The maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations below 40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments should be guided by trough levels; downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed in concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, particularly in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor for recurrence if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **General Precautions (Oral vs. IV):** The safety of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended for metabolic patients, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
Based on placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients, the following events occurred regardless of causality [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Chest pain (12%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%) |

### Other Reported Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (post-marketing) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent: body odor, gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not formally categorised under letter system in provided source, but clinical data suggests the following]: Reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See **Section 3: Dosage and Administration** for weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for carnitine deficiency in ESRD patients on dialysis. Note that metabolites may accumulate with oral dosing in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
    - **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x/week), trough levels increased from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While IV levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on clinical outcomes in this specific population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Protect from freezing [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
