# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory guidance into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing or dispensing in a specific jurisdiction. Not all information (e.g., Australian or European specificities) was available in the provided source material; where information is absent, it is marked as "Not specified in available sources."

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly in source, but standard for Levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL single-dose vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often provided in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
    - **Frequency:** Doses should be administered every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less frequently than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Up to 300 mg/kg has been administered [US].

- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Adjustment:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- Plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained prior to starting therapy [US].
- **Target Levels:** Plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels is recommended [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of intravenous administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Oral Levocarnitine in Renal Impairment:** The safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>5% in ESRD clinical trials):**
    - **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
- **Other Reported Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting, gastritis, and body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified, but text indicates usage only if clearly needed]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, Saccharomyces, Schizosaccharomyces) were negative. Long-term animal carnitine potential studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in source material beyond general precautions; however, dose selection should generally be cautious [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** ESRD patients require specific post-dialysis dosing (10–20 mg/kg). Note the warning regarding metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) with oral forms in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg confirmed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) in PVC bags [US].
