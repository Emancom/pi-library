# Editorial Product Information: Yaz

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration) and US (Food and Drug Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information for **Yaz** (drospirenone/ethinylestradiol) across multiple regulatory jurisdictions. It is intended as a clinically accurate reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official Product Information approved in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to their local regulatory authority’s approved labeling for prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Drospirenone (3 mg) and Ethinylestradiol (as betadex clathrate) (0.02 mg).
- **Chemical Class:** Combined Oral Contraceptive (COC); Drospirenone is a spironolactone analogue with anti-mineralocorticoid and anti-androgenic activity.
- **Brand Name:** **Yaz**
- **ATC Code:** G03AA12
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated tablets. Each blister pack contains 28 tablets: 24 light pink active tablets (3 mg drospirenone/0.02 mg ethinylestradiol) and 4 white inert/placebo tablets.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bayer HealthCare Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US]; Bayer Australia Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Oral Contraception:** Indicated for the prevention of pregnancy in females of reproductive potential.
- **Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD):** Treatment of symptoms of PMDD in women who choose to use an oral contraceptive for birth control. Efficacy beyond 3 months has not been evaluated, and the product is not indicated for premenstrual syndrome (PMS).
- **Acne Vulgaris:** Treatment of moderate acne vulgaris in females who have achieved menarche and are at least 14 years old [US] or who seek oral contraception [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One tablet is taken daily for 28 consecutive days at the same time each day (preferably after the evening meal or at bedtime).
- **Cycle Instructions:** Tablets must be taken in the order directed on the pack (24 active tablets followed by 4 placebo tablets).
- **Initiation (New Users):**
    - **Day 1 Start:** Start on the first day of the menstrual cycle. Contraceptive protection is immediate.
    - **Sunday Start [US]:** Start on the first Sunday after the onset of menstruation. A non-hormonal back-up method (e.g., condoms) must be used for the first 7 days.
- **Missed Dose Instructions:**
    - **One active tablet missed:** Take as soon as remembered. Take the next tablet at the regular time (may result in two tablets in one day). No back-up method is required.
    - **Two active tablets missed (Week 1 or 2):** Take two tablets on the day remembered and two tablets the next day. Use a back-up method for 7 days.
    - **Two active tablets missed (Week 3 or 4):** [US: Discard rest of pack and start new pack (Day 1 starters) OR keep taking until Sunday, then start new pack (Sunday starters). Use back-up for 7 days.] [Australian: Detailed rules based on the 24/4 regimen are provided; generally, the placebo interval must be omitted to maintain efficacy.]
- **Gastrointestinal Disturbances:** If vomiting or severe diarrhoea occurs within 3–4 hours of tablet intake, absorption may be incomplete. Follow missed pill instructions.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Vascular Disease:** High risk of arterial or venous thrombotic diseases (VTE/ATE), including current or history of DVT, PE, myocardial infarction, stroke, or transient ischaemic attack (TIA).
- **Known Predisposition to Thrombosis:** Such as Factor V Leiden, protein C/S deficiency, or antithrombin-III deficiency.
- **Renal and Adrenal Status:** Renal impairment, acute renal failure, or adrenal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Status:** Severe hepatic disease (until liver function returns to normal), liver tumours (benign or malignant), or current liver disease.
- **Malignancy:** Current or history of breast cancer or other sex steroid-influenced malignancies.
- **Vascular/Neurological Conditions:** Migraine with focal neurological symptoms (or any migraine if >35 years old [US]); uncontrolled hypertension; diabetes mellitus with vascular involvement.
- **Other:** Undiagnosed abnormal uterine bleeding; known or suspected pregnancy; hypersensitivity to ingredients; pancreatitis associated with severe hypertriglyceridemia.
- **Concomitant Medication:** Use of Hepatitis C drug combinations containing ombitasvir, paritaprevir, or dasabuvir.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]:** **Cigarette smoking increases the risk of serious cardiovascular events from COC use.** This risk increases with age (especially >35 years) and with the number of cigarettes smoked. Women over 35 who smoke should not use **Yaz**.
- **Thromboembolic Risk:** Use is associated with an increased risk of VTE/ATE. Some studies suggest a higher risk of VTE with drospirenone-containing COCs compared to those containing levonorgestrel. Discontinue at least 4 weeks before and 2 weeks after major surgery.
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Drospirenone has anti-mineralocorticoid activity. Monitor serum potassium during the first cycle in high-risk patients (e.g., those on long-term treatment with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or potassium-sparing diuretics).
- **Liver Disease:** Discontinue if jaundice or significant liver function disturbances develop.
- **Metabolic Effects:** Monitor prediabetic and diabetic women. COCs may decrease glucose tolerance. 
- **Depression:** Carefully observe women with a history of depression; discontinue if it recurs to a serious degree.
- **Chloasma:** Women with a tendency to chloasma should avoid sun/UV exposure.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Menstrual irregularities (including spotting/metrorrhagia), Nausea [PMDD trials].
- **Common (1–10%):** Headache/migraine, breast pain/tenderness, mood changes (depression, emotional lability), nausea/vomiting [Contraception/Acne trials].
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Abdominal pain, dizziness, acne, pruritus, rash, weight increase, asthenia, decreased libido, vaginal candidiasis.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Thromboembolic events (VTE/ATE), hypertension, tachycardia, hyperkalaemia, gallbladder disease, chloasma, erythema multiforme.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Myocardial infarction, stroke, deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, hepatic adenomas, and hypertensive crisis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Substances like phenytoin, barbiturates, carbamazepine, rifampin, and St. John’s Wort can increase clearance of sex hormones, leading to breakthrough bleeding or contraceptive failure. Use back-up contraception during and for 28 days after use.
- **Enzyme Inhibitors:** Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin) can increase plasma levels of drospirenone.
- **Potassium-Increasing Drugs:** Potential for hyperkalaemia when co-administered with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, potassium-sparing diuretics, or NSAIDs.
- **Effect on Other Drugs:** COCs may increase levels of cyclosporin or decrease levels of lamotrigine (potentially reducing seizure control).
- **HCV Therapy:** Co-administration with ombitasvir/paritaprevir/dasabuvir may cause significant ALT elevations.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - [Australian: Category B3]
    - [US: Contraindicated; discontinue immediately if pregnancy is confirmed.]
    - Epidemiological data do not suggest an increased risk of birth defects if taken inadvertently during early pregnancy.
- **Breastfeeding:** COCs can reduce milk production. Small amounts of steroids are excreted in breast milk. Use is generally not recommended until the mother has completely weaned the child.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established for women of reproductive age. Not indicated before menarche. [US: Acne indication limited to ≥14 years].
- **Elderly Use:** Not indicated after menopause.
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with renal impairment or acute renal failure.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with hepatic disease as long as liver function values have not returned to normal. Drospirenone exposure is increased 3-fold in moderate impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Primarily suppresses ovulation via inhibition of gonadotropins. Secondary mechanisms include changes in cervical secretion (increasing difficulty for sperm entry) and endometrial changes.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Drospirenone (76% bioavailability) and ethinylestradiol (40% bioavailability) are rapidly absorbed. Peak levels reached in 1–2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Drospirenone binds to serum albumin (not SHBG); ethinylestradiol binds highly to albumin and induces SHBG.
    - **Metabolism:** Drospirenone is metabolised by CYP3A4. Ethinylestradiol undergoes significant first-pass metabolism.
    - **Elimination:** Drospirenone terminal half-life is ~30 hours; excreted in urine and faeces. Ethinylestradiol terminal half-life is ~24 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Contraception:** Large multicentre trials established efficacy with a Pearl Index ranging from 0.41 to 1.41 [US/Australian].
- **PMDD:** Two trials showed significant improvement in Daily Record of Severity of Problems (DRSP) scores for **Yaz** versus placebo over 3 cycles.
- **Acne:** Two 6-month trials demonstrated a significant reduction in inflammatory and non-inflammatory lesions compared to placebo, with higher "clear/almost clear" ratings on the Investigator's Global Assessment.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Australian:** Store below 30°C.
- **US:** Store at 25°C (77°F); excursions permitted to 15–30°C (59–86°F).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **US:** Rx-only.
