# Editorial Product Information: Yupelri (revefenacin)

## Editorial Note
This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of product information for **Yupelri (revefenacin)**, intended for use by healthcare professionals. Information has been synthesized from the official Product Information document provided by the US (FDA). This is an editorial synthesis and should not be considered a substitute for the official Product Information document required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
-   **Active ingredient (INN/generic name) and chemical class:** Revefenacin, a long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA), also referred to as an anticholinergic.
-   **Brand name(s) by jurisdiction:** **Yupelri** [US]
-   **ATC code:** Not specified in available sources.
-   **Dosage form(s) and strength(s) by jurisdiction:** Inhalation solution, 175 mcg revefenacin per 3 mL in unit-dose vials [US].
-   **Route(s) of administration:** Oral inhalation.
-   **Manufacturer/sponsor by jurisdiction:** Mylan Specialty L.P. (Manufactured for) and licensed from Theravance Biopharma Ireland Limited [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
-   **Yupelri** is indicated for the maintenance treatment of patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
-   **General dosing (adult):** The recommended dosage is one 175 mcg **Yupelri** unit-dose vial, administered by oral inhalation once daily using a standard jet nebulizer with a mouthpiece connected to an air compressor [US].
-   **Administration instructions:**
    -   For oral inhalation use only; **Yupelri** must not be swallowed or injected [US].
    -   Administer via a standard jet nebulizer with a mouthpiece and an air compressor. The safety and efficacy have been established with specific nebulizer systems (PARI LC® Sprint nebulizer and PARI Trek® S compressor), and have not been established for non-compressor based nebulizer systems [US].
    -   Do not mix **Yupelri** with other medications in the nebulizer, as drug compatibility, efficacy, and safety have not been established [US].
    -   The unit-dose vial should only be removed from its foil pouch and opened immediately before use. The vial and any residual contents must be discarded after a single use [US].
    -   Do not use if the solution is not clear and colorless [US].
-   **Titration schedules:** No titration schedule is applicable; **Yupelri** is administered as a fixed once-daily dose [US].
-   **Missed dose instructions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
-   **Yupelri** is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to revefenacin or any component of the product [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
-   **Boxed warnings:** No specific boxed warnings are detailed in the provided document.
-   **General warnings and precautions:**
    -   **Not for Acute Symptoms:** **Yupelri** is a once-daily maintenance treatment for COPD and should not be used for the relief of acute bronchospasm or for acutely deteriorating COPD. Extra doses should not be used for acute symptoms, which should be treated with an inhaled, short-acting beta2-agonist. Increasing the daily dose beyond recommended levels is not appropriate for worsening symptoms [US].
    -   **Paradoxical Bronchospasm:** As with other inhaled medications, **Yupelri** can cause life-threatening paradoxical bronchospasm. If this occurs, discontinue **Yupelri** immediately, treat with an inhaled short-acting bronchodilator, and institute alternative therapy [US].
    -   **Worsening of Narrow-Angle Glaucoma:** Use with caution in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma. Patients should be alert for symptoms such as eye pain or discomfort, blurred vision, visual halos, or colored images associated with red eyes and corneal edema. Advise patients to seek immediate medical attention if these symptoms develop [US].
    -   **Worsening of Urinary Retention:** Use with caution in patients with urinary retention, particularly those with prostatic hyperplasia or bladder-neck obstruction. Patients should be alert for symptoms such as difficulty or painful urination. Advise patients to seek immediate medical attention if these symptoms develop [US].
    -   **Immediate Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Immediate hypersensitivity reactions may occur. If such a reaction occurs, discontinue **Yupelri** and consider alternative treatments [US].
-   **Monitoring requirements:** Patients should be monitored for signs and symptoms of paradoxical bronchospasm, narrow-angle glaucoma, urinary retention, and hypersensitivity reactions [US].
-   **Specific clinical precautions:** Caution is advised in patients with pre-existing narrow-angle glaucoma, prostatic hyperplasia, or bladder-neck obstruction due to the potential for worsening conditions [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions with an incidence of ≥2% and more common than placebo, based on 12-week placebo-controlled trials [US]:

-   **Common (1–10%):**
    -   Cough (4%)
    -   Nasopharyngitis (4%)
    -   Upper respiratory tract infection (3%)
    -   Headache (4%)
    -   Back pain (2%)
-   **Other adverse reactions (incidence ≥1.0%, <2.0%, and >placebo) included:** Hypertension, dizziness, oropharyngeal pain, and bronchitis [US].
-   **Postmarketing Experience:** Dry mouth has been reported during post-approval use [US].
-   **Serious adverse effects:** Clinically significant adverse reactions described in Warnings and Precautions (Section 5) include paradoxical bronchospasm, worsening of narrow-angle glaucoma, worsening of urinary retention, and immediate hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
-   **Pharmacokinetic interactions:**
    -   **Transporter-Related Drug Interactions:** Coadministration of **Yupelri** with OATP1B1 and OATP1B3 inhibitors (e.g., rifampicin, cyclosporine) is not recommended, as it may lead to increased systemic exposure of the active metabolite of revefenacin [US]. Revefenacin and its active metabolite do not inhibit CYP450 isoforms (CYP1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4/5) nor do they induce CYP1A2, 2B6, or 3A4/5 [US]. Revefenacin is a substrate of P-gp and BCRP, but neither revefenacin nor its active metabolite inhibits these efflux transporters or the uptake transporters OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OAT1, OAT3, or OCT2 [US].
-   **Pharmacodynamic interactions:**
    -   **Other Anticholinergics:** Avoid coadministration of **Yupelri** with other anticholinergic-containing medications due to the potential for additive anticholinergic adverse effects (e.g., worsening glaucoma or urinary retention) [US].
-   **Clinically significant interactions with management notes:**
    -   OATP1B1/OATP1B3 inhibitors: Coadministration not recommended [US].
    -   Other anticholinergics: Avoid coadministration [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
-   **Pregnancy:**
    -   **Risk Summary:** There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of **Yupelri** in pregnant women. Animal reproduction studies (subcutaneous administration in rats and rabbits) showed no evidence of fetal harm at exposures significantly higher than the maximum recommended human dose. Women should be advised to contact their physician if they become pregnant during treatment [US].
    -   **Pregnancy Category:** The provided document does not use a letter-based pregnancy category system (e.g., A, B, C, D, X) but rather the current [US] FDA "Risk Summary, Clinical Considerations, Data" format.
-   **Breastfeeding:**
    -   **Risk Summary:** There is no information regarding the presence of revefenacin in human milk, its effects on the breastfed infant, or its effects on milk production. Revefenacin was detected in the milk of lactating rats. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding, the mother's clinical need for **Yupelri**, and any potential adverse effects on the breastfed infant from **Yupelri** or the underlying maternal condition should be considered [US].
-   **Fertility:** In nonclinical studies, revefenacin had no effects on male or female fertility or reproductive performance in rats at subcutaneous doses approximately 30 times the maximum recommended human dose [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
-   **Paediatric use:** The safety and effectiveness of **Yupelri** have not been established in paediatric patients, and it is not indicated for use in children [US].
-   **Elderly use:** No dosage adjustment is required in geriatric patients. Clinical trials included 441 subjects aged 65 years and older (101 subjects aged 75 years and older), showing no overall differences in safety or effectiveness compared to younger subjects. However, greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
-   **Renal impairment:** No dosage adjustment is required for patients with renal impairment. Monitor for systemic antimuscarinic side effects in COPD patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min) [US].
-   **Hepatic impairment:** Avoid use in patients with any degree of hepatic impairment. While systemic exposure of revefenacin is unchanged in moderate hepatic impairment, the active metabolite's exposure is increased. The safety of **Yupelri** has not been evaluated in COPD patients with mild-to-severe hepatic impairment, nor in subjects with severe hepatic impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
-   **Mechanism of action:** Revefenacin is a long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA). It exerts its pharmacological effects in the airways by inhibiting M3 receptors on smooth muscle, leading to bronchodilation. Its antagonism is competitive and reversible [US].
-   **Pharmacodynamics:**
    -   **Cardiac Electrophysiology:** A single supratherapeutic dose of 700 mcg (4 times the recommended dose) of revefenacin did not have any effect on QTc interval prolongation in healthy subjects [US].
-   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    -   **Absorption:** Following inhaled administration, peak plasma concentrations (Cmax) of revefenacin and its active metabolite occurred between 14 to 41 minutes. The absolute bioavailability after oral administration is low (<3%) [US].
    -   **Distribution:** Revefenacin has an extensive volume of distribution (218 L). Plasma protein binding is approximately 71% for revefenacin and 42% for its active metabolite [US].
    -   **Metabolism:** Revefenacin is primarily metabolized via hydrolysis of its primary amide to a carboxylic acid, which is its major active metabolite. Plasma exposures of the active metabolite typically exceed those of revefenacin by 4- to 6-fold (based on AUC) and can contribute to systemic antimuscarinic effects [US].
    -   **Elimination:** The terminal half-life of revefenacin and its active metabolite after once-daily dosing in COPD patients ranges from 22 to 70 hours. Following intravenous administration, approximately 54% of total radioactivity was recovered in feces and 27% in urine. Minimal renal excretion (<1%) of revefenacin and its active metabolite occurs after inhaled administration in COPD patients [US].
    -   **Special Populations:** Population pharmacokinetic analysis revealed no clinically significant effects of age, gender, smoking status, or weight on systemic exposure of revefenacin or its active metabolite [US].
    -   **Hepatic Impairment:** In moderate hepatic impairment, revefenacin exposure is unchanged, but active metabolite exposure is increased [US].
    -   **Renal Impairment:** In severe renal impairment, Cmax of revefenacin increased 1.5-fold and active metabolite Cmax up to 2-fold. AUC of revefenacin increased up to 2.3-fold and active metabolite AUC up to 2.5-fold. **Yupelri** has not been evaluated in end-stage renal disease [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
-   **Key pivotal trial summaries:**
    -   **Yupelri** efficacy was established in two 12-week, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, Phase 3 trials (Trial 1 and Trial 2) in patients with moderate to very severe COPD. A total of 1,229 subjects were enrolled, with 395 receiving **Yupelri** 175 mcg once daily.
    -   The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in trough (pre-dose) forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1) at Day 85. In both trials, **Yupelri** 175 mcg demonstrated significant improvement in lung function compared to placebo [US].
    -   LS mean differences in trough FEV1 from placebo at Day 85 were 146 mL (95% CI: 103.7, 188.8) in Trial 1 and 147 mL (95% CI: 97.0, 197.1) in Trial 2 [US].
    -   Peak FEV1 improvement on Day 1 relative to placebo was 133 mL and 129 mL in Trials 1 and 2, respectively [US].
    -   Improvements in the St. George's Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ) responder rate (improvement ≥4 points) were observed in Trial 1 (49% vs 34% for placebo; Odds Ratio: 2.11) and Trial 2 (45% vs 39% for placebo; Odds Ratio: 1.31) [US].
    -   A 52-week, open-label, active-controlled safety study (n=1,055) comparing **Yupelri** to tiotropium showed a consistent safety profile with the 12-week studies [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
-   **Storage temperature and conditions:** Store **Yupelri** in its protective foil pouch at room temperature, between 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F). Protect from direct sunlight and excessive heat [US].
-   **Shelf life:** Do not use **Yupelri** after the expiration date printed on the foil pouch and vial [US].
-   **Any special handling:** The unit-dose vial should only be removed from the foil pouch and opened immediately before use. Discard the vial and any residual content after use. Discard any solution that is not clear and colorless [US].
