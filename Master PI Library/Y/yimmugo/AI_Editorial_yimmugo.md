# Editorial Product Information: YIMMUGO (immune globulin intravenous, human - dira)

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs), specifically clinical pharmacists and prescribers.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in any particular region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Human immune globulin G (IgG). 
- **Chemical Class:** Plasma derivative; polyclonal immunoglobulin G.
- **Brand Name:** **YIMMUGO** [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (typically J06BA02 for IGIV).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 10% liquid solution (100 mg/mL) for intravenous injection. Available in:
    - 5 g in 50 mL
    - 10 g in 100 mL
    - 20 g in 200 mL
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Biotest AG (Manufactured in Germany); Distributed by Kedrion Biopharma Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Primary Humoral Immunodeficiency (PI):** Indicated for the treatment of PI in adults and paediatric patients 2 years of age or older [US]. This includes, but is not limited to:
    - Congenital agammaglobulinemia.
    - Common variable immunodeficiency (CVID).
    - X-linked agammaglobulinemia (XLA).
    - Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome.
    - Severe combined immunodeficiencies (SCID).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (PI)
The recommended dose for PI is **300–800 mg/kg (3–8 mL/kg)** administered every 3 to 4 weeks [US]. Dosage should be adjusted over time based on clinical response and the achievement of desired trough IgG levels.

### Administration and Titration
**YIMMUGO** must be administered at room or body temperature. It should not be mixed with other IGIV products or medications.

| Infusion Number | Initial Rate | Maintenance Rate (if tolerated) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st Infusion** | 0.5 mg/kg/min (0.005 mL/kg/min) for 30 minutes | Increase every 30 minutes up to 3.0 mg/kg/min (0.03 mL/kg/min) |
| **2nd Infusion onwards** | 0.5 mg/kg/min (0.005 mL/kg/min) for 30 minutes | Gradually increase up to 13 mg/kg/min (0.13 mL/kg/min) |

### Measles Exposure Management
- **Post-exposure Prophylaxis:** If a patient is exposed to measles, a dose of 400 mg/kg administered within 6 days of exposure may be prudent.
- **High-risk Maintenance:** For patients at risk of future exposure receiving < 530 mg/kg every 3-4 weeks, the dose should be increased to at least 530 mg/kg to maintain protective serum levels (>240 mIU/mL) [US].

### Preparation and Handling
- Inspect visually; solution should be clear to slightly opalescent and colorless to pale yellow.
- **Do not shake.**
- **Do not dilute.**
- Single-use only; discard any unused portion.

## 4. Contraindications
- History of anaphylactic or severe systemic reactions to human immune globulin [US].
- IgA-deficient patients who possess antibodies against IgA and a history of hypersensitivity [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Thrombosis, Renal Dysfunction, and Acute Renal Failure [US]
- **Thrombosis:** May occur regardless of the route of administration. Risk factors include advanced age, prolonged immobilisation, hypercoagulable states, history of thrombosis, use of estrogens, indwelling central catheters, and cardiovascular risk factors.
- **Renal Dysfunction/Failure:** Acute renal failure, osmotic nephrosis, and death can occur. Risk is higher with products containing sucrose (Note: **YIMMUGO** does **not** contain sucrose).
- **Risk Mitigation:** For at-risk patients, use the minimum dose and infusion rate practicable. Ensure adequate hydration and monitor for symptoms of thrombosis and hyperviscosity.

### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity:** Severe reactions, including anaphylaxis, may occur. Epinephrine must be available.
- **Hemolysis:** IGIV products can contain blood group antibodies (hemolysins). Monitor for clinical signs of hemolysis and significant drops in hemoglobin/hematocrit.
- **Aseptic Meningitis Syndrome (AMS):** Reported with IGIV, usually within hours to 2 days post-treatment. More frequent with high doses (2 g/kg) or rapid infusion.
- **Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI):** Characterised by severe respiratory distress and pulmonary edema. Typically occurs within 1–6 hours post-infusion.
- **Transmissible Infectious Agents:** As a product derived from human blood, there is a theoretical risk of transmitting infectious agents (viruses, vCJD agent). 
- **Hyperproteinemia/Viscosity/Hyponatremia:** May occur; clinically distinguish true hyponatremia from pseudohyponatremia to avoid inappropriate treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Reactions
In clinical trials, the most common reactions ($\ge$ 5% of patients) included:
- Headache (19%)
- Upper respiratory tract infections (12%)
- Fatigue (8%)
- Nausea (6%)
- Increased blood pressure (6%)

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylactic reaction [US].
- Severe neutropenia [US].
- Hemolysis [US].

### Postmarketing Experience (IGIV Class)
Observed reactions include cardiac arrest, thromboembolism, vascular collapse, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, acute renal failure, pancytopenia, and ARDS [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Live Virus Vaccines:** Immunoglobulin may impair the efficacy of live attenuated vaccines (e.g., measles, mumps, rubella, varicella) for 3 months or longer. The immunizing physician should be informed of recent IGIV therapy.
- **Loop Diuretics:** Concomitant use should be avoided as it may increase blood viscosity and the risk of thromboembolic events [US].
- **Serological Testing:** Passively transferred antibodies may yield false-positive results in serological tests (e.g., Coombs’ test) [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not assigned under PLLR]. No human data are available to determine drug-associated risks. Animal reproduction studies have not been conducted. IgG is known to cross the placenta increasingly after 30 weeks of gestation. Use only if clearly needed.
- **Breastfeeding:** IgG is excreted into human milk. Consider the developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother’s clinical need for the drug and potential adverse effects on the infant.
- **Fertility:** No animal studies have evaluated effects on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Studied in 18 patients (ages 2 to 16). Dosing requirements were found to be similar to adults. Safety and efficacy in children under 2 years have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$ 65 years):** Limited data available. Dose selection should be cautious, generally starting at the low end of the range due to higher frequencies of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Use the minimum infusion rate practicable. Monitor renal function (BUN, serum creatinine) and urine output [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically detailed; generally follows cautious geriatric/renal dosing principles.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Provides a broad spectrum of opsonizing and neutralizing IgG antibodies against various pathogens and toxins. It also possesses immunomodulatory effects, though these are not fully elucidated.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Restores abnormally low IgG levels to the normal range. Contains IgG subclasses in similar proportions to normal human plasma.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Mean Half-life (Adults):** 24.8 to 29.9 days [US].
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is approximately 53.5–69.4 mL/kg [US].
    - **Note:** No clinically relevant differences in PK were observed between 3-week and 4-week dosing schedules.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Trial 991 (NCT02810444):** An open-label, multinational study of 67 patients with PI.
- **Primary Endpoint:** Rate of serious bacterial infections (SBI).
- **Results:** The annualized rate of SBIs was 0.07 per person-year (upper one-sided 99% CI: 0.21), which met the predefined success criterion of < 1.0 SBI per year. All five SBIs observed during the trial were bacterial pneumonia [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (36°F to 46°F) [US].
- **Room Temperature Option:** Within its shelf life, the product may be stored at $\le$ 25°C (up to 77°F) for a **single period not exceeding 12 months**. Once removed from refrigeration, it must not be returned.
- **Protection:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Do not freeze.**

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as **Rx-only** [US].
