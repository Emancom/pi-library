# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises the available clinical data into a concise reference.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
*   **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
*   **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors):** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
*   **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
*   **Acute Management:** A loading dose is often administered in patients with severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and no less than every 6 hours [US].
*   **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
*   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2-to-3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
*   **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation of therapy may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations below the normal range (40 to 50 µmol/L). Adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
*   **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of injection. If a severe reaction occurs, treatment must be discontinued immediately [US].
*   **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients both with and without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
*   **Monitoring Requirements:**
    *   **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** It is recommended to obtain levels prior to therapy and then weekly or monthly. The target plasma free carnitine concentration is between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
    *   **Clinical Monitoring:** Routine monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and overall clinical status [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
In clinical trials of dialysis patients, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
*   **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%).
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%).
*   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%).
*   **Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough (10–18%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
*   Anaphylaxis and other severe hypersensitivity reactions [US].
*   Seizures [US].

### Other Effects
*   Transient nausea and vomiting, gastritis, and a "fishy" body odor have been observed [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
*   **Management:** It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not explicitly lettered, but evaluated as follows]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal constituent of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, exogenous administration increases milk carnitine concentrations. The risks to the infant of excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies revealed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing in paediatric patients is based on body weight, identical to the adult protocol for metabolic disorders (50 mg/kg) [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
*   **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide). **Intravenous** administration is indicated for carnitine deficiency in dialysis patients [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine helps clear accumulated acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitines, which are then excreted. This prevents the autointoxication and metabolic acidosis associated with organic acidurias [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
*   **Elimination:** Following a single 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours, and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Bioavailability Study:** In healthy volunteers (n=15), oral tablets and solution were bioequivalent, with absolute bioavailability of approximately 15% after correcting for endogenous levels [US].
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), mean pre-dialysis trough levels increased from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While increases in carnitine levels are proven, the direct impact on signs, symptoms, or clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
*   **Preservation:** The formulation contains no preservative; any unused portion of a single-dose vial must be discarded [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Rx-only [US].
*   **Poison/Schedule Classification:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
