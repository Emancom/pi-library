# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals. It synthesises available regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered based on clinical requirements [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### General Instructions
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to parenteral therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Inspection:** Inspect parenterals visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known based on available clinical data [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** While not directly applicable to the injection, note that chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event (≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, back pain, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, palpitations, tachycardia, cardiovascular disorder |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, anorexia, constipation |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight change |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, rhinitis, cough increase, dyspnea, sinusitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash |
| **Other** | Leg cramps, taste perversion, urinary tract infection |

### Other Reported Effects
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
- **Less Frequent:** Gastritis and body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. **Recommendation:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned (Current US labeling format). 
    - **Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. Weigh the risks to the child against the benefits to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients; see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing for ESRD patients is specifically addressed (see Section 3). The safety and efficacy of the *oral* formulation have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with metabolic defects or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids and clears toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times per week after dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations from 20 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L after 8 weeks. Trough concentrations remained stable or increased over the course of the study [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL concentration). Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard unused portion as it contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only) [US].
