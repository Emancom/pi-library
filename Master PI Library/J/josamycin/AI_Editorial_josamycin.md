# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified (available as generic/USP-titled injection) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight). Administration should occur as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].

### Dosing by Indication
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and never less frequent than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maintenance (Metabolic):** Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the range of 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been used [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration before use [US].
- For ESRD, initiation of therapy may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations below 40–50 µmol/L [US].
- Dose adjustments in ESRD (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Caution:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before starting therapy and monitor weekly/monthly. Monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects

| System Organ Class | Very Common (>10%) | Common (1–10%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General/Body as a Whole** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Chest pain (12%), Asthenia (9%) | Accidental injury, Fever, Abdominal pain, Back pain, Allergic reaction |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%) | Tachycardia, Palpitations, Arrhythmia, Atrial fibrillation |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%) | Nausea, Anorexia, Dyspepsia, Melena |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | - | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%), Peripheral edema (5%), Weight increase/decrease |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%) | Insomnia, Depression, Anxiety, Vertigo, Drug dependence |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough (9%), Rhinitis (9%) | Dyspnea (7%), Bronchitis, Sinusitis |
| **Other** | - | Pruritus, Rash, Eye disorder, Urinary tract infection, Kidney failure |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/unspecified]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk. The risks to the infant of excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Dosing is adjusted based on trough plasma carnitine concentrations [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), mean pre-dialysis levocarnitine levels rose from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-dependent increases in trough concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the effect on clinical outcomes in this specific population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
