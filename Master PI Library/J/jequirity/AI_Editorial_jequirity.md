# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically applicable format. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference by healthcare professionals and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose is **50 mg/kg** administered as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - Maximum administered dose reported is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: **10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg** (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended. Assessment should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including **anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm**, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Precaution):** While this PI covers the injection, it is noted that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials of ESRD patients (occurring at ≥5% frequency) include:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction (Frequency ≥5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, dyspepsia, anorexia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, depression, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash |

- **Serious/Specific Effects:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent body odor and gastritis [US]. Seizures and hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis) are significant risks identified post-marketing.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Noted as **Category not specified** (historical context: animal studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm). Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal constituent of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Consider the risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant versus the benefit of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), which applies to paediatric populations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** In ESRD patients on dialysis, the injection is indicated for carnitine deficiency. However, caution is advised regarding metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) if oral forms are used chronically in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, facilitating oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with defects in fatty acid metabolism or organic acidopathies, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of toxic acyl-CoA esters by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Clinical efficacy was demonstrated in a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg levocarnitine three times weekly. Treatment resulted in significant increases in plasma levocarnitine concentrations (trough levels increased from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L over 8 weeks) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) at room temperature [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Rx-only.
- **Poison/Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
