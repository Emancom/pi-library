# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for regional legal compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Loading/Maintenance:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing intervals should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Dose Range:** Subsequent daily doses typically range from 50 mg/kg to 300 mg/kg based on clinical requirement. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Adjustments:** Dose reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Monitor plasma carnitine concentrations weekly or monthly. Free carnitine levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in both patients with and without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a known seizure history, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral formulation precaution):** While the IV form is indicated for ESRD, high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain are common. Body odor and gastritis are reported less frequently [US].
- **Trial Data (ESRD Patients):** Adverse events occurring at ≥5% (regardless of causality) in clinical trials include:
    - **General:** Chest pain (12%), Flu syndrome (25%), Headache (22%), Infection (15%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%).
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%).
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%).
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), Hypervolemia (5%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%).
    - **Neurologic:** Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. Frequent monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Noted as [US Category not assigned; text states "only if clearly needed"]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk; however, supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant should be weighed against maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no evidence of impaired fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** IV formulation is specifically used in ESRD; however, caution is advised regarding toxic metabolite accumulation if oral forms are used chronically in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, the mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours and the terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in this population remains undetermined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx only) [US].
