# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official Product Information for levocarnitine, designed as a concise reference for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States (FDA) source was provided for evaluation.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (L-carnitine); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Intravenous Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg based on dry body weight.

### Administration Instructions
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels and monitor weekly/monthly. Free carnitine levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

### Titration
- **ESRD:** Dose adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, potentially down to 5 mg/kg, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine concentrations (target: 40–50 µmol/L) [US].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- None [US].

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Neurologic Effects (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **INR Monitoring:** Increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) has been reported when used concurrently with warfarin. Monitor INR levels at initiation and following dose adjustments [US].
- **Plasma Carnitine:** Regular monitoring of trough levels is required to guide dosing, particularly in ESRD and metabolic patients [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
Data derived from clinical trials in ESRD patients on hemodialysis [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), fever (7%), asthenia (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), chest pain (12%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Anaphylaxis and Severe Hypersensitivity:** Rare but potentially life-threatening [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with and without history [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Concomitant use may lead to an increased INR and risk of bleeding. Close monitoring of anticoagulation status is mandatory [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound, reducing the likelihood of displacement-based interactions [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified by letter, but indicated for use "only if clearly needed." 
    - **Data:** Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human surface area dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine after exogenous administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal therapy against potential infant exposure [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impairment in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg), which applies to paediatric patients. Use in ESRD paediatric patients is implied by weight-based dosing (10–20 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects over 65 to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **Intravenous:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis.
    - **Oral:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High oral doses in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with specific metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; distribution half-life is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients (n=12), 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean trough carnitine levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].
- **Stability:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL in PVC bags [US].

- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
