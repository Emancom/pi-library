# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the current local PI for specific legal and regulatory requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP), a carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    - Loading doses are often used in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours. 
    - Subsequent daily doses range between 50 mg/kg and a maximum recorded dose of 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments: Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations [US].

### Monitoring and Administration
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating parenteral therapy.
- Monitor plasma free carnitine levels weekly or monthly; levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. If severe reactions occur, treatment must be discontinued [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy [US].
- **Metabolic Metabolite Accumulation:** Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of the patients treated.

- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (30%), headache (22%), flu syndrome (25%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough (9%), dyspnea (7%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Abdominal pain, nausea, gastritis, body odor, asthenia, fever, infection, tachycardia, hypercalcemia, and dizziness [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** 
    - Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and increased seizure activity [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustment in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specifically assigned (lettered categories discontinued); however, reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - Not specifically studied in humans. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. 
    - The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug should be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg in metabolic disorders); see Section 3 for details [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Information not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - Use with caution; high oral doses may result in metabolite accumulation. 
    - Intravenous levocarnitine is removed by hemodialysis. 
    - Specific dosing is provided for ESRD patients (see Section 3) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Information not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine helps alleviate metabolic abnormalities in patients with inborn errors (e.g., glutaric aciduria II, methyl malonic aciduria) where toxic organic acids accumulate.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV thrice weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in ESRD patients showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks. While carnitine concentrations increased, the effect on clinical outcomes in this population remains undetermined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].
