# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information has been synthesized from the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonized editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It aims to provide a clear, clinically accurate synthesis of the available regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a supplement to, not a substitute for, the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg may be required based on clinical response.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
    - **Administration:** Administer as a slow 2–3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Dose Adjustment:** Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate medical treatment [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:**
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels.
    - Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target range 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Caution):** While this PI covers the injection, it is noted that chronic high-dose **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequency is derived from clinical trials in ESRD patients on hemodialysis.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Injection site reaction (33–38%), pain (30–35%), pharyngitis (15–27%), headache (3–37%), hypertension (18–21%), nausea (5–12%), vomiting (9–21%), diarrhea (9–35%), dizziness (10–18%), cough (10–18%) [US]. |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, infection, arrhythmia, tachycardia, hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, anemia, pruritus [US]. |
| **Serious / Postmarketing** | Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema, and seizures [US]. |

*Note: Body odor has been reported as a less frequent adverse reaction [US].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. Monitoring of INR is recommended at treatment initiation and after dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Levocarnitine is not protein-bound [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Not formally assigned a letter category in this version of labeling. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (3.8x human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is naturally present in human milk. Supplemental studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after exogenous administration. In nursing mothers, the risk of excess carnitine intake by the infant must be weighed against the maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg). See Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Used therapeutically in ESRD patients post-dialysis. Lower maintenance doses (5 mg/kg) may be required based on trough levels [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities caused by the accumulation of toxic organic acids and restores plasma carnitine levels depleted by dialysis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; volume of distribution not specified.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% excreted in urine over 24 hours following a single IV dose. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L by week 8.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Vial Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison/Schedule classification not applicable; regulated under NDC 36000-368-10 [US].
