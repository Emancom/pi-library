# Editorial Product Information: ibuprofen + codeine

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **ibuprofen + codeine**
- Chemical class:
  - Ibuprofen: non‑steroidal anti‑inflammatory drug (NSAID)
  - Codeine: opioid analgesic (codeine phosphate hemihydrate referenced in sources)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Oral

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Temporary relief of acute moderate pain and inflammation in patients over 12 years of age.
- Use should be limited to patients for whom other treatment options (including non‑opioid analgesics) are ineffective, not tolerated or otherwise inadequate to provide appropriate pain management.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
- Strength (per tablet as provided in sources): Ibuprofen 200 mg and codeine phosphate 12.8 mg.
- General dosing (adults and children ≥ 12 years):
  - Initial dose: two tablets taken with fluid.
  - Maintenance: one or two tablets every 4 hours as necessary.
  - Maximum: 6 tablets in any 24‑hour period.
  - Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration necessary to relieve symptoms.
- Administration instructions:
  - Take with fluid.
  - Patients should be counselled to avoid alcohol while taking the product.
- Titration / cessation:
  - When discontinuing in a patient who may be physically dependent, do not stop abruptly; taper gradually.
  - General tapering guidance reported: dose reduction of no more than 10–25% every 2–4 weeks, individualized to patient response and withdrawal symptoms.
- Dosing by indication: Not specified in available sources beyond the general regimen above.
- Missed dose instructions: Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
(All contraindications listed in source material are included.)
- Known hypersensitivity or idiosyncratic reaction to ibuprofen, codeine, other opioid analgesics, or any excipient.
- History of hypersensitivity reactions (e.g., asthma, rhinitis, angioedema, bronchospasm or urticaria) in response to ibuprofen, acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin) or other NSAIDs.
- Active, or history of recurrent, peptic ulcer/haemorrhage; active gastrointestinal bleeding or peptic ulceration.
- Severe respiratory disease, acute respiratory disease, or respiratory depression.
- Chronic constipation and active alcoholism.
- During labour when delivery of a premature infant is anticipated (risk of neonatal withdrawal).
- Diarrhoea caused by pseudomembranous colitis or poisoning (until causative organism/toxin eliminated), since codeine may slow elimination and prolong diarrhoea.
- Concomitant use with other NSAIDs, including COX‑2 selective inhibitors.
- Significant heart disease/cardiovascular conditions (see Warnings — regional specificity below).
- Renal problems (see Warnings).
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- Use of ibuprofen during the third trimester of pregnancy.
- Concomitant treatment with monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs), or within 14 days of stopping MAOI therapy.
- Treatment of perioperative pain in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery.
- Use of codeine‑containing products is contraindicated in women during breastfeeding.
- Patients aged below 12 years.
- Patients aged 12–18 years in whom respiratory function might be compromised, including post‑tonsillectomy and/or adenoidectomy for obstructive sleep apnoea.
- Patients who are CYP2D6 ultra‑rapid metabolisers.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed / highlighted warnings
- **Hazardous and harmful use / addiction potential:** Contains an opioid; risk of misuse, abuse and addiction exists even at recommended doses. Assess patient risk prior to prescribing and monitor regularly.
- **Life‑threatening respiratory depression:** Serious, life‑threatening or fatal respiratory depression may occur, particularly on initiation or following dose increases and in patients with risk factors (see below).
- **Concomitant use with benzodiazepines and other CNS depressants:** Co‑administration with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, antihistamines, tricyclic antidepressants, antipsychotics, cannabis, other CNS depressants, or alcohol can result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma and death. Limit dosages and durations; monitor closely.

(These boxed warnings are presented in the source material as principal safety concerns.) [Australian]

### General warnings and precautions
- Gastrointestinal:
  - NSAID therapy can cause gastrointestinal bleeding, ulceration and perforation, which may be fatal and can occur at any time, with or without warning symptoms.
  - Risk increases with higher NSAID doses, prior history of ulcer/haemorrhage, and in the elderly. Use the lowest effective dose; monitor for abdominal symptoms and GI bleeding.
  - Exercise caution in patients with inflammatory bowel disease (ulcerative colitis, Crohn’s disease).
  - Avoid concomitant medications that increase bleeding/ulcer risk (e.g., oral corticosteroids, anticoagulants such as warfarin, selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, antiplatelet agents).
  - If GI bleeding or ulceration occurs, withdraw therapy.
- Respiratory:
  - Bronchospasm may be precipitated in patients with or a history of bronchial asthma or allergic disease.
  - Opioid component can cause respiratory depression — exercise caution in patients with compromised respiratory function, severe COPD, or acute respiratory disease. Opioids are contraindicated in severe respiratory disease.
- Hepatic:
  - Use with caution in hepatic impairment; monitor for signs of hepatotoxicity (see Adverse Effects for symptom list).
  - Severe hepatic impairment is a contraindication.
- Renal:
  - May impair renal function; renal function may deteriorate, especially in dehydrated patients and in paediatric patients.
  - Use with caution and monitor renal function in at‑risk patients.
- Cardiovascular:
  - NSAIDs have been associated with increased risk of serious cardiovascular thrombotic events, myocardial infarction and stroke, which may increase with dose and duration. Patients with cardiovascular disease or risk factors may be at greater risk.
  - Fluid retention, hypertension and oedema have been reported; NSAIDs may blunt the effect of antihypertensive agents.
  - Use with caution in patients with hypertension or fluid retention; avoid in certain cardiac conditions as noted under Contraindications.
- Neurologic / psychiatric:
  - Opioid therapy can cause tolerance, physical dependence and withdrawal on cessation or rapid dose reduction. Monitor for signs of misuse, abuse and dependence.
  - Opioid‑induced hyperalgesia may occur, particularly at high doses; consider dose reduction or opioid rotation if suspected.
  - Opioids can cause central sleep‑related breathing disorders including central sleep apnoea and sleep‑related hypoxaemia; opioid use increases CSA risk in a dose‑dependent manner.
- Skin hypersensitivity:
  - Severe cutaneous reactions (including acute generalised exanthematous pustulosis [AGEP], exfoliative dermatitis, Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms [DRESS], and toxic epidermal necrolysis) have been reported. Most cases occur early in therapy (majority within first month). Discontinue at first sign of rash or mucosal lesions.
- Masking of infection:
  - Ibuprofen may mask signs of infection, potentially delaying diagnosis and treatment (examples noted include bacterial pneumonia and bacterial complications of varicella).
- Special situations:
  - Exercise caution after recent gastrointestinal surgery or in paralytic ileus — codeine may reduce gastrointestinal motility.
  - Use with caution in patients with prostatic hypertrophy (risk of urinary retention), hypotension, hypothyroidism, raised intracranial pressure or head injury, adrenocortical insufficiency, and myasthenia gravis.
  - Accidental ingestion, especially by children, can result in fatal opioid overdose. Counsel patients on safe storage and disposal.
- CYP2D6 metabolism:
  - Codeine is metabolised by CYP2D6; patients who are CYP2D6 ultra‑rapid metabolisers are at increased risk of higher-than-expected morphine exposure and life‑threatening adverse events — contraindicated in ultra‑rapid metabolisers.
- Use in chronic non‑cancer pain:
  - Opioid use for chronic non‑cancer pain is generally not recommended; if considered, use only after comprehensive assessment and after maximising non‑opioid and non‑pharmacological options; treat as a time‑limited trial with defined objectives and monitoring.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor for signs of respiratory depression, especially at initiation and after dose increases.
- Monitor for signs of misuse, abuse, and dependence.
- Monitor gastrointestinal symptoms and signs of bleeding.
- Monitor renal function in patients with risk factors and during prolonged use.
- Monitor hepatic function and for clinical signs/symptoms of hepatotoxicity.
- Monitor blood pressure and fluid status in patients at risk of cardiovascular events.
- Monitor for severe skin reactions and discontinue immediately if suspected.
- In patients on concomitant CNS depressants, monitor level of sedation and respiratory function closely.

### Notable serious adverse events highlighted in the sources
- Fatal or life‑threatening respiratory depression.
- Fatal gastrointestinal bleeding, ulceration or perforation.
- Severe cutaneous adverse reactions (AGEP, DRESS, TEN).
- Serious cardiovascular thrombotic events (myocardial infarction, stroke).
- Hepatotoxicity potentially leading to severe liver injury.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacodynamic interactions:
  - Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, other opioids, gabapentinoids, antihistamines, tricyclic antidepressants, antipsychotics, sedatives, hypnotics, centrally‑acting antiemetics, cannabis, alcohol and other CNS depressants may result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma and death. If co‑administration is necessary, use the lowest effective doses for the shortest duration and monitor closely.
  - Concomitant use with other NSAIDs (including COX‑2 selective inhibitors) increases the risk of adverse reactions (contraindicated).
  - Concomitant use with oral corticosteroids, anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin), selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors and antiplatelet agents (e.g., aspirin) may increase the risk of gastrointestinal ulceration or bleeding. Exercise caution and monitor.
  - NSAIDs may blunt the effect of antihypertensive medications; monitor blood pressure and volume status.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions:
  - Codeine is metabolised by CYP2D6; patients who are CYP2D6 ultra‑rapid metabolisers are at increased risk of excessive opioid effects — product is contraindicated in CYP2D6 ultra‑rapid metabolisers.
  - Concomitant use with monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) is contraindicated (within 14 days of stopping MAOI therapy).
- Management notes:
  - When prescribing with other CNS depressants, counsel patients and caregivers regarding additive sedation and respiratory depression and advise avoidance of alcohol.
  - Consider dose reductions and enhanced monitoring when agents with overlapping toxicities are co‑prescribed.
- Additional pharmacokinetic interaction details (e.g., specific CYP inhibitors/inducers, quantitative effects) are: Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - Use of ibuprofen is contraindicated during the third trimester of pregnancy.
  - Use during labour when delivery of a premature infant is anticipated is contraindicated because codeine may produce neonatal withdrawal.
  - Pregnancy risk categorisation systems (e.g., Australian category, US lettered categories) and other region‑specific pregnancy labeling: Not specified in available sources.
- Breastfeeding:
  - Use of codeine‑containing products is contraindicated in women during breastfeeding (risk of opioid exposure to infant and life‑threatening effects in the infant).
- Fertility:
  - Fertility data / effects on fertility: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
- Paediatric use:
  - Contraindicated in children aged below 12 years.
  - Contraindicated in adolescents aged 12–18 years in whom respiratory function might be compromised, including post‑tonsillectomy and/or adenoidectomy for obstructive sleep apnoea, due to increased risk of serious and life‑threatening adverse reactions.
  - Renal function deterioration risk is emphasised in dehydrated paediatric patients.
- Elderly:
  - Increased risk of gastrointestinal bleeding, ulceration and perforation. Use lowest effective dose; exercise caution. Monitor renal, hepatic and cardiovascular status as appropriate.
- Renal impairment:
  - Use with caution; renal function may deteriorate. Specific dosing adjustments by eGFR or CKD stage: Not specified in available sources.
- Hepatic impairment:
  - Use with caution in hepatic impairment; monitor for hepatotoxicity. Severe hepatic impairment is a contraindication. Specific dosing adjustments by severity: Not specified in available sources.
