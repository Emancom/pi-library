# Editorial Product Information: Interferon Gamma-1b

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions’ Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: Australian (TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of interferon gamma-1b for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This synthesis is an editorial summary intended for informational purposes and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Interferon gamma-1b (recombinant human).
- **Chemical Class:** Biologic response modifier; single-chain polypeptide (140 amino acids).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Imukin** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L03AB03 [Not specified in available source, provided for clinical reference].
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection containing 2 x 10⁶ IU (100 µg) per 0.5 mL [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection. (Note: IV and IM routes have been used in oncology studies but are not approved for the primary indication).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Link Medical Products Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Granulomatous Disease (CGD):** Indicated as an adjunct to reduce the frequency of serious infections in patients with CGD [Australian].
- **Note:** While approved for both adults and children, clinical benefits have been most significantly demonstrated in the paediatric population [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children)
Injections should be administered three times weekly (e.g., Monday, Wednesday, Friday).
- **Body Surface Area (BSA) > 0.5 m²:** 1 x 10⁶ IU (50 µg)/m² [Australian].
- **Body Surface Area (BSA) ≤ 0.5 m²:** 3 x 10⁴ IU (1.5 µg)/kg per dose [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Administer via subcutaneous injection.
- Recommended sites include the right and left deltoid regions and the anterior thigh.
- The product may be administered by a physician, nurse, family member, or the patient following appropriate training.
- Vials do not contain a preservative; each vial is for single-use only and must be discarded immediately after opening.
- Do not shake the vial vigorously.
- **Incompatibilities:** **Imukin** must not be mixed with other medications in the same syringe.

### Clinical Management
- Treatment should continue during infectious complications of CGD.
- If severe reactions occur, therapy should be temporarily discontinued until the reaction abates.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known acute hypersensitivity to interferon gamma-1b.
- Known hypersensitivity to closely related interferons.
- Hypersensitivity to any excipient (mannitol, sodium succinate hexahydrate, succinic acid, polysorbate 20) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiac Disease:** Use with caution in patients with pre-existing ischaemia, congestive heart failure, or arrhythmias. While not directly cardiotoxic, the "flu-like" symptoms (fever, chills) associated with interferon gamma-1b may exacerbate underlying cardiac conditions.
- **CNS Disorders:** Exercise caution in patients with known seizure disorders or compromised central nervous system function. Decreased mental status, gait disturbance, and dizziness have occurred, particularly at doses exceeding 250 µg/m²/day.
- **Myelosuppression:** Reversible neutropenia and thrombocytopenia have been observed and may be dose-related. Caution is required in patients with pre-existing myelosuppression.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Transaminase elevations (AST/ALT) have been observed as early as seven days after starting therapy. Risk appears higher in infants < 1 year old.
- **Latex Allergy:** The vial stopper contains natural rubber (latex), which may trigger allergic reactions in sensitive individuals [Australian].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** In addition to standard CGD monitoring, the following should be performed at baseline and periodically during treatment:
    - Haematology (CBC, differential, platelet count).
    - Blood chemistry (Renal and Liver function tests).
    - Urinalysis.
- **Immunogenicity:** As an exogenous protein, interferon gamma-1b may lead to antibody formation. Periodic monitoring for antibodies should be considered.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typically dose-, route-, and schedule-dependent. Constitutional "flu-like" symptoms (fever, headache, chills, myalgia, fatigue) are the most common and may be mitigated by bedtime administration or paracetamol.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Fever, headache, chills, fatigue, injection site pain, rash, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, increased hepatic enzymes. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Depression, abdominal pain, myalgia, arthralgia, back pain. |
| **Frequency Not Known** | Neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, hyponatraemia\*, hyperglycaemia\*, hypertriglyceridaemia\*, confusional state\*, convulsion\*, Parkinsonian gait/tremor\*, cardiac failure\*, MI\*, TIA\*, DVT\*, pulmonary embolism\*, interstitial lung disease\*, pancreatitis\*, hepatic failure\*, reversible renal failure\*. |

*\*Note: These reactions were primarily observed in clinical trials for non-registered indications and often at doses higher than recommended [Australian].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Vaccines/Heterologous Serum Proteins:** Avoid simultaneous administration due to the risk of an amplified immune response.
- **Myelosuppressive Agents:** Caution is advised when co-administering with other drugs that suppress bone marrow function.
- **Cytochrome P-450 System:** Interferon gamma-1b may alter the half-lives of drugs metabolised by the CYP450 system.
- **Hepatotoxic/Nephotoxic Drugs:** Theoretically, concurrent use may affect the clearance of interferon gamma-1b.
- **Neurotoxic/Cardiotoxic Drugs:** Concurrent use may increase systemic toxicity.
- **Antibiotics/Glucocorticoids:** Interferon gamma-1b does **not** reduce the efficacy of these agents in CGD patients.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category B3.** Studies in primates showed an increased incidence of abortions at high doses. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if potential benefits outweigh the risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended due to unknown risks to the newborn.
- **Fertility:** Animal data suggests higher levels of interferon gamma may impair male or female fertility. The long-term effect on fertility in younger patients is unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for use in children. A higher incidence of transaminase elevations has been noted in infants under 1 year of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in the source document.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution. There is a possibility of interferon gamma accumulation in patients with serious liver disease or severe renal insufficiency.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A biologic response modifier. It enhances macrophage cytotoxicity by increasing the respiratory burst (generation of toxic oxygen metabolites) and increasing HLA-DR and Fc receptor expression. The exact mechanism of action in CGD remains unknown.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly cleared after IV administration; slowly cleared but well-absorbed after SC/IM administration. SC bioavailability is >89%.
    - **Peak Plasma Concentration (Cmax):** Observed at approximately 8 hours (SC) or 4 hours (IM).
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is ~12.4 L (IV) and ~47.9 L (SC).
    - **Elimination:** Mean elimination half-life is 38 minutes (IV), 2.9 hours (IM), and 5.9 hours (SC). Clearance is handled by the liver and kidneys.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CGD Pivotal Trial:** A placebo-controlled trial demonstrated that interferon gamma-1b significantly reduced the frequency of serious infections over a 12-month period. Most participants were also receiving prophylactic antimicrobials. Clinical benefits were established despite the study not confirming specific immunomodulatory effects on superoxide production.
- **Paediatric Data:** Data from 37 CGD patients under age 3 (pooled from uncontrolled post-marketing studies) showed infection rates similar to those seen in controlled trials.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C.
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Do not shake vigorously.
- **Stability:** Once removed from refrigeration, unused vials should not be left at room temperature for more than 12 hours.
- **Shelf Life:** 3 years [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
