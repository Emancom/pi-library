# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesised from the following jurisdictional Product Information (PI): **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, intended to facilitate clinical decision-making. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 
- *Note: While the user prompt referenced Australian sources for a different drug (Imipenem/Cilastatin), the source material provided for analysis was exclusively the US FDA-approved labeling for Levocarnitine. Consequently, this editorial primarily reflects United States clinical standards and regulatory information.*

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (also known as L-carnitine).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).

### Administration Instructions
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h) and never less than every 6 hours (q6h).
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before use.

### Monitoring
- **Baseline:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before starting therapy.
- **Ongoing:** Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels.
- **Target Range:** Free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These may occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** While this editorial concerns the IV formulation, it is noted that safety for *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. High doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (Clinical Trials in ESRD)
The following table lists adverse events reported in $\ge$ 5% of patients in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, regardless of causality [US]:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency $\ge$ 5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Abdominal pain, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain. |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia, arrhythmia [Placebo-rate comparable]. |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, dyspepsia. |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia, peripheral edema, weight changes. |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, insomnia, paresthesia, depression. |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis. |
| **Skin** | Pruritus, rash. |

### Serious or Notable Reactions
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis (less frequent).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category:** Not explicitly lettered in the provided source, but clinical data is limited.
    - **Animal Data:** Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. 
    - Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine after exogenous administration. 
    - **Recommendation:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation for the mother. Consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, etc.) were negative. No long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is guided by the same indications as adults; see Section 3 for weight-based dosing (50 mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source; general clinical caution is advised.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **IV Formulation:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis.
    - **Dose Adjustments:** In ESRD, if trough (pre-dialysis) carnitine levels are below normal (40-50 µmol/L), start at 10-20 mg/kg. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) can be made as early as the third week of therapy [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into the mitochondria for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (feces). 
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).
    - **Half-life:** Mean distribution $t_{1/2}$ is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination $t_{1/2}$ is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed that mean pre-dialysis carnitine levels rose from ~20 µmol/L to 190 $\pm$ 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability (Admixture):** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL. Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources [US].
