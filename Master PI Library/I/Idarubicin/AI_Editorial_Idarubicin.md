# Editorial Product Information: Idarubicin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory requirements and clinical recommendations.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis for informational purposes and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Idarubicin hydrochloride (a semi-synthetic antineoplastic anthracycline).
- **Brand Name:** **Zavedos** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01DB06.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Solution for injection: 5 mg/5 mL and 10 mg/10 mL vials [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) injection/infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Myelogenous Leukaemia (AML):** Indicated for remission induction in adults with previously untreated AML or for remission induction in relapsed or refractory patients. It may be used as monotherapy or within combination chemotherapy regimens containing other cytotoxic agents [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Induction Therapy:** 12 mg/m² daily for three days via slow intravenous injection (10–15 minutes). 
- **Combination Therapy:** Typically administered with cytarabine 100 mg/m² daily, given by continuous infusion for seven days.
- **Subsequent Courses:** A second course may be administered if there is unequivocal evidence of remaining leukaemia. If the patient experienced severe mucositis during the first course, the second course should be delayed until recovery, and a 25% dose reduction is recommended.

### Administration Instructions
- **Strictly Intravenous:** Must not be given by any other route.
- **Method:** Administer via the tubing of a freely running intravenous infusion of 0.9% sodium chloride. 
- **Site Selection:** Use a large vein to minimize the risk of thrombosis or perivenous extravasation.
- **Extravasation Management:** If signs occur (stinging/burning), terminate the infusion immediately and restart in another vein. Recommended treatment includes intermittent ice packs (30 minutes immediately, then 30 minutes 4 times daily for 3 days) and elevation of the extremity.

## 4. Contraindications
- Severe renal impairment [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- Uncontrolled infections [Australian].
- Hypersensitivity to idarubicin, other anthracyclines, or any excipients [Australian].
- Severe myocardial insufficiency or recent myocardial infarction [Australian].
- Severe arrhythmias [Australian].
- Persistent myelosuppression [Australian].
- Previous treatment with maximum cumulative doses of idarubicin and/or other anthracyclines or anthracenediones [Australian].
- Pregnancy or women wishing to become pregnant [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** Not specified in available sources, however, mandatory monitoring for toxicity under the direction of clinicians experienced in leukaemia chemotherapy is emphasized.
- **Haematologic Toxicity:** Idarubicin is a potent bone marrow suppressant. Leucopenia is usually severe; nadirs for leucocytes and platelets typically occur 10–14 days post-dose, with recovery by the third week.
- **Cardiotoxicity:** Risk of potentially fatal congestive heart failure (CHF), arrhythmias, and cardiomyopathies. 
    - **Early (Acute) Events:** Sinus tachycardia and ECG abnormalities (ST-T wave changes).
    - **Late (Delayed) Events:** Reduced Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF). 
    - **Cumulative Limits:** Risk increases with cumulative dose. Limits are not strictly defined, but cardiotoxicity has been reported at doses of 150–290 mg/m². Total cumulative doses up to 400 mg/m² generally have a low probability of cardiotoxicity.
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Baseline and periodic LVEF assessment (MUGA or ECHO) and frequent haematological monitoring (granulocytes, red cells, platelets) are required.
- **Secondary Leukaemia:** Reported in patients treated with anthracyclines, particularly when used in combination with DNA-damaging agents or after heavy pre-treatment.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Hyperuricaemia may occur due to rapid lysis of neoplastic cells. Monitoring of uric acid, potassium, calcium, and phosphate is necessary.
- **Vaccinations:** Avoid live or live-attenuated vaccines in immunocompromised patients due to the risk of fatal infection.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%) or Major Effects
- **Haematologic:** Anaemia, leucopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and bone marrow depression.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, mucositis/stomatitis (appearing 3–10 days post-treatment), abdominal pain, and diarrhoea.
- **Dermatologic:** Alopecia.

### Common (1–10%)
- **Infections:** Sepsis/septicaemia.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Oesophagitis, GI tract bleeding.
- **Dermatologic:** Rash, itch, skin/nail hyperpigmentation, urticaria, acral erythema.
- **Renal:** Red coloration of urine (1–2 days post-dose).

### Rare or Serious
- **Cardiac:** Cardiomyopathy, CHF, myocardial infarction, pericarditis, myocarditis.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Severe enterocolitis with perforation (neutropenic enterocolitis).
- **Vascular:** Phlebitis, thrombophlebitis, thromboembolism (including pulmonary embolism), shock.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Myelosuppressive Agents:** Additive effects when used with other agents having similar action or radiotherapy (especially if within 2–3 weeks of idarubicin).
- **Cardiotoxic Agents:** Concomitant use with other anthracyclines, cardioactive compounds (e.g., calcium channel blockers), or trastuzumab requires close cardiac monitoring. Trastuzumab may persist in circulation for 7 months; avoid anthracyclines during this window if possible.
- **Hepatic/Renal Modifiers:** Drugs affecting liver or kidney function may alter idarubicin metabolism and toxicity.
- **Incompatibilities:** Must not be mixed with heparin (causes precipitation) or alkaline solutions (causes degradation).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Idarubicin is genotoxic, teratogenic, and embryotoxic in animal studies. It should not be used during pregnancy. If pregnancy occurs, the patient must be informed of the potential hazard to the foetus.
- **Contraception:**
    - **Females:** Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 6.5 months after the final dose [Australian].
    - **Males:** Use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 3.5 months after the final dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown, but expected based on other anthracyclines. Breastfeeding should be avoided during treatment and for at least 14 days after the last dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Idarubicin may induce chromosomal damage in human spermatozoa and cause testicular atrophy in males. Fertility preservation should be discussed prior to treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Increased susceptibility to anthracycline-induced cardiotoxicity; long-term periodic cardiac evaluation is required [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased frequency of cardiac events reported in patients over age 60.
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. In moderate impairment (Creatinine Clearance <60 mL/min), idarubicin and idarubicinol AUC increase significantly (38% and 120% respectively); dose reductions should be considered.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment. For patients with bilirubin levels between 20.4–51.0 micromoles/L, a 50% dose reduction is generally employed for anthracyclines [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** DNA intercalating agent that reacts with topoisomerase II and inhibits nucleic acid synthesis. High lipophilicity results in rapid cellular uptake.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Triphasic disposition in plasma. Very high protein binding (97% for idarubicin; 94% for idarubicinol). Extensive tissue binding; concentrations in nucleated blood and bone marrow cells are >100 times plasma levels.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolized to an active metabolite, idarubicinol.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted via bile and urine (mostly as idarubicinol).
    - **Half-life:** Parent drug (plasma): 10–35 hours; Active metabolite (plasma): 41–69 hours; Cellular half-life: ~15 hours (parent) and ~72 hours (metabolite).

## 11. Clinical Trials

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze.
- **Light:** Protect vials from light.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
- **Handling:** Cytotoxic agent. Use goggles, masks, gloves, and protective gowns. Pregnant staff must be excluded from handling the drug.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
