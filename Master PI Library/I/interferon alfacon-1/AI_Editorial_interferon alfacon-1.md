# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following provided jurisdiction: United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer intravenously [United States].
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives [United States].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection or by infusion [United States].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Dosing Interval:** Administer every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) [United States].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are generally 50 mg/kg or as clinically required; the maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [United States].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow (2 to 3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [United States].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [United States].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral or IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with pre-existing conditions, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been reported [United States].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Monitor weekly and monthly, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [United States].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** The safety/efficacy of the oral formulation has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (30–49%), hypertension (18–21%), diarrhea (9–35%), headache (3–37%), pharyngitis (15–33%), and nausea (5–12%) [United States].
- **Common (1–10%):** Abdominal pain, vomiting, fever, accidental injury, asthenia, chest pain, flu syndrome, and infection [United States].
- **Specific Clinical Effects:** 
    - Transient nausea and vomiting.
    - Gastritis and body odor (infrequent) [United States].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, and seizures [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in the current PI, but notes that animal reproductive studies in rats and rabbits (up to 3.8 times human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess intake must be weighed against maternal benefit [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to metabolic disorder dosing (50 mg/kg) [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Intravenous levocarnitine is specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. However, oral levocarnitine is not recommended for chronic use in patients with severely compromised renal function due to metabolite accumulation [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available source.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. it facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dialysis Studies:** In two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg of levocarnitine injection three times weekly after dialysis showed significant increases in trough carnitine concentrations. Trough levels typically reached 116–456 µmol/L after 12–24 weeks of therapy (normal range 40–50 µmol/L) [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [United States].
- **Special Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [United States].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL at room temperature [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only.
