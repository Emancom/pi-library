# Editorial Product Information: Interferon beta-1a

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of the drug's profile for healthcare professionals. 
- This document is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any specific region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Interferon beta-1a (rch), a 166 amino acid glycoprotein produced by recombinant DNA technology using Chinese Hamster Ovary cells.
- **Chemical Class:** Cytokine; Type I Interferon.
- **Brand Name(s):** **AVONEX** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L03AB03.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection. Each 0.5 mL contains 30 µg of interferon beta-1a (6 million IU of antiviral activity). Available in single-use pre-filled syringes and pre-filled pens.
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Biogen Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsing Forms of Multiple Sclerosis (MS):** Treatment of patients with relapsing MS to reduce the frequency of clinical exacerbations and delay the progression of physical disability.
- **Single Demyelinating Event:** Treatment of patients who have experienced a single demyelinating event and are at high risk of developing clinically definite MS (based on characteristic brain MRI abnormalities).
- **Secondary Progressive MS (SPMS):** Treatment of SPMS in patients where relapse is still a feature of the disease. **AVONEX** 60 µg is specifically indicated for this population [Australian]. Note: Therapy should not be initiated in SPMS patients who have not experienced a relapse in the previous 12 months [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dosage:** 30 µg (6 million IU) injected intramuscularly once weekly.
- **SPMS Dosage:** 60 µg (12 million IU) injected intramuscularly once weekly for patients with moderate severity (EDSS 3.5–6.5) and recent/continuing relapses [Australian].

### Titration Schedule
To reduce the incidence and severity of flu-like symptoms, titration is recommended at the initiation of therapy:
- **Week 1:** 1/4 dose (7.5 µg)
- **Week 2:** 1/2 dose (15 µg)
- **Week 3:** 3/4 dose (22.5 µg)
- **Week 4 onwards:** Full dose (30 µg)

### Administration Instructions
- **Sites:** Thigh or upper arm. The buttock is not recommended.
- **Technique:** Intramuscular injection only. Avoid subcutaneous injection due to the risk of local adverse effects.
- **Preparation:** Allow the syringe/pen to reach room temperature (15°C to 30°C) for approximately 30 minutes before use. Do not use external heat sources.
- **Ancillary Therapy:** Antipyretic analgesics may be administered prior to injection and for 24 hours post-injection to mitigate flu-like symptoms.

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available source.

## 4. Contraindications
- History of hypersensitivity to natural or recombinant interferon beta or any excipients.
- Current severe depression and/or suicidal ideation.
- Initiation during pregnancy or in women planning to become pregnant [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Psychiatric Disorders:** Use with caution in patients with previous or current depressive disorders or suicidal ideation. Depression and suicide are more frequent in the MS population and associated with interferon use. Discontinuation should be considered if depression occurs.
- **Seizure Disorders:** Caution is required in patients with a history of seizures or those on antiepileptic therapy, especially if poorly controlled. Discontinue therapy if new seizures develop until an etiology is established.
- **Hepatic Injury:** Rare cases of serious injury, including autoimmune hepatitis and hepatic failure, have been reported. Monitor liver function tests (LFTs) prior to initiation and periodically.
- **Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH):** Cases have been reported, sometimes several years after starting treatment. Monitor for symptoms (e.g., dyspnea, fatigue) and discontinue if PAH is confirmed.
- **Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA):** Including fatal cases of TTP or HUS. Monitor for new-onset hypertension, impaired renal function, and thrombocytopenia.
- **Nephrotic Syndrome:** Various nephropathies have been reported. Monitor for edema, proteinuria, and renal impairment.

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Cardiac Disease:** Monitor patients with angina, CHF, or arrhythmia closely. Flu-like symptoms may stress patients with pre-existing cardiac conditions.
- **Myelosuppression/Renal Failure:** Use with extra caution in patients with severe renal failure or severe myelosuppression.

### Monitoring Requirements
- Complete and differential white blood cell counts.
- Platelet counts.
- Blood chemistry, including LFTs.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- Flu-like symptoms (headache, fever, chills, myalgia, asthenia).
- Muscle spasticity, nausea, and pain.
- Depression [Australian: up to 20% in some studies].

### Common (1–10%)
- Injection site reactions (pain, inflammation).
- Gastrointestinal: Diarrhea, vomiting, anorexia.
- Nervous System: Dizziness, insomnia, hypoesthesia.
- Respiratory: Upper respiratory tract infection, sinusitis, bronchitis, rhinorrhea.
- Skin: Alopecia, increased sweating, rash.
- Other: Anemia, urinary tract infection, musculoskeletal stiffness.

### Uncommon (0.1–1%)
- Urogenital: Metrorrhagia or menorrhagia.

### Rare (<0.1%) to Very Rare
- **Serious:** Injection site necrosis, hepatic failure, autoimmune hepatitis, PAH, TMA, nephrotic syndrome, congestive heart failure, cardiomyopathy, and anaphylaxis.
- **Transient Neurological Symptoms:** Episodes of hypertonia or severe muscle weakness preventing voluntary movement (usually early in therapy).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP450 Metabolism:** Interferons may reduce the activity of hepatic cytochrome P-450 oxidase-mediated enzymes. Use caution with drugs having a narrow therapeutic index cleared by this system (e.g., antiepileptics, certain antidepressants).
- **Corticosteroids/ACTH:** Can be administered concomitantly during MS relapses.
- **Myelosuppressive Agents:** Increased monitoring is required if used in combination with agents that suppress bone marrow.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Interferon beta-1a has shown abortifacient potential in animal studies (rhesus monkeys). It is contraindicated to initiate treatment during pregnancy or in patients planning pregnancy. If a patient becomes pregnant, therapy should generally be discontinued unless the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if the drug is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug.
- **Fertility:** Anovulatory cycles were observed in animal studies at high doses; significance for human female fertility is unknown. Effects on male fertility are unknown.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No formal trials in children <12 years. Limited data suggest the safety profile in adolescents (12–18 years) receiving 30 µg IM weekly is similar to adults.
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of patients aged 65 and over to determine if they respond differently than younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** Use with extra caution in patients with severe renal failure.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Initiate with caution in patients with active liver disease or a history of significant liver disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Interferon beta-1a binds to specific cell surface receptors, initiating a cascade of intracellular events leading to the expression of interferon-induced genes (e.g., 2’/5'-oligoadenylate synthetase, neopterin, $\beta_2$-microglobulin). These mediate antiviral, antiproliferative, and immunomodulatory activities.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Following IM injection of 30 µg, serum levels are low/at limits of detection.
    - **Elimination:** Serum half-life is approximately 10 hours. 
    - **Pharmacodynamics:** Biological response markers (neopterin) increase within 12 hours and remain elevated for at least 4 days, peaking at 48 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study NS26321:** A 2-year study in 301 relapsing MS patients showed a 37% reduction in the risk of disability progression and a 32% reduction in annual exacerbation rates compared to placebo.
- **Study C95-812:** In patients with a single demyelinating event, interferon beta-1a reduced the rate of development of clinically definite MS by 44% over 2 years.
- **Study C97-830:** In SPMS patients, 60 µg weekly reduced disease progression by 27–40% (based on MSFC scores), specifically in those who had experienced a relapse in the year prior to initiation.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze).
- **Light:** Store in the original package to protect from light.
- **Alternative Storage:** Pre-filled syringes and pens may be stored at room temperature (below 30°C) for a single period of up to 7 days.
- **Shelf Life:** 36 months [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
