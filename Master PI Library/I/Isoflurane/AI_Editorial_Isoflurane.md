# Editorial Product Information: Isoflurane

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of isoflurane product information for healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, safety warnings, and administration guidelines from the provided regulatory source.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for final prescribing authority.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Isoflurane
- **Chemical Class:** Halogenated ether (1-chloro-2,2,2-trifluoroethyl difluoromethyl ether)
- **Brand Names:** **Aerrane** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** N01AB06
- **Dosage Form:** Liquid for inhalation (1 mL/mL)
- **Route of Administration:** Inhalation
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- Induction and maintenance of general inhalation anaesthesia [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
Isoflurane must be administered only by personnel trained in general anaesthesia. Facilities for airway maintenance, artificial ventilation, oxygen enrichment, and circulatory resuscitation must be immediately available. **Aerrane** must be delivered via vaporisers specifically calibrated for isoflurane to ensure precise concentration control.

### Induction
- **Adults:** The starting concentration is typically 0.5%. Surgical anaesthesia is usually achieved within 7 to 10 minutes using concentrations of 1.3% to 3.0%.
- **Premedication:** To avoid coughing, breath-holding, or laryngospasm (risks of which increase with isoflurane concentration), the use of a short-acting barbiturate, propofol, etomidate, or midazolam is recommended prior to inhalation induction.

### Maintenance
- **With Nitrous Oxide:** 1.0% to 2.5% isoflurane in a mixture of $N_2O$ and $O_2$.
- **With Pure Oxygen:** 1.5% to 3.5% isoflurane may be required.

### Recovery
- Concentration should be reduced to 0.5% toward the end of the procedure or 0% during wound closure to facilitate prompt recovery.
- Following cessation, the patient’s airways should be ventilated with 100% oxygen until full awakening.

### Minimum Alveolar Concentration (MAC)
MAC values decrease with age (except in neonates). Representative values in 100% $O_2$:
- **Neonates:** 1.60%
- **1–6 months:** 1.87%
- **10–15 years:** 1.16%
- **26 ± 4 years:** 1.28%
- **64 ± 5 years:** 1.05%

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to isoflurane or other halogenated anaesthetics.
- Known or suspected genetic disposition toward malignant hyperthermia.
- History of malignant hyperthermia or history of liver dysfunction, jaundice, unexplained fever, leukocytosis, or eosinophilia following previous administration of halogenated anaesthetics.
- Confirmed hepatitis due to halogenated inhalational anaesthetics.
- General anaesthesia contraindications.
- Use in obstetric operations [Australian].
- Concomitant use with nonselective Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Malignant Hyperthermia (MH):** In susceptible individuals, isoflurane may trigger a skeletal muscle hypermetabolic state. Clinical signs include hypercapnia, muscle rigidity, tachycardia, and cyanosis. Treatment requires discontinuation of isoflurane and administration of IV dantrolene sodium.
- **Perioperative Hyperkalaemia:** Rare cases of increased serum potassium resulting in arrhythmias and death have occurred in paediatric patients, particularly those with latent or overt neuromuscular disease (e.g., Duchenne muscular dystrophy).
- **Hepatic Reactions:** Cases of fatal hepatic necrosis and hepatic failure have been reported. Caution is advised in patients with pre-existing liver disease or those receiving drugs known to cause hepatic dysfunction.
- **Respiratory Depression:** Isoflurane is a profound respiratory depressant; this effect is intensified by narcotic premedication. Respiration must be continuously monitored and assisted if necessary.
- **Neurosurgery:** Isoflurane increases cerebral blood flow at deeper levels of anaesthesia, which may increase intracranial pressure (ICP). This can typically be mitigated by hyperventilation before or during anaesthesia.
- **Coronary Artery Disease:** Isoflurane can cause coronary vasodilation and has the potential for "coronary steal" in animal models; use with caution in patients with coronary artery disease or subendocardial ischaemia.
- **CO2 Absorbents:** Interaction with dry/desiccated carbon dioxide absorbents (e.g., soda lime) can form carbon monoxide, leading to elevated carboxyhaemoglobin levels. Ensure absorbents are fresh and moist.
- **QT Prolongation:** Very rare reports of QT prolongation and torsade de pointes exist.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally dose-dependent and represent extensions of pharmacological effects.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Shivering/chills, nausea (recovery), coughing (induction), breath-holding, agitation (induction), increased white blood cell count (postoperative). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Delirium, vomiting (recovery), laryngospasm, intraoperative arrhythmias, movements during maintenance. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Postoperative hypotension, mood changes, nightmares, retching, diaphoresis, convulsive pattern on ECG. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Postoperative hypertension, seizures, bronchospasm. |
| **Post-marketing / Serious** | Malignant hyperthermia, anaphylaxis, hepatic failure/necrosis, cardiac arrest, ventricular fibrillation, QT prolongation, hyperkalaemia, rhabdomyolysis, acute renal failure (secondary to hypotension). |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Nonselective MAOIs:** Contraindicated; risk of haemodynamic crisis. Discontinue MAOIs 15 days before surgery [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blocking Agents:** Isoflurane markedly potentiates both depolarising and non-depolarising relaxants. Doses of these agents should be reduced to approximately 1/3 to 1/2 of the usual dose.
- **Sympathomimetics:**
    - **Adrenaline:** Risk of ventricular arrhythmias. Limit adrenaline dose to 0.1 mg within 10 minutes or 0.3 mg within one hour in adults [Australian].
    - **Beta-sympathomimetics (Isoprenaline):** Risk of serious ventricular arrhythmia.
- **Beta-blockers:** May exaggerate hypotension and negative inotropic effects.
- **Isoniazid:** Increased risk of hepatotoxicity. Treatment should be suspended one week before surgery and resumed 15 days later [Australian].
- **St John’s Wort:** Risk of severe hypotension and delayed emergence.
- **Opioids and Nitrous Oxide:** Decrease the MAC of isoflurane.
- **Calcium Antagonists:** Risk of marked hypotension, particularly with dihydropyridines.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Australian Category B1.
    - Isoflurane crosses the placenta and may cause CNS/respiratory depression in the newborn.
    - It exerts a relaxant effect on uterine smooth muscle, potentially increasing blood loss during abortions or uterine curettage.
    - Use must be avoided during pregnancy unless clearly necessary [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Breastfeeding should be withheld for 12 hours after the termination of anaesthesia [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:**
    - MAC is higher in infants than in adults.
    - **Neurotoxicity:** Repeated or prolonged exposure to anaesthetics in early life/late pregnancy may be associated with cognitive deficits.
    - High risk of hyperkalaemia in children with underlying muscular dystrophies.
- **Elderly Use:** MAC is significantly lower in the elderly. No other specific data provided [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No specific dosing adjustments provided; however, caution is advised due to the risk of halogenated-anaesthetic-induced hepatotoxicity.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhalation anaesthetic that induces a rapid loss of consciousness and diminishes pharyngeal and laryngeal reflexes.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces dose-dependent reduction in blood pressure and respiratory rate. It has a mildly irritating ether-like odour which may limit induction speed.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Metabolism:** Minimal (approx. 0.2%). The primary metabolite is trifluoroacetic acid.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 95% is recovered unchanged in expired air.
    - **Fluoride Levels:** Peak serum inorganic fluoride levels average < 5 µmol/L and return to normal within 24 hours.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- Keep the bottle in an upright position in its original container.
- Ensure the cap is firm but not overly tight to avoid leakage.
