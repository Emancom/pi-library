# Product Information: Indocyanine Green

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Generic Name:** Indocyanine green (ICG)
**Active Ingredient:** Each vial contains either 10 mg or 25 mg of indocyanine green as a sterile, lyophilized green powder.
**Excipients:** Formulations contain no more than 5% sodium iodide. For pH adjustment, sodium hydroxide or hydrochloric acid may be used.

## 2. Approved Indications
Indocyanine green is an optical imaging dye indicated for:

*   **Vascular and Tissue Perfusion Imaging:** Visualisation of micro- and macro-vasculature, blood flow, and tissue perfusion in adult and paediatric patients (aged one month and older). This includes use during vascular, gastrointestinal, organ transplant, and reconstructive surgeries [TGA, FDA].
*   **Extrahepatic Biliary Visualisation:** Identification of extrahepatic biliary ducts in adults and paediatric patients aged 12 years and older [TGA, FDA].
*   **Lymphatic Mapping:** Delineation of lymph nodes and lymphatic vessels in adult women with solid cervical or uterine tumours where lymphatic mapping is part of intraoperative surgical management [TGA, FDA].
*   **Ophthalmic Angiography:** Visualisation of choroidal vasculature in adult and paediatric patients [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
Indocyanine green is strictly contraindicated in patients with a known history of hypersensitivity to indocyanine green or sodium iodide. Previous documented reactions have included life-threatening anaphylaxis.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
Severe hypersensitivity reactions, including urticaria, respiratory distress, and fatal anaphylaxis, have been reported following intravenous administration. Clinical settings using this agent must be equipped with ready access to cardiopulmonary resuscitation personnel and emergency equipment. Continuous monitoring for signs of allergic reaction is mandatory during and immediately after administration.

### Interference with Thyroid Studies
Due to the sodium iodide content in the formulation, the iodine-binding capacity of thyroid tissue may be suppressed. Patients should not undergo radioactive iodine uptake studies for at least one week following the administration of indocyanine green [TGA, FDA].

### Special Populations
*   **Geriatric Use:** While no overall differences in efficacy or safety have been observed between elderly and younger patients, caution is advised in dose selection for older individuals. Consideration should be given to the increased prevalence of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function in this population [TGA, FDA].
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy for tissue perfusion have been established for infants aged one month and older. For biliary duct visualisation, use is limited to those 12 years and older. Safety has not been established for lymphatic mapping in paediatric patients [TGA, FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
Indocyanine green is intended for intravenous or interstitial administration. The total dose should generally not exceed 2 mg/kg [TGA, FDA].

### Reconstitution Instructions
The powder should be reconstituted using Sterile Water for Injections.
*   **For 2.5 mg/mL solution:** Dissolve 25 mg in 10 mL of diluent.
*   **For 1.25 mg/mL solution:** Dissolve 25 mg in 20 mL of diluent (typically used for lymphatic mapping).
*   **Inspection:** The resulting solution should be a clear, deep green. If precipitation is observed, swirl gently; if it persists, discard the vial.

### Indication-Specific Dosing
*   **Vessels and Perfusion (Adults/Pediatrics >1 month):** 1.25 mg to 5 mg per imaging sequence. For extremity perfusion via the skin, 3.75 mg to 10 mg is recommended. Follow with a 10 mL bolus of 0.9% sodium chloride.
*   **Extrahepatic Biliary Ducts:** A single 2.5 mg dose administered intravenously at least 45 minutes prior to the surgical procedure.
*   **Lymphatic Mapping (Cervical/Uterine):** 5 mg total dose administered interstitially into the cervix. This is delivered as four 1 mL injections (1.25 mg/mL) at the 3 and 9 o'clock positions, using both superficial (1–3 mm) and deep (1–3 cm) injection techniques.
*   **Ophthalmic Angiography:** Doses up to 40 mg in 2 mL of diluent, followed immediately by a 5 mL saline flush [FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most significant adverse reactions involve the immune system. Reported events include:
*   **Immune System Disorders:** Anaphylaxis, anaphylactoid reactions, and urticaria.
*   **General:** Rare reports of nausea and hot flashes.

Because these reactions are often reported voluntarily from a population of uncertain size, frequency cannot always be reliably estimated.

## 7. Drug Interactions
No specific medicinal drug-drug interactions have been identified. However, the primary interaction is diagnostic: ICG interferes with the accuracy of radioactive iodine uptake tests for a duration of one week [TGA, FDA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**Pregnancy Category B2 [TGA].** There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Limited scientific literature suggests that when administered during labor, there is no significant placental transfer of the drug. ICG should only be utilized during pregnancy if the potential clinical benefit clearly outweighs the risks.

### Breastfeeding
Data regarding the presence of ICG in human milk or its effects on milk production are unavailable. While cases in literature have not reported adverse events in breastfed infants, clinicians should consider the developmental health benefits of breastfeeding alongside the mother’s clinical need for the imaging agent.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** After intravenous injection, ICG binds rapidly and extensively to plasma proteins (98%), primarily albumin. It remains almost entirely within the intravascular compartment.
*   **Metabolism:** The agent does not undergo significant metabolism.
*   **Excretion:** ICG is taken up from the plasma almost exclusively by hepatic parenchymal cells and is secreted unchanged into the bile. It does not undergo enterohepatic circulation or renal excretion.
*   **Elimination:** The plasma fractional disappearance rate may be higher in women than in men, though overall clearance remains similar across sexes [TGA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Indocyanine green is a fluorescent dye. When bound to proteins in the blood or lymph and exposed to near-infrared (NIR) light, it absorbs energy at approximately 805 nm. It then emits fluorescence at a peak wavelength of approximately 830 nm. This emitted light is captured by specialized imaging systems to provide real-time visualisation of anatomical structures and fluid flow.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened vials:** Store below 25°C [TGA] or between 20°C and 25°C [FDA]. Protect from light.
*   **Reconstituted solution:** The product contains no antimicrobial preservatives. The solution must be used as soon as possible after preparation and should be discarded if not used within 6 hours. Do not use after the expiration date printed on the packaging.
