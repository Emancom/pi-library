# Originality Audit Report: indocyanine green

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 58/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 7
- **Overall Assessment:** Concern

## Verbatim Copied Passages
The following passages are identical or near-identical to the source regulatory documents:

1.  **Hypersensitivity Equipment:** "Clinical settings using this agent must be equipped with ready access to cardiopulmonary resuscitation personnel and emergency equipment."
    *   **Source:** Matches Document 1 (TGA, Page 4) and Document 2 (FDA, Page 6): "Always have cardiopulmonary resuscitation personnel and equipment readily available..."
    *   **Location:** Section 4, Hypersensitivity Reactions.

2.  **Lymphatic Mapping Technique:** "...at the 3 and 9 o'clock positions, using both superficial (1–3 mm) and deep (1–3 cm) injection techniques."
    *   **Source:** Matches Document 1 (TGA, Page 3) and Document 2 (FDA, Page 5): "...at the 3 o’ clock and the 9 o’clock positions with a superficial (1 mm to 3 mm) and a deep (1 cm to 3 cm) injection at each position."
    *   **Location:** Section 5, Indication-Specific Dosing.

3.  **Adverse Effects Disclaimer:** "Because these reactions are often reported voluntarily from a population of uncertain size, frequency cannot always be reliably estimated."
    *   **Source:** Matches Document 1 (TGA, Page 6) and Document 2 (FDA, Page 6): "Because these reactions are reported voluntarily from a population of uncertain size, it is not always possible to reliably estimate their frequency..."
    *   **Location:** Section 6, Adverse Effects.

4.  **Pharmacokinetics (Binding):** "...binds rapidly and extensively to plasma proteins (98%), primarily albumin. It remains almost entirely within the intravascular compartment."
    *   **Source:** Matches Document 1 (TGA, Page 10) and Document 2 (FDA, Page 9): "...indocyanine green binds to plasma proteins (98%) and is largely confined to the intravascular compartment."
    *   **Location:** Section 9, Pharmacokinetics.

5.  **Pharmacokinetics (Excretion):** "...taken up from the plasma almost exclusively by hepatic parenchymal cells and is secreted unchanged into the bile. It does not undergo enterohepatic circulation or renal excretion."
    *   **Source:** Matches Document 1 (TGA, Page 11) and Document 2 (FDA, Page 9): "Indocyanine green is taken up from the plasma almost exclusively by the hepatic parenchymal cells and is secreted entirely into the bile... It does not undergo... enterohepatic circulation."
    *   **Location:** Section 9, Pharmacokinetics.

6.  **Mechanism of Action:** "When bound to proteins in the blood or lymph and exposed to near-infrared (NIR) light, it absorbs energy at approximately 805 nm. It then emits fluorescence at a peak wavelength of approximately 830 nm."
    *   **Source:** Matches Document 1 (TGA, Page 6) and Document 2 (FDA, Page 9): "When bound to proteins in plasma or in lymph fluid, indocyanine green absorbs light in the near-infrared region at 805 nm, and emits fluorescence (light) at a slightly longer wavelength, with peak emission at 830 nm."
    *   **Location:** Section 10, Mechanism of Action.

7.  **Reconstitution Stability:** "The solution must be used as soon as possible after preparation and should be discarded if not used within 6 hours."
    *   **Source:** Matches Document 1 (TGA, Page 11) and Document 2 (FDA, Page 5): "...use... as soon as practicable after preparation... use within 6 hours."
    *   **Location:** Section 11, Storage Conditions.

## Close Paraphrases
1.  **Geriatric Use:** The editorial’s advice on dose selection due to "increased prevalence of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function" mirrors the source text's "reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function" almost exactly, maintaining the same list order.
2.  **Indications for Vessels:** The phrasing "Visualisation of micro- and macro-vasculature, blood flow, and tissue perfusion" is a very close paraphrase of the FDA’s "Fluorescence imaging of vessels (micro- and macro-vasculature), blood flow and tissue perfusion."

## Originality Strengths
- **Editorial Synthesis:** The "Editorial Note" is original and effectively sets the stage for the unified document.
- **Structural Layout:** The editorial successfully merges two different PI formats (TGA and FDA) into a cohesive 11-section structure that does not strictly follow either source's layout.
- **Dosage Consolidation:** The "Indication-Specific Dosing" section successfully creates a quick-reference list by extracting data from narrative paragraphs in the sources.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Harmonized Formulation:** The editorial notes the availability of both 10 mg and 25 mg vials (FDA) while including the specific 5% sodium iodide limit noted in the TGA PI.
- **Combined Dosing:** It merges TGA and FDA dosing for vessels and biliary ducts, noting the slight terminology differences (e.g., TGA's "Sterile Water for Injections" vs FDA's "Sterile Water for Injection").
- **Ophthalmic Context:** The editorial correctly identifies Ophthalmic Angiography as an FDA-specific indication, as it is absent from the TGA therapeutic indications section (Document 1, Section 4.1).
- **Pregnancy Categorization:** The editorial includes the TGA Pregnancy Category B2, which is a specific Australian regulatory designation not found in the US FDA text.

## Recommendation
**REVISION NEEDED**

While the document is an excellent clinical synthesis, it relies too heavily on verbatim regulatory "boilerplate" for its descriptive sections. To pass a strict originality audit:
1.  **Rewrite Section 9 (Pharmacokinetics)** and **Section 10 (Mechanism of Action)** using original phrasing to describe the protein binding and light absorption/emission.
2.  **Rephrase Section 6 (Adverse Effects)** to avoid the verbatim "voluntary reporting" disclaimer, which is a standard regulatory sentence.
3.  **Restructure the Hypersensitivity warning** in Section 4 to communicate the need for emergency equipment without using the exact phrasing found in both the TGA and FDA labels.
