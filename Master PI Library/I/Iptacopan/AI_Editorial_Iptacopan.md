# Editorial Product Information: Iptacopan

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **iptacopan** intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single, structured reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, full Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Iptacopan (present as iptacopan hydrochloride monohydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Proximal complement inhibitor; Factor B inhibitor.
- **Brand Name(s):** **FABHALTA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L04AJ08.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 200 mg hard capsules. Capsules are pale yellow, opaque, imprinted with "LNP200" and "NVR" [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Iptacopan** is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with:
- **Paroxysmal Nocturnal Haemoglobinuria (PNH)** [Australian-approved].
- **Complement 3 Glomerulopathy (C3G)** [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- The standard recommended dose is **200 mg taken orally twice daily**.
- **Administration:** Capsules may be taken with or without food and should be swallowed whole [Australian].

### Switching from Anti-C5 Therapy (PNH)
To mitigate the risk of hemolysis during transition from eculizumab or ravulizumab:
- **From eculizumab:** Start **iptacopan** no later than 1 week after the final eculizumab dose.
- **From ravulizumab:** Start **iptacopan** no later than 6 weeks after the final ravulizumab dose.
- For other PNH therapies, consider the dosing interval and mechanism of action of the previous agent [Australian].

### Missed Dose Instructions
- Patients should take a missed dose as soon as possible, even if shortly before the next scheduled dose. The regular schedule should then be resumed.

### Adherence
- Chronic treatment is required; discontinuation is not recommended unless clinically indicated. In PNH, missing several consecutive doses increases the risk of serious hemolysis.

### C3G Specifics
- In clinical studies, patients were required to be on a stable, maximally tolerated dose of an ACE inhibitor or ARB for at least 90 days prior to initiation [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
**Iptacopan** is contraindicated in the following scenarios:
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients.
- Patients without current vaccinations against *Neisseria meningitidis* and *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, unless the risk of delaying treatment outweighs the risk of infection.
- Initiation in patients with unresolved serious infections caused by encapsulated bacteria (*S. pneumoniae*, *N. meningitidis*, or *H. influenzae* type B).
- Combination use with other complement inhibitors, unless there is a clear medical justification [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [Australian]
**SERIOUS INFECTIONS CAUSED BY ENCAPSULATED BACTERIA**
**Iptacopan** may predispose patients to life-threatening or fatal infections caused by *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Neisseria meningitidis*, and *Haemophilus influenzae* type B.
- **Vaccination:** Patients must be vaccinated/revaccinated against these pathogens according to national guidelines (e.g., *Australian Immunisation Handbook*) at least 2 weeks before the first dose.
- **Prophylaxis:** If urgent treatment is required and initiated <2 weeks after vaccination, appropriate prophylactic antibiotics must be administered for 2 weeks.
- **Monitoring:** Vaccination does not eliminate risk. Monitor for early signs of infection and evaluate immediately if suspected.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **PNH Monitoring:** Regularly monitor lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels.
- **Discontinuation in PNH:** If treatment is stopped, monitor closely for at least 2 weeks for signs of hemolysis (e.g., elevated LDH, drop in hemoglobin, fatigue, abdominal pain, or major adverse vascular events).
- **C3G Limitations:** Limited experience in patients with native kidney C3G and proteinuria <1 g/g, or in recurrent C3G following kidney transplantation.

### Monitoring Requirements
- LDH levels (PNH).
- Signs of serious infection (fever, headache with nausea/stiffness, etc.).
- Blood pressure and lipids (increases observed in PNH trials) [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Distribution (PNH and C3G)
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Upper respiratory tract infections (URTI), headache, diarrhea, abdominal pain. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, arthralgia, urinary tract infection, dizziness, decreased platelet count, bronchitis. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Pneumococcal infection (observed in C3G pool) [Australian]. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Serious Infections:** Bacterial pneumonia and bacteremia secondary to encapsulated organisms (e.g., *S. pneumoniae*) have been reported even in vaccinated patients.
- **Hemolysis:** Clinical breakthrough hemolysis may occur upon discontinuation or non-adherence.

### Laboratory Changes
- **Lipids/Blood Pressure:** Mean increases in total cholesterol, LDL-C, and diastolic blood pressure were noted in PNH trials, correlating with hemoglobin improvement. These were not considered clinically relevant in C3G studies [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Iptacopan as a Substrate:** Iptacopan is a substrate for CYP2C8 (98% of oxidative metabolism) and transporters P-gp, BCRP, MRP2, OATP1B1, and OATP1B3.
- **Strong Inducers:** Co-administration with strong inducers of CYP2C8, UGT1A1, P-gp, BCRP, or OATP1B1/3 (e.g., rifampicin) is **not recommended** as it may reduce **iptacopan** efficacy [Australian].
- **Transporter Inhibitors:** Co-administration with cyclosporine (OATP1B1/1B3 inhibitor) or clopidogrel (CYP2C8 inhibitor) increased iptacopan AUC by 50% and 36% respectively, which was not deemed clinically significant [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Iptacopan may induce CYP3A4. Exercise caution when co-administering with narrow therapeutic index CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., cyclosporine, tacrolimus, carbamazepine, fentanyl).
- **CYP2C8 Substrates:** Iptacopan may inhibit CYP2C8. Use caution with substrates such as repaglinide or paclitaxel.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Data are insufficient to inform drug-associated risks. Animal studies showed no malformations but did show post-implantation loss at high doses (18-fold MRHD). Untreated PNH/C3G poses significant risks to the mother and fetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Transfer into human milk is unknown. The benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need and potential risk of infection in the infant.
- **Fertility:** No human data. Animal studies showed reversible histopathological changes in the male reproductive system at $\ge$ 10-fold MRHD, but no impact on sperm parameters or fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Geriatric Use ($\ge$65 years):** Limited data available. No dose adjustment is required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment required for mild (eGFR 60-89) or moderate (eGFR 30-59) impairment. No data available for severe impairment (eGFR <30) or dialysis; no recommendations can be given [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment required for mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B). **Not recommended** for patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C) [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Iptacopan is a proximal inhibitor of the alternative complement pathway. It binds to Factor B, preventing the formation of the C3 convertase (C3bBb). In PNH, this controls both C3-mediated extravascular hemolysis and terminal complement-mediated intravascular hemolysis. In C3G, it reduces systemic C3 cleavage and glomerular C3 deposition.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ $\approx$ 2 hours. Steady-state reached in 5 days. Not affected by food.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein bound (75-93%). $V_d$ $\approx$ 288 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily oxidative (CYP2C8 98%; CYP2D6 2%).
    - **Elimination:** $t_{1/2}$ $\approx$ 25 hours. Excretion is 71.5% faecal and 24.8% urinary [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **APPLY-PNH:** A Phase 3 study in patients with residual anaemia despite anti-C5 therapy. Iptacopan was superior to anti-C5 therapy in achieving hemoglobin improvement ($\ge$2 g/dL increase) and transfusion avoidance.
- **APPOINT-PNH:** A Phase 3 study in complement-inhibitor naïve patients. 92.2% of patients achieved significant hemoglobin improvement.
- **APPEAR-C3G:** A Phase 3 study in C3G. Iptacopan demonstrated a 35.1% reduction in 24-hour UPCR (proteinuria) compared to placebo at 6 months [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 30°C.
- Keep in the original package to protect from moisture.
- Keep out of reach of children.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction

- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring in Australia (Black Triangle Scheme) [Australian].
