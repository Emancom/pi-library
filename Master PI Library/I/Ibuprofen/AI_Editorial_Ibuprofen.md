# Editorial Product Information: Ibuprofen and Codeine Phosphate Hemihydrate

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA).
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial overview of the clinical and regulatory profile of the ibuprofen and codeine phosphate combination, synthesised for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s approved PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Ibuprofen (NSAID; propionic acid derivative) and Codeine phosphate hemihydrate (Opioid analgesic).
- **Strength:** 200 mg Ibuprofen / 12.8 mg Codeine phosphate hemihydrate per tablet [Australian].
- **Brand Names:** **Amcal Ibuprofen Plus Codeine** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N02AJ09 (Codeine and ibuprofen).
- **Dosage Form:** White to off-white, capsule-shaped, biconvex film-coated tablets with a central breakline [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Cipla Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acute Moderate Pain and Inflammation:** Indicated for the temporary relief of acute moderate pain and inflammation in patients over 12 years of age [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Adults and Children >12 Years:**
  - **Initial Dose:** Two tablets taken with fluid.
  - **Subsequent Dosing:** One or two tablets every 4 hours as required.
  - **Maximum Dose:** 6 tablets in a 24-hour period.
- **Administration Instructions:** The lowest effective dose should be used for the shortest duration necessary to achieve symptomatic relief.
- **Ceasing Therapy:** In patients potentially physically dependent on opioids, the dose should be tapered by 10% to 25% every 2 to 4 weeks to avoid withdrawal symptoms; abrupt cessation is not recommended [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Age-Specific:** 
  - Patients aged <12 years [Australian].
  - Patients aged 12–18 years with compromised respiratory function, including post-tonsillectomy or adenoidectomy for obstructive sleep apnoea [Australian].
- **Metabolism:** CYP2D6 ultra-rapid metabolisers [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ibuprofen, codeine, other opioids, or NSAIDs (e.g., history of asthma, rhinitis, or urticaria following aspirin/NSAID use).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Active or recurrent peptic ulcer/haemorrhage; active gastrointestinal bleeding.
- **Respiratory:** Severe or acute respiratory disease; respiratory depression.
- **Medical Conditions:** Chronic constipation, active alcoholism, or severe hepatic impairment.
- **Concomitant Medications:** Use with other NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors); use of MAOIs or within 14 days of discontinuing MAOIs.
- **Pregnancy/Lactation:** Third trimester of pregnancy; breastfeeding [Australian].
- **Surgical:** Treatment of perioperative pain in the setting of Coronary Artery Bypass Surgery (CABG).
- **Infection/Illness:** Diarrhoea caused by pseudomembranous colitis or poisoning until the toxin is eliminated.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [Australian]:**
  - **Hazardous and Harmful Use:** Poses risks of addiction, abuse, and misuse, which can lead to overdose and death.
  - **Respiratory Depression:** Serious, life-threatening, or fatal respiratory depression may occur.
  - **CNS Depressants:** Concomitant use with benzodiazepines, gabapentinoids, or alcohol may result in profound sedation, coma, and death.
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Effects:** Risk of fatal GI bleeding, ulceration, and perforation. Use with caution in patients with a history of ulcerative colitis or Crohn’s disease.
- **Cardiovascular (CV) Effects:** NSAIDs are associated with an increased risk of serious CV thrombotic events, including myocardial infarction and stroke.
- **Severe Skin Reactions:** Potentially fatal reactions including Acute Generalised Exanthematous Pustulosis (AGEP), Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS), Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) have been reported.
- **Central Sleep Apnoea (CSA):** Opioids can cause dose-dependent sleep-related breathing disorders.
- **Infection:** Ibuprofen may mask the signs of underlying infection.
- **Specific Clinical Precautions:** Caution in patients with systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), mixed connective tissue disease (increased risk of aseptic meningitis), hypotension, hypothyroidism, raised intracranial pressure, or prostatic hypertrophy.

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Respiratory depression, GI perforation, myocardial infarction, stroke, and exfoliative dermatitis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
  - **CNS Depressants (Alcohol, Benzodiazepines):** Potentiation of sedation and respiratory depression.
  - **Antihypertensives (ACEIs, Beta-blockers, Diuretics):** Ibuprofen may reduce efficacy; risk of "triple whammy" renal failure when combined with ACEIs and diuretics.
  - **Anticoagulants (Warfarin):** Increased risk of severe bleeding.
  - **Serotonergic Drugs:** Risk of serotonin syndrome when combined with opioids.
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
  - **Lithium/Methotrexate:** Ibuprofen reduces renal clearance, increasing plasma levels and toxicity risk.
  - **CYP2D6 Inhibitors (e.g., Quinidine, Abiraterone):** May reduce the analgesic effect of codeine by preventing conversion to morphine.
  - **Aspirin:** Ibuprofen may inhibit the anti-platelet effect of low-dose aspirin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **Category C [Australian]:** Ibuprofen is contraindicated in the third trimester due to risks of premature closure of the ductus arteriosus, renal dysfunction, and prolonged labour. Opioids may cause neonatal withdrawal. Use in the first 6 months only if benefits outweigh risks.
- **Breastfeeding:** **Contraindicated [Australian].** Risk of morphine toxicity and respiratory depression in the infant, particularly if the mother is a CYP2D6 ultra-rapid metaboliser.
- **Fertility:** NSAIDs may reversibly impair female fertility via effects on ovulation [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated in children under 12 years and those under 18 years post-tonsillectomy/adenoidectomy [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of CV, GI, and renal adverse effects. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration.
- **Renal Impairment:** Avoid use in severe renal impairment. Risk of further deterioration in renal function.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment. Caution required in milder cases due to hepatotoxicity risks.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
  - **Ibuprofen:** Peripheral inhibition of cyclooxygenases (COX-1 and COX-2) resulting in reduced prostaglandin synthesis.
  - **Codeine:** Centrally acting analgesic; approximately one-sixth the potency of morphine. Efficacy depends on conversion to morphine via CYP2D6.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
  - **Absorption:** Ibuprofen peak levels at 1–2 hours; Codeine peak levels at ~1 hour.
  - **Metabolism:** Ibuprofen (liver, 90% to inactive metabolites); Codeine (O- and N-demethylation in liver to morphine, norcodeine, etc.).
  - **Excretion:** Both primarily renal. Ibuprofen half-life: 1.9–2.2 hours; Codeine half-life: 2–4 hours.
