# Editorial Product Information: Idarucizumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following official Product Information (PI) document:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: "Product Information — PRAXBIND (Idarucizumab)"
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official PI when prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI to ensure compliance with regional legal and regulatory requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Idarucizumab (rch), a humanised monoclonal antibody fragment (Fab).
- **Chemical Class**: Antidote; specific reversal agent for dabigatran.
- **Brand Name**: **PRAXBIND** [Australian].
- **ATC Code**: V03AB37.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: Solution for injection/infusion; 2.5 g/50 mL (50 mg/mL) per vial.
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Boehringer Ingelheim Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Idarucizumab** is indicated for patients treated with dabigatran etexilate (**PRADAXA**) when rapid reversal of its anticoagulant effects is required for:
- Emergency surgery or urgent procedures.
- Life-threatening or uncontrolled bleeding.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Dose**: 5 g (provided as 2 x 2.5 g/50 mL vials).
- **Administration Method**:
    - Administered intravenously as two consecutive infusions (5–10 minutes each) or as two consecutive bolus injections.
- **Repeat Dosing**: A second 5 g dose may be considered if:
    - Clinically relevant bleeding recurs alongside prolonged clotting times (e.g., aPTT, dTT, or ECT).
    - A second emergency procedure is required and clotting times remain prolonged.
- **Restarting Antithrombotic Therapy**: To reduce thromboembolic risk, anticoagulant therapy (e.g., dabigatran) can be initiated 24 hours after **PRAXBIND** administration, provided the patient is clinically stable and adequate haemostasis has been achieved.
- **Administration Notes**: 
    - The IV line must be flushed with sterile 0.9% sodium chloride before and after administration.
    - Do not mix with other medicinal products.

## 4. Contraindications
- None identified in the available source [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Thromboembolic Events**: Reversing dabigatran exposes patients to the underlying thrombotic risk of their condition. Resumption of anticoagulation should be considered as soon as medically appropriate.
- **Hypersensitivity**: If an anaphylactic or serious allergic reaction occurs, discontinue administration immediately and initiate appropriate therapy.
- **Hereditary Fructose Intolerance (HFI)**: The 5 g dose contains 4 g of sorbitol as an excipient. In patients with HFI, parenteral sorbitol is associated with risks of hypoglycaemia, hypophosphatemia, metabolic acidosis, acute liver failure, and death. Benefit must be weighed against risk in these patients.
- **Proteinuria**: Transient proteinuria may occur as a physiologic reaction to renal protein overflow; this is not indicative of permanent renal damage.
- **Re-elevation of Coagulation Parameters**: In some patients, unbound dabigatran concentrations may recur 1–24 hours post-dose, leading to renewed prolongation of clotting tests.
- **Sodium Content**: Contains 2.2 mmol (50 mg) sodium per dose; consider for patients on a controlled sodium diet.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile was evaluated in healthy volunteers and patients (RE-VERSE AD study).
- **Infections**: Urinary tract infection (11.3%), Pneumonia (8.0%).
- **Gastrointestinal**: Constipation (10.5%), Nausea (7.8%), Diarrhoea (6.4%).
- **General Disorders**: Pyrexia (6.0%), Peripheral oedema (6.4%).
- **Nervous System**: Headache (6.8%), Dizziness (4.0%).
- **Vascular**: Hypotension (6.8%).
- **Serious Adverse Effects**: Thrombotic events occurred in 6.8% of patients (34/503), though these were generally attributed to the underlying medical condition rather than the drug.
- **Hypersensitivity**: Rare reports of bronchospasm, rash, and pruritus. Anaphylactic reactions have been reported (0.2%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**: No formal studies performed; however, interactions are considered unlikely due to the high specificity of binding to dabigatran.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions**: No interactions were observed with volume expanders, coagulation factor concentrates, or other anticoagulants (e.g., heparin, Factor Xa inhibitors) in preclinical models.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **Australian Category B2**. 
    - No clinical data available. It should only be used if the potential benefit outweighs the risk.
- **Breastfeeding**: It is unknown whether idarucizumab is excreted in human milk.
- **Fertility**: No human data available; preclinical studies showed no adverse effects on reproductive tissues or embryo-fetal development.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy have not been established. A single 17-year-old female (weight >70kg) was successfully treated in a clinical trial setting [Australian].
- **Elderly Use**: No dose adjustment required. Age does not have a clinically meaningful effect on pharmacokinetics.
- **Renal Impairment**: No dose adjustment is required. While renal impairment increases idarucizumab exposure (AUC increases by ~38% in mild, ~90% in moderate, and ~146% in severe impairment), this does not impact the reversal effect.
- **Hepatic Impairment**: No dose adjustment required. Hepatic impairment did not meaningfully affect the pharmacokinetics of idarucizumab.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Idarucizumab is a specific reversal agent for dabigatran. It binds to dabigatran and its metabolites with an affinity approximately 300 times higher than that of dabigatran for thrombin, neutralising the anticoagulant effect.
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Multiphasic kinetics; Vss of 8.9 L.
    - **Metabolism**: Biodegradation into small peptides and amino acids via protein catabolism.
    - **Elimination**: Primarily renal. Total clearance is 47.0 mL/min. Initial half-life is 47 minutes; terminal half-life is 10.3 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **RE-VERSE AD Study**: A prospective, open-label trial of 503 patients requiring urgent dabigatran reversal.
- **Primary Outcome**: Maximum percentage reversal of dabigatran within 4 hours.
- **Results**: Complete reversal was achieved in 82–99% of evaluable patients (based on dTT, ECT, and aPTT). Reversal was evident immediately after administration. Normal haemostasis was achieved in 80.3% of patients with serious bleeding and 93.4% of patients requiring procedures.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage**: Store in a refrigerator at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze.
- **Light Protection**: Store in the original package to protect from light.
- **Stability**: Prior to use, unopened vials may be kept at room temperature (up to 30°C) for up to 48 hours if protected from light. Once opened, the solution is stable for up to 6 hours at room temperature.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule**: **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Legal Classification**: Rx-only.
