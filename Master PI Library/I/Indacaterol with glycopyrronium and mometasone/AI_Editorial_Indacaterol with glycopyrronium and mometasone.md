# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising available clinical data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or prescribing information required for clinical practice in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with a confirmed inborn error of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer via the **intravenous route only** [US].
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crisis, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Dosing Frequency:** Should be administered every 3 or 4 hours (q3h/q4h) and never less than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels.
- Perform weekly and monthly monitoring including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity Reactions
- Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients [US].
- Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before any attempt at re-administration [US].

### Neurologic Reactions (Seizures)
- Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].

### General Precautions
- **Renal Impairment & Oral Levocarnitine:** The safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral formulations in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]), which are normally renally excreted [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD Trials)
Based on placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (21%), injection site reaction (38%), pain (35%), headache (37%), fever (12%), asthenia (12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (21%), hypotension (19%), chest pain (15%), tachycardia (9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (35%), vomiting (21%), nausea (12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (27%), cough increase (18%), dyspnea (11%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (15%), hypervolemia (12%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (18%), paresthesia (12%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent gastritis.
- **Other:** Body odor [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Warfarin
- Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- **Category:** Not explicitly lettered in available source, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits (at 3.8 times the human dose) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Recommendation:** Should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].

### Breastfeeding
- Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied.
- Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations following administration.
- **Management:** Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother; consider discontinuation of nursing or the drug [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 for ESRD dosing and Section 5 for precautions regarding oral metabolites [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production.
- Promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels).
- **Half-life:** Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study (ESRD):** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly), trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Evaluated 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 24 weeks, showing a dose-proportional increase in trough carnitine concentrations [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentration) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Preservative:** None; discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
