## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion.
    - A loading dose may be utilized in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - Frequency: Every 3 or 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and never less than every 6 hours (q6h).
    - Maximum reported dose: 300 mg/kg.
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations and may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy.

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration before use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable and compatible for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks/benefits before re-administration [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** In metabolic disorders, monitor plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L), blood chemistry, and vital signs weekly or monthly.
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Precaution):** While this PI focuses on the injection, clinicians should note that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may result in the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions reported in $\ge$ 5% of patients in clinical trials (ESRD population) regardless of causality include:

| Body System | Adverse Event (Incidence $\ge$ 5%) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Body as a Whole** | Abdominal pain (9%), Asthenia (9%), Chest pain (12%), Fever (7%), Headache (22%), Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%) |
| **Digestive** | Nausea (8%), Vomiting (15%), Diarrhea (16%) |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hypervolemia (5%) |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness (13%), Depression (5%), Paresthesia (5%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%), Rhinitis (9%) |
| **Skin** | Pruritus (5%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Anaphylaxis and seizures [US].
- **Other:** Transient nausea/vomiting and body odor have been observed.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Recommendation:** Monitor INR levels after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a lettered category in the provided source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
    - **Clinical Guidance:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for inborn errors of metabolism apply to paediatric patients. Specific age-range safety profiles for ESRD were not detailed.
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing for ESRD patients is specific (10-20 mg/kg post-dialysis). The injection avoids the metabolite accumulation seen with high-dose oral therapy in renal failure [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, levocarnitine injection (10, 20, or 40 mg/kg) significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough concentrations remained stable or increased over 24 weeks of therapy. While biochemical efficacy was demonstrated, clinical outcomes regarding specific signs and symptoms of carnitine deficiency in this population have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **US:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources for other jurisdictions.
