# Editorial Product Information: Ivosidenib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a structured reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the local approved product information for definitive legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ivosidenib (a small molecule inhibitor of mutant isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 [IDH1] enzymes).
- **Brand Name:** **TIBSOVO** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XX62.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Film-coated tablets containing 250 mg of ivosidenib [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Servier Laboratories (Aust.) Pty. Ltd. [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cholangiocarcinoma:** Treatment of adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic cholangiocarcinoma carrying an isocitrate dehydrogenase-1 (IDH1) R132 mutation, following at least one prior line of systemic therapy [Australian].
- **Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML):** Treatment of AML carrying an IDH1 R132 mutation in the following settings [Australian]:
    - As monotherapy or in combination with azacitidine for newly diagnosed patients ineligible for intensive induction chemotherapy.
    - As monotherapy for patients with relapsed or refractory AML.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Dose:** 500 mg (two 250 mg tablets) orally once daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole; do not split, crush, or chew.
    - Administer at approximately the same time each day.
    - May be taken with or without food, but **must not** be taken with a high-fat meal due to increased drug exposure [Australian].
- **Treatment Duration:**
    - **Cholangiocarcinoma:** Continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
    - **AML:** Continue for a minimum of six months to allow for clinical response, unless disease progression or unacceptable toxicity occurs [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** 
    - If a dose is missed by <12 hours, take it as soon as possible. 
    - If >12 hours have elapsed, skip the dose and resume the next scheduled dose. 
    - If vomiting occurs after a dose, do not take a replacement; wait for the next scheduled dose [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy (AML):** When used with azacitidine, azacitidine is dosed at 75 mg/m² (IV or SC) daily on Days 1–7 (or 1–5, then 8–9) of a 28-day cycle [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ivosidenib or any of the excipients (including lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [Australian]:** 
    - **Differentiation Syndrome (DS):** Can be fatal in AML patients. Symptoms include leukocytosis, pyrexia, hypoxia, hypotension, dyspnoea, fluid overload, and pleural/pericardial effusion.
    - **QTc Interval Prolongation:** Dose-dependent prolongation may occur, potentially leading to fatal ventricular arrhythmias.
- **Differentiation Syndrome Management:** If suspected, initiate systemic corticosteroids (e.g., dexamethasone 10 mg IV every 12 hours) and haemodynamic monitoring for at least three days or until symptoms resolve. If Grade 3+ symptoms persist >48 hours after starting steroids, interrupt ivosidenib [Australian].
- **QTc Monitoring:** Perform ECG at baseline, weekly for the first three weeks, and monthly thereafter. Correct electrolyte abnormalities (potassium, magnesium, calcium) at baseline and monitor throughout [Australian].
- **Guillain-Barré Syndrome:** Has occurred uncommonly. Monitor for motor/sensory neuropathy (weakness, paraesthesia, difficulty breathing). Permanently discontinue if diagnosed [Australian].
- **Non-infectious Leukocytosis:** May require treatment with hydroxyurea [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect profiles vary by indication and use of combination therapy.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Cholangiocarcinoma:** Pneumonia, ascites, hyperbilirubinaemia, and jaundice cholestatic (≥2%) [Australian].
- **AML:** Differentiation syndrome (8–18%), QTc prolongation, and febrile neutropenia [Australian].

### Common Adverse Effects (≥10%)
| System Organ Class | Adverse Events |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea, nausea, abdominal pain, vomiting, constipation, mucositis |
| **General** | Fatigue, oedema, pyrexia, chest pain |
| **Respiratory** | Cough, dyspnoea, pleural effusion |
| **Nervous System** | Headache, dizziness, neuropathy |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Arthralgia, myalgia |
| **Metabolism** | Decreased appetite, tumour lysis syndrome (AML) |
| **Skin** | Rash, pruritus |
| **Investigations** | QTc prolongation, weight decreased |

### Laboratory Abnormalities (Very Common >10%)
- Decreased: Haemoglobin, lymphocytes, neutrophils, phosphate, magnesium, potassium, sodium, calcium.
- Increased: AST, bilirubin, glucose, uric acid, alkaline phosphatase, creatinine [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Greatly increase ivosidenib exposure. Avoid co-administration. If unavoidable, reduce ivosidenib dose to 250 mg daily. If the inhibitor is stopped, resume 500 mg ivosidenib after 5 half-lives of the inhibitor [Australian].
- **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** Decrease ivosidenib exposure and efficacy. Avoid co-administration [Australian].
- **CYP Substrates:** Ivosidenib induces CYP3A4, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, and CYP2C9. It may reduce the efficacy of hormonal contraceptives and substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., alfentanil, cyclosporin, sirolimus) [Australian].
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Avoid concomitant use with anti-arrhythmics, fluoroquinolones, and 5-HT3 antagonists [Australian].
- **Transporters:** Ivosidenib inhibits OAT3 and P-gp. Avoid co-administration with dabigatran due to increased risk of haemorrhage [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Based on animal data, ivosidenib may cause fetal harm (embryofetal mortality/growth alteration). Verify pregnancy status before starting. 
- **Contraception:** Advise females of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 1 month after the final dose. Note that ivosidenib reduces the efficacy of hormonal contraceptives; barrier methods are recommended [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety in humans is unknown. Due to potential risks, breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment and for at least one month after the last dose [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No human data. Animal studies showed reversible uterine atrophy in females and testicular degeneration in males [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness observed in patients ≥65 years [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild to moderate impairment. Not studied in severe renal impairment (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²) or patients requiring dialysis; use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild (Child-Pugh A) or moderate (Child-Pugh B) impairment. Not studied in severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C); use with caution [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selectively inhibits mutant IDH1 enzymes (R132C, R132L, R132G, R132H, and R132S). In AML, this reduces 2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG) levels and promotes the differentiation of blast cells into mature myeloid cells [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Tmax is 2 hours (cholangiocarcinoma) to 3 hours (AML). High-fat meals increase Cmax by ~98% and AUC by ~24%.
    - **Distribution:** 92–96% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 with minor contributions from N-dealkylation and hydrolytic pathways.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~129 hours (cholangiocarcinoma) and 58–98 hours (AML). Excreted in faeces (77%) and urine (17%) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Cholangiocarcinoma (ClarIDHy):** A Phase 3 trial (N=185) demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in progression-free survival (PFS) for ivosidenib versus placebo (HR 0.37; p <0.0001) in patients with IDH1-mutated disease [Australian].
- **Newly Diagnosed AML (Study AG120-C-009):** A Phase 3 trial (N=146) evaluating ivosidenib + azacitidine vs placebo + azacitidine. The combination showed superior event-free survival (HR 0.33; p=0.0011) and median overall survival (24.0 vs 7.9 months) [Australian].
- **Relapsed/Refractory AML (Study AG120-C-001):** A Phase 1 trial (N=174) demonstrated a complete remission (CR) + CR with partial haematologic recovery (CRh) rate of 32.8% [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C in a dry place. Keep the bottle tightly closed to protect from moisture [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
