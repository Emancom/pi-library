## 3. Approved Indications
- Nutritional support of infants (including low birth weight) and young children requiring total parenteral nutrition (TPN) via central or peripheral infusion.
- Prevention of nitrogen and weight loss or treatment of negative nitrogen balance in infants and young children where:
  - the alimentary tract cannot or should not be used or adequate protein intake is not feasible by enteral routes;
  - gastrointestinal absorption of protein is impaired; or
  - protein requirements are substantially increased (for example, extensive burns).
- Use as part of a TPN regimen providing protein (amino acids) in conjunction with nonprotein calories (e.g., dextrose), electrolytes, vitamins, minerals, and, if indicated, fat emulsion. Essential fatty acids must be provided separately.
- Regional approvals/differences: Not specified in available sources (indications above reflect the available source).

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (pediatric)
- Infants up to 10 kg: up to 2.5 g amino acids/kg/day.
- Infants and children >10 kg: up to 25 g amino acids/day for the first 10 kg of body weight plus 1.0 to 1.25 g amino acids/kg/day for each additional kg over 10 kg.
- Initial dose: commonly 1 g/kg/day, increased gradually by 0.5 g/kg/day increments to desired intake.

### Caloric and fluid guidance
- Nonprotein calories: approximately 100–130 kcal/kg/day (from dextrose and lipid emulsion).
- Fat emulsion: may provide up to 60% of total daily calories; do not exceed 4 g fat/kg/day.
- Recommended fluid intake for infants receiving central venous TPN: approximately 125 mL/kg/day (range 100–175 mL/kg/day), individualized per clinical condition.

### Administration instructions
- Amino acid solution should be diluted with appropriate dextrose/carbohydrate solutions before administration according to the required final dextrose concentration and osmolarity.
- Central venous infusion: recommended for hypertonic admixtures and for patients requiring high caloric/protein support or long-term TPN. Ensure tip of central catheter is properly positioned (superior vena cava) and administered by personnel trained in central venous techniques.
- Peripheral parenteral nutrition: permissible for moderately catabolic or depleted patients when central access is not indicated. Final dextrose concentration typically 5–10%; concurrent lipid emulsion is recommended to reduce venous irritation.
- Initial infusion rates should be slow and increased gradually to target daily volumes (example guidance: increase toward 60–125 mL/kg/day as tolerated). If administration falls behind schedule, do not attempt to "catch up" by increasing rates beyond recommended tolerance.
- Inspect final admixture visually for cloudiness or precipitation immediately after mixing and periodically during administration. Do not use if precipitate or incompatibility is observed.
- Do not withdraw blood for laboratory chemistries through the same venous line delivering the amino acid infusion to avoid interference with nitrogen measurements.
- Additives (electrolytes, vitamins, trace elements, medications) may be incompatible. Use aseptic technique when introducing additives; mix thoroughly and use immediately. Consult pharmacy if available.

### Additives and supplements
- Cysteine: addition of cysteine hydrochloride is recommended for infants, particularly preterm neonates. Suggested ratio (from available source): 500 mg cysteine hydrochloride injection for every 12.5 g (179 mL) of this amino acid solution; do not exceed concentrations that may cause insolubility.
- Electrolytes (sodium, potassium, chloride, phosphate, magnesium, calcium) should be added based on laboratory monitoring and clinical status. Note: this amino acid formulation contains acetate (from lysine acetate) but no added chloride; contains no added phosphorus — phosphate supplementation may be required.
- Trace metals (zinc, copper, manganese, chromium) and full vitamin supplementation should be provided for patients anticipated to receive long-term parenteral nutrition.

### Titration schedules
- Increase amino acid and dextrose intake gradually based on tolerance and serial monitoring of blood glucose, urine glucose, and other metabolic parameters.
- Provide insulin when hyperglycemia occurs or is anticipated due to high dextrose infusion rates.

## 5. Contraindications
- Untreated anuria.
- Hepatic coma.
- Known inborn errors of amino acid metabolism, including disorders of branched-chain amino acid metabolism such as maple syrup urine disease and isovaleric acidemia.
- Hypersensitivity to one or more of the amino acids present in the solution.
- Additional regional or jurisdiction-specific contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/major warnings
- **Aluminum content:** This product contains aluminum that may be toxic. Aluminum may reach toxic levels with prolonged parenteral administration in patients with impaired kidney function. Premature neonates are particularly at risk. Research indicates that parenteral aluminum exposure greater than approximately 4–5 mcg/kg/day can lead to accumulation associated with central nervous system and bone toxicity; tissue loading may occur at lower rates. [US]
- **Hyperammonemia in infants:** Administration may precipitate or exacerbate hyperammonemia, particularly in infants and in patients with undiagnosed metabolic defects. Blood ammonia should be measured frequently in infants; reduce amino acid dosage if hyperammonemia develops.

### General warnings and precautions
- Parenteral nutrition requires specialized knowledge and frequent clinical and laboratory monitoring (see Monitoring requirements below).
- Use caution in patients with renal impairment or azotemia; amino acid administration can increase blood urea nitrogen. Patients with azotemia should not receive amino acid infusions without careful adjustment of total nitrogen intake.
- Use caution in hepatic insufficiency: infusion may result in plasma amino acid imbalances, hyperammonemia, prerenal azotemia, stupor, and coma.
- Fluid and solute overload: administration may cause dilutional electrolyte abnormalities, overhydration, congestive states, or pulmonary edema. Exercise caution in cardiac insufficiency.
- Hyperglycemia risk: particularly with high dextrose delivery or in diabetic/pre-diabetic patients; insulin may be required. Rebound hypoglycemia may occur if hypertonic dextrose is abruptly discontinued—provide 5% dextrose as transition when stopping hypertonic dextrose.
- Essential fatty acid deficiency (EFAD) risk with prolonged TPN; ensure provision of linoleic acid via lipid emulsion (typically providing 4–10% of total caloric intake as linoleic acid).
- Peripheral infusion: requires appropriate dilution and provision of adequate calories; monitor infusion site frequently for infiltration, phlebitis, or thrombosis.
- Additives and incompatibilities: calcium and phosphate may precipitate if mixed improperly; some additives may be incompatible—spread electrolyte additives through the day and consider alternate infusion bottles for potentially incompatible ions.
- Septic risk: constant risk of sepsis with parenteral nutrition via central venous catheters; strict aseptic technique, catheter care, and monitoring are essential.
- Central venous catheter complications: placement and maintenance are associated with risks such as pneumothorax, hemothorax, hydrothorax, arterial puncture, brachial plexus injury, catheter malposition, arteriovenous fistula, phlebitis, thrombosis, and air or catheter emboli. Central venous administration should be performed by personnel familiar with technique and complications.
- Inspection of admixtures: color variation (pale yellow to yellow) may be normal; however, visual inspection for particulate matter and discoloration before administration is required.

### Monitoring requirements
- Frequent laboratory and clinical evaluation recommended: blood glucose, serum proteins, kidney and liver function tests, serum electrolytes, blood urea nitrogen, blood ammonia (particularly frequent in infants), hemogram, arterial/venous blood gases or total CO2, serum osmolality, and blood cultures if infection suspected.
- Monitor urinary osmolality and urine glucose as clinically indicated.
- Inspect venous access sites frequently and change infusion sites if thrombosis or phlebitis occurs.
- Monitor serum levels of calcium and phosphorus when adding phosphate; monitor trace metals and vitamins during long-term therapy.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Avoid administering hypertonic mixtures via peripheral veins—prefer central access for hypertonic solutions.
- When adding cysteine hydrochloride, adhere to recommended concentrations to avoid precipitation or insolubility.
- If signs of an adverse reaction occur during infusion, discontinue infusion, evaluate the patient, institute supportive measures, and retain remaining fluid for examination if indicated.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Pharmacodynamic and administration interactions (from available sources):
  - Concurrent administration of hypertonic dextrose and this amino acid solution may require insulin administration to prevent or treat hyperglycemia.
  - Additives (electrolytes, vitamins, trace elements) alter the overall electrolyte and solute load and must be accounted for in total daily intake.
  - Calcium and phosphate: risk of precipitation when combined improperly; consider alternating infusate containers or sequencing additions to avoid precipitation.
  - Irritating medications or solutions (e.g., some antibiotics) administered through the same peripheral site may increase local venous irritation, phlebitis, or thrombosis; consider separate venous access.
  - Incompatibilities with other additives: the final admixture should be inspected for cloudiness or precipitation immediately after mixing and periodically during administration; consult pharmacy if available.
- Pharmacokinetic interactions: Not specified in available sources.
- Clinically significant interactions and management:
  - Monitor blood glucose closely when administering high dextrose loads and provide insulin as needed.
  - Avoid coadministration of incompatible additives; when adding calcium and phosphate, follow protocols to minimize precipitation risk.
  - When adding cysteine hydrochloride, adhere to recommended concentrations to avoid insolubility.
- Regional interaction differences: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Indicated for infants (including low birth weight) and young children requiring TPN.
- Dosing and administration guidance provided in Section 4 above (up to 2.5 g/kg/day for infants ≤10 kg; dosing schema for >10 kg).
- Cysteine supplementation recommended for infants, particularly preterm neonates (suggested ratio: 500 mg cysteine HCl per 12.5 g [179 mL] of amino acid solution).
- Monitoring: frequent blood ammonia measurements are essential in infants due to risk of hyperammonemia; clinical and laboratory monitoring is emphasized.
- Taurine: included in the mixture; taurine plays a role in bile salt conjugation in the newborn and central nervous system development.

### Elderly

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Provides a balanced mixture of essential and nonessential amino acids (including taurine) that serve as a parenteral nitrogen/protein source to support protein synthesis, nitrogen balance, and growth in infants and young children requiring TPN.

### Pharmacodynamics
- When administered with cysteine hydrochloride and appropriate calories, plasma amino acid concentrations have been observed to approximate profiles of breast-fed infants in clinical studies cited in the available source; associated with improvements in weight gain, nitrogen balance, and serum protein concentrations in infants requiring TPN.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Absorption: Not applicable for intravenous administration beyond immediate availability in central circulation.
- Distribution, metabolism, elimination: Not specified in available sources in quantitative pharmacokinetic terms. Amino acids are metabolized and incorporated into protein or used as substrates for energy and intermediary metabolism per normal physiology.
- Acetate component: acetate is provided from lysine acetate (amount reported as 32.5 mEq/L in the available source); it is thought that acetate under conditions of parenteral nutrition does not significantly affect net acid-base balance when renal and respiratory functions are normal, although confirmatory experimental evidence is not available.
- Aluminum content: product contains aluminum (reported no more than 25 mcg/L in the available source).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Available source summarizes clinical experience and studies in infants requiring TPN:
  - Infants receiving this amino acid formulation (with cysteine hydrochloride additive and appropriate dextrose, electrolytes, vitamins, and minerals) achieved plasma amino acid concentrations approximating those of normal breast- or formula-fed infants.
  - Reported clinical outcomes included improved weight gain, improved nitrogen balance, and increased serum protein concentrations consistent with improving nutritional status.
