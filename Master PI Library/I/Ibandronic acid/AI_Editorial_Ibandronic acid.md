# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)** for **Levocarnitine Injection** (Baxter Healthcare Corporation). 
- Note: While the user instructions mentioned a document for Ibandronic Acid from Australia, the provided source material exclusively pertains to **Levocarnitine Injection**. Consequently, this harmonized document reflects the clinical data for Levocarnitine.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonized editorial overview for healthcare professionals.
- This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Metabolic Disorders:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Standard Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized based on clinical requirement.

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40–50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to therapy. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine levels (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate appropriate medical management [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a known history, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Safety of oral levocarnitine has not been established in renal impairment. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients (frequency ≥5%, regardless of causality):
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (30%), chest pain (12%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), infection (15%), abdominal pain (21%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%), arrhythmia (2–5%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16–35%), vomiting (15–21%), nausea (8–12%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%).
- **Neurologic:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Neurological: Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US]: Not specified by letter category, but indicated for use only if clearly needed. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm.
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of carnitine following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant of excess carnitine must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Dosage and Administration for metabolic disorders; specific age-based adjustments are not detailed beyond weight-based dosing [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Oral formulations are cautioned against in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis), trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg three times weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Clinical Outcome Note:** While IV levocarnitine effectively increases plasma carnitine concentrations in ESRD patients, the impact on specific signs, symptoms, or clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservatives.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted to concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s [US].
