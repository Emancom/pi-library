# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following provided Product Information (PI) document: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and professional reference. It is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local regulatory documentation before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/generic).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule / amino acid derivative ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Sponsor: Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Administration:** Administer via slow intravenous bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.

### Dosing by Indication
- **Metabolic Disorders:**
    - **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
    - **Acute Management:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. Dosing intervals should be every 3 or 4 hours and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses may be required. The maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - **Timing:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - **Adjustments:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough carnitine concentrations and may occur as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Monitoring
- Pre-treatment plasma carnitine concentrations should be obtained.
- Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Neurological (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Cautions:** Note that the safety of the *oral* formulation in renal impairment has not been established; chronic high-dose oral carnitine in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine and trimethylamine-N-oxide) [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Periodic monitoring of plasma carnitine levels and clinical status is essential to prevent overtreatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology of metabolic disorders or ESRD.

| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, abdominal pain, asthenia, accidental injury |

### Serious/Infrequent Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, body odor (associated with carnitine therapy) [US].
- **Neurological:** Seizures.
- **Immunological:** Anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine in patients on anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Pregnancy Category not specified in modern labeling, but historically classified under Category B findings]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal treatment. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, yeast) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg (see Section 3). The safety and efficacy in the paediatric dialysis population have not been specifically detailed outside general dosing guidelines [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Dose adjustments are based on pre-dialysis trough concentrations [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine may promote the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with metabolic defects by forming acylcarnitine esters that are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Studies:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis (20 mg/kg three times weekly) showed that trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg post-dialysis showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations at 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
