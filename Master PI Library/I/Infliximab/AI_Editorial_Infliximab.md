# Editorial Product Information: Infliximab

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of infliximab product information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. This synthesis is based on the Australian PI for **Inflectra**, an approved biosimilar to the reference product **Remicade**.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Infliximab (rmc)
- **Chemical Class:** Chimeric human-murine IgG1 monoclonal antibody
- **Brand Name(s):** **Inflectra** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L04AB02
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Lyophilised powder for injection; 100 mg per single-use glass vial. Upon reconstitution, the solution contains 10 mg/mL of infliximab [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
Infliximab is indicated for:
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis (Adults):** In combination with methotrexate to reduce signs/symptoms and prevent structural joint damage in patients with active disease (regardless of prior methotrexate exposure) [Australian].
- **Ankylosing Spondylitis:** Reduction of signs/symptoms and improvement of physical function in patients with active disease [Australian].
- **Psoriatic Arthritis:** Treatment of signs/symptoms and improvement of physical function in adults with active and progressive disease who responded inadequately to DMARDs. May be used with or without methotrexate [Australian].
- **Psoriasis:** Treatment of adults with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis who are candidates for phototherapy or systemic therapy [Australian].
- **Crohn’s Disease (Adult and Paediatric 6–17 years):** Treatment of moderate to severe active disease to reduce signs/symptoms and induce/maintain clinical remission in patients with inadequate response to conventional therapies [Australian].
- **Refractory Fistulising Crohn’s Disease (Adults):** Reducing the number of draining enterocutaneous and rectovaginal fistulas and maintaining closure [Australian].
- **Ulcerative Colitis (Adult and Paediatric 6–17 years):** Treatment of moderate to severe active disease in patients with inadequate response to conventional therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** 3 mg/kg at 0, 2, and 6 weeks, then every 8 weeks. Dose may be adjusted in 1.5 mg/kg increments up to 7.5 mg/kg [Australian].
- **Ankylosing Spondylitis:** 5 mg/kg at 0, 2, and 6 weeks, then every 6 weeks [Australian].
- **Psoriatic Arthritis, Psoriasis, Crohn’s Disease, and Ulcerative Colitis:** 5 mg/kg at 0, 2, and 6 weeks, then every 8 weeks [Australian].
- **Fistulising Crohn’s Disease:** 5 mg/kg at 0, 2, and 6 weeks, then every 8 weeks. If response is lost during maintenance, dose may be increased to 10 mg/kg [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Infusion Rate:** Administer over not less than 2 hours.
- **Shortened Infusions:** In selected adults who have tolerated at least 3 initial 2-hour infusions, maintenance doses (≤6 mg/kg) may be infused over not less than 1 hour [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Observe patients for at least 1–2 hours post-infusion for side effects.
- **Preparation:** Reconstitute with 10 mL Water for Injections; further dilute in 0.9% sodium chloride to a final volume of 250 mL. Use a low protein-binding filter (pore size ≤1.2 μm).

## 4. Contraindications
- Severe infections, including sepsis, abscesses, tuberculosis (TB), and opportunistic infections [Australian].
- History of hypersensitivity to infliximab, other murine proteins, or any excipient [Australian].
- Concurrent administration with anakinra [Australian].
- Moderate to severe congestive heart failure (NYHA Class III/IV) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Infections:** Patients are at high risk for serious bacterial, mycobacterial (TB), invasive fungal, and viral infections. TB screening (skin test and chest X-ray) is mandatory before treatment [Australian].
- **Malignancies:** Increased risk of lymphoma and other malignancies. Post-marketing cases of fatal hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTCL) have occurred, primarily in adolescent/young adult males with IBD treated concomitantly with azathioprine or 6-mercaptopurine [Australian].
- **Infusion/Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Acute (within 2 hours) and delayed reactions (serum-sickness-like, 3–12 days post-infusion) can occur. Re-administration after a drug-free interval >16 weeks is not recommended due to increased risk of hypersensitivity [Australian].
- **Neurological Events:** Potential for new onset or exacerbation of CNS demyelinating disorders (e.g., multiple sclerosis, optic neuritis) and peripheral demyelinating disorders (e.g., Guillain-Barré syndrome) [Australian].
- **Hepatobiliary Events:** Severe hepatic reactions, including liver failure and reactivation of Hepatitis B, have been reported. Monitor ALT/AST; discontinue if ALT ≥5x ULN [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Events |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Viral infection (influenza, herpes), headache, upper respiratory tract infection, sinusitis, nausea, abdominal pain, infusion-related reactions, pain. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Neutropaenia, leukopaenia, anaemia, vertigo, flushing, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, abnormal hepatic function, rash, pruritus, urticaria, fatigue, pyrexia. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Sepsis, abscess, cellulitis, depression, confusion, agitation, seizure, hypertension, hypotension, syncope, constipation, cholecystitis, alopecia. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Tuberculosis, lymphoma, leukemia, meningitis, cardiac failure, arrhythmia, interstitial lung disease, hepatitis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Sepsis, opportunistic infections, TB, lymphoma, HSTCL, and severe infusion reactions (anaphylaxis) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Anakinra:** Concurrent use is contraindicated due to increased infection risk without added benefit [Australian].
- **Abatacept:** Concomitant use is not recommended due to increased risk of serious infections [Australian].
- **Methotrexate:** Reduces the formation of antibodies against infliximab and increases its plasma concentration [Australian].
- **Live Vaccines:** Should not be given concurrently with infliximab [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Infliximab crosses the placenta and has been detected in infants up to 12 months after birth. It is not recommended during pregnancy unless clearly necessary. Infants exposed *in utero* should not receive live vaccines for 12 months after birth [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Infliximab is excreted in human milk at low levels (up to 5% of maternal serum levels). Consideration should be given to the benefit of breastfeeding for the infant and the benefit of therapy for the mother [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal studies [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for Crohn's disease and Ulcerative Colitis (ages 6–17 years). Not recommended for children <6 years or for other indications [Australian]. Paediatric patients reported higher rates of some adverse events (e.g., anaemia, blood in stool, neutropaenia) compared to adults [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher incidence of serious infections in patients ≥65 years. Exercise extreme caution [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied; exercise caution due to potentially altered function in the elderly [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Infliximab is a chimeric monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity to both soluble and transmembrane forms of TNFα (but not TNFβ). It neutralises the biological activity of TNFα, a key pro-inflammatory cytokine [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Distribution:** Predominantly distributed within the vascular compartment (Vss: 3.0 to 4.1 litres) [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 8 to 9.5 days [Australian].
    - **Pharmacodynamics:** Treatment reduces infiltration of inflammatory cells and reduces levels of serum IL-6 and C-reactive protein (CRP) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PLANET RA:** A Phase III trial in 606 patients with rheumatoid arthritis demonstrated therapeutic equivalence between **Inflectra** and **Remicade** (ACR20 response at Week 30: 60.9% vs. 58.6%) [Australian].
- **PLANET AS:** A Phase I trial in 250 patients with ankylosing spondylitis demonstrated pharmacokinetic bioequivalence and comparable efficacy/safety between **Inflectra** and **Remicade** [Australian].
- **REACH Trial:** Demonstrated efficacy in paediatric Crohn’s disease (clinical response at Week 10: 88.4%) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Refrigerate; do not freeze [Australian].
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Contains no preservative. Use as soon as practicable. Can be held at 2°C to 8°C for no more than 24 hours if necessary [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4** (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
