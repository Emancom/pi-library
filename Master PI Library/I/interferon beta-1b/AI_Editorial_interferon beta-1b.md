# Editorial Product Information: Interferon beta-1b

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from official regulatory documents provided by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It combines the clinical guidance and safety data from both jurisdictions to provide a comprehensive overview for healthcare professionals.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Betaferon [TGA] / Betaseron [FDA]
**Active Ingredient:** Interferon beta-1b

Interferon beta-1b is a purified, sterile, lyophilized protein product produced via recombinant DNA technology using a specific strain of *Escherichia coli*. The molecule consists of 165 amino acids with a molecular weight of approximately 18,500 daltons. It differs from natural human interferon beta in three specific ways:
1. Serine is substituted for cysteine at position 17.
2. It lacks the N-terminal methionine.
3. It is not glycosylated (lacks carbohydrate moieties).

The product is supplied in single-use vials containing 0.3 mg (9.6 million IU) of interferon beta-1b, formulated to deliver 0.25 mg (8 million IU) upon reconstitution with the provided diluent.

## 2. Approved Indications
Interferon beta-1b is indicated for the management of patients with relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis (MS). Specific clinical indications include:

*   **Clinically Isolated Syndrome (CIS):** Treatment of patients experiencing a single clinical event suggestive of MS who possess at least two clinically silent MRI lesions characteristic of the disease, provided alternative diagnoses are excluded.
*   **Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis (RRMS):** To reduce the frequency and severity of clinical relapses in ambulatory patients. The TGA specifically notes its use in patients characterized by at least two attacks of neurologic dysfunction within a two-year period [TGA].
*   **Secondary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis (SPMS):** For the reduction of relapse frequency and to slow the progression of physical disability in patients with active disease.

## 3. Contraindications
Therapy is contraindicated in patients with a known history of hypersensitivity to:
*   Natural or recombinant interferon beta.
*   Human albumin.
*   Mannitol.
*   Any other component of the formulation.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Hepatic Injury
Asymptomatic elevations in serum transaminases are common. However, severe hepatic injury, including rare cases of hepatic failure and autoimmune hepatitis, has been reported [FDA]. Liver function tests (ALT, AST, and Gamma-GT) should be monitored at one, three, and six months post-initiation, and periodically thereafter. Caution is advised when used alongside other hepatotoxic substances, including alcohol.

### Depression and Suicide
Patients should be warned of the risk of depression and suicidal ideation. Treatment should be administered with caution to those with a history of depressive disorders. If a patient develops significant depression, cessation of therapy should be considered.

### Cardiovascular Disorders
Caution is required in patients with pre-existing significant cardiac disease, such as congestive heart failure (CHF), coronary artery disease, or arrhythmias. While the drug has no known direct cardiac toxicity, the "flu-like" symptoms (fever, chills, tachycardia) associated with initiation can exert significant stress on the cardiovascular system. Rare cases of cardiomyopathy have been reported; therapy should be discontinued if a causal link is suspected.

### Injection Site Reactions and Necrosis
Injection site reactions occur in a majority of patients. Injection site necrosis (ISN) has been reported in approximately 4% of patients, typically within the first four months [FDA]. Necrosis may involve muscle fascia and fat, potentially requiring debridement or skin grafting. Patients must use aseptic techniques and rotate injection sites regularly.

### Hematologic Disorders
Leukopenia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia are known risks. Complete blood counts (CBC), including differential white cell and platelet counts, should be monitored regularly.

### Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA)
Cases of TMA, including fatal outcomes, have been reported weeks to years after starting treatment. Clinicians should monitor for new-onset hypertension, impaired renal function, and thrombocytopenia.

### Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH)
PAH has been reported with interferon beta products. It can develop at various time points, even several years after starting therapy. Treatment should be discontinued if PAH is confirmed.

### Other Rare Conditions
*   **Capillary Leak Syndrome:** Associated with pre-existing monoclonal gammopathy; can be fatal [TGA].
*   **Seizures:** Caution should be exercised in patients with a history of seizures.
*   **Drug-induced Lupus Erythematosus:** Therapy should be stopped if new characteristic signs/symptoms and positive serologic testing occur [FDA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Recommended Dose
The standard dose is **0.25 mg (8 million IU)** injected subcutaneously every other day.

### Dose Titration
To improve tolerability and reduce the incidence of flu-like symptoms, a six-week titration schedule is recommended at the start of therapy:
*   **Weeks 1–2:** 0.0625 mg (0.25 mL)
*   **Weeks 3–4:** 0.125 mg (0.5 mL)
*   **Weeks 5–6:** 0.1875 mg (0.75 mL)
*   **Week 7+:** 0.25 mg (1.0 mL)

### Reconstitution
The lyophilized powder must be reconstituted with 1.2 mL of the supplied diluent (0.54% sodium chloride). The vial should be swirled gently to dissolve; do not shake, as this can cause foaming. The resulting solution should be clear and colorless to light yellow.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequently reported adverse reactions include:
*   **Injection site reactions:** Redness, swelling, pain, and inflammation (reported in up to 78–85% of patients).
*   **Flu-like symptom complex:** Fever, chills, myalgia, malaise, and sweating. These symptoms generally decrease in frequency over time.
*   **Laboratory abnormalities:** Lymphopenia, neutropenia, and elevated liver enzymes (ALT/AST).
*   **Nervous system:** Headache, insomnia, hypertonia, and incoordination.
*   **Other:** Abdominal pain, rash, and asthenia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
No formal clinical drug interaction studies have been conducted. Interferon beta-1b may reduce the activity of hepatic cytochrome P450-dependent enzymes; therefore, caution is advised when co-administering drugs with a narrow therapeutic index that rely on this system for clearance. 

Corticosteroids or ACTH used for treating relapses are well-tolerated when used concurrently for up to 28 days.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**TGA Pregnancy Category D.** Data from animal studies (rhesus monkeys) indicate that interferon beta-1b is not teratogenic but does possess significant abortifacient activity at doses 0.03 to 1 times the human dose (based on body surface area) [FDA]. It is not known if the drug causes fetal harm in humans, though large observational studies have not generally indicated a drug-associated risk of major birth defects. Discontinuation of therapy should be considered if a patient becomes pregnant.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown whether interferon beta-1b is excreted in human milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, a decision must be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, weighing the maternal benefit against the risks to the child.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
Following a subcutaneous dose of 0.25 mg, serum levels are often low or undetectable. In healthy volunteers receiving higher doses (0.5 mg), peak serum concentrations occurred between 1 and 8 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life ranges from 8 minutes to 4.3 hours. Bioavailability is approximately 50%. There is no evidence of drug accumulation in the serum following every-other-day dosing.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Interferon beta-1b belongs to the family of cytokines. While its exact mechanism in MS is not fully elucidated, it is known to interact with specific cell surface receptors to induce the expression of various gene products (e.g., neopterin, β2-microglobulin). Its biological effects include:
*   Enhancement of suppressor T-cell activity.
*   Reduction of pro-inflammatory cytokine production.
*   Down-regulation of antigen presentation.
*   Inhibition of lymphocyte trafficking into the central nervous system.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Vials:** Store at room temperature (2°C to 30°C). Do not freeze.
*   **Reconstituted Solution:** If not used immediately, the solution should be refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) and must be used within 3 hours. It contains no preservatives.
