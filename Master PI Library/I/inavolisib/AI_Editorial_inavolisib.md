# Editorial Product Information: Inavolisib

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by integrating clinical and regulatory data from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). This document is intended for healthcare professionals and provides a consolidated overview of the safety and efficacy profile of inavolisib. Where regional guidance differs, the more specific or conservative recommendation is provided with the source agency noted in brackets.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** ITOVEBI  
**Active Ingredient:** Inavolisib  

Inavolisib is a kinase inhibitor available as film-coated tablets in 3 mg and 9 mg strengths. The 3 mg tablets are red and round; the 9 mg tablets are pink and oval-shaped.

## 2. Approved Indications
Inavolisib is indicated for use in combination with palbociclib and fulvestrant for the treatment of adult patients who meet the following criteria:
*   Presence of one or more *PIK3CA* mutations, as detected by a validated assay [TGA] or an FDA-approved test [FDA].
*   Hormone receptor (HR)-positive and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative breast cancer.
*   Disease is locally advanced or metastatic.
*   Disease has recurred during or within 12 months of completing adjuvant endocrine therapy (endocrine-resistant).

## 3. Contraindications
Inavolisib is contraindicated in patients with a history of known hypersensitivity to the active ingredient or to any of the excipients, including lactose [TGA]. 

*Note: The FDA labeling identifies no specific contraindications.*

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Hyperglycemia
Severe and potentially fatal hyperglycemia, including ketoacidosis, has been reported. Inavolisib should not be initiated in patients with uncontrolled blood glucose levels. 
*   **Monitoring:** Test fasting plasma glucose (FPG) and HbA1c prior to treatment. Optimize glucose levels before starting therapy. 
*   **Ongoing Surveillance:** Monitor FPG every three days for the first week, then weekly for the next three weeks, then every two to four weeks thereafter. HbA1c should be checked every three months.
*   **Management:** Prophylaxis with metformin should be considered for patients with risk factors (e.g., BMI ≥ 30, HbA1c ≥ 5.7%, or history of gestational diabetes).

### Stomatitis
Inflammation of the oral mucosa is common. The use of alcohol-free corticosteroid mouthwash is recommended for prophylaxis or at the first sign of symptoms. Patients should avoid mouthwashes containing peroxide or alcohol.

### Diarrhea
Diarrhea may be severe, leading to dehydration and secondary acute kidney injury. Patients should be advised to begin anti-diarrheal therapy and increase fluid intake at the first sign of loose stools.

### Embryofetal Toxicity
Based on animal data, inavolisib can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status in females of reproductive potential before starting treatment.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
The recommended dose is **9 mg orally once daily**, taken with or without food. Tablets must be swallowed whole and should not be crushed, chewed, or split. 

Inavolisib is administered in combination with:
*   **Palbociclib:** 125 mg daily for 21 days, followed by 7 days off.
*   **Fulvestrant:** 500 mg intramuscularly on Days 1 and 15 of Cycle 1, and Day 1 of subsequent 28-day cycles.
*   **LHRH Agonists:** Pre/perimenopausal women and men should receive a luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone agonist according to local clinical practice.

### Missed or Vomited Doses
*   **Missed Dose:** If within 9 hours of the usual time, the dose should be taken immediately. If more than 9 hours have passed, the dose should be skipped.
*   **Vomiting:** If vomiting occurs after administration, do not take an additional dose; resume the regular schedule the following day.

### Renal Impairment
*   **Mild (eGFR 60 to < 90 mL/min):** No dose adjustment.
*   **Moderate (eGFR 30 to < 60 mL/min):** Reduce starting dose to 6 mg daily.
*   **Severe (eGFR < 30 mL/min):** Reduce starting dose to 3 mg daily [FDA]. Safety and efficacy have not been established in this population [TGA].

### Hepatic Impairment
No adjustment is required for mild impairment. Inavolisib has not been studied in patients with moderate to severe hepatic impairment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The safety profile is based on the INAVO120 study. The most frequently reported adverse reactions (≥ 20%) include:
*   **Laboratory Abnormalities:** Decreased neutrophils, decreased hemoglobin, increased fasting glucose, decreased platelets, decreased lymphocytes, decreased calcium, decreased potassium, increased ALT, increased creatinine, and decreased sodium.
*   **Clinical Symptoms:** Stomatitis, diarrhea, fatigue, nausea, rash, decreased appetite, and headache.

Fatal adverse reactions occurred in 3.7% of patients, including COVID-19 infection and gastrointestinal or cerebral hemorrhage.

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Effect of Other Drugs on Inavolisib
*   **CYP Enzymes:** Inavolisib is not a major substrate for CYP450 enzymes; therefore, CYP inhibitors or inducers are unlikely to affect its exposure.
*   **Acid-Reducing Agents:** Co-administration with proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) does not result in clinically significant changes in inavolisib exposure.
*   **Transporters:** Inavolisib is a substrate of P-gp and BCRP, but clinical interactions with inhibitors of these transporters are not expected to be significant.

### Effect of Inavolisib on Other Drugs
*   In vitro data suggest inavolisib may induce CYP3A4, CYP2B6, CYP2C8, CYP2C9, and CYP2C19. It is also a time-dependent inhibitor of CYP3A.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
Inavolisib is assigned to **Pregnancy Category D [TGA]**. It is not recommended for use during pregnancy. Female patients should use effective **non-hormonal** contraception during treatment and for **2 weeks [TGA]** or **1 week [FDA]** after the final dose. Male patients with female partners of reproductive potential should use condoms for the same duration.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if inavolisib is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for **1 week** following the last dose.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 76%. Median Tmax is 3 hours. Steady state is generally reached by Day 5.
*   **Distribution:** Plasma protein binding is approximately 37%. The apparent volume of distribution is 155 L.
*   **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized via hydrolysis; minimal metabolism by CYP3A.
*   **Elimination:** The mean elimination half-life is approximately 15 to 16.4 hours. Clearance is roughly 8.8 L/hr. Excretion is balanced between urine (49%) and feces (48%).

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Inavolisib is a highly potent inhibitor of the PI3K alpha isoform (p110α). It features a dual mechanism:
1.  **Kinase Inhibition:** It blocks the PI3K signaling pathway, which is frequently hyperactive in *PIK3CA*-mutated breast cancers.
2.  **Degradation:** It promotes the specific degradation of the mutated p110α protein.
This combined action reduces cellular proliferation and triggers apoptosis in cancer cells.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **FDA Recommendation:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F), with excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
*   **TGA Recommendation:** Store below 30°C.
*   **General Instructions:** Keep the medication in its original container to protect the tablets from moisture. Ensure the bottle remains tightly closed.
