# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine clinical information for use by healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis of available data and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Prescribing Information required for clinical practice in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection (solution), 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Initial Management:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours and never less frequent than every 6 hours [US].
- **Titration:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain around 50 mg/kg but may be adjusted based on clinical response and plasma carnitine levels. The maximum reported dose is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40 to 50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levels [US].

### General Administration Notes
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions manifest within minutes of intravenous administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor the patient if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Context):** While this editorial concerns the injection, note that safety for high-dose oral levocarnitine has not been established in renal failure; chronic oral use in ESRD may lead to accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].
- **Monitoring:** Pre-therapy plasma carnitine levels should be obtained. Regular monitoring (weekly/monthly) of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect (Frequency ≥5% in ESRD Trials) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, abdominal pain, asthenia |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, anorexia |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Nervous System** | Dizziness, depression, paresthesia |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis |

- **Serious/Notable Effects:**
    - **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent: body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dosage adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category C (implied)]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows suggest increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefits. Consideration may be given to discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg), which applies to paediatric patients [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Oral formulations should be avoided in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Levocarnitine clears acyl-CoA compounds, preventing autointoxication and life-threatening acidosis in patients with fatty acid metabolism defects [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly post-dialysis), mean pre-dialysis levocarnitine levels rose from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses 3x weekly showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only).
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
