# Editorial Product Information: Icatibant

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- Information from other major regulatory authorities (e.g., US FDA, European EMA) was not provided for this specific synthesis; therefore, clinical recommendations primarily reflect Australian regulatory approvals and guidelines.
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Icatibant (as icatibant acetate).
- **Chemical Class:** Synthetic decapeptide; selective competitive bradykinin type 2 (B2) receptor antagonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** Cipla Icatibant [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B06AC02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Solution for injection in a single-use pre-filled syringe. Each 3 mL syringe contains 30 mg of icatibant (10 mg/mL).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous (SC) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Cipla Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hereditary Angioedema (HAE):** Symptomatic treatment of acute attacks of HAE in adults, adolescents, and children aged 2 years and older with C1-esterase-inhibitor deficiency [Australian-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Dosing
- **Recommended Dose:** A single 30 mg subcutaneous injection.
- **Repeat Dosing:** In cases of insufficient relief or symptom recurrence, a second 30 mg injection may be administered after 6 hours. A third dose may be given after a further 6 hours if necessary.
- **Maximum Dose:** No more than 3 injections (90 mg) should be administered in a 24-hour period.
- **Monthly Limit:** In clinical trials, no more than 8 injections per month were administered.

### Paediatric Dosing (2 to 17 years)
Dosing is weight-based. Only one dose per HAE attack has been studied in this population.
| Body Weight | Dose (Injection Volume) |
| :--- | :--- |
| 12 kg to 25 kg | 10 mg (1.0 mL) |
| 26 kg to 40 kg | 15 mg (1.5 mL) |
| 41 kg to 50 kg | 20 mg (2.0 mL) |
| 51 kg to 65 kg | 25 mg (2.5 mL) |
| >65 kg | 30 mg (3.0 mL) |

### Administration Instructions
- **Site:** Subcutaneous injection preferably in the abdominal area.
- **Rate:** Inject slowly due to the volume (3 mL).
- **Self-Administration:** Adults may self-administer after proper training. For children (2-17 years), administration should be performed by a healthcare professional or a trained caregiver.
- **Laryngeal Attacks:** Patients with symptoms involving the face, lips, or pharyngolaryngeal area must seek urgent medical attention immediately after self-injection and be managed in a medical institution.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance (icatibant) or any of the excipients (glacial acetic acid, sodium hydroxide, sodium chloride).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Ischaemic Heart Disease:** Icatibant may aggravate cardiac ischaemia. Non-clinical models suggest that B2 receptor antagonism can deteriorate cardiac function and decrease coronary blood flow under ischaemic conditions. Exercise caution in patients with acute ischaemic heart disease or unstable angina pectoris.
- **Stroke:** There is a theoretical risk that icatibant may attenuate the late-phase neuroprotective effects of bradykinin. Caution is advised when administering in the weeks following a stroke.
- **Laryngeal Symptoms:** Because laryngeal attacks can be life-threatening, patients must be monitored in an appropriate medical facility until deemed safe for discharge.
- **Excipients:** Contains less than 1 mmol (23 mg) of sodium per 3 mL, making it essentially "sodium-free."

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Injection Site Reactions
Almost all patients (up to 97%) experience injection site reactions, including erythema, swelling, burning, itching, and cutaneous pain. These are typically mild to moderate and transient.

### Systemic Adverse Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reactions |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Nausea, pyrexia, dizziness, headache, rash, pruritus, erythema, increased transaminases, increased blood creatinine phosphokinase |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Vomiting, asthenia, fatigue, herpes zoster, pharyngitis, weight increase, hyperglycaemia, muscle spasm, proteinuria, asthma, nasal congestion |
| **Unknown** | Urticaria (post-marketing) |

- **Immunogenicity:** Rare cases of transient positivity for anti-icatibant antibodies were observed in trials; however, no loss of efficacy or anaphylactic reactions were reported.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **ACE Inhibitors:** There is a theoretical risk that icatibant may antagonise the antihypertensive effects of ACE inhibitors. Furthermore, ACE inhibitors are generally avoided in HAE patients as they can induce/exacerbate attacks.
- **CYP450 System:** Icatibant is not an inhibitor or inducer of major CYP450 isoenzymes (1A2, 2A6, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, 3A4). Pharmacokinetic interactions are not expected.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C [Australian]**. There are no clinical data on exposed pregnancies. Animal studies show pre- and post-implantation loss and effects on parturition at high exposures. Icatibant should only be used if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus (e.g., life-threatening laryngeal attacks).
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if icatibant is excreted in human milk. It is recommended that breastfeeding be withheld and milk expressed and discarded for **12 hours** following treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** High-dose animal studies showed reversible effects on sexual maturation and reproductive organs. A small human study using a high-intensity dosing regimen showed no significant changes in reproductive hormones or sperm parameters.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for children $\ge$ 2 years and $\ge$ 12 kg. Safety and efficacy not established in children < 2 years or < 12 kg.
- **Elderly Use (>65 years):** Increased systemic exposure (50–60% higher) has been observed in elderly patients (75–80 years). The clinical significance is unknown; however, no specific dose adjustment is currently recommended.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required based on limited data.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dosage adjustment required across a wide range of hepatic insufficiency.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Icatibant is a selective competitive antagonist at the bradykinin B2 receptor. By blocking the binding of bradykinin (the primary mediator of HAE symptoms), it prevents further vasodilation and oedema formation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is 97%; $T_{max}$ is ~0.5 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution ($V_{ss}$) is 20–25 L. Protein binding is 44%.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by proteolytic enzymes to inactive metabolites.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal excretion of metabolites (<10% unchanged drug). Terminal half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is 1–2 hours. Clearance is 15–20 L/h.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **FAST-1 and FAST-2 (Adults):** Pivotal Phase III trials demonstrated that icatibant significantly reduced the median time to onset of symptom relief. In FAST-2 (tranexamic acid comparator), relief began at 2.0 hours vs. 12.0 hours. In FAST-1 (placebo comparator), relief began at 2.5 hours vs. 4.6 hours.
- **HGT-FIR-086 (Paediatric):** An open-label study of 32 paediatric patients (ages 2–17) showed a median time to symptom relief of 1.0 hour. The safety profile was consistent with that seen in adults.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Each syringe is for single use only; any residue must be discarded.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Subject to additional monitoring by the TGA in Australia.
