# Editorial Product Information: Ibrutinib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs). 
- **Important Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and does not replace the official PI or the legal requirements for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority’s current approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ibrutinib, a small-molecule inhibitor of Bruton’s tyrosine kinase (BTK).
- **Chemical Class:** Protein kinase inhibitor (Antineoplastic agent).
- **Brand Name:** **IMBRUVICA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01XE27.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Hard Capsules:** 140 mg.
    - **Film-coated Tablets:** 140 mg, 280 mg, 420 mg, and 560 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Janssen-Cilag Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Mantle Cell Lymphoma (MCL):** Treatment of patients who have received at least one prior therapy [Australian].
- **Waldenström’s Macroglobulinemia (WM):** 
    - As a single agent for adult patients who have received at least one prior therapy, or as first-line treatment for those unsuitable for chemo-immunotherapy.
    - In combination with rituximab for adult patients [Australian].
- **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL) / Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma (SLL):**
    - As a single agent or in combination with rituximab, obinutuzumab, or venetoclax for previously untreated patients.
    - As a single agent or in combination with bendamustine and rituximab (BR) for patients who have received at least one prior therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **MCL:** 560 mg once daily.
- **CLL/SLL and WM:** 420 mg once daily.
- **Administration:** Administer orally at approximately the same time each day with a glass of water. Capsules or tablets must be swallowed whole; do not open, break, or chew. Can be taken with or without food.
- **Dietary Restrictions:** Avoid grapefruit juice and Seville oranges during treatment due to potential increases in ibrutinib exposure.

### Dosing with Venetoclax (CLL/SLL)
- Ibrutinib is administered as a single agent for the first 3 cycles (28-day cycles), followed by 12 cycles of ibrutinib in combination with venetoclax [Australian].

### Dose Modifications for Toxicity
Therapy should be withheld for Grade 2 or higher cardiac failure, Grade 3 or higher non-haematological toxicities, or Grade 4 haematological toxicities. Once recovered to Grade 1 or baseline, resume at the following reduced tiers:
- **MCL Tiered Reductions:** 560 mg (starting) $\rightarrow$ 420 mg $\rightarrow$ 280 mg $\rightarrow$ Discontinue.
- **CLL/SLL/WM Tiered Reductions:** 420 mg (starting) $\rightarrow$ 280 mg $\rightarrow$ 140 mg $\rightarrow$ Discontinue.

### Missed Dose
- If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible on the same day. Resume the normal schedule the next day. Do not take extra doses to compensate.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to ibrutinib or any excipients in the formulation (e.g., anaphylactic reactions).
- Concomitant use of preparations containing **St. John’s Wort** [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Bleeding Events:** Major and minor haemorrhages (some fatal) have occurred. Risk increases with concomitant antiplatelet or anticoagulant use. Withhold ibrutinib 3–7 days pre- and post-surgery depending on bleeding risk.
- **Cardiac Events:** Fatal and serious arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation, atrial flutter, ventricular tachyarrhythmia) and cardiac failure have been reported. Monitor for clinical deterioration.
- **Infections:** Increased risk of sepsis, bacterial, viral (including Hepatitis E and HBV reactivation), and fungal infections. Monitor for fever and neutropenia.
- **Cytopenias:** Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and anaemia require monthly monitoring of CBC.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Drug-induced liver injury (DILI) and hepatic failure have been reported. Assess LFTs before and during treatment.
- **Other Precautions:** 
    - **Leukostasis:** Risk if circulating lymphocytes > 400 x 10⁹/L.
    - **PML:** Monitor for new or worsening neurological/cognitive symptoms.
    - **Hypertension:** New-onset or worsening hypertension occurs commonly; regular monitoring required.
    - **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Monitor patients with high tumour burden.
    - **Secondary Malignancies:** Increased incidence of non-melanoma skin cancer.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are grouped by frequency based on clinical trial data (N=1981):

| Frequency | Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Diarrhoea, musculoskeletal pain, haemorrhage/bruising, rash, nausea, neutropenia, arthralgia, upper respiratory tract infection, thrombocytopenia, headache, dizziness, pyrexia, peripheral oedema. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Sepsis, UTI, non-melanoma skin cancer, febrile neutropenia, lymphocytosis, tumour lysis syndrome, hyponatraemia, hypertension, blurred vision, atrial fibrillation, stomatitis, vomiting, constipation. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Leukostasis syndrome, angioedema, interstitial lung disease (ILD), hepatic failure, peripheral neuropathy, eye haemorrhage. |
| **Rare (<0.1%) / Post-marketing** | Splenic rupture (post-discontinuation), Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), neutrophilic dermatoses, HLH. |

- **Serious ADRs:** 6% of patients discontinued treatment due to ADRs, primarily pneumonia, atrial fibrillation, and haemorrhage [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** 
    - **Strong Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin):** Avoid if possible. If required for short-term use (≤7 days), withhold ibrutinib. If required long-term, reduce ibrutinib dose to 140 mg.
    - **Moderate Inhibitors (e.g., erythromycin, diltiazem, fluconazole):** Reduce ibrutinib dose to 280 mg (CLL/SLL/WM) or 140 mg (MCL) [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Avoid strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin) as they can decrease ibrutinib exposure by ~90%.
- **P-gp and BCRP Substrates:** Ibrutinib may increase concentrations of substrates with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., digoxin, methotrexate). Space administration by at least 6 hours.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Animal studies demonstrate foetal harm (malformations and post-implantation loss). Ibrutinib should not be used during pregnancy. 
    - **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use highly effective contraception during and for 3 months after treatment. Men should not father a child during this same period.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if ibrutinib is excreted in human milk. Breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment.
- **Fertility:** No effects observed in animal studies, but human data are unavailable.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in patients ≤ 18 years [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment required (age ≥ 65 years). Note that Grade 3+ pneumonia and thrombocytopenia occur more frequently in the elderly.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild-to-moderate impairment (CrCl > 30 mL/min). No data available for severe impairment or dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Child-Pugh Class A (Mild):** 280 mg daily.
    - **Child-Pugh Class B (Moderate):** 140 mg daily.
    - **Child-Pugh Class C (Severe):** Not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Forms a covalent bond with cysteine residue (Cys 481) in the BTK active site, leading to sustained inhibition of B-cell receptor signalling, trafficking, and adhesion.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed ($T_{max}$ 1–2 hours). Bioavailability is low (2.9%) but doubles with food.
    - **Distribution:** High plasma protein binding (97.3%). $V_d$ is ~10,000 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A4 to a dihydrodiol metabolite (15x less potent).
    - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (80%); terminal half-life is 4–6 hours.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** May cause a reversible increase in lymphocyte counts (lymphocytosis) shortly after initiation, which should not be confused with disease progression.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **MCL (PCYC-1104-CA):** Single-arm study showed an ORR of 67.6% and median DOR of 17.5 months.
- **CLL/SLL (RESONATE-2):** Phase 3 study vs chlorambucil showed an 84% reduction in risk of progression/death (HR 0.16).
- **WM (iNNOVATE):** Ibrutinib + rituximab showed a 92% ORR compared to 47% in the placebo + rituximab arm [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Do not open or crush tablets/capsules.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (S4)** - Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
