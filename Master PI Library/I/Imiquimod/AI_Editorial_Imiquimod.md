# Editorial Product Information: Imiquimod

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**. 
- At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI was provided for review. Consequently, information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) is marked as "Not specified in available sources" unless common clinical knowledge regarding the international profile is relevant to the provided text.
- **Purpose:** This document serves as a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Imiquimod (1-(2-methylpropyl)-1H-imidazo [4,5-c]quinolin-4-amine).
- **Chemical Class:** Immune response modifier; imidazoquinoline.
- **Brand Name(s):** Aldara [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** D06BB10.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 5% (50 mg/g) cream provided in 250 mg single-use sachets or a multi-dose pump [Australian]. 
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** iNova Pharmaceuticals (Australia) Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Actinic Keratosis (AK):** Treatment of solar (actinic) keratosis on the face and scalp [Australian].
- **Superficial Basal Cell Carcinoma (sBCC):** Primary treatment of confirmed sBCC where surgical intervention is deemed inappropriate [Australian].
- **External Genital/Perianal Warts:** Treatment of external genital and perianal warts (*condyloma acuminata*) in adults [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Instructions
Patients should wash the treatment area with mild soap and water and dry thoroughly before application. The cream should be applied prior to sleep and left on the skin for approximately 8 hours (range 6–10 hours). During this period, showering or bathing should be avoided. Following the treatment period, the cream must be removed with mild soap and water. Hand washing before and after application is required [Australian].

### Actinic Keratosis (AK)
The treatment area must not exceed 25 cm². Two regimens are approved [Australian]:
- **Cyclical:** Apply 3 times per week for 4 weeks. After a 4-week treatment-free period, assess clearance. If lesions persist, repeat for one additional 4-week course.
- **Continuous:** Apply 3 times per week for up to 16 weeks.

### Superficial Basal Cell Carcinoma (sBCC)
- **Dosing:** Apply once daily for 5 consecutive days per week for a total of 6 weeks.
- **Administration:** The cream should cover the lesion and include a 1 cm margin of surrounding skin. Clinical assessment of the response should occur 6–12 weeks after treatment completion [Australian].

### External Genital/Perianal Warts
- **Dosing:** Apply 3 times per week (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri). 
- **Duration:** Continue until total clearance is achieved or for a maximum of 16 weeks. 
- **Note:** Do not occlude the area. Use a thin layer; excessive cream should be avoided [Australian].

### Missed Doses
If a dose is missed, the patient should apply the cream as soon as they remember and then continue the regular schedule. However, the cream should not be applied more than once daily. For AK and sBCC, treatment periods should not be extended to make up for missed doses or rest periods [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to imiquimod or any excipients in the formulation (e.g., methyl hydroxybenzoate, propyl hydroxybenzoate, cetyl alcohol, stearyl alcohol) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Local Skin Reactions (LSRs):** Erythema, erosion, excoriation, and oedema are common and represent the pharmacological activity of the drug. If severe, a "rest period" of several days may be taken. Treatment can resume once the reaction subsides [Australian].
- **Systemic Reactions:** Rarely, intense local inflammatory reactions may be accompanied by flu-like symptoms (malaise, pyrexia, nausea, myalgias). Dosing interruption should be considered [Australian].
- **UV Exposure:** Imiquimod may heighten sunburn susceptibility. Patients should minimise or avoid exposure to natural or artificial sunlight (sunlamps) and use protective clothing [Australian].
- **Anatomic Restrictions:** Not recommended for use on internal tissues (urethral, intravaginal, cervical, rectal, or intra-anal) due to potential for systemic absorption and local intolerance. Avoid contact with eyes, lips, and nostrils [Australian].
- **Specific Pathologies:** 
    - Not recommended for AK with marked hyperkeratosis (e.g., cutaneous horns) [Australian].
    - Not recommended for BCC subtypes other than the superficial variant [Australian].
- **Immunocompromised Patients:** Safety and efficacy have not been established; use is generally not recommended in this population [Australian].
- **Autoimmune/Transplant Patients:** Use with caution as imiquimod may exacerbate inflammatory skin conditions or autoimmune diseases and may theoretically risk organ rejection or graft-versus-host disease [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are primarily local, reflecting the drug's mechanism as an immune response modifier.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Application site pruritus, burning, and erythema [Australian]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Application site pain, tenderness, irritation, scabbing; headache, nausea, myalgia, and fatigue [Australian]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Lymphadenopathy, diarrhoea, vomiting, flu-like symptoms, dizziness, and insomnia [Australian]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Rare reports of permanent pigmentation changes (hypopigmentation or hyperpigmentation) and serious skin reactions such as Erythema Multiforme, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, and Cutaneous Lupus Erythematosus [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Minimal systemic absorption occurs (<1%); therefore, drug-drug interactions are unlikely. In vitro studies show imiquimod protein binding (90–95%) is not affected by common medications like paracetamol, ibuprofen, or warfarin [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Due to its immunostimulating properties, use caution in patients receiving immunosuppressive medications [Australian].
- **Barrier Contraception:** Imiquimod may weaken condoms and vaginal diaphragms; concurrent use is not recommended [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** Animal studies showed no teratogenicity, but reduced pup weight was noted at maternally toxic doses. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Imiquimod is not recommended for use during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if topically applied imiquimod is excreted in human milk. Use is not recommended during lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of reproduction was seen in rat studies at doses up to 8 times the human equivalent [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients under 18 years have not been established; use is not recommended. It should not be used for *molluscum contagiosum* [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between patients >65 years and younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some individuals cannot be ruled out [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources; however, low systemic absorption suggests dose adjustment may not be required.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Imiquimod is an immune response modifier with no direct antiviral or antitumour activity. It acts as an agonist for Toll-like receptor 7 (TLR7), stimulating innate and adaptive immunity. This leads to the induction of cytokines, including interferon-alpha (IFN-α), tumour necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), and various interleukins [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Minimal (<1%) through the skin.
    - **Distribution:** Protein binding is 90–95%. 
    - **Elimination:** Following minimal absorption, it is primarily excreted in urine and faeces [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases markers for IFN-γ and induces cellular infiltration (T-cells, dendritic cells, macrophages) into lesions, leading to tumour or viral-infected cell destruction [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Actinic Keratosis:** In cyclical 4-week trials, the complete clearance rate was 54.4% for imiquimod vs 8.3% for vehicle. One-year recurrence rates for cleared patients were approximately 27% [Australian].
- **Superficial BCC:** 5x/week treatment for 6 weeks resulted in a histological clearance rate of 82% compared to 3% for vehicle. Higher clearance was associated with moderate-to-severe local skin reactions [Australian].
- **Genital Warts:** In a double-blind trial, total wart clearance was achieved in 50% of imiquimod patients vs 11% for vehicle. Females showed higher clearance rates (72%) compared to males (33%) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Sachets:** Store below 25°C. Do not freeze. Single-use only; discard any remaining cream after opening [Australian].
- **Pump:** Store below 25°C. Do not freeze. Discard 4 weeks after opening [Australian].
