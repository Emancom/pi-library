# Editorial Product Information: Istradefylline

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial synthesis is based on Product Information (PI) provided by the United States (FDA).
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of istradefylline for healthcare professionals. It synthesizes clinical data, dosing recommendations, and safety information.
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information when prescribing in a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Istradefylline (a xanthine derivative).
*   **Chemical Class:** Adenosine receptor antagonist.
*   **Brand Names:** **NOURIANZ** [US].
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **Film-coated tablets:** 20 mg (peach-colored, pillow-shaped) and 40 mg (peach-colored, almond-shaped) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Oral.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Kyowa Kirin, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Parkinson’s Disease:** Indicated as adjunctive treatment to levodopa/carbidopa in adult patients experiencing "off" episodes [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
*   **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended starting dosage is 20 mg orally once daily. Based on clinical response and tolerability, the dose may be increased to a maximum of 40 mg once daily [US].
*   **Administration Instructions:** Tablets may be taken with or without food.
*   **Titration:** Initial dose titration is not mandatory [US].
*   **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.
*   **Dose Adjustments for Tobacco Use:**
    *   In patients smoking 20 or more cigarettes per day (or equivalent tobacco use), the recommended dosage is 40 mg once daily due to decreased systemic exposure [US].
*   **Dose Adjustments for Drug Interactions:**
    *   **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Maximum recommended dose is 20 mg once daily [US].
    *   **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers:** Use should be avoided [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   **None listed:** There are no known contraindications identified in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Dyskinesia:** Istradefylline may cause or exacerbate pre-existing dyskinesia. In clinical trials, the incidence of dyskinesia was higher in patients treated with 20 mg (15%) and 40 mg (17%) compared to placebo (8%). Monitor patients closely [US].
*   **Hallucinations and Psychotic Behavior:** There is a risk of new or worsening psychosis. Patients with major psychotic disorders should generally not be treated with istradefylline. Consider dose reduction or discontinuation if hallucinations or psychotic behaviors occur [US].
*   **Impulse Control and Compulsive Behaviors:** Patients may experience intense urges (e.g., gambling, sexual urges, spending money, binge eating). Prescribers should specifically ask patients or caregivers about the development of these behaviors. Dose reduction or cessation may be necessary [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are reported from pooled 12-week placebo-controlled trials [US].

| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | | | |
| Dyskinesia | 15% | 17% | 8% |
| **Common (1–10%)** | | | |
| Dizziness | 3% | 6% | 4% |
| Constipation | 5% | 6% | 3% |

*   **Serious Adverse Effects:** Primarily related to neuropsychiatric events including hallucinations and severe dyskinesia leading to treatment discontinuation [US].
*   **Postmarketing Experience:** Increased libido has been identified during post-approval use outside of the United States [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Strong CYP3A4 Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin):** Coadministration increases istradefylline exposure (AUC) by approximately 2.5-fold. Limit istradefylline dose to 20 mg [US].
*   **Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin, carbamazepine, St. John’s wort):** Coadministration significantly reduces istradefylline exposure (AUC decreased by ~81%). Avoid concomitant use [US].
*   **Effect on CYP3A4 Substrates:** Istradefylline 40 mg may increase exposure to CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., atorvastatin increased by 1.5-fold). Monitor for adverse reactions of the concomitant drug [US].
*   **P-glycoprotein (P-gp) Substrates:** Coadministration with P-gp substrates (e.g., digoxin) may increase substrate concentrations (digoxin AUC increased by 21%). Monitor for substrate toxicity [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** [US: No specific letter category]. Based on animal data (rat and rabbit), istradefylline may cause fetal harm, including teratogenicity and embryofetal mortality at clinically relevant exposures. Use is not recommended in pregnant women, and women of childbearing potential should use contraception during treatment [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** No data are available regarding the presence of istradefylline in human milk or its effects on the breastfed infant. In rats, the drug was present in milk at levels up to 10 times higher than maternal plasma. The benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother's clinical need for the drug [US].
*   **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed decreased fertility and increased preimplantation loss at high doses [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required based on age. In trials, 53% of patients were ≥65 years, and no overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed compared to younger patients [US].
*   **Renal Impairment:** 
    *   **Mild, Moderate, or Severe (CrCL 15-89 mL/min):** No dosage adjustment required.
    *   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Not studied; use with caution [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:**
    *   **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dosage adjustment required.
    *   **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Maximum recommended dose is 20 mg once daily.
    *   **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Avoid use; not studied in this population [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
*   **Mechanism of Action:** Istradefylline is an adenosine A2A receptor antagonist. The precise mechanism by which it exerts its therapeutic effect in Parkinson’s disease is unknown [US].
*   **Pharmacodynamics:** No clinically significant QTc prolongation was observed at doses up to 160 mg (4 times the maximum recommended dose) [US].
*   **Pharmacokinetics:**
    *   **Absorption:** Tmax is approximately 4 hours (fasted). A high-fat meal increases Cmax by 1.64-fold and AUC by 1.25-fold, though this is not considered clinically significant.
    *   **Distribution:** 98% plasma protein bound. Apparent volume of distribution (Vd/F) is ~557 L.
    *   **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP1A1 and CYP3A4.
    *   **Elimination:** Mean terminal half-life is approximately 83 hours. Elimination is via feces (48%) and urine (39%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **Trial Design:** Efficacy was established in four 12-week, randomized, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies. 
*   **Population:** Patients on stable levodopa/carbidopa with at least 2 hours of "off" time per day.
*   **Primary Outcome:** Change from baseline in daily awake "off" time.
*   **Results:** In all four studies, istradefylline (20 mg or 40 mg) demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in "off" time compared to placebo. Reductions in "off" time ranged from approximately 0.45 to 0.92 hours depending on the study and dose [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Storage:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F). Excursions are permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Classification: Rx-only (Prescription drug) [US].
