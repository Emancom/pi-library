**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from official regulatory documents provided by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It provides a comprehensive overview of insulin degludec, incorporating specific details for both the monocomponent formulation and the co-formulation with insulin aspart where applicable.

# 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Insulin degludec (available as a monocomponent or in a 70/30 co-formulation with insulin aspart).
**Active Ingredient:** Insulin degludec (produced by recombinant DNA technology in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*).

# 2. Approved Indications
Insulin degludec is indicated to improve glycemic control in adults and pediatric patients with diabetes mellitus. 
*   **Pediatric Age Limits:** Approved for use in patients aged 1 year and older [FDA], though some co-formulations are indicated from 6 years and older [TGA].
*   **Limitations:** This medication is not recommended for the treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis [FDA].

# 3. Contraindications
Insulin degludec is strictly contraindicated in the following circumstances:
*   During active episodes of hypoglycemia [FDA].
*   In patients with a known hypersensitivity to insulin degludec or any of the product’s excipients (such as glycerol, metacresol, phenol, or zinc acetate).

# 4. Warnings and Precautions
*   **Never Share Delivery Devices:** To prevent the transmission of blood-borne pathogens, insulin pens, syringes, and needles must never be shared between patients, even if the needle is replaced [FDA].
*   **Dosing Errors:** Patients must be educated to always check the insulin label before injection to avoid accidental mix-ups with other insulin products. The medication must never be transferred from a pre-filled pen into a syringe, as this can lead to severe overdosage [FDA].
*   **Hypoglycemia:** This is the most frequent adverse reaction. The risk increases with intensive glycemic control and may be delayed due to the long-acting nature of the drug. Awareness of symptoms may be reduced in patients with long-standing diabetes or those taking antiadrenergic drugs (e.g., beta-blockers).
*   **Hypokalemia:** All insulin products can cause a shift in potassium from the extracellular to the intracellular space. If untreated, this may lead to respiratory paralysis, ventricular arrhythmia, or death. Use with caution in patients at risk for low potassium levels [FDA].
*   **Regimen Changes:** Any change in insulin strength, manufacturer, or type should be conducted under strict medical supervision and may require an increase in blood glucose monitoring frequency.
*   **Fluid Retention and Heart Failure:** When used in combination with Thiazolidinediones (TZDs), patients should be observed for signs of heart failure. If cardiac function deteriorates, TZD dosage reduction or discontinuation should be considered [FDA].
*   **Injection Site Rotation:** To reduce the risk of lipodystrophy and localized cutaneous amyloidosis, patients must rotate injection sites within the same anatomical region. Injecting into affected areas may lead to hyperglycemia, while a sudden shift to an unaffected area may cause hypoglycemia [TGA].

# 5. Dosage and Administration
Insulin degludec is for subcutaneous administration only. It must not be administered intravenously or via an insulin infusion pump.
*   **Adult Dosing (Monocomponent):** Administered subcutaneously once daily at any time of day [FDA].
*   **Pediatric Dosing:** Administered once daily at the same time each day [FDA]. 
*   **Co-formulation Dosing (70/30):** Usually administered once or twice daily with the main meal(s) [TGA].
*   **Missed Doses:** If a dose is missed, adult patients should take it as soon as they remember, provided there is at least an 8-hour interval between injections [FDA]. For co-formulations, the missed dose can be taken with the next main meal [TGA].
*   **Initiation:** In insulin-naïve patients with Type 2 diabetes, the recommended starting dose is 10 units. For Type 1 diabetes, insulin degludec should typically be started at one-third to one-half of the total daily insulin requirement, with the remainder supplied by short-acting insulin [FDA].

# 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Hypoglycemia:** The most common side effect. Severe episodes may lead to unconsciousness, convulsions, or death.
*   **Common Clinical Findings:** Nasopharyngitis, headache, upper respiratory tract infections, and diarrhea [TGA].
*   **Injection Site Reactions:** May include hematoma, pain, hemorrhage, erythema, and nodules. These are typically mild and transitory.
*   **Metabolic/Dermatologic:** Lipodystrophy, peripheral edema, and weight gain. In clinical trials, average weight gain was 1.8 kg for Type 1 and 3.0 kg for Type 2 patients after 52 weeks [FDA].
*   **Immune System:** Rare cases of generalized hypersensitivity, including urticaria, swelling of the tongue, and itching [TGA].

# 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Increased Hypoglycemic Risk:** Substances that may reduce insulin requirements include oral antidiabetic drugs, ACE inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, MAOIs, salicylates, and sulfonamide antibiotics.
*   **Decreased Blood Glucose Lowering Effect:** Substances that may increase insulin requirements include atypical antipsychotics, corticosteroids, danazol, diuretics, estrogens/oral contraceptives, protease inhibitors, and thyroid hormones [FDA].
*   **Masking of Symptoms:** Beta-blockers and clonidine may blunt the physiological warning signs of hypoglycemia.
*   **Alcohol:** May either intensify or reduce the hypoglycemic effect of insulin.

# 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Classified as Pregnancy Category B3 [TGA]. Human data from randomized controlled trials have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage [FDA]. However, as animal studies have shown skeletal abnormalities in rats (likely secondary to maternal hypoglycemia), use during pregnancy should only be considered if the benefit outweighs the potential risk.
*   **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown whether insulin degludec is excreted in human milk. While it is present in rat milk at concentrations lower than in plasma, the developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the mother’s clinical need for the drug [FDA].

# 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Following subcutaneous injection, insulin degludec forms soluble, stable multi-hexamers, creating a depot in the subcutaneous tissue. Monomers gradually separate and are continuously absorbed into the circulation.
*   **Steady State:** Reached after 2 to 4 days of daily administration [FDA].
*   **Distribution:** Highly bound to albumin (>99%) in human plasma.
*   **Elimination:** The half-life is approximately 25 hours, independent of the dose.
*   **Special Populations:** No clinically relevant differences in pharmacokinetics were observed based on age, sex, race, or hepatic/renal impairment [TGA/FDA].

# 10. Mechanism of Action
Insulin degludec binds specifically to the human insulin receptor, stimulating peripheral glucose uptake by skeletal muscle and fat. It simultaneously inhibits hepatic glucose output. These actions result in a lowering of blood glucose. The unique molecular structure of degludec ensures a flat and stable glucose-lowering profile that lasts beyond 24 hours.

# 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Unopened (Refrigerated):** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze or store near the cooling element.
*   **In-Use (Opened):** May be kept at room temperature (up to 30°C) or refrigerated (2°C to 8°C).
*   **Discard Timeline:** 
    *   **Monocomponent:** Discard 56 days (8 weeks) after opening [FDA].
    *   **Co-formulation:** Discard 28 days (4 weeks) after opening [TGA].
*   **Light Protection:** Keep the pen cap on or store vials in the original carton to protect from light.
