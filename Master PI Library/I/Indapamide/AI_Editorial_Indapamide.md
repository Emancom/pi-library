# Editorial Product Information: Indapamide

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of indapamide product information for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data to support safe and effective prescribing and dispensing.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) legally approved for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Indapamide hemihydrate
- **Chemical Class:** Non-thiazide indole derivative of chlorosulfonamide
- **Brand Names:** APO-INDAPAMIDE SR [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C03BA11
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 1.5 mg modified-release (SR) film-coated tablets [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Indicated for the treatment of hypertension, either as a sole therapeutic agent or as an initial agent in multiple-drug regimens [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** One 1.5 mg modified-release tablet daily, preferably taken in the morning [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** Tablets must be swallowed whole and not chewed or crushed to maintain the modified-release profile [Australian].
- **Clinical Response:** The antihypertensive effect is progressive. Optimum reduction in blood pressure is typically observed after four weeks, with potential further reductions occurring over the following four to six weeks [Australian].
- **Dose Escalation:** Increasing the dose beyond 1.5 mg daily is not recommended. Higher doses do not provide significantly greater antihypertensive effects but do increase the risk of diuretic effects and electrolyte disturbances [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** May be combined with beta-blockers, methyldopa, clonidine, prazosin, and ACE inhibitors. Combination with other diuretics is not recommended due to the risk of severe electrolyte imbalances [Australian].
- **Discontinuation:** Indapamide exhibits a carry-over hypotensive effect lasting approximately 1 to 2 weeks after treatment cessation [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to indapamide, other sulfonamide derivatives, or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].
- Severe renal failure, anuria, or progressive/severe oliguria [Australian].
- Hepatic coma, hepatic encephalopathy, or severe impairment of liver function [Australian].
- Hypokalaemia [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Electrolyte Monitoring:** Patients must be monitored for hyponatraemia, hypochloraemia, and hypokalaemia. Plasma sodium should be measured before starting treatment and at regular intervals thereafter. Hyponatraemia may initially be asymptomatic but can lead to dehydration and orthostatic hypotension [Australian].
- **Hypokalaemia:** A major risk of thiazide-related diuretics. Frequent monitoring is required in high-risk groups (elderly, malnourished, cirrhotic patients, or those with coronary artery disease/heart failure). Hypokalaemia increases the risk of digitalis toxicity and potentially fatal *Torsades de pointes* [Australian].
- **Hepatic Encephalopathy:** In patients with impaired liver function, thiazide-related diuretics can trigger hepatic encephalopathy. Treatment must be stopped immediately if this occurs [Australian].
- **Ocular Effects:** Sulfonamide derivatives can cause an idiosyncratic reaction leading to choroidal effusion, acute myopia, and secondary angle-closure glaucoma. Symptoms (acute vision loss or ocular pain) typically occur within hours to weeks of initiation. Discontinue treatment immediately [Australian].
- **Hyperuricaemia and Gout:** May increase serum uric acid levels; use with caution in patients prone to gout attacks [Australian].
- **Photosensitivity:** Reactions have been reported; if they occur, treatment should be stopped. If re-administration is necessary, UV protection is recommended [Australian].
- **Lactose Intolerance:** Contains lactose monohydrate; not suitable for patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].
- **Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE):** Sulfonamide derivatives may exacerbate or activate SLE [Australian].
- **Athletes:** May produce a positive reaction in doping tests [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse effects are mild, transient, and dose-dependent. Frequencies below are based on clinical trial data [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypokalaemia, macropapular rashes, hypersensitivity reactions, fatigue, headache, dizziness, muscle cramps. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hyponatraemia, vomiting, dyspepsia, abdominal pain, drowsiness, sleepiness, insomnia, anxiety, weakness, visual impairment, cystitis, erectile dysfunction. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Hypochloraemia, hypomagnesaemia, vertigo, paresthesia, nausea, constipation, dry mouth. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia, haemolytic anaemia, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia, hypercalcaemia, arrhythmia, palpitations, chest pain, hypotension, pancreatitis, abnormal hepatic function, angioedema, urticaria, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), renal failure. |
| **Not Known** | Syncope, myopia, blurred vision, acute angle-closure glaucoma, choroidal effusion, *Torsades de pointes*, hepatic encephalopathy, hepatitis, worsening of SLE, rhabdomyolysis, QT prolongation, increased blood glucose/uric acid. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Lithium:** Combined use is not recommended due to reduced renal clearance of lithium, increasing the risk of toxicity [Australian].
- **Torsades de pointes-inducing drugs:** Avoid combination with Class Ia/Ic/III antiarrhythmics, certain antipsychotics, antidepressants, and antimicrobial agents (e.g., erythromycin IV, fluoroquinolones) due to increased risk of ventricular arrhythmias [Australian].
- **NSAIDs (Systemic) / COX-2 Inhibitors:** May reduce the antihypertensive effect of indapamide and increase the risk of acute renal failure in dehydrated patients [Australian].
- **ACE Inhibitors:** Risk of sudden hypotension and/or acute renal failure if initiated in patients with pre-existing sodium depletion [Australian].
- **Digitalis Preparations:** Hypokalaemia and hypomagnesaemia predispose patients to digitalis toxicity. Monitor K+, Mg2+, and ECG [Australian].
- **Metformin:** Avoid use if plasma creatinine exceeds 135 μmol/L (men) or 110 μmol/L (women) due to increased risk of lactic acidosis [Australian].
- **Iodinated Contrast Media:** Ensure adequate hydration before use to prevent acute renal failure [Australian].
- **Baclofen:** Potentiates the antihypertensive effect [Australian].
- **Corticosteroids (Systemic):** May decrease the antihypertensive effect due to water and sodium retention [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category C** [Australian]. Indapamide should be avoided in pregnant women and must not be used to treat gestational oedema. Thiazide-related diuretics can cause feto-placental ischaemia, growth retardation, and neonatal thrombocytopenia [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Not recommended. Indapamide is excreted in human breast milk and may suppress lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) showed no impairment of fertility at doses up to 25 mg/kg/day, though a reduction in implantation sites was noted at the highest doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Indapamide may be used if renal function is normal or only minimally impaired. Plasma creatinine must be adjusted for age, weight, and gender [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe renal failure. If azotaemia and oliguria increase during treatment, the drug should be discontinued [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment or hepatic coma due to the risk of encephalopathy [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Indapamide is a sulfonamide diuretic with both vascular and renal activity. It reduces vascular reactivity to pressor amines and acts on the proximal segment of the distal tubule to increase the excretion of sodium and chloride [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** At the 1.5 mg dose, it provides maximal antihypertensive effect with minimal diuretic activity. It does not adversely affect lipid profiles or glucose tolerance [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and totally absorbed; peak serum levels occur approximately 12 hours after dosing [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** 79% protein bound; highly bound to red blood cells (80%) and specifically to carbonic anhydrase (98%) [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised in the liver; only 5–7% is excreted unchanged in the urine [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 70% is eliminated via urine and 22% via faeces as inactive metabolites. The mean elimination half-life is 18 hours (range 14–24 hours) [Australian].
