# Editorial Product Information: Indinavir

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for professional use and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Indinavir sulfate (inhibitor of HIV-1 protease).
- **Brand Name(s):** Crixivan [US].
- **ATC Code:** J05AE02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Oral capsules containing 100 mg, 200 mg, and 400 mg of indinavir (as 125 mg, 250 mg, and 500 mg indinavir sulfate, respectively) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Merck & Co., Inc. (distributed by STAT Rx USA LLC in provided source) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **HIV-1 Infection:** Indicated in combination with other antiretroviral agents for the treatment of HIV infection [US].
- *Note:* Clinical trials demonstrated reduction in AIDS-defining illnesses or death and prolonged suppression of HIV RNA [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 800 mg (usually two 400-mg capsules) administered orally every 8 hours [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - For optimal absorption, administer without food, with water, 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal [US].
    - Alternatively, may be taken with other liquids (skim milk, juice, coffee, tea) or a light meal (e.g., dry toast with jelly, apple juice, cornflakes with skim milk) [US]. 
    - **Avoid high-calorie, high-fat, or high-protein meals**, as these significantly reduce absorption [US].
- **Hydration:** To minimise the risk of nephrolithiasis, adults should drink at least 1.5 liters of liquids every 24 hours [US].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed by >2 hours, the patient should skip it and take the next dose at the regular time. If missed by <2 hours, the dose should be taken immediately [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Clinically significant hypersensitivity to indinavir or any component of the formulation [US].
- **CYP3A4 Interactions:** Coadministration is contraindicated with drugs highly dependent on CYP3A4 for clearance, where elevated levels may lead to serious or life-threatening events [US]:
    - **Alpha 1-adrenoreceptor antagonists:** Alfuzosin.
    - **Antiarrhythmics:** Amiodarone.
    - **Ergot derivatives:** Dihydroergotamine, ergonovine, ergotamine, methylergonovine.
    - **GI motility agents:** Cisapride.
    - **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors:** Lovastatin, simvastatin.
    - **Neuroleptics:** Pimozide.
    - **PDE5 Inhibitors:** Sildenafil (specifically when used for pulmonary arterial hypertension/Revatio).
    - **Sedative/Hypnotics:** Oral midazolam, triazolam, alprazolam.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Nephrolithiasis/Urolithiasis:** Occurs in ~12.4% of adults and 29% of pediatric patients. May be associated with renal insufficiency or acute renal failure. Temporary interruption (1–3 days) or discontinuation may be required [US].
- **Hemolytic Anemia:** Acute cases, including fatalities, have been reported. Discontinue if diagnosed [US].
- **Hepatitis:** Reports of hepatic failure and death, though often confounded by other medical conditions [US].
- **Hyperglycemia:** New-onset diabetes or exacerbation of existing diabetes and hyperglycemia reported [US].
- **Indirect Hyperbilirubinemia:** Frequently occurs; predominantly elevated indirect bilirubin (~14%) [US].
- **Tubulointerstitial Nephritis:** Reported with medullary calcification and cortical atrophy in patients with asymptomatic severe leukocyturia [US].
- **Immune Reconstitution Syndrome:** Inflammatory responses to residual opportunistic infections (e.g., MAC, CMV, PCP, TB) or autoimmune disorders may occur [US].
- **Fat Redistribution:** Central obesity, "buffalo hump," and peripheral wasting observed [US].
- **Hemophilia:** Spontaneous bleeding episodes reported in patients with hemophilia A and B [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Very Common (>10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea (11.7–31.9%), abdominal pain (16.0–16.6%) [US].
- **Laboratory:** Indirect hyperbilirubinemia (approx. 14%), total serum bilirubin >2.5x ULN (approx. 10–12%) [US].
- **Urogenital:** Nephrolithiasis (approx. 12.4% overall; up to 34% in some trials) [US].

### Common (1–10%)
- **Gastrointestinal:** Vomiting (8.4–17.8%), diarrhea (0.9–3.3%), acid regurgitation, anorexia, dyspepsia [US].
- **Neurological:** Headache (5.4–9.6%), dizziness (3.0–3.9%), somnolence, oral paresthesia [US].
- **Musculoskeletal:** Back pain (0.9–8.4%), arthralgia [US].
- **Skin:** Pruritus (0.5–4.2%), rash, dry skin [US].
- **General:** Asthenia/fatigue (2.1–4.2%), malaise, fever [US].
- **Other:** Taste perversion [US].

### Post-Marketing/Serious Effects
- **Cardiovascular:** Myocardial infarction, angina pectoris, cerebrovascular disorder.
- **Dermatological:** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, erythema multiforme.
- **Genitourinary:** Acute renal failure, interstitial nephritis, crystalluria.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibition:** Indinavir is a potent inhibitor of CYP3A4. Coadministration with drugs metabolized by this pathway may significantly increase their plasma concentrations [US].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Drugs that induce CYP3A4 (e.g., **Rifampin, St. John’s Wort**) significantly decrease indinavir levels and are **not recommended** [US].
- **Specific Clinical Managements:**
    - **Ritonavir:** Increases indinavir concentrations; dosing not fully established [US].
    - **Delavirdine:** Increases indinavir levels; consider reducing indinavir dose to 600 mg q8h [US].
    - **Efavirenz:** Decreases indinavir levels; increasing dose to 1000 mg q8h does not compensate for induction [US].
    - **Ketoconazole/Itraconazole:** Increases indinavir levels; reduce indinavir dose to 600 mg q8h [US].
    - **Rifabutin:** Decrease rifabutin dose by half and increase indinavir dose to 1000 mg q8h [US].
    - **PDE5 Inhibitors (Sildenafil, Tadalafil, Vardenafil):** Use with extreme caution. Do not exceed: 25 mg sildenafil per 48h; 10 mg tadalafil per 72h; or 2.5 mg vardenafil per 24h [US].
    - **Didanosine:** Administer at least 1 hour apart on an empty stomach due to buffer interference [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **US Category C.** 
    - Indinavir plasma exposure is significantly lower (approx. 74% lower AUC) during pregnancy than postpartum. 
    - Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk; not recommended as primary therapy due to low antepartum levels [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Mothers should be instructed **not to breastfeed** to avoid postnatal transmission of HIV-1 and due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants (excreted in rat milk; human excretion unknown) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Optimal dosing not established. A dose of 500 mg/m² every 8 hours has been studied in children aged 3–18 years. Note significantly higher risk of nephrolithiasis (29%) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient subjects ≥65 years. Dose selection should be cautious due to increased frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics not studied. Use with caution due to nephrolithiasis risk [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** In patients with mild-to-moderate insufficiency due to cirrhosis, reduce dosage to **600 mg every 8 hours**. Not studied in severe impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits HIV protease, preventing the cleavage of viral polyproteins into functional proteins, resulting in the formation of immature, non-infectious viral particles [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid in fasted state (Tmax 0.8h). Significant food effect (77% reduction in AUC with high-fat meals) [US].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 60% protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP3A4 [US].
    - **Elimination:** Half-life ~1.8h. <20% excreted unchanged in urine [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ACTG 320:** Multicenter, randomized, double-blind trial. Combined indinavir/zidovudine/lamivudine reduced risk of AIDS-defining illness or death by approx. 50% compared to zidovudine/lamivudine alone in PI-naive patients [US].
- **Study 028:** Demonstrated superior viral suppression and CD4+ cell count increases with indinavir/zidovudine compared to zidovudine alone [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at room temperature (15–30°C / 59–86°F) [US].
- **Moisture Sensitivity:** Store in the original container. The desiccant should remain in the bottle to protect from moisture [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
