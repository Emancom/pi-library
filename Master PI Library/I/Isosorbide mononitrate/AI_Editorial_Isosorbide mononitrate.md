# Editorial Product Information: Isosorbide Mononitrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Isosorbide mononitrate (an organic nitrate and the active metabolite of isosorbide dinitrate).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Isosorbide Mononitrate [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C01DA14.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sustained-release (SR) tablets containing 60 mg of isosorbide mononitrate [Australian]. 
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Angina Pectoris Prophylaxis:** Indicated for the long-term, prophylactic treatment of angina pectoris [Australian].
- **Limitation of Use:** Not indicated for the management of acute anginal attacks. In the event of an acute attack, rapid-acting nitrates (e.g., sublingual or buccal glyceryl trinitrate) must be used [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The standard dose is 60 mg (one tablet) once daily.
- **Dose Titration:** If necessary, the dose may be increased to 120 mg (two tablets) once daily. Both tablets should be taken simultaneously [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets must be swallowed whole with half a glass of liquid.
    - Tablets must **not** be chewed or crushed.
    - **Note on Halving Tablets:** While the Australian formulation is scored, there is insufficient evidence to guarantee that a halved tablet delivers exactly half the dose or maintains the same release rate. Dissolution may be slightly faster in halved tablets [Australian].
- **Frequency:** Must be administered **once daily** only. Twice-daily dosing is not recommended as it prevents the "nitrate-low" period required to reduce the risk of tolerance [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to nitrates or any excipients (including lactose) [Australian].
- **Circulatory Failure/Shock:** Including cardiogenic shock, shock, and severe hypotension [Australian].
- **Structural Heart Disease:** Obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, pericarditis, aortic stenosis, cardiac tamponade, and mitral stenosis [Australian].
- **Anaemia:** Severe anaemia [Australian].
- **Concomitant Medications:** Phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5) inhibitors (e.g., sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil) are strictly contraindicated due to the risk of severe hypotension, syncope, or myocardial infarction [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Nitrate Tolerance:** Tolerance to antianginal effects may develop with higher doses (>120 mg/day) or more frequent dosing (e.g., twice daily). Once-daily dosing is essential to maintain a period of low nitrate concentration [Australian].
- **Hypotension:** Severe hypotension, particularly with upright posture, can occur even with low doses. Paradoxical bradycardia and increased angina may accompany nitrate-induced hypotension. Caution is advised in patients who are volume-depleted or already hypotensive [Australian].
- **Myocardial Infarction (MI) and Cardiac Failure:** Benefits in these settings are not established. Use is generally not recommended because the effects are difficult to terminate rapidly. If used, intensive clinical and haemodynamic monitoring is required [Australian].
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** Although rebound phenomena are not clearly established for the SR formulation, withdrawal should be gradual to prevent the exacerbation of anginal symptoms [Australian].
- **Industrial Exposure:** Physical dependence has been observed in industrial workers with long-term exposure to organic nitrates; temporary withdrawal has been linked to chest pain and MI [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are most common during the initiation of therapy and are typically related to vasodilatory activity.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (>10%) | Headache (up to 30%; usually diminishes with continued treatment) |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Hypotension (4-5%), dizziness, nausea, tiredness, sleep disturbances (6%), gastrointestinal disturbances (6%), poor appetite (2.5%), tachycardia |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Vertigo, fainting, vomiting, diarrhoea, heartburn, rash, pruritus |
| **Very Rare** (<0.1%) | Myalgia |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Syncope and myocardial infarction (specifically when combined with PDE5 inhibitors) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **PDE5 Inhibitors:** Potentiate vasodilatory effects; combined use is contraindicated [Australian].
- **Sulfhydryl-Containing Compounds:** The metabolism of nitrates to nitric oxide is sulfhydryl-dependent. Exogenous sources (e.g., N-acetylcysteine, methionine, captopril) may prolong exercise time when combined with isosorbide mononitrate [Australian].
- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** 
    - Verapamil-type (e.g., gallopamil) may further improve left ventricular function parameters. 
    - Combined use with any calcium antagonist may cause marked symptomatic orthostatic hypotension, requiring dose adjustments [Australian].
- **Propranolol:** In patients with cirrhosis, adding isosorbide mononitrate to propranolol significantly reduces portal pressure and hepatic blood flow [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** May increase the frequency of hypotension and lightheadedness [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. Safety has not been established. Use only if clinical benefits outweigh potential risks; specific Segment I and III studies are lacking [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Use is **not recommended** as there is no documentation regarding the passage of the drug into breast milk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use is not recommended due to a lack of data [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No dose reduction is generally required, unless severe renal impairment is present [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** While immediate-release studies showed no major PK changes, some data suggest a prolonged half-life and higher plasma concentrations in severe impairment. Reduced dosage may be appropriate for the SR formulation to avoid accumulation [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Long-term therapy has been shown to decrease portal pressure in patients with cirrhosis and portal hypertension [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Isosorbide mononitrate is a nitric oxide donor. It acts as a physiological substitute for endothelium-derived relaxing factor (EDRF). It produces venous and arterial dilatation, reducing cardiac workload (preload and afterload) and improving the oxygen supply/demand balance [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** Approximately 85% [Australian].
    - **Absorption:** Gradual, non-pH dependent release from a hydrophobic cellulose matrix; completed in approximately 10 hours. Absorption is not affected by food [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Low protein binding (<5%); volume of distribution is ~0.6 L/kg [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via hydrolysis and conjugation in the liver [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Elimination half-life is approximately 5 hours. Metabolites are excreted via kidneys; only 2% is excreted intact [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** Placebo-controlled studies demonstrate significant increases in exercise capacity for patients with angina pectoris, both as monotherapy and when added to beta-blockers [Australian].
- **Note:** Specific pivotal trial names/designs were not specified in the provided source.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from light and moisture [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **S4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
