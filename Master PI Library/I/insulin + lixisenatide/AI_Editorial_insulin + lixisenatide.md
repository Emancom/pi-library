# Editorial Product Information: insulin + lixisenatide

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **insulin + lixisenatide**
- Chemical class:
  - insulin component: insulin glargine (long-acting insulin analog)
  - incretin component: lixisenatide (glucagon-like peptide‑1 [GLP-1] receptor agonist)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: subcutaneous injection

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2016.
- Date of last revision/update reported in available source: March 2026.
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources for any jurisdiction.
- Other jurisdictional registration dates or revision histories: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Indicated as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus.
- Limitations of use:
  - Concomitant use with any other product containing a GLP‑1 receptor agonist (including lixisenatide-containing products) is not recommended.
  - Not recommended for the treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis.
  - Has not been studied in combination with prandial (mealtime) insulin.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Administer once daily by subcutaneous injection within the hour prior to the first meal of the day.
- Usual dosing range delivered: 15 to 60 units per injection (each unit refers to the insulin glargine component; corresponding lixisenatide microgram content varies by total units).
- Maximum recommended daily dose: 60 units (corresponding to 60 units insulin glargine and 20 mcg lixisenatide).

### Recommended starting doses
- Patients naive to basal insulin or GLP‑1 receptor agonist, patients currently on a GLP‑1 receptor agonist, or patients on <30 units/day of basal insulin:
  - Start at 15 units once daily (15 units insulin glargine + 5 mcg lixisenatide).
- Patients currently on 30 to 60 units/day of basal insulin (with or without a GLP‑1 receptor agonist):
  - Start at 30 units once daily (30 units insulin glargine + 10 mcg lixisenatide).
- Discontinue prior basal insulin or prior GLP‑1 receptor agonist therapy before initiating insulin + lixisenatide.

### Unit-to-lixisenatide mapping
- The combination delivers increasing lixisenatide microgram amounts across the dosing range. Representative mapping (units : lixisenatide mcg) — doses relevant to clinical use:
  - 15 units → 5.0 mcg
  - 30 units → 10.0 mcg
  - 45 units → 15.0 mcg
  - 60 units → 20.0 mcg (maximum daily dose)
- Intermediate unit increments correspond to proportional lixisenatide microgram amounts (e.g., 31 units ≈ 10.3 mcg; 59 units ≈ 19.7 mcg). Use the specific product dosing window for exact values.

### Titration
- After initiation, titrate by 2 to 4 units once weekly based on fasting plasma glucose, metabolic needs, and glycemic goals.
- Recommended weekly titration algorithm (self‑monitored fasting plasma glucose):
  - Above target range: increase by +2 to +4 units (equivalent increase in both insulin glargine and lixisenatide components).
  - Within target range: no change.
  - Below target range: decrease by -2 to -4 units.
- Individualize titration when there are changes in physical activity, meal patterns, concomitant medications, renal or hepatic function, or during acute illness.

### Administration instructions
- Inject subcutaneously into the abdominal area, thigh, or upper arm. Rotate injection sites within the same region to reduce risk of lipodystrophy and localized cutaneous amyloidosis. Do not inject into areas of lipodystrophy or localized cutaneous amyloidosis.
- Visually inspect the solution prior to injection. Use only if the solution is clear and colorless to almost colorless.
- Do not administer intravenously or via an insulin infusion pump.
- Do not dilute or mix with other insulin products or solutions.
- Do not split a dose.
- The delivery device is intended for single‑patient use only. Never share pens between patients.
- During any change in insulin regimen (strength, manufacturer, type, injection site, or method), increase the frequency of glucose monitoring.

### Missed dose
- If a dose is missed, resume the once‑daily regimen at the next scheduled dose. Do not administer an extra dose to make up for a missed dose.

## 5. Contraindications
- During episodes of hypoglycemia.
- Known serious hypersensitivity to insulin glargine, lixisenatide, or any excipient in the product (including history of anaphylaxis or angioedema attributable to either component).
- Any additional jurisdiction‑specific contraindications: Not specified in available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- None specified in available sources as a jurisdictional boxed warning. (Serious risks are described below under Warnings and Precautions.)

### Important general warnings and precautions
- **Anaphylaxis and serious hypersensitivity reactions:** Severe, life‑threatening allergic reactions including anaphylaxis, angioedema, bronchospasm, hypotension, and shock can occur. Discontinue therapy and seek immediate medical attention if hypersensitivity is suspected. Exercise caution in patients with a history of anaphylaxis to another GLP‑1 receptor agonist (predisposition unknown).
- **Acute pancreatitis:** Cases of acute pancreatitis, including fatal and hemorrhagic/necrotizing pancreatitis, have been observed with GLP‑1 receptor agonists. Monitor for persistent severe abdominal pain (which may radiate to the back), with or without nausea/vomiting; discontinue if pancreatitis is suspected.
- **Never share injection devices:** Do not share pens between patients to avoid transmission of blood‑borne pathogens.
- **Hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia with changes in insulin regimen:** Changes in insulin therapy (strength, manufacturer, type, injection site, or administration method) can affect glycemic control. Make regimen changes under close medical supervision and increase blood glucose monitoring.
- **Overdose and medication errors:** The product contains two active drugs. Exceeding the maximum daily dose (60 units; 20 mcg lixisenatide) or concomitant use with other GLP‑1 receptor agonists can lead to overdose-related adverse events. Instruct patients to check labels carefully to avoid mix‑ups with other insulin products.
- **Hypoglycemia:** Hypoglycemia is the most common adverse reaction. Severe hypoglycemia can be life‑threatening. Risk factors include intensive glycemic control, changes in dosing, irregular meals, increased physical activity, renal or hepatic impairment, and use of medications that blunt hypoglycemia awareness (e.g., beta‑blockers).
- **Acute kidney injury due to volume depletion:** Postmarketing reports describe acute kidney injury, sometimes requiring dialysis, typically associated with volume depletion from severe gastrointestinal adverse events (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea). Monitor renal function in patients with adverse GI events and during initiation and dose escalation. Not recommended in end‑stage renal disease.
- **Severe gastrointestinal adverse reactions:** GLP‑1 receptor agonists have been associated with gastrointestinal adverse reactions, sometimes severe. Not recommended in patients with severe gastroparesis.
- **Immunogenicity:** Patients may develop anti‑insulin and anti‑lixisenatide antibodies. In pooled analysis of lixisenatide studies, 70% were antibody positive at Week 24; 2.4% had high concentrations (>100 nmol/L) associated with attenuated glycemic response. Consider alternative antidiabetic therapy if there is loss of efficacy or significant allergic/injection‑site reactions.
- **Hypokalemia:** Insulin therapy can cause a shift of potassium intracellularly and may precipitate hypokalemia, which can be life‑threatening. Monitor and manage potassium in at‑risk patients.
- **Fluid retention and heart failure with PPAR‑gamma agonists:** Concomitant use of PPAR‑gamma agonists (e.g., thiazolidinediones) can cause fluid retention and may exacerbate or precipitate heart failure. Observe for signs and consider dose reduction or discontinuation of contributing agents if heart failure occurs.
- **Acute gallbladder disease:** Cases of cholelithiasis and cholecystitis have been reported. Evaluate gallbladder if suspected.
- **Pulmonary aspiration during general anesthesia or deep sedation:** There have been reports of pulmonary aspiration in patients receiving GLP‑1 receptor agonists undergoing elective procedures. Advise patients to inform providers of planned surgeries or procedures.

### Monitoring requirements
- Frequent self‑monitoring of blood glucose, particularly during initiation, titration, or any change to insulin regimen.
- Monitor renal function in patients with severe GI events or at initiation/escalation; avoid in end‑stage renal disease.
- Monitor electrolytes (potassium) in patients at risk of hypokalemia.
- Monitor for signs/symptoms of pancreatitis, hypersensitivity, gallbladder disease, and heart failure.
- Monitor injection sites for lipodystrophy and localized cutaneous amyloidosis.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Use with caution in visually impaired patients who may rely on audible dosing clicks.
- Delay in gastric emptying caused by lixisenatide may affect absorption of orally administered medications (see Drug Interactions).
- Not recommended in severe gastroparesis.
- Use caution and increased glucose monitoring in patients with renal or hepatic impairment due to higher hypoglycemia risk.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic interactions
- Drugs that affect glucose metabolism (e.g., other antidiabetics, corticosteroids): may necessitate insulin dose adjustment; increase frequency of blood glucose monitoring.
- Antiadrenergic drugs (e.g., beta‑blockers, clonidine, guanethidine, reserpine): may reduce the signs and symptoms of hypoglycemia; monitor closely.
- Concomitant use with PPAR‑gamma agonists (thiazolidinediones): may increase risk of fluid retention and heart failure; monitor for heart failure signs and consider dose reduction or discontinuation if heart failure occurs.

### Pharmacokinetic / absorption interactions
- Lixisenatide delays gastric emptying and may affect the absorption of concomitantly administered oral medications.
- For oral contraceptives and other oral medications (examples given: some antibiotics and acetaminophen), administration timing recommendations provided:
  - Take such oral medications at least 1 hour prior to insulin + lixisenatide administration or 11 hours after administration. (Timing caveat reflects source labeling.)
- Impact on other oral drugs (e.g., narrow therapeutic index agents): Not specified in available sources; consider monitoring and adjusting as appropriate.

### Clinically significant interactions and management
- Avoid coadministration with other GLP‑1 receptor agonists (including lixisenatide‑containing products): not recommended due to overlapping effects and risk of excess lixisenatide exposure.
- Avoid exceeding the maximum lixisenatide dose (20 mcg/day) or using in combination with other GLP‑1 receptor agonists to prevent overdose of incretin component.
- When starting, stopping, or changing doses of interacting drugs, increase frequency of glucose monitoring and adjust insulin dose as needed.

### Jurisdictional interaction notes
- No jurisdiction‑specific interaction differences are specified in available sources.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- **insulin + lixisenatide** combines a long‑acting insulin analog (insulin glargine) to provide basal insulin replacement with a GLP‑1 receptor agonist (lixisenatide) that augments glucose‑dependent insulin secretion and delays gastric emptying.
- Lixisenatide's delay in gastric emptying contributes to postprandial glucose lowering and can affect absorption of oral medications.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Detailed pharmacokinetic parameters (absorption rates, distribution, metabolism pathways, elimination half‑lives) for the combination product: Not specified in available sources.
- Clinical note: the long‑acting effect of the insulin glargine component may prolong hypoglycemia and delay recovery from hypoglycemia.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Detailed pharmacodynamic profiles: Not specified in available sources beyond the mechanism described above and clinical effects on fasting and postprandial glycemia implied by combination of basal insulin and GLP‑1 receptor agonist.
