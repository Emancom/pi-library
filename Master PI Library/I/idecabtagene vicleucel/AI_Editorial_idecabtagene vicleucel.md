# Editorial Product Information: Idecabtagene Vicleucel

## Editorial Note
- This Editorial Product Information document has been synthesized from the following jurisdiction-specific official Product Information (PI):
    - **United States (FDA)**: ABECMA (idecabtagene vicleucel) suspension for intravenous infusion.
- **Purpose**: This is a harmonised editorial overview designed for use by healthcare professionals. It provides a clinically accurate synthesis of the pharmacological profile, administration protocols, and safety considerations.
- **Note**: This document is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the United States (FDA) PI was provided for synthesis; references to other jurisdictions are noted as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Idecabtagene vicleucel.
- **Chemical Class**: B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA)-directed genetically modified autologous T cell immunotherapy.
- **Brand Name**: ABECMA [US].
- **ATC Code**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength**: A cell suspension for intravenous infusion. Each dose contains between 300 to 510 × 10⁶ chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-positive T cells. It is supplied in 50 mL, 250 mL, or 500 mL infusion bags [US].
- **Route of Administration**: Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Celgene Corporation, a Bristol-Myers Squibb Company [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsed or Refractory Multiple Myeloma**: Indicated for the treatment of adult patients who have received at least two prior lines of therapy. These prior therapies must include an immunomodulatory agent, a proteasome inhibitor, and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult)**: For autologous use only. The recommended dose range is 300 to 510 × 10⁶ CAR-positive T cells [US].
- **Pre-infusion Procedures**:
    - **Lymphodepleting Chemotherapy**: Administer cyclophosphamide (300 mg/m² IV) and fludarabine (30 mg/m² IV) daily for three days. ABECMA infusion should occur two days after completing this regimen [US].
    - **Premedication**: Administer acetaminophen (650 mg orally) and an H1-antihistamine (e.g., diphenhydramine 12.5 mg IV or 25-50 mg orally) approximately 30 to 60 minutes prior to infusion [US].
- **Administration Instructions**:
    - Do **NOT** use a leukodepleting filter [US].
    - Confirm patient identity matches the identifiers on the cassette and infusion bag prior to thawing.
    - Infuse the entire contents within one hour after the start of thawing. Use gravity flow.
    - Rinse the tubing with 30 to 60 mL of normal saline after infusion to ensure complete dose delivery [US].
- **Monitoring**: Patients should be monitored daily for at least seven days following infusion at a qualified healthcare facility. They should remain in proximity to the facility for at least one week [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None listed [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings [US]**:
    - **Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS)**: May be fatal or life-threatening. Do not administer to patients with active infections or inflammatory disorders.
    - **Neurologic Toxicities (ICANS)**: May be severe or life-threatening. Can occur concurrently with CRS or after its resolution.
    - **HLH/MAS**: Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis/Macrophage Activation Syndrome can occur and may be fatal.
    - **Prolonged Cytopenia**: Severe neutropenia and thrombocytopenia persisting beyond Week 4 post-infusion.
    - **Secondary Hematological Malignancies**: T-cell malignancies have occurred following treatment with BCMA-directed therapies.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Monitor for reactions during infusion; dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) in the product may cause sensitivity [US].
- **Infections**: Severe, life-threatening, or fatal infections have occurred. Monitor for signs/symptoms and treat appropriately.
- **Hypogammaglobulinemia**: Plasma cell aplasia can lead to low IgG levels. Monitor levels and consider IVIG replacement [US].
- **Driving/Machines**: Advise patients to avoid driving or operating heavy machinery for at least one week after infusion [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%)**: Pyrexia (91%), CRS (89% - any grade), infections (61%), fatigue (33%–45%), musculoskeletal pain (36%–45%), hypogammaglobulinemia (41%–48%), diarrhea (31%–35%), nausea (27%–29%), headache (23%–24%), encephalopathy (22%–26%), hypotension (17%–36%), and tachycardia (19%–32%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%)**: Febrile neutropenia (as a non-laboratory AR, 16%–51%), cough, dyspnea, and dizziness [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects**: CRS Grade 3 or higher (7%–10%), ICANS Grade 3 or higher (4%), and fatal infections (4.3%) [US].
- **Laboratory Abnormalities (Grade 3/4)**: Lymphopenia (92%–98%), leukopenia (96%), neutropenia (96%), thrombocytopenia (59%–63%), and anaemia (52%–63%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Live Vaccines**: Safety has not been studied. Vaccination is not recommended for at least six weeks prior to lymphodepletion, during treatment, and until immune recovery [US].
- **HIV Testing Interference**: Some commercial HIV nucleic acid tests may yield false-positive results due to limited genetic sequence identity between the lentiviral vector used in ABECMA and HIV [US].
- **Corticosteroids**: Avoid prophylactic use of dexamethasone or other systemic corticosteroids as they may interfere with ABECMA activity [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: No available data in pregnant women. Not recommended. Based on the mechanism of action, it may cause fetal toxicity (e.g., B-cell depletion).
    - **Category**: Not assigned a lettered category in the US PI, but identified as having potential fetal risk [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Data on the presence of ABECMA in human milk is unavailable. The developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug [US].
- **Fertility**: No data available on the effects of ABECMA on fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Safety and efficacy have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use**: No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between patients ≥65 years and younger patients, although all 5 cases of Grade 3 neurotoxicity in Study 2 occurred in patients ≥65 years [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment**: No specific studies conducted. Use caution; fludarabine (part of lymphodepletion) requires dose adjustment in renal impairment [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: A BCMA-directed CAR-T cell therapy. The CAR construct includes an anti-BCMA scFv domain for specificity, a CD3-zeta activation domain, and a 4-1BB costimulatory domain. Binding to BCMA on myeloma cells leads to T-cell activation, proliferation, and cytolytic killing of target cells [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**: 
    - **Expansion**: Peak expansion (Tmax) occurs at a median of 11 days post-infusion. 
    - **Persistence**: CAR-T cells can persist in peripheral blood for up to 1 year [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Rapid decreases in soluble BCMA and bone marrow CD138+ cells were observed within the first month [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 1 (KarMMa-3)**: A randomized, open-label trial (N=386) comparing ABECMA to standard regimens.
    - **Primary Outcome**: Median Progression-Free Survival (PFS) was 13.3 months for ABECMA vs 4.4 months for standard therapy (HR 0.49).
    - **ORR**: 71% in the ABECMA arm [US].
- **Study 2 (KarMMa)**: A single-arm study (N=127) in patients with 3+ prior lines of therapy.
    - **ORR**: 72% (95% CI: 62, 81).
    - **Median Duration of Response**: 11 months [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage**: Must be stored in the vapor phase of liquid nitrogen (≤ -130°C).
- **Shelf Life**: Not specified as a date, but product remains stable until the expiration date on the label when stored correctly [US].
- **Handling**: Thaw at 37°C just prior to infusion. Do not wash or spin down cells [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification**: 
    - **United States**: Rx-only; requires administration at a certified healthcare facility under a Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) [US].
- **Poison Schedule**: Not specified in available sources [Australian].
