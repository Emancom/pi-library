# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **US (FDA)**. 
- Information regarding other major jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided for this specific harmonisation and is therefore not included.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. It is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn metabolic errors [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. It should be administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered in severe cases, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Hemodialysis:** Administer as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration before administration [US].

### Monitoring and Adjustments
- **Plasma Concentration:** In metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain baseline carnitine levels and perform weekly/monthly monitoring. The target plasma free carnitine concentration is 35 to 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose Titration (ESRD):** Adjustments may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels below 40–50 µmol/L. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Contraindications:** None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These typically occur in ESRD patients and may manifest within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** While this concerns the injection, the PI notes that chronic high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are presented based on clinical trials in patients on chronic hemodialysis.

| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction (33–38%), Pharyngitis (15–27%), Hypertension (18–21%), Headache (12–37%), Nausea (5–12%), Vomiting (9–21%), Diarrhea (9–35%), Asthenia (8–12%), Dizziness (10–18%), Pain (21–35%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, Fever, Flu syndrome, Chest pain, Palpitations, Tachycardia, Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Peripheral edema, Depression, Dyspnea, Rhitus, Pruritus, Eye disorder |
| **Serious Adverse Effects** | Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, seizures [US]. |

*Editorial Note: Incidence rates are difficult to estimate precisely due to confounding effects of underlying renal pathology [US].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine. It is recommended to monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category B equivalent]: Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. However, there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased carnitine concentrations in milk following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine must be weighed against maternal benefits [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is calculated by weight (50 mg/kg) similar to adults [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. Safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), mean pre-dialysis plasma levocarnitine concentrations increased from 20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks. While levocarnitine effectively increases carnitine concentrations, the impact on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Stability:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature when mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s in PVC bags (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Prescription Only (Rx-only) [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not applicable for US jurisdiction; see local schedules for other regions.
