# Editorial Product Information: Imatinib

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of imatinib product information for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and administration guidelines from the provided source.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Imatinib (as mesilate).
- **Chemical Class:** Phenylaminopyrimidine derivative; protein-tyrosine kinase inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** ARX-IMATINIB [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01EA01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - Hard Gelatin Capsules: 50 mg, 100 mg, and 400 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Imatinib is indicated for:
- **Chronic Myeloid Leukaemia (CML):** Treatment of patients with CML (newly diagnosed, accelerated phase, or blast crisis).
- **Philadelphia chromosome positive Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia (Ph+ ALL):**
    - Newly diagnosed adult and paediatric patients (integrated with chemotherapy).
    - Relapsed or refractory Ph+ ALL in adults (monotherapy).
- **Myelodysplastic/Myeloproliferative Diseases (MDS/MPD):** Adult patients with diseases associated with Platelet-Derived Growth Factor Receptor (PDGFR) gene re-arrangements where conventional therapies have failed.
- **Aggressive Systemic Mastocytosis (ASM):** Adult patients where conventional therapies have failed.
- **Hypereosinophilic Syndrome (HES) and/or Chronic Eosinophilic Leukaemia (CEL):** Adult patients.
- **Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumours (GIST):** 
    - KIT (CD117) positive unresectable and/or metastatic malignant GIST.
    - Adjuvant treatment of adult patients following complete gross resection of KIT (CD117) positive primary GIST at high risk of recurrence.
- **Dermatofibrosarcoma Protuberans (DFSP):** Adult patients with unresectable, recurrent, and/or metastatic disease.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **CML (Chronic Phase):** 400 mg once daily.
- **CML (Accelerated Phase/Blast Crisis):** 600 mg once daily.
- **Ph+ ALL:** 600 mg once daily.
- **MDS/MPD and ASM:** 400 mg once daily (starting dose of 100 mg for ASM with eosinophilia related to FIP1L1-PDGFRα fusion kinase).
- **HES/CEL:** 400 mg once daily (starting dose of 100 mg if FIP1L1-PDGFRα fusion kinase is demonstrated).
- **GIST (Unresectable/Metastatic):** 400 mg or 600 mg once daily.
- **GIST (Adjuvant):** 400 mg once daily.
- **DFSP:** 800 mg daily (administered as 400 mg twice daily).

### Administration Instructions
- Administer with a meal and a large glass of water to reduce gastrointestinal irritation.
- Doses of 400 mg or 600 mg are taken once daily; 800 mg doses should be split (400 mg twice daily).
- **For patients unable to swallow:** Capsule contents may be dispersed in water or apple juice (approx. 50 mL for 100 mg, 200 mL for 400 mg) and consumed immediately.
- **Handling:** Avoid skin/eye contact or inhalation of capsule contents. Wash hands immediately after handling [Australian].

### Dose Adjustments for Toxicity
- **Hepatotoxicity:** If bilirubin >3x IULN or transaminases >5x IULN, withhold imatinib until levels return to <1.5x IULN (bilirubin) and <2.5x IULN (transaminases). Resume at a reduced dose (e.g., 400 mg to 300 mg).
- **Haematological Toxicity:** Treatment should be interrupted or dose-reduced for severe neutropenia (ANC <1.0 x $10^9$/L) or thrombocytopenia (platelets <50 x $10^9$/L) according to indication-specific thresholds [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to imatinib mesilate or any excipients.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Fluid Retention and Oedema:** Frequently associated with imatinib; severe fluid retention (pleural effusion, pericardial effusion, pulmonary oedema, ascites) occurs in 2–8% of CML patients. Monitor weight regularly.
- **Hepatitis B Reactivation:** Reactivation can occur in chronic carriers. Screen patients for HBV infection before treatment; monitor carriers closely during and for several months after therapy.
- **Haematological Toxicity:** Neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and anaemia occur frequently, especially in advanced CML/Ph+ ALL. Perform complete blood counts weekly for the first month, then bi-weekly for the second month, and periodically thereafter.
- **GI Haemorrhage and Perforation:** Reported in GIST patients; may be disease-related. Monitor for gastrointestinal symptoms.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Monitor liver function tests (transaminases, bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase) regularly.
- **Cardiac Failure:** Severe congestive heart failure and left ventricular dysfunction reported. Monitor patients with known cardiac disease or renal failure.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome:** Correct dehydration and treat high uric acid levels prior to initiation.
- **Growth Retardation:** Reported in children and pre-adolescents; monitor growth closely in paediatric patients.
- **Phototoxicity:** Exposure to direct sunlight should be minimised. Use protective clothing and SPF sunscreen.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, headache, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, abdominal pain, periorbital oedema, dermatitis/eczema/rash, muscle spasms/cramps, musculoskeletal pain (including arthralgia, myalgia, bone pain), fluid retention, fatigue, weight increase.
- **Common (1–10%):** Pancytopenia, febrile neutropenia, anorexia, insomnia, dizziness, paraesthesia, taste disturbance, lacrimation increased, conjunctivitis, eyelid oedema, flushing, dyspnoea, epistaxis, abdominal distension, flatulence, constipation, gastro-oesophageal reflux, dry mouth, gastritis, increased hepatic enzymes, hyperbilirubinaemia, pruritus, face oedema, alopecia, night sweats, pyrexia, weakness, rigors.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Gastrointestinal haemorrhage, cerebral haemorrhage, cardiac arrest, myocardial infarction, acute renal failure, hepatic failure/necrosis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, and anaphylactic shock (very rare).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, erythromycin, clarithromycin, grapefruit juice) May increase imatinib plasma concentrations.
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, dexamethasone, phenytoin, carbamazepine, St John’s Wort) May significantly reduce imatinib exposure. Avoid concomitant use.
- **Substrates of CYP3A4:** Imatinib increases exposure to drugs metabolised by CYP3A4 (e.g., simvastatin, cyclosporin, pimozide).
- **Warfarin:** Imatinib may inhibit CYP2C9; monitor INR closely if warfarin is used.
- **Paracetamol:** Use with caution; imatinib inhibits paracetamol O-glucuronidation in vitro. A fatal case of acute liver failure was reported with regular paracetamol use [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Imatinib is teratogenic in animals. Post-marketing reports include spontaneous abortions and congenital anomalies. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk to the foetus. Women of childbearing potential must use highly effective contraception during and for at least 15 days after treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** Both imatinib and its active metabolite are excreted in human milk. Due to unknown effects on infants, breastfeeding is not recommended.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies show decreased testicular/epididymal weights and sperm motility at high doses.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Efficacy demonstrated in Ph+ CML and Ph+ ALL. Dose is based on Body Surface Area (BSA): 340 mg/$m^2$ daily (not to exceed 600 mg). No data for children <2 years with CML or <1 year with Ph+ ALL.
- **Elderly Use:** No significant age-related pharmacokinetic differences. Over 20% of clinical trial participants were ≥65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** Imatinib is not significantly excreted by the kidneys. Exposure may increase 1.5–2 fold in mild/moderate impairment. Start at 400 mg/day; use with caution.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Metabolised by the liver. In severe impairment, exposure increases by ~45%. For mild/moderate impairment, use 400 mg daily; for severe impairment, start at 300 mg daily.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the BCR-ABL tyrosine kinase (created by the Philadelphia chromosome), as well as KIT (stem cell factor receptor) and PDGFR (platelet-derived growth factor receptor) kinases. It inhibits proliferation and induces apoptosis in cell lines expressing these targets.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 98% bioavailability; $C_{max}$ reached in ~2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 95% plasma protein binding (albumin and alpha1-acid glycoprotein).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP3A4. Main metabolite is N-desmethylated piperazine derivative.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~18 hours. 81% eliminated in 7 days (68% faeces, 13% urine).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **CML (Study 0106):** Phase III trial showed imatinib (400 mg) was superior to IFN + Ara-C in achieving Complete Haematological Response (96.6% vs 56.6%) and Major Cytogenetic Response (88.6% vs 23.3%).
- **GIST (Study Z9001):** Adjuvant treatment for 1 year significantly prolonged recurrence-free survival compared to placebo (HR 0.40).
- **GIST (SSG XVIII/AIO):** 36 months of adjuvant imatinib showed superior recurrence-free survival and overall survival compared to 12 months.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Protect from moisture.
- Shelf life is specified on the secondary packaging [Australian].
