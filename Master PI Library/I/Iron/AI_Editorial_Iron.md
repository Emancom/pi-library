# Editorial Product Information: Iron Polymaltose

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of iron polymaltose for healthcare professionals, synthesizing clinical data and regulatory requirements.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Iron (III) hydroxide polymaltose complex (AAN). 
- **Chemical Class:** Macromolecular spherocolloidal complex of iron (III) hydroxide and carbohydrate polymaltose (ferric hydroxide and isomaltose).
- **Brand Names:** **Ferrosig** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** B03AC [Not explicitly stated in source, but standard for parenteral iron].
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile aqueous solution for injection: 100 mg elemental iron (as iron polymaltose) per 2 mL ampoule (50 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion and deep Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sigma Company Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Iron polymaltose** is indicated for the treatment of iron deficiency anaemia in patients where:
- Oral iron therapy is contraindicated.
- Enteric absorption of iron is clinically defective.
- Patient non-compliance or persistent gastrointestinal intolerance renders oral therapy impractical [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults and Children)
The total dose required for a complete restoration of iron (haemoglobin and iron stores) is calculated using the **Ganzoni Formula**:
> **Total Iron Dose (mg)** = body weight (kg) x (target Hb – actual Hb in g/L) x 0.24* + iron depot (mg)

*Factor 0.24 = 0.0034 x 0.07 x 1000 (Iron content of Hb ≈ 0.34%; Blood volume ≈ 7% of body weight).

**Iron Depot Targets [Australian]:**
- **Weight ≤ 34 kg:** Target Hb = 130 g/L; Iron depot = 15 mg/kg.
- **Weight > 34 kg:** Target Hb = 150 g/L; Iron depot = 500 mg.

### Administration Instructions
- **Intramuscular (IM):** Must be administered via deep ventro-gluteal injection (Hochstetter technique) to avoid pain and skin discolouration. Injection into the arm or exposed areas is strictly contraindicated. An "S-shaped" puncture channel (Z-track technique) must be used.
- **Intravenous (IV):** Recommended only when the IM route is impractical and bone marrow stores are confirmed empty. Must be administered in a hospital setting. The total dose is added to 500 mL of sterile normal saline (up to 2500 mg iron per 500 mL) [Australian].

### Titration and Rates
- **IV Infusion Rate:** The first 50 mL should be infused slowly (5–10 drops/minute). If tolerated, the rate may be increased to 30 drops/minute (based on a drop volume of 0.067 mL).
- **IM Frequency:** 2 mL every second day until total dose reached, or 4 mL at longer intervals.

### Maximum Single Daily IM Doses
- **Infants (<5 kg):** 0.5 mL (25 mg iron).
- **Children (5–10 kg):** 1.0 mL (50 mg iron).
- **Patients (10–45 kg):** 2.0 mL (100 mg iron).
- **Adults:** 4.0 mL (200 mg iron).

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to iron (III) hydroxide polymaltose complex.
- Anaemia not caused by iron deficiency (e.g., haemolytic, megaloblastic/B12 deficiency, hypoplasia, erythropoiesis disturbances).
- Evidence of iron overload (e.g., haemochromatosis, haemosiderosis).
- Chronic polyarthritis.
- Bronchial asthma.
- Acute phase infectious renal complaints.
- Uncontrolled hyperparathyroidism.
- Decompensated hepatic cirrhosis.
- Infectious hepatitis.
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated during the first trimester [Australian].
- Avoid administration in patients with severe inflammation or infection of the liver or kidney, as elemental iron accumulates in inflamed tissues.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Hypersensitivity and Anaphylaxis
- **Boxed Warning:** Not applicable, but significant warnings for potentially fatal anaphylactoid reactions are present.
- **Kounis Syndrome:** There are reports of hypersensitivity progressing to acute allergic coronary arteriospasm (Kounis syndrome), which may result in myocardial infarction [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Patients must be monitored for at least 30 minutes following administration. Facilities for cardio-pulmonary resuscitation and adrenaline must be immediately available.
- **Test Dose:** A 25 mg iron test dose is recommended before the first therapeutic dose.

### General Precautions
- **Infection Risk:** Iron may increase the pathogenicity of certain microorganisms. IM use in neonates has been linked to increased Gram-negative sepsis (*E. coli*).
- **Inflammatory Disorders:** Patients with rheumatoid arthritis, lupus erythematosus, or ankylosing spondylitis are at higher risk for delayed reactions, including joint pain exacerbation.
- **Pathology Interference:** Large doses (≥250 mg iron) may discolour serum brown if sampled within 4 hours. May cause false elevations in bilirubin and false decreases in serum calcium. Serum iron measurements may be unreliable for 3 weeks post-dose.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are reported infrequently and may be delayed by 1–2 days [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Oral Iron:** Parenteral iron reduces the absorption of concomitant oral iron. Oral therapy should not commence until at least one week after the last injection [Australian].
- **ACE Inhibitors:** Concomitant use may increase the incidence of adverse effects (e.g., erythema, abdominal cramps, nausea, hypotension).
- **Chemical Incompatibility:** Do not mix IV infusions with acidic substances or strong reducing agents, as toxic iron compounds may be liberated.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.** 
    - Contraindicated in the first trimester. 
    - Second/Third Trimester: Use only if benefits clearly outweigh risks (specifically the risk of maternal anaphylaxis and subsequent foetal bradycardia).
- **Breastfeeding:** No data available in source documents [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available in source documents.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in neonates is associated with increased sepsis risk. Dosage must be strictly weight-based (see Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** Specific data not available; patients should consult a practitioner prior to use [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in decompensated cirrhosis and acute infectious renal complaints. Use with caution in hepatic insufficiency.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Provides utilisable iron to target tissues. The complex delivers iron to transferrin (transport protein) and ferritin (storage protein), which is then incorporated into haemoglobin during erythropoiesis.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** IM injection evokes a local inflammatory response; the complex is transported via lymphatics to the blood.
    - **Distribution:** Reaches maximum serum concentration ~24 hours post-IM. Taken up by the reticulo-endothelial system and ionized to Fe3+. 
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 22.4 hours. Very small amounts are excreted; the body lacks a dedicated iron excretion mechanism, leading to overload risk if intake is excessive.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pharmacokinetic Study:** A study of 12 anaemic women (20–45 years) receiving 100 mg IV infusion showed a mean Cmax of 25.1 µg/mL, Tmax of 0.75 hours, and mean residence time of 20.2 hours [Australian].
- No further clinical trial data provided in source.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Protect from light. Store in the original package.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
