# Editorial Product Information: **indacaterol + mometasone**

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **indacaterol + mometasone**
- Chemical class:
  - Indacaterol — long-acting beta2-adrenergic agonist (LABA)
  - Mometasone furoate — inhaled corticosteroid (ICS)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: inhalation (oral inhalation of the capsule contents using the supplied inhalation device)
- Excipients with known effect: contains lactose (monohydrate) approximately 25 mg per capsule (relevant for patients with severe lactose intolerance)

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Maintenance treatment of asthma in adults and adolescents 12 years of age and older where combination therapy with a long-acting beta2-agonist and an inhaled corticosteroid is appropriate:
  - Patients inadequately controlled with inhaled corticosteroids and as‑needed short‑acting beta2‑agonists.
  - Patients inadequately controlled with a long‑acting beta2‑agonist plus low‑dose inhaled corticosteroid and as‑needed short‑acting beta2‑agonists.
- Region-specific notes: Not specified in available sources that any other indications differ by jurisdiction.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult and adolescent ≥12 years)
- Recommended as a once‑daily inhalation of the contents of one capsule.
- Available delivered-dose strengths described in sources: 125 µg indacaterol with either 62.5 µg, 127.5 µg, or 260 µg mometasone furoate (delivered doses). Use the strength appropriate to the required inhaled corticosteroid dose (low, medium, high).
- Maximum recommended dose: once daily dose with indacaterol 125 µg + mometasone 260 µg (i.e., the stated high‑dose combination) — this is the maximum recommended dose in the available sources.

### Dosing by corticosteroid requirement
- Low ICS requirement: indacaterol 125 µg + mometasone 62.5 µg once daily.
- Medium ICS requirement: indacaterol 125 µg + mometasone 127.5 µg once daily.
- High ICS requirement: indacaterol 125 µg + mometasone 260 µg once daily.

### Administration instructions
- For inhalation only. **Do not swallow capsules.**
- Capsules must be administered only using the supplied inhalation device provided with each prescription.
- Instruct patients on correct inhalation technique; verify patients are not swallowing the capsule if there is no clinical benefit.
- After inhalation, patients should rinse their mouth with water and spit out (do not swallow) to reduce the risk of oropharyngeal candidiasis.
- Atective dosing schedule: administer at the same time each day; may be given irrespective of time of day.

### Missed dose
- If a dose is missed, take it as soon as possible. Do not take more than one dose in a 24‑hour period.

### Titration
- Titrate the inhaled corticosteroid component to the lowest effective dose that maintains asthma control. Specific titration schedules are not provided in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to indacaterol, mometasone (mometasone furoate), or any excipient (including lactose).
- No additional jurisdiction‑specific contraindications are specified in the available sources.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed/Highlighted warnings
- **Not for relief of acute asthma symptoms or acute episodes of bronchospasm.** A short‑acting bronchodilator must be available for rescue therapy.
- **Potential for paradoxical bronchospasm** — may be life‑threatening; discontinue treatment immediately if it occurs and institute alternative therapy.

### General warnings and precautions
- Asthma deterioration: increasing use of short‑acting bronchodilators indicates worsening control — review patient therapy.
- Do not stop therapy abruptly without physician supervision; discontinuation may lead to recurrence of symptoms.
- Hypersensitivity reactions including angioedema and anaphylaxis have been reported; discontinue if allergic reaction suspected.
- Cardiovascular effects: LABAs may produce clinically significant cardiovascular effects (e.g., increased pulse, blood pressure, ECG changes). Use with caution in patients with coronary artery disease, recent myocardial infarction, cardiac arrhythmias, or hypertension.
- QT interval: use with caution in patients with known or suspected QT prolongation or who are receiving medicinal products that may prolong the QT interval.
- Hypokalaemia: beta2‑agonists may produce hypokalaemia; effects may be potentiated by hypoxia or concomitant therapies (see Interactions). Clinically relevant hypokalaemia was not observed in trials at recommended doses but caution is warranted in severe disease or with interacting drugs.
- Hyperglycaemia: inhalation of beta2‑agonists and corticosteroids may increase plasma glucose; monitor diabetic patients more closely when initiating therapy.
- Systemic corticosteroid effects: with high doses or prolonged therapy, systemic corticosteroid effects may occur — possible effects include Cushing’s syndrome/features, adrenal suppression, growth retardation in children/adolescents, decreased bone mineral density, cataracts, glaucoma, and neuropsychiatric effects (e.g., psychomotor hyperactivity, sleep disorders, anxiety, depression, aggression). Titrate ICS dose to lowest effective dose.
- Pulmonary infections: administer with caution in patients with active or latent pulmonary tuberculosis or chronic/untreated infections.
- Hypersensitivity to milk proteins/lactose: contains lactose — caution in patients with severe lactose intolerance.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor asthma control and need for rescue medication.
- Monitor serum potassium in patients at risk for hypokalaemia (severe asthma exacerbation, concomitant medications).
- Monitor plasma glucose in diabetic patients after initiating therapy.
- Monitor for signs of adrenal suppression and systemic corticosteroid effects with high-dose or prolonged use.
- In children/adolescents, monitor linear growth periodically.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Caution in patients with convulsive disorders or thyrotoxicosis and in those unusually responsive to beta2‑agonists.
- Use with caution in elderly patients in accordance with usual clinical judgement (no routine dose adjustment required).
- Patients with severe hepatic impairment: no data available — use only if expected benefit outweighs potential risk.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Clinically significant pharmacokinetic drug interactions at therapeutic inhaled doses are considered unlikely due to low systemic concentrations after inhalation.
- Metabolic pathways: clearance of indacaterol involves CYP3A4 and P‑glycoprotein (P‑gp); mometasone furoate clearance involves CYP3A4.
- Inhibition of CYP3A4 and P‑gp can increase systemic exposure of indacaterol or mometasone furoate up to approximately two‑fold:
  - For indacaterol: the magnitude of exposure increase due to interactions is not considered to raise safety concerns based on clinical experience at higher indacaterol doses (experience cited up to 600 µg in studies).
  - For mometasone furoate: although inhaled systemic concentrations are very low, co‑administration with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, itraconazole, nelfinavir, ritonavir, cobicistat) may increase systemic exposure to mometasone furoate and thus the potential for systemic corticosteroid effects; caution is advised.
- Statement in sources: inhibition of CYP3A4 and P‑gp has no impact on safety of therapeutic doses, but increased systemic exposure is possible and must be considered clinically.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions and other clinically significant interactions
- Other long‑acting beta2‑agonists: co‑administration with other LABAs has not been studied and is not recommended as it may potentiate adverse reactions.
- Beta‑adrenergic blockers: beta‑blockers may antagonise the bronchodilatory effects; avoid co‑administration unless compelling reasons exist. If necessary, cardioselective beta‑blockers should be preferred and used with caution.
- QT‑prolonging drugs / MAO inhibitors / tricyclic antidepressants: use with caution — these agents may potentiate cardiovascular effects (including QT prolongation) when used with beta2‑agonists.
- Hypokalaemic agents: concomitant use with methylxanthines, systemic corticosteroids, or non‑potassium‑sparing diuretics may potentiate hypokalaemia from beta2‑agonists — monitor serum potassium as clinically indicated.
- No clinically significant interaction data available for other commonly co‑prescribed agents — specific interaction studies with the combination product were not conducted; interaction potential is inferred from component drugs.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy category (jurisdictional): [Australian] Pregnancy Category B3.
- Risk summary:
  - Human data are insufficient to establish risk for pregnancy when using the combination or its individual components.
  - Animal data:
    - Indacaterol: not teratogenic in rats or rabbits after subcutaneous dosing at high multiples of human exposure; some skeletal variations/retarded ossification observed in rabbits at high doses; impaired learning and decreased fertility observed in rat offspring at high maternal exposures.
    - Mometasone furoate: teratogenic in rodents and rabbits (e.g., cleft palate in mice, umbilical hernia in rats) and associated with reduced fetal growth and offspring survival in animal studies.
- Clinical recommendation: use during pregnancy only if the expected benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus. Poorly controlled asthma in pregnancy poses risks; maintain optimal asthma control.

### Breastfeeding
- Human data on passage into breast milk are not available in the sources.
- Animal data: indacaterol and mometasone furoate detected in milk of lactating rats; adverse effects (reduced pup weight gain, impaired learning, decreased fertility) observed in rat pups exposed to indacaterol during pregnancy and lactation.
- Clinical recommendation: weigh benefits of breastfeeding and maternal need for therapy against potential risk to the infant.

### Fertility
- Indacaterol: no adverse effects on fertility observed in rats at tested doses; however, pups from rats exposed to indacaterol showed impaired learning and decreased fertility at high exposure levels.
- Mometasone furoate: at exposure levels associated with systemic corticosteroid toxicity produced progestogenic effects in female reproductive tract and mammary glands in animal studies; fertility unimpaired in rat reproductive toxicity study.
- Human fertility effects: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Approved for use in adolescents and children aged 12 years and older at the same posology as adults.
- Safety and efficacy have not been established in children under 12 years of age.
- Monitor growth in children and adolescents receiving prolonged treatment with inhaled corticosteroids.

### Elderly
- No dose adjustment required for patients aged 65 years or older. Use standard clinical judgment; monitor for cardiovascular effects.

### Renal impairment
- No dose adjustment required in patients with renal impairment. Specific dosing by eGFR/stage is not provided.

### Hepatic impairment
- Mild to moderate hepatic impairment: no dose adjustment required.
- Severe hepatic impairment: no clinical data available; use only if expected benefit outweighs potential risk.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Indacaterol: a long‑acting beta2‑adrenergic agonist producing bronchodilation via relaxation of airway smooth muscle.
- Mometasone furoate: an inhaled corticosteroid exerting anti‑inflammatory effects in the airways.

### Pharmacokinetics
- After inhaled administration, systemic plasma concentrations of both components are low.
- Clearance pathways:
  - Indacaterol: clearance involves CYP3A4 and P‑glycoprotein.
  - Mometasone furoate: clearance involves CYP3A4.
- Inhibition of CYP3A4 and P‑gp can increase systemic exposures (up to approximately two‑fold for the components) — see Interactions.
- Specifics of absorption (bioavailability), distribution volumes, half‑lives, and routes of elimination are not fully specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Beta2‑agonist class effects: potential cardiovascular effects (tachycardia, changes in blood pressure), hypokalaemia, hyperglycaemia.
- Inhaled corticosteroid class effects: potential for local (oropharyngeal candidiasis, dysphonia) and systemic corticosteroid effects at high doses or prolonged exposure.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Clinical safety profile derived from three Phase 3 studies including a total of 2,497 adult and adolescent patients with asthma treated with the combination at various mometasone dose levels for up to 52 weeks.
- Pivotal studies referenced in available sources:
  - PALLADIUM: 52‑week study — provided cumulative incidence data of adverse drug reactions used for safety profiling and frequency estimates listed above.
  - QUARTZ: 12‑week study — adverse event profile broadly similar to longer studies (some events not observed in QUARTZ).
- Trial designs, primary efficacy endpoints, efficacy outcomes, and detailed statistical results are not specified in available sources.
