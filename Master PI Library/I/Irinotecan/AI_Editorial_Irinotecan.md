# Editorial Product Information: Irinotecan

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of irinotecan product information for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and dosing strategies into a single coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis based on available regulatory data and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the local PI for the most current legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Irinotecan hydrochloride trihydrate
- **Chemical Class:** Topoisomerase I inhibitor (camptothecin derivative)
- **Brand Names:** **Irinotecan Accord** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01CE02
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Concentrated injection: 40 mg/2 mL, 100 mg/5 mL, 300 mg/15 mL, and 500 mg/25 mL single-dose vials [Australian]
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion (after dilution)
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Accord Healthcare Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metastatic Colorectal Carcinoma:** 
    - As a component of first-line therapy for patients with metastatic carcinoma of the colon or rectum [Australian].
    - For patients with metastatic carcinoma of the colon or rectum whose disease has recurred or progressed following initial therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Irinotecan is administered via IV infusion over 90 minutes. Premedication with antiemetic agents is recommended. Prophylactic or therapeutic atropine (0.25 to 1 mg IV or SC) should be considered for patients experiencing cholinergic symptoms.

### Combination Therapy Regimens
- **Regimen 1 (Saltz Regimen):** 125 mg/m² IV over 90 min on days 1, 8, 15, and 22, followed by a 2-week rest (6-week cycle) [Australian].
- **Regimen 2 (de Gramont Regimen):** 180 mg/m² IV over 90 min on day 1, administered every 2 weeks. Includes concurrent leucovorin and 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) bolus/infusion [Australian].

### Single Agent Therapy Regimens
- **Weekly Schedule:** 125 mg/m² IV over 90 min on days 1, 8, 15, and 22, followed by a 2-week rest [Australian].
- **Once Every 3 Weeks:** 350 mg/m² IV over 90 min once every 3 weeks [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Dilution:** Must be diluted in 5% Glucose Injection (preferred) or 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection to a final concentration of 0.12 to 2.8 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Stability:** Diluted solution is stable for up to 28 days if refrigerated (5°C) and protected from light, but should ideally be used within 24 hours to minimise microbiological hazard [Australian].

### Dose Modifications
- Treatment should not begin until the absolute neutrophil count (ANC) is ≥1.5 x 10⁹/L, platelets are ≥100 x 10⁹/L, and treatment-related diarrhoea is fully resolved.
- Subsequent doses should be reduced (usually by 20–25 mg/m² or one dose level) based on the worst preceding toxicity (NCI Grade 3 or 4) [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **irinotecan** or its excipients [Australian].
- Pregnancy and lactation [Australian].
- Women who intend to become pregnant [Australian].
- Severe bone marrow failure [Australian].
- Chronic inflammatory bowel disease and/or bowel obstruction (until resolved) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** Not specified in provided Australian source, though severe toxicities are highlighted as "Special Warnings."
- **Diarrhoea:** 
    - **Early (Cholinergic):** Occurs during or shortly after infusion. Treat with atropine.
    - **Late:** Occurs >24 hours after administration; can be life-threatening. Treat promptly with high-dose loperamide (4 mg at onset, then 2 mg every 2 hours until 12 hours diarrhoea-free) [Australian].
- **Haematology:** Severe neutropenia is common. Deaths due to sepsis have been reported. Monitor WBC, haemoglobin, and platelets before each dose [Australian].
- **Reduced UGT1A1 Activity:** Patients who are homozygous for the UGT1A1*28 or *6 alleles are at increased risk of severe neutropenia and diarrhoea. A reduced starting dose should be considered for these populations [Australian].
- **Interstitial Pulmonary Disease (IPD):** Uncommon but potentially fatal. Closely monitor patients with pre-existing lung disease or those using pneumotoxic drugs [Australian].
- **Extravasation:** Monitor infusion site; if extravasation occurs, flush with sterile water and apply ice [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies vary by regimen (combination vs. single agent).
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Late diarrhoea (88% weekly), nausea (86%), vomiting (67%), abdominal pain/cramping, anorexia, early diarrhoea, constipation.
    - **Haematological:** Leukopenia (63%), anaemia (60%), neutropenia (54%).
    - **General:** Asthenia (76%), alopecia (60%), fever.
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - **Infections:** Minor infections, neutropenic infection (2%).
    - **Metabolic:** Increased bilirubin, weight reduction, dehydration.
    - **Respiratory:** Dyspnoea, increased coughing.
- **Uncommon/Rare (<1%):**
    - Thromboembolic events, myocardial infarction, acute renal failure, symptomatic pancreatitis, and interstitial pulmonary disease [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP3A4 and UGT1A1 Inhibitors:** Co-administration with **ketoconazole** or **atazanavir** increases exposure to the active metabolite SN-38. Discontinue ketoconazole at least 1 week prior to starting **irinotecan** [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** Anticonvulsants (carbamazepine, phenobarbital, phenytoin) and **St. John’s Wort** significantly reduce exposure to SN-38. Avoid concurrent use [Australian].
- **Neuromuscular Blocking Agents:** **Irinotecan** has anticholinesterase activity and may prolong the effects of suxamethonium or antagonise non-depolarising blockers [Australian].
- **Bevacizumab:** No significant pharmacokinetic interaction demonstrated [Australian].
- **Laxatives:** Concurrent use may worsen the severity of diarrhoea [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D** [Australian]: Drugs which have caused or are suspected to cause an increased incidence of human foetal malformations or irreversible damage. 
    - Females of reproductive potential should use effective contraception during and for 6 months after treatment. Males should use contraception for 3 months after the last dose [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Excretion in human milk has been documented; potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Multiple daily doses in animals caused atrophy of male reproductive organs [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Patients ≥65 years are at higher risk of early and late diarrhoea. Close monitoring and lower starting doses (e.g., 300 mg/m² for the Q3W regimen) are recommended [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not studied; use with caution. Not recommended for patients on dialysis [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance is decreased in patients with hyperbilirubinemia. 
    - If bilirubin is >1.5 to 3.0 x ULN, dose reduction is required. 
    - If bilirubin is >3.0 x ULN, use is not recommended [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Irinotecan** is a water-soluble precursor of the lipophilic metabolite **SN-38**. Both molecules bind to the topoisomerase I-DNA complex, preventing the religation of single-strand breaks. This leads to double-strand DNA damage and cell death during synthesis [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via carboxylesterase (to active SN-38) and CYP3A4 (to inactive metabolites APC and NPC). SN-38 is conjugated by UGT1A1 to form an inactive glucuronide (SN-38G) [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Moderate plasma protein binding (30–68%); SN-38 is highly bound (~95%).
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is ~6–12 hours for **irinotecan** and ~10–20 hours for SN-38. Excretion is via urine (11–20%) and bile [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **First-line Metastatic Colorectal Cancer:** Phase III trials compared **irinotecan** + 5-FU/LV vs. 5-FU/LV alone. The combination significantly improved objective response rates (39% vs. 21%), time to progression (7.0 vs. 4.3 months), and median survival (14.8 vs. 12.6 months) in the Saltz regimen study [Australian].
- **Second-line Therapy:** Studies in patients progressing after 5-FU showed a survival advantage for **irinotecan** over best supportive care (9.2 vs. 6.5 months) and over infusional 5-FU (10.8 vs. 8.5 months) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store below 25°C. Do not freeze. Protect from light [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
