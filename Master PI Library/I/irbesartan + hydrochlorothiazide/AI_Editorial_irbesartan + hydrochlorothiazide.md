# Editorial Product Information: irbesartan + hydrochlorothiazide

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **irbesartan + hydrochlorothiazide**
- Chemical class:
  - Irbesartan: non-peptide angiotensin II receptor (AT1 subtype) antagonist (angiotensin receptor blocker).
  - Hydrochlorothiazide: thiazide diuretic (sulfonamide-derived).
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Oral.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources.
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources.
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of hypertension.
  - Note: Initiation with the fixed-dose combination is not recommended (i.e., treatment should not be initiated with this fixed-dose combination).  
- Additional jurisdictional indications or restrictions: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Available fixed-combination strengths described in source material: 150 mg irbesartan / 12.5 mg hydrochlorothiazide; 300 mg irbesartan / 12.5 mg hydrochlorothiazide; 300 mg irbesartan / 25 mg hydrochlorothiazide.
- Dosing principles:
  - The fixed-dose combination should not be initiated as first-line therapy.
  - The combination may be substituted for the individually titrated components at the same dose level.
  - Doses higher than irbesartan 300 mg / hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg once daily are not recommended.
- Titration by clinical effect (examples provided):
  - Use 150/12.5 mg for patients inadequately controlled on hydrochlorothiazide alone or irbesartan 150 mg alone.
  - Use 300/12.5 mg for patients inadequately controlled on irbesartan 300 mg alone or on irbesartan/hydrochlorothiazide 150/12.5 mg.
  - Use 300/25 mg for patients inadequately controlled on irbesartan/hydrochlorothiazide 300/12.5 mg.
- Concomitant therapy: When necessary, the fixed combination may be used with another antihypertensive agent.

### Administration instructions
- Daily dose may be administered with or without food.
- In severely volume- or sodium-depleted patients, correct depletion before initiating therapy.

### Missed dose instructions
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
List of contraindications (all jurisdictions combined; jurisdictional specifics noted when provided):
- Hypersensitivity to **irbesartan**, to **sulfonamide-derived drugs** (e.g., thiazides), or to any component of the product.
- Anuria.
- Concomitant use with aliskiren-containing medicines in patients with diabetes or with moderate to severe renal impairment. [general; jurisdiction attribution per source: stated without regional limitation]
- Concomitant use with ACE inhibitors in patients with diabetic nephropathy. [general; jurisdiction attribution per source: stated without regional limitation]
- Pregnancy (see Section 9). **Use in pregnancy is contraindicated.**

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings
- No boxed/black‑box warnings specified in available sources.

### General warnings and precautions
- Correct volume and/or sodium depletion before initiating therapy in volume-depleted patients; symptomatic hypotension can occur, particularly in patients who are sodium/volume depleted.
- Renal function:
  - Changes in renal function may occur; patients dependent on renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (e.g., renal artery stenosis, severe congestive heart failure) may be at risk of oliguria, progressive azotemia, acute renal failure and/or death.
  - Not recommended for patients with severe renal disease (creatinine clearance ≤ 30 mL/min).
  - Use with caution in patients with creatinine clearance >30 and <60 mL/min (limited experience).
  - No experience in recent renal transplant patients.
- Hyperkalaemia:
  - Concomitant use with potassium-sparing diuretics, potassium supplements, salt substitutes containing potassium, or other agents that increase potassium may raise serum potassium; monitor potassium.
- Dual RAAS blockade:
  - Combining this product with ACE inhibitors or aliskiren is not recommended because of increased risk of hypotension, hyperkalaemia and renal function changes. Use with aliskiren is contraindicated in patients with diabetes mellitus or moderate to severe renal impairment. Use with an ACE inhibitor is contraindicated in patients with diabetic nephropathy.
- Skin cancer risk:
  - An increased risk of non-melanoma skin cancer (basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma) has been observed with increasing cumulative hydrochlorothiazide exposure; photosensitizing properties of hydrochlorothiazide may be a mechanism. Patients should be informed and advised on sun/UV protection and skin self-examination; reconsider hydrochlorothiazide use in patients with prior non-melanoma skin cancer.
- Photosensitivity:
  - Photosensitivity reactions have been reported with thiazide diuretics; discontinue if photosensitivity occurs.
- Intestinal angioedema:
  - Reported with angiotensin II receptor antagonists; present with abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea. Discontinue if diagnosed.
- Hypersensitivity history:
  - Hypersensitivity reactions more likely in patients with past allergy or bronchial asthma.
- Choroidal effusion / acute angle‑closure glaucoma / acute myopia:
  - Hydrochlorothiazide may cause idiosyncratic reaction resulting in choroidal effusion, visual field defects, secondary acute angle-closure glaucoma and/or acute myopia. Symptoms typically occur hours to weeks after initiation; discontinue therapy if suspected.
- Acute respiratory toxicity:
  - Severe acute respiratory toxicity, including ARDS and pulmonary oedema, have been reported after hydrochlorothiazide; withdraw if suspected. Hydrochlorothiazide must not be given to patients who previously experienced ARDS following hydrochlorothiazide or another thiazide.
- Psoriasis:
  - May exacerbate psoriasis or a history of psoriasis; weigh risks versus benefits.
- Metabolic and endocrine effects:
  - Thiazide component can cause hyperuricaemia (may precipitate gout), increase cholesterol and triglycerides (noted to be minimal at hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg), may increase insulin requirements in diabetic patients and unmask latent diabetes.
- Systemic lupus erythematosus:
  - Exacerbation or activation reported with thiazides.
- Heart failure / cardiac arrhythmia:
  - Safety in heart failure not fully defined; sudden death observed in some heart failure studies—use caution. Caution in ventricular dysfunction or arrhythmias.
- Postsympathectomy:
  - Antihypertensive effect of thiazides may be increased.
- Use in hepatic impairment:
  - Use with caution in patients with impaired hepatic function or progressive liver disease; minor fluid/electrolyte changes may precipitate hepatic coma. No dosage reduction thought necessary in mild to moderate hepatic impairment, but use caution in severe hepatic impairment due to hydrochlorothiazide.
- Use in the elderly:
  - No overall differences in efficacy or safety between older (≥65 years) and younger patients in studied populations; no dose reduction generally necessary for irbesartan 150 mg / hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg.
- Paediatric use:
  - Safety and effectiveness in paediatric patients not established.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic monitoring as clinically indicated:
  - Serum electrolytes (potassium, sodium, chloride), magnesium, calcium.
  - Renal function (serum creatinine, BUN).
  - Liver function tests.
  - Blood glucose and lipid profile in at-risk patients.
  - Uric acid in patients at risk for gout.
  - Periodic skin examinations for new or suspicious lesions.
  - Serum lithium levels if co-administered with lithium.

### Specific clinical precautions
- Correct volume/sodium depletion before initiation.
- Exercise caution when co-administering with other antihypertensives, NSAIDs, lithium, potassium-sparing agents or potassium supplements, and agents affecting renal function.
- Discontinue hydrochlorothiazide before parathyroid function tests (thiazides decrease urinary calcium excretion).
- Consider discontinuation if photosensitivity, choroidal effusion, intestinal angioedema, or ARDS suspected.

## 8. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic interactions
- Irbesartan metabolism and CYPs:
  - Irbesartan is primarily metabolised by CYP2C9. Based on in vitro data, no interactions expected with drugs metabolised by CYP1A1, CYP1A2, CYP2A6, CYP2B6, CYP2D6, CYP2E1 or CYP3A4. Clinical studies reported no significant PK/PD interactions with warfarin (CYP2C9 substrate).
- Irbesartan and OATP1B1:
  - Irbesartan has potential to inhibit OATP1B1; clinical data: irbesartan increased repaglinide Cmax and AUC in one study (Cmax 1.8‑fold; AUC 1.3‑fold) when irbesartan given 1 hour before repaglinide; another study showed no relevant interaction when co-administered. Use caution and consider repaglinide dose adjustment when co-administered. [No region-specific distinction provided]
- Digoxin:
  - Irbesartan does not affect digoxin pharmacokinetics.
- Nifedipine and hydrochlorothiazide:
  - Pharmacokinetics of irbesartan not affected by coadministration with nifedipine or hydrochlorothiazide.
- Colestyramine/colestipol:
  - These resins may delay or decrease absorption of hydrochlorothiazide; take irbesartan + hydrochlorothiazide at least one hour before or four hours after these agents.

### Pharmacodynamic interactions and clinically significant interactions
- Potassium-increasing agents:
  - Concomitant use with potassium-sparing diuretics, potassium supplements, salt substitutes containing potassium, trimethoprim-containing medicines, or other medicinal products that may increase potassium can increase serum potassium; monitor closely.
- Lithium:
  - Diuretics reduce renal clearance of lithium and increase risk of lithium toxicity. Increases in serum lithium concentrations and lithium toxicity have been reported with concomitant use with irbesartan; co-administration should be approached with caution and frequent monitoring of serum lithium levels is recommended.
- NSAIDs (including selective COX-2 inhibitors):
  - NSAIDs can reduce the antihypertensive effect of thiazide diuretics and can increase risk of renal impairment when combined with RAAS inhibitors and thiazides; monitor renal function and blood pressure.
- Antidiabetic agents:
  - Thiazides may elevate blood glucose; dose adjustments of antidiabetic agents may be necessary. Irbesartan may induce hypoglycaemia (particularly in patients treated for diabetes); monitor and adjust antidiabetic therapy (e.g., repaglinide, insulin) as required.
- Antigout medication:
  - Hydrochlorothiazide may raise uric acid levels; antigout therapy dosage adjustments may be needed.
- Cardiac glycosides and antiarrhythmics:
  - Diuretic-induced hypokalaemia may accentuate cardiac arrhythmias (e.g., digoxin, sotalol).
- Calcium and vitamin D:
  - Thiazides may increase serum calcium; monitor calcium and adjust calcium/vitamin D therapy if required.
- Combination of ACE inhibitors / ARBs / Aliskiren with NSAIDs and diuretics:
  - Concomitant use can increase risk of renal impairment including acute renal failure; use caution, especially in the elderly, volume-depleted, or pre-existing renal impairment; monitor renal function after initiation and periodically thereafter.
- Diazoxide:
  - Hydrochlorothiazide may interact with diazoxide; monitor blood glucose, uric acid and blood pressure.
- Others:
  - Concomitant therapy with other antihypertensive drugs: thiazide component may potentiate the actions of other antihypertensive agents, especially ganglionic or peripheral adrenergic-blocking drugs.

## 10. Use in Special Populations
### Paediatric use
- Safety and effectiveness have not been established in paediatric patients. Use in children: Not specified beyond "not established."

### Elderly use
- No dose reduction is generally necessary for daily dosage of irbesartan 150 mg / hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg in the elderly. No overall differences in efficacy or safety were observed between older (≥65 years) and younger patients in clinical studies.

### Renal impairment
- Severe renal impairment (creatinine clearance ≤ 30 mL/min): product is not recommended / contraindicated.
- Mild-to-moderate renal impairment (creatinine clearance > 30 mL/min): no dosage reduction generally necessary for irbesartan 150 mg / hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg.
- Limited experience in patients with creatinine clearance >30 and <60 mL/min: use with caution.
- Recent renal transplant patients: no experience reported.

### Hepatic impairment
- Pharmacokinetics of neither irbesartan nor hydrochlorothiazide are markedly affected by mild-to-moderate hepatic impairment; no dosage reduction thought necessary in mild-to-moderate hepatic impairment.
- Use with caution in patients with severe hepatic impairment because hydrochlorothiazide may precipitate fluid and electrolyte disturbances that can precipitate hepatic coma.

## 11. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of action
- Irbesartan:
  - Selective antagonist of angiotensin II at the AT1 receptor subtype; blocks the vasoconstrictor and aldosterone‑secreting effects of angiotensin II.
- Hydrochlorothiazide:
  - Thiazide diuretic that inhibits sodium reabsorption in the distal tubules, increasing excretion of sodium and water; causes diuresis and reduces blood pressure by decreasing intravascular volume and peripheral vascular resistance with chronic use.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Irbesartan:
  - Physical/chemical properties reported: relatively non‑polar, partition coefficient (octanol-water) approximately 10.1 at pH 7.4; slightly soluble in alcohol and methylene chloride; practically insoluble in water.
  - Primary metabolism: predominantly by CYP2C9 (clinical data referenced).
  - Other kinetic parameters (absorption rates, bioavailability, Tmax, half-life, elimination routes): Not specified in available sources.
- Hydrochlorothiazide:
  - Physical/chemical properties reported: slightly soluble in water; freely soluble in sodium hydroxide solution.
  - Absorption, distribution, metabolism and elimination specifics: Not specified in available sources.

### Pharmacodynamics
- Antihypertensive effect results from combined vasodilation/blockade of angiotensin II effects (irbesartan) and diuresis/reduction in intravascular volume/peripheral resistance (hydrochlorothiazide).
- Addition of irbesartan may reduce the hypokalaemic effect associated with hydrochlorothiazide.

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Detailed pivotal trial summaries and primary outcome data: Not specified in available sources.
- Summary of available trial-related observations from source material:
  - In premarketing controlled clinical trials:
    - Minor increases in blood urea nitrogen (BUN) observed in 2.3% of patients treated with the combination.
    - Minor increases in serum creatinine observed in 1.1% of patients; one discontinuation due to minor creatinine increase.
    - Incidence of hypokalaemia (serum potassium <3.5 mmol/L) was 7.5% with the combination versus 6.0% for placebo.
    - Incidence of hyperkalaemia (serum potassium >5.7 mmol/L) was <1.0% with the combination versus 1.7% for placebo.
    - Mean decrease in haemoglobin of approximately 0.2 g/dL in patients receiving the combination (rarely clinically important).
  - No patients discontinued in trials due to increases or decreases in serum potassium reported in available source material.
  - No overall efficacy/safety differences observed between older (≥65 years) and younger patients in clinical studies mentioned.
