# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the official product information for **Levocarnitine** injection. It is intended for use by healthcare professionals as a concise, clinically accurate reference.
- **Note:** This editorial summary is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. The provided source material for this synthesis was limited to the US (FDA) documentation for Baxter Levocarnitine Injection.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring carrier molecule (amino acid derivative) required for mammalian energy metabolism.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **Carnitine Deficiency in End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administer intravenously as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].

### Inborn Errors of Metabolism (Metabolic Disorders)
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
- **Acute/Loading Phase:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by the equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Dosing Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h); frequency should never be less than every 6 hours (q6h).
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended in the range of 50 mg/kg or as clinically required.
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration timing:** Administer as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, potentially down to 5 mg/kg, based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

### Monitoring
- **Baseline:** Obtain plasma carnitine concentrations before initiating parenteral therapy.
- **Frequency:** Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended.
- **Targets:** Plasma free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue the drug and initiate appropriate medical treatment [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with pre-existing conditions, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral vs. IV Formulation in Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high doses of oral levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Monitoring:** Monitor blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations regularly.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

| Frequency | Adverse Effects [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (≥5%)** | **Body as a whole:** Abdominal pain, asthenia, chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain. |
| | **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia. |
| | **Digestive:** Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, anorexia, dyspepsia. |
| | **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia. |
| | **Nervous/Respiratory:** Dizziness, insomnia, pharyngitis, cough increase, dyspnea, rhinitis. |
| **Uncommon/Rare** | Body odor, gastritis, seizures, anaphylaxis, drug dependence. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned/unspecified]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk after exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg) as per adult recommendations [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources; general caution regarding hepatic, renal, or cardiac function is typically advised.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Note the warning regarding the accumulation of metabolites if the oral form is used in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** It promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids in patients with defects in fatty acid metabolism or organic acidopathies by forming acylcarnitine esters which are rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV administration, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine over 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg post-dialysis), trough levocarnitine levels increased from 20 µmol/L at baseline to 190 ± 55 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Studies:** Trials using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks.
- **Note:** While levocarnitine increases carnitine concentrations in ESRD, the effect of supplemental carnitine on clinical outcomes or specific signs/symptoms of deficiency in this population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservative. Levocarnitine is compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s for 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison Schedule: Not applicable.
