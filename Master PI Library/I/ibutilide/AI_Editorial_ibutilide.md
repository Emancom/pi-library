# Editorial Product Information: Ibutilide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis was developed using official Product Information from the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ibutilide for use by healthcare professionals. It aims to consolidate clinical guidance into a structured, professional format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's approved labelling for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ibutilide fumarate (a methanesulfonamide derivative). It is a racemic mixture of (+) and (−) enantiomers.
- **Chemical Class:** Class III antiarrhythmic agent (Vaughan Williams Classification).
- **Brand Names:** Noted as generic **Ibutilide Fumarate Injection** (distributed by Avet Pharmaceuticals Inc.) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection, solution. 0.1 mg/mL of ibutilide fumarate (equivalent to 0.087 mg/mL of ibutilide free base) provided in 10 mL single-dose vials (1 mg total) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Emcure Pharmaceuticals Ltd. for Avet Pharmaceuticals Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Conversion of Atrial Arrhythmias:** Indicated for the rapid conversion of recent-onset atrial fibrillation or atrial flutter to sinus rhythm [US].
- **Clinical Limitations:** Effectiveness has not been established for arrhythmias lasting more than 90 days. Patients with chronic atrial fibrillation are less likely to remain in sinus rhythm post-conversion [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
The dose is administered as a 10-minute intravenous infusion.
- **Patients weighing $\ge$ 60 kg:** 1 mg (one full 10 mL vial).
- **Patients weighing < 60 kg:** 0.01 mg/kg (0.1 mL/kg).

### Administration Instructions
- **Termination of Infusion:** The infusion must be stopped immediately if the presenting arrhythmia is terminated, or if the patient develops sustained/nonsustained ventricular tachycardia or marked QT/QTc prolongation [US].
- **Repeat Dosing:** If the arrhythmia does not terminate within 10 minutes after the end of the initial infusion, a second 10-minute infusion of equal strength may be administered 10 minutes after the completion of the first [US].
- **Dilution:** May be administered undiluted or diluted in 50 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride or 5% Dextrose. Admixtures are stable for 24 hours at room temperature or 48 hours under refrigeration [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ibutilide fumarate or any of the inactive ingredients (sodium acetate trihydrate, sodium chloride, hydrochloric acid, or sodium hydroxide) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning: Proarrhythmia [US]
Ibutilide can cause potentially fatal arrhythmias, specifically sustained polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (Torsades de Pointes), often associated with QT prolongation. In clinical trials, these occurred in 1.7% of patients.
- **Environment:** Must be administered in a setting with continuous ECG monitoring and by personnel trained in the management of acute ventricular arrhythmias.
- **Equipment:** A cardioverter/defibrillator and medications for treating sustained VT must be immediately available.

### General Warnings
- **Monitoring:** Continuous ECG monitoring is required for at least 4 hours following the infusion or until the QTc interval has returned to baseline. Longer monitoring is necessary if any arrhythmic activity is observed [US].
- **Electrolyte Management:** Hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia must be corrected prior to administration to reduce proarrhythmic risk [US].
- **Heart Block:** Reversible heart block (first, second, or third degree) has been reported in approximately 1.5% of patients [US].
- **Anticoagulation:** Patients with atrial fibrillation lasting more than 2–3 days should be adequately anticoagulated (generally for at least 2 weeks) before attempted conversion [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Sustained Polymorphic Ventricular Tachycardia:** 1.7% [US].
- **Nonsustained Polymorphic VT:** 2.7% [US].
- **Nonsustained Monomorphic VT:** 4.9% [US].
- **AV Block:** 1.5% [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Class IA and III Antiarrhythmics:** Drugs such as amiodarone, sotalol, quinidine, disopyramide, and procainamide should not be given concomitantly or within 4 hours post-ibutilide infusion due to the risk of prolonged refractoriness [US].
- **QT-Prolonging Drugs:** Potential for proarrhythmia increases when used with phenothiazines, tricyclic/tetracyclic antidepressants, and certain H1-receptor antagonist antihistamines [US].

### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Digoxin/Calcium Channel Blockers/Beta-blockers:** No significant effects on the safety or efficacy of ibutilide were noted when co-administered with these agents [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Not formally assigned a letter category in the provided source, but noted to be teratogenic (adactyly, scoliosis) and embryocidal in rat studies. Use only if potential benefit outweighs risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion into human milk is unstudied. Breastfeeding is discouraged during therapy [US].
- **Fertility:** No drug-related effects on fertility or mating were observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between elderly and younger patients, though greater sensitivity in some older individuals cannot be ruled out [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance is independent of renal function (CrCl 21–140 mL/min); dosage adjustment is not required [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied; however, as the drug is primarily metabolised via the liver, patients with abnormal liver function should be monitored via telemetry for longer than the standard 4-hour period [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Unlike most Class III agents that block potassium currents, ibutilide delays repolarization by activating a slow, inward sodium current. This results in a dose-related increase in atrial and ventricular action potential duration and refractoriness [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces mild slowing of the sinus rate and AV conduction. It has no clinically significant effect on QRS duration at standard doses [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Large steady-state volume of distribution (approx. 11 L/kg). Protein binding is low (approx. 40%).
    - **Metabolism:** High systemic plasma clearance (approx. 29 mL/min/kg) resembling liver blood flow. Eight metabolites formed via $\omega$-oxidation.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (82% of dose, with 7% excreted as unchanged drug). Mean elimination half-life is 6 hours (range 2–12 hours) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Efficacy:** In pivotal trials, conversion to sinus rhythm usually occurred within 30 minutes of starting the infusion. 
- **Atrial Flutter vs. Fibrillation:** Ibutilide is significantly more effective in converting atrial flutter (success rates 48–70%) than atrial fibrillation (success rates 22–43%) [US].
- **Post-Cardiac Surgery:** Trials in patients 1–7 days post-cardiac surgery showed successful conversion rates for atrial flutter of up to 78% [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F). Keep the vial in the carton until use [US].
- **Admixtures:** Chemically and physically stable for 24 hours at room temperature ($15^\circ$ to $30^\circ$C) and 48 hours under refrigeration ($2^\circ$ to $8^\circ$C).

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only) [US].
