# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial product information is synthesised from official regulatory documentation provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for specific legal and prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); chemical name: (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dose:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or via infusion. 
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less frequently than every 6 hours (via infusion or IV injection).
- **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg.
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2–3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels (normal range 40–50 µmol/L). Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have occurred, particularly in ESRD patients. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks before re-administration [US].
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity of seizures has been observed following levocarnitine administration [US].
- **Metabolite Accumulation:** Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in the urine [US].

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; less frequent reports of body odor and gastritis.
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (see Warnings and Precautions).
- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis and other hypersensitivity reactions [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels in patients on warfarin therapy following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Classification:** Not explicitly assigned a letter category in the current source, but reproductive studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations of levocarnitine after exogenous administration. 
    - **Recommendation:** Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant; consider discontinuing nursing or the drug [US].
- **Fertility:** Mutagenicity tests (Salmonella, etc.) were negative. Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Dosage and Administration for metabolic disorders [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily used to treat deficiency in ESRD patients post-dialysis. Oral formulations should be used with caution due to metabolite accumulation (TMA/TMAO) in patients with severe renal impairment [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine helps clear accumulated acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis) showed a significant increase in plasma carnitine concentrations. Trough levels rose from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg in ESRD patients demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
