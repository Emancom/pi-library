# Originality Audit Report: isosorbide

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 84/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 2
- **Overall Assessment:** Pass

## Verbatim Copied Passages
1. **Passage:** "hypotension induced by isosorbide mononitrate may be accompanied by paradoxical bradycardia and increased angina pectoris."
   - **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 4.4, Page 2 ("Hypotension" subsection).
   - **Location in Editorial:** Section 5 (Warnings and Precautions), bullet point 3.

2. **Passage:** "Isosorbide mononitrate is the major active metabolite of isosorbide dinitrate"
   - **Source:** Document 2 (FDA), Section "Clinical Pharmacology", Page 1.
   - **Location in Editorial:** Section 10 (Pharmacology), bullet point 1.

## Close Paraphrases
1. **Dosage Titration (IR):** The phrasing "starting dose of 5 mg... appropriate for patients of small stature, titrated to at least 10 mg by the second or third day" closely mirrors FDA Page 8 ("A starting dose of 5 mg... might be appropriate for persons of particularly small stature but should be increased to at least 10 mg by the second or third day of therapy"). 
2. **Tablet Splitting (SR):** The statement "insufficient evidence that a halved tablet delivers exactly half the dose or maintains the same release rate" is a slight simplification of TGA Page 1 ("There is insufficient evidence to show that one halved tablet... delivers exactly half the dose of one full tablet, or whether the rate of release is the same").
3. **Renal Impairment (SR):** The guidance "potential accumulation should be borne in mind... in whom a reduced dosage may be appropriate" in the TGA PI is echoed in the editorial's Section 9.

## Originality Strengths
- **Structural Synthesis:** The editorial does an excellent job of organizing two very different documents (one for SR 60mg, one for IR 10/20mg) into a single, cohesive clinical guide.
- **Data Reconciliation:** Section 10 and 11 successfully merge disparate pharmacokinetic values (e.g., 85% vs 100% bioavailability) and clinical trial percentages (rounding 42.7% to ~43%) into a readable summary.
- **Adverse Effects Table:** The conversion of textual data from both the TGA (SR) and FDA (IR) into a unified frequency table (Section 6) is an original and effective synthesis.
- **Regulatory Context:** The addition of the Editorial Note, ATC codes, and the jurisdiction-specific regulatory status (Section 13) shows value-add beyond the source texts.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Harmonisation:** The editorial explicitly contrasts the "Nitrate-low" strategy of the Australian SR formulation with the "Nitrate-free interval" of the US IR formulation, providing context not found in either individual document.
- **Omissions:** The editorial omits highly specific inactive ingredient lists (found in both PIs) and complex chemical nomenclature (empirical formulas) to maintain a clinical focus.
- **Formatting:** Information buried in long paragraphs (e.g., industrial worker warnings, sulfhydryl interactions) has been extracted and bulleted for clarity.

## Recommendation
**PASS.** 

The editorial is a genuine synthesis that provides significant editorial value through harmonisation and formatting. While there is one verbatim technical warning and one near-verbatim dosage instruction, these are standard medical descriptions where accuracy is prioritized over stylistic variation. 

**Suggested Minor Revision:** 
To achieve a perfect score, consider slightly rephrasing the "Paradoxical Bradycardia" warning in Section 5 to break the exact word sequence of the TGA PI (e.g., "Patients experiencing nitrate-induced hypotension may also exhibit paradoxical bradycardia and a worsening of anginal symptoms").
