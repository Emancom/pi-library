# Editorial Product Information: Isosorbide Mononitrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis incorporates Product Information (PI) from the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA, Australia) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA, United States).
- The purpose of this document is to provide healthcare professionals with a harmonised, clinically focused overview of isosorbide mononitrate, reconciling differences between regional formulations (sustained-release vs. immediate-release).
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the local approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Isosorbide mononitrate (an organic nitrate and active metabolite of isosorbide dinitrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Vasodilator.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Isosorbide Mononitrate [TGA]; various generics available [FDA].
- **ATC Code:** C01DA14.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Sustained Release (SR) Tablets:** 60 mg [TGA].
    - **Immediate Release (IR) Tablets:** 10 mg and 20 mg [FDA].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Sponsor/Manufacturer:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [TGA]; ANI Pharmaceuticals, Inc. / Coupler LLC [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Prophylactic treatment of angina pectoris:** Approved for the prevention of anginal episodes [TGA, FDA].
- **Note on Acute Use:** Isosorbide mononitrate is **not** indicated for the immediate relief of acute anginal attacks due to its delayed onset of action. Sublingual or buccal glyceryl trinitrate should be used for acute episodes [TGA, FDA].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
Dosing strategies differ significantly between formulations to prevent the development of nitrate tolerance.

### Sustained Release (SR) [TGA]
- **General Dose:** 60 mg (one tablet) once daily.
- **Maximum Dose:** May be increased to 120 mg (two tablets) taken once daily at the same time.
- **Administration:** Should be swallowed whole with half a glass of fluid. Do **not** crush or chew.
- **Frequency:** Must not be administered twice daily. The once-daily regimen ensures a "nitrate-low" period to maintain efficacy.

### Immediate Release (IR) [FDA]
- **General Dose:** 20 mg twice daily.
- **Asymmetric Dosing:** To avoid tolerance, the two doses must be taken 7 hours apart (e.g., on awakening and 7 hours later), providing a 17-hour nitrate-free interval.
- **Initial Titration:** A starting dose of 5 mg (half of a 10 mg tablet) may be appropriate for patients of small stature, titrated to at least 10 mg by the second or third day.

### Administration Notes
- **Missed Dose:** Not specifically detailed; however, maintenance of the nitrate-free interval is critical for both formulations.
- **Splitting Tablets:** [TGA] notes that while the 60 mg SR tablet is scored, there is insufficient evidence that a halved tablet delivers exactly half the dose or maintains the same release rate.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to nitrates or any tablet excipients.
- **PDE-5 Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with sildenafil, tadalafil, or vardenafil is strictly contraindicated due to the risk of severe hypotension, syncope, or myocardial infarction [TGA, FDA].
- **Soluble Guanylate Cyclase (sGC) Stimulators:** Concomitant use with riociguat is contraindicated [FDA].
- **Acute Cardiac/Circulatory Conditions [TGA]:** 
    - Shock (including cardiogenic shock).
    - Hypotension.
    - Obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.
    - Pericarditis, cardiac tamponade, or aortic/mitral stenosis.
    - Severe anaemia.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Nitrate Tolerance:** Continuous 24-hour nitrate exposure leads to refractory tolerance. Efficacy is only maintained by observing a daily nitrate-free or nitrate-low interval [TGA, FDA].
- **Hypotension:** Severe hypotension, particularly orthostatic, may occur even with small doses. Use with extreme caution in volume-depleted patients or those with pre-existing hypotension.
- **Paradoxical Bradycardia:** Nitrate-induced hypotension may be accompanied by paradoxical bradycardia and increased angina pectoris [TGA, FDA].
- **Acute Myocardial Infarction (MI):** Benefits in acute MI or congestive heart failure are not established. Use in these settings requires intensive clinical and haemodynamic monitoring [TGA, FDA].
- **Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy:** Nitrates may aggravate angina caused by this condition [FDA].
- **Abrupt Withdrawal:** While no rebound phenomenon is clearly established for the SR formulation, abrupt withdrawal is not recommended as it may exacerbate anginal symptoms [TGA].
- **Industrial Workers:** Physical dependence can occur in workers exposed to high doses of organic nitrates; withdrawal can trigger chest pain or MI [TGA, FDA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally related to vasodilation and are most common during the early stages of treatment.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache (up to 30–35%); often diminishes with continued therapy [TGA, FDA]. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypotension (4–5%), dizziness, fatigue/tiredness, nausea, sleep disturbances (6%), GI disturbances (6%), poor appetite [TGA, FDA]. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Tachycardia, vomiting, diarrhoea, vertigo, fainting, heartburn, rash, pruritus [TGA]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Methemoglobinemia (extremely rare at clinical doses) [FDA]. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Myalgia [TGA]. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Syncope, severe hypotension, and reports of myocardial infarction or arrhythmias (reported at <1% frequency) [FDA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **PDE-5 Inhibitors:** Potentiate vasodilatory effects; strictly contraindicated.
- **sGC Stimulators (Riociguat):** Risk of severe hypotension; contraindicated [FDA].
- **Alcohol:** May increase hypotensive and sedative effects [TGA, FDA].
- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** May cause marked symptomatic orthostatic hypotension; dose adjustments may be necessary [TGA, FDA].
- **Sulfhydryl Compounds:** N-acetylcysteine, methionine, or captopril may prolong the exercise-enhancing effects of nitrates [TGA].
- **Propranolol:** Concomitant use in patients with cirrhosis and portal hypertension may significantly reduce portal pressure and hepatic blood flow [TGA].
- **Metoprolol:** No significant pharmacokinetic interaction noted [FDA].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B2:** Safety not established. Use only if benefits outweigh risks [TGA].
    - **US Guidance:** No evidence of teratogenicity in rats or rabbits at high doses. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [FDA].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if isosorbide mononitrate is excreted in human milk. Use is not recommended [TGA] or should be approached with caution [FDA].
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility observed in rat studies at doses up to 120 mg/kg/day [FDA].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established; use is not recommended [TGA, FDA].
- **Elderly Use:** Generally no dose reduction is required unless severe renal impairment is present [TGA]. FDA recommends cautious dose selection, starting at the low end of the range due to potential age-related organ dysfunction.
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - **SR formulation:** Use with caution in severe impairment due to potential accumulation; reduced dosage may be appropriate [TGA].
    - **IR formulation:** Dosage adjustment generally not necessary [FDA].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics in cirrhosis are similar to healthy volunteers [FDA]. Use with caution in settings where portal hypertension is managed [TGA].
- **Haemodialysis:** Isosorbide mononitrate is significantly removed during dialysis, but supplemental dosing is not considered necessary [FDA].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Relaxes vascular smooth muscle, leading to peripheral venous and arterial dilatation. This reduces preload (by increasing venous pooling) and afterload, thereby decreasing myocardial oxygen demand. It also dilates coronary arteries.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and complete from the GI tract (Bioavailability: ~85% for SR [TGA]; ~100% for IR [FDA]). Absorption is not affected by food.
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is ~0.6 L/kg; protein binding is <5%.
    - **Metabolism:** Undergoes denitration and conjugation in the liver; no first-pass effect.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (93% within 48 hours); elimination half-life is approximately 5 hours. Only 2% is excreted unchanged.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Antianginal Efficacy:** Single 20 mg IR doses increased exercise capacity by ~43% at 1 hour and ~25% at 8 hours post-dose [FDA].
- **Sustained Release Data:** SR tablets significantly increase exercise capacity in patients both with and without concomitant beta-blocker therapy [TGA].
- **Tolerance Observations:** In trials, efficacy of continuously delivered nitrates (24-hour coverage) was indistinguishable from placebo after 24 hours [FDA].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [TGA]; 20°C to 25°C [FDA].
- **Protection:** Protect from moisture and light [TGA]. Keep container tightly closed [FDA].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Poisons Schedule S4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **United States:** Rx Only.
