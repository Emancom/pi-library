## 2. Active Ingredient

### Editorial Version
The active therapeutic moiety is isosorbide mononitrate, an organic nitrate and the primary active metabolite of isosorbide dinitrate. It is chemically defined as 1,4:3,6-dianhydro-D-glucitol 5-nitrate, with an empirical formula of $C_6H_9NO_6$ and a molecular weight of 191.14. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

## 4. Therapeutic Indications

### Editorial Version
Isosorbide mononitrate is indicated for the prophylactic management and long-term treatment of angina pectoris (including angina due to coronary artery disease). (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

## 5. Dosage and Administration

### Editorial Version
Dosing strategies for isosorbide mononitrate are formulation-specific and must be designed to include a daily nitrate-low or nitrate-free interval to minimize the development of pharmacological tolerance. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Sustained-Release Formulation (Australian Product Information):**
*   **Standard Dose:** 60 mg (one tablet) once daily.
*   **Titration:** May be increased to 120 mg (two tablets) once daily. Both tablets must be taken simultaneously.
*   **Administration:** Tablets should be swallowed whole with half a glass of fluid. They must not be crushed or chewed. While tablets are scored, there is insufficient evidence to guarantee that a halved tablet delivers exactly half the dose or maintains the same release kinetics.

**Immediate-Release Formulation (United States Prescribing Information):**
*   **Standard Regimen:** 20 mg twice daily.
*   **Administration Schedule:** To maintain efficacy, the two doses must be administered 7 hours apart (e.g., on awakening and 7 hours later). This asymmetric schedule provides a 17-hour nitrate-free gap.
*   **Initial Dosing:** A starting dose of 5 mg (half of a 10 mg tablet) may be considered for patients of small stature, though this should be titrated to at least 10 mg by the second or third day of treatment.

----------------------------------------

**United States Prescribing Information**
• Details the 17-hour gap required for IR tablets.

## 6. Dose Adjustments

### Editorial Version
**Geriatric Patients:** In general, no specific dose reduction is required for elderly patients. However, the United States Prescribing Information recommends caution, typically initiating therapy at the lower end of the dosing range to account for the increased prevalence of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction and concomitant therapies. The Australian Product Information suggests dose reductions only if severe renal impairment is present. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Renal Impairment:**
*   **Immediate-Release:** Dosage adjustments are generally not considered necessary. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Sustained-Release:** While pharmacokinetics of the IR form are not significantly altered by renal failure, data for the SR matrix is lacking. Clinicians should consider the possibility of drug accumulation in severe renal impairment; a reduced dosage may be appropriate. (Source: Australian Product Information)

**Hepatic Impairment:** Dosage adjustments are generally not required for patients with altered hepatic function or cirrhosis. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

## 7. Contraindications

### Editorial Version
Isosorbide mononitrate is contraindicated in patients with:
*   Known hypersensitivity to isosorbide mononitrate, other organic nitrates, or any excipients.
*   **Concomitant use of Phosphodiesterase Type 5 (PDE5) Inhibitors:** Including sildenafil, tadalafil, and vardenafil. Co-administration can cause extreme vasodilation, resulting in severe hypotension, syncope, or myocardial infarction.
*   **Concomitant use of Soluble Guanylate Cyclase (sGC) Stimulators:** Such as riociguat, due to the risk of severe hypotension. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Acute Circulatory Failure/Shock:** Including cardiogenic shock and severe hypotension.
*   **Severe Anaemia.**
*   **Obstructive Cardiac Conditions:** Including obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, constrictive pericarditis, aortic stenosis, cardiac tamponade, and mitral stenosis. (Source: Australian Product Information)

## 8. Warnings and Precautions

### Editorial Version
**Development of Tolerance:** Continuous 24-hour nitrate exposure leads to pharmacological tolerance, rendering the drug ineffective. Efficacy is only restored after a nitrate-free interval of several hours. Clinicians must strictly adhere to prescribed dosing schedules (once daily for SR; asymmetric twice daily for IR). (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Hypotension and Paradoxical Bradycardia:** Even low doses may induce severe hypotension, particularly in an upright posture. This may be accompanied by paradoxical bradycardia and intensified angina. Caution is required in patients with volume depletion or pre-existing hypotension. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Acute Myocardial Infarction (MI) and Heart Failure:** Isosorbide mononitrate is not recommended in the acute phase of MI or congestive heart failure (CHF) as its effects are not easily or rapidly terminated. If used, intensive clinical and hemodynamic monitoring is mandatory to prevent tachycardia and hypotension. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy:** Nitrate therapy may exacerbate angina caused by hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)

**Abrupt Withdrawal:** Although rebound phenomena are not clearly established for the SR formulation, abrupt cessation is discouraged to avoid potential exacerbation of anginal symptoms. (Source: Australian Product Information)

**Cerebral Arteriosclerosis:** Use with caution in patients with severe cerebral arteriosclerosis. (Source: Australian Product Information)

**Industrial Workers:** Long-term exposure to high doses of organic nitrates in industrial settings can lead to physical dependence. Withdrawal in these individuals has been associated with chest pain, MI, and sudden death. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

----------------------------------------

**Australian Product Information**

## 9. Pregnancy

### Editorial Version
**Pregnancy Category B2 (Australia):** The safety of isosorbide mononitrate during human pregnancy has not been established. There is a lack of Segment I and III studies. Animal reproduction studies (rats and rabbits) at high doses showed no evidence of fetal harm; however, in rats, oral doses of 540 mg/kg/day during late gestation and lactation were associated with reduced neonatal survival and development. Isosorbide mononitrate should be utilized during pregnancy only if the potential clinical benefits clearly outweigh the risks to the fetus. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

## 14. Drug Interactions

### Editorial Version
*   **PDE5 Inhibitors (Sildenafil, Tadalafil, Vardenafil):** Contraindicated. Risk of life-threatening hypotension. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Riociguat:** Contraindicated. Risk of severe hypotension. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)

*   **Metoprolol:** Co-administration does not significantly alter isosorbide mononitrate pharmacokinetics. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)

## 17. Overdose

### Editorial Version
**Symptoms:** The primary symptom is a pulsing/throbbing headache. Progressive symptoms include excitation, flushing, cold sweats, nausea, vomiting, vertigo, palpitations, visual disturbances, syncope, tachycardia, and a profound fall in blood pressure. In extreme cases, increased intracranial pressure (confusion, fever), paralysis, seizures, coma, and death may occur. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Treatment:**
1.  **Absorption Control:** Activated charcoal may be administered within 1-2 hours of ingestion if the patient is conscious and the airway is protected. (Source: Australian Product Information)
2.  **Hemodynamic Support:** Place the patient in a supine position with legs elevated to increase central fluid volume. Intravenous normal saline or similar fluids should be administered.
3.  **Vasoconstrictors:** Use of epinephrine or other arterial vasoconstrictors is likely harmful and should be avoided. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
4.  **Methemoglobinemia Treatment:** If clinically significant methemoglobinemia is diagnosed (suspected if blood is "chocolate brown"), the treatment of choice is methylene blue (1-2 mg/kg IV). (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
5.  **Dialysis:** Isosorbide mononitrate is significantly removed during hemodialysis. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)

## 18. Mechanism of Action

### Editorial Version
Isosorbide mononitrate is an organic nitrate that acts as a donor of nitric oxide (NO). NO stimulates guanylate cyclase, increasing intracellular cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP), which leads to dephosphorylation of myosin light chains and relaxation of vascular smooth muscle.

The drug exerts effects on both arteries and veins, though peripheral venous dilatation predominates. This results in:
*   **Reduced Preload:** Peripheral venous pooling decreases venous return, reducing left ventricular end-diastolic pressure and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure.
*   **Reduced Afterload:** Arteriolar relaxation reduces systemic vascular resistance and arterial pressure.
*   **Improved Myocardial Oxygen Balance:** Reduction in cardiac workload and intramural pressure improves sub-endocardial blood flow and oxygen supply/demand.

(Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

----------------------------------------

### Regional Sources

**Australian Product Information**
• Describes organic nitrates as a "physiological substitute" for Endothelium Derived Relaxing Factor (EDRF).

**United States Prescribing Information**
• Focuses on the cGMP pathway and the relative importance of preload vs afterload.

----------------------------------------

### Editorial Notes
• Mechanistic descriptions merged to provide both the cellular pathway and the hemodynamic outcome.

## 19. Pharmacodynamics

### Editorial Version
Dosing regimens must prevent continuous high plasma concentrations. Well-controlled trials demonstrate that antianginal efficacy is lost within 24 hours of continuous therapy regardless of dose escalation. Efficacy is restored only after a period where nitrates are absent from the body.

For IR tablets, antianginal activity begins approximately 1 hour after dosing, with peak effects observed between 1 and 4 hours. For SR tablets, the nitrate-low period achieved with once-daily dosing prevents the development of tolerance. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

## 20. Pharmacokinetics

### Editorial Version
**Absorption:** Isosorbide mononitrate is rapidly and completely absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract.
*   **Bioavailability:** Nearly 100% for IR tablets; approximately 85% for SR tablets.
*   **Peak Plasma Concentrations ($T_{max}$):** ~30–60 minutes for IR; ~4 hours for SR.
*   **SR Release Mechanism:** Gradual, non-pH dependent release via diffusion from a hydrophobic matrix, completed over approximately 10 hours.

**Distribution:**
*   **Protein Binding:** < 5%.
*   **Volume of Distribution ($V_d$):** Approximately 0.6 L/kg (distributed into total body water).

**Metabolism:** Unlike isosorbide dinitrate, the mononitrate does not undergo first-pass hepatic metabolism. It is primarily metabolized in the liver via denitration to isosorbide and sorbitol, and by conjugation to glucuronides. None of the metabolites are vasoactive.

**Elimination:**
*   **Half-life ($t_{1/2}$):** Approximately 5 hours.
*   **Excretion:** Predominantly renal (93% within 48 hours); only 2% is excreted as unchanged drug.
*   **Clearance:** Not significantly altered by age, or by renal, hepatic, or cardiac dysfunction.

(Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

## 21. Clinical Trials

### Editorial Version
**Immediate-Release:** A multicenter placebo-controlled trial in 214 patients with stable angina showed that 10 mg or 20 mg twice daily (7 hours apart) was safe and effective. Efficacy was greatest on Day 1. By Day 14, exercise tolerance remained statistically significant but was approximately half of that seen on Day 1. By Day 21, the effect was 60–70% of the initial response. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)

**Sustained-Release:** Placebo-controlled studies confirmed that SR tablets significantly increase exercise capacity in patients with angina pectoris, both as monotherapy and when added to $\beta$-blocker therapy. (Source: Australian Product Information)

## 27. Patient Counselling Information

### Editorial Version
*   **Efficacy and Schedule:** Patients must be informed that the antianginal efficacy is dependent on following the exact dosing schedule. Missing the "nitrate-free" interval will make the medicine stop working.
*   **Headaches:** Daily headaches are common at the start of therapy and serve as a marker of drug activity. These should be treated with aspirin or acetaminophen. Patients should not alter their nitrate schedule to avoid headaches, as this may lead to loss of anginal protection.
*   **Orthostatic Hypotension:** Advise patients to rise slowly from a sitting or lying position to prevent dizziness or fainting, especially after consuming alcohol.
*   **Administration (SR):** Instruct patients to swallow SR tablets whole and not to crush or chew them.
*   **Acute Symptoms:** Remind patients that this medication will not stop an ongoing heart attack or acute angina episode; they must have a rapid-acting nitrate available.
*   **Driving:** Caution patients about possible dizziness when starting therapy until their individual response is known.

(Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
