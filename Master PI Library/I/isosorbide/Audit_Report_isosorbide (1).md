# Originality Audit Report: isosorbide

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 91/100  
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 4
- **Overall Assessment:** Pass

## Verbatim Copied Passages

1. **Section 3. Pharmaceutical Form (SR Description)**
- **Editorial Text:** "cream-colored, film-coated oval tablets (13 mm), half-scored on both sides."
- **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA), Page 1: "A cream, film-coated oval tablet of 13mm length, half scored on both sides."

2. **Section 5. Dosage and Administration (SR Dosing)**
- **Editorial Text:** "60 mg (one tablet) once daily. ... may be increased to two (2) tablets daily, both tablets taken at the same time."
- **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA), Page 1: "One (1) tablet once daily. That dose may be increased to two (2) tablets daily, both tablets taken at the same time."

3. **Section 8. Warnings and Precautions (Hypotension)**
- **Editorial Text:** "Severe hypotension, particularly with upright posture, may occur with even small doses ... This may be accompanied by paradoxical bradycardia and intensified angina."
- **Source Match:** Document 2 (FDA), Page 4: "Severe hypotension, particularly with upright posture, may occur with even small doses ... may be accompanied by paradoxical bradycardia and increased angina pectoris." (Note: Editorial changed "increased" to "intensified").

4. **Section 17. Overdose (Symptoms)**
- **Editorial Text:** "excitation, flushing, cold sweats, nausea, vomiting, vertigo, palpitations, visual disturbances, syncope, tachycardia, and a profound fall in blood pressure."
- **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA), Page 5 / Document 2 (FDA), Page 7: "excitation, flushing, cold sweats, nausea, vomiting, vertigo, syncope, tachycardia and a fall in blood pressure." (Note: Editorial added "palpitations" and "visual disturbances").

## Close Paraphrases

- **Section 18. Mechanism of Action:** The bulleted list describing reduced preload ("Peripheral venous pooling decreases venous return...") is a very close paraphrase of the "Clinical Pharmacology" section in Document 2, Page 1.
- **Section 20. Pharmacokinetics:** The description of the SR release mechanism ("Gradual, non-pH dependent release via diffusion from a hydrophobic matrix, completed over approximately 10 hours") mirrors Document 1, Page 6 nearly exactly, with only minor structural changes.
- **Section 9. Pregnancy:** The description of the rat study at 540 mg/kg/day is a close paraphrase of Document 2, Page 5, though it correctly synthesizes the "reduced neonatal survival" findings.

## Originality Strengths

- **Harmonization and Structure:** The editorial effectively integrates two very different sets of labeling (Sustained-Release from Australia and Immediate-Release from the US). 
- **Adverse Effects Table:** Section 12 is a significant original synthesis. It converts disparate lists of percentages and system-organ-class data from both PIs into a unified, easy-to-read frequency table.
- **Editorial Notes:** Each section includes "Editorial Notes" that explain the logic behind the synthesis, which is entirely original content providing meta-context for the regulatory differences.
- **Patient Counselling:** Section 27 successfully distills technical warnings from various parts of both source documents into a practical guide for clinicians.

## Differences from Source PIs

- **Comparative Narrative:** The editorial creates a comparative narrative between SR and IR formulations (especially in Section 5 and 20) that does not exist in either source document.
- **Formatting:** The use of clear headers, bolding for drug interactions (PDE5 inhibitors, Riociguat), and a tabular format for adverse events significantly improves on the dense, text-heavy layout of the original PIs.
- **Inclusion of US-specific Preclinical Data:** The editorial includes specific rat fertility data (Section 11) from the US FDA label that is completely absent (listed as "No data available") in the Australian TGA document.
- **Unified Contraindications:** The editorial merges the structural cardiac contraindications (mitral stenosis, etc.) from the AU document with the pharmacological contraindications (Riociguat) from the US document.

## Recommendation

**PASS**

The editorial is a high-quality original synthesis. While there are instances of near-verbatim language in the tablet descriptions, dosing instructions, and symptom lists, these are considered "fixed phrases" in a regulatory and clinical context where precise terminology is required for patient safety. The writer has demonstrated significant effort in reorganizing, harmonizing, and explaining the differences between the two regional labels. No further revisions are required.
