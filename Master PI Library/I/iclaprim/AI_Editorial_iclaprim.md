# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for healthcare professionals, synthesising information from the United States (FDA) Product Information.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not specified in provided source).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Intravenous Injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Pediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours.
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - Maximum recorded dose: 300 mg/kg/day [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administration: Slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments: May be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations (target range 40 µmol/L to 50 µmol/L) [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, particularly in ESRD patients. Some reactions manifest within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, therapy must be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizures:** Both new-onset seizures and increased seizure frequency or severity in patients with pre-existing seizure activity have been reported with both oral and intravenous levocarnitine [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Note):** While this PI concerns the injection, it notes that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
Adverse events reported in ESRD patients regardless of causality include:
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Chest pain (12%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Abdominal pain (9%).
- **Nervous System:** Headache (22%), Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Dyspnea (7%).
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Asthenia (9%).

### Serious and Postmarketing Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis (less frequent).
- **Endocrine/Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (9%), Hypervolemia (5%).
- **Body Odor:** Reported with levocarnitine use.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have been observed when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified in current labeling format]. Animal reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Used for inborn errors of metabolism. Secondary deficiency in premature infants and newborns is defined as plasma concentrations below age-related normals [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specific dosing provided for ESRD patients on hemodialysis. The safety and efficacy of the *oral* formulation have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and [³H]-γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients on hemodialysis results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L.
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks. However, clinical outcomes (signs and symptoms) in the ESRD population related to these increases have not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
