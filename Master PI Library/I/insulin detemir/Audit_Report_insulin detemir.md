## Originality Strengths

The AI successfully identifies a major regulatory discrepancy (TGA’s 28-day vs. FDA’s 42-day in-use discard time) and creates a "Conservative Discard Timeline" recommendation that harmonizes the two. ## Differences from Source PIs

*   **Harmonization of Units:** The editorial consistently uses "units" or "units/kg," whereas Document 1 (TGA) uses "U" and Document 2 (FDA) uses "units."
*   **Abridged Adverse Effects:** The editorial presents a simplified, categorized list of adverse effects (Section 6) rather than the extensive tabular data found in the source PIs (e.g., TGA Table 2, FDA Table 1).
*   **Agency Callouts:** The editorial adds bracketed citations (e.g., `[FDA]`, `[TGA/FDA]`) which do not exist in the source files, to help the reader distinguish between regulatory sources.
*   **Omission of Commercial Details:** The editorial omits specific NDC numbers (FDA) and sponsor addresses (TGA), focusing strictly on clinical and safety data.

## Recommendation

**CONCERN**

The editorial requires **minor revision** to pass an originality audit. 

While the document succeeds in its stated goal of integrating two regulatory sources, several high-risk "Safety Warning" blocks are verbatim copies of the FDA or TGA text. In regulatory writing, precise language is often required for safety; however, for a document to be considered "original editorial synthesis," these blocks should be re-phrased to avoid 1:1 text matches.

**Required Changes:**
1.  **Rewrite the Hypokalemia warning** (Section 4) to use different sentence structures while retaining the clinical risk.
2.  **Rewrite the "Never Share" warning** (Section 4) to use original phrasing (e.g., "To prevent the spread of blood-borne illnesses, delivery devices—including pens and syringes—must be reserved for use by a single patient only").
3.  **Restructure the Pharmacokinetics data** (Section 9) into a more narrative summary rather than a list of extracted statistics.
