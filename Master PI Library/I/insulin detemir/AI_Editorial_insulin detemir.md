# Editorial Product Information: Insulin Detemir (Levemir)

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been compiled by integrating clinical data, safety warnings, and administration guidelines from the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Where regulatory recommendations differ, the more conservative or comprehensive guidance has been cited with the respective agency noted in brackets.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Levemir  
**Active Ingredient:** Insulin detemir (rys)  

Insulin detemir is a soluble, long-acting basal human insulin analogue produced by recombinant DNA technology in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*. It is supplied as a sterile, clear, colorless, neutral solution for subcutaneous injection (100 units/mL).

## 2. Approved Indications
Levemir is indicated for the treatment of diabetes mellitus in both adults and pediatric patients to improve glycemic control.

**Limitations of Use:**
*   Levemir is not recommended for the treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
Levemir is contraindicated:
*   In patients with known hypersensitivity to insulin detemir or any of its excipients.
*   During episodes of hypoglycemia [FDA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions

### Never Share Delivery Devices
Levemir FlexPen or FlexTouch prefilled pens must never be shared between patients, even if the needle is changed. Similarly, vials and syringes must not be shared. Sharing carries a significant risk of transmitting blood-borne pathogens.

### Hypoglycemia
Hypoglycemia is the most frequent adverse reaction. Severe episodes can lead to seizures, loss of consciousness, and death. Patients with longstanding diabetes or those using certain medications (e.g., beta-blockers) may have reduced awareness of hypoglycemic symptoms.

### Hyperglycemia and Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)
Inadequate dosing or discontinuation of treatment—particularly in Type 1 diabetes—may lead to hyperglycemia and DKA, which are potentially life-threatening conditions.

### Medication Errors and Accidental Mix-ups
Accidental mix-ups between Levemir and other insulins (particularly rapid-acting insulins) have been reported. Patients must be instructed to check the insulin label before every injection.

### Administration Safety
*   **Route:** For subcutaneous administration only. Do NOT administer intravenously or intramuscularly, as this may result in severe hypoglycemia.
*   **Infusion Pumps:** Levemir must not be used in insulin infusion pumps.
*   **Dilution/Mixing:** Do not dilute or mix Levemir with any other insulin or solution [FDA].

### Hypokalemia
All insulin products cause a shift in potassium from the extracellular to the intracellular space, potentially leading to hypokalemia. If untreated, this can cause respiratory paralysis, ventricular arrhythmia, and death. Monitor potassium levels in patients at risk.

### Fluid Retention and Congestive Heart Failure
Cases of heart failure have been reported when thiazolidinediones (TZDs) are used in combination with insulin. Patients should be observed for signs of heart failure (e.g., edema, weight gain, shortness of breath). If cardiac symptoms deteriorate, TZD discontinuation should be considered.

### Skin and Subcutaneous Tissue Disorders
Continuous rotation of the injection site within a given area is required to reduce the risk of developing lipodystrophy and cutaneous amyloidosis. Injecting into affected areas may delay insulin absorption and worsen glycemic control.

## 5. Dosage and Administration

### General Dosing
Levemir is a long-acting basal insulin administered subcutaneously once or twice daily. Dosage must be individualized based on the patient's metabolic needs, blood glucose monitoring, and glycemic goals. 
*   **Once-daily dosing:** Administer with the evening meal or at bedtime.
*   **Twice-daily dosing:** The evening dose can be administered with the evening meal, at bedtime, or 12 hours after the morning dose.

### Initiation in Insulin-Naïve Patients
*   **Type 1 Diabetes:** The recommended starting dose is approximately one-third to one-half of the total daily insulin requirement. The remainder should be administered as short-acting pre-meal insulin. A common starting rule is 0.2 to 0.4 units/kg of body weight [FDA].
*   **Type 2 Diabetes:** Initiation is recommended at 10 units (or 0.1–0.2 units/kg) once daily [TGA/FDA].

### Titration and Adjustments
Dose adjustments are necessary with changes in physical activity, meal patterns, renal or hepatic function, or during concurrent illness. In Type 2 diabetes, titration should be based on individual needs; TGA guidelines suggest a specific algorithm based on pre-breakfast self-monitored plasma glucose (SMPG).

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Metabolism:** Hypoglycemia (very common).
*   **Immune System:** Localized or generalized allergic reactions, urticaria, rash, and (rarely) anaphylaxis.
*   **Injection Site:** Redness, pain, inflammation, bruising, and itching.
*   **Skin:** Lipodystrophy and cutaneous amyloidosis at the injection site.
*   **Nervous System:** Peripheral neuropathy (painful neuropathy), often associated with rapid improvements in glycemic control.
*   **Respiratory:** Upper respiratory tract infection (URTI), pharyngitis, and sinusitis were frequently reported in clinical trials.
*   **General:** Peripheral edema and weight gain (though weight gain is typically less with detemir compared to NPH insulin).

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Increased Hypoglycemic Effect:** Oral antidiabetic drugs (OADs), ACE inhibitors, ARBs, MAOIs, GLP-1 receptor agonists, salicylates, and sulfonamide antibiotics.
*   **Decreased Hypoglycemic Effect:** Corticosteroids, thiazides, estrogens, thyroid hormones, sympathomimetics, and growth hormone.
*   **Masking Symptoms:** Beta-blockers, clonidine, and guanethidine may blunt the autonomic warning signs of hypoglycemia.
*   **Variable Effects:** Alcohol and octreotide may either increase or decrease insulin requirements.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

### Pregnancy
**TGA Category A.** 
Clinical trials (including Study 1687) comparing Levemir to NPH insulin in pregnant women with Type 1 diabetes showed similar HbA1c control and no increase in malformations or fetal toxicity. While data suggest no clear drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage, insulin requirements usually decrease in the first trimester and increase during the second and third trimesters.

### Breastfeeding
Exogenous insulin, including detemir, is transferred into human milk. No adverse effects on the breastfed infant have been reported. Lactating women may require adjustments in dose and diet.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Maximum serum concentration is reached 6 to 8 hours after subcutaneous injection. Bioavailability is approximately 60% [FDA].
*   **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (>98% [FDA]; >97% [TGA]), primarily to albumin. The apparent volume of distribution is approximately 0.1 L/kg.
*   **Metabolism:** Degradation is similar to human insulin; all identified metabolites are inactive.
*   **Elimination:** The terminal half-life is approximately 5 to 7 hours, depending on the dose.
*   **Special Populations:** No clinically relevant differences in pharmacokinetics were observed based on gender or race. Clearance may be reduced in elderly subjects [FDA].

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Levemir is a long-acting basal insulin. Its primary activity is the regulation of glucose metabolism. It binds to insulin receptors on muscle and fat cells to facilitate glucose uptake and inhibits hepatic glucose output.

The prolonged duration of action is mediated by:
1.  Strong self-association of insulin detemir molecules at the injection site.
2.  Binding to plasma albumin via a fatty acid side-chain at the B29 position.
These mechanisms ensure a slower distribution to peripheral target tissues compared to NPH insulin.

## 11. Storage Conditions

### Unopened (Not in-use)
Store in a refrigerator between 2°C and 8°C. Do not freeze. Keep in the original carton to protect from light.

### Opened (In-use or carried as a spare)
*   **Vials:** May be stored in a refrigerator or at room temperature (up to 30°C) for up to 42 days [FDA].
*   **FlexPen/FlexTouch:** Store at room temperature (up to 30°C). Do not refrigerate once in use [FDA]. 

**Conservative Discard Timeline:** 
While the FDA allows 42 days for in-use storage, the TGA stipulates that any remaining insulin must be discarded after 4 weeks (28 days) [TGA]. Healthcare professionals should advise patients based on local regulatory standards; the 28-day limit is the most conservative approach.
