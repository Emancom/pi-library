# Editorial Product Information: immunoglobulin (human)

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **immunoglobulin (human)**
- Chemical class: Human normal immunoglobulin G (IgG) (commercial presentation described as 20% w/v in available source)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Subcutaneous administration only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Date of first registration/approval by jurisdiction: Not specified in available sources
- Date of last revision/update of each source PI: Not specified in available sources
- Any withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Replacement therapy in patients (adult and paediatric) for:
  - Primary immunodeficiency diseases (PID)
  - Symptomatic hypogammaglobulinaemia secondary to underlying disease or treatment

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General dosing (adult and paediatric)
- Product contains IgG at 200 mg/mL (20% w/v). Dose is usually expressed in g/kg or mL.
- Loading dose (if required): at least 0.2 to 0.5 g/kg (equivalent to approximately 1 to 2.5 mL/kg). This may be split over several days.
  - Maximal recommended daily loading dose: 0.1 to 0.15 g/kg.
- Maintenance dosing: adjust to achieve appropriate trough IgG levels; cumulative monthly dose generally in the range of 0.3 to 1.0 g/kg.
- Trough target: aim for an IgG trough of at least 5–6 g/L and preferably within the age reference interval. Individualise dose according to pharmacokinetic and clinical response; increase dose if infection rate indicates need for higher troughs.

### Dose conversion for switching regimens
- Switching from other subcutaneous immunoglobulin (SCIG):
  - Weekly dose (g) of this product = weekly dose (g) of prior SCIG.
  - To convert grams to mL: multiply grams by 5 (i.e., 1 g = 5 mL).
- Biweekly dosing: multiply the calculated weekly dose by 2.
- Frequent dosing (2–7 times/week): divide the calculated weekly dose by the desired number of weekly administrations.
- Switching from intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG):
  - Begin subcutaneous therapy one week after the last IVIG infusion.
  - Initial weekly subcutaneous dose (g) = previous IVIG dose (g) divided by the number of weeks between IVIG doses.

### Administration instructions
- For subcutaneous use only. **Do not administer intravascularly or intramuscularly.**
- Inspect product visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration; do not use if observed.
- Do not dilute the product.
- After drawing into a syringe, start infusion promptly; it is suggested to complete administration within 2 hours because siliconised syringes can lead to particle formation.
- Single-patient use only; discard any residue.

### Infusion technique and rates
- Administration may be device-assisted (infusion pump) or manual (syringe).
- Device-assisted:
  - Initial recommended rate: 10 mL/h per infusion site.
  - If tolerated, increase at ≥10-minute intervals up to 20 mL/h per site for the initial two infusions.
  - For subsequent infusions, increase as tolerated.
  - More than one pump may be used simultaneously.
- Manual administration (syringe):
  - Proposed maximum infusion rate approximately 1–2 mL per minute.
  - Adjust rate according to local tolerance.
- Infusion site volumes:
  - In infants/children: change site every 5–15 mL.
  - In adults: doses >30 mL may be divided according to patient preference.
  - No specified limit on the number of infusion sites.
- Patients initiating home or manual administration must be trained and medically stable; manual administration may be associated with increased adverse events.

### Monitoring during and after infusion
- Patients should be closely monitored for adverse reactions throughout infusion.
- Patients who are naive to human normal immunoglobulin, switched from another product, or who have had a long interval since last infusion: monitor during the first infusion and for the first hour after the first infusion.
- All other patients should be observed for at least 20 minutes after administration.
- Record product name and batch number each time of administration to maintain product-patient linkage.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 5. Contraindications
- Known anaphylactic or severe hypersensitivity reactions to the active substance (immunoglobulin (human)) or any excipient.
- Severe IgA deficiency with a documented history of hypersensitivity to human immunoglobulin products (patients with anti-IgA antibodies and previous severe reactions should generally not receive this product).

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed/critical warnings
- **Do not administer intravascularly or intramuscularly.** Accidental intravascular administration may result in shock.
- **Hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis:** True hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylactic shock and sudden hypotension, have been reported, even in patients who tolerated prior infusions. Discontinue immediately if severe hypersensitivity is suspected.

### General warnings and precautions
- Closely monitor patients, particularly at initiation, after switching products, or after prolonged intervals without treatment.
- Start infusions slowly and increase rate only as tolerated.
- Record product name and batch number for each administration.
- Manual administration: patients should be medically stable and adequately trained; increased number/severity of adverse events reported with manual administration.

### Thromboembolism
- Arterial and venous thromboembolic events (myocardial infarction, stroke, deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism) have been associated with immunoglobulin therapy.
- Exercise caution in patients with pre-existing risk factors (advanced age, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, history of vascular disease or thrombosis, known thrombophilia, prolonged immobilisation, severe hypovolaemia, conditions increasing blood viscosity).
- Ensure adequate hydration prior to administration; monitor for signs/symptoms of thrombosis and consider assessing blood viscosity in at-risk patients.
- Inform patients about signs of thromboembolism (shortness of breath, limb pain/swelling, focal neurological deficits, chest pain) and advise immediate medical contact if symptoms occur.

### Renal complications
- Severe renal adverse reactions (including acute renal failure, acute tubular necrosis, proximal tubular nephropathy, osmotic nephrosis) have been reported with immunoglobulin products, particularly those containing sucrose. (The described product does not contain sucrose.)
- Risk factors: pre-existing renal insufficiency, diabetes mellitus, hypovolaemia, concomitant nephrotoxic drugs, age >65, sepsis, hyperviscosity, paraproteinaemia.
- Monitor renal function in patients at risk; do not exceed recommended doses and use the minimum infusion rate practicable.

### Aseptic meningitis syndrome (AMS)
- AMS has been reported following immunoglobulin treatment; onset typically within several hours to 2 days. Symptoms: severe headache, neck stiffness, drowsiness, fever, photophobia, nausea, vomiting.
- AMS may remit within several days after discontinuation of therapy; cerebrospinal fluid may show pleocytosis and elevated protein.

### Haemolysis
- Product contains blood group antibodies which can cause positive direct antiglobulin tests (DAT) and, rarely, haemolysis (delayed haemolytic anaemia or acute intravascular haemolysis).
- Monitor for signs of haemolytic anaemia after therapy.

### Transmissible agents
- Product prepared from human plasma; despite donor selection and viral inactivation/removal steps, potential transmission of infectious agents cannot be completely excluded. Measures are considered effective for many known viruses, but absolute elimination of risk is not possible.

### Effects on laboratory tests
- Passive transfer of antibodies can confound serological assays (e.g., hepatitis A/B, measles, varicella) and tests for red cell antibodies (e.g., DAT/Coombs).
- Administration can cause false-positive results in assays that detect beta-D-glucans for fungal infection diagnostics for several weeks post-infusion.

### Special population cautions
- Paediatric: listed precautions apply to children; product not evaluated in patients <2 years in the available data, though experience with immunoglobulins suggests no expected harm.
- Elderly: no differences in safety or efficacy observed in studied subjects ≥65 years, but monitor those at increased risk of renal or thrombotic complications.

### Monitoring requirements
- Monitor vital signs and observe for hypersensitivity during and after infusion per monitoring guidance above.
- Monitor renal function and urine output in at-risk patients.
- Monitor signs/symptoms of thrombosis.
- Check haemoglobin/haematocrit and hemolysis markers if haemolysis suspected.
- Measure trough IgG levels to guide dosing.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Vaccines:
  - Administration of immunoglobulin may impair the efficacy of live attenuated virus vaccines (measles, rubella, mumps, varicella) for at least 6 weeks and up to 3 months after immunoglobulin administration.
  - For measles vaccine, impairment may persist for up to 1 year; antibody status should be checked in patients receiving measles vaccine after immunoglobulin.
  - Recommended interval before live attenuated vaccines: 3 months after administration of this product; measles-specific consideration up to 1 year.
- Laboratory interactions:
  - Passive transfer of antibodies may produce misleading serology results (e.g., hepatitis A/B, measles, varicella) and interfere with tests for red cell antibodies (e.g., DAT).
  - Product administration can cause false-positive beta-D-glucan assay results for some weeks post-infusion.
- Other interactions: Not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- Pregnancy:
  - Safety in controlled human pregnancy trials not established in available sources; use only if clearly needed and after weighing potential risks and benefits.
  - Immunoglobulins cross the placenta, with increasing transfer during the third trimester.
  - Clinical experience with immunoglobulins suggests no harmful effects on pregnancy, fetus, or neonate, but controlled data not available.
  - Pregnancy category/classification (jurisdictional systems) not specified in available sources.
- Breastfeeding:
  - Immunoglobulins are excreted into human milk and may transfer protective antibodies to the neonate.
  - Use only if clearly needed; balance potential risks and benefits.
- Fertility:
  - Product contains a human plasma–derived native protein and is not anticipated to adversely affect fertility.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Posology for children and adolescents (0–18 years) is not different from that of adults when adjusted for body weight and clinical outcome.
- Clinical studies included paediatric subjects (detailed under Clinical Trials). Product not evaluated in patients <2 years of age in available studies; experience with immunoglobulins suggests no expected harmful effects for children aged 0 to <2 years, but specific data are limited.

### Elderly
- Studies included subjects ≥65 years; no differences in safety/efficacy observed in the available data.
- Monitor elderly patients at increased risk for renal failure or thrombotic events.

### Renal impairment
- Specific dosing adjustments by eGFR or renal stage: Not specified in available sources.
- Use caution in patients with renal impairment; monitor renal function and avoid exceeding recommended doses or using high infusion rates.

### Hepatic impairment

## 11. Pharmacology

### Mechanism / pharmacodynamic action
- Product provides human IgG. Indicated as replacement therapy to correct deficient humoral immunity in primary immunodeficiency and symptomatic secondary hypogammaglobulinaemia. (Detailed molecular mechanism beyond replacement therapy: Not specified in available sources.)

### Composition / notable constituents
- IgG concentration reported as 20% w/v (200 mg/mL) with at least 98% IgG of total protein.
- Reported IgG subclass distribution in the available material: IgG1 ≥56.9%; IgG2 ≥26.6%; IgG3 ≥3.4%; IgG4 ≥1.7%.
- Contains trace amounts of IgA (reported level in source: ≤280 mcg/mL).

## 12. Clinical Trials
- Two pivotal prospective, open-label, non-controlled, multicentre studies evaluated subcutaneous administration in subjects with primary immunodeficiency disorders:
  - Total subjects across studies: 122 with PID.
  - Local adverse reactions were mostly mild; one subject discontinued due to a local adverse reaction.
  - 112 of 122 subjects completed the studies.
  - No deaths or serious adverse events related to the study product reported.
- Paediatric enrolment across the two pivotal studies included:
  - Study 170903: 25 paediatric subjects (5 aged 2 to <6 years; 8 aged 6 to <12 years; 12 aged 12 to <18 years)
  - Study 170904: 28 paediatric subjects (1 aged <6 years; 14 aged 6 to <12 years; 13 aged 12 to <18 years)
