# Editorial Product Information: Irbesartan

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the clinical pharmacology, administration, and safety profile of irbesartan, synthesized for use by healthcare professionals.
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian (TGA) source documentation was provided for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Irbesartan (Angiotensin II receptor antagonist, AT1 subtype)
- **Brand Name(s):** Abisart [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** C09CA04
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Film-coated tablets: 75 mg, 150 mg, and 300 mg [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sun Pharma ANZ Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Treatment of essential hypertension.
- **Diabetic Nephropathy:** Delaying the progression of renal disease in hypertensive type II diabetic patients presenting with persistent micro-albuminuria (≥ 30 mg/24 hours) or urinary protein > 900 mg/24 hours [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):**
    - The standard starting and maintenance dose is 150 mg once daily.
    - If the blood pressure response is inadequate, the dose may be increased to 300 mg once daily.
- **Indication-Specific Dosing:**
    - **Hypertensive Type II Diabetic Renal Disease:** 300 mg once daily is the preferred maintenance dose [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** May be taken with or without food.
- **Special Populations:**
    - **Intravascular Volume/Sodium Depletion:** Correct depletion before initiation or consider a lower starting dose of 75 mg.
    - **Haemodialysis:** A starting dose of 75 mg is recommended [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to irbesartan or any excipients (including lactose) [Australian].
- **Dual Blockade:**
    - Do not co-administer with aliskiren-containing products in patients with diabetes or moderate-to-severe renal impairment.
    - Do not co-administer with ACE inhibitors in patients with diabetic nephropathy [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated during pregnancy (see Section 8).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Hypotension:** Symptomatic hypotension may occur in patients with volume or sodium depletion (e.g., those on vigorous diuretics, salt restriction, or dialysis).
- **Renal Function:** Use with caution in patients whose renal function depends on the RAAS (e.g., renal artery stenosis, severe congestive heart failure). There is an increased risk of oliguria, progressive azotaemia, and acute renal failure.
- **Hyperkalaemia:** Risk is increased in patients with renal impairment, heart failure, or type II diabetes. Regular monitoring of serum potassium is advised.
- **Intestinal Angioedema:** Reported with ARBs; presents as abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting. Discontinue immediately if suspected [Australian].
- **Psoriasis:** May exacerbate psoriasis; weigh benefits against risks in these patients.
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Irbesartan may induce hypoglycaemia, particularly in diabetic patients. Dose adjustments for insulin or repaglinide may be necessary [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Contains lactose monohydrate; unsuitable for patients with galactose intolerance, Lapp lactase deficiency, or glucose-galactose malabsorption.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse events are generally mild and transient. 

### Frequency Category (Hypertension Trials)
| Body System | Common (1–10%) | Uncommon (0.1–1%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, Headache | Orthostatic dizziness, somnolence, vertigo |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Diarrhoea, Nausea/Vomiting | Constipation, flatulence, dry mouth |
| **Respiratory** | Upper respiratory infection | Epistaxis, dyspnoea |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Musculoskeletal pain, CPK increase | Muscle cramp |
| **General** | Fatigue | Flushing, chest pain, oedema |

- **Serious/Post-marketing Effects:** Anaphylaxis, angioedema, hepatoxicity (jaundice, hepatitis), thrombocytopenia, rhabdomyolysis, and impaired renal function [Australian].
- **Specific to Diabetic Renal Disease:** Orthostatic symptoms (dizziness/hypotension) were reported more frequently in diabetic patients than in the general hypertensive population [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **RAAS Dual Blockade:** Combining irbesartan with ACE inhibitors or aliskiren increases risks of hypotension, hyperkalaemia, and renal dysfunction.
- **Potassium-Sparing Agents:** Concomitant use with potassium-sparing diuretics, potassium supplements, or salt substitutes containing potassium may lead to severe hyperkalaemia.
- **Lithium:** May increase serum lithium concentrations and toxicity; monitor lithium levels closely.
- **NSAIDs (including COX-2 inhibitors):** May attenuate the antihypertensive effect and increase the risk of renal impairment (especially in the elderly or volume-depleted).
- **Repaglinide:** Irbesartan may inhibit OATP1B1, potentially increasing repaglinide exposure. One study showed a 1.8-fold increase in repaglinide Cmax when irbesartan was given 1 hour prior [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **D** [Australian].
- **Pregnancy Recommendation:** Drugs acting on the RAAS can cause foetal/neonatal morbidity and death. Use during the second and third trimesters is associated with hypotension, neonatal skull hypoplasia, anuria, renal failure, and oligohydramnios. If pregnancy is detected, discontinue irbesartan immediately.
- **Breastfeeding:** Irbesartan is excreted in the milk of lactating rats. It is unknown if it is excreted in human milk. A decision should be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the importance of the treatment to the mother [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed, though AUC/Cmax may be 20-50% higher than in younger subjects. No dose adjustment is generally required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment needed regardless of degree, unless volume depletion is present. Irbesartan is not removed by haemodialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for mild to moderate hepatic cirrhosis. Data for severe impairment is not specified.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective, non-peptide antagonist of the Angiotensin II (AT1 subtype) receptor. It blocks vasoconstriction and aldosterone-secreting effects of Angiotensin II without requiring metabolic activation. It does not inhibit ACE or affect other hormone receptors or ion channels involved in cardiovascular regulation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed; absolute bioavailability 60-80%. Peak plasma concentrations occur at 1.5–2 hours. Not affected by food.
    - **Distribution:** 90% protein bound. Volume of distribution is 53–93 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by the liver via glucuronide conjugation and oxidation (primarily CYP2C9).
    - **Excretion:** Biliary (80%) and renal (20%). Terminal elimination half-life is 11–15 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **IDNT (Irbesartan Diabetic Nephropathy Trial):** A trial in 1715 hypertensive type II diabetics showing that irbesartan (titrated to 300 mg) reduced the relative risk of the composite endpoint (doubling of serum creatinine, ESRD, or all-cause mortality) by 20% compared to placebo and 23% compared to amlodipine [Australian].
- **IRMA 2 (Irbesartan in Patients with type 2 Diabetes and Microalbuminuria):** A trial in 590 patients showing that irbesartan 300 mg reduced the risk of progression to clinical (overt) proteinuria by 70% compared to placebo [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging.
