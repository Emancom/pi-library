# Editorial Product Information: Idelalisib

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised clinical overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory guidance and clinical data.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official PI. Clinicians must consult the current approved PI in their specific jurisdiction before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Idelalisib (isoform-selective, small-molecule inhibitor of phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase p110δ [PI3Kδ])
- **Brand Name:** Zydelig [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** L01XX47
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **100 mg film-coated tablets:** Oval-shaped, orange, debossed with 'GSI' and '100'.
    - **150 mg film-coated tablets:** Oval-shaped, pink, debossed with 'GSI' and '150'.
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Gilead Sciences Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
Idelalisib is indicated for the following uses [Australian]:
- **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL) / Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma (SLL):** In combination with **rituximab**, for the treatment of adult patients upon relapse for whom chemo-immunotherapy is not considered suitable.
- **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (CLL) / Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma (SLL):** In combination with **ofatumumab**, for the treatment of adult patients upon relapse for whom chemo-immunotherapy is not considered suitable.
- **Follicular Lymphoma (FL):** As monotherapy for patients whose disease is refractory to at least two prior systemic therapies. Disease must be refractory to both **rituximab** and an alkylating agent.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dose is 150 mg taken orally twice daily [Australian].
- **Administration:** Tablets should be swallowed whole and may be taken with or without food.
- **Duration of Therapy:** Treatment should continue until disease progression or the development of unacceptable toxicity.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specifically detailed in the source; standard clinical practice for BID dosing applies unless otherwise specified by local protocols.
- **Dose Modifications:** Required for specific toxicities including hepatotoxicity, bilirubin elevation, diarrhoea/colitis, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, rash, and pneumonitis. Dose reductions generally involve tapering to 100 mg twice daily [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipient (e.g., sunset yellow FCF aluminium lake in 100 mg tablets) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Serious Infections:** [Australian Boxed Warning] Idelalisib can cause serious and fatal infections (e.g., sepsis, pneumonia). Treatment must not be initiated in patients with active systemic infection. 
    - **PJP Prophylaxis:** Mandatory for all patients throughout treatment and for 2 to 6 months after cessation.
    - **CMV Monitoring:** Assess CMV status at baseline. Monthly clinical and laboratory monitoring is recommended for seropositive patients. 
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Grade 3 or 4 elevations in ALT/AST (>5x ULN) are common, typically occurring within the first 12 weeks. Monitor ALT, AST, and total bilirubin every 2 weeks for the first 3 months, then as clinically indicated.
- **Diarrhoea/Colitis:** Severe diarrhoea (Grade $\ge 3$) occurs in approximately 18% of patients. Late-onset diarrhoea (median 1.9 months) may not respond to standard antidiarrhoeals and may require enteric budesonide or systemic steroids. 
- **Pneumonitis:** Fatal cases have occurred. If symptomatic pneumonitis is suspected, interrupt treatment. If organising pneumonia is diagnosed, discontinue permanently.
- **Severe Cutaneous Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), and DRESS have been reported. Discontinue permanently for severe reactions.
- **Intestinal Perforation:** Fatal cases reported. Advise patients to report new abdominal pain, nausea, or vomiting immediately.
- **Hepatitis Reactivation:** Screen all patients for HBV and HCV before starting treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are based on CLL and FL clinical trials [Australian]:
- **Very Common (>10%):** Diarrhoea/colitis, rash, pyrexia, nausea, cough, fatigue, abdominal pain, pneumonia, neutropenia, lymphocytosis (transient pharmacodynamic effect), increased ALT/AST, hypertriglyceridemia.
- **Common (1–10%):** Sepsis, sinusitis, urinary tract infection, bronchitis, stomatitis, arthralgia, decreased appetite, dehydration, insomnia.
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Intestinal perforation, fatal pneumonitis, SJS/TEN, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML), and febrile neutropenia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Effect of Other Drugs on Idelalisib:**
    - **CYP3A Inducers:** Co-administration with strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St. John's Wort) reduces idelalisib AUC by ~75%. Avoid co-administration.
    - **CYP3A Inhibitors:** Co-administration with strong inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole) increases idelalisib AUC by ~79%. Intensified monitoring for toxicity is recommended.
- **Effect of Idelalisib on Other Drugs:**
    - **CYP3A Substrates:** Idelalisib is a **strong CYP3A inhibitor**. It increases midazolam (sensitive substrate) AUC by 440%. 
    - **Caution:** Use with caution when co-administering with sensitive CYP3A substrates or those with a narrow therapeutic index (e.g., alfentanil, sirolimus, tacrolimus, ergotamine).

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. Animal studies show embryofetal lethality and teratogenicity at clinical exposures. Highly effective contraception is required during treatment and for 1 month after the last dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if idelalisib is excreted in human milk. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, breastfeeding should be discontinued during treatment.
- **Fertility:** Animal data show seminiferous tubular atrophy and hypospermatogenesis; however, no effect on mating or fertility was seen in rats at 8-15 times clinical exposure.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children under 18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No specific dose adjustment is required. In CLL trials, patients $\ge 65$ years had higher rates of serious adverse reactions (68% vs 60%) compared to younger patients.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is necessary for mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment (CrCl 15–29 mL/min).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild/moderate impairment. Insufficient data for severe impairment; caution and intensified monitoring are recommended if used.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selectively inhibits PI3Kδ kinase, which is hyperactive in B-cell malignancies. This inhibits the PI3Kδ/Akt signalling pathway, inducing apoptosis and inhibiting proliferation and homing of malignant B-cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma concentrations occur 0.5–4 hours post-dose. Can be taken without regard to food (high-fat meal increases AUC by 36%).
    - **Distribution:** 93% to 94% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via aldehyde oxidase; minor metabolism via CYP3A and UGT1A4.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 8.2 hours. Primarily excreted in faeces (78%) and urine (15%).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study 312-0116 (CLL):** A Phase 3, double-blind study of idelalisib + rituximab vs. placebo + rituximab in 220 patients. The trial was stopped early for overwhelming efficacy. Median PFS was 19.4 months (Idelalisib arm) vs. 6.5 months (Placebo arm) (HR 0.15; p<0.0001).
- **Study 312-0119 (CLL):** An open-label study of idelalisib + ofatumumab in 261 patients. Median PFS was 16.3 months vs. 8.0 months (HR 0.27; p<0.0001).
- **Study 101-09 (iNHL/FL):** A single-arm trial in 125 patients refractory to rituximab and an alkylating agent. In the FL subgroup (n=72), the Overall Response Rate (ORR) was 55.6% with a median duration of response of 14.7 months.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C.
- **Container:** High-density polyethylene (HDPE) bottles containing 60 tablets with a child-resistant closure.
