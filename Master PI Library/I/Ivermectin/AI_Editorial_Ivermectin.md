# Editorial Product Information: Ivermectin

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended to replace the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing within any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ivermectin, a semi-synthetic derivative belonging to the avermectin class.
- **Chemical Class:** Avermectin.
- **Brand Names:** **SOOLANTRA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** D11AX22.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 10 mg/g (1.0% w/w) hydrophilic cream [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical (Cutaneous).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Galderma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Papulopustular Rosacea:** Indicated for the topical treatment of inflammatory lesions associated with rosacea in adult patients aged 18 years and older [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** Apply a thin layer to the face once daily for a treatment course of up to 4 months. The course may be repeated if necessary [Australian].
- **Dosing Instructions:**
    - Use five small, pea-sized amounts (approximately 1 g total).
    - Apply to the forehead, chin, nose, and each cheek.
    - Spread thinly across the entire face, strictly avoiding the eyes and lips.
    - Apply only to the face.
- **Treatment Duration:** If no clinical improvement is observed after 3 months, treatment should be discontinued [Australian].
- **Administration Notes:**
    - Hands must be washed immediately following application.
    - Cosmetics and high-SPF sunscreens may be applied once the cream has completely dried [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to ivermectin or any of the excipients (e.g., cetyl alcohol, stearyl alcohol, methyl/propyl parahydroxybenzoate, propylene glycol) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None identified in the source documentation.
- **General Warnings:**
    - **Excipient Reactions:** Contains cetyl and stearyl alcohol (may cause contact dermatitis), methyl and propyl parahydroxybenzoate (may cause allergic reactions, potentially delayed), and propylene glycol (may cause skin irritation) [Australian].
    - **Rosacea Aggravation:** Patients may experience a transient worsening of rosacea, typically resolving within one week. This is often attributed to a reaction against dying *Demodex* mites [Australian].
    - **Severe Dermal Reactions:** In the event of severe irritation or strong dermal reactions, treatment should be discontinued.
- **Monitoring:** Liver transaminase levels (elevations reported, frequency "not known") [Australian].
- **Specific Precautions:**
    - Not for oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use.
    - Avoid unprotected exposure to UV radiation (including sunlight); use of high-SPF sunscreen is recommended [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Overall, topical ivermectin is well-tolerated. Local skin reactions are typically mild to moderate and transient.

### Related Adverse Reactions (<1% Frequency)
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Skin irritation, pruritus, dry skin, rosacea aggravation [Australian].
- **Frequency Not Known:** Erythema, allergic dermatitis, contact dermatitis, swelling of the face, increased transaminases [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Ivermectin is primarily metabolised by CYP3A4. Caution is advised when co-administered with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors as plasma exposure may increase significantly [Australian]. 
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Concomitant use with other topical or systemic rosacea treatments has not been investigated [Australian].
- **In Vitro Data:** At therapeutic concentrations, ivermectin does not inhibit or induce CYP450 enzymes [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B3 [Australian]:** There are no adequate/well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies (oral) have shown developmental toxicity (cleft palate in rats; carpal flexures in rabbits) at doses significantly higher than the maximum human topical dose. Use only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Ivermectin is excreted in human milk following oral administration; excretion after topical use has not been evaluated. A risk to the nursing infant cannot be excluded. A clinical decision must be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or the medication [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data show no effect on male or female fertility at oral doses approximately 484 times the human topical exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents under 18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dosage adjustment is required. Effectiveness and safety profiles in patients ≥65 years are comparable to younger adults [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not studied in this population; caution is advised [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not studied in this population; caution is advised [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The exact mechanism for treating rosacea is unknown. It is attributed to a combination of anti-inflammatory properties (inhibition of lipopolysaccharide-induced inflammatory cytokines) and its anti-parasitic effect (causing the death of *Demodex* mites via glutamate-gated chloride channels) [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Steady-state is reached within 2 weeks. Peak plasma concentrations (Cmax) occur within 10 ± 8 hours. No systemic accumulation occurs over 52 weeks of treatment [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** >99% bound to plasma proteins (primarily albumin). Distribution across the blood-brain barrier and placenta is limited by P-glycoprotein efflux transporters [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP3A4. Two major metabolites: 3’’-O-demethyl ivermectin and 4a-hydroxy ivermectin [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life averages 6 days [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies 1 & 2:** Two 12-week, randomised, vehicle-controlled trials (n=1371). Ivermectin 1% cream was significantly superior to vehicle in "Success Rate" (IGA score "Clear" or "Almost Clear") and reduction of inflammatory lesion counts (p<0.001) as early as Week 4 [Australian].
- **Active-Controlled Study (Study 3):** A 16-week study (n=962) comparing ivermectin 1% cream (once daily) to metronidazole 0.75% cream (twice daily). Ivermectin demonstrated statistical superiority in reducing lesion counts (83.0% vs 73.7%, p<0.001) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Handling:** Do not freeze [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (S4) – Prescription Only Medicine [Australian].
