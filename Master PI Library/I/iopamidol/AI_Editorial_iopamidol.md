# Editorial Product Information: Iopamidol

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of iopamidol (specifically ISOVUE® Imaging Bulk Package) for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference by clinicians and is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this publication, only the United States PI was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Iopamidol (a non-ionic, water-soluble, tri-iodinated radiographic contrast medium).
- **Brand Name:** ISOVUE® [US].
- **ATC Code:** V08AB03.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Injection:** Clear, colorless to pale yellow solution.
    - **Strengths:** 300 mg Iodine/mL (612 mg iopamidol/mL) and 370 mg Iodine/mL (755 mg iopamidol/mL).
    - **Packaging:** Imaging Bulk Package (IBP) in 200 mL and 500 mL sizes [US].
- **Routes of Administration:** Intra-arterial and Intravenous.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bracco Diagnostics Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Intra-arterial Procedures:**
- Cerebral arteriography (Adults) [US].
- Peripheral arteriography (Adults) [US].
- Selective visceral arteriography and aortography (Adults) [US].
- Coronary arteriography and cardiac ventriculography (Adults) [US].
- Angiocardiography (Pediatric patients) [US].

**Intravenous Procedures:**
- Excretory urography (Adults and Pediatric patients) [US].
- Computed Tomography (CT) of the head and body (Adults and Pediatric patients) [US].
- Peripheral venography (Adults) [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
Dosage must be individualised based on age, body weight, vessel size, blood flow rate, and the specific pathology under examination. Patients must be adequately hydrated before and after administration.

### Adult Dosage Highlights [US]
| Procedure | Concentration (mg Iodine/mL) | Volume per Injection | Max Cumulative Dose |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Cerebral Arteriography** | 300 | 8–12 mL | 90 mL |
| **Peripheral Arteriography** | 300 | 5–50 mL | 250 mL |
| **Visceral/Aortography** | 370 | Up to 10–50 mL | 225 mL |
| **Coronary/Ventriculography** | 370 | 2–50 mL | 200 mL |
| **Excretory Urography** | 300 or 370 | 50–100 mL | — |
| **CT (Head/Body)** | 300 or 370 | 80–200 mL | — |
| **Peripheral Venography** | 300 | 15–100 mL/limb | 230 mL |

### Pediatric Dosage Highlights [US]
- **Angiocardiography (IA):** 0.5 mL/kg to 2 mL/kg per injection. Cumulative doses must not exceed age-specific limits (e.g., 40 mL for <2 years; 125 mL for 10–18 years).
- **Excretory Urography (IV):** 1 mL/kg to 3 mL/kg; maximum 100 mL.
- **CT Head/Body (IV):** 1 mL/kg to 3 mL/kg; maximum 100 mL.

### Administration Instructions
- **IBP Specifics:** Use only with automated contrast injection systems or approved transfer sets. 
- **Handling:** Use aseptic technique. Swab stopper with 70% isopropyl alcohol. Penetrate the closure only once.
- **Stability:** Once punctured, the IBP must be used within 10 hours. Discard unused portions.
- **Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration. Do not use if present.
- **Temperature:** May be administered at room temperature (20°C–25°C) or warmed to body temperature (37°C).

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available [US] source.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **BOXED WARNING (Intrathecal Risk):** Inadvertent intrathecal administration of ISOVUE can be fatal. It may cause convulsions, cerebral hemorrhage, coma, paralysis, arachnoiditis, acute renal failure, cardiac arrest, rhabdomyolysis, hyperthermia, and brain edema. This product is for intra-arterial or intravenous use only [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Life-threatening or fatal anaphylactic reactions can occur. Risks are higher in patients with previous contrast reactions, asthma, or multiple allergies. Resuscitation equipment must be immediately available.
- **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI):** Iodinated contrast can cause renal failure. Risk factors include pre-existing renal impairment, diabetes, dehydration, and high doses. Use the lowest necessary dose.
- **Cardiovascular Risk:** Can cause hemodynamic disturbances, shock, cardiac arrest, arrhythmias, and myocardial ischemia.
- **Thromboembolic Events:** Serious events (MI, stroke) may occur during angiographic procedures. Use meticulous technique and avoid prolonged contact between blood and the contrast media in syringes.
- **Thyroid Dysfunction:**
    - **Pediatric (0–3 years):** Risk of hypothyroidism or transient thyroid suppression. Monitor thyroid function based on risk factors (e.g., prematurity) [US].
    - **Thyroid Storm:** Risk in patients with hyperthyroidism or autonomous thyroid nodules.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** Stevens-Johnson syndrome, TEN, AGEP, and DRESS may occur from 1 hour to several weeks post-administration.
- **Special Populations Warning:** Hypertensive crisis may occur in patients with pheochromocytoma. Sickle cell crisis may be promoted by intravascular administration in homozygous patients.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common (Incidence >1%) [US]
- Pain (2.8%)
- Hot flashes (1.5%)
- Burning sensation (1.4%)
- Nausea (1.2%)
- Warmth (1.1%)

### Serious or Uncommon (≤1% or Postmarketing)
- **Cardiovascular:** Cardiac arrest, tachycardia, hypotension, myocardial infarction, circulatory collapse, ventricular fibrillation.
- **Respiratory:** Throat constriction, dyspnea, pulmonary edema, bronchospasm, respiratory arrest.
- **Neurological:** Seizures, coma, syncope, tremors, encephalopathy.
- **Dermatological:** Rash, urticaria, pruritus, SCAR (SJS/TEN).
- **Other:** Taste alterations, visual disturbances, urinary retention, injection site reactions (pain, swelling, extravasation leads to necrosis).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Metformin:** In patients with renal impairment, iopamidol can worsen renal function, increasing the risk of metformin-induced lactic acidosis. Discontinue metformin at the time of or prior to the procedure for patients with eGFR 30–60 mL/min/1.73m² or those receiving IA contrast. Re-evaluate renal function after 48 hours [US].
- **Radioactive Iodine:** Iopamidol may interfere with thyroid uptake of I-131 or I-123. Avoid thyroid therapy or testing for 6 weeks following administration.
- **Laboratory Tests:** Can interfere with protein-bound iodine tests for up to 16 days. Thyroid function tests not dependent on iodine estimations (e.g., T3 resin uptake, free T4) are unaffected.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Not formally categorised under lettered system in current labeling]. Data from decades of use have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. Iopamidol crosses the placenta.
- **Breastfeeding:** Iopamidol is present in human milk in very low amounts and is poorly absorbed by the infant's GI tract. Interruption of breastfeeding is not required, but a lactating woman may choose to pump and discard milk for 10 hours post-dose to minimize exposure [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies in rats showed no adverse effects on fertility or reproductive performance.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Established for angiocardiography (IA), excretory urography (IV), and CT (IV). Safety for other procedures (e.g., cerebral/peripheral arteriography) is not established. Use with caution in neonates and patients <12 months [US].
- **Geriatric Use:** Patients ≥65 years are at higher risk for renal impairment. Care should be taken with dose selection and renal monitoring.
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance decreases with the severity of impairment. Patients are at increased risk for AKI. Iopamidol can be removed via dialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified as a primary contraindication, but caution is advised in patients with combined renal and hepatic disease [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The iodine atoms attenuate X-rays in proportion to the contrast concentration, allowing for visualization of blood vessels and body structures.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Enhancement is related to iodine concentration; however, time course and exposure-response relationships are not fully characterized.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Rapidly distributes into vascular and extracellular fluid. It does not significantly bind to serum proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Does not undergo significant metabolism or deiodination.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily renal. In patients with normal renal function, ~80–90% is excreted in urine within 8 hours. The plasma half-life is approximately 2 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Adult Safety Study:** Evaluated 2,246 adult patients. The most frequent reactions were pain and thermal sensations [US].
- **Pediatric Study:** In a trial of 76 pediatric patients undergoing angiocardiography, 2.6% experienced worsening cyanosis and peripheral perfusion [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Light:** Protect from light.
- **Shelf Life:** After penetration of the IBP closure, the product must be used or discarded within 10 hours.

- **United States:** Prescription only (Rx only).
