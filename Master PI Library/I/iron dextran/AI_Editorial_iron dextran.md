# Editorial Product Information: Iron Dextran

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Iron dextran (a complex of ferric hydroxide and dextran).
- **Chemical Class:** Parenteral iron replacement product.
- **Brand Name(s):** **INFeD** [US].
- **ATC Code:** B03AC [Not specified in available source, provided for reference].
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile, slightly viscous dark brown liquid for injection; 100 mg elemental iron per 2 mL (50 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) or Intramuscular (IM) injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Patheon Italia S.p.A. (Manufacturer); AbbVie Inc. (Distributor); Henry Schein, Inc. (Repacker) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Iron Deficiency:** Treatment of documented iron deficiency in adult and paediatric patients aged 4 months and older who meet the following criteria [US]:
    - Intolerance to oral iron therapy.
    - Unsatisfactory clinical response to oral iron therapy.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 General Pre-treatment Requirements
- Discontinue all oral iron products prior to **INFeD** administration.
- Establish baseline haematological parameters (Hb, Hct) and iron stores (serum iron, TIBC, transferrin saturation).
- **Test Dose:** A test dose of 0.5 mL must be administered prior to the first therapeutic dose via the intended route (IV or IM). Resuscitation equipment must be available. Observe the patient for at least one hour before administering the remaining dose [US].

### 3.2 Dosing for Iron Deficiency Anemia
The total dose required for restoration of haemoglobin and replenishment of iron stores is calculated based on Lean Body Weight (LBW) and observed haemoglobin. 

**Standard Formulas:**
- **Adults and Children >15 kg:** 
  $Dose (mL) = 0.0442 \times (Desired Hb - Observed Hb) \times LBW + (0.26 \times LBW)$
  *(Desired Hb for >15 kg is 14.8 g/dL)*
- **Children 5 to 15 kg:** 
  $Dose (mL) = 0.0442 \times (Desired Hb - Observed Hb) \times W + (0.26 \times W)$
  *(Desired Hb for children $\le$15 kg is 12 g/dL; W = body weight)*

### 3.3 Dosing for Blood Loss
Iron required (mg) = [Blood loss (mL) $\times$ haematocrit]
- **INFeD** Dose (mL) = Replacement Iron (mg) / 50 mg/mL.

### 3.4 Administration Instructions
- **Intravenous:** Administer undiluted at a rate not exceeding 50 mg (1 mL) per minute. Maximum daily dose is 2 mL [US].
- **Intramuscular:** Inject only into the upper outer quadrant of the buttock using a Z-track technique (2-inch or 3-inch 19/20 gauge needle). Never inject into the arm or exposed areas.
    - **Max Daily IM Dose:** Infants <5 kg: 0.5 mL; Children <10 kg: 1 mL; Others: 2 mL [US].
- **Note:** Do not mix with other medications or add to parenteral nutrition.

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to iron dextran [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### 5.1 Boxed Warning: Anaphylactic-Type Reactions [US]
- **Fatal reactions** have occurred following parenteral administration, even in patients who tolerated the test dose.
- Administration must only occur in settings with immediate access to resuscitation equipment and personnel trained in treating anaphylaxis.
- Patients with a history of drug allergies or multiple allergies are at increased risk.

### 5.2 Delayed Reactions
- Typically occur 24–48 hours post-administration (more common with large IV doses); symptoms include arthralgia, backache, chills, fever, headache, and myalgia. Symptoms usually subside within 3–4 days [US].

### 5.3 Special Precautions
- **Liver Impairment:** Monitor for iron toxicity in patients with serious hepatic dysfunction [US].
- **Infectious Kidney Disease:** Avoid use during the acute phase.
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** Adverse reactions may exacerbate pre-existing cardiovascular complications.
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** May cause acute exacerbation of joint pain and swelling.
- **Iron Overload:** Excessive therapy causes iatrogenic hemosiderosis. Do not treat patients with evidence of iron overload.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Most Common:** Nausea, vomiting, chest pain, backache, dizziness, pruritus, flushing, hypotension, and dyspnea [US].
- **Serious Reactions:**
    - **Cardiovascular:** Cardiac arrest, tachycardia, shock, acute myocardial ischemia, in-stent thrombosis.
    - **Respiratory:** Respiratory arrest, bronchospasm, wheezing.
    - **Neurological:** Convulsions, seizures, syncope, unconsciousness.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Abdominal pain, diarrhoea.
    - **Administration Site:** Sterile abscess, skin staining (brown discolouration), inflammation, or atrophy (IM site).
- **Fetal Safety:** Fetal bradycardia has been reported following maternal hypersensitivity [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **ACE Inhibitors:** Concomitant use may increase the risk of anaphylactic-type reactions [US].
- **Drug/Laboratory Interference:** 
    - **Serum Bilirubin:** May cause falsely elevated values.
    - **Serum Calcium:** May cause falsely decreased values.
    - **Serum Iron:** Colorimetric assays may be unreliable for 3 weeks post-administration.
    - **Bone Scans:** May show dense crescentic activity in the buttocks (IM) or reduced bony uptake and excessive blood pool activity in the presence of high ferritin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Category:** Not explicitly lettered in revised US labeling, but notes potential for fetal bradycardia and serious maternal/fetal consequences following hypersensitivity reactions.
    - **Data:** Animal studies show teratogenicity/embryocidal effects at 3x human doses. Human data is insufficient to assess major birth defect risks [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Trace amounts of unmetabolized iron dextran are excreted in human milk. Benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need and potential risks [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Indicated for $\ge$4 months of age.
    - **Neonatal Warning:** Use in neonates (particularly IM) has been associated with increased gram-negative sepsis (primarily *E. coli*) [Note: Source attributes this to reports from New Zealand].
- **Elderly:** Clinical studies did not identify specific differences in response, though caution is generally advised for age-related organ dysfunction [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Iron dextran is not removed by haemodialysis.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Increased risk of toxicity; require careful monitoring [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The iron dextran complex is split by the reticuloendothelial system into iron and dextran. The released iron replenishes depleted stores and is incorporated into haemoglobin [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Therapeutic response is evidenced by increased reticulocyte counts and serum ferritin levels.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Following IM injection, majority absorbed within 72 hours; remainder over 3–4 weeks via capillaries and lymphatics.
    - **Distribution:** Bound to ferritin or hemosiderin; minor binding to transferrin.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life of total iron (bound and circulating) is approximately 20 hours. Negligible urinary or alimentary excretion [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Inspect visually for particulate matter or discolouration. Discard unused portions.
