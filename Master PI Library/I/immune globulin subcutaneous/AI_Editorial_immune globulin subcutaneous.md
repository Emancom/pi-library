# Editorial Product Information: immune globulin subcutaneous

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **immune globulin subcutaneous**
- Chemical class: Human immunoglobulin G (IgG) concentrate for replacement therapy (20% solution; 200 mg/mL).  
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources.
- Route(s) of administration: Subcutaneous infusion only.

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2019 [US].
- Most recent revision/update of available source PI: July 2024 [US].
- Withdrawals, suspensions, or restrictions: Not specified in available sources.

## 3. Approved Indications
- Treatment of primary humoral immunodeficiency (primary immunodeficiency requiring IgG replacement) in patients 2 years of age and older. This includes, but is not limited to, congenital agammaglobulinemia, common variable immunodeficiency, X‑linked agammaglobulinemia, Wiskott‑Aldrich syndrome, and severe combined immunodeficiencies. [US]
- Use for indications beyond primary humoral immunodeficiency or approvals in other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Dosage and Administration

### General principles
- Product concentration: 20% (200 mg/mL); to convert grams of IgG to mL, multiply grams by 5.
- Dosing intervals supported: from daily up to every two weeks (biweekly), administered regularly to achieve steady‑state exposure.
- Individualize dose by clinical response and serum IgG trough concentrations; monitor troughs to guide adjustments.

### Switching from intravenous immune globulin (IVIG)
- Begin subcutaneous therapy one week after the last IVIG infusion.
- Calculate the initial weekly subcutaneous dose:
  - Initial weekly dose (g) = Prior IVIG dose (g) ÷ Number of weeks between IVIG infusions × 1.37 (dose adjustment factor).
  - Convert to mL: grams × 5 = mL.
- Alternative regular dosing intervals:
  - Frequent dosing (2–7 times/week): divide weekly dose by number of administrations/week.
  - Biweekly dosing: multiply weekly dose by 2.

### Switching from prior subcutaneous immune globulin (IGSC)
- Use the same weekly dose in grams as the prior IGSC regimen.
- Convert grams to mL by multiplying by 5.
- Alternative regular dosing intervals handled as above.

### Treatment‑naïve patients (primary immunodeficiency)
- Loading: 150 mg/kg/day (0.15 g/kg/day) for 5 consecutive days.
- Maintenance: begin weekly administrations of 150 mg/kg/week starting on Day 8.
- Monitor IgG trough levels every 2 weeks for the first 8 weeks of dosing.

### Dose adjustments
- Adjust weekly dose based on the difference between the patient’s actual serum IgG trough and the target trough.

### Administration technique and limits
- Prior to administration, allow product to reach ambient room temperature. Do not shake. Do not dilute. Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration; do not use if cloudy or turbid.
- Use aseptic technique. Prime administration tubing with product to expel air. Ensure no air remains in tubing/needle.
- Injection angle: insert needle at 90° into subcutaneous tissue while pinching skin (pinch at least 1 inch/2.5 cm).
- After insertion, check for blood return; if blood is present, remove and discard needle and tubing and select a new site.
- Up to 6 infusion sites may be used simultaneously. Sites should be at least 2 inches (5 cm) apart and avoid bony prominences, large superficial veins, scars, inflamed or infected areas.
- Maximum volume per infusion site: 25 mL for all ages.
- Maximum infusion rate per infusion site:
  - Children 2 to <10 years: ≤25 mL/hour per site.
  - Adults and children ≥10 years: ≤35 mL/hour per site.
- Record infusion details (lot number, expiry, dose, date/time, site(s), adverse effects) in patient treatment log.
- Discard partially used vials; vial is single‑use only.
- Timing after vial entry/transfer:
  - Administer within 8 hours after beginning infusion preparation (i.e., once transferred to a syringe).
  - If using siliconized syringes, use as soon as practicable and within 2 hours to minimise particle formation.
- Do not mix with other medications, including other immune globulin products.

## 5. Contraindications
- History of anaphylactic or severe systemic reactions to human immune globulin products.  
- Known hypersensitivity to any inactive ingredient contained in the product (example listed: polysorbate 80).  
- IgA‑deficient patients with documented anti‑IgA antibodies and a history of hypersensitivity to human immune globulin: contraindicated.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions

### Boxed/major warnings
- **Thrombosis:** Thrombosis has been reported with immune globulin products, including subcutaneous formulations. Risk factors include advanced age, prolonged immobilisation, hypercoagulable states, prior venous or arterial thrombosis, estrogen use, indwelling central vascular catheters, hyperviscosity, and cardiovascular risk factors. Thrombosis may occur in the absence of known risk factors. For patients at risk, administer the minimum effective dose and the lowest infusion rate practicable; ensure adequate hydration before administration and monitor for signs/symptoms of thrombosis. [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- Aseptic meningitis syndrome (AMS) has been reported within hours to 2 days after immune globulin administration; consider AMS in patients with severe headache, nuchal rigidity, photophobia, fever, and perform appropriate neurologic evaluation including CSF studies where indicated.
- Hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, may occur. Patients with anti‑IgA antibodies are at increased risk.
- Renal dysfunction/failure, acute tubular necrosis and osmotic nephrosis have been reported with certain IVIG products (notably those containing sucrose). The product described does not contain sucrose; nonetheless, ensure patients are not volume depleted prior to administration, monitor renal function in patients with preexisting renal insufficiency or risk factors (e.g., diabetes, age >65, sepsis, paraproteinemia, nephrotoxic drugs), and consider lower, more frequent dosing in at‑risk patients.
- Hemolysis (delayed or acute) has been reported; risk factors include high doses (≥2 g/kg), non‑O blood group, and underlying inflammatory states. Monitor for clinical signs of hemolysis and perform confirmatory laboratory testing if suspected.
- Transfusion‑related acute lung injury (TRALI) has been reported; typically occurs within 1–6 hours of administration and presents with noncardiogenic pulmonary edema, hypoxemia and respiratory distress. Monitor and manage supportively (oxygen, ventilatory support) if suspected.
- Because product is derived from human plasma, there is a theoretical risk of transmission of infectious agents (viruses, variant Creutzfeldt‑Jakob disease particle). No cases linked to the product are reported in the available sources.
- Passive transfer of antibodies can interfere with serologic testing and live vaccine responses; plan vaccinations accordingly and interpret serologic tests with caution.
- Geriatric patients: in patients >65 years, do not exceed recommended doses and infuse at the minimum rate practicable.

### Monitoring requirements
- Baseline and periodic monitoring of serum IgG trough concentrations to guide dose and interval adjustments (frequent monitoring every 2 weeks during initial 8 weeks for treatment‑naïve patients).
- Baseline and periodic renal function monitoring (BUN/serum creatinine, urine output) in patients at risk for renal dysfunction.
- Assess for signs/symptoms of thrombosis; consider baseline blood viscosity assessment in patients with cryoglobulins, marked hypertriglyceridemia, or monoclonal gammopathies.
- Monitor for signs of hemolysis and perform direct antiglobulin (Coombs’) testing if clinically indicated.
- Observe for hypersensitivity and infusion‑related local and systemic reactions during and after infusion.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Passive transfer of antibodies may transiently interfere with serologic testing results and may confound interpretation of serologic assays (including blood typing and direct/indirect Coombs’ tests).
- Immunization: passive antibody transfer can transiently interfere with the efficacy of live attenuated virus vaccines (examples cited: measles, mumps, rubella, varicella); plan timing of vaccinations and antibody administration accordingly.

## 10. Use in Special Populations

### Paediatric use
- Approved for use in patients aged 2 years and older for primary humoral immunodeficiency [US].
- Pediatric administration parameters: maximum 25 mL per infusion site; maximum rate ≤25 mL/hr per site for ages 2 to <10 years; up to 6 infusion sites; rotate and select fewer sites or smaller volume/site as clinically appropriate for children.
