# Editorial Product Information: Isradipine

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) from the **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating regulatory information into a single, clinically focused reference. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is a clinical synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Isradipine (a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker).
- **Chemical Class:** 3,5-Pyridinedicarboxylic acid, 4-(4-benzofurazanyl)-1,4-dihydro-2,6-dimethyl-, methyl 1-methylethyl ester.
- **Brand Name(s):** Isradipine Capsules (generic) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Oral Capsules:** 2.5 mg and 5 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Teva Pharmaceuticals USA, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** Indicated for the management of hypertension. It may be utilised as monotherapy or concurrently with thiazide-type diuretics [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** 
    - **Initial Dose:** 2.5 mg twice daily (b.i.d.) [US].
    - **Maintenance:** If an adequate blood pressure response is not achieved after 2–4 weeks, the dose may be adjusted in increments of 5 mg/day at intervals of 2–4 weeks [US].
- **Maximum Dose:** 20 mg/day [US].
- **Note:** Most patients do not show additional therapeutic response at doses exceeding 10 mg/day, and the frequency of adverse effects increases above this threshold [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** The capsules should be taken orally. Food increases the time to reach peak concentrations by approximately one hour but does not affect the total bioavailability (AUC) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to isradipine or any of the inactive ingredients in the formulation (e.g., anhydrous lactose, gelatin, sodium lauryl sulfate) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None [US].
- **Symptomatic Hypotension:** Due to the reduction in peripheral resistance, isradipine may occasionally cause symptomatic hypotension. Syncope and severe dizziness are rare but possible, particularly during initial titration [US].
- **Congestive Heart Failure (CHF):** While isradipine reduces afterload without impairing contractility in most patients, it exhibits negative inotropic effects *in vitro* at high doses. Caution is advised when treating patients with CHF, especially when combined with beta-blockers [US].
- **Monitoring:** Periodic monitoring of blood pressure is required during dose titration.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Most adverse reactions are mild, transient, and related to the vasodilatory properties of the drug.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache (13.7%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness (7.3%), Edema (7.2%), Palpitations (4.0%), Fatigue (3.9%), Flushing (2.6%), Chest Pain (2.4%), Nausea (1.8%), Dyspnea (1.8%), Abdominal Discomfort (1.7%), Tachycardia (1.5%), Rash (1.5%), Pollakiuria (1.5%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pruritus, urticaria, cramps in legs/feet, cough, hypotension, constipation, diarrhea, drowsiness, insomnia, depression [US]. |
| **Rare (<0.1%) / Serious** | Atrial fibrillation, ventricular fibrillation, myocardial infarction, heart failure, transient ischemic attack, stroke [US]. |

*Note: Edema, palpitations, fatigue, and flushing are dose-related, particularly at doses of 15–20 mg/day [US].*

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cimetidine:** Concurrent use increases isradipine mean peak plasma concentrations (36%) and AUC (50%). Downward dose adjustment of isradipine may be required [US].
- **Rifampicin:** Significantly reduces or abolishes isradipine plasma levels due to increased metabolism. Avoid concurrent use if possible [US].
- **Beta-blockers (e.g., Propranolol):** Steady-state coadministration does not significantly alter bioavailability. However, severe hypotension has been reported under fentanyl anesthesia in patients receiving both beta-blockers and calcium channel blockers [US].
- **Nitroglycerin:** Safely coadministered without significant interaction [US].
- **Digoxin/Warfarin:** No clinically relevant pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic interactions observed [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category C:** Animal studies have shown reduced maternal weight gain and decreased pup survival at high doses. There are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if isradipine is excreted in human milk. Due to the potential for adverse effects in nursing infants, a decision should be made whether to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the importance of the medication to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility was observed in rat studies at doses up to 60 mg/kg/day [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in patients under 18 years of age have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Bioavailability (AUC) and peak concentrations (Cmax) are increased in patients over 65 years of age. While the starting dose remains 2.5 mg b.i.d., clinicians should be mindful of the increased exposure [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Bioavailability increases by 45% in mild renal impairment (CrCl 30–80 mL/min). In severe renal failure on hemodialysis, AUC may be 20–50% lower. Starting dose: 2.5 mg b.i.d. [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** AUC and Cmax are increased by 52% and 32%, respectively. Starting dose: 2.5 mg b.i.d. [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker that inhibits the influx of calcium into cardiac and smooth muscle. It primarily induces peripheral vasodilation, reducing systemic vascular resistance [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Produces dose-related reductions in supine and standing blood pressure within 2–3 hours of administration. Chronic use is associated with a small increase in resting pulse rate (3–5 bpm) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** 90–95% absorbed; subject to extensive first-pass metabolism. Bioavailability is 15–24% [US].
    - **Distribution:** 95% bound to plasma proteins [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Completely metabolised in the liver; no unchanged drug is detected in the urine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic; early half-life of 1.5–2 hours, terminal half-life of ~8 hours. 60–65% excreted in urine, 25–30% in feces [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Hypertension Trials:** Double-blind studies confirmed efficacy as monotherapy and in combination with thiazide-type diuretics. Response at trough (prior to the next dose) was found to be over 50% of the peak effect. Antihypertensive effects are dose-related between 5 mg and 10 mg daily [US].
- **Hemodynamics:** Studies in patients with normal left ventricular function showed increased cardiac index and stroke volume index with decreased systemic, coronary, and pulmonary vascular resistance [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F).
- **Dispensing:** Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container as defined in the USP [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** 
    - **United States:** Prescription only (Rx only).
