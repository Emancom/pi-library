# Editorial Product Information: Idursulfase

## Editorial Note
- This editorial document has been synthesized from the following regulatory source: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **idursulfase** intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a clinical guideline or a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians must consult the current approved PI in their region before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Idursulfase.
- **Chemical Class:** Purified recombinant form of the human lysosomal enzyme iduronate-2-sulfatase (I2S).
- **Brand Name:** **ELAPRASE** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AB09.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 6 mg/3 mL (2 mg/mL) concentrate for intravenous (IV) solution for infusion. The solution is sterile, aqueous, and clear to slightly opalescent.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hunter Syndrome (Mucopolysaccharidosis II, MPS II):** Indicated for the long-term treatment of patients with a confirmed diagnosis of Hunter syndrome [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Recommended Dose:** 0.5 mg/kg of body weight administered once every week.

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Use aseptic technique. Calculate the volume required based on patient weight (0.5 mg/kg). Round up to the nearest whole vial if necessary. Dilute the total volume in 100 mL of 0.9% Sodium Chloride for Injection.
- **Handling:** Do **not** shake the vial or the diluted solution. 
- **Infusion Set:** Use of an infusion set equipped with a 0.2-micrometer (µm) low-protein binding filter is recommended.
- **Infusion Rate:**
    - Initial rate: 8 mL/hr for the first 15 minutes.
    - If tolerated, the rate may be increased by 8 mL/hr increments every 15 minutes.
    - Maximum rate: 100 mL/hr.
- **Duration:** The total volume should ideally be delivered over 3 hours. This may be reduced to no less than 1 hour in patients who tolerate the infusion well. However, in children under 5 years of age, infusion durations of less than 3 hours are not recommended [Australian].
- **Max Duration:** Infusion time should not exceed 8 hours.

### Home Infusion
- May be considered for patients who have received at least 6 months of in-clinic treatment, are tolerating infusions well, have stable airway disease, and have been free of infusion-related reactions (IRRs) for 6 months. Must be administered by a trained healthcare professional with access to emergency medical services and resuscitative equipment [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Severe, life-threatening hypersensitivity (anaphylactic reaction) to idursulfase or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylactoid/Anaphylactic Reactions:** Serious and potentially life-threatening reactions have been observed. Symptoms may include respiratory distress, hypoxia, hypotension, seizure, or angioedema. 
- **Late-Emergent/Biphasic Reactions:** Clinicians should be aware that anaphylactic reactions can occur up to 24 hours after the initial infusion and recovery. Patients who experience severe initial reactions may require prolonged observation [Australian].
- **Cardiorespiratory Risk:** Patients with underlying severe obstructive airway disease or compromised cardiac function are at increased risk of serious exacerbation during infusion. Caution is required in patients for whom fluid restriction is indicated.
- **Acute Febrile Illness:** Delaying the infusion should be considered for patients presenting with acute febrile respiratory illness.
- **Genotype-Specific Risks:** Patients with complete deletion/large rearrangement genotypes have a higher probability of developing antibodies (including neutralising antibodies) and a higher risk of infusion-related adverse events. These patients may also show a muted clinical response [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Infusion-Related Reactions (IRRs)
IRRs are the most common adverse events. They are defined as any adverse event occurring on the day of infusion. The frequency of IRRs tends to decrease over time with continued treatment.

### Frequency Summary
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Headache, hypertension, flushing, wheezing, dyspnoea, vomiting, abdominal pain, nausea, dyspepsia, urticaria, rash, pruritus, erythema, pyrexia, chest pain, infusion site swelling. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Cyanosis, arrhythmia, tachycardia, hypotension, hypoxia, tachypnoea, bronchospasm, swollen tongue, face oedema, peripheral oedema. |
| **Not Known** | Anaphylactoid/anaphylactic reactions. |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hypoxic episodes:** Particularly in patients with severe underlying obstructive airway disease.
- **Seizures:** Reported in association with severe infusion reactions.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Formal Studies:** No formal drug interaction studies have been conducted.
- **Metabolic Pathway:** As a recombinant protein, idursulfase is an unlikely candidate for cytochrome P450-mediated drug interactions [Australian].
- **Compatibility:** Idursulfase must not be infused with other products in the same intravenous tubing.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B2** [Australian]. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women. Animal studies indicate idursulfase is present in foetal circulation in utero. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is not known if idursulfase is excreted in human milk, though it is excreted in animal milk. Breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired male fertility was seen in rat studies at doses up to 5 mg/kg twice weekly.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy established in patients aged 16 months to 18 years. Use in children <16 months has not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No data available for patients aged 65 and over.
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is required, as idursulfase is not cleared through renal or hepatic mechanisms [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Idursulfase provides an exogenous source of the lysosomal enzyme I2S. This enzyme cleaves the terminal 2-sulfate groups from the glycosaminoglycans (GAG) dermatan sulphate and heparan sulphate. In Hunter syndrome, the absence of this enzyme leads to GAG accumulation in various cells, causing organomegaly and tissue destruction.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Uptake into lysosomes is mediated by mannose-6-phosphate (M6P) receptors on the cell surface.
- **Half-life:** Approximately 44–48 minutes in adults; significantly longer (approximately 160 minutes) in children aged 16 months to 7.5 years [Australian].
- **Clearance:** Antibody-positive patients may exhibit significantly higher clearance rates.
- **Elimination:** Not cleared through renal or hepatic pathways.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **TKT024 (Pivotal Study):** A 53-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled study of 96 patients (ages 5–31). Weekly 0.5 mg/kg dosing showed statistically significant improvement in a composite endpoint of 6-minute walk test (6MWT) distance and percentage predicted Forced Vital Capacity (FVC) compared to placebo (p=0.0049).
- **HGT-ELA-038:** An open-label safety study in 28 male patients aged 16 months to 7.5 years. The safety profile was similar to that seen in older children, with a primary focus on GAG clearance and liver/spleen volume reduction [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store under refrigeration at 2°C to 8°C. Do **not** freeze or shake. Protect from light.
- **Diluted Solution:** Contains no preservatives. Should be used as soon as practicable. If storage is necessary, the diluted solution is stable for up to 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
- **Legal Status:** Registered for use in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) since February 2008.
