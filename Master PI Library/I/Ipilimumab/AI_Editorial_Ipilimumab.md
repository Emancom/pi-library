# Editorial Product Information: Ipilimumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial was developed using the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **ipilimumab** for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data.
- **Prescribing Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and should not be used as a substitute for the official, current Product Information specific to the jurisdiction in which a healthcare professional is practicing. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ipilimumab (rch), a recombinant, fully human monoclonal IgG1 kappa immunoglobulin.
- **Chemical Class:** Cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated antigen 4 (CTLA-4) immune checkpoint inhibitor.
- **Brand Name:** **Yervoy** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01FX04.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrate solution for intravenous (IV) infusion (5 mg/mL). Available in 50 mg/10 mL and 200 mg/40 mL single-use vials.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bristol-Myers Squibb [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Ipilimumab** is indicated for the treatment of several malignancies, often in combination with nivolumab:

- **Melanoma:** 
    - Monotherapy for unresectable or metastatic disease [Australian].
    - Combination with nivolumab for unresectable or metastatic disease [Australian].
- **Renal Cell Carcinoma (RCC):** Combination with nivolumab for previously untreated, intermediate/poor-risk advanced disease [Australian].
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** First-line treatment in combination with nivolumab and two cycles of platinum-doublet chemotherapy for metastatic or recurrent disease (limited to patients without EGFR or ALK genomic aberrations) [Australian].
- **Malignant Pleural Mesothelioma (MPM):** First-line treatment in combination with nivolumab for unresectable disease [Australian].
- **Oesophageal Squamous Cell Carcinoma (OSCC):** First-line treatment in combination with nivolumab for unresectable advanced, recurrent, or metastatic disease where PD-L1 expression is $\ge 1\%$ [Australian].
- **Colorectal Cancer (CRC):** Treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic disease that is Microsatellite Instability-High (MSI-H) or Mismatch Repair Deficient (dMMR), in combination with nivolumab [Australian].
- **Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC):** First-line treatment in combination with nivolumab for unresectable or metastatic disease [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Melanoma Monotherapy:** 3 mg/kg IV over 30 minutes every 3 weeks for a total of 4 doses. 
- **Melanoma Combination:** Nivolumab 1 mg/kg followed by **ipilimumab** 3 mg/kg every 3 weeks for 4 doses, followed by nivolumab monotherapy.
- **RCC Combination:** Nivolumab 3 mg/kg followed by **ipilimumab** 1 mg/kg every 3 weeks for 4 doses, followed by nivolumab monotherapy.
- **NSCLC Combination:** Nivolumab 360 mg every 3 weeks with **ipilimumab** 1 mg/kg every 6 weeks, plus 2 cycles of chemotherapy.
- **HCC Combination:** **Ipilimumab** 3 mg/kg followed by nivolumab 1 mg/kg every 3 weeks for 4 doses, followed by nivolumab monotherapy.

### Administration Instructions
- **Do not** administer as an IV push or bolus.
- Infusion must be via a dedicated line with a sterile, non-pyrogenic, low-protein-binding in-line filter (0.2 $\mu$m to 1.2 $\mu$m).
- When used in combination with nivolumab, nivolumab must be administered first, followed by **ipilimumab** after a minimum 30-minute interval.

### Treatment Modifications
Dose reductions are not recommended. Management of immune-related adverse reactions (irARs) requires withholding doses or permanent discontinuation based on severity (Grade 2–4). If a dose is withheld, it should be resumed once the reaction resolves to Grade 0–1 or baseline and corticosteroid tapering is complete.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **ipilimumab** or any of the excipients (e.g., tris(hydroxymethyl)aminomethane hydrochloride, sodium chloride, mannitol, pentetic acid, polysorbate 80).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [Australian]
**Immune-Related Adverse Reactions (irARs):** **Ipilimumab** can cause severe, life-threatening, or fatal immune-mediated reactions in any organ system. While most occur during treatment, some may manifest weeks or months after the last dose. Early diagnosis and appropriate management (typically with high-dose corticosteroids) are critical.

### General Warnings
- **Gastrointestinal:** Severe colitis and intestinal perforation have been reported. Monitor for diarrhoea, blood in stools, and abdominal pain.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Monitor LFTs before each dose. Severe (Grade 3–4) transaminase elevations require permanent discontinuation.
- **Dermatologic:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) have occurred. Withhold for Grade 3 rash; discontinue for Grade 4 or confirmed SJS/TEN.
- **Endocrinopathies:** Can cause hypophysitis, adrenal insufficiency, and Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus. Monitor for headache, fatigue, and electrolyte imbalances.
- **Neurologic:** Rare cases of Guillain-Barré syndrome, myasthenia gravis, and encephalitis have been reported.
- **Infusion Reactions:** Severe reactions require immediate discontinuation and medical intervention.

### Monitoring Requirements
- Liver function tests (LFTs) and thyroid function tests must be evaluated at baseline and before each dose.
- Continuous monitoring for signs of colitis, dermatitis, and other organ-specific inflammatory events is required throughout and after treatment.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are more frequent and severe when **ipilimumab** is used in combination with nivolumab compared to monotherapy.

| Frequency | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Fatigue, diarrhoea, rash, pruritus, nausea, vomiting, decreased appetite, pyrexia, headache, insomnia, cough, and abdominal pain. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Colitis, hepatotoxicity (increased ALT/AST), hypothyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, hypophysitis, dehydration, and infusion-related reactions. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pancreatitis, uveitis, Guillain-Barré syndrome, sarcoidosis, and renal failure. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Myositis, myocarditis, toxic epidermal necrolysis, and encephalitis. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:** Gastrointestinal perforation, fatal hepatic failure, and severe pneumonitis (primarily in combination therapy).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** **Ipilimumab** is a monoclonal antibody and is not metabolised by cytochrome P450 enzymes. It does not significantly affect the PK of dacarbazine or paclitaxel/carboplatin.
- **Corticosteroids:** Use of systemic corticosteroids at baseline should be avoided due to potential interference with efficacy, but they are recommended for managing irARs once treatment has started.
- **Anticoagulants:** Concurrent use may increase the risk of gastrointestinal haemorrhage.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. There are no data on use in pregnant women. Animal studies (monkeys) showed increased rates of abortion and stillbirth in the third trimester. Use is not recommended unless clinical benefit outweighs potential fetal risk.
- **Breastfeeding:** Human IgG1 is known to be secreted in breast milk; therefore, a risk to the infant cannot be excluded. Discontinuation of breastfeeding is recommended during treatment.
- **Fertility:** No specific human studies have been performed.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or efficacy between patients $\ge 65$ and younger patients. No dose adjustment is required.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is needed for patients with mild to moderate renal dysfunction. It has not been studied in severe renal impairment.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is needed for mild hepatic impairment. **Ipilimumab** has not been studied in moderate to severe hepatic impairment.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Ipilimumab** blocks the T-cell inhibitory signal induced by the CTLA-4 pathway. This blockade increases the number of reactive T-effector cells and reduces T-regulatory cell function, enhancing the anti-tumour immune response.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is 7.2 L.
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is approximately 20 days. Clearance is 12.1 mL/h.
    - **Accumulation:** Approximately 1.7-fold accumulation after 4 doses (3 mg/kg q3w).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Melanoma (MDX010-20):** A Phase 3 study in previously treated patients showed a significant OS benefit for **ipilimumab** 3 mg/kg (median OS 10 months) compared to an experimental gp100 vaccine (median OS 6 months).
- **Melanoma Combination (CA209067):** The combination of **ipilimumab** and nivolumab demonstrated superior PFS and OS compared to **ipilimumab** monotherapy.
- **RCC (CA209214):** In intermediate/poor-risk patients, nivolumab + **ipilimumab** showed superior OS (Median not reached) compared to sunitinib (25.9 months).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate). Do not freeze.
- **Protection:** Keep in the original package to protect from light.
- **In-use Stability:** After dilution, the solution is stable for 24 hours at 2°C to 25°C.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australian Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine).
- **Legal Classification:** Restricted to use by specialist physicians experienced in cancer treatment.
