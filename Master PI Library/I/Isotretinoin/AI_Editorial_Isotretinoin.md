# Editorial Product Information: Isotretinoin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data and regulatory requirements into a single reference point.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Isotretinoin (13-cis-retinoic acid).
- **Chemical Class:** Retinoid.
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Isotretinoin [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** D10BA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Soft gelatin capsules: 10 mg and 20 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Severe Cystic Acne:** Indicated for patients with severe cystic acne that has proven unresponsive to conventional therapies, including systemic antibiotics [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Adolescents >12 years):** 
    - **Initial Dose:** 0.5 mg/kg daily for the first 2–4 weeks to assess responsiveness and tolerance.
    - **Maintenance Range:** 0.1 mg/kg/day to 1.0 mg/kg/day, depending on individual response.
    - **Duration:** A standard treatment course typically lasts 16 weeks. 
- **Administration Instructions:** Capsules must be taken with food. They may be administered as a single daily dose or split into two divided doses.
- **Course Completion and Retreatment:** 
    - If a second course is required, a minimum rest period of 8 weeks [Australian: also described as 2 months] must be observed after the first course, as clinical improvement may continue after the drug is discontinued.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Absolutely contraindicated in females who are pregnant or of childbearing potential unless all criteria of a pregnancy prevention program are met [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated due to the drug’s lipophilic nature and potential for passage into breast milk [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in patients with severely impaired liver function [Australian].
- **Hyperlipidemia:** Contraindicated in patients with chronic, abnormally elevated blood lipid values [Australian].
- **Hypervitaminosis A:** Contraindicated in patients with pre-existing vitamin A toxicity [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to isotretinoin, other retinoids, or any excipients (notably soya, as capsules contain soya oil) [Australian].
- **Drug Interactions:** Concomitant use with tetracyclines (risk of benign intracranial hypertension) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Teratogenicity (Major Warning):** Isotretinoin is a potent teratogen. Major foetal abnormalities (CNS, craniofacial, cardiac, and glandular) are documented. Pregnancy must be excluded within 2 weeks of starting therapy and avoided for at least one month after cessation.
- **Psychiatric Disorders:** Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of depression. Reports include depression, anxiety, aggressive tendencies, psychosis, and rare instances of suicidal ideation or suicide attempts.
- **Benign Intracranial Hypertension (Pseudotumor Cerebri):** Associated with isotretinoin use; risk increases with concomitant tetracycline use. Discontinue immediately if symptoms (papilloedema, headache, nausea) occur.
- **Dermatologic Reactions:** Serious skin reactions (e.g., Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis) have been reported. Initial acne flares are common. Patients should avoid wax epilation or laser/chemical dermabrasion for 5–6 months post-therapy due to scarring risk.
- **Musculoskeletal Changes:** High doses may cause hyperostosis, premature epiphyseal closure in children, and calcification of tendons.
- **Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD):** Associated with new-onset IBD in patients without prior history.
- **Blood Donation:** Patients must not donate blood during and for one month after treatment to prevent exposure to pregnant recipients.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally dose-related and mirror Vitamin A toxicity.

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Cheilitis (>90%), dry skin/mucosa, epistaxis (30%), conjunctivitis, elevated ESR (40%), increased triglycerides/cholesterol, elevated AST/ALT (15%). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Myalgia, arthralgia, headache, skin fragility, peeling of palms/soles. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Benign intracranial hypertension, suicidal ideation, anaphylaxis, hepatitis, rhabdomyolysis, impaired night vision, hearing loss. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Pancreatitis (associated with high triglycerides), Stevens-Johnson Syndrome, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis, and severe haematologic decreases (neutropenia/anaemia) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Vitamin A:** Avoid concurrent use to prevent additive toxic effects (hypervitaminosis A).
- **Tetracyclines (e.g., Minocycline, Doxycycline):** Contraindicated due to increased risk of benign intracranial hypertension.
- **Topical Anti-acne Agents:** Use with caution; concurrent keratolytic or exfoliative agents may increase local irritation.
- **Progesterone-only Contraceptives ("Minipills"):** Microdosed progesterone efficacy may be diminished; these should not be used as a sole contraceptive method [Australian].
- **Systemic Corticosteroids/Phenytoin:** Use with caution as these may contribute to bone loss/osteoporosis [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category X [Australian]**. Highly teratogenic. Effective contraception (preferably two forms) is mandatory for one month before, during, and one month after treatment. Monthly pregnancy tests are strongly recommended.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated; passage into human milk is highly likely due to lipophilicity [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No significant changes noted in human sperm count or motility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended for children under 12 years of age. Use in adolescents (12–17 years) requires careful consideration due to risks of premature epiphyseal closure and growth retardation [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in the source document [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Pharmacokinetics are not significantly altered by renal failure. However, it is recommended to start at a lower dose in patients with severe renal insufficiency and titrate to tolerance [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A retinoid that inhibits sebaceous gland function and keratinisation. It reduces sebum secretion by decreasing sebaceous gland size and differentiation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Bioavailability is 1.5 to 2 times higher when taken with food. Tmax is approximately 2.9 to 3.2 hours.
    - **Distribution:** 99.9% plasma protein-bound (primarily albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolite is 4-oxo-isotretinoin. Neither the parent drug nor metabolites inhibit or induce CYP 2C9 [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Terminal half-life is 10–20 hours. Excreted in roughly equal amounts in urine and faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Summary:** Specific pivotal trial data summaries were not provided in the available source document. The document notes that clinical improvement is associated with the reversible reduction of sebum secretion [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C.
- **Protection:** Keep protected from light and moisture.
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified; refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian]**.
- **Prescriber Restrictions:** Should only be prescribed by physicians experienced in using systemic retinoids and who understand the risks of teratogenicity [Australian].
