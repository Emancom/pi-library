## Pharmaceutical Form

### Editorial Version
Iodixanol is supplied as a sterile, pyrogen-free, clear, and colorless to pale yellow aqueous solution for injection. It is an iso-osmolar and isotonic solution with a pH adjusted to 7.4 (range 6.8 to 7.7) using hydrochloric acid and/or sodium hydroxide. The solution contains no preservatives and is terminally sterilized by autoclaving. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

## Therapeutic Indications

### Editorial Version
Iodixanol is indicated for diagnostic use in the following procedures:

#### Intra-arterial Procedures
*   **Adults and Pediatrics (≥12 years):** Digital subtraction angiography (IA-DSA) using 270 or 320 mg I/mL; angiocardiography (left ventriculography and selective coronary arteriography), peripheral arteriography, visceral arteriography, and cerebral arteriography using 320 mg I/mL. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Adults (General):** Used for angiocardiography, peripheral/visceral/cerebral arteriography, and venography. Supports both conventional radiography and IA-DSA. (Source: Australian Product Information)
*   **Pediatrics (<12 years):** Angiocardiography, cerebral arteriography, and visceral arteriography using 320 mg I/mL. (Source: United States Prescribing Information) In Australia, also indicated for general cardioangiography. (Source: Australian Product Information)

#### Intravenous Procedures
*   **Adults and Pediatrics (≥12 years):** Computed Tomography (CT) of the head and body (270 or 320 mg I/mL); excretory urography (270 or 320 mg I/mL); peripheral venography (270 mg I/mL). (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Coronary Computed Tomography Angiography (CCTA):** To assist in evaluating suspected coronary artery disease in adults and pediatrics ≥12 years (320 mg I/mL). (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Pediatrics (<12 years):** CT imaging of the head and body and excretory urography (270 mg I/mL). (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Pediatrics (AU specific):** Studies of the upper gastrointestinal tract. (Source: Australian Product Information)

## Dosage and Administration

### Editorial Version
#### Administration Guidelines
*   **Personnel:** Procedures must be performed by trained personnel with a thorough knowledge of the specific radiological procedure. (Source: Australian Product Information)
*   **Preparation:** Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration. Use only if the container is intact. The product should be drawn into the syringe immediately before use. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Temperature:** May be warmed to body temperature (37°C) before administration to optimize viscosity and patient comfort. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Hydration:** Ensure adequate hydration before and after administration to minimize the risk of contrast-induced acute kidney injury. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Dose Individualization:** Dosing must be tailored based on age, body weight, vessel size, blood flow rate, cardiac output, the area to be examined, and the technical equipment used. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

#### Adult Dosage Recommendations
The maximum total dose of iodine for adults is **80 grams**. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)

## Contraindications

### Editorial Version
*   **Intrathecal Use:** Iodixanol is contraindicated for intrathecal administration. Inadvertent intrathecal use may result in death, convulsions, cerebral hemorrhage, coma, paralysis, arachnoiditis, acute renal failure, cardiac arrest, rhabdomyolysis, hyperthermia, and brain edema. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to the active substance, iodine, or any excipient. A history of serious hypersensitivity reactions to iodixanol is a contraindication. (Source: Australian Product Information)
*   **Thyroid Disorders:** Manifest thyrotoxicosis. (Source: Australian Product Information)

## Warnings and Precautions

### Editorial Version
#### Hypersensitivity Reactions
Serious, life-threatening, or fatal anaphylactoid reactions can occur, typically within 3 minutes of injection but potentially up to several hours later. Patients with a history of allergy, asthma, or prior reactions to contrast media are at increased risk. Premedication with corticosteroids or H1/H2 antagonists may reduce the severity but does not prevent serious reactions. Emergency equipment and trained personnel must be immediately available. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

#### Contrast-Induced Acute Kidney Injury (CIAKI)
Iodixanol can cause acute kidney injury, including renal failure. Risk factors include pre-existing renal impairment, dehydration, diabetes mellitus, congestive heart failure, advanced vascular disease, elderly age, and concomitant nephrotoxic drugs or diuretics. Avoid preparatory dehydration and ensure adequate hydration (e.g., at least 100 mL/hour of saline for up to 24 hours post-procedure). (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

#### Cardiovascular Risks
Hemodynamic disturbances, including shock, cardiac arrest, serious arrhythmias, and myocardial ischemia, may occur, particularly during coronary arteriography. Exercise extreme caution in patients with serious cardiac disease, pulmonary hypertension, or stenotic vascular beds. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

#### Neurological Effects and Encephalopathy
Contrast-induced encephalopathy has been reported, manifesting as headache, visual disturbances, seizures, or coma. Patients with a disrupted blood-brain barrier (BBB) are at higher risk. If suspected, discontinue iodixanol and initiate medical management. (Source: Australian Product Information)

#### Thyroid Dysfunction
*   **Hyperthyroidism:** Administration may trigger thyroid storm in patients with hyperthyroidism or autonomously functioning nodules. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Pediatrics (0–3 Years):** Thyroid dysfunction (hypothyroidism or transient suppression) may occur after exposure. Individualized monitoring is required, especially for preterm neonates. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

#### Thromboembolic Events
Serious thromboembolic events, including myocardial infarction and stroke, have been reported during angiographic procedures. Meticulous technique, frequent catheter flushing with heparinized saline, and minimizing procedure length are essential. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

#### Specific Populations and Conditions
*   **Pheochromocytoma:** May trigger hypertensive crisis; assess blood pressure throughout and keep treatment measures ready. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Sickle Cell Disease:** Contrast media may promote sickling in homozygous patients. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Multiple Myeloma:** Risk of renal failure; dehydration should be avoided. (Source: Australian Product Information)
*   **Homocystinuria:** Avoid angiography if possible due to increased risk of embolism. (Source: Australian Product Information)

## Pregnancy

### Editorial Version
*   **Australian Category B1:** Animal studies have not shown evidence of fetal harm.
*   **Risk Summary:** There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal reproduction studies (rats and rabbits at doses up to 2 g Iodine/kg/day) showed no developmental toxicity or harm to the fetus. Iodixanol should only be used during pregnancy if the potential benefit justifies the risk and the procedure is considered essential by a physician. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

## Breastfeeding

### Editorial Version
The amount of iodixanol excreted in human milk is very low, and gastrointestinal absorption by the infant is poor. While interruption of breastfeeding is generally not considered necessary, a lactating woman may choose to pump and discard breast milk for 10 hours (approximately 5 elimination half-lives) after administration to minimize potential exposure. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

## Drug Interactions

### Editorial Version
*   **Metformin:** In patients with renal impairment, contrast media may worsen renal function, leading to metformin accumulation and lactic acidosis. Stop metformin at the time of or prior to iodixanol administration in patients with eGFR 30–60 mL/min/1.73m², or those with hepatic impairment/heart failure. Re-evaluate eGFR 48 hours post-procedure; restart only when renal function is stable. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Interleukin-2 (IL-2):** Patients treated with IL-2 within the preceding two weeks have an increased risk of delayed reactions, such as flu-like symptoms or skin eruptions. (Source: Australian Product Information)
*   **Beta-adrenergic Blockers:** May lower the threshold for bronchospasm and reduce the efficacy of epinephrine (adrenaline) used to treat hypersensitivity reactions. Atypical symptoms of anaphylaxis may be misinterpreted as a vagal reaction. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Radioactive Iodine:** Contrast media may interfere with thyroid uptake of radioactive iodine (I-131 or I-123) for 6 to 8 weeks, potentially decreasing the efficacy of thyroid carcinoma treatment or diagnosis. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Oral Cholecystographic Agents:** Renal toxicity has been reported in patients with liver dysfunction who received these agents followed by intravascular contrast. Postpone iodixanol if these agents were recently administered. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Inotropic Agents:** Concurrent use with contrast may produce a paradoxical depressant response in the ischemic myocardium. (Source: Australian Product Information)

## Laboratory Test Interference

### Editorial Version

## Overdose

### Editorial Version
Overdosage is unlikely in patients with normal renal function. The primary risks involve the pulmonary and cardiovascular systems. In the event of an overdose:
1. Support all vital functions.
2. Institute prompt symptomatic therapy.
3. Compensate for water and electrolyte losses via infusion.
4. Monitor renal function for at least 3 days.
5. Hemodialysis may be used to remove iodixanol.

There is no specific antidote. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

## Mechanism of Action

### Editorial Version
Iodixanol is an organic iodine compound. The organically bound iodine attenuates X-rays as they pass through the body, providing opacification of vascular structures and organs. This allows for radiographic visualization of internal structures until the agent is diluted by hemodilution or excreted. In the brain, iodixanol does not cross an intact blood-brain barrier but will accumulate in the interstitial space in areas of BBB disruption. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

## Pharmacokinetics

### Editorial Version
*   **Distribution:** Rapidly distributes into the extracellular fluid. The mean distribution half-life is approximately 21 minutes. It does not bind significantly to human plasma proteins (<2%). (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Metabolism:** No significant metabolism, deiodination, or biotransformation occurs. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Elimination:** Primarily excreted unchanged via the kidneys through glomerular filtration. In healthy adults, approximately 97% of the dose is recovered in the urine within 24 hours. About 1.2% is excreted in feces within 72 hours. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Half-life:** The mean elimination half-life in adults is approximately 2.1 hours.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Half-life is prolonged. In patients with a mean creatinine clearance of 13.6 mL/min, the plasma half-life increases to approximately 23 hours. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Pediatrics:** Elimination half-life is inversely related to age, being longest in newborns (approx. 4.1 hours) and decreasing toward adult values by age 12. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

## Storage and Handling

### Editorial Version
