# Unified Editorial Product Information: Insulin Lispro

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Humalog® / Humalog® Mix50/50™
**Active Ingredient:** Insulin lispro (recombinant DNA origin)

Insulin lispro is a human insulin analogue produced by recombinant DNA technology. It is characterized by the reversal of the amino acids proline and lysine at positions B28 and B29 of the insulin chain. 

This PI encompasses two distinct formulations:
*   **Humalog U200:** A clear, concentrated solution containing 200 units/mL of insulin lispro.
*   **Humalog Mix50/50:** A white, cloudy suspension consisting of 50% insulin lispro protamine (intermediate-acting) and 50% insulin lispro (rapid-acting), containing 100 units/mL.

## 2. Approved Indications
Insulin lispro is indicated for the treatment of adults and children [TGA] with diabetes mellitus to improve glycemic control and maintain normal glucose homeostasis. 

**Limitations of Use:** The fixed proportions of rapid and intermediate-acting insulin in Mix50/50 do not allow for independent adjustment of basal versus prandial requirements [FDA].

## 3. Contraindications
*   **Hypoglycemia:** Insulin lispro must not be administered during episodes of hypoglycemia.
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to insulin lispro or any of the excipients (e.g., metacresol, glycerol, zinc oxide, or protamine sulfate in the mix formulation).

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Medication Errors and Administration
*   **Product Sharing:** Insulin pens or syringes must never be shared between patients, even if the needle is changed, due to the risk of transmitting blood-borne pathogens.
*   **Dose Conversion (U200):** The Humalog U200 KwikPen is designed to deliver the dialed dose without conversion. Patients must **never** transfer U200 insulin from a KwikPen to a syringe, as standard syringe markings will result in a severe, potentially fatal overdose.
*   **Pumps and IV Use:** 
    *   Humalog U200 and Humalog Mix50/50 must **not** be administered intravenously.
    *   Neither formulation is suitable for use in continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (CSII) pumps [TGA/FDA].

### Clinical Management
*   **Regimen Changes:** Any change in insulin strength, manufacturer, type, or species should be performed under strict medical supervision. Increased frequency of blood glucose monitoring is required during the transition.
*   **Hypokalemia:** All insulin products cause a shift in potassium from extracellular to intracellular space. If left untreated, this may lead to respiratory paralysis, ventricular arrhythmia, and death. Use with caution in patients at risk of hypokalemia (e.g., those using potassium-lowering medications).
*   **Fluid Retention and Heart Failure:** Cases of heart failure have been reported when insulin is used in combination with Thiazolidinediones (TZDs), particularly in patients with pre-existing cardiac disease. Patients should be observed for signs of heart failure (e.g., edema, sudden weight gain, dyspnea).

### Injection Site Reactions
*   **Lipodystrophy and Amyloidosis:** Subcutaneous administration can lead to lipoatrophy, lipohypertrophy, or localized cutaneous amyloidosis. Patients must rotate injection sites within the chosen area (abdomen, thigh, upper arm, or buttocks) to reduce this risk.
*   **Absorption Issues:** Injecting into areas with these reactions may delay insulin absorption. A sudden change in injection site to an unaffected area has been reported to cause hypoglycemia [TGA].

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Administration Instructions
Insulin lispro is for **subcutaneous injection only**. 
*   **Timing:** Administer within 15 minutes before a meal. The U200 formulation may also be given within 20 minutes of starting a meal if necessary [TGA].
*   **Preparation (Mix50/50):** The suspension must be resuspended by rolling and inverting the device at least 10 times until the liquid appears uniformly white and cloudy.

### Dosage Individualization
Dose requirements vary based on metabolic needs, blood glucose monitoring results, and glycemic goals. 
*   **U200:** Generally reserved for patients requiring more than 20 units of rapid-acting insulin daily.
*   **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Requirements may be reduced due to decreased gluconeogenesis and insulin metabolism. However, in some cases of chronic hepatic impairment, increased insulin resistance may actually increase requirements [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Hypoglycemia:** The most common adverse reaction. Severe episodes can lead to seizures, loss of consciousness, and death.
*   **Allergic Reactions:** 
    *   *Local:* Redness, swelling, and itching at the injection site (usually resolves within days).
    *   *Systemic:* Generalised rash, shortness of breath, wheezing, hypotension, or anaphylaxis.
*   **Metabolic:** Weight gain and peripheral edema, particularly when glycemic control is rapidly improved.
*   **Immunogenicity:** Administration may induce the formation of anti-insulin antibodies.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Increased Hypoglycemic Risk:** Antidiabetic agents, ACE inhibitors, salicylates, sulfonamide antibiotics, monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs), and alcohol.
*   **Decreased Hypoglycemic Effect:** Oral contraceptives, corticosteroids, thyroid replacement therapy, danazol, and sympathomimetic agents (e.g., adrenaline, salbutamol).
*   **Masking of Symptoms:** Beta-blockers, clonidine, and reserpine may blunt the warning signs of hypoglycemia.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**Pregnancy Category A [TGA].** 
Insulin lispro may be used during pregnancy. Data from a large body of pregnancies suggest no increased risk of congenital malformations compared to other insulins. Insulin requirements typically decrease in the first trimester and increase during the second and third trimesters. Intensive glucose control is essential to reduce risks to both mother and fetus.

### Breastfeeding
It is unknown if insulin lispro is excreted in human milk in significant amounts, though human insulin is known to pass into breast milk. Breastfeeding women may require adjustments in insulin dose or diet. The benefits of breastfeeding should be weighed against the clinical need for the drug.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Insulin lispro has a faster rate of absorption compared to regular human insulin.
    *   *U200:* Reaches peak serum concentration at approximately 1 hour with a duration of action between 3.5 to 4.5 hours.
    *   *Mix50/50:* Exhibits a dual-phase absorption profile. The rapid phase (lispro) provides quick onset, while the protamine phase provides prolonged activity. Detectable activity lasts for a median of 22 hours [FDA].
*   **Metabolism:** The metabolism of insulin lispro is identical to that of regular human insulin.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
The primary activity of insulin lispro is the regulation of glucose metabolism. It lowers blood glucose by:
1.  Stimulating peripheral glucose uptake, particularly by skeletal muscle and adipose tissue.
2.  Inhibiting hepatic glucose production.
3.  Inhibiting lipolysis and proteolysis while enhancing protein synthesis.

## 11. Storage Conditions
### Unopened (Not In-Use)
Store in a refrigerator between **2°C and 8°C**. Do not freeze. Protect from direct heat and light.

### Opened (In-Use)
*   **Humalog U200 KwikPen:** Store below 30°C for up to **28 days**. Do not refrigerate the pen once in use [TGA].
*   **Humalog Mix50/50 Vial:** May be refrigerated or stored at room temperature (below 30°C) for up to **28 days**.
*   **Humalog Mix50/50 KwikPen:** Store at room temperature (below 30°C) for a maximum of **10 days**. **Do not refrigerate** the in-use pen [FDA].
