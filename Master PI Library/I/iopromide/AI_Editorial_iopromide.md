## Active Ingredient

**Editorial Version**

Iopromide is a non-ionic, water-soluble, triiodinated X-ray contrast medium derived from isophthalic acid. It has a molecular weight of 791.12. The iodine content of the iopromide molecule is approximately 48.12% (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

## Pharmaceutical Form

**Editorial Version**

Iopromide is supplied as a clear, colorless to slightly yellow, sterile, pyrogen-free aqueous solution for injection or infusion. The solution is free from particulate matter and has a pH range of 6.5 to 8.0.

Available iodine concentrations:
*   **150 mg I/mL** (Australian Product Information)
*   **240 mg I/mL** (Australian Product Information)
*   **300 mg I/mL** (Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **370 mg I/mL** (Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Physicochemical Properties:**

| Property | 150 mg I/mL | 240 mg I/mL | 300 mg I/mL | 370 mg I/mL |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Viscosity (cP) at 20°C** | 2.3 | 4.9 | 8.9 – 9.2 | 22 |
| **Viscosity (cP) at 37°C** | 1.5 | 2.8 | 4.7 – 4.9 | 10 |

## Therapeutic Indications

**Editorial Version**

Iopromide is indicated for a wide range of radiographic procedures involving intravascular or intrathecal (concentration-dependent) administration.

**Intravascular Procedures (Adults)**
*   Cerebral, peripheral, visceral, and coronary arteriography.
*   Aortography and left ventriculography.
*   Excretory urography.
*   Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT) of the head and body.
*   Contrast-enhanced mammography (CEM) as an adjunct to standard mammography and/or ultrasound to visualize breast lesions (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).
*   Venography (Source: Australian Product Information).

**Intravascular Procedures (Pediatrics)**
*   **Aged 2 years and older:** Radiographic evaluation of cardiac chambers and related arteries, excretory urography, and CECT of the head and body (Source: United States Prescribing Information).
*   **General Pediatric Use:** AU labeling indicates use in all angiographic and urographic examinations and CECT enhancement, including newborns and infants (Source: Australian Product Information).

**Intrathecal Procedures (Adults)**
*   Lumbar myelography (limited to the **240 mg I/mL** concentration) (Source: Australian Product Information).
*   *Note:* The United States Prescribing Information contains a boxed warning stating iopromide is **not** approved for intrathecal use.

## Dosage and Administration

**Editorial Version**

### General Administration Guidelines
Iopromide must be visually inspected for particulate matter, crystallization, or discoloration prior to use; do not use if the solution is cloudy or contains sediment. Warm the medium to body temperature (37°C) before administration to reduce viscosity and improve patient tolerance. Use aseptic technique for all handling (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

**Hydration:** Ensure patients are adequately hydrated before and after administration to minimize the risk of Post-Contrast Acute Kidney Injury (PC-AKI) (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

### Intravascular Dosage: Adults
Doses should be individualized based on age, body weight, cardiac output, and the specific vascular region/technique.

#### Angiography and Ventriculography
| Procedure | Concentration (mg I/mL) | Volume per Injection (mL) | Max Total Dose (mL) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Cerebral Arteriography** | 300 | 3 – 12 | 150 |
| **Aortic Arch** | 300 | 20 – 50 | - |
| **Coronary Arteriography** | 370 | 3 – 14 | 225 |
| **Left Ventriculography** | 370 | 30 – 60 | 225 |
| **Aortography/Visceral** | 300 or 370 | Individualized | 225 – 250 |
| **Peripheral Arteriography** | 300 | 5 – 40 | 250 |
| **Venography (Upper)** | 300 | 8 – 12 | - |
| **Venography (Lower)** | 240 or 300 | 20 – 80 | - |

(Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

#### Contrast-Enhanced CT (CECT)
*   **CT Head/Body:** 50 – 200 mL of 300 mg I/mL OR 41 – 162 mL of 370 mg I/mL.
*   **Excretory Urography:** 1 mL/kg body weight (using 300 mg I/mL). Maximum total dose: 100 mL (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).
*   **Contrast Mammography:** 1.5 mL/kg body weight (using 300 or 370 mg I/mL) administered at 2 – 4 mL/second. Maximum dose: 150 mL (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

### Intravascular Dosage: Pediatrics
**Pediatrics (Aged 2 years and older):**
*   **Cardiac Chambers/Arteries (370 mg I/mL):** 1 – 2 mL/kg. Max: 4 mL/kg.
*   **Excretory Urography (300 mg I/mL):** 1 – 2 mL/kg. Max: 3 mL/kg.
*   **CECT Head/Body (300 mg I/mL):** 1 – 2 mL/kg. Max: 3 mL/kg.
(Source: United States Prescribing Information)

**Newborns, Infants, and Children (Australia-specific):**
*   **Newborns (<1 month):** 1.2 g I/kg (e.g., 4.0 mL/kg of 300 mg I/mL).
*   **Infants (1 month – 2 years):** 1.0 g I/kg (e.g., 3.0 mL/kg of 300 mg I/mL).
*   **Children (2 – 11 years):** 0.5 g I/kg (e.g., 1.5 mL/kg of 300 mg I/mL).
(Source: Australian Product Information)

### Intrathecal Dosage (Lumbar Myelography)
*   **Adults only:** Use concentration **240 mg I/mL**.
*   **Dose:** Up to 12.5 mL (Maximum 3 g iodine).
*   **Post-procedure:** Keep patient upright or head elevated (15°) for 6 hours, followed by 18 hours rest.
(Source: Australian Product Information)

## Dose Adjustments

**Editorial Version**

**Renal Impairment**
Iopromide is eliminated almost exclusively by the kidneys. In patients with pre-existing renal impairment, the minimum effective dose should be used. Ensure adequate hydration as a priority (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

**Geriatric Use**
Dosage selection for elderly patients should be cautious, typically starting at the lower end of the dosing range, reflecting the higher frequency of decreased renal function. Monitoring renal function may be beneficial (Source: United States Prescribing Information).

**Metformin Management**
*   **eGFR <30 $mL/min/1.73 m^2$ (intravenous/second-pass arterial) or eGFR <45 $mL/min/1.73 m^2$ (first-pass arterial):** Withhold metformin at the time of or prior to the procedure and for 48 hours following. Reinstitute only after confirming renal function is stable (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

## Contraindications

**Editorial Version**

1.  **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to iopromide or any of the excipients.
2.  **Myelography (Immediate Repeat):** Immediate repeat myelography is contraindicated due to the risk of overdosage (Source: Australian Product Information).
3.  **Intrathecal Use (Concentration Specific):** Concentrations 300 mg I/mL and 370 mg I/mL are contraindicated for intrathecal use (Source: Australian Product Information).
4.  **Intrathecal Use (United States):** In the US, iopromide is not approved for any intrathecal use (Source: United States Prescribing Information).

## Warnings and Precautions

**Editorial Version**

### WARNING: NOT FOR INTRATHECAL USE (United States)
**In the United States, iopromide is not approved for intrathecal use. Inadvertent intrathecal administration can cause death, convulsions, cerebral hemorrhage, coma, paralysis, arachnoiditis, acute renal failure, cardiac arrest, seizures, rhabdomyolysis, hyperthermia, and brain edema.** (Source: United States Prescribing Information).

### Hypersensitivity Reactions
Severe, life-threatening, or fatal anaphylactoid reactions can occur, typically within 60 minutes, though delayed reactions may emerge days later. Risk is increased in patients with a history of asthma, allergic disorders, or previous contrast reactions.
*   **Preparedness:** Emergency resuscitation equipment and trained personnel must be available.
*   **Premedication:** Corticosteroids/antihistamines may reduce incidence but do not prevent all serious reactions.
*   **Beta-blockers:** Patients on beta-blockers may be resistant to the treatment effects of epinephrine/beta-agonists during anaphylaxis (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

### Post-Contrast Acute Kidney Injury (PC-AKI)
Acute renal failure may occur. Risk factors include pre-existing renal insufficiency, diabetes, dehydration, congestive heart failure, multiple myeloma, and large or repetitive contrast doses. Use the lowest necessary dose and ensure hydration. Avoid laxatives or diuretics before the procedure (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

### Cardiovascular and Thromboembolic Risks
*   **Hemodynamics:** Iopromide increases circulatory osmotic load. Patients with congestive heart failure, valvular disease, or pulmonary hypertension are at risk for pulmonary edema, arrhythmia, and cardiac arrest.
*   **Thromboembolism:** Meticulous angiographic technique is required to minimize thromboembolic events such as myocardial infarction or stroke. Avoid contact between blood and syringes containing contrast (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

### Thyroid Dysfunction
*   **Thyroid Storm:** Use with extreme caution in patients with hyperthyroidism or autonomously functioning nodules.
*   **Hypothyroidism:** Monitoring is required in neonates and infants (0–3 years), as iodine exposure may induce transient hypothyroidism (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

### Central Nervous System Disorders
Patients with seizure disorders, brain tumors, or acute ischemia are at higher risk for neurological complications and seizures. Permeability of the blood-brain barrier may be increased, facilitating contrast entry into cerebral tissue (Source: Australian Product Information).

### Other Warnings
*   **Pheochromocytoma:** Risk of hypertensive crisis. Administer the minimum dose and consider premedication with alpha-blockers.
*   **Myasthenia Gravis:** Contrast administration may aggravate symptoms.
*   **Severe Cutaneous Reactions:** Cases of Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and DRESS have been reported. Avoid readministration in patients with a history of SCAR (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).
*   **Sickle Cell Disease:** Contrast media may promote sickling in homozygous patients. Ensure adequate hydration (Source: United States Prescribing Information).
*   **Extravasation:** May cause inflammation, skin necrosis, or compartment syndrome. Monitor the injection site closely (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

## Pregnancy, Breastfeeding, and Fertility

**Editorial Version**

### Pregnancy
**Australian Category: B2.**
There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in humans. Animal reproduction studies (rats and rabbits) showed no teratogenic effects at doses up to 3.7 g I/kg. Iopromide crosses the placenta and reaches fetal tissues in small amounts. It should be used only if essential to the patient's welfare. Radiography of the abdomen is generally considered contraindicated during pregnancy (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

### Breastfeeding
Iopromide is poorly excreted in human breast milk and absorption from the infant's gastrointestinal tract is minimal; harm to the nursed infant is unlikely. 
*   **Clinical Consideration:** A lactating woman may consider interrupting breastfeeding and discarding milk for 12 to 24 hours (approximately 5 half-lives) post-administration to further minimize infant exposure (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

### Fertility
Long-term studies have not been performed to evaluate the effects of iopromide on human fertility (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

## Adverse Effects

**Editorial Version**

The safety profile of iopromide is derived from trials in over 3,900 patients and post-marketing data from over 74,000 patients (Source: Australian Product Information).

**Common Adverse Reactions (>1%)**
Headache, nausea, vasodilatation/feeling hot, vomiting, back pain, urinary urgency, chest pain, dysgeusia, abnormal vision, and injection site reactions (pain, swelling, warmth) (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

**Serious Adverse Reactions**
*   **Immune:** Anaphylactoid shock, respiratory arrest, bronchospasm, laryngeal/pharyngeal edema, angioedema.
*   **Cardiac:** Cardiac arrest, myocardial infarction/ischemia, arrhythmia, heart failure, bradycardia, cyanosis.
*   **Neurological:** Coma, stroke, cerebral ischemia/infarction, brain edema, convulsions, paralysis.
*   **Vascular:** Shock, thromboembolic events, vasospasm.
*   **Respiratory:** Pulmonary edema, respiratory insufficiency, aspiration.
*   **Renal:** Acute renal failure.
*   **Skin:** SJS/TEN, DRESS, AGEP (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

**Specific to Intrathecal Use (AU only)**
*   Chemical meningitis, meningism, psychosis, paraplegia, back pain, and micturition disorders (Source: Australian Product Information).

## Drug Interactions

**Editorial Version**

*   **Metformin:** Increased risk of lactic acidosis if iopromide-induced renal impairment occurs. See *Dose Adjustments* for withholding guidelines.
*   **Interleukin-2:** Previous treatment (up to several weeks prior) is associated with an increased risk of delayed contrast reactions (e.g., skin rash, fever, flu-like symptoms).
*   **Beta-blockers:** May increase the risk and severity of hypersensitivity reactions and cause resistance to epinephrine/beta-agonists used in resuscitation.
*   **Neuroleptics and Antidepressants:** May lower the seizure threshold, increasing the risk of contrast-related neurological reactions.
*   **Radioactive Iodine:** Iopromide interferes with thyroid uptake of radioisotopes (I-123, I-131). Diagnostic and therapeutic efficacy may be reduced for up to 6 weeks (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

## Laboratory Test Interference

**Editorial Version**

*   **Thyroid Function Tests:** Iopromide will temporarily increase protein-bound iodine. Tests independent of iodine (e.g., $T_3$ resin uptake, free thyroxine) are not affected. *   **Investigations:** Transient fluctuations in body temperature, blood LDH, urea, hemoglobin, and white cell count have been reported (Source: Australian Product Information).

## Mechanism of Action

**Editorial Version**

Iopromide is a non-ionic contrast agent that opacifies vascular and bodily structures containing the drug by attenuating X-ray photons. In the presence of an intact blood-brain barrier (BBB), it does not diffuse into the brain; however, accumulation occurs in regions of BBB disruption (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

## Pharmacokinetics

**Editorial Version**

*   **Distribution:** Rapidly distributes into the extracellular space ($V_d$ ~16 L). Protein binding is negligible (~1%). It does not cross an intact BBB.
*   **Metabolism:** Iopromide is not metabolized.
*   **Excretion:** Eliminated almost entirely (97-98%) by the kidneys via glomerular filtration. Terminal half-life is approximately 2 hours in patients with normal renal function. Less than 2% is recovered in feces within 3 days.
*   **Renal Impairment:** Elimination is prolonged proportional to the reduction in GFR. In severe impairment, half-life may increase to approximately 11.6 hours. Iopromide is removed by hemodialysis (~60% removal over 3 hours) (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

## Pharmaceutical Particulars

**Editorial Version**

**Excipients**
Trometamol (tromethamine), sodium calcium edetate, hydrochloric acid and/or sodium hydroxide (for pH adjustment), and water for injections. Contains no antimicrobial preservatives (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

**Storage**
*   **Australia:** Store below 30°C. Protect from light and secondary X-rays.

*   **Single-use containers:** Discard any unused portion immediately after first opening.
*   **Pharmacy/Imaging Bulk Packages:** Once punctured, the contents must be used within 10 hours. Discard any unused portion thereafter (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

**Incompatibilities**
Do not mix iopromide with any other medicines or nutritional admixtures to avoid potential incompatibilities (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information).

## Patient Counselling Information

**Editorial Version**

*   **Allergic Reactions:** Advise patients that serious reactions can occur both during and after the procedure. Instruct them to report symptoms such as rash, difficulty breathing, or swelling immediately.
