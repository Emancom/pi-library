# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document provided for the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration). 
- **Note:** At the time of preparation, only the US PI was provided for review. Consequently, jurisdictional comparisons (e.g., Australian, European, Japanese) are not present in this version.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data into a concise reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a intended to be a substitute for the official, current Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    - Dosing frequency should be every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
    - Subsequent daily doses typically remain in the 50 mg/kg range, though therapy may require adjustment. The maximum recorded dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - The recommended starting dose is 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
    - Administer as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations prior to initiating parenteral therapy. Free plasma carnitine levels should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **Visual Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [US].
- **Compatibility:** Levocarnitine is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when diluted in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL [US].

### Titration and Adjustments
- In dialysis patients, dose adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment must be discontinued immediately upon a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure history. In those with a history of epilepsy, an increase in seizure frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Monitoring Requirements:** Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations is recommended [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are frequently confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic errors.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), Pain (30–49%), Headache (up to 37% in specific cohorts), Flu syndrome (25–40%), Chest pain (6–15%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (14–21%), Hypotension (3–19%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), Dyspnea (3–19%), Cough increase (10–18%).
- **Nervous:** Dizziness (10–18%).

### Common (1–10%)
- **Body as a whole:** Abdominal pain, Fever, Asthenia, Infection, Accidental injury.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, Palpitations, Arrhythmia, Atrial fibrillation.
- **Digestive:** Constipation, Dyspepsia, Anorexia, Melena.
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, Hyperkalemia, Hypervolemia, Peripheral edema, Weight changes.
- **Nervous:** Paresthesia, Insomnia, Depression, Anxiety.
- **Skin:** Pruritus, Rash.

### Postmarketing and Serious Effects
- **Neurologic:** Seizures.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, transient nausea/vomiting, and "fishy" body odor have been noted [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiation or dose adjustment of levocarnitine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a letter category in the current text, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following administration. The benefits to the mother must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders is weight-based (50 mg/kg); see Section 3 for details [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
    - The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. 
    - Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites: trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally excreted in urine [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (found in urine) and [3H]-γ-butyrobetaine (found in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an intravenous dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Bioavailability:** Absolute bioavailability of oral formulations is low, approximately 15–16% [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV levocarnitine three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis plasma concentrations from ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study of ~90 patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg post-dialysis demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks. However, the impact of this increase on specific clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing.
- **Stability:** Discard any unused portion of a single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only (Prescription only). NDC 36000-368-10 (10-vial carton) [US].
