# Editorial Product Information: Insulin Glulisine (Apidra)

**Editorial Note:**  
This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from the official regulatory documents provided by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It combines the clinical, pharmacological, and safety data from both regions to provide a comprehensive reference for healthcare professionals. Where regional guidance differs, the more specific or conservative recommendation is provided and noted accordingly.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Apidra  
**Active Ingredient:** Insulin glulisine  

Apidra is a rapid-acting recombinant human insulin analogue produced by recombinant DNA technology using *Escherichia coli*. It is supplied as a sterile, clear, colorless aqueous solution for injection (100 IU/mL).

## 2. Approved Indications
Insulin glulisine is indicated to improve glycemic control in adult and pediatric patients with diabetes mellitus (Type 1 or Type 2) who require insulin for the management of hyperglycemia. 

**Pediatric Use:** While the FDA indication broadly covers pediatric patients, clinical evidence specifically supports use in children aged 4 years and older [TGA/FDA]. Safety and efficacy have not been established in children under the age of 4 [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
Apidra is contraindicated in the following circumstances:
*   During episodes of hypoglycemia [FDA].
*   In patients with a known hypersensitivity to insulin glulisine or any of the excipients (including metacresol, trometamol, sodium chloride, and polysorbate 20).

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### General Management
Apidra must be used in a regimen that includes an intermediate- or long-acting insulin or a basal insulin analogue to maintain adequate glucose control due to its short duration of action. Any change in insulin strength, manufacturer, type, or species may necessitate a dosage adjustment under strict medical supervision.

### Hypoglycemia and Hyperglycemia
Hypoglycemia is the most frequent adverse reaction and can be life-threatening. Awareness symptoms may be altered or less pronounced in patients with long-standing diabetes, diabetic neuropathy, or those taking certain medications such as beta-blockers. 

Inadvertent administration of the wrong insulin (medication error) is a common cause of severe hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia. Patients must check the insulin label before every injection.

### Injection Site and Administration Safety
*   **Never Share Devices:** Apidra SoloStar pens or syringes must never be shared between patients, even if the needle is changed, due to the risk of transmitting blood-borne pathogens [FDA].
*   **Site Rotation:** Patients must rotate injection sites within the chosen area (abdomen, thigh, or deltoid) to reduce the risk of lipodystrophy and localized cutaneous amyloidosis. Injecting into affected areas may delay absorption and worsen glycemic control [TGA/FDA].

### Specific Risks
*   **Hypokalemia:** All insulins can cause a shift in potassium from the extracellular to the intracellular space. Untreated hypokalemia may lead to respiratory paralysis, ventricular arrhythmia, and death. Use caution in patients at risk, such as those taking potassium-lowering medications [FDA].
*   **Heart Failure:** Fluid retention and heart failure can occur when insulin is used in combination with thiazolidinediones (TZDs). Patients should be observed for signs of heart failure (e.g., shortness of breath, edema); TZD dosage reduction or discontinuation may be required [FDA].
*   **Pump Malfunction:** For patients using continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (CSII), pump or infusion set failure can rapidly lead to hyperglycemia and ketoacidosis.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
Dosage should be individualized based on metabolic needs, blood glucose monitoring, and glycemic goals. 

### Timing of Administration
Apidra should be administered subcutaneously within 15 minutes before a meal or within 20 minutes after starting a meal [FDA].

### Routes of Administration
1.  **Subcutaneous Injection:** Administer into the abdominal wall, thigh, or deltoid.
2.  **Continuous Subcutaneous Infusion (Insulin Pump):** Apidra may be used in compatible external pumps. The reservoir must be changed at least every 48 hours [FDA]. Do not dilute or mix with other insulins in the pump.
3.  **Intravenous Administration:** Must be performed only under medical supervision. Dilute to concentrations between 0.05 and 1 unit/mL in 0.9% sodium chloride using polyvinyl chloride (PVC) infusion bags [FDA].

### Mixing
Apidra can be mixed only with NPH human insulin for subcutaneous injection. Apidra should be drawn into the syringe first and the mixture injected immediately. Mixed insulins must not be administered intravenously.

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Metabolic:** Hypoglycemia (very common); weight gain; peripheral edema.
*   **Injection Site:** Localized reactions including redness, swelling, itching, and lipodystrophy.
*   **Immune System:** Localized or systemic hypersensitivity. Systemic reactions may include generalized rash, shortness of breath, wheezing, tachycardia, and anaphylaxis (rare but potentially life-threatening).
*   **Clinical Trial Observations:** Common adverse events (>5%) reported in clinical studies include nasopharyngitis, upper respiratory tract infections, influenza, and arthralgia.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Substances that may increase the blood-glucose-lowering effect (risk of hypoglycemia):** Oral antidiabetic agents, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin II receptor blockers, fibrates, fluoxetine, MAO inhibitors, salicylates, and sulfonamide antibiotics.
*   **Substances that may decrease the blood-glucose-lowering effect:** Corticosteroids, atypical antipsychotics (e.g., olanzapine, clozapine), diuretics, estrogens/progestogens, thyroid hormones, and sympathomimetic agents (e.g., epinephrine, salbutamol).
*   **Substances that may blunt signs of hypoglycemia:** Beta-blockers, clonidine, guanethidine, and reserpine.
*   **Variable effects:** Alcohol and lithium salts may either potentiate or weaken the effect of insulin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**Category B3 [TGA].** There are no adequate, well-controlled clinical studies in pregnant women. Insulin requirements typically decrease in the first trimester and increase during the second and third trimesters. Intensive glucose monitoring is essential. Animal studies show that at high doses, skeletal defects occurred in rabbits, though these were associated with maternal hypoglycemia [TGA].

### Breastfeeding
It is not known whether insulin glulisine is excreted in human milk, though many drugs, including human insulin, are. Lactating women may require adjustments in insulin dosage and dietary intake.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Following subcutaneous administration, Apidra is absorbed approximately twice as fast as regular human insulin, reaching peak concentrations in approximately 55–60 minutes.
*   **Bioavailability:** The absolute bioavailability is approximately 70% and is consistent across injection sites (abdomen 73%, deltoid 71%, thigh 68%).
*   **Distribution/Elimination:** Following intravenous administration, the volume of distribution is 13 L. The elimination half-life after subcutaneous injection is significantly shorter (approx. 42 minutes) than that of regular human insulin (approx. 86 minutes) [FDA].
*   **Special Populations:** Pharmacokinetic properties are generally maintained in patients with renal impairment, though insulin requirements may be reduced.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
The primary activity of insulin glulisine is the regulation of glucose metabolism. It lowers blood glucose by:
1.  Stimulating peripheral glucose uptake by skeletal muscle and adipose tissue.
2.  Inhibiting hepatic glucose production.
3.  Inhibiting lipolysis and proteolysis while enhancing protein synthesis.

Apidra is equipotent to regular human insulin when administered intravenously but has a more rapid onset and shorter duration of action when given subcutaneously.

## 11. Storage Conditions
### Unopened (Not in-use)
Store in a refrigerator between 2°C and 8°C. Do not freeze. Protect from light.

### Opened (In-use)
*   **Vials:** May be stored refrigerated or at room temperature (up to 25°C/77°F) for up to 28 days.
*   **Pens/Cartridges:** Must NOT be refrigerated once in use. Store at room temperature (up to 25°C/77°F) for up to 28 days.
*   **Insulin Pump:** Change the insulin in the reservoir at least every 48 hours. Discard if exposed to temperatures exceeding 37°C (98.6°F) [FDA].
