# Originality Audit Report: insulin glulisine

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 72/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 5
- **Overall Assessment:** Concern

## Verbatim Copied Passages

1.  **Safety Warning (Never Share Devices)**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "Apidra SoloStar pens or syringes must never be shared between patients, even if the needle is changed, due to the risk of transmitting blood-borne pathogens."
    *   **Source Match:** Document 2 (FDA), Section 5.1: "APIDRA SoloStar pens must never be shared between patients, even if the needle is changed... Sharing poses a risk for transmission of blood-borne pathogens."
    *   **Location:** Section 4, "Injection Site and Administration Safety".

2.  **Clinical Warning (Hypokalemia)**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "All insulins can cause a shift in potassium from the extracellular to the intracellular space. Untreated hypokalemia may lead to respiratory paralysis, ventricular arrhythmia, and death."
    *   **Source Match:** Document 2 (FDA), Section 5.5: "All insulins... cause a shift in potassium from the extracellular to intracellular space, possibly leading to hypokalemia. Untreated hypokalemia may cause respiratory paralysis, ventricular arrhythmia, and death."
    *   **Location:** Section 4, "Specific Risks".

3.  **Dosing Timing**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "within 15 minutes before a meal or within 20 minutes after starting a meal"
    *   **Source Match:** Document 2 (FDA), Section 2.2: "Inject within 15 minutes before a meal or within 20 minutes after starting a meal."
    *   **Location:** Section 5, "Timing of Administration".

4.  **Intravenous Dilution Instructions**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "Dilute to concentrations between 0.05 and 1 unit/mL in 0.9% sodium chloride using polyvinyl chloride (PVC) infusion bags"
    *   **Source Match:** Document 2 (FDA), Section 2.2: "diluting to concentrations from 0.05 to 1 unit/mL APIDRA in 0.9% sodium chloride injection, USP using polyvinyl chloride (PVC) infusion bags."
    *   **Location:** Section 5, "Routes of Administration (3)".

5.  **Mechanism of Action**
    *   **Editorial Text:** "Stimulating peripheral glucose uptake by skeletal muscle and adipose tissue. Inhibiting hepatic glucose production. Inhibiting lipolysis and proteolysis while enhancing protein synthesis."
    *   **Source Match:** Document 1 (TGA), Section 5.1 / Document 2 (FDA), Section 12.1: "lower blood glucose by stimulating peripheral glucose uptake by skeletal muscle and fat, and by inhibiting hepatic glucose production. Insulins inhibit lipolysis... inhibit proteolysis and enhance protein synthesis."
    *   **Location:** Section 10, "Mechanism of Action".

## Close Paraphrases
*   **Adverse Effects (Common Events):** The list of nasopharyngitis, URI, influenza, and arthralgia (Section 6) mirrors the exact order and content of Table 1 in the FDA document and Table 1 in the TGA document.
*   **Pregnancy Category B3:** The description of skeletal defects in rabbits associated with maternal hypoglycemia (Section 8) is very close to the phrasing in TGA Section 4.6.
*   **Pharmacokinetics:** The bioavailability percentages (Abdomen 73%, Deltoid 71%, Thigh 68%) are taken directly from the data tables without modification in Section 9.

## Originality Strengths
*   **Structural Synthesis:** The editorial successfully creates a new organizational flow that does not strictly follow the "Full Prescribing Information" or "Product Information" templates of either agency.
*   **Regional Harmonization:** The editorial does excellent work in Section 2 (Approved Indications) by reconciling the FDA's broad pediatric indication with the TGA's specific "4 years and older" clinical evidence threshold.
*   **Drug Interactions:** Section 7 categorizes substances by their effect on blood glucose (increase vs. decrease effect) in a clearer, bulleted synthesis than the source tables.

## Differences from Source PIs
*   **Unified Formatting:** Merges the TGA "Pregnancy Category B3" with the FDA's more descriptive "Risk Summary" data.
*   **Editorial Notes:** Adds a framing introduction explaining the synthesis of Australian and US data.
*   **Conciseness:** Omitted extensive clinical trial tables (e.g., Study 3001, 3002, 3004 results) in favor of a summary of common adverse events and PK/PD values.
*   **Terminology:** Standardizes "human insulin" and "basal insulin analogue" across the text where sources occasionally used brand-specific or more varied terminology.

## Recommendation
**REVISION NEEDED.** 

While the editorial is a high-quality synthesis of information, it fails the "strict originality" test by copying several critical safety warnings and technical instructions verbatim from the FDA document (specifically the Hypokalemia warning and IV dilution instructions). In regulatory writing, while accuracy is paramount, an "editorial product" should rephrase these standard blocks to avoid copyright/plagiarism concerns while maintaining clinical accuracy.

**Action items:**
1.  Rephrase the "Hypokalemia" and "Never Share Devices" warnings to capture the medical necessity without using the exact FDA sentence structures.
2.  Rewrite the "Mechanism of Action" and "IV Administration" sections to be descriptive of the process rather than a direct copy of the technical manual instructions.
