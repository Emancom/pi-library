# Editorial Product Information: Itraconazole

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single, accessible reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for professional reference. It does not replace the official PI used for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult local regulations for legal prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Itraconazole, a synthetic triazole antifungal agent.
- **Chemical Class:** Triazole derivative.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Itraconazole [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** J02A C02.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 100 mg hard gelatin capsules filled with pellets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Itraconazole is indicated for use in adults for the following [Australian-approved]:
- **Superficial Dermatomycoses:** When unresponsive to topical treatment (e.g., Tinea corporis, Tinea cruris, Tinea pedis, Tinea manus).
- **Fungal Keratitis:** Progressing rapidly, sight-threatening, or unresponsive to topical therapy.
- **Pityriasis Versicolor:** Unresponsive to other treatments.
- **Candidiasis:** Vulvovaginal candidiasis (unresponsive to topical therapy), oral candidiasis in immunocompromised patients, and oropharyngeal/oesophageal candidiasis when first-line systemic therapy is inappropriate.
- **Onychomycosis:** Caused by dermatophytes.
- **Systemic Mycoses:** Including systemic aspergillosis, histoplasmosis (including maintenance in AIDS patients), and lymphocutaneous/cutaneous sporotrichosis.
- **Non-invasive Candidiasis:** In non-neutropenic patients when first-line systemic therapy is ineffective or inappropriate.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
For maximal absorption, capsules **must be taken immediately after a full meal** and swallowed whole [Australian]. 

### Adult Dosing Schedules [Australian]
- **Tinea corporis/cruris:** 100 mg daily for 2 weeks.
- **Tinea pedis/manus:** 100 mg daily for 4 weeks.
- **Fungal keratitis:** 200 mg once daily for 3 weeks.
- **Pityriasis versicolor:** 200 mg once daily for 1 week.
- **Vulvovaginal candidiasis:** 200 mg twice daily for 1 day, OR 200 mg once daily for 3 days.
- **Oral candidiasis (Immunocompromised):** 100–200 mg daily for 4 weeks.
- **Onychomycosis (Continuous):** 200 mg once daily for 3 months.
- **Onychomycosis (Pulse Therapy):** 200 mg twice daily for 1 week.
    - Fingernails: Two pulses (separated by 3 drug-free weeks).
    - Toenails: Three pulses (separated by 3 drug-free weeks).
- **Systemic Aspergillosis:** 200 mg once daily; increase to 200 mg twice daily for invasive disease.
- **Histoplasmosis:** 200 mg once or twice daily.

### Administration Nuances
- **Decreased Gastric Acidity:** Absorption is impaired. If using acid-neutralising medicines (e.g., aluminium hydroxide), administer them at least 2 hours after itraconazole. In patients with achlorhydria or those on PPIs/H2-antagonists, it is advised to administer the capsules with a cola beverage [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **CYP3A4 Substrates:** Co-administration with drugs that may lead to QT prolongation and ventricular tachyarrhythmias (e.g., terfenadine, astemizole, cisapride, quinidine, pimozide, domperidone).
- **HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors:** Simvastatin and lovastatin.
- **Ergot Alkaloids:** Ergotamine, dihydroergotamine.
- **Specific Medications:** Lurasidone, ticagrelor, oral midazolam, triazolam, and others [Refer to Section 7].
- **Cardiac Dysfunction:** Patients with evidence of ventricular dysfunction, such as congestive heart failure (CHF), or a history of CHF (except in life-threatening infections) [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated except in life-threatening systemic mycoses [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known allergy to the drug or excipients (e.g., sugars and sulfites).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiac Effects:** Itraconazole has a negative inotropic effect and has been associated with reports of CHF. Risk increases with a total daily dose of 400 mg. Use with extreme caution in patients with CHF risk factors (cardiac disease, renal failure, COPD).
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Rare cases of serious hepatotoxicity, including fatal acute liver failure, have occurred. Monitor liver function in patients with pre-existing abnormalities. Patients should report symptoms such as anorexia, nausea, jaundice, or dark urine immediately.
- **Hearing Loss:** Transient or permanent hearing loss has been reported; typically resolves upon discontinuation but may persist.
- **Interchangeability:** Itraconazole capsules and oral solution should **not** be used interchangeably due to differences in bioavailability [Australian].
- **Neuropathy:** Discontinue treatment if peripheral neuropathy occurs.
- **Cross-resistance:** In systemic candidiasis, sensitivity testing is recommended if fluconazole-resistant strains are suspected.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Common (>1%):** Headache, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, dyspepsia, and reversible increases in hepatic enzymes [Australian].
- **Uncommon (<1%):** Sinusitis, upper respiratory tract infection, flatulence, abnormal hepatic function, pollakiuria, and erectile dysfunction.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Allergic reactions (pruritus, rash, angioedema), menstrual disorders.
- **Very Rare (<0.01%):** Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, anaphylaxis, hypokalaemia, peripheral neuropathy, visual disturbances, tinnitus, congestive heart failure, pulmonary oedema, pancreatitis, and serious skin reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis).

## 7. Drug Interactions
Itraconazole is a potent inhibitor of **CYP3A4** and **P-glycoprotein**.
- **Contraindicated for 2 weeks after treatment:** Many interactions persist after stopping itraconazole due to its long half-life.
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Rifampicin, phenytoin, and carbamazepine significantly reduce itraconazole bioavailability. Efficacy may be compromised.
- **Enzyme Inhibitors:** Erythromycin and clarithromycin may increase itraconazole concentrations.
- **Calcium Channel Blockers:** Increased risk of CHF; caution is required with dihydropyridines and verapamil.
- **Specific Interaction Management:**
    - **Anticoagulants:** Monitor for bleeding (e.g., warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban).
    - **Digoxin:** Monitor for toxicity; dose reduction may be required.
    - **Statins:** Lovastatin/simvastatin are strictly contraindicated [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B3 [Australian]**. Teratogenic effects (skeletal defects, encephaloceles) were observed in animal studies. Itraconazole must not be used unless for life-threatening infections where benefit outweighs foetal risk. Women of childbearing potential must use highly effective contraception during and for one menstrual cycle after therapy.
- **Breastfeeding:** Small amounts are excreted in human milk (calculated infant exposure is ~450 times lower than maternal). Weigh the benefits of therapy against the potential risks of breastfeeding [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established. Not recommended unless benefits outweigh risks. Note: Bone toxicity observed in rat studies [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Limited data. Use only if benefits outweigh risks; consider higher frequency of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction.
- **Renal Impairment:** Bioavailability may be reduced. Monitor closely; dose adjustment may be considered.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Predominantly metabolised in the liver. Prolonged elimination half-life observed in cirrhotic patients (approx. 37 hours). Monitor for both hepatotoxicity and drug interactions.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Inhibits the cytochrome P450-dependent synthesis of ergosterol, a vital component of fungal cell membranes.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~55%; maximal when taken with food.
    - **Distribution:** Extensive tissue distribution; concentrations in lungs, liver, and bone are 2–3 times higher than plasma. Minimal penetration into CSF.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively hepatic via CYP3A4; major metabolite is hydroxy-itraconazole (active).
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic; terminal half-life of 1.5 to 2 days. 35% excreted as metabolites in urine; 3–18% as parent drug in faeces.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Histoplasmosis:** Studies showed ~100% response in 136 patients (including HIV-infected) at doses of 200–400 mg/day for 8 months.
- **Onychomycosis:** 12-week continuous treatment (200 mg/day) achieved mycological cure in 54% of patients. Pulse therapy (3 pulses for toenails) showed similar efficacy to continuous treatment.
- **Aspergillosis:** Overall response rate of 63% across nine open-label studies for various syndromes (e.g., pulmonary aspergilloma, invasive disease).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C.
- **Conditions:** Protect from light and moisture.
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on packaging [Australian].
