# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine Injection

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)** via Baxter Healthcare Corporation.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for specific legal and prescribing requirements in their region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter) [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation (Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd) [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Inborn Errors of Metabolism):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Administration
- Administered intravenously as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [United States].
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration [United States].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [United States].
- **Acute Crisis:** A loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [United States].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are recommended in the range of 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. The highest reported dose administered is 300 mg/kg [United States].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine levels (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L), blood chemistry, and vital signs weekly or monthly [United States].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [United States].
- **Administration Timing:** Administer into the venous return line after each dialysis session [United States].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma concentrations <40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) should be guided by trough levels and may be implemented as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [United States].

### Missed Dose
- Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** Not specified as a formal contraindication in the provided source, but the source notes "None known" under the Contraindications section [United States]. However, see Warnings regarding hypersensitivity reactions.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions can occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [United States].
- **Neurological (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [United States].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Caution):** While the injection is indicated for ESRD, the source notes that chronic high-dose **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [United States].
- **Monitoring:** Pre-dialysis carnitine levels should be monitored to guide dosing in ESRD [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Commonly Observed:** Transient nausea and vomiting. Body odor and gastritis are also reported but occur less frequently [United States].
- **Clinical Trial Data (ESRD/Dialysis):** In double-blind, placebo-controlled trials, the following adverse events occurred at a frequency of $\ge$5% (regardless of causality) [United States]:
    - **Body as a whole:** Abdominal pain (21%), Headache (37% at 20mg dose), Infection (24%), Injection site reaction (38%), Pain (35%), Fever, Asthenia.
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (15–19%), Tachycardia (5–9%), Chest pain.
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%).
    - **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), Hypervolemia (3–12%).
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%).
    - **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), Paresthesia (3–12%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Clinical Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not assigned by letter, but noted as use only if clearly needed]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits to the mother [United States].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders are based on mg/kg, which applies to paediatric patients [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific dose adjustments for the elderly are not detailed in the available source; however, clinical monitoring is advised as per standard adult protocols.
- **Renal Impairment:** Safely used in ESRD patients on dialysis (primary indication). The safety of chronic **oral** administration in renal failure is not established due to metabolite accumulation [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn metabolic errors, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [United States].
    - **Elimination:** Following a 20 mg/kg IV bolus, approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly showed that trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [United States].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses over 24 weeks demonstrated dose-proportionate increases in trough carnitine concentrations [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [United States].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) at room temperature [United States].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion [United States].
