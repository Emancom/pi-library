# Editorial Product Information: Ipratropium Bromide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ipratropium bromide for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises available data into a single clinical reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for definitive regulatory guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ipratropium bromide (as monohydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Anticholinergic (parasympatholytic) bronchodilator; a quaternary isopropyl derivative of atropine.
- **Brand Name(s):** Apo-Ipratropium [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Nebuliser solution: 250 µg/mL and 500 µg/mL (equivalent to anhydrous ipratropium bromide) [Australian]. 
    - The solution is a clear, aqueous, isotonic, and preservative-free liquid supplied in single-use plastic ampoules (1 mL) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Inhalation via nebulisation.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Aspen Pharma Pty Ltd (Licensee: Apotex Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Ipratropium bromide nebuliser solution is indicated for the following conditions [Australian]:
- Management of moderate asthmatic attacks.
- Chronic forms of asthma.
- Asthma in patients with diminished cardiac reserve.
- Chronic obstructive bronchitis associated with bronchospasm.
- Management of bronchospasm occurring during or after surgery.
- Use during assisted ventilation with a respirator.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adults)
- **Standard Dose:** 250 µg to 500 µg administered every 6 hours [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** Daily doses exceeding 2 mg should be administered under medical supervision [Australian].
- **Titration:** In cases of moderate bronchospasm or during assisted ventilation, the lower range (250 µg) is recommended. For severely distressed patients, 500 µg is used to achieve optimal bronchodilation [Australian].
- **Frequency:** Inhalation may be repeated every 2 hours if necessary [Australian].

### Paediatric Dosing
- **Children under 12 years:** 250 µg administered in the same manner as adults [Australian].
- **Maximum Dose:** Daily doses exceeding 1 mg should be administered under medical supervision [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Method:** Administered via commercially available nebulising devices. If wall oxygen is utilised, a flow rate of 4 to 6 L/min is recommended [Australian].
- **Dilution:** The dose should be diluted to a final volume of 2 to 3 mL with normal saline and nebulised until the entire volume is inhaled [Australian].
- **Preparation:** Solutions must be freshly prepared; any remaining solution in the nebuliser must be discarded after use [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to atropine or its derivatives (e.g., ipratropium) [Australian].
- Hypersensitivity to any excipients in the formulation (e.g., sodium chloride, hydrochloric acid) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **General Anticholinergic Effects:** Use with caution in patients where anticholinergic effects may exacerbate pre-existing conditions, such as narrow iridocorneal angles (risk of acute angle-closure glaucoma), urinary retention, or constipation [Australian].
- **Ocular Complications:** Direct contact of the solution or mist with the eyes must be avoided. Ocular exposure can lead to mydriasis, increased intraocular pressure, and acute angle-closure glaucoma. Patients must be instructed on the correct use of the nebuliser and mask [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Immediate hypersensitivity reactions (urticaria, angioedema, rash, bronchospasm, oropharyngeal oedema, and anaphylaxis) may occur [Australian].
- **Cystic Fibrosis:** Patients with cystic fibrosis may be more susceptible to gastrointestinal motility disturbances [Australian].
- **Prostatic Hyperplasia:** Use with caution in patients with prostatic hyperplasia or bladder-neck obstruction [Australian].
- **Paradoxical Bronchospasm:** As with other inhalation therapies, paradoxical bronchoconstriction may occur and requires immediate cessation of the drug [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by system organ class [Australian]:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Headache, dizziness (common in trials) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Tachycardia, palpitations, supraventricular tachycardia, atrial fibrillation (rarely reported in susceptible patients) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea (common); dry mouth, constipation, diarrhoea, vomiting (rarely reported) |
| **Respiratory** | Cough, throat irritation, paradoxical bronchoconstriction, laryngospasm |
| **Skin/Appendages** | Urticaria (including giant urticaria), pruritus, rash, angioedema of the tongue, lips, and face |
| **Special Senses** | Mydriasis, increased intraocular pressure, eye pain, blurred vision, visual halos, acute angle-closure glaucoma (associated with direct eye contact) |
| **Urogenital** | Urinary retention (rarely reported; increased risk with outflow tract obstruction) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Beta-blockers:** Ipratropium remains effective for bronchodilation even in patients receiving beta-blocking agents [Australian].
- **Beta-adrenergics and Xanthines:** These preparations may intensify the bronchodilatory effects of ipratropium [Australian].
- **Combination Risk:** The risk of acute glaucoma in patients with a history of narrow-angle glaucoma may be increased if nebulised ipratropium is administered simultaneously with beta-mimetics [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Classified as **Category B1** [Australian]. Care is recommended, particularly during the first trimester. Animal studies (rats, mice, rabbits) have shown no embryotoxic or teratogenic effects [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies regarding the excretion of ipratropium into human breast milk are unavailable. Use with caution [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Data regarding effects on human fertility are not available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Recommended dose for children under 12 is 250 µg [Australian]. (See Section 3).
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available in the provided source [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments for renal or hepatic impairment are not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ipratropium bromide is an anticholinergic agent that inhibits cholinergic bronchomotor tone by blocking vagal reflexes that mediate bronchoconstriction. It has lower lipid solubility compared to atropine due to its quaternary isopropyl group [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - **Onset of action:** 3 to 5 minutes [Australian].
    - **Peak response:** 1.5 to 2 hours [Australian].
    - **Duration of action:** Up to 6 hours [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Approximately 5% of an inhaled dose (via MDI) is absorbed systemically; plasma levels are very low. Systemic levels may be slightly higher with nebulisation due to the larger doses used [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Most of the inhaled dose is swallowed, unabsorbed, and excreted in the faeces [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Eight metabolites with little to no anticholinergic activity have been identified [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** The elimination half-life is approximately 3.5 hours (range 1.5 to 4 hours) in healthy volunteers [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific pivotal trial data are detailed in the provided source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** 2 years [Australian].
- **Handling:** Protect from light. Ampoules are for single use only; discard any unused portion [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
