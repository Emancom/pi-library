# Editorial Product Information: Indometacin

## Editorial Note
- The following editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)** regulatory authority. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical, pharmacological, and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details and legal requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Indometacin (a methylated indole derivative).
- **Chemical Class:** Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID).
- **Brand Names:** **Arthrexin** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** M01AB01.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Hard capsules: 25 mg [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Alphapharm Pty Ltd (trading as Viatris) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Indometacin** is indicated for the treatment of active stages of the following conditions [Australian]:
- Rheumatoid arthritis.
- Osteoarthritis.
- Degenerative joint disease of the hip.
- Ankylosing spondylitis.
- Acute gouty arthritis.
- Acute musculoskeletal disorders (e.g., bursitis, tendonitis, synovitis, tenosynovitis, capsulitis of the shoulder, sprains, and strains).
- Low back pain (lumbago).
- Inflammation, pain, and oedema following orthopaedic surgical procedures or nonsurgical reduction/immobilisation of fractures or dislocations.
- Pain and associated symptoms of primary dysmenorrhoea.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
The recommended daily dosage ranges from 50 mg to 200 mg in divided doses. Dosage must be individualised based on patient response and tolerance. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest possible duration.

### Dosing by Indication [Australian]
- **Chronic Rheumatic Disorders:** Therapy should be initiated at low doses and increased gradually over one month if necessary.
- **Persistent Night Pain/Morning Stiffness:** A dose of up to 100 mg at bedtime may be utilised.
- **Acute Gouty Arthritis:** 150 mg to 200 mg daily until all symptoms subside.
- **Primary Dysmenorrhoea:** 25 mg three times daily, starting with the onset of symptoms and continuing for the duration of the cramps/bleeding.

### Administration Instructions
- To minimise gastrointestinal (GI) irritation, **indometacin** should be taken with food, milk, or an antacid [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to indometacin or any excipients [Australian].
- History of acute asthma, urticaria, or rhinitis precipitated by acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin) or other NSAIDs [Australian].
- Severe heart failure [Australian].
- Severe hepatic impairment [Australian].
- Active peptic ulcer or a history of recurrent gastrointestinal ulceration [Australian].
- Treatment of peri-operative pain in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular (CV) Thrombotic Events:** NSAIDs may increase the risk of serious CV events, including myocardial infarction and stroke. Risk increases with dose, duration, and pre-existing CV risk factors [Australian].
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Effects:** Risk of potentially fatal GI bleeding, ulceration, and perforation. Use with extreme caution in patients with a history of GI disease or the elderly [Australian].
- **Hypertension:** May lead to new-onset or worsening of pre-existing hypertension. Blood pressure should be monitored closely [Australian].
- **Severe Skin Reactions:** Potentially fatal reactions, including exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS), may occur without warning [Australian].
- **Renal Effects:** Long-term use may result in renal papillary necrosis. Use with caution in patients with renal impairment, heart failure, or dehydration. Close monitoring of renal function is required in advanced renal disease [Australian].
- **Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects:** Headache, dizziness, and light-headedness are common. Patients should be cautioned against operating motor vehicles if these occur [Australian].
- **Ocular Effects:** Corneal deposits and retinal disturbances have been observed with prolonged therapy. Periodic ophthalmological exams are recommended for long-term users [Australian].
- **Masking of Infection:** The anti-inflammatory and antipyretic properties of **indometacin** may mask signs of infection [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **CNS (Very Common/Common):** Headache (usually early in treatment), dizziness, light-headedness, depression, vertigo, and fatigue [Australian].
- **GI (Very Common/Common):** Nausea, anorexia, vomiting, epigastric distress, abdominal pain, constipation, and diarrhoea [Australian].
- **CV/Renal (Uncommon/Infrequent):** Oedema, hypertension, tachycardia, arrhythmia, and BUN elevation [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity (Infrequent/Rare):** Pruritus, urticaria, angiitis, asthma, and anaphylaxis [Australian].
- **Haematological (Infrequent/Rare):** Leucopenia, purpura, aplastic/haemolytic anaemia, and thrombocytopenia [Australian].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Peptic ulceration, GI haemorrhage, SJS/TEN/DRESS, acute renal failure, and hepatotoxicity (rarely fatal) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Acetylsalicylic Acid (Aspirin):** Not recommended; increases GI toxicity without therapeutic gain and may lower indometacin blood levels [Australian].
- **Diflunisal:** Concomitant use is associated with fatal GI haemorrhage and significantly increased indometacin plasma levels; avoid use [Australian].
- **Other NSAIDs:** Avoid due to increased GI toxicity [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants (e.g., Warfarin):** Increased risk of serious, potentially fatal haemorrhage. Monitor prothrombin time closely [Australian].
- **Lithium:** Increases plasma lithium levels and reduces renal clearance; monitor for lithium toxicity [Australian].
- **Methotrexate:** May increase methotrexate toxicity by decreasing tubular secretion [Australian].
- **Ciclosporin:** Increased risk of nephrotoxicity [Australian].
- **Diuretics and Antihypertensives:** Indometacin may attenuate the effects of loop, potassium-sparing, and thiazide diuretics, as well as ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and beta-blockers [Australian].
- **"Triple Whammy":** Concurrent use of an ACE inhibitor/ARB, a diuretic, and an NSAID increases the risk of renal impairment significantly [Australian].
- **Digoxin:** May raise serum digoxin concentrations and prolong half-life [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category C** [Australian]. 
    - NSAIDs should not be used in the third trimester as they may cause premature closure of the ductus arteriosus and pulmonary hypertension [Australian]. 
    - From 20 weeks gestation, use may cause fetal renal dysfunction leading to oligohydramnios [Australian].
    - Generally not recommended throughout pregnancy unless the benefit outweighs the risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Indometacin is excreted in breast milk; use is not recommended during lactation [Australian].
- **Fertility:** May delay or prevent ovulation; consider withdrawal in women having difficulty conceiving [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety in children under two years of age has not been established. Closely monitor liver function if used in older children [Australian].
- **Elderly:** Increased risk of adverse effects; use with greater caution and at the lowest effective dose [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not recommended in advanced renal disease. If used, monitor renal function closely [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated in severe hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A potent inhibitor of prostaglandin synthesis in vitro and in vivo. Provides anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antipyretic effects [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Virtually 100% bioavailable; peak plasma concentrations reached in ~2 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 90% protein bound [Australian].
    - **Metabolism:** Undergoes demethylation and debenzoylation to unconjugated metabolites [Australian].
    - **Elimination:** Excreted via renal (60%), biliary, and faecal (33%) routes. Undergoes enterohepatic circulation. Mean half-life is ~4.5 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Anti-Inflammatory Activity:** Demonstrated in animal models (carrageenin-induced oedema) to be more potent than aspirin or hydrocortisone. Correlation with human antirheumatic activity is well-established [Australian].
- **Antipyretic/Analgesic:** Clinical observation confirms efficacy in various febrile conditions and inflammatory pain [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** **Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine)** [Australian].
