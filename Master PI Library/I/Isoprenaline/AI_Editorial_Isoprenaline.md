# Editorial Product Information: Isoprenaline

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the Australian PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Isoprenaline hydrochloride (a synthetic sympathomimetic amine).
- **Brand Names:** Cipla Isoprenaline [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Solution for injection: 0.2 mg (200 micrograms) in 1 mL (1:5000) [Australian].
  - Solution for injection: 1.0 mg in 5 mL (1:5000) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (infusion or bolus), intramuscular, subcutaneous, and intracardiac (emergency use only).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Cipla Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Isoprenaline is indicated for the following clinical scenarios:
- **Heart Block:** Treatment of mild or transient episodes that do not require electrical therapy; management of serious episodes of heart block and Adams-Stokes attacks (except those caused by ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation) [Australian].
- **Cardiac Arrest:** Temporary use until electric shock or pacemaker therapy is available [Australian].
- **Bronchospasm:** Management of bronchospasm occurring during anaesthesia [Australian].
- **Shock and Hypoperfusion:** As an adjunct to fluid/electrolyte replacement and other therapies in the treatment of hypovolaemic and septic shock, low cardiac output (hypoperfusion) states, congestive heart failure, and cardiogenic shock [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
Therapy should be initiated at the lowest recommended dose and titrated gradually based on clinical response and intensive patient monitoring.

### Adult Dosage
- **Shock and Hypoperfusion States:**
  - **IV Infusion:** Dilute 1 mg (5 mL) in 500 mL of 5% Glucose. Initial rate: 0.5 μg per minute. Rates exceeding 30 μg/min may be used in advanced shock.
- **Heart Block, Adams-Stokes Attacks, and Cardiac Arrest:**
  - **IV Bolus:** Dilute 0.2 mg (1 mL) to 10 mL (with Sodium Chloride or 5% Glucose). Initial dose: 0.02 mg to 0.06 mg. Range: 0.01 mg to 0.2 mg.
  - **IV Infusion:** Dilute 2 mg (10 mL) in 500 mL 5% Glucose. Initial rate: 5 μg/min.
  - **Intramuscular:** 0.2 mg (undiluted). Range: 0.02 mg to 1 mg.
  - **Subcutaneous:** 0.2 mg (undiluted). Range: 0.15 mg to 0.2 mg.
  - **Intracardiac:** 0.02 mg (0.1 mL undiluted) in dire emergencies.
- **Bronchospasm during Anaesthesia:**
  - **IV Bolus:** Dilute 0.2 mg (1 mL) to 10 mL. Initial dose: 0.01 mg to 0.02 mg; repeat as necessary.

### Paediatric Dosage
- Controlled studies are unavailable; however, the American Heart Association recommends an initial infusion rate of 0.1 μg/kg/min to 1.0 μg/kg/min [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter and discolouration before use. 
- Solutions must be clear and colourless.
- Rate of infusion should be adjusted based on heart rate, central venous pressure (CVP), systemic blood pressure, and urine flow.

## 4. Contraindications
Isoprenaline is contraindicated in patients with:
- Tachyarrhythmias, tachycardia, or heart block caused by digitalis intoxication [Australian].
- Ventricular arrhythmias requiring inotropic therapy [Australian].
- Recent myocardial infarction [Australian].
- Angina pectoris [Australian].
- Hypersensitivity to isoprenaline or any excipients in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **Myocardial Demand:** Isoprenaline increases myocardial work and oxygen consumption, which may be detrimental in patients with coronary artery occlusion or cardiogenic shock [Australian].
- **Paradoxical Effects:** In patients with organic disease of the A-V node, the drug may paradoxically precipitate Adams-Stokes seizures [Australian].
- **Shock Management:** Volume expansion must precede isoprenaline use to ensure adequate intravascular filling [Australian].
- **Monitoring:** Continuous monitoring of ECG, heart rate (maintain <110 bpm), systemic blood pressure, CVP, urine flow, and blood gases is essential [Australian].
- **Asthma:** Use in severe asthma requires concurrent oxygen administration. Monitor for myocardial ischaemia (ECG changes and CPK-MB elevation), especially in children [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Serious adverse effects are reported as infrequent.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, palpitations, angina, Adams-Stokes attacks, hypertension, hypotension, ventricular arrhythmias, and pulmonary oedema. Isoprenaline may increase ischaemic injury in the setting of acute MI [Australian].
- **CNS:** Nervousness, headache, dizziness, restlessness, tension, fear, light-headedness, asthenia, and rarely, tinnitus [Australian].
- **General:** Hot flashes, skin flushing, sweating, mild tremors, weakness, nausea, and vomiting [Australian].
- **Serious Effects:** Fatal cardiac dysrhythmia and myocardial necrosis have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Adrenaline/Digitalis:** Do not administer simultaneously with isoprenaline due to additive cardiac stimulation and risk of serious arrhythmias. May be used alternately with an appropriate interval [Australian].
- **Inhalational Anaesthetics (e.g., Halothane, Cyclopropane):** Sensitise the myocardium to sympathomimetics; use with extreme caution or avoid [Australian].
- **MAOIs and Chlorpromazine:** May magnify the effects of isoprenaline [Australian].
- **Methylxanthines (Aminophylline/Theophylline) and Corticosteroids:** Concurrent use may lead to additive cardiotoxicity, myocardial necrosis, and death [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian] Category A. No evidence of teratogenic effects in over 25 years of use; however, benefit must outweigh risk [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if isoprenaline is excreted in breast milk. Exercise caution [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and dosage not established by controlled trials; follow clinical guidelines (e.g., AHA) and monitor for ischaemia in asthma [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** May be more sensitive to sympathomimetics; careful dose adjustment is required, particularly in those with hypertension, diabetes, or cardiovascular disease [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments not specified in available sources, though metabolism is primarily hepatic (COMT) and excretion is renal [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A potent, non-selective beta-adrenergic agonist. It acts on $\beta_1$-receptors in the heart (positive inotropic/chronotropic effects) and $\beta_2$-receptors in the bronchi, gastrointestinal tract, and skeletal muscle vasculature [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Increases cardiac output and venous return. Lowers peripheral vascular resistance and diastolic blood pressure [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
  - **Half-life:** Brief (minutes after IV; up to 2 hours after SC).
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily via catechol-ortho-methyl transferase (COMT) in the liver to 3-O-methylisoprenaline (weak beta-blocking activity).
  - **Elimination:** Metabolites excreted through the kidneys [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No clinical trial data were specified in the available source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light. Keep in the original outer carton [Australian].
- **Stability of Diluted Solutions:** Should be prepared immediately before use. Diluted solutions are stable at 25°C for 24 hours [Australian].
- **Handling:** Single-use only. Discard any residue [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Australia:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
