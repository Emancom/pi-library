# Editorial Product Information: Ivabradine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for ivabradine. It is designed as a high-level clinical reference for healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is intended for informational purposes and does not replace the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing decisions in any specific region. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ivabradine (present as hydrochloride).
- **Chemical Class:** Hyperpolarisation-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated (HCN) channel blocker; selective cardiac pacemaker $I_f$ current inhibitor.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Ivabradine [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C01EB17.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 5 mg (salmon, capsule-shaped, scored) and 7.5 mg (salmon, round) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Chronic Stable Angina:** Symptomatic treatment of chronic stable angina pectoris due to atherosclerotic coronary artery disease in adult patients with normal sinus rhythm and a resting heart rate $\ge$ 70 bpm. Indicated for patients who are intolerant of or have a contraindication to beta-blockers, or in combination with atenolol 50 mg once daily when symptoms are inadequately controlled [Australian].
- **Chronic Heart Failure:** Treatment of symptomatic chronic heart failure (NYHA Class II or III) with documented left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) $\le$ 35% in adult patients in sinus rhythm with a resting heart rate $\ge$ 77 bpm, in combination with optimal standard therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Should be taken twice daily, once in the morning and once in the evening, with food to reduce intra-individual variability in exposure [Australian].

### Chronic Stable Angina
- **Initial Dose:** $\le$ 5 mg twice daily for patients under 75 years of age [Australian].
- **Titration:** After 3–4 weeks, if the patient remains symptomatic and the initial dose is well-tolerated (resting heart rate remains > 60 bpm), the dose may be increased to 7.5 mg twice daily.
- **Maintenance Dose:** Should not exceed 7.5 mg twice daily.
- **Discontinuation:** Treatment should be stopped if symptoms do not improve within 3 months, or if the heart rate reduction is not clinically relevant [Australian].

### Chronic Heart Failure
- **Initial Dose:** 5 mg twice daily [Australian].
- **Titration:** After 2 weeks, the dose should be reviewed. If resting heart rate is persistently > 60 bpm, increase to 7.5 mg twice daily. If heart rate is between 50–60 bpm, maintain 5 mg twice daily. If heart rate is < 50 bpm or symptoms of bradycardia occur, decrease to 2.5 mg twice daily [Australian].

### Missed Dose
- The next scheduled dose should be taken at the usual time; doses must not be doubled to make up for a missed dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to ivabradine or any excipients [Australian].
- Resting heart rate < 70 bpm prior to treatment initiation [Australian].
- Sick sinus syndrome or sino-atrial block [Australian].
- 3rd-degree atrioventricular (AV) block [Australian].
- Pacemaker dependence (conditions where the sinus node is no longer the primary pacemaker) [Australian].
- Unstable or acute heart failure [Australian].
- Unstable angina or acute myocardial infarction [Australian].
- Cardiogenic shock [Australian].
- Severe hypotension (< 90/50 mmHg) [Australian].
- Severe hepatic insufficiency [Australian].
- Combination with potent CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, macrolides, HIV protease inhibitors) [Australian].
- Combination with verapamil or diltiazem (moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors with heart rate reducing properties) [Australian].
- Pregnancy, lactation, and women of childbearing potential not using effective contraception [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Cardiovascular Outcomes:** Ivabradine provides symptomatic relief in chronic stable angina but has not demonstrated benefits on cardiovascular outcomes such as myocardial infarction or cardiovascular death [Australian].
- **Bradycardia:** Ivabradine must not be initiated if the resting heart rate is < 70 bpm. If heart rate falls persistently below 50 bpm or bradycardia symptoms (dizziness, fatigue, hypotension) occur, the dose must be downward titrated or discontinued [Australian].
- **Atrial Fibrillation (AF):** Ivabradine increases the risk of developing AF. Regular clinical monitoring for AF (e.g., ECG) is recommended. If AF develops, the benefit-risk of continued treatment must be reconsidered [Australian].
- **QT Prolongation:** Use should be avoided in patients with congenital long QT syndrome or those taking QT-prolonging medicines. Heart rate reduction by ivabradine may exacerbate QT prolongation [Australian].
- **Visual Effects:** Ivabradine affects retinal function via interaction with $I_h$ currents. Phosphenes (luminous phenomena) are common. Caution is advised in patients with retinitis pigmentosa [Australian].
- **Lactose Intolerance:** Tablets contain lactose; not recommended for patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Table
| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Luminous phenomena (phosphenes) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Bradycardia, first-degree AV block, ventricular extrasystoles, blurred vision, headache, dizziness, atrial fibrillation |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Palpitations, supraventricular extrasystoles, nausea, constipation, diarrhoea, vertigo, dyspnoea, muscle cramps, hyperuricaemia, elevated creatinine |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Angioedema, rash, erythema, pruritus, urticaria, malaise |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | 2nd and 3rd-degree AV block, sick sinus syndrome |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Atrial fibrillation, symptomatic bradycardia, and unstable angina [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4 Inhibitors:** Ivabradine is metabolised solely by CYP3A4.
    - **Potent Inhibitors:** (e.g., ketoconazole, erythromycin) are contraindicated as they can increase ivabradine exposure 7 to 8-fold [Australian].
    - **Moderate Inhibitors:** (e.g., verapamil, diltiazem) are contraindicated due to additive heart rate lowering effects. Other moderate inhibitors (e.g., grapefruit juice) should be avoided or used with extreme caution [Australian].
- **CYP3A4 Inducers:** (e.g., rifampicin, phenytoin, St John’s Wort) may decrease ivabradine exposure; dose titration may be required [Australian].

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **QT-Prolonging Medicines:** Avoid concomitant use (e.g., quinidine, sotalol, amiodarone, tricyclic antidepressants) [Australian].
- **Potassium-Depleting Diuretics:** Hypokalaemia combined with ivabradine-induced bradycardia increases the risk of severe arrhythmias [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Contraindicated. Animal studies show embryotoxicity and teratogenicity (cardiac defects, ectrodactylia) at exposures near clinical levels.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Ivabradine is excreted in milk; animal studies showed postnatal mortality and enlarged hearts in offspring [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No effect on male or female fertility was observed in rat studies at supra-therapeutic doses [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in children and adolescents; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** In patients $\ge$ 75 years, a lower starting dose (2.5 mg twice daily) should be considered before up-titration [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment required for CrCl > 15 mL/min. Use with caution in patients with CrCl < 15 mL/min due to lack of data [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild impairment. Caution in moderate impairment. Contraindicated in severe hepatic insufficiency [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selective and specific inhibition of the cardiac pacemaker $I_f$ current, which controls spontaneous diastolic depolarisation in the sinus node, thereby reducing heart rate.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and almost completely absorbed (peak plasma level in 1 hour fasting). Bioavailability is ~40% due to first-pass metabolism. Food increases exposure by 20–30% [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** ~70% plasma protein bound; volume of distribution ~100 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by CYP3A4 only. Major active metabolite is N-desmethylated derivative (S18982).
    - **Elimination:** Main half-life is 2 hours; effective half-life is 11 hours. Excreted equally in faeces and urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **BEAUTIFUL Study:** Evaluated ivabradine in patients with CAD and LVD (LVEF < 40%). Showed no difference in the primary composite endpoint of CV death, MI, or HF hospitalisation [Australian].
- **SHIFT Study:** Evaluated ivabradine in 6,505 patients with CHF (LVEF $\le$ 35%, HR $\ge$ 70 bpm). Demonstrated an 18% relative risk reduction in the primary composite endpoint (CV mortality or hospitalisation for worsening HF), primarily driven by a reduction in HF hospitalisations [Australian].
- **SIGNIFY Study:** Evaluated patients with stable CAD without clinical HF. Used higher-than-approved doses and found no benefit, with an increased risk of the primary composite endpoint in a subgroup with symptomatic angina (CCS class $\ge$ II) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on packaging.
- **Packaging:** Calendar packs of 14 or 56 tablets in aluminium/aluminium blisters [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription-only Medicine) [Australian].
