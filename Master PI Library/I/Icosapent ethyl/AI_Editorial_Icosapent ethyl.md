# Editorial Product Information: Icosapent Ethyl

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory guidelines for final prescribing decisions.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Icosapent ethyl (a stable ethyl ester of the omega-3 fatty acid, eicosapentaenoic acid [EPA]).
- **Brand Name(s):** **VAZKEPA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 998 mg soft capsules (oblong, 25 x 10 mm) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Seqirus Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**VAZKEPA** is indicated to reduce the risk of cardiovascular (CV) events in adult patients currently treated with a statin who are at high cardiovascular risk, defined by:
- Elevated triglycerides (≥ 1.7 mmol/L); AND
- Established cardiovascular disease, OR
- Diabetes plus at least one additional cardiovascular risk factor [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended dose is 4 capsules daily, administered as two 998 mg capsules twice daily [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Capsules must be taken with or following a meal.
    - Capsules must be swallowed whole; they should not be broken, crushed, dissolved, or chewed [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as remembered. However, if a full day's dose is missed, the next dose should not be doubled [Australian].
- **Titration:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance, icosapent ethyl.
- Hypersensitivity to soya or any excipients [Australian].
- Patients with an allergy to peanuts (due to the presence of soya lecithin) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Allergies to Fish/Shellfish:** Icosapent ethyl is derived from fish oil. While the risk is unknown, it should be used with caution in patients with known fish or shellfish hypersensitivity [Australian].
- **Atrial Fibrillation or Flutter:** Treatment is associated with an increased risk of atrial fibrillation or flutter requiring hospitalisation. This risk is higher in patients with a prior history of these conditions. Clinical monitoring (and ECG evaluation where indicated) is recommended for symptoms such as palpitations, dyspnoea, or irregular pulse [Australian].
- **Bleeding:** There is an increased incidence of bleeding. Patients taking concomitant antithrombotic agents (antiplatelets, aspirin, or anticoagulants) are at higher risk and should be monitored periodically [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** ALT and AST levels should be monitored before starting treatment and at clinically indicated intervals during therapy [Australian].
- **Excipient Warnings:**
    - **Sorbitol/Maltitol:** Contains 83 mg sorbitol and 30 mg maltitol per capsule. Patients with hereditary fructose intolerance (HFI) should not take this medicine.
    - **Soya Lecithin:** Contraindicated in patients with soya or peanut allergies [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily based on the REDUCE-IT trial (median follow-up 4.9 years).

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (≥10%)** | Bleeding (11.8%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Peripheral oedema (7.8%), Atrial fibrillation or flutter (5.8%), Constipation (5.4%), Musculoskeletal pain (4.3%), Gout (4.3%), Rash (3.0%), Eructation |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Hypersensitivity, Dysgeusia (fishy taste) |
| **Not Known** | Pharyngeal swelling |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Highlighted events include serious bleeding (3.4% when used with antithrombotics) and atrial fibrillation/flutter requiring 24-hour hospitalisation (3%) [Australian].
- **Post-marketing Experience:** Increased blood triglycerides, arthralgia, diarrhoea, and abdominal discomfort have been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Cytochrome P450 (CYP) Enzymes:** Clinical studies at 4 g/day showed no interactions with omeprazole, rosiglitazone, warfarin, or atorvastatin. In vitro, EPA is a weak inhibitor of CYP1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, and 3A [Australian].
- **Transporters:** In vitro, EPA did not significantly inhibit P-gp, BCRP, OAT1, OCT2, OATP1B1, or OATP1B3, but showed mild inhibition of OAT3 [Australian].
- **Antithrombotics:** Potential pharmacodynamic interaction resulting in increased bleeding risk [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B1:** Limited data in pregnant women. Animal studies showed the drug crosses the placenta. It is preferable to avoid use during pregnancy unless the benefits clearly outweigh the risks to the fetus [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** EPA is excreted in human milk. A risk to the suckling child cannot be excluded. A decision must be made to either discontinue breastfeeding or discontinue the drug, considering the benefit of each to the mother and child [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies (rats) at doses up to 3000 mg/kg/day showed no effect on male or female fertility [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No relevant use in children <18 years of age for reducing CV risk [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required based on age [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment is recommended, though ALT/AST monitoring is required [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Multi-factorial, including an improved lipoprotein profile (reduced TG-rich lipoproteins), anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects, reduction of macrophage accumulation, and improved endothelial function. It reduces TG by suppressing hepatic VLDL synthesis and increasing TG clearance [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** De-esterified to the active metabolite, EPA, during absorption in the small intestine. Peak plasma levels (Tmax) are reached in approximately 5 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution is ~88 L. Highly protein bound (>99%).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by the liver via beta-oxidation (similar to dietary fatty acids).
    - **Elimination:** Total plasma clearance is ~684 mL/hr. The elimination half-life (t½) is approximately 89 hours. It does not undergo renal excretion [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **REDUCE-IT Trial:** A multinational, double-blind study of 8,179 statin-treated adults with LDL-C > 1.03 mmol/L and ≤ 2.59 mmol/L and elevated triglycerides (≥ 1.53 mmol/L to < 5.64 mmol/L).
- **Primary Endpoint:** 5-point MACE (CV death, MI, stroke, coronary revascularisation, or unstable angina). Icosapent ethyl 4 g/day showed a significant reduction (HR 0.75; 95% CI: 0.68, 0.83; p<0.0001).
- **Secondary Endpoint:** 3-point MACE (CV death, MI, stroke). Significant reduction observed (HR 0.74; 95% CI: 0.65, 0.83; p<0.0001) [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 30°C.
- **Protection:** Keep bottles tightly closed and store blisters in the original package to protect from moisture [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
