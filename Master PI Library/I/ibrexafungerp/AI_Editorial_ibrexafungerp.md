# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived solely from the Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a single clinical reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing within a particular region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for fatty acid transport; inner salt of (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium.
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources (Generic Levocarnitine Injection by Baxter Healthcare) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (Typically A16AA01).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often required in patients experiencing severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administer every 3 to 4 hours; intervals should not be less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg or as clinically required. Doses as high as 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine level: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levels <40–50 µmol/L. Adjustments should be guided by trough concentrations; downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of treatment [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm have occurred, particularly in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and consider the risks versus benefits before any rechallenge [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Precaution):** While this document concerns the injection, clinicians should note that high-dose *oral* levocarnitine is not recommended in patients with severe renal impairment due to the accumulation of metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (ESRD/Dialysis Population)
In clinical trials of patients on hemodialysis, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality):
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33–59%), pain (21–49%), headache (3–37%), fever (5–12%), abdominal pain (5–21%), asthenia (8–12%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–33%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
- **Neurological:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].

### Other Notable Effects
- **Body Odor:** Reported as a less frequent reaction [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%) [US].
- **Serious Reactions:** Anaphylaxis and seizures (post-marketing) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category B equivalent]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/well-controlled human studies; use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, carnitine concentrations in milk increase following exogenous administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of maternal treatment [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 (Dosage and Administration) for weight-based dosing; safety and efficacy are established for the indicated uses [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used specifically in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3). The safety of *oral* levocarnitine in renal failure has not been evaluated [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Carnitine deficiency is biochemically defined as plasma levels <20 µmol/L at one week post-term [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** Clinical trials demonstrated that 20 mg/kg doses after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine levels. Trough levels remained stable or increased over 8 to 24 weeks of therapy.
- **Note:** While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations was proven, the specific effect on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in the ESRD population has not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion. The formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
