# Editorial Product Information: Isosorbide dinitrate

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) documentation from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised, clinically focused editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the Australian source was available for analysis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Isosorbide dinitrate (an organic nitrate ester).
- **Chemical Class:** Vasodilator / Antianginal.
- **Brand Names:** Isordil [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** C01DA08.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - Sublingual Tablets: 5 mg [Australian].
    - Oral Tablets: 10 mg, 30 mg, 40 mg [Australian]. *Note: 10 mg, 30 mg, and 40 mg oral strengths are currently not marketed in Australia but remain registered.*
- **Routes of Administration:** Sublingual and Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Angina Pectoris:** Treatment and prophylaxis of various forms including classic effort-associated, chronic stable, vaso-spastic (Prinzmetal’s/variant), unstable angina, and angina decubitus [Australian].
- **Myocardial Ischaemia:** Treatment of ischaemia resulting from ischaemic heart disease [Australian].
- **Left Ventricular Failure (LVF):** Management of acute and chronic LVF, either as monotherapy or as adjunctive therapy in congestive heart failure [Australian].
- **Route-Specific Indications [Australian]:**
    - **Sublingual:** Indicated for acute treatment of anginal attacks and rapid stabilisation in LVF.
    - **Oral:** Indicated for the prophylactic treatment of angina and maintenance therapy for chronic LVF. Oral tablets are not intended for the immediate relief of acute anginal episodes.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
To prevent the development of nitrate tolerance, a nitrate-free interval of at least 8 hours every 24 hours is recommended [Australian].

### Adult Dosing [Australian]
- **Angina Pectoris & Myocardial Ischaemia:**
    - **Sublingual:** 5 to 10 mg every two to three hours for acute attacks or prophylactically before activities likely to provoke angina.
    - **Oral:** 5 to 30 mg four times daily (average maintenance: 10 mg four times daily).
- **Left Ventricular Failure (Congestive Heart Failure):**
    - Dosage must be individualised based on clinical response and haemodynamic monitoring.
    - **Acute LVF:** Sublingual 5 to 10 mg every two hours or as required.
    - **Chronic LVF:** 
        - Initial: Sublingual 5 to 10 mg every two hours to stabilise symptoms.
        - Maintenance: Oral 20 to 40 mg four times daily or as required.

### Administration Instructions
- Sublingual tablets may be swallowed to supplement oral therapy or for angina prevention, though onset will be delayed compared to sublingual use [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity or idiosyncratic reaction to isosorbide dinitrate or other organic nitrates [Australian].
- **Phosphodiesterase Inhibitors:** Concomitant use with sildenafil, tadalafil, or vardenafil (risk of severe hypotension, syncope, or myocardial ischaemia) [Australian].
- **Haemodynamic Instability:** Acute circulatory failure (shock, vascular collapse), hypotension, or uncorrected hypovolaemia [Australian].
- **Cardiac Obstruction/Failure:** Constrictive pericarditis, pericardial tamponade, hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy (HOCM), and isolated right ventricular failure (especially in acute MI) [Australian].
- **Increased Intracranial Pressure:** Intracranial hypertension, head trauma, or cerebral haemorrhage [Australian].
- **Other:** Severe anaemia, arterial hypoxaemia, and aortic/mitral valve stenosis [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypotension:** Risk of precipitous fall in blood pressure, particularly in patients with severe atherosclerosis or renal insufficiency. Paradoxical bradycardia and increased angina may occur [Australian].
- **Withdrawal:** Nitrate dependence may occur. Discontinuation in angina patients should be gradual over two weeks to prevent rebound angina [Australian].
- **Tolerance:** Partial tolerance to antianginal effects and cross-tolerance with other nitrates may develop [Australian].
- **Acute Myocardial Infarction:** Safety is not fully established in the unstable phase of MI; use requires accurate diagnosis and potentially haemodynamic monitoring [Australian].
- **Haemolytic Anaemia:** Reported in patients with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) deficiency [Australian].
- **Hypoxaemia:** May decrease arterial oxygen tension, potentially opposing antianginal effects in hypoxic patients [Australian].
- **Specific Clinical Cautions:** Use with caution in patients with hypothyroidism, hypothermia, malnutrition, or angle-closure glaucoma [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Categories [Australian]
- **Very Common (>10%):** Headache (may be persistent but often declines with time).
- **Common (1–10%):** Dizziness (especially postural), fatigue, palpitations, orthostatic hypotension, and flushing.
- **Less Common/Rare:** 
    - **Cardiovascular:** Peripheral oedema, temporary hypoxaemia, collapse (with bradyarrhythmia/syncope), reflex tachycardia.
    - **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, heartburn (due to sphincter relaxation), pallor, and excessive perspiration.
    - **Dermatological:** Rash, exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome.
    - **Haematological:** Haemolytic anaemia (in G6PD deficiency).
- **Serious Reactions:** Severe hypotension leading to enhanced ischaemia; reflex tachycardia exacerbating injury in acute MI.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Phosphodiesterase (PDE5) Inhibitors:** Significant potentiation of hypotensive effects; strictly contraindicated [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives & Phenothiazines:** Potential for additive hypotensive effects [Australian].
- **Alcohol:** May enhance vasodilatory effects and cause hypotension [Australian].
- **Tricyclic Antidepressants & Anti-cholinergics:** May potentiate hypotensive effects [Australian].
- **NSAIDs:** May attenuate the therapeutic effect of isosorbide dinitrate [Australian].
- **Beta-blockers:** Concomitant use may require dose reduction of isosorbide dinitrate to avoid excessive hypotension [Australian].
- **Physiological Antagonism:** Nitrates may act as antagonists to noradrenaline, acetylcholine, and histamine [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category B1** [Australian]. Safety is not established. Use only if potential benefits outweigh hazards to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use is not recommended for nursing mothers as excretion in breast milk is unknown [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not recommended; safety and efficacy have not been demonstrated [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Start at the low end of the dosing range due to higher frequency of hepatic, renal, or cardiac dysfunction and comorbid conditions [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Dose reduction may be necessary due to hepatic metabolism [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** While single doses show no PK change, chronic dosing may lead to accumulation of active mononitrate metabolites [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Relaxes vascular smooth muscle. Venodilation reduces venous return (preload) and filling pressures. Arteriolar dilation reduces systemic vascular resistance (afterload). This reduces myocardial oxygen demand and may redistribute blood flow to ischaemic areas [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics [Australian]:**
    - **Absorption:** Faster via sublingual route; oral bioavailability is approximately 20%.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolised into active mononitrate metabolites (2- and 5-isosorbide mononitrate).
    - **Elimination:** Biexponential decay. Initial half-life is ~1.1 hours; terminal half-life after chronic dosing is ~7.7 hours.
    - **Metabolite Half-lives:** 2-mononitrate (~3 hours); 5-mononitrate (4–6 hours).

## 11. Clinical Trials
- No specific pivotal trial data provided in the primary source document [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Sublingual Tablets (5 mg):** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Oral Tablets (10 mg, 30 mg, 40 mg):** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].
