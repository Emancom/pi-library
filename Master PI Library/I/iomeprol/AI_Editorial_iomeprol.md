# Editorial Product Information: Iomeprol

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA).
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of iomeprol, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the United States Product Information was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Iomeprol (tri-iodinated, non-ionic radiographic contrast agent).
- **Brand Name(s):** Iomervu [United States].
- **ATC Code:** V08AB10.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Injection solution in single-dose vials or bottles [United States].
    - Available concentrations: 250 mg Iodine/mL, 300 mg Iodine/mL, 350 mg Iodine/mL, and 400 mg Iodine/mL [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intra-arterial and Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Bracco Diagnostics Inc. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
Iomeprol is a radiographic contrast agent indicated for the following procedures [All United States-approved]:

**Intra-arterial Procedures:**
- Cerebral arteriography (including digital subtraction angiography [IA-DSA]) in adult and paediatric patients.
- Visceral and peripheral arteriography and aortography (including IA-DSA) in adult and paediatric patients.
- Coronary arteriography and cardiac ventriculography in adults.
- Evaluation of cardiac chambers and related arteries in paediatric patients.

**Intravenous Procedures:**
- Computed tomography (CT) of the head and body in adult and paediatric patients.
- CT angiography (CTA) of intracranial, visceral, and lower extremity arteries in adult and paediatric patients.
- Coronary CT angiography (CCTA) in adult and paediatric patients.
- CT urography in adult and paediatric patients.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Dosage must be individualised based on the specific imaging procedure, patient age, body weight, vessel size, blood flow rate, and the area being examined [United States].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Use aseptic technique.
    - Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration; the solution should be clear and colorless to pale yellow.
    - May be administered at room temperature (20°C to 25°C) or body temperature (37°C).
    - Do not mix with other drugs or total nutritional admixtures.
    - Hydrate patients adequately before and after administration to reduce renal risk.
- **Maximum Total Dose:** Varies by procedure; for adults, maximums range from 200 mL to 286 mL depending on iodine concentration and procedure type [United States].
- **Paediatric Dosing:** Generally based on volume per kg of body weight (e.g., 0.5 mL/kg to 2 mL/kg for many procedures), not to exceed adult maximums [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- There are no listed contraindications in the available source [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [United States]: RISKS ASSOCIATED WITH INTRATHECAL ADMINISTRATION.** Iomeprol is for intra-arterial or intravenous use only. Inadvertent intrathecal administration may result in death, convulsions, cerebral haemorrhage, coma, paralysis, arachnoiditis, acute renal failure, cardiac arrest, seizures, rhabdomyolysis, hyperthermia, and brain oedema.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Life-threatening or fatal anaphylaxis may occur. Risk is higher in patients with previous contrast reactions or known allergic disorders. Emergency resuscitation equipment must be available.
- **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI):** Risk factors include pre-existing renal impairment, dehydration, diabetes, heart failure, advanced age, and use of nephrotoxic drugs. Use the lowest necessary dose and ensure hydration.
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Hemodynamic disturbances, shock, and cardiac arrest may occur, particularly in patients with pre-existing heart disease.
- **Thromboembolic Events:** Serious thromboembolic events, including MI and stroke, can occur during angiographic procedures. Meticulous technique is required to minimise risk.
- **Thyroid Dysfunction:** 
    - **Thyroid Storm:** Risk in patients with hyperthyroidism or autonomous thyroid nodules.
    - **Paediatrics (0–3 years):** Hypothyroidism or transient suppression has been reported. Individualised monitoring is recommended for this age group [United States].
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCAR):** Including SJS/TEN, AGEP, and DRESS may develop weeks after administration.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reaction data from clinical trials (4,621 adult patients) [United States]:

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Feeling hot (2%), Headache (1.2%), Nausea (1%) |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Chest pain (0.6%), Back pain (0.5%), Vomiting (0.5%) |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Ventricular fibrillation, coronary arteriospasm, cardiac arrest, stroke, anaphylaxis, acute kidney injury, and severe cutaneous reactions [United States].
- **Paediatric Profile:** Character and quality of reactions in 184 paediatric patients were similar to those seen in adults [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Metformin:** Contrast-induced renal impairment can lead to metformin accumulation and lactic acidosis. Metformin should be withheld at the time of or prior to the procedure in patients with eGFR between 30-60 mL/min/1.73m², history of hepatic impairment/alcoholism, or those receiving intra-arterial contrast. Re-evaluate eGFR 48 hours post-procedure [United States].
- **Radioactive Iodine:** Iomeprol may interfere with thyroid uptake of radioactive iodine for up to 6 weeks.
- **Laboratory Interference:** 
    - **Protein-Bound Iodine (PBI) Test:** Do not perform for at least 16 days following administration.
    - **Thyroid Function:** Tests not dependent on iodine (e.g., T3 resin uptake, free T4) are unaffected.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** Available human data have not identified a drug-associated risk of major birth defects or miscarriage. Animal studies showed iomeprol crosses the placenta but produced no developmental toxicity at 0.45 times the maximum human dose [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Iomeprol is likely present in human milk in very low amounts and is poorly absorbed by the infant's GI tract. Interruption of breastfeeding is not mandatory, but a lactating woman may consider pumping and discarding milk for 10 hours (~5 half-lives) post-administration to minimise exposure [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for various IA and IV procedures. High-risk paediatric groups include those <12 months of age, or those with asthma, heart disease, or renal immaturity [United States].
- **Geriatric Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed compared to younger patients, but caution is advised due to the higher prevalence of renal impairment in the elderly [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance is significantly reduced as impairment increases (half-life increases 6.4-fold in severe impairment). Iomeprol is dialyzable; plasma concentrations decrease by ~83% during a 2-hour haemodialysis session [United States].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Opacifies vessels and body structures by attenuating X-ray photons, allowing radiographic visualisation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 0.28 L/kg. It does not bind to plasma proteins.
    - **Metabolism:** Not significantly metabolised.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 90% is excreted unchanged in the urine within 24 hours. The elimination half-life is approximately 1.8 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Intra-arterial Studies:** Clinical trials for cerebral, visceral, and coronary arteriography demonstrated "adequate" visualization in 93% to 100% of patients across concentrations [United States].
- **Intravenous Studies:** CT of the head and body showed adequate visualization in 98% to 100%. CT angiography for peripheral arterial disease showed high sensitivity (90-99%) and specificity (91-97%) for detecting significant stenosis [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C.
- Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Do not freeze.**
- Single-dose containers; discard any unused portion.
