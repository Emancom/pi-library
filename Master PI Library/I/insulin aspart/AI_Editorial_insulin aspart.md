# Product Information: Insulin Aspart

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** Insulin aspart (rys)
**Active Ingredient:** Insulin aspart

Insulin aspart is a human insulin analogue created via recombinant DNA technology using *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*. It differs from natural human insulin by the replacement of the amino acid proline with aspartic acid at position B28. This modification reduces the tendency of the molecules to form hexamers, resulting in faster absorption into the bloodstream following subcutaneous injection compared to soluble human insulin.

## 2. Approved Indications
Insulin aspart is indicated for the treatment of diabetes mellitus in adults, adolescents, and children. 

*   **Age Limitations:** Clinical guidance suggests use in pediatric patients aged 1 year and above [TGA]. 
*   **Clinical Goal:** It is used to improve glycemic control by providing a rapid onset of action to manage postprandial glucose excursions.

## 3. Contraindications
Insulin aspart is strictly contraindicated in the following circumstances:
*   During active episodes of hypoglycemia.
*   In patients with a known hypersensitivity to insulin aspart or any of the excipients (e.g., glycerol, phenol, metacresol, zinc, or niacinamide in specific formulations).

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### General Management
*   **Never Share Devices:** To prevent the transmission of blood-borne pathogens, insulin aspart pre-filled pens, cartridges, and needles must never be shared between patients, even if the needle is replaced [FDA].
*   **Hypoglycemia:** This is the most common adverse reaction. The timing of hypoglycemia usually reflects the time-action profile of the insulin; for insulin aspart, this may occur earlier after an injection compared to regular human insulin.
*   **Regimen Changes:** Any change in insulin strength, manufacturer, type, or method of administration should be performed under strict medical supervision.
*   **Hypokalemia:** All insulin products cause a shift in potassium from the extracellular to the intracellular space. Untreated hypokalemia may lead to respiratory paralysis, ventricular arrhythmia, or death. Caution is advised in patients at risk for low potassium levels [FDA].

### Tissue Disorders
*   **Lipodystrophy and Cutaneous Amyloidosis:** Patients must rotate injection sites within the same region to reduce the risk of developing skin lumps or pits. Injecting into affected areas may delay insulin absorption and worsen glycemic control.

### Concomitant Conditions
*   **Thiazolidinediones (TZDs):** When used in combination with TZDs, patients should be observed for signs of heart failure, weight gain, and edema. If cardiac symptoms deteriorate, TZDs should be discontinued.
*   **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Requirements for insulin may be reduced in patients with kidney or liver failure. Frequent glucose monitoring and dose adjustments are necessary in these populations.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Administration Timing
*   **Pre-meal:** Generally injected subcutaneously 5–10 minutes before a meal [FDA].
*   **Ultra-fast Formulations:** Certain formulations may be administered 0–2 minutes before the start of a meal or up to 20 minutes after starting the meal [TGA].

### Dosage Determination
Dosage is highly individualized based on the patient's metabolic needs, blood glucose monitoring results, and glycemic targets. 
*   **Type 1 Diabetes:** The average daily insulin requirement is usually between 0.5 and 1.0 unit/kg. In a basal-bolus regimen, insulin aspart should ideally account for approximately 50% of the total daily requirement.
*   **Type 2 Diabetes:** Suggested initial doses may start at 4 units at one or more meals, with subsequent titration based on glycemic targets.

### Routes of Administration
1.  **Subcutaneous Injection:** Administered in the abdominal wall, upper arm, thigh, or buttocks. 
2.  **Continuous Subcutaneous Insulin Infusion (CSII):** May be used in compatible pump systems. Do not dilute or mix with other insulins when used in a pump.
3.  **Intravenous Use:** Must be performed by healthcare professionals under close clinical supervision of glucose and potassium levels. Concentrations of 0.5 unit/mL to 1.0 unit/mL are standard for infusion systems [TGA].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*   **Metabolism:** Very common ($\ge$10%) incidence of hypoglycemia.
*   **General:** Injection/infusion site reactions (redness, inflammation, bruising), peripheral edema, and weight gain.
*   **Immune System:** Uncommon reports of urticaria, rash, or eruptions. Rare instances of generalized hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis) which can be life-threatening.
*   **Eye Disorders:** Refraction anomalies and worsening of diabetic retinopathy may occur upon initial intensification of therapy.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Reduced Insulin Requirement:** Oral antidiabetic agents, ACE inhibitors, salicylates, anabolic steroids, sulfonamides, and MAOIs.
*   **Increased Insulin Requirement:** Oral contraceptives, thiazides, glucocorticoids, thyroid hormones, and sympathomimetics.
*   **Masked Symptoms:** Beta-blockers may mask the adrenergic warning symptoms of hypoglycemia (e.g., palpitations, tremors).
*   **Variable Effects:** Alcohol and octreotide may either increase or decrease insulin requirements.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
Insulin aspart is classified as **Pregnancy Category A** [TGA]. Large clinical trials have shown no adverse effects of insulin aspart on the pregnancy or the health of the fetus/newborn compared to soluble human insulin. Insulin requirements typically fall in the first trimester and increase during the second and third trimesters.

### Breastfeeding
There are no known restrictions on the use of insulin aspart during lactation. Insulin treatment for the mother does not pose a risk to the breastfed infant, though dosage adjustments for the mother may be required.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Following subcutaneous injection, insulin aspart reaches peak serum concentrations roughly twice as fast as regular human insulin. The median time to maximum concentration ($T_{max}$) is approximately 40–50 minutes [FDA].
*   **Distribution:** Insulin aspart has a low binding affinity to plasma proteins (<10%).
*   **Elimination:** The average apparent half-life is approximately 57 to 81 minutes depending on the specific study population [FDA/TGA].
*   **Duration:** The duration of action is shorter than regular human insulin, typically lasting between 3 and 5 hours.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
The primary activity of insulin aspart is the regulation of glucose metabolism. It binds to insulin receptors on muscle and fat cells, facilitating the cellular uptake of glucose. Simultaneously, it inhibits the output of glucose from the liver. It also promotes protein synthesis and inhibits lipolysis and proteolysis, thereby exerting an anabolic effect.

## 11. Storage Conditions
### Unopened (Not In-Use)
*   Store in a refrigerator between 2°C and 8°C. 
*   Do not freeze. Keep away from the cooling element. 
*   Keep the product in its outer carton/cap on to protect it from light.

### Opened (In-Use)
*   **Vials, Pens, and Cartridges:** Can be stored at room temperature (up to 30°C) for a maximum of 28 days/4 weeks. 
*   **Pumps:** When used in a pump reservoir, the insulin should be replaced at least every 7 to 9 days [FDA] or every 6 days [TGA], depending on the specific pump and reservoir instructions.
*   Discard any insulin exposed to excessive heat or light.
