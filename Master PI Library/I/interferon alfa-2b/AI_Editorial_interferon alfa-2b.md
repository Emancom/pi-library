# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesizes regulatory information to facilitate clinical decision-making.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not replace the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for full prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corp.) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous (IV).
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours). 
    - Subsequent daily maintenance doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilized [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** 
    - Recommended starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
    - Administration: Slow IV bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    - Dose adjustments may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations [US].

### Monitoring and Titration
- **Target Levels:** For metabolic disorders, free carnitine concentrations should ideally be maintained between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
- **ESRD Monitoring:** Therapy may be initiated if trough levels are below 40–50 µmol/L. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) is recommended based on clinical response and plasma levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations ranging from 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- Not specified in available sources.

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These often occur within minutes of IV administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued immediately upon signs of a severe reaction [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Levocarnitine may increase the frequency or severity of seizures in patients with pre-existing seizure disorders. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history of epilepsy [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Monitor blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations regularly [US].
- **International Normalized Ratio (INR):** Monitor INR in patients concurrently receiving warfarin [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disease.

| Body System | Adverse Event (Frequency ≥5% in ESRD Trials) [US] |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, fever, flu syndrome, headache, infection, injection site reaction, pain, abdominal pain, asthenia |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, hypotension, tachycardia |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, vomiting, nausea, dyspepsia, anorexia |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness, paresthesia, depression |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis, cough, dyspnea, rhinitis |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Anaphylaxis and severe respiratory distress (bronchospasm/laryngeal edema) [US].
- Seizures [US].
- Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis occur less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Increased INR has been reported when co-administered with levocarnitine. Frequent INR monitoring is recommended following initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No specific protein-binding interactions are expected, as levocarnitine is not plasma protein-bound [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not explicitly lettered, but animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if levocarnitine is excreted in human milk. In dairy cows, milk carnitine concentrations increase following administration. The benefits of maternal supplementation must be weighed against potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg) as in adults for metabolic disorders. Safety and efficacy follow adult parameters [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in provided sources; however, dose selection should generally be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of decreased hepatic, renal, or cardiac function.
- **Renal Impairment:**
    - IV administration is indicated for ESRD patients on dialysis. 
    - **Note on Oral Formulation:** Chronic high-dose oral levocarnitine is cautioned in patients with severe renal impairment or ESRD due to the potential accumulation of toxic metabolites trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. This facilitates beta-oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; volume of distribution is not specified, but it concentrates in skeletal and cardiac muscle [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted renally within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Trials:** Two double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in chronic hemodialysis patients demonstrated that levocarnitine injection (10, 20, and 40 mg/kg) significantly increased plasma carnitine concentrations. However, the specific impact on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, malaise) in this population was not definitively determined in these studies [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C [US].
- **Special Precautions:** Protect from freezing. Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
