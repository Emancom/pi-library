# Originality Audit Report: insulin glargine

## Audit Summary
- **Originality Score:** 68/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 5
- **Overall Assessment:** Concern

## Verbatim Copied Passages
1.  **Chemical Description (Section 1):** 
    *   **Editorial:** "Insulin glargine differs from naturally occurring human insulin through the replacement of asparagine with glycine at position A21 and the addition of two arginines at the C-terminus of the B-chain."
    *   **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), p. 24: "Insulin glargine differs from human insulin in that the amino acid asparagine at position A21 is replaced by glycine and two arginines are added to the C-terminus of the B-chain."
2.  **Hypokalemia Warning (Section 4):**
    *   **Editorial:** "All insulin products cause a shift in potassium from the extracellular to the intracellular space. If untreated, this may lead to respiratory paralysis, ventricular arrhythmia, and death."
    *   **Source:** Document 2 (FDA), p. 6: "All insulins... cause a shift in potassium from the extracellular to intracellular space, possibly leading to hypokalemia. Untreated hypokalemia may cause respiratory paralysis, ventricular arrhythmia, and death."
3.  **NPH Switch Dosage (Section 5):**
    *   **Editorial:** "To reduce the risk of hypoglycemia, the initial total daily dose of insulin glargine should be reduced by approximately 20% compared to the previous total daily NPH dose."
    *   **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), p. 2: "In studies when patients were transferred from twice-daily NPH... the initial dose (IU) was usually reduced by approximately 20% (compared to total daily IU of NPH human insulin)."
4.  **ORIGIN Study Results (Section 6):**
    *   **Editorial:** "Long-term studies (ORIGIN) showed a median weight gain of 1.4kg over six years."
    *   **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), p. 10: "over the course of this 6 year study... weight gain of 1.4kg in Optisulin group."
5.  **Pentamidine Interaction (Section 7):**
    *   **Editorial:** "Pentamidine may cause hypoglycemia followed by hyperglycemia."
    *   **Source:** Document 1 (TGA), p. 7: "Pentamidine may cause hypoglycaemia, which may be sometimes followed by hyperglycaemia."

## Close Paraphrases
*   **Section 2 (Limitations of Use):** The statement "Intravenous short-acting insulin is the preferred treatment in such cases" closely mimics TGA p. 5: "Instead, intravenous human insulin is recommended in such cases."
*   **Section 5 (Type 1 Initiation):** "initiated at approximately one-third of the total daily insulin requirement" is a direct lift of the numeric instruction in FDA p. 4.
*   **Section 8 (Pregnancy Requirements):** "Insulin requirements usually decrease in the first trimester and increase during the second and third trimesters" is nearly identical to TGA p. 7-8.
*   **Section 11 (Storage Table):** The structure of the "Storage Table Summary" is a condensed version of the table on FDA p. 24.

## Originality Strengths
*   **Harmonization:** The editorial successfully identifies specific differences between the TGA and FDA (e.g., the pediatric age threshold of 6 years in Australia vs. the broader US indication).
*   **Structural Synthesis:** The document creates a logical flow by merging "Warnings" from the FDA with "Clinical Trial Observations" from the TGA, which are often separated in the raw source files.
*   **Simplified Language:** In the "Injection Site Management" and "Contraindications" sections, the writer uses more direct, professional summary language rather than the legalistic tone of the PIs.

## Differences from Source PIs
*   **Consolidation:** The editorial merges the "Precautions" and "Warnings" sections which are distinct and repetitive in the source PIs.
*   **Metric Conversion:** The editorial standardizes terms like "prefilled pens" and "cartridges" across both regional sections.
*   **Omission of Commercial Names:** The editorial correctly omits regional brand names like "Optisulin" and "Langlara" to focus on the active ingredient (Insulin glargine).
*   **Data Merging:** Section 5 combines TGA’s specific instruction on 300 units/mL glargine with FDA’s general initiation guidelines for insulin-naïve patients.

## Recommendation
**Concern — Needs Revision.**

While the editorial provides a high-quality synthesis of two complex documents, it contains several "anchor phrases" that are copied verbatim from the source PIs (particularly the chemical description and the hypokalemia warning). 

**Required Changes:**
1.  **Section 1:** Rewrite the chemical structural description to avoid the "A21/B-chain" sentence structure found in Document 1.
2.  **Section 4:** Paraphrase the "Hypokalemia" and "TZD" warnings. While the medical facts must remain, the specific sequence ("respiratory paralysis, ventricular arrhythmia, and death") is a verbatim FDA string.
3.  **Section 5:** Re-word the 20% reduction instruction to be an original summary rather than a direct quote of the TGA study result phrasing.
4.  **Section 8:** Rewrite the pregnancy metabolic stages in the writer's own voice.
