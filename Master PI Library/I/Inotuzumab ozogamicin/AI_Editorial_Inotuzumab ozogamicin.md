# Editorial Product Information: Inotuzumab ozogamicin

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical data and regulatory specifics into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for full legal and prescribing compliance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Inotuzumab ozogamicin; a CD22-directed antibody-drug conjugate (ADC).
- **Chemical Class:** It consists of a recombinant humanised IgG4 kappa antibody (inotuzumab) covalently linked to N-acetyl-gamma-calicheamicin (a cytotoxic semi-synthetic calicheamicin derivative) via an acid-cleavable linker.
- **Brand Name:** **BESPONSA** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01FX07.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for injection; 1 mg per single-dose vial. Upon reconstitution (4 mL), the concentration is 0.25 mg/mL.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Pfizer Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsed or Refractory B-cell Precursor Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia (ALL):** Indicated for the treatment of adults with relapsed or refractory CD22-positive B-cell precursor ALL [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Treatment is administered in 3- to 4-week cycles. Dose calculation is based on Body Surface Area (BSA).
- **Cycle 1:** Total dose of 1.8 mg/m² per cycle, administered as three divided doses:
    - Day 1: 0.8 mg/m²
    - Day 8: 0.5 mg/m²
    - Day 15: 0.5 mg/m²
    - *Duration:* 21 days (may be extended to 28 days if remission is achieved or to allow recovery from toxicity).
- **Subsequent Cycles:**
    - **Patients achieving CR/CRi:** 1.5 mg/m² total (0.5 mg/m² on Days 1, 8, and 15).
    - **Patients not achieving CR/CRi:** 1.8 mg/m² total (0.8 mg/m² on Day 1; 0.5 mg/m² on Days 8 and 15).
    - *Duration:* 28 days (including a 7-day treatment-free interval).

### Duration of Treatment
- **Proceeding to HSCT:** Recommended duration is 2 cycles. A 3rd cycle may be considered for patients failing to achieve MRD negativity and CR/CRi after 2 cycles.
- **Not proceeding to HSCT:** Maximum of 6 cycles.
- **Discontinuation:** Patients not achieving CR/CRi within 3 cycles should discontinue treatment.

### Premedication and Cytoreduction
- **Cytoreduction:** For patients with peripheral blast counts >10,000/mm³, cytoreduction (hydroxyurea, steroids, or vincristine) is recommended prior to the first dose.
- **Premedication:** Corticosteroids, antipyretics, and antihistamines should be administered prior to infusion to mitigate reaction risks.

### Administration Instructions
- Administer as a 1-hour IV infusion at a rate of 50 mL/h.
- **Do not** administer as an IV push or bolus.
- **Do not** mix with other medicinal products.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to inotuzumab ozogamicin or any excipients (trometamol, sucrose, polysorbate 80, sodium chloride).
- History of confirmed severe or ongoing veno-occlusive liver disease (VOD) or sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS).
- Serious ongoing hepatic disease (e.g., cirrhosis, active hepatitis, nodular regenerative hyperplasia).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [Australian]
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Fatal and life-threatening hepatic VOD/SOS has occurred.
- **Post-HSCT Mortality:** An increased risk of non-relapse mortality has been observed following haematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT).

### General Warnings
- **VOD/SOS:** Risk is highest post-HSCT. Conditioning regimens containing two alkylating agents and elevated pre-HSCT bilirubin levels are significant risk factors. Avoid dual-alkylator conditioning.
- **Myelosuppression:** Severe neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and anaemia are common. Complications include febrile neutropenia and fatal infections (e.g., sepsis, pneumonia).
- **Infusion-Related Reactions:** Most common in Cycle 1. Monitor patients for at least 1 hour post-infusion.
- **QT Interval Prolongation:** Administer with caution in patients with a history of QT prolongation or those taking pro-arrhythmic medications.
- **Tumour Lysis Syndrome (TLS):** Monitor for symptoms of TLS, particularly in patients with high tumour burden.
- **Amylase/Lipase Elevation:** Increases have been reported; evaluate for potential hepatobiliary disease.
- **Immunisations:** Live viral vaccines are not recommended for at least 2 weeks prior to treatment, during treatment, and until B-cell recovery.

### Monitoring Requirements
- Liver function tests (ALT, AST, bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase) prior to and following each dose.
- Complete blood counts (CBC) prior to each dose.
- Electrolytes and ECGs prior to treatment and periodically during therapy.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Category (Very Common >10%)
| System Organ Class | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Infections** | Infection (48%), Febrile neutropenia (26%) |
| **Blood/Lymphatic** | Thrombocytopenia (51%), Neutropenia (49%), Anaemia (36%), Leukopenia (35%), Lymphopenia (18%) |
| **Gastrointestinal** | Nausea (31%), Abdominal pain (23%), Diarrhoea (17%), Constipation (16%), Vomiting (15%), Stomatitis (13%) |
| **Hepatobiliary** | Hyperbilirubinaemia (21%), Transaminases increased (26%), GGT increased (21%) |
| **General** | Fatigue (35%), Pyrexia (32%), Chills (11%) |
| **Nervous System** | Headache (28%) |
| **Vascular** | Haemorrhage (33%) |

### Serious and Common (<10%)
- **Serious:** VOD/SOS (14% overall in clinical trials; 23% in post-HSCT patients).
- **Common:** Pancytopenia (2%), TLS (2%), Infusion-related reactions (2%), Lipase increased (9%), Amylase increased (5%), QT prolongation (1%).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Effect of other drugs:** N-acetyl-gamma-calicheamicin dimethylhydrazide is metabolised via non-enzymatic reduction; inhibitors/inducers of CYP or UGT enzymes are unlikely to alter exposure.
- **Effect on other drugs:** In vitro data suggest low potential to inhibit or induce major CYP enzymes (1A2, 2B6, 3A4) or UGT enzymes at clinical concentrations. It has low potential to inhibit major transporters (P-gp, BCRP, OATs, OCT2, OATPs).

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **QT Prolonging Drugs:** Concomitant use with medicinal products known to prolong the QT interval or induce Torsades de Pointes (e.g., certain antiarrhythmics, antibiotics) should be carefully considered and monitored via ECG.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category D [Australian]:** Based on animal data (embryo-fetal mortality and fetotoxicity in rats), the drug can cause fetal harm. 
    - **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use effective contraception during treatment and for at least 8 months after the final dose. Men with female partners of childbearing potential should use contraception for at least 5 months after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been established. Breastfeeding is contraindicated during treatment and for at least 2 months after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** Based on animal findings, both male and female fertility may be compromised. Seek advice on fertility preservation prior to treatment.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years of age have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No adjustment to the starting dose is required based on age. However, increased age is associated with a higher risk of VOD/SOS post-HSCT.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild, moderate, or severe impairment (CLcr 15-89 mL/min). Not studied in end-stage renal disease.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild:** No adjustment required (Bilirubin ≤1.5x ULN and AST/ALT ≤2.5x ULN).
    - **Moderate/Severe:** Limited safety data; dosing should be interrupted until bilirubin recovers to ≤1.5x ULN and AST/ALT to ≤2.5x ULN. Permanent discontinuation is required if levels do not recover.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The antibody binds to CD22 on B-cells, is internalised, and releases calicheamicin. This cytotoxic agent induces double-stranded DNA breaks, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Characterised by the depletion of CD22-positive leukaemic blasts.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Total volume of distribution is ~12 L; 97% protein bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Non-enzymatic reduction of the calicheamicin component.
    - **Elimination:** Characterised by a 2-compartment model. Terminal half-life is 12.3 days. Steady-state is reached by Cycle 4.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Study B1931022:** A Phase 3, open-label, randomised trial comparing **BESPONSA** to investigator’s choice of chemotherapy (FLAG, MXN/Ara-C, or HIDAC) in adults with relapsed/refractory ALL.
- **Primary Outcomes:**
    - **CR/CRi Rate:** 80.7% for **BESPONSA** vs. 29.4% for chemotherapy.
    - **MRD Negativity:** 78.4% of responders in the **BESPONSA** arm vs. 28.1% in the chemotherapy arm.
    - **Overall Survival (OS):** Median OS was 7.7 months (**BESPONSA**) vs. 6.2 months (chemotherapy). While numerically higher, the OS difference did not meet the pre-specified boundary for statistical significance.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Refrigerate; do not freeze. Protect from light.
- **Reconstituted/Diluted Solution:**
    - Maximum time from reconstitution to end of administration is 8 hours.
    - Maximum time between reconstitution and dilution is 4 hours.
    - Reconstituted solution may be refrigerated (2-8°C) for up to 4 hours.
    - Diluted solution may be stored at room temperature (20-25°C) or refrigerated (2-8°C).
