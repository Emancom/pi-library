# Editorial Product Information: Imiglucerase

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) document from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating clinical and regulatory data.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes and does not replace the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the Australian PI was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Imiglucerase-rch (recombinant human β-glucocerebrosidase). It is a macrophage-targeted, recombinant variant produced in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells.
- **Brand Name:** **Cerezyme** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A16AB02.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** White to off-white sterile lyophilised powder for reconstitution. Each vial contains a nominal value of 400 Units.
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** sanofi-aventis australia pty ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Cerezyme** is indicated for long-term enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) in patients with a confirmed diagnosis of:
- **Type 1 Gaucher disease** (non-neuronopathic) [Australian-approved].
- **Type 3 Gaucher disease** (chronic neuronopathic) [Australian-approved].

Use is restricted to patients exhibiting clinically significant non-neurological manifestations, including anaemia, thrombocytopenia, bone disease, or organomegaly (hepatomegaly/splenomegaly).

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Individualisation:** Dosages must be tailored to the individual patient's clinical manifestations and therapeutic goals. 
- **Initial Dosage Range:** 2.5 U/kg three times weekly up to 60 U/kg once every two weeks.
- **Standard Dosing:** 60 U/kg every two weeks is the regimen supported by the most extensive clinical data.
- **Disease Severity:** Higher doses or increased frequency may be required for severe disease.
- **Response Monitoring:** Doses may be adjusted based on the achievement of therapeutic goals as determined by comprehensive clinical evaluations.
- **Efficacy Note:** Doses of 60 U/kg every two weeks have demonstrated improvement in haematological/visceral parameters within 6 months and have arrested or improved bone disease. Low-dose regimens (15 U/kg every two weeks) improve haematological and visceral parameters but generally do not impact bone parameters.

### Administration Instructions
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with 10.2 mL of water for injections. Avoid forceful impact and foaming; mix gently. The final concentration is 40 U/mL.
- **Dilution:** Withdraw the required volume and dilute with 0.9% sodium chloride to a total volume of 100–200 mL.
- **Infusion:** Administer via intravenous infusion over 1 to 2 hours.
- **Filtration:** Use of an in-line, low protein-binding 0.2 μm filter is recommended.
- **Home Infusion:** May be considered for patients tolerating infusions well for several months, provided they are administered by a trained healthcare professional with immediate access to emergency medical treatment.

## 4. Contraindications
- There are no known absolute contraindications [Australian].
- **Note:** Treatment should be re-evaluated if there is significant clinical evidence of hypersensitivity to the product.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Knowledgeable Supervision:** Therapy should be directed by physicians experienced in Gaucher disease management.
- **Immunogenicity:** Approximately 15% of patients develop IgG antibodies, typically within the first 6 months of therapy. Antibody formation is rare after 12 months. Periodic monitoring for IgG antibodies is suggested during the first year.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis and anaphylactic shock have been reported. Patients with antibodies are at higher risk. If a reaction occurs, the infusion should be slowed or stopped, and appropriate treatment (e.g., antihistamines, corticosteroids) initiated.
- **Infusion-Associated Reactions (IARs):** Common symptoms include pruritus, rash, angioedema, flushing, chest discomfort, and transient hypertension.
- **Pulmonary Hypertension:** Observed in <1% of the population. While it is a known complication of Gaucher disease (especially post-splenectomy), a causal relationship with **Cerezyme** has not been established.
- **Neurologic Symptoms:** There is insufficient evidence to suggest that imiglucerase improves neurological symptoms in Type 2 or Type 3 Gaucher disease.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **General Frequency:** Approximately 13.8% of patients experience treatment-related adverse events.
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Symptoms suggestive of hypersensitivity (6.6%): pruritus, flushing, urticaria, angioedema, dyspnoea, coughing, cyanosis, and blood pressure changes.
    - Other reactions (6.2%): nausea, abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhoea, rash, fatigue, headache, fever, dizziness, chills, backache, and tachycardia.
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** 
    - Infusion site reactions: discomfort, burning, swelling, or sterile abscess at the site of venipuncture.
    - Pulmonary hypertension.
    - Anaphylactoid reactions.
- **Specific Observations:** One case of "nail disorder" was reported in a Japanese post-marketing study of a Type 3 patient [Japanese/Australian source].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Dynamic Interactions:** No formal interaction studies have been conducted. 
- **Food Interactions:** Interactions with food are considered unlikely.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy Category:** **Category B2** [Australian].
- **Clinical Data:** Post-marketing data from >150 pregnancies suggest no increase in malformations or fetal death compared to the general population. 
- **Recommendations:** **Cerezyme** should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed. Gaucher disease may exacerbate during pregnancy (increased risk of skeletal events and cytopenia); therefore, continuing treatment in pregnant women should be considered to maintain optimal health.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if imiglucerase is excreted in human milk. However, the enzyme is likely to be digested in the infant's gastrointestinal tract. Exercise caution when administering to nursing mothers.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** No specific paediatric data provided in the core document, though clinical trials included patients as young as 12 [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No specific data available [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Dosing adjustments for renal or hepatic impairment are not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Imiglucerase replaces deficient β-glucocerebrosidase, catalysing the hydrolysis of glucocerebroside into glucose and ceramide. This prevents the accumulation of glucocerebroside in tissue macrophages (Gaucher cells).
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces organomegaly, improves haematological parameters (haemoglobin, platelets), and improves bone mineral density. It also decreases chitotriosidase, a biomarker of macrophage lipid accumulation.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Steady State:** Achieved within 30 minutes of infusion.
    - **Elimination:** Rapid decline in plasma activity after infusion.
    - **Half-life:** 3.6 to 10.4 minutes.
    - **Clearance:** Mean 14.5 ± 4.0 mL/min/kg.
    - **Volume of Distribution:** Mean 0.12 ± 0.02 L/kg.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Trial (RC91-0110):** A 6-month study of 30 patients (15 receiving **Cerezyme** 60 U/kg every 2 weeks). Showed significant improvement in haemoglobin (20% mean increase), platelets (33% increase), and reduction in liver (-11%) and spleen (-35%) volumes.
- **Dosing Comparison (RC92-301):** Evaluated 15 U/kg every 2 weeks vs. 2.5 U/kg three times weekly. Both showed visceral and haematological improvement, though bone manifestations were less responsive to lower doses.
- **Registry Data (ICGG):** Analysis of 528 patients confirmed dose-dependent responses, with 60 U/kg every 2 weeks providing faster and greater maximum effects, particularly for bone mineral density.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store between 2°C and 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze). Shelf life is 3 years.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Stable for up to 12 hours at room temperature (25°C) or 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C. However, it should be diluted promptly and not stored for subsequent use.
- **Diluted Solution:** Chemical stability for up to 24 hours at 2°C to 8°C (protected from light). From a microbiological perspective, use within 3 hours is recommended unless reconstitution/dilution occurred under controlled aseptic conditions.
