# Editorial Product Information: Ifosfamide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview of **ifosfamide** for healthcare professionals, incorporating available regulatory data into a single, clinically focused reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for informational purposes for healthcare professionals. It does not replace the official PI or local regulatory guidelines required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ifosfamide (a synthetic analogue of cyclophosphamide belonging to the nitrogen mustard chemical class).
- **Brand Name(s):** **HOLOXAN** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01AA06.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Powder for concentrate for solution for infusion in 500 mg, 1 g, and 2 g vials [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous infusion.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Pty Limited [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Ifosfamide** is indicated for the treatment of tumours sensitive to the agent, used either as monotherapy or in combination with other cytotoxic drugs. Documented responsive tumour types include [Australian]:
- Germ cell tumours
- Sarcomas
- Lymphomas
- Ovarian cancer
- Cervical cancer
- Lung cancer (some activity)
- Breast cancer (some activity)

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
Dosage must be strictly individualised based on the indication, patient health status, organ function, and haematological parameters. Standard regimens include [Australian]:
- **Fractionated Dose:** 8–10 g/m² per course, divided into equal daily doses over five consecutive days.
- **Continuous Infusion:** 5–6 g/m² (maximum 10 g) administered as a 24-hour infusion.
- **Cycle Frequency:** Typically repeated every 2–4 weeks for intermittent therapy or 3–4 weeks for 24-hour infusions, provided haematological recovery has occurred (WBC >4x10⁹/L and platelets >100x10⁹/L).

### Administration Instructions
- **Uroprotection:** **Ifosfamide** must always be administered concurrently with the uroprotector **mesna** to prevent haemorrhagic cystitis [Australian].
- **Hydration:** Vigorous oral or parenteral hydration is required during and immediately after administration to force diuresis.
- **Reconstitution:** Reconstitute with sterile water for injection to a concentration of 40 mg/mL. Further dilute to 3–4 mg/mL in 0.9% sodium chloride, 5% glucose, or Lactated Ringer’s solution [Australian].
- **Compatibility:** **Ifosfamide** is incompatible with cisplatin solutions [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
[Australian-approved contraindications]:
- Known hypersensitivity to **ifosfamide**.
- Severely impaired bone marrow function (especially in patients previously treated with radiotherapy or cytotoxic agents).
- Active inflammation of the urinary bladder (cystitis).
- Impaired renal function or obstructions of urinary flow.
- Severe hepatic impairment.
- Acute infections.
- Pregnancy and lactation.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in available sources.
- **CNS Toxicity:** Encephalopathy and other neurotoxic effects (e.g., confusion, somnolence, coma) may occur within hours to days of administration. Risk factors include renal impairment, low serum albumin, and prior use of nephrotoxic drugs like cisplatin. Treatment must be discontinued if encephalopathy develops [Australian].
- **Urotoxicity:** Haemorrhagic cystitis is a dose-limiting toxicity. Urinalysis is required before every dose; if haematuria (>10 RBCs per high power field) is present, treatment must be withheld until resolution.
- **Nephrotoxicity:** Both glomerular and tubular dysfunction can occur, potentially leading to Fanconi syndrome, rickets (children), or osteomalacia (adults).
- **Myelosuppression:** Significant reduction in leucocyte counts is expected; the nadir typically occurs 8–10 days after starting a course [Australian].
- **Cardiotoxicity:** Risk is increased in patients with pre-existing cardiac disease or those previously treated with anthracyclines or radiation to the heart region.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are categorised by frequency [Australian]:

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Myelosuppression, leucopenia, neutropenia, alopecia, nausea, vomiting, encephalopathy, drowsiness, haematuria (micro-hematuria), fever. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Thrombocytopenia, metabolic acidosis, infections, haemorrhagic cystitis, nephropathies, tubular dysfunction. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Pneumonia, sepsis, secondary tumours, anorexia, hallucinations, depressive psychosis, disorientation, arrhythmia, heart failure, diarrhoea, constipation, liver dysfunction. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Anaemia, hypersensitivity reactions, SIADH, hyponatremia, dehydration, cerebellar syndrome, hypotension, pulmonary disorder, stomatitis, rash, cramps, rickets, osteomalacia. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Anaphylactic shock, hypokalemia, coma, seizures, thromboembolism, acute pancreatitis, toxic skin reactions, rhabdomyolysis, Fanconi syndrome. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **CYP2B6 Interactions:** Bupropion and other substrates/inhibitors of CYP2B6 may interfere with **ifosfamide** metabolism [Australian].
- **Enzyme Inducers:** Prior or concurrent treatment with carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, or rifampicin may induce microsomal enzymes, potentially increasing the formation of toxic metabolites.
- **Nephrotoxic/Neurotoxic Agents:** Concomitant use of cisplatin, aminoglycosides, acyclovir, or amphotericin B increases the risk of renal and CNS toxicity.
- **CNS Actants:** Caution is advised when using antiemetics, tranquillizers, or antihistamines due to additive CNS effects.
- **Grapefruit:** May reduce the metabolic activation and efficacy of **ifosfamide**; avoid consumption.
- **Warfarin:** May increase the risk of bleeding.
- **Vaccines:** Reduced response to vaccines; live vaccines may cause vaccine-induced infections [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D [Australian]**. Animal studies show embryotoxic and teratogenic effects. **Ifosfamide** can cause fetal damage. It should not be used in pregnancy unless the benefit outweighs the high risk to the fetus. 
- **Contraception:** Women must use reliable contraception during and for 12 months after treatment. Men should use contraception during and for 6 months after treatment.
- **Breastfeeding:** **Ifosfamide** is excreted in breast milk. Breastfeeding is strongly discouraged due to potential serious adverse events and tumorigenicity.
- **Fertility:** Commonly causes azoospermia or oligospermia (sometimes irreversible) in men and amenorrhoea or reduced estrogen levels in women. Sperm preservation should be discussed before starting therapy [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Children, especially those under 3 years of age, are at a higher risk of nephrotoxicity. Long-term monitoring of renal function and bone health (to detect rickets) is required [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Dose adjustments should be considered to account for the higher frequency of hepatic, renal, and cardiac dysfunction in this population.
- **Renal Impairment:** Decreased excretion leads to higher plasma levels of metabolites, increasing the risk of neurotoxicity and haematotoxicity. **Ifosfamide** is dialyzable.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Severe impairment may decrease the activation of **ifosfamide**, potentially reducing therapeutic efficacy. It also increases the risk of CNS toxicity.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Ifosfamide** is a pro-drug requiring activation by hepatic microsomal enzymes (hydroxylation) to form 4-hydroxyifosfamide. This eventually produces the cytotoxic ifosphoramide mustard, which interacts with DNA to form cross-links and prevent cell division [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP isoenzymes.
  - **Elimination:** Biphasic decay at high doses (half-life ~15 hours); monoexponential at lower doses (half-life ~7 hours).
  - **Excretion:** Primarily renal.

## 11. Clinical Trials

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Five years for the unopened vial.
- **Stability:** Reconstituted and diluted solutions should ideally be used immediately (within 6–8 hours) to reduce microbiological risk, although they are chemically stable for 24 hours when refrigerated.
