**Product Name**

**Editorial Version**

Ioversol is a non-ionic, water-soluble, monomeric radiopaque contrast agent available under the brand name Optiray. It is formulated in various concentrations of organically bound iodine (300 mg/mL, 320 mg/mL, and 350 mg/mL) to facilitate radiographic visualization of the vascular system and internal organs. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Therapeutic Indications**

**Editorial Version**

**Adult Patients**
Ioversol is indicated for use in a wide range of intravascular radiographic procedures:
*   **Intra-arterial:** Cerebral, coronary, peripheral, visceral, and renal arteriography; aortography; and left ventriculography. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Intravenous:** Computed tomography (CT) of the head and body; venography; and excretory urography. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA):** Indicated for both intra-arterial (IA-DSA) and intravenous (IV-DSA) techniques. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Pediatric Patients**
*   **Angiocardiography:** Indicated for children using 320 or 350 mg I/mL strengths. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **CT and Urography:** Indicated for contrast-enhanced CT of the head/body and excretory urography in children using the 320 mg I/mL strength. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Dosage and Administration**

**Editorial Version**

**General Principles**
Administer the lowest dose required to achieve diagnostic-quality visualization. Contrast media should ideally be at or near body temperature upon injection. Solutions must be inspected visually for discoloration or particulate matter before use; do not use if observed. All unused portions must be discarded. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Adult Dosing (Intra-arterial)**
*   **Cerebral Arteriography:** 2–12 mL (carotid/vertebral); 20–50 mL (aortic arch). Total procedural dose usually <150 mL (Australia) or 200 mL (US).
*   **Peripheral Arteriography:** 20–90 mL (aorta-iliac); 10–50 mL (common iliac/femoral); 15–30 mL (subclavian/brachial). Total procedural dose usually <150 mL (Australia) or 250 mL (US).
*   **Visceral/Renal Arteriography and Aortography:** 10–80 mL (aorta); 12–60 mL (coeliac/superior mesenteric); 6–15 mL (renal/inferior mesenteric). Total procedural dose usually <150 mL (Australia) or 250 mL (US).
*   **Coronary Arteriography:** 2–10 mL (left coronary); 1–10 mL (right coronary).
*   **Left Ventriculography:** 30–50 mL.
*   **Combined Cardiac Procedures:** Total dose <150 mL (Australia) or 250 mL (US).

**Adult Dosing (Intravenous)**
*   **Venography:** 50–100 mL per extremity. Max 150 mL (Australia) or 250 mL (US).
*   **Computed Tomography (CT):** 50–150 mL (Head/Body infusion); 25–75 mL (Body bolus). Max 150 mL.
*   **Excretory Urography:** 50–75 mL (routine). For high-dose urography in impaired renal function or elderly, 1.4–2 mL/kg (max 150 mL).

**Pediatric Dosing**
*   **Angiocardiography:** Usual dose 1.25 mL/kg (range 1–1.5 mL/kg). Cumulative max 5 mL/kg, not to exceed 75 mL (Australia) or 250 mL (US).
*   **CT Imaging:** 1.3–2.4 mL/kg (Head) and 1.7–2.4 mL/kg (Body). US range: 1–3 mL/kg.
*   **Excretory Urography:** 1–1.5 mL/kg (range 0.5–3 mL/kg). Max 3 mL/kg. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Contraindications**

**Editorial Version**

Ioversol is contraindicated in the following conditions:
*   Known hypersensitivity to ioversol or any component of the formulation. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   Manifest or symptomatic hyperthyroidism. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

The Australian Product Information additionally identifies the following contraindications:
*   Thyrotoxicosis.
*   Anuria.
*   Decompensated cardiac insufficiency.
*   Specific procedural contraindications: Carotid artery angiography during the progressive period of a stroke; coronary arteriography within the first four weeks following a myocardial infarction.
*   Infection or open injury in or near the region to be examined. (Source: Australian Product Information)

**Warnings and Precautions**

**Editorial Version**

**BOXED WARNING (United States): NOT FOR INTRATHECAL USE**
Inadvertent intrathecal administration may result in death, convulsions, cerebral hemorrhage, coma, paralysis, arachnoiditis, acute renal failure, cardiac arrest, seizures, rhabdomyolysis, hyperthermia, and brain edema. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)

**Hypersensitivity Reactions**
Serious, life-threatening, and fatal reactions, including anaphylaxis and shock, may occur. Most develop within 1 to 3 minutes, but delayed reactions are possible. High-risk factors include a history of previous contrast reaction, known iodine sensitivity, or history of asthma and allergies. Premedication with corticosteroids or antihistamines may reduce severity but does not prevent serious reactions. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Contrast-Induced Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)**
Ioversol may cause acute renal failure. Risk factors include pre-existing renal impairment, diabetes, dehydration, congestive heart failure, advanced vascular disease, elderly age, and concomitant use of nephrotoxic drugs or diuretics. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Cardiovascular Reactions**
Ioversol increases circulatory osmotic load. It may cause hemodynamic disturbances, shock, cardiac arrest, arrhythmias, and myocardial ischemia. Monitor patients with cardiac disease closely. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Thromboembolic Events**
Serious events, including stroke and MI, have occurred during catheterization. Non-ionic media have less in vitro anticoagulant effect than ionic media. Maintain meticulous technique and avoid prolonged contact between blood and the contrast medium in syringes. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Thyroid Dysfunction**
*   **Thyroid Storm:** Risk of thyroid storm in patients with hyperthyroidism or autonomous nodules.
*   **Pediatric Patients (0–3 years):** High risk of transient hypothyroidism or thyroid suppression. Monitor thyroid function based on risk factors (e.g., prematurity). (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs)**
Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), Acute Generalized Exanthematous Pustulosis (AGEP), and DRESS syndrome may develop from 1 hour to several weeks post-administration. Discontinue if SCAR is suspected. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Central Nervous System (CNS) Disorders**
Encephalopathy, manifesting as headache, cortical blindness, seizures, or coma, has been reported. Risk is higher with blood-brain barrier disruption. Do not repeat administration if encephalopathy is suspected. (Source: Australian Product Information)

**Pregnancy**

**Editorial Version**

**Pregnancy Category B1 (Australia) / Risk Summary (US)**
There are no adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Animal studies using doses up to 3.2 g I/kg showed no teratogenicity or adverse developmental effects. Ioversol crosses the placenta and can be visualized in the fetal digestive tract. Use during pregnancy only if clearly required. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Neonatal Monitoring**
If iodinated contrast was administered during pregnancy, monitor the neonate's thyroid function during the first week of life, with repeat monitoring at 2 and 4 weeks of age. (Source: Australian Product Information)

**Breastfeeding**

**Editorial Version**

Ioversol is likely excreted into human milk in very small amounts, as is common with many iodinated contrast agents. Because of the potential for adverse reactions in the infant, caution should be exercised. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Clinical Consideration**
Interruption of breastfeeding is not mandatory due to low infant exposure. However, a lactating woman may choose to pump and discard milk for 8 hours (approximately 5 half-lives) post-administration to minimize exposure. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Drug Interactions**

**Editorial Version**

*   **Metformin:** Risk of lactic acidosis if contrast causes renal failure. In patients with eGFR 30–60 mL/min/1.73m², or those receiving intra-arterial contrast, stop metformin at the time of the procedure. Withhold for 48 hours and resume only after renal function is confirmed stable. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Radioactive Iodine:** Contrast may interfere with thyroid uptake of I-131 for 6–8 weeks (US) or up to 16 days (Australia). This reduces therapeutic efficacy in thyroid carcinoma. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Interleukin:** Patients treated with Interleukin may have a higher incidence of delayed reactions (e.g., skin rash, hypotension). (Source: Australian Product Information)
*   **Oral Cholecystographic Agents:** Reported renal toxicity when followed by intravascular contrast. Postpone ioversol in patients who recently received these agents. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Vasopressors:** Arterial injection of contrast following vasopressors strongly potentiates neurotoxic effects. (Source: Australian Product Information)
*   **Adrenergic Beta-Blockers:** May increase the risk of severe hypersensitivity reactions and interfere with treatment. (Source: Australian Product Information)

**Pharmacokinetics**

**Editorial Version**

**Distribution**
Ioversol follows an open two-compartment model with first-order elimination. The volume of distribution is approximately 0.26 L/kg, consistent with extracellular space distribution. Protein binding is negligible (0–13%). (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Metabolism**
Ioversol does not undergo significant metabolism, deiodination, or biotransformation. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Elimination**
The biological half-life is approximately 1.5 hours. In patients with normal renal function, >95% of the dose is excreted unchanged in the urine within 24 hours. Peak urine concentrations occur within the first two hours. Faecal elimination is negligible. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Pharmacodynamics / Opacification**
Visualization of the renal parenchyma occurs within 30–60 seconds of injection. Optimal opacification of the calyces and pelves occurs within 5–15 minutes. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Patient Counselling Information**

**Editorial Version**

Patients should be advised to:
1.  Inform the clinician of pregnancy or breastfeeding status.
2.  Disclose history of asthma, allergies, diabetes, multiple myeloma, pheochromocytoma, or sickle cell disease.
3.  Report any history of previous reactions to contrast media.
4.  Maintain adequate hydration before and after the procedure.
5.  Report any delayed symptoms, such as rash or difficulty breathing, immediately.
6.  Avoid driving or operating machinery for 30–60 minutes post-procedure due to the risk of early reactions. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
