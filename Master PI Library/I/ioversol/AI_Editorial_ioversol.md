# Editorial Product Information: Ioversol

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Regulatory Agencies:** Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA - Australia); Food and Drug Administration (FDA - United States).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ioversol Product Information (PI) for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, safety warnings, and jurisdictional variations into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official, current PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ioversol
- **Chemical Class:** Non-ionic, water-soluble, tri-iodinated X-ray contrast medium.
- **Brand Names:** **Optiray** (Global)
- **ATC Code:** V08AB07
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Optiray 300** (300 mg iodine/mL; ioversol 64%): [FDA]
    - **Optiray 320** (320 mg iodine/mL; ioversol 68%): [TGA, FDA]
    - **Optiray 350** (350 mg iodine/mL; ioversol 74%): [TGA, FDA]
- **Routes of Administration:** Intra-arterial (IA) injection or infusion; Intravenous (IV) injection or infusion.
- **Sponsors:** Guerbet Australia Pty Ltd [TGA]; Liebel-Flarsheim Company LLC [FDA].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Intra-arterial Procedures (Adults):**
- Cerebral arteriography (Optiray 320)
- Peripheral arteriography (Optiray 320, 350)
- Visceral and renal arteriography, including aortography (Optiray 320, 350 [TGA])
- Coronary arteriography and left ventriculography (Optiray 320, 350)

**Intra-arterial Procedures (Pediatrics):**
- Angiocardiography (Optiray 320, 350)

**Intravenous Procedures (Adults):**
- Computed Tomography (CT) imaging of the head and body (Optiray 320, 350)
- Venography (Optiray 320, 350)
- Intravenous excretory urography (Optiray 320, 350)
- Intravenous Digital Subtraction Angiography (IV-DSA) (Optiray 350 [FDA])

**Intravenous Procedures (Pediatrics):**
- CT imaging of the head and body (Optiray 320)
- Intravenous excretory urography (Optiray 320)

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Principles
- Use the lowest dose necessary to achieve adequate visualisation.
- Solutions should be at or near body temperature before injection to minimise viscosity.
- Hydrate patients adequately before and after administration to reduce the risk of contrast-induced nephropathy.
- **Maximum Cumulative Dose:** Usually 150 mL [TGA] to 250 mL [FDA] per procedure in adults.

### Adult Dosing (Typical Ranges)
| Procedure | Strength (Optiray) | Usual Volume/Dose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cerebral Arteriography** | 320 | 2–12 mL (carotid/vertebral); 20–50 mL (aortic arch) |
| **Peripheral Arteriography** | 320/350 | 20–90 mL (aorta-iliac); 10–50 mL (femoral) |
| **Coronary Arteriography** | 320/350 | 2–10 mL (left); 1–10 mL (right) |
| **Left Ventriculography** | 320/350 | 30–50 mL |
| **CT Head/Body (Infusion)** | 320/350 | 50–150 mL |
| **Venography** | 320/350 | 50–100 mL per extremity |
| **Excretory Urography** | 320/350 | 50–75 mL (standard); up to 1.5–2 mL/kg (high dose) |

### Pediatric Dosing
- **Angiocardiography:** 1.25 mL/kg (range 1–1.5 mL/kg). Maximum cumulative dose 5 mL/kg, not to exceed 250 mL [FDA] or 75 mL [TGA].
- **CT Head/Body Imaging:** 1.5 to 2 mL/kg (range 1–3 mL/kg) of Optiray 320.
- **Excretory Urography:** 1 to 1.5 mL/kg of Optiray 320 (range 0.5–3 mL/kg).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity:** To ioversol, iodine, or any excipients.
- **Thyroid Disorders:** Symptomatic/Manifest hyperthyroidism or thyrotoxicosis.
- **Renal/Cardiac:** Anuria; Decompensated cardiac insufficiency [TGA].
- **Procedure-Specific [TGA]:** Carotid angiography during stroke progression; coronary arteriography within 4 weeks of myocardial infarction; presence of infection or open injury near the examination site.

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warning [FDA]
> **NOT FOR INTRATHECAL USE:** Inadvertent intrathecal administration may cause death, convulsions, cerebral haemorrhage, coma, paralysis, arachnoiditis, acute renal failure, cardiac arrest, seizures, rhabdomyolysis, hyperthermia, and brain oedema.

### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Life-threatening or fatal anaphylactoid reactions can occur. Resuscitation equipment must be immediately available. Most reactions occur within 10 minutes, but delayed reactions can occur.
- **Contrast-Induced Acute Kidney Injury:** Risk is elevated in patients with pre-existing renal impairment, diabetes, dehydration, or multiple myeloma.
- **Cardiovascular Risk:** May cause haemodynamic disturbances, arrhythmia, or cardiac arrest, particularly in patients with congestive heart failure.
- **Thromboembolic Events:** Serious events including MI and stroke have been reported. Meticulous angiographic technique is required to minimise clotting.
- **Thyroid Storm:** Risk in patients with hyperthyroidism or autonomous thyroid nodules.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** SJS, TEN, AGEP, and DRESS may develop from 1 hour to several weeks post-injection.

### Monitoring
- Monitor vital signs and ECG routinely during coronary procedures.
- Observe patients for at least 30–60 minutes post-administration for signs of delayed hypersensitivity.

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Feeling hot [TGA], flush [TGA].
- **Common (1–10%):** Nausea, pain (general or at injection site), angina pectoris (1.6% in coronary studies).
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):** Vomiting, headache, dizziness, paraesthesia, urticaria, erythema, pruritus, blood pressure increase.
- **Rare (<0.1%):** Hypotension, bradycardia, syncope, tremor, rhinitis, laryngeal oedema, nasal congestion, shivering/chills.
- **Serious Post-Marketing Reports:** Cardiac arrest, ventricular fibrillation, seizure, contrast-induced encephalopathy, temporary blindness, respiratory arrest, acute kidney injury, SJS/TEN.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Metformin:** Increased risk of lactic acidosis if contrast-induced renal failure occurs. Withhold metformin at the time of the procedure and for 48 hours afterward; resume only after renal function is confirmed stable.
- **Interleukin-2:** Increased incidence of delayed contrast reactions (e.g., skin rash, hypotension).
- **Cholecystographic Agents (Oral):** Increased risk of renal toxicity in patients with liver dysfunction. Postpone ioversol if these have been recently administered.
- **Vasopressors:** May strongly potentiate neurological effects during intra-arterial injection.
- **Radioactive Iodine:** Contrast media interferes with thyroid uptake of I-131 for 2–8 weeks.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **TGA Category B1:** No teratogenic effects in animals. Should be used in pregnancy only if clearly needed.
    - **FDA:** Insufficient human data to determine risk. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no adverse developmental effects at doses up to 0.7 times the human dose.
    - **Neonatal Monitoring:** Monitor thyroid function in neonates if exposed in utero.
- **Breastfeeding:** Ioversol is excreted in human milk in very small amounts. Interruption of breastfeeding is generally not required, but a lactating woman may consider pumping and discarding milk for 8 hours [FDA] to minimise exposure.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies indicate no effect on male or female fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatrics:** Safety established for children >1 month. Higher risk of adverse events in those <12 months or with asthma, heart failure, or renal impairment. Monitor thyroid function (T4/TSH) in children 0–3 years of age 7–10 days and 1 month post-exposure.
- **Elderly:** Use conservative dosing. Higher risk of renal impairment and cardiovascular sensitivity. 
- **Renal Impairment:** Clearance is significantly prolonged. Ioversol is dialyzable. Avoid nephrotoxic co-medications.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Caution is required, especially in combined renal and hepatic disease.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Opacifies vessels and organs in the path of the contrast flow, enabling radiographic visualisation.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Enhancement is directly related to iodine content. Peak blood levels occur immediately post-bolus.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution is 0.26 L/kg (extracellular space). Protein binding is very low (9–13% [TGA]; negligible [FDA]).
    - **Metabolism:** No significant metabolism or biotransformation occurs.
    - **Elimination:** >95% excreted unchanged by the kidneys within 24 hours. Half-life is approximately 1.5 hours in healthy adults.

## 11. Clinical Trials
Clinical trial data across both agencies involve over 4,000 adult patients [FDA] and 1,000+ [TGA], demonstrating that ioversol results in fewer haemodynamic and ECG changes compared to older ionic contrast agents. Paediatric studies (n=128 [TGA]; n=311 [FDA]) showed similar safety profiles to adults, with nausea and fever being the most common pediatric adverse events.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [TGA, FDA]. Excursions to 30°C permitted [FDA].
- **Light:** Protect from strong daylight and secondary X-rays.
- **Handling:** Do not freeze. Discard if crystallization occurs. 
- **Stability:** Pharmacy Bulk Packages must be used within 4 hours of penetration [FDA] or 24 hours in a multi-dispensing device [TGA].
