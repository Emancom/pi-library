# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**. 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a clinically focused format. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the United States PI was available for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Name:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection, USP [United States].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [United States].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic treatment of patients with genetic metabolic defects resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [United States].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) on Hemodialysis:** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
Dosage is calculated based on body weight and clinical response.

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [United States].
- **Administration:** Delivered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by equivalent doses divided every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h). 
- **Minimum Frequency:** Doses should not be spaced further than every 6 hours (q6h).
- **Subsequent Dosing:** Daily maintenance doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement [United States].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [United States].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Titration:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma concentrations <40–50 µmol/L. Dose adjustments (e.g., reduction to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough levocarnitine levels.

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discolouration before use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as it contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings
- **None specified in available sources.**

### General Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal oedema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These can occur within minutes of intravenous administration, particularly in ESRD patients on dialysis. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and do not re-administer without a careful risk-benefit analysis [United States].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine (oral and IV) has been associated with seizures in patients with or without a history of seizure activity. In those with pre-existing conditions, an increase in seizure frequency or severity has been observed [United States].

### Monitoring Requirements
- **Plasma Carnitine Levels:** It is recommended to obtain baseline levels before starting parenteral therapy.
- **Ongoing Monitoring:** Weekly and monthly monitoring should include plasma free carnitine concentrations (target range: 35 to 60 µmol/L), blood chemistries, and vital signs [United States].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Renal Impairment (Oral vs. IV):** While the IV form is used in ESRD, the safety of chronic *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. High oral doses in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [United States].

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Seizures.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate that levocarnitine may increase the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustment in patients receiving warfarin [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [United States: Category not assigned by letter, but the PI states the drug should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed.] Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following administration. The risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [United States].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy are established for use in paediatric patients for both metabolic disorders and ESRD carnitine deficiency (see section 3 for weight-based dosing).
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** See "ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis" in Dosage and Administration (Section 3) and Precautions (Section 5).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels). Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours. Total body clearance is approximately 4.00 L/h [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients on dialysis significantly increases plasma carnitine concentrations.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Pharmacokinetic Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis showed that trough (pre-dialysis) levels rose from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L after 8 weeks [United States].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses (N~30 per group) demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [United States].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [United States].
