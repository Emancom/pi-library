# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions’ Product Information documents contributed to this editorial: United States (FDA). 
- **Note:** At the time of this editorial synthesis, only the United States (FDA) documentation was available for review. Information from other major regulatory bodies (e.g., Australian TGA, European EMA, Health Canada) was not specified in the available source material.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview intended for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial resource and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport; quaternary ammonium compound.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US]; Manufactured by Baxter Pharmaceuticals India Private Ltd [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic management of patients with genetic metabolic defects resulting in secondary deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **General Dosing:** Recommended dose is 50 mg/kg.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow bolus injection (2–3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often followed by equivalent doses administered every 3 or 4 hours (never less frequently than every 6 hours) over the first 24 hours.
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses typically remain at 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised based on clinical requirement [US].
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) and monitor blood chemistry/vital signs weekly or monthly [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight.
- **Administration:** Administer as a slow (2–3 minute) bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Adjustment:** Dose adjustments should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma levocarnitine levels. If levels are below normal (40–50 µmol/L), initiation or upward adjustment is indicated. Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be performed as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough concentrations [US].

### General Instructions
- **Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known:** There are currently no listed contraindications in the available source documentation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue the drug immediately and initiate appropriate medical care. Caution is advised if re-administration is considered [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients both with and without pre-existing seizure history. In patients with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine):** While this PI concerns the injection, note that chronic high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** In dialysis patients, monitor trough levocarnitine levels regularly.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are often confounded by the underlying pathology of ESRD or metabolic disorders.

### General Population
- **Common:** Transient nausea and vomiting.
- **Less Frequent:** Body odor, gastritis.

### ESRD Patients (Trials)
In clinical trials of ESRD patients, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% (regardless of causality) [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%), flu syndrome (15–29%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), chest pain (6–15%), tachycardia (5–9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%).

### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Seizures:** Reported in post-marketing surveillance [US].
- **Anaphylaxis:** Including life-threatening respiratory distress [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned by letter, but descriptive]. Animal studies (rats/rabbits) at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. No adequate/well-controlled human studies exist. Use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers are unavailable. Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine concentrations in milk following administration. Weigh the benefits of maternal supplementation against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is based on mg/kg body weight as described in Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Specific clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects. Dose selection should be cautious, usually starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** See Section 3 for ESRD-specific dosing. The safety of high-dose *oral* therapy is not established in renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound. Mean distribution half-life is 0.585 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Administration to ESRD patients on hemodialysis results in increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x weekly), trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. 
- **Dose-Ranging:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].
- **Note:** While levocarnitine effectively increases plasma concentrations in ESRD, the impact on specific clinical outcomes in this population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Once mixed in Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL), the solution is stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Rx-only. Poison schedule not applicable.
