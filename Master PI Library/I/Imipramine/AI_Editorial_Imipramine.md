# Editorial Product Information: Imipramine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, intended to consolidate clinical guidance and regulatory requirements into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical resource only. It is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing and dispensing in any specific region.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Imipramine hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Tricyclic antidepressant (TCA); dibenzazepine derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Tofranil [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06AA02.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sugar-coated tablets: 10 mg (triangular, red-brown) and 25 mg (round, reddish-brown) [Australian].
- **Routes of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amdipharm Mercury (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Major Depression:** Management of depressive illness [Australian].
- **Nocturnal Enuresis:** Adjunctive treatment in children aged 5 years and older, provided organic causes have been excluded [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult Depression)
Dosage must be individualised, aiming for the lowest effective dose.
- **Ambulatory Patients:** Initial dose of 25 mg up to three times daily. Increase stepwise to 150–200 mg daily by the end of the first week. Maintenance dose typically ranges from 50–100 mg daily [Australian].
- **Hospitalised Patients:** Initial dose of 25 mg three times daily. Increase stepwise by 25 mg daily until a dose of 200 mg is reached. Severe cases may require up to 100 mg three times daily. Maintenance dose is generally 100 mg daily [Australian].

### Nocturnal Enuresis (Children 5 years and over)
Administer as a single dose after the evening meal. For children who wet the bed early in the night, part of the dose should be given at 4:00 PM.
- **5–8 years:** 20–30 mg daily [Australian].
- **9–12 years:** 25–50 mg daily [Australian].
- **Older children:** 25–75 mg daily [Australian].
- **Maximum Pediatric Dose:** Do not exceed 2.5 mg/kg daily [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- Tablets should be swallowed whole. 
- **Tapering:** Abrupt withdrawal must be avoided. Medication should be tapered gradually to prevent withdrawal symptoms [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to imipramine or excipients (e.g., lactose, sucrose) [Australian].
- Demonstrated cross-sensitivity to other tricyclic antidepressants in the dibenzazepine group [Australian].
- **MAOIs:** Concomitant use with irreversible MAO inhibitors, or within 14 days of stopping/starting treatment. A 24-hour interval is required when switching from the reversible MAO-A inhibitor moclobemide [Australian].
- **Myocardial Infarction:** Use during the acute stage and recovery period [Australian].
- **Psychiatric use in minors:** Use in patients under 18 years of age for the treatment of depression or other psychiatric disorders [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidality Risk:** Clinical worsening and suicidal ideation/behaviour may occur in both children and adults. Close monitoring is mandatory, particularly during the first few months of therapy and at times of dose adjustments. Prescriptions should be for the smallest quantity consistent with safety [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** TCAs carry risks of sinus tachycardia, orthostatic hypotension, and conduction delays. Caution is required in patients with cardiovascular disease or the elderly. ECG monitoring is required if arrhythmias or QTc prolongation risks are present [Australian].
- **Seizure Threshold:** Imipramine may lower the convulsion threshold; use with caution in patients with epilepsy or brain damage [Australian].
- **Anticholinergic Risks:** Use with caution in patients with narrow-angle glaucoma, urinary retention (e.g., prostatic hypertrophy), or chronic constipation (risk of paralytic ileus) [Australian].
- **Serotonin Syndrome:** Risk increases when combined with other serotonergic agents (SSRIs, SNRIs). Symptoms include mental status changes, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular hyperactivity [Australian].
- **Bipolar Disorder:** Treatment may precipitate a manic or hypomanic episode in patients with underlying bipolar disorder [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are common and often related to the drug’s pharmacological properties (anticholinergic, antihistaminic).

| Frequency | Adverse Effects |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Tremor, sinus tachycardia, hot flushes, orthostatic hypotension, dry mouth, constipation, hyperhidrosis, weight gain, ECG abnormalities (ST/T wave changes). |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Dizziness, headache, sedation, somnolence, paraesthesia, blurred vision, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, fatigue, anorexia, restlessness, hallucinations, anxiety. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Not specifically categorised in available sources. |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Convulsions, psychotic disorder. |
| **Very Rare (<0.01%)** | Agranulocytosis, anaphylactic reaction, SIADH, cardiac failure, QTc prolongation, Torsades de pointes, stroke, hepatitis, paralytic ileus, glaucoma, sudden death. |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Bone marrow depression, severe cardiac arrhythmias, and suicidal behaviours [Australian].
- **Withdrawal Symptoms:** Nausea, headache, malaise, and anxiety may occur upon abrupt discontinuation [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **MAOIs:** Risk of hypertensive crisis, hyperpyrexia, and death [Australian].
- **CYP2D6/1A2 Inhibitors (e.g., SSRIs):** Fluoxetine, paroxetine, and fluvoxamine may significantly increase imipramine plasma concentrations, requiring dose reduction [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** Potentiation of alcohol, barbiturates, and benzodiazepines [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** Enhanced effects, increasing risk of paralytic ileus or glaucoma attacks [Australian].
- **Antihypertensives:** Imipramine may diminish the effects of clonidine, guanethidine, and methyldopa [Australian].
- **Sympathomimetics:** Potentiation of cardiovascular effects (e.g., adrenaline, noradrenaline) [Australian].
- **QT-prolonging drugs:** Increased risk of Torsades de pointes when combined with thioridazine, cisapride, or cotrimoxazole [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category C]. Limited experience. Isolated reports of adverse fetal effects. Use should be avoided unless benefits clearly outweigh risks.
- **Neonatal Effects:** Infants of mothers taking imipramine until delivery may experience withdrawal symptoms (dyspnoea, lethargy, irritability, hypotension/hypertension). It is recommended to gradually withdraw imipramine at least 7 weeks before the expected date of delivery [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Imipramine and its metabolite desmethylimipramine pass into breast milk in small amounts. Infants should be weaned or the drug withdrawn due to potential risks [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Contraindicated for depression under age 18. Approved for nocturnal enuresis in children ≥5 years. Caution regarding cardiotoxicity at doses exceeding 2.5 mg/kg [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased sensitivity to side effects (sedation, hypotension, anticholinergic effects). Start at a low dose (10 mg/day) and increase cautiously to an optimum of 30–50 mg/day [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Monitoring is recommended; however, specific dosing adjustments are not provided in the primary source [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Periodic checking of hepatic enzyme levels is recommended in patients with known liver disease [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A "mixed" re-uptake inhibitor that increases synaptic concentrations of noradrenaline and serotonin (5-HT) by blocking their neuronal re-uptake. It also possesses alpha-adrenolytic, antihistaminic, and anticholinergic properties [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapidly and well absorbed orally; undergoes significant first-pass metabolism in the liver.
    - **Metabolism:** Partially converted to its active metabolite, desmethylimipramine.
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (86%); volume of distribution is approximately 21 L/kg.
    - **Excretion:** Approximately 80% eliminated in urine and 20% in faeces, mostly as inactive metabolites. Plasma half-life is approximately 20 hours [Australian].
