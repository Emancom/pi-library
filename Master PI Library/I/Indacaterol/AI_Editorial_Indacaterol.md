# Editorial Product Information: Indacaterol / Mometasone Furoate

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the following official source: Australian (TGA) Product Information for **Atectura Breezhaler**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the fixed-dose combination of indacaterol and mometasone furoate for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This synthesis is an editorial summary and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing within any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should refer to the local approved PI for definitive guidance.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Indacaterol (as acetate) and Mometasone furoate.
- **Chemical Class:** Indacaterol is a Long-Acting Beta2-Adrenoceptor Agonist (LABA); Mometasone furoate is an inhaled synthetic corticosteroid (ICS).
- **Brand Name:** **Atectura Breezhaler** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** R03AK14.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** Inhalation powder in hard capsules. Strengths are expressed as delivered dose (the dose leaving the mouthpiece):
    - 125 mcg / 62.5 mcg
    - 125 mcg / 127.5 mcg
    - 125 mcg / 260 mcg
    - *Note: Each capsule contains a metered dose of 150 mcg indacaterol (as 173 mcg acetate) combined with either 80 mcg, 160 mcg, or 320 mcg of mometasone furoate.*
- **Route of Administration:** Oral inhalation only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Novartis Pharmaceuticals Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Maintenance Treatment of Asthma:** Indicated for the once-daily maintenance treatment of asthma in adults and adolescents aged 12 years and older where the use of a combination of a LABA and an ICS is appropriate:
    - Patients not adequately controlled with inhaled corticosteroids and "as needed" inhaled short-acting beta2-agonists (SABA).
    - Patients not adequately controlled with LABA and low-dose ICS plus "as needed" SABA.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** One capsule to be inhaled once daily at the same time each day.
- **Dosing by Requirement:**
    - **125/62.5 mcg:** Recommended for patients requiring a LABA and a low-dose ICS.
    - **125/127.5 mcg:** Recommended for patients requiring a LABA and a medium-dose ICS.
    - **125/260 mcg:** Recommended for patients requiring a LABA and a high-dose ICS.
- **Maximum Dose:** 125/260 mcg once daily.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - For oral inhalation only; capsules must **not** be swallowed.
    - Must be administered using the provided inhaler device.
    - Patients should rinse their mouth with water (without swallowing) after inhalation to reduce the risk of oropharyngeal candidiasis.
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, it should be taken as soon as possible. Patients should be instructed not to take more than one dose per day.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to indacaterol, mometasone furoate, or any excipients (e.g., lactose) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Symptoms:** This medication is for maintenance treatment and must not be used to treat acute asthma symptoms or episodes of bronchospasm, which require a SABA.
- **Deterioration of Disease:** Patients should not stop therapy abruptly. If asthma remains uncontrolled or worsens, clinical review is required.
- **Paradoxical Bronchospasm:** As with other inhalation therapies, paradoxical bronchospasm may occur and can be life-threatening. If this occurs, the drug should be discontinued immediately and alternative therapy started.
- **Cardiovascular Effects:** Beta2-agonists may produce clinically significant cardiovascular effects (increased pulse rate, blood pressure, ECG changes). Use with caution in patients with cardiovascular disorders (coronary artery disease, arrhythmia, hypertension), convulsive disorders, or thyrotoxicosis.
- **Hypokalaemia:** Beta2-agonists may induce significant hypokalaemia, potentially leading to adverse cardiovascular effects.
- **Hyperglycaemia:** High doses of inhaled LABA and ICS may increase plasma glucose. Close monitoring is recommended for diabetic patients.
- **Systemic Corticosteroid Effects:** These may occur, particularly at high doses for prolonged periods, including Cushing’s syndrome, adrenal suppression, growth retardation (pediatrics), decreased bone mineral density, cataracts, and glaucoma.
- **Infections:** Use with caution in patients with pulmonary tuberculosis or chronic/untreated infections.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The following frequencies are based on clinical trial data (e.g., PALLADIUM study) [Australian]:
- **Common (1–10%):**
    - Headache
    - Oropharyngeal pain (including throat irritation)
    - Dysphonia
    - Musculoskeletal pain (back pain, myalgia)
    - Hypersensitivity (drug eruption, rash, urticaria)
- **Uncommon (0.1–1%):**
    - Oral candidiasis
    - Hyperglycaemia
    - Tachycardia
    - Angioedema
    - Rash and Pruritus
    - Muscle spasms

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** 
    - **CYP3A4 and P-gp Inhibitors:** Inhibition of these pathways may increase systemic exposure to indacaterol and mometasone up to two-fold. While generally considered safe for indacaterol, caution is advised when using strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, ritonavir) due to the potential for increased systemic mometasone effects.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Beta-Adrenergic Blockers:** May weaken or antagonise the effect of indacaterol. Avoid use unless compelling; cardioselective beta-blockers are preferred if necessary.
    - **QTc Prolonging Drugs:** (e.g., MAOIs, tricyclic antidepressants) may potentiate the effect of LABAs on the QT interval, increasing ventricular arrhythmia risk.
    - **Hypokalaemic Agents:** (e.g., methylxanthine derivatives, steroids, non-potassium-sparing diuretics) may potentiate the hypokalaemic effect of beta2-agonists.
    - **Other LABAs:** Co-administration is not recommended due to the risk of additive adverse effects.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category B3.**
    - Data in pregnant women are limited. Animal studies for mometasone have shown foetal malformations (typical of corticosteroids). Indacaterol may inhibit labour due to uterine relaxant effects. Use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the foetus.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if indacaterol or mometasone are excreted in human milk. Other inhaled corticosteroids are known to be transferred into milk. A risk-benefit assessment regarding the importance of the drug to the mother versus the benefit of breastfeeding to the child should be performed.
- **Fertility:** No adverse effects on fertility were observed in rat studies.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for adolescents aged 12 years and older at the same dose as adults. Safety and efficacy in children under 12 have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required in patients aged 65 years or older.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** 
    - **Mild to Moderate:** No dose adjustment required. 
    - **Severe:** No specific data available; use only if the benefit outweighs the potential risk.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Indacaterol:** A LABA that acts locally in the lung by stimulating intracellular adenyl cyclase, leading to increased cyclic AMP levels and relaxation of bronchial smooth muscle.
    - **Mometasone furoate:** A synthetic corticosteroid with high affinity for glucocorticoid receptors, exerting local anti-inflammatory effects and inhibiting the release of inflammatory mediators (leukotrienes and cytokines).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Median time to peak plasma concentration is ~15 minutes (indacaterol) and ~1 hour (mometasone). Absolute bioavailability is ~45% for indacaterol and <10% for mometasone.
    - **Distribution:** Indacaterol has an extensive volume of distribution (2,361 to 2,557 L); mometasone is highly protein-bound (98-99%).
    - **Metabolism:** Indacaterol is primarily metabolised via UGT1A1 and CYP3A4. Mometasone is extensively metabolised by CYP3A4.
    - **Elimination:** Indacaterol is mainly excreted in faeces (unchanged); half-life is 40–52 hours. Mometasone is mainly excreted in faeces; terminal half-life is ~4.5 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **PALLADIUM Study:** A 52-week pivotal study in 2,216 patients. Both medium (125/127.5 mcg) and high (125/260 mcg) doses demonstrated statistically significant improvements in trough FEV1 at week 26 compared to mometasone furoate monotherapy. Improvements in the Asthma Control Questionnaire (ACQ-7) and reductions in exacerbation rates were also observed.
- **QUARTZ Study:** A 12-week study in 802 patients. The low dose (125/62.5 mcg) demonstrated superior trough FEV1 (improvement of 182 mL) and ACQ-7 scores compared to low-dose mometasone furoate monotherapy.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Protect from moisture and light. 
- Capsules must remain in the blister pack until immediately before use.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Medicine) [Australian].
