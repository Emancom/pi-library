# Editorial Product Information: Ivacaftor

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of ivacaftor product information, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and serves as a clinical reference. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents for definitive prescribing requirements.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ivacaftor (a selective potentiator of the Cystic Fibrosis Transmembrane Conductance Regulator [CFTR] protein).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Kalydeco** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
  - **Film-coated tablets:** 150 mg [Australian].
  - **Granules (in unit dose sachets):** 13.4 mg, 25 mg, 50 mg, and 75 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Vertex Pharmaceuticals (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Cystic Fibrosis (CF):** Treatment of CF in patients aged 1 month and older who possess at least one mutation in the *CFTR* gene shown to be responsive to ivacaftor potentiation based on clinical or *in vitro* assay data [Australian].
- **Note:** In patients with the *R117H* mutation, poly-T variant analysis is required. Asymptomatic patients with *R117H-7T* should have a confirmed diagnosis via sweat test before initiation [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
Ivacaftor must be administered with fat-containing food (e.g., meals prepared with butter/oils, eggs, nuts, whole milk, or meats). Grapefruit-containing products should be avoided [Australian].

### Dosing by Weight and Age
- **1 month to <2 months ($\ge$3 kg):** 13.4 mg granules every 24 hours.
- **2 months to <4 months ($\ge$3 kg):** 13.4 mg granules every 12 hours.
- **4 months to <6 months ($\ge$5 kg):** 25 mg granules every 12 hours.
- **6 months and older:**
  - **5 kg to <7 kg:** 25 mg granules every 12 hours.
  - **7 kg to <14 kg:** 50 mg granules every 12 hours.
  - **14 kg to <25 kg:** 75 mg granules every 12 hours.
  - **$\ge$25 kg:** 150 mg tablet every 12 hours.

### Administration Instructions
- **Tablets:** Swallow whole; do not chew, break, or dissolve [Australian].
- **Granules:** Mix the entire sachet with 5 mL of age-appropriate soft food or liquid (at or below room temperature). Consume completely within one hour of mixing [Australian].

### Missed Dose
Not specified in available sources (Consult local PI).

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to the active substance or any excipients [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Transaminase Elevations:** Liver function tests (ALT/AST) must be performed prior to initiation, every 3 months during the first year of treatment, and annually thereafter. More frequent monitoring is required for patients with a history of elevations. Interrupt treatment if transaminases exceed 5x the Upper Limit of Normal (ULN) [Australian].
- **Cataracts:** Non-congenital lens opacities have been reported in paediatric patients. Baseline and follow-up ophthalmological examinations are recommended [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Contains lactose; use with caution in patients with hereditary galactose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Adjust dose according to severity (see Section 9). Use is not recommended in infants <4 months with any hepatic impairment [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are based on pooled Phase 3 clinical trial data.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nasopharyngitis, upper respiratory tract infection, headache, nasal congestion, oropharyngeal pain, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, rash |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Rhinitis, dizziness, ear discomfort, ear pain, tinnitus, tympanic membrane hyperaemia, sinus congestion, pharyngeal erythema, bacteria in sputum |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Ear congestion, vestibular disorder, breast inflammation, breast mass, gynaecomastia, nipple disorder/pain |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Abdominal pain and transaminase elevations [Australian].
- **Post-Marketing:** Anaphylaxis has been reported [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **CYP3A4/5 Inducers:** Co-administration with strong inducers (e.g., rifampicin, St. John’s Wort, carbamazepine) is not recommended, as it significantly reduces ivacaftor exposure and efficacy [Australian].
- **CYP3A4/5 Inhibitors:** 
  - **Strong Inhibitors (e.g., ketoconazole, clarithromycin):** Reduce ivacaftor dose to twice weekly (approximately 3–4 days apart) for patients $\ge$6 months.
  - **Moderate Inhibitors (e.g., fluconazole, erythromycin):** Reduce ivacaftor dose to once daily for patients $\ge$6 months.
  - For infants 4–6 months, use inhibitors only if benefits outweigh risks, with reduced dosing frequency [Australian].
- **Ivacaftor as Inhibitor:** Ivacaftor may increase exposure to CYP3A and P-gp substrates. Monitor substrates with narrow therapeutic indices (e.g., digoxin, cyclosporine, tacrolimus) [Australian].
- **Warfarin:** Monitor INR as ivacaftor may inhibit CYP2C9 [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category B3]. Studies in animals have shown increased fetal damage at high doses (associated with maternal toxicity), but significance in humans is uncertain. Use only if potential benefits justify the risks [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Ivacaftor and its metabolites are excreted in the milk of lactating rats. Use in humans only if the benefit outweighs the potential risk [Australian].
- **Fertility:** Animal data showed impaired fertility/reproductive performance in rats at high doses (10x human exposure). No effects seen at 6x human exposure [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** 
  - Approved for infants $\ge$1 month. 
  - Efficacy not established in children <1 month or in those <18 years with the *R117H* mutation [Australian].
- **Elderly Use ($\ge$65 years):** Efficacy and safety have not been evaluated [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** 
  - **Mild-to-moderate:** No dose adjustment required.
  - **Severe ($\le$30 mL/min) or End-stage:** Use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:**
  - **Mild (Child-Pugh A):** No dose adjustment required for patients $\ge$6 months.
  - **Moderate (Child-Pugh B):** Reduce dose (e.g., 150 mg once daily for adults).
  - **Severe (Child-Pugh C):** Not recommended unless benefits outweigh risks; if used, start with a reduced dose and frequency [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A CFTR potentiator that increases the "open-probability" of the CFTR channel gate, enhancing chloride transport at the cell surface in specific mutant forms [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
  - **Absorption:** $T_{max}$ ~4 hours; exposure increases 2.5- to 4-fold with fat [Australian].
  - **Distribution:** ~99% plasma protein bound.
  - **Metabolism:** Extensively metabolised by CYP3A to M1 (active, 1/6th potency) and M6 (inactive) [Australian].
  - **Elimination:** Primarily faecal (88%). Half-life is ~12 hours [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **STRIVE and ENVISION:** Pivotal Phase 3 trials in patients with the *G551D* mutation. Ivacaftor demonstrated rapid (Day 15) and sustained (48 weeks) improvements in FEV1, reduced pulmonary exacerbations, and increased BMI compared to placebo [Australian].
- **KONNECTION:** Crossover study in patients with non-*G551D* gating mutations; showed significant FEV1 improvement (+10.7 percentage points) [Australian].
- **KONDUCT:** Trial in patients with the *R117H* mutation; showed significant FEV1 improvement in adults but not in patients aged 6–11 years [Australian].
- **ARRIVAL:** Open-label study in infants; confirmed safety and reduction in sweat chloride [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 30°C [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Not specified; refer to packaging for expiry date [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
