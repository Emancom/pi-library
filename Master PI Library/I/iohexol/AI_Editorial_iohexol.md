**Therapeutic Indications**

**Editorial Version**

Iohexol is a diagnostic radiographic contrast agent indicated for use in adults and paediatric patients for the following procedures:

**Intravascular Use**
*   **Adults:** Angiography (including cerebral, peripheral, visceral, and selective coronary arteriography), aortography, cardiac ventriculography, excretory urography, and contrast enhancement in Computed Tomography (CT) of the head and body.
*   **Paediatrics:** Angiography (including cardiac ventriculography and pulmonary angiography), excretory urography, and CT enhancement of the head and body.
*   **Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA):** Head, neck, abdominal, renal, and peripheral vessels via intra-arterial (IA) or intravenous (IV) routes.

**Intrathecal Use**
*   Myelography (lumbar, thoracic, cervical, total columnar) and CT myelography.
*   CT cisternography. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Oral and Body Cavity Use**
*   **Adults:** Arthrography, endoscopic retrograde pancreatography (ERP), endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP), herniography, and hysterosalpingography.
*   **Gastrointestinal Studies:** Radiographic examination of the GI tract (adults, children, and premature infants).
*   **Paediatrics:** Voiding cystourethrography (VCU).
*   **CT Enhancement:** Abdominal/pelvic CT in conjunction with IV administration. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Dosage and Administration**

**Editorial Version**

### General Principles
Dosage must be individualised based on the specific procedure, patient age, body weight, renal function, and the equipment/technique employed. Use the lowest dose necessary to achieve adequate visualisation; maximum volumes are rarely required. 

Prior to administration:
*   Ensure the patient is adequately hydrated.
*   Inspect the solution for particulate matter and discolouration.
*   Warming the contrast to body temperature (37°C) reduces viscosity and improves patient tolerance.
*   Maintain aseptic technique; discard any unused portion as the product contains no preservatives.

### Recommended Adult Intravascular Dosing
| Indication | Concentration (mg I/mL) | Typical Volume/Dose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Excretory Urography** | 300 or 350 | 0.6–1.2 mL/kg (Max 102 mL in US; 40–80 mL usual in AU) |
| **Cerebral Angiography** | 300 | 5–10 mL per injection |
| **Cardiac Ventriculography** | 350 | 40 mL (Range 30–60 mL); Max 250 mL |
| **Coronary Arteriography** | 350 | 1.5–8 mL per injection |
| **Aortography/Visceral** | 300 or 350 | 30–80 mL total (AU); US specifies 50-80 mL for aorta |
| **Peripheral Arteriography**| 300 or 350 | 20–90 mL total |
| **CT (Head)** | 240, 300, 350 | 70–150 mL (300 mg I/mL) or 120-250 mL infusion (240 mg I/mL) |
| **CT (Body)** | 300 or 350 | 50–200 mL |
| **Phlebography** | 240 or 300 | 20–150 mL per leg |

### Recommended Adult Intrathecal Dosing
Administer slowly over 1–2 minutes.
*   **Lumbar Myelography:** 180 mg I/mL (10–17 mL) or 240 mg I/mL (7–12.5 mL).
*   **Thoracic Myelography:** 240 mg I/mL (6–12.5 mL) or 300 mg I/mL (6–10 mL).
*   **Cervical Myelography:** 240 mg I/mL (6–12.5 mL) or 300 mg I/mL (4–10 mL).
*   **CT Cisternography:** 180 mg I/mL (10–17 mL) or 240 mg I/mL (7–12.5 mL).

### Body Cavity and Oral Dosing
*   **Arthrography:** Volume depends on joint size (e.g., Knee: 5–15 mL; Shoulder: 3–10 mL).
*   **ERCP:** 240 mg I/mL (10–50 mL).
*   **Hysterosalpingography:** 240 or 300 mg I/mL (15–50 mL).
*   **Oral GI (Adults):** Undiluted 350 mg I/mL (50–100 mL).
*   **CT Abdomen (Oral Dilute):** 500–1000 mL of a 6–12 mg I/mL solution. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Dose Adjustments**

**Editorial Version**

**Renal Impairment**
Use the minimum effective dose. Clearance is significantly delayed in patients with renal dysfunction. Ensure adequate pre- and post-procedural hydration. In patients with an eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m², iodinated contrast should generally be avoided unless essential. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Hepatic Impairment**
Patients with combined renal and hepatic dysfunction exhibit significantly reduced clearance. Exercise extreme caution in severe hepatic disturbance. (Source: Australian Product Information)

**Paediatric Dosing**
*   **Excretory Urography:** 0.5 mL/kg to 3 mL/kg (Max 116 mL).
*   **Intrathecal (2 weeks to 18 years):** Dose by age/weight using 180 mg I/mL (ranges from 2 mL to 15 mL).
*   **Oral GI:** 2–4 mL/kg (based on age; infants < 3 months use 180 mg I/mL). (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Contraindications**

**Editorial Version**

*   **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to iohexol or any excipient. History of a prior serious reaction to iohexol. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **General Medical Conditions:** Thyrotoxicosis, anuria, or decompensated cardiac insufficiency. (Source: Australian Product Information)
*   **Specific Procedures:**
    *   **Intrathecal:** Do not co-administer with corticosteroids. Avoid immediate repeat myelography if technical failure occurs (due to overdose risk).
    *   **Hysterosalpingography:** Contraindicated during pregnancy, suspected pregnancy, menstruation (or if imminent), presence of genital tract infection, or within 30 days of conization/curettage. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
    *   **US Specific:** Reproductive tract neoplasia (known or suspected) due to risk of peritoneal spread. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
    *   **Australian Specific:** Carotid angiography during the progressive period of stroke; coronary arteriography within 4 weeks of myocardial infarction; infection or open injury in the exam area. (Source: Australian Product Information)

**Warnings and Precautions**

**Editorial Version**

### WARNING: Intrathecal Administration Risks
**Severe complications, including death, convulsions, cerebral haemorrhage, coma, paralysis, arachnoiditis, acute renal failure, cardiac arrest, rhabdomyolysis, hyperthermia, and brain oedema, can occur if the incorrect concentration or presentation is used. Ensure only recommended concentrations (140 mg I/mL and 350 mg I/mL are NOT for intrathecal use in the US) are administered.** (Source: United States Prescribing Information; Australian Product Information)

### Critical Precautions
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Life-threatening anaphylactic or anaphylactoid reactions may occur regardless of dose or route. Risk is higher in patients with asthma or history of allergy. Premedication with corticosteroids or H1/H2 antagonists may be considered but does not prevent serious reactions. Maintain emergency resuscitation equipment and a patent IV access.
*   **Acute Kidney Injury (AKI):** Contrast-induced nephropathy may occur. Risk factors include pre-existing renal impairment, diabetes, dehydration, congestive heart failure, and advanced age. Minimize dose and ensure adequate hydration. Avoid nephrotoxic drugs, diuretics, or major surgery until the contrast has cleared.
*   **Cardiovascular Effects:** Hemodynamic disturbances, serious arrhythmias, and cardiac arrest have occurred. Exercise caution in patients with severe cardiac disease or pulmonary hypertension.
*   **Thyroid Dysfunction:**
    *   **Adults:** Risk of thyroid storm in hyperthyroid patients.
    *   **Paediatrics (0–3 years):** Hypothyroidism or transient thyroid suppression may occur. This can be harmful to development. Monitor thyroid function following exposure, especially in neonates.
*   **Seizure Risk:** Patients with a history of epilepsy, acute cerebral pathology, or alcohol/drug withdrawal are at increased risk. Discontinue medications that lower the seizure threshold (e.g., phenothiazines, MAOIs, tricyclics) 48 hours before and 24 hours after intrathecal administration.
*   **Metformin-Associated Lactic Acidosis:** Contrast-induced renal impairment can lead to metformin accumulation. (See Drug Interactions).
*   **Phaeochromocytoma:** May trigger a hypertensive crisis; alpha-blockers should be administered prophylactically during interventional procedures. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Pregnancy**

**Editorial Version**

**Pregnancy Category: B (equivalent)**
Iohexol crosses the placenta and is visualized in the fetal digestive tract. While animal studies (rats/rabbits) showed no evidence of teratogenicity or impaired fertility at high doses, adequate and well-controlled studies in pregnant women are lacking. 

Use in pregnancy only if the potential benefit outweighs the risk and is deemed essential by a physician. Radiation exposure should generally be avoided during pregnancy. Newborns exposed *in utero* should be tested for hypothyroidism. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Adverse Effects**

**Editorial Version**

Adverse reactions to iohexol range from mild physiological responses to fatal anaphylaxis. Most reactions occur within 30 minutes, though delayed reactions (hours to days) are possible.

### Common/Very Frequent Reactions
*   **Intravascular:** Feeling of heat, nausea, transient altered taste.
*   **Intrathecal:** Headache (may be severe/lasting), nausea, vomiting, dizziness, back/neck pain.
*   **Oral:** Diarrhoea (42% adults, 36% peds), nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain.
*   **Arthrography:** Local pain, swelling, heat.

### Systemic Reactions (MedDRA Frequency)
*   **Immune:** Anaphylactic/anaphylactoid reactions (Rare to Very Rare).
*   **Nervous System:** Syncope, convulsions, transient contrast-induced encephalopathy (amnesia, hallucinations, paralysis), meningism (Intrathecal).
*   **Cardiac:** Arrhythmias, bradycardia, myocardial infarction, cardiac arrest (Very Rare).
*   **Vascular:** Hypertension, hypotension, flushing, thromboembolism.
*   **Respiratory:** Dyspnoea, cough, bronchospasm, non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema.
*   **Renal:** Acute kidney injury, transient creatinine increase.
*   **Skin:** Rash, pruritus, urticaria, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome/Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (Very Rare).
*   **Endocrine:** Hypothyroidism (Uncommon), Hyperthyroidism. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Drug Interactions**

**Editorial Version**

*   **Metformin:** Risk of lactic acidosis if contrast-induced renal failure occurs. 
    *   *eGFR > 60 mL/min:* Metformin may continue normally.
    *   *eGFR 30–59 mL/min:* Stop metformin 48 hours prior to or at the time of the procedure. Re-evaluate eGFR 48 hours post-procedure; restart only if renal function is stable. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Interleukin-2:** Patients treated with IL-2 within the previous 2 weeks have an increased risk of delayed skin reactions or flu-like symptoms. (Source: Australian Product Information)
*   **Beta-Blockers:** May lower the seizure threshold and increase the risk/severity of anaphylaxis. Patients may be less responsive to adrenaline (epinephrine); glucagon may be required. (Source: Australian Product Information)
*   **Radioactive Iodine:** Iohexol interferes with the uptake of I-131 and I-123 for up to 6 weeks. (Source: United States Prescribing Information)
*   **Phenothiazines/Neuroleptics:** May lower seizure threshold in intrathecal use. (Source: Australian Product Information)

**Overdose**

**Editorial Version**

Symptomatic overdosing is unlikely in intravascular use unless the patient receives > 2000 mg I/kg. In children, accidental overdose may occur during complex angiographic procedures.

**Clinical Manifestations:** Cyanosis, bradycardia, pulmonary haemorrhage, acidosis, convulsions, coma, and cardiac arrest.

**Management:**
*   Correct water and electrolyte imbalances.
*   Monitor renal function for at least 3 days.
*   Haemodialysis may be used to clear excessive contrast.
*   No specific antidote exists; support vital functions.
*   **Intrathecal:** Max recommended dose is 3 g of iodine. Overdose may lead to severe CNS reactions. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Mechanism of Action**

**Editorial Version**

Iohexol is an organic iodine compound. The iodine atoms attenuate (block) X-rays as they pass through the body. This provides a direct opacification of blood vessels, body cavities, and the gastrointestinal tract, allowing for radiographic visualisation of structures that do not contain iodine. The contrast effect is time-dependent and proportional to the iodine concentration. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)

**Pharmacokinetics**

**Editorial Version**

*   **Distribution:** Iohexol is rapidly distributed into the extracellular fluid. It does not cross the intact blood-brain barrier significantly (except for intrathecal use). Protein binding is negligible (< 2%).
*   **Metabolism:** No significant metabolism, deiodination, or biotransformation occurs.
*   **Excretion:** Excreted unchanged via glomerular filtration. In patients with normal renal function, ~90% of an IV dose is excreted within 24 hours. Peak urine concentration occurs within 1 hour.
*   **Elimination Half-life:** Approximately 2 hours (normal renal function).
*   **Intrathecal Absorption:** Absorbed from the CSF into the bloodstream and eliminated renally. Mean peak serum concentration occurs at ~2.2 hours. (Source: Australian Product Information; United States Prescribing Information)
