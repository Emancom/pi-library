# Originality Audit Report: iohexol

- **Originality Score:** 88/100
- **Verbatim Copied Passages Found:** 3
- **Overall Assessment:** Pass

    - **Location:** Warnings and Precautions section (Boxed Warning box).

    - **Note:** Standard chemical nomenclature; usually exempt from plagiarism in medical writing, but a verbatim match.

## Originality Strengths
- **Indication Synthesis:** The editorial does an excellent job of merging disparate lists of indications (e.g., combining the Australian inclusion of "premature babies" for GI studies with the US inclusion of "CT cisternography" for specific pediatric age groups).
- **Dosing Tables:** The "Recommended Adult Intravascular Dosing" table is a successful original synthesis. It extracts data from multiple tables in both sources and presents them in a unified, comparative format that does not exist in either source.
- **Harmonized Adverse Effects:** The MedDRA System Organ Class breakdown effectively consolidates frequency data from the Australian PI with specific pediatric trial rates from the US PI.

## Differences from Source PIs
- **Harmonized Dosing:** The editorial explicitly notes the differences in volume between the US and AU urography recommendations (0.6-1.2 mL/kg vs 40-80 mL).
- **Merged Precautions:** The editorial combines the "Sickle Cell Crisis" warning (US unique) with the "Contrast Encephalopathy" and "Beta-Blocker atypical anaphylaxis" warnings (AU unique) into a single high-yield list.
- **Metric Consolidation:** The editorial standardizes units and formatting, such as identifying the 140 mg I/mL concentration as the specific isotonic version noted in the Australian PI.
- **Structural Reorganization:** The editorial moves "Patient Counselling Information" to the end and extracts "Regional Sources" notes for each section to provide transparency, which is an entirely original structural choice.

## Recommendation
**PASS**

The editorial is a high-quality original synthesis. The verbatim passages identified are limited to highly technical data (chemical names) or critical regulatory safety warnings (the FDA Boxed Warning list), where modification might actually compromise medical accuracy or regulatory intent. The vast majority of the text—particularly the complex dosing and indications sections—represents an original reorganization and rewording of the clinical data. No revision is required.
