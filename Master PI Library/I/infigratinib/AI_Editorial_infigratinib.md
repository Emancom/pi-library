# Editorial Product Information: Infigratinib

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** TRUSELTIQ™  
**Active Ingredient:** Infigratinib (as phosphate)  
**Pharmaceutical Form:** Hard gelatin capsules (25 mg and 100 mg).  
**Excipients:** Contains lactose monohydrate.

## 2. Approved Indications
Infigratinib is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with previously treated, unresectable locally advanced or metastatic cholangiocarcinoma harboring a fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 (FGFR2) fusion or other rearrangement.

**Clinical Requirement:** Patient selection must be confirmed by a validated diagnostic test [TGA/FDA]. Continued approval for this indication may be contingent upon verification of clinical benefit in confirmatory trials (Provisional/Accelerated Approval status).

## 3. Contraindications
Infigratinib is contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity to the active substance or any of the excipients listed in the formulation [TGA]. No other specific contraindications are listed by the FDA.

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Ocular Disorders
Infigratinib can cause Central Serous Retinopathy (CSR) or Retinal Pigment Epithelial Detachment (RPED), which may lead to visual disturbances such as blurred vision. 
*   **Monitoring:** A comprehensive ophthalmic examination, including optical coherence tomography (OCT), is required at baseline, at 1 month, at 3 months, and every 3 months thereafter.
*   **Urgent Evaluation:** Patients reporting new visual symptoms should be evaluated urgently and followed every 3 weeks until symptoms resolve or treatment is discontinued.
*   **Dry Eye:** This occurred in 24% of patients [TGA]. Use of ocular demulcents is recommended as needed.

### Hyperphosphataemia and Soft Tissue Mineralisation
Increased serum phosphate levels are a primary pharmacodynamic effect of FGFR inhibition. 
*   **Incidence:** Reported in approximately 82% of patients, with a median onset of 8 days [TGA].
*   **Risks:** Prolonged hyperphosphataemia can lead to soft tissue mineralisation, cutaneous calcinosis, vascular calcification, and myocardial calcification.
*   **Management:** Monitor phosphate levels throughout treatment. Initiate a low-phosphate diet and phosphate-lowering therapy when serum phosphate exceeds 5.5 mg/dL.

### Embryo-Fetal Toxicity
Based on its mechanism of action and animal studies, infigratinib can cause significant fetal harm, including malformations and fetal death. Pregnancy must be excluded before treatment initiation.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Recommended Schedule
The standard dose is **125 mg orally once daily for 21 consecutive days, followed by 7 days off**, in 28-day cycles. Treatment should continue until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

### Administration Instructions
*   Take on an empty stomach, at least 1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal.
*   Administer at approximately the same time each day (preferably in the morning).
*   Capsules must be swallowed whole; do not crush, chew, or dissolve.
*   **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed by ≥4 hours, skip the dose and resume the regular schedule the following day.

### Dose Modifications for Special Populations
*   **Renal Impairment:** For patients with mild to moderate renal impairment (CLcr 30 to 89 mL/min), the recommended dose is reduced to **100 mg** once daily (21/7 schedule) [TGA/FDA].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:**
    *   Mild (Total Bilirubin >ULN to 1.5x ULN or AST >ULN): **100 mg** once daily.
    *   Moderate (Total Bilirubin >1.5 to 3x ULN): **75 mg** once daily.

### Dose Reductions for Toxicity
1.  **First reduction:** 100 mg
2.  **Second reduction:** 75 mg
3.  **Third reduction:** 50 mg (Discontinue if further reduction is required).

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most frequently reported adverse reactions (≥15% of patients) include:
*   **Metabolic:** Hyperphosphataemia, hypercalcaemia, hypophosphataemia, decreased appetite.
*   **Dermatologic:** Nail toxicity (onycholysis, paronychia), alopecia, palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia (PPE), dry skin.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Stomatitis, constipation, dry mouth, diarrhea, nausea, abdominal pain.
*   **General:** Fatigue, edema, pyrexia, dysgeusia.
*   **Serious Reactions:** Sepsis, anemia, and increased blood creatinine. Fatal sepsis occurred in 0.9% of trial participants [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
### CYP3A4 Inhibitors and Inducers
*   **Inhibitors:** Avoid concomitant use with strong or moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g., itraconazole), as these significantly increase infigratinib exposure and toxicity risk.
*   **Inducers:** Avoid concomitant use with strong or moderate CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampin), as these may decrease efficacy.

### Gastric Acid Reducing Agents
Infigratinib solubility is pH-dependent. 
*   **PPIs:** Avoid coadministration.
*   **H2-Antagonists:** Stagger administration; take infigratinib 2 hours before or 10 hours after the H2-antagonist.
*   **Antacids:** Stagger administration; take infigratinib 2 hours before or 2 hours after antacids.

### Food Interactions
Avoid grapefruit and grapefruit products during treatment, as they may increase plasma concentrations.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** Classified as Category D [TGA]. Advise patients of potential fetal risk.
*   **Contraception:** 
    *   **Females:** Use effective contraception during treatment and for 1 month after the final dose.
    *   **Males:** Use effective contraception when partnered with females of reproductive potential during treatment and for 1 month after the final dose.
*   **Breastfeeding:** Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in the infant, breastfeeding is not recommended during treatment and for 1 month following the last dose.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
*   **Absorption:** Median Tmax is 6 hours. A high-fat, high-calorie meal increases AUC by 80-120%.
*   **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound (96.8%), primarily to lipoproteins. The apparent volume of distribution is approximately 1600 L.
*   **Metabolism:** Predominantly metabolized via CYP3A4 (approximately 94% of total clearance).
*   **Excretion:** Primarily eliminated in feces (77%, with 3.4% unchanged) and urine (7.2%). The terminal half-life is approximately 33.5 hours.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
Infigratinib is a small-molecule kinase inhibitor that selectively targets Fibroblast Growth Factor Receptors (FGFR) 1, 2, and 3. By inhibiting these receptors, infigratinib disrupts downstream signaling pathways that lead to cell proliferation and survival in tumors with constitutive FGFR activation. It shows minimal activity against FGFR4 or other unrelated kinases at clinical concentrations.

## 11. Storage Conditions
Store TRUSELTIQ™ capsules below 25°C. Keep the medication in its original blister pack to protect from light and moisture. Dispose of any unused medicine according to local clinical guidelines.
