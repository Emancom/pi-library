# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
*   **Contributing Jurisdictions:** This editorial is based on the Product Information (PI) from the United States (FDA). 
*   **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
*   **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction. At the time of compilation, only the US (FDA) source document was available for synthesis.

## 1. Product Identification
*   **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (also known as L-carnitine).
*   **Chemical Class:** A naturally occurring amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
*   **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter).
*   **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
*   **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    *   **United States:** Single-dose vials containing 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
*   **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) use only.
*   **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** Treatment of primary or secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism (acute and chronic management) [US].
*   **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
*   **Metabolic Disorders:** The standard recommended dose is 50 mg/kg. This may be administered as a slow bolus (over 2 to 3 minutes) or via infusion. 
    *   **Severe Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent total dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. 
    *   **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and no less frequently than every 6 hours.
    *   **Maximum Dose:** The highest reported clinical dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
*   **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    *   **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
    *   **Dose Adjustments:** Downward titration (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Administration Instructions
*   Visual inspection for particulate matter or discoloration is required before use.
*   Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation lacks preservatives.

### Monitoring
*   **Plasma Concentrations:** For metabolic disorders, it is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels and monitor weekly/monthly. Target free carnitine levels should be between 35 and 60 µmol/L [US].
*   **Dialysis Patients:** Initiation is typically prompted by trough plasma levels falling below the normal range (40 to 50 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
*   None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Warnings
*   **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, and laryngeal edema, have been reported, primarily in patients with ESRD on dialysis. These can occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, the drug must be discontinued immediately and appropriate medical management initiated [US].

### General Precautions
*   **Seizures:** Levocarnitine has been associated with seizure activity in patients both with and without a prior history of seizures. In patients with pre-existing seizure disorders, an increase in the frequency or severity of seizures has been observed [US].
*   **TMA Accumulation (Oral Formulation Context):** While this document focuses on the injection, it is noted that chronic high-dose administration of **oral** levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are usually excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most commonly reported adverse effects (≥5%) in clinical trials of dialysis patients included:
*   **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), headache (22%), chest pain (12%), and asthenia (9%).
*   **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
*   **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%).
*   **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).

| Frequency | Adverse Event |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Injection site reaction, pain, headache, hypertension, pharyngitis, diarrhea, vomiting, hypotension, chest pain |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Abdominal pain, asthenia, fever, nausea, infection, dizziness, tachycardia, hypercalcemia |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Not specified in available sources |

*   **Specific Effects:** Transient nausea, vomiting, and "fishy" body odor have been observed.

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. 
*   **Management:** Clinicians should monitor INR levels regularly after initiating levocarnitine or following any dose adjustments in patients receiving anticoagulant therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
*   **Pregnancy:** 
    *   **US Classification:** Category not explicitly lettered in current PI, but animal studies in rats and rabbits using up to 3.8 times the human dose revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. There are no adequate/well-controlled human studies. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
*   **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. However, the effects of supplemental carnitine in nursing mothers have not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, exogenous carnitine increases milk concentrations. The risks to the infant from excess carnitine should be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother [US].
*   **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
*   **Paediatric Use:** Dosing recommendations for metabolic disorders apply to paediatric patients. Safety and efficacy in children for ESRD-related deficiency are consistent with adult profiles [US].
*   **Elderly Use:** Specific geriatric trials were not detailed in the source material.
*   **Renal Impairment:** 
    *   **Injection:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis.
    *   **Oral:** Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency [US].
*   **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance essential for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrates for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also assists in clearing accumulated acyl-CoA esters, which can be toxic.

### Pharmacokinetics
*   **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin. The distribution half-life following a slow IV bolus (20 mg/kg) is approximately 0.585 hours [US].
*   **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
*   **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
*   **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times per week after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine levels from a baseline of ~20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) up to trough levels of 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks [US].
*   **Dose-Ranging Data:** Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
*   **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
*   **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations between 0.5 mg/mL and 8.0 mg/mL [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
*   **United States:** Prescription only (Rx Only).
