# Editorial Product Information: Ioflupane I 123

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of the Product Information (PI) for **ioflupane I 123** (DaTSCAN). It is intended as a clinical reference for healthcare professionals to provide a coherent overview of the drug’s regulatory and clinical profile.
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial synthesis is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI. Clinicians must consult the approved labelling in their specific region before prescribing or administering this agent. Information regarding other jurisdictions (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) was not available in the provided source and is therefore omitted or noted as not specified.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Ioflupane I 123 (N-ω-fluoropropyl-2β-carbomethoxy-3β-(4-[¹²³I]iodophenyl)nortropane).
- **Chemical Class:** Radiopharmaceutical; cocaine analogue.
- **Brand Names:** DaTSCAN [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Injection:** 185 MBq (5 mCi) in 2.5 mL at a concentration of 74 MBq/mL (2 mCi/mL) at calibration time [United States].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Medi-Physics Inc. dba GE Healthcare [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Ioflupane I 123** is a radioactive diagnostic agent indicated as an adjunct to other diagnostic evaluations for the visualization of striatal dopamine transporters (DaT) using single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) brain imaging in:
- Adult patients with suspected Parkinsonian syndromes (PS) [United States].
- Adult patients with suspected dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB) [United States].

*Note: The agent is used to assist in the evaluation of dopaminergic neurodegeneration but is not designed to distinguish between specific Parkinsonian syndromes (e.g., Parkinson’s disease vs. Multiple System Atrophy) [United States].*

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing:** 111 MBq to 185 MBq (3 mCi to 5 mCi) [United States].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Administer via slow intravenous injection over at least 20 seconds [United States].
    - **Thyroid Blockade:** Administer a thyroid-blocking agent (e.g., Potassium Iodide Oral Solution or Lugol’s Solution equivalent to 100 mg iodide) at least one hour before the dose of ioflupane I 123 to minimize the risk of thyroid uptake of Iodine-123 [United States].
    - **Hydration:** Encourage the patient to hydrate and void frequently both before and for the 48 hours following administration to minimize radiation exposure [United States].
- **Imaging Schedule:** Begin SPECT imaging between 3 and 6 hours post-injection [United States].
- **Radiation Safety:** Use waterproof gloves and effective radiation shielding (including lead-glass syringe shields) during handling and administration [United States].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known serious hypersensitivity to **ioflupane I 123** [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Dyspnea, edema, rash, erythema, and pruritus have been reported. Advanced life-support treatment measures should be available prior to administration [United States].
- **Thyroid Accumulation:** Up to 6% of the dose may be free iodide (I-123). Failure to block the thyroid may increase the long-term risk of thyroid neoplasia [United States].
- **Radiation Risk:** This agent contributes to the patient's cumulative radiation exposure, which is associated with an increased risk of cancer. Safe handling is required to protect healthcare providers [United States].
- **Drug Interpretation Interference:** Various drugs may bind to the dopamine transporter and interfere with imaging results (see Section 7) [United States].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Frequency $\le$1%:** 
    - Headache
    - Nausea
    - Vertigo
    - Dry mouth
    - Dizziness
- **Postmarketing Experience (Frequency not established):**
    - **Immune System:** Hypersensitivity reactions (e.g., dyspnea, edema, rash).
    - **General:** Injection site pain.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Drugs with high affinity for the dopamine transporter may interfere with image interpretation.
- **Clinically Significant Interactions:**
    - **Stimulants and anorectics:** Amphetamine, methamphetamine, methylphenidate, phentermine, modafinil, armodafinil, and cocaine.
    - **Antidepressants:** Bupropion, amoxapine, sertraline.
    - **SSRIs/SNRIs:** Citalopram, paroxetine, venlafaxine, and desvenlafaxine have been associated with altered uptake.
    - **Others:** Benztropine, buspirone, mazindol, selegiline.
- **Management:** Consider discontinuing potentially interfering drugs for at least 5 biological half-lives prior to **ioflupane I 123** administration, provided the clinical condition allows for safe suspension [United States].
- **Dopamine Agonists/Antagonists:** The impact of these agents on imaging has not been established [United States].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** May cause fetal harm. Radioactive iodine crosses the placenta and can permanently impair fetal thyroid function. Assess pregnancy status before administration in females of reproductive potential [United States].
    - **Category:** Not assigned a letter category in the current US labelling, but noted as having no available data in pregnant women to evaluate for drug-associated risks [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** Iodine-123 is present in human milk. To minimize radiation exposure to the infant, breastfeeding should be interrupted, and breast milk should be pumped and discarded for at least 6 days (>10 physical half-lives) after administration [United States].
- **Fertility:** Studies to assess the impairment of fertility have not been performed [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy have not been established in paediatric patients [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** In clinical studies, no overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between subjects aged 65+ and younger subjects [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** **Ioflupane I 123** is excreted by the kidneys. Severe renal impairment may lead to increased radiation exposure and potentially altered images [United States].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Ioflupane binds reversibly to the human presynaptic dopamine transporter (DaT). In patients with Parkinsonian syndromes or DLB, the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the striata results in reduced DaT binding, which is visualized via SPECT [United States].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Given the very small mass of ioflupane administered, no pharmacologic effects are expected [United States].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Only 5% of radioactivity remains in the blood at 5 minutes post-injection. Brain uptake reaches ~7% at 10 minutes and decreases to ~3% at 5 hours [United States].
    - **Metabolism:** Not specified in available sources.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 60% of injected radioactivity is excreted in the urine within 48 hours, with fecal excretion estimated at 14%. The physical half-life of I-123 is 13.2 hours [United States].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Parkinsonian Syndromes (PS):** Evaluated in two multicenter, open-label studies (N=284). Imaging was compared to clinical diagnostic standards. Positive percent agreement (abnormal image in PS patients) ranged from 77% to 97% across readers in different studies [United States].
- **Dementia with Lewy Bodies (DLB):** In a study of 55 patients with autopsy-confirmed diagnoses, the sensitivity for DLB was 80% and specificity was 92% [United States].
- **Limitations:** The effectiveness of ioflupane I 123 as a screening tool, a confirmatory test, or for monitoring disease progression has not been established [United States].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [United States].
- **Handling:** Store within the original lead container or equivalent radiation shielding. Do not use after the expiration date and time stated on the label [United States].
- **Preservatives:** This product does not contain a preservative [United States].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only. Radiopharmaceuticals must be handled only by qualified, licensed healthcare providers [United States].
