# Editorial Product Information: immune globulin intravenous (human)

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **immune globulin intravenous (human)**
- Chemical class: Human normal immunoglobulin preparation (predominantly IgG; 10% IgG reported as 100 mg/mL in available sources)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intravenous only

## 2. Regulatory History
- Initial U.S. approval: 2024 (as stated in available sources) [US]
- Date of last revision/update reported in source PI: Revised 3/2026 [US]
- Withdrawal, suspension, or restriction history: Not specified in available sources
- Other jurisdictions' registration dates or revision history: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Replacement therapy for primary humoral immunodeficiency (PI) in patients 2 years of age and older, including but not limited to:
  - Congenital agammaglobulinemia
  - Common variable immunodeficiency (CVID)
  - X-linked agammaglobulinemia (XLA)
  - Wiskott–Aldrich syndrome
  - Severe combined immunodeficiencies (SCID)
  - Indication as stated: [US]

- Measles exposure guidance (clinical prophylaxis context):
  - An extra dose as soon as possible and within 6 days of measles exposure may be prudent.
  - A dose of 400 mg/kg should provide a serum measles antibody level >240 mIU/mL for at least ~2 weeks.
  - If a patient is at risk of future measles exposure and receives doses <530 mg/kg every 3–4 weeks, increasing the dose to at least 530 mg/kg is advised to provide ~240 mIU/mL for ≈22 days after infusion.
  - (Guidance presented in source PI) [US]

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (primary humoral immunodeficiency)
- Recommended dose range: 300–800 mg/kg (3–8 mL/kg) administered every 3–4 weeks. (Dose may be adjusted to achieve desired trough levels and clinical response.) [US]

### Infusion rate / titration schedule
- First infusion:
  - Start at 0.5 mg/kg/min (0.005 mL/kg/min) for 30 minutes.
  - If tolerated, gradually increase every 30 minutes up to 3.0 mg/kg/min (0.03 mL/kg/min).
- From the second infusion onward:
  - Start at 0.5 mg/kg/min (0.005 mL/kg/min) for 30 minutes.
  - If tolerated, gradually increase up to 13 mg/kg/min (0.13 mL/kg/min).
- For patients at risk of thrombosis, renal dysfunction, or acute renal failure: administer at the minimum dose and infusion rate practicable and ensure adequate hydration prior to infusion. Consider discontinuing if renal function deteriorates. [US]

### Measles exposure (see Section 3)
- See Section 3 for recommended doses for post‑exposure prophylaxis and for maintaining measles antibody levels.

### Preparation and handling
- Inspect visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration whenever container and solution permit.
- Do not use if liquid is cloudy, turbid, discolored, or contains visible particulates.
- Do not shake.
- Product should be at room or body temperature at time of administration.
- Do not mix with other IGIV products or other intravenous medications.
- Do not dilute.
- Single‑use only; promptly use any vial that has been entered and discard partially used vials and unused product per local requirements.
- Do not use after expiration date.

### Administration practicalities
- Monitor vital signs throughout infusion.
- Slow or stop the infusion if adverse reactions occur. If symptoms resolve promptly, infusion may be resumed at a lower rate that is comfortable for the patient.
- Hydrate patients adequately before initiating infusion.
- Ensure patients with pre‑existing renal insufficiency are not volume depleted prior to administration.
- For patients judged at risk for renal dysfunction or thrombotic events, administer at the minimum infusion rate practicable and consider discontinuation if renal function deteriorates.

### Missed dose
- Not specified in available sources

## 5. Contraindications
- History of anaphylactic or severe systemic reaction to human immune globulin. (Absolute contraindication)
- IgA‑deficient patients with known antibodies to IgA and a history of hypersensitivity reactions.  
  - Note: Product contains ≤300 μg/mL IgA per available source; patients with anti‑IgA may be at greater risk for severe hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis.

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed warnings / major safety warnings
- **Thrombosis, renal dysfunction and acute renal failure**:  
  - Thrombosis may occur with administration of IGIV products, including this product. For patients at risk of thrombosis, renal dysfunction or acute renal failure, administer at the minimum dose and infusion rate practicable. Ensure adequate hydration prior to administration and monitor for signs and symptoms of thrombosis. Assess blood viscosity in patients at risk for hyperviscosity. [US]
  - Renal dysfunction, acute renal failure, osmotic nephrosis, and death have been reported with IGIV products; renal dysfunction and acute renal failure have been reported more commonly with sucrose‑containing IGIV products (this product does not contain sucrose per available source). Ensure patients are not volume depleted before administration; consider discontinuing product if renal function deteriorates. [US]

### General warnings and precautions
- Hypersensitivity / anaphylaxis:
  - Severe hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, may occur. Epinephrine should be available for immediate use. Discontinue infusion if hypersensitivity occurs and institute appropriate treatment.
- Hemolysis:
  - Hemolysis (intravascular or due to enhanced RBC sequestration) has been reported. IGIVs may contain blood‑group antibodies that can coat RBCs causing positive direct antiglobulin tests and hemolysis. Risk factors include high doses (e.g., ≥2 g/kg given as single or divided administration) and non‑O blood group; underlying inflammatory states may increase risk. Monitor for signs/symptoms of hemolysis and confirm with laboratory testing. If transfusion is needed in patients with hemolysis, perform cross‑matching to avoid exacerbation.
- Thrombosis:
  - Thrombotic events (venous and arterial) have occurred with IGIV therapy. Risk factors include advanced age, prolonged immobilization, hypercoagulable conditions, prior thrombosis, estrogen use, indwelling central catheters, hyperviscosity, cardiovascular risk factors; thrombosis may occur without known risk factors. Consider baseline blood viscosity assessment in patients at risk (e.g., cryoglobulins, marked hypertriglyceridemia/chylomicronemia, monoclonal gammopathies).
- Renal failure:
  - See boxed warning; monitor renal function and urine output closely in at‑risk patients (e.g., pre‑existing renal insufficiency, diabetes mellitus, hypovolemia, obesity, concomitant nephrotoxic drugs, age >65). Measure BUN and serum creatinine before the initial infusion and periodically thereafter. Consider discontinuing product if renal function deteriorates.
- Hyperproteinemia / increased serum viscosity / hyponatremia:
  - IGIV can cause hyperproteinemia, increased serum viscosity and hyponatremia or pseudohyponatremia. Distinguish true hyponatremia from pseudohyponatremia due to hyperproteinemia; inappropriate treatment of pseudohyponatremia may lead to volume depletion and increased thrombotic risk.
- Aseptic meningitis syndrome (AMS):
  - AMS (severe headache, nuchal rigidity, photophobia, fever, nausea/vomiting, etc.) has been reported, typically within hours to 2 days after IGIV infusion. AMS has been associated with high doses (e.g., 2 g/kg) and/or rapid infusion. Perform neurological evaluation and CSF studies to exclude other causes when AMS is suspected; AMS usually resolves after discontinuation of IGIV.
- Transfusion‑Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI):
  - Noncardiogenic pulmonary edema/TRALI may occur within 1–6 hours of infusion; presents with severe respiratory distress, hypoxemia, pulmonary edema, normal left ventricular function and fever. If suspected, stop IGIV immediately and evaluate for anti‑neutrophil and anti‑HLA antibodies in product and patient serum; manage in ICU with supportive care.
- Transmissible infectious agents:
  - Product is derived from human plasma and may carry a risk of transmitting infectious agents, including viruses and prion agents (e.g., vCJD/CJD), despite donor screening and manufacturing viral inactivation/removal steps. Discuss risks/benefits with patients.
- Laboratory monitoring and interference:
  - Periodic renal function monitoring (BUN, serum creatinine, urine output) is advised for patients at increased risk of renal failure.
  - Consider baseline blood viscosity assessment for patients at risk of hyperviscosity.
  - If hemolysis suspected, perform confirmatory testing (e.g., Coombs’ test, hemoglobin/hematocrit).
  - Passive transfer of antibodies may confound serological testing, including positive direct/indirect Coombs tests and false‑positive serologies.

### Monitoring requirements (summary)
- Assess renal function (BUN, serum creatinine) before initial infusion and periodically thereafter in at‑risk patients.
- Monitor urine output.
- Monitor vital signs during infusion.
- Consider baseline blood viscosity in at‑risk patients (cryoglobulins, marked hypertriglyceridemia, monoclonal gammopathies).
- If TRALI suspected, perform testing for anti‑neutrophil and anti‑HLA antibodies in product and patient serum.
- If hemolysis suspected, perform hemolysis laboratory testing and direct antiglobulin (Coombs’) testing.

## 7. Adverse Effects
- Adverse reactions observed in clinical trials and reported postmarketing are extensive. Frequencies are provided where available from the clinical trial dataset; many postmarketing events do not have reliable incidence estimates and are listed as frequency unknown (postmarketing reports).

Clinical trial summary (PI clinical study in primary humoral immunodeficiency):
- Open‑label, prospective, multicenter, multinational trial in 67 patients (including 12 children and 6 adolescents), doses 200–800 mg/kg every 3–4 weeks for ~12 months; 923 infusions total.
- Overall: ≥1 infusion‑related adverse reaction occurred in 39/67 patients (58%) and 93/923 infusions (10%).
- Adverse reactions occurring in ≥5% of patients:
  - Headache: 13/67 patients (19%) — Very common (>10%)
  - Upper respiratory tract infections: 8/67 patients (12%) — Very common (>10%)
  - Fatigue: 5/67 patients (8%) — Common (1–10%)
  - Nausea: 4/67 patients (6%) — Common (1–10%)
  - Increased blood pressure: 4/67 patients (6%) — Common (1–10%)
- Serious adverse events related to product in trial: anaphylactic reaction; severe neutropenia — both led to discontinuation. One patient had mild hemolysis and positive Coombs’ test.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Do not mix with other intravenous drugs or other IGIV products; administer separately from other medications.
- Live virus vaccines:
  - Passive transfer of antibodies may transiently reduce the efficacy of live attenuated virus vaccines (e.g., measles, mumps, rubella, varicella) because high levels of passively acquired antibody can interfere with active immune responses. Inform immunizing physicians of recent IGIV therapy.
- Serological testing:
  - Passive transfer of antibodies may lead to false‑positive or misleading serological test results (including for erythrocyte antigens A, B, D and other serologies) and may cause positive direct or indirect Coombs’ tests.
- Loop diuretics:
  - Interaction noted; avoid concomitant use (details of interaction management not specified).
- Other intravenous products:
  - Clinical studies have not evaluated mixing this product with other intravenous drugs and solutions; administer separately.
- Any additional pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic interactions beyond the above: Not specified in available sources
