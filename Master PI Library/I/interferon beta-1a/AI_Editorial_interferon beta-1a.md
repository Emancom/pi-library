# Editorial Product Information: Interferon beta-1a (AVONEX)

**Editorial Note:** This unified Product Information (PI) document has been synthesized from official regulatory documents provided by the Australian Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) and the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Where clinical recommendations or regulatory requirements differ between these agencies, the most comprehensive or conservative information has been selected and the source agency is indicated in brackets.

---

## 1. Product Name and Active Ingredient
**Product Name:** AVONEX  
**Active Ingredient:** Interferon beta-1a  

AVONEX is a 166 amino acid glycoprotein produced by recombinant DNA technology using genetically engineered Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells. It is supplied as a sterile solution for intramuscular (IM) injection. Each 0.5 mL dose contains 30 micrograms (µg) of interferon beta-1a, corresponding to approximately 6 million International Units (IU) of antiviral activity.

## 2. Approved Indications
AVONEX is indicated for the treatment of relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis (MS) in adults. This includes:
*   **Relapsing-Remitting MS:** To slow the accumulation of physical disability and decrease the frequency of clinical exacerbations.
*   **Clinically Isolated Syndrome (CIS):** For patients who have experienced a single demyelinating event and have brain MRI abnormalities characteristic of MS, placing them at high risk for developing clinically definite MS.
*   **Active Secondary Progressive MS (SPMS):** For patients where relapse is still a feature of the disease [FDA/TGA]. 

**Note on SPMS:** Treatment with 60 µg once weekly is specifically indicated in Australia for patients with SPMS of moderate severity (EDSS 3.5-6.5) who have experienced a relapse within the previous 12 months [TGA].

## 3. Contraindications
AVONEX is contraindicated in the following patient populations:
*   **Hypersensitivity:** Patients with a history of hypersensitivity to natural or recombinant interferon beta, or any excipients (such as arginine hydrochloride, sodium acetate, or polysorbate 20).
*   **Mental Health:** Patients with current severe depression and/or suicidal ideation [TGA].
*   **Pregnancy:** Treatment initiation is contraindicated during pregnancy, and for women planning to become pregnant while on therapy [TGA].
*   **Albumin Sensitivity:** The formerly available lyophilized vial formulation (which contained albumin) is contraindicated in patients with a history of hypersensitivity to human albumin [FDA].

## 4. Warnings and Precautions
### Depression, Suicide, and Psychotic Disorders
AVONEX should be used with extreme caution in patients with a history of depression or other psychiatric disorders. Depression and suicidal ideation occur with increased frequency in the MS population and have been reported in association with interferon use. Patients should be advised to report any new symptoms of depression, anxiety, or psychosis immediately. Discontinuation of therapy should be considered if severe psychiatric symptoms occur.

### Hepatic Injury
Severe hepatic injury, including rare cases of autoimmune hepatitis and hepatic failure, has been reported. Asymptomatic elevations in transaminases are common. Liver function tests (LFTs) should be performed at baseline and monitored periodically. Treatment should be discontinued if jaundice appears or if transaminase levels increase significantly.

### Seizure Disorders
Caution is required when administering AVONEX to patients with a history of seizures, particularly if their epilepsy is not adequately controlled. If a patient develops new seizures during therapy, AVONEX should be discontinued while an etiological investigation is conducted.

### Cardiac Disease
While AVONEX does not have direct cardiac toxicity, the flu-like symptoms associated with the drug may prove stressful to patients with underlying cardiac conditions such as congestive heart failure, angina, or arrhythmia. These patients should be monitored closely for clinical worsening.

### Thrombotic Microangiopathy (TMA)
Cases of TMA, including fatal cases of thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) and hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS), have been reported. Clinical features include new-onset hypertension, impaired renal function, and thrombocytopenia. Discontinuation of treatment is required if TMA is diagnosed.

### Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH)
PAH has been reported in patients treated with interferon beta products. It may occur several years after starting treatment. Patients developing symptoms such as dyspnea or unexplained fatigue should be assessed for PAH. If confirmed, AVONEX must be discontinued.

### Laboratory Monitoring
In addition to LFTs, clinicians should perform complete and differential white blood cell counts and platelet counts. Patients with myelosuppression may require more intensive monitoring.

## 5. Dosage and Administration
### Standard Dosage
The recommended dosage for relapsing forms of MS is **30 µg (6 million IU) injected intramuscularly once a week**. 

### Titration Schedule
To reduce the incidence and severity of flu-like symptoms, therapy should be titrated during initiation. Both agencies recommend a 4-week titration schedule:
*   **Week 1:** 7.5 µg (1/4 dose)
*   **Week 2:** 15 µg (1/2 dose)
*   **Week 3:** 22.5 µg (3/4 dose)
*   **Week 4+:** 30 µg (Full dose)

Titration kits (e.g., AVOSTARTGRIP or AVOSTARTCLIP) are available for use with pre-filled syringes.

### Administration Instructions
*   **Route:** Intramuscular (IM) injection only.
*   **Sites:** The thigh or upper arm are recommended. The buttock is not considered a suitable site [TGA]. Injection sites should be rotated weekly.
*   **Supervision:** The first injection should be performed under the supervision of a qualified healthcare professional.
*   **Premedication:** Use of antipyretics or analgesics on treatment days can help ameliorate flu-like symptoms.

## 6. Adverse Effects
The most common adverse reactions associated with AVONEX are:
*   **Flu-like Syndrome:** Fever, chills, myalgia, headache, and asthenia. These symptoms usually occur within hours of injection and typically diminish in frequency with continued treatment.
*   **Injection Site Reactions:** Pain, inflammation, and bruising. Rare cases of injection site necrosis have been reported.
*   **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea.
*   **Nervous System:** Dizziness, muscle spasticity, and insomnia.
*   **Skin:** Alopecia, rash (including vesicular rash), and increased sweating.
*   **Urogenital:** Urinary tract infections and, uncommonly, metrorrhagia or menorrhagia [TGA].

## 7. Drug Interactions
*   **CYP450 Metabolism:** Interferons may reduce the activity of hepatic cytochrome P-450-dependent enzymes. Caution should be exercised when co-administering drugs with a narrow therapeutic index that are largely dependent on the CYP450 system for clearance (e.g., some antiepileptics and antidepressants).
*   **Hepatotoxic Agents:** Caution is advised when used concomitantly with other drugs associated with hepatic injury.
*   **Myelosuppressive Agents:** Proper monitoring is required when given in combination with agents that suppress bone marrow function.
*   **Corticosteroids:** Clinical trials indicate that AVONEX can be safely co-administered with corticosteroids or ACTH during MS relapses.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
### Pregnancy
**TGA (Australia):** Classified as Category D. AVONEX is contraindicated during pregnancy and in women planning to conceive. 
**FDA (USA):** Epidemiological data do not show a clear relationship with major congenital malformations; however, animal studies have demonstrated abortifacient potential at high doses. 
**Consensus Recommendation:** Women of childbearing potential should use effective contraception. If pregnancy occurs, the patient should be informed of the potential risks to the fetus and discontinuation of therapy is generally recommended unless the clinical benefit clearly outweighs the risk.

### Breastfeeding
Interferon beta-1a is excreted in human milk at very low levels. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants, a decision should be made whether to discontinue breastfeeding or discontinue the drug, taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother.

## 9. Pharmacokinetics
Following IM administration of a 30 µg dose:
*   **Peak Serum Levels:** Generally reached 15 hours post-dose (range 6-36 hours) [FDA].
*   **Elimination Half-life:** Reported as approximately 10 hours [TGA] to 19 hours [FDA].
*   **Biological Markers:** Biological response markers, such as neopterin and $\beta_2$-microglobulin, increase within 12 hours and remain elevated for at least 4 days. Peak biological activity is typically observed 48 hours after dosing.

## 10. Mechanism of Action
The exact mechanism by which AVONEX exerts its effects in MS is unknown. Interferons are cytokines that bind to specific cell surface receptors, initiating a complex cascade of intracellular events. This leads to the expression of interferon-induced gene products and markers which possess immunomodulatory, antiviral, and antiproliferative activities. In MS, these effects are thought to modulate the inflammatory processes that contribute to demyelination.

## 11. Storage Conditions
*   **Refrigeration:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. **Do not freeze.**
*   **Light Protection:** Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
*   **Room Temperature:** If refrigeration is unavailable, AVONEX may be stored at room temperature (up to 25°C/77°F [FDA] or 30°C/86°F [TGA]) for a single period of up to 7 days. Once removed from refrigeration, it must not be stored above these temperatures or returned to the refrigerator.
*   **Disposal:** Any unused portion or expired product should be discarded in an appropriate puncture-resistant container.
