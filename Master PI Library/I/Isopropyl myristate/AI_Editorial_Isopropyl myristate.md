# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The following jurisdictions' Product Information (PI) documents contributed to this editorial: **US (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of levocarnitine prescribing information, designed as a concise reference for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN/USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - **Injection:** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from inborn errors of metabolism [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing
- **Administration:** Administer intravenously as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].

### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg [US].
- **Acute Metabolic Crisis:** A loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours. Administration should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and no less frequently than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maintenance:** Daily doses typically range around 50 mg/kg, though up to 300 mg/kg has been administered in clinical practice [US].
- **Monitoring:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Initial Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration Timing:** Administer into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Serious Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Treatment must be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].

### General Precautions
- **Neurologic Reactions (Seizures):** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Levocarnitine Note):** While this editorial covers the injection, healthcare providers should note that high-dose *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severe renal impairment may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Very Common (>1%)
Adverse effects listed were reported in clinical trials for ESRD patients regardless of causality:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%) [US].
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%) [US].
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].

### Less Common and Postmarketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Gastritis, body odor (transient) [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures (regardless of prior history) [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia, hypervolemia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified; formally Category B equivalent]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine milk concentrations following administration. The risk to the infant of excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefit of maternal supplementation. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal reproductive studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Refer to the "Metabolic Disorders" dosing section; specific paediatric-only trials are not detailed, but dosing is weight-based (mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in available sources [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Safely used in ESRD patients on dialysis (primary indication). Note that the safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated specifically for renal insufficiency [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring carrier molecule required for mammalian energy metabolism. it facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane for beta-oxidation and energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly eliminated [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous levels) [US].
    - **Half-life:** Distribution half-life is 0.585 hours; mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD PK Study:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients on hemodialysis receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly showed that mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations increased from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L (baseline) to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Special Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing. 
- **Handling:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentrations) for up to 24 hours in PVC bags at room temperature [US].
