# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the **United States (FDA)**. No other jurisdictional documents (e.g., Australian, European, Canadian) were available for comparison at the time of writing.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (carrier molecule for fatty acid transport).
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
  - Sterile injection solution: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose divided over the next 24 hours. Dosing frequency should be every 3 to 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Max Dose:** The highest reported administered dose is 300 mg/kg [US].
- **Monitoring:** Pre-therapy plasma carnitine levels are recommended. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustments:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy if levels are adequate [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use. 
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known at the time of publication [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. These can occur within minutes of intravenous administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue immediately and initiate appropriate medical treatment [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure history. In those with known seizure disorders, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **General Precautions (Oral vs. IV):** The safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].

### Common Adverse Effects (≥5% in ESRD clinical trials)
Adverse events reported in ESRD patients on dialysis regardless of causality [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (21%), headache (37%), infection (24%), injection site reaction (38%), pain (35%), fever (12%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18-21%), hypotension (19%), tachycardia (5-9%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (35%), vomiting (21%), nausea (12%).
- **Metabolic:** Hypercalcemia (15%), hypervolemia (12%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (10-18%), paresthesia (12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (27%), cough increase (18%), dyspnea (11%).

### Other Reported Effects
- Transient nausea and vomiting.
- Body odor and gastritis [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have been observed when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
  - **US Category:** Not specifically assigned a letter in this document, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. 
  - **Clinical Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows indicate milk concentrations increase following exogenous administration. In nursing mothers, the benefits of supplementation to the mother must be weighed against the potential risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal studies (rats/rabbits) [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight. See Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dosing in ESRD is specifically indicated (see Section 3). Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral formulations are used in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Carnitine deficiency is defined biochemically as plasma concentrations <20 µmol/L. IV administration increases plasma carnitine levels rapidly [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
  - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound; does not bind to albumin [US].
  - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
  - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis (trough) levels from 21.3 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg demonstrated dose-dependent increases in trough carnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Precautions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL concentration) for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
