# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of Levocarnitine injection, synthesized from official Product Information for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. At the time of this writing, only the United States (FDA) source documentation was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine ((R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt).
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency (Metabolic):** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - Recommended dose: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
    - Loading doses may be used in severe metabolic crises, followed by equivalent doses divided every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) [US].
    - Maximum reported dose: 300 mg/kg [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:**
    - Starting dose: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) [US].
    - Administration: Slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - Dose adjustments: Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy based on trough (pre-dialysis) concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration [US].
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial as the formulation contains no preservatives [US].

### Monitoring
- **Metabolic:** Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations. Weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) is recommended [US].
- **ESRD:** Therapy initiation may be prompted by pre-dialysis plasma concentrations below 40 to 50 µmol/L [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occur within minutes of administration. Discontinue immediately if a reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizures:** Reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Oral Formulation Limitation:** The safety and efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral use in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event frequencies are based on clinical trials in patients on chronic hemodialysis.
- **Very Common (>10%):**
    - **General:** Injection site reaction (27–38%), Pain (21–35%), Headache (3–37%), Flu syndrome (15–29%), Chest pain (6–15%), Infection (10–24%), Abdominal pain (5–21%) [US].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18–21%), Hypotension (3–19%) [US].
    - **Digestive:** Diarrhea (9–35%), Vomiting (9–21%), Nausea (5–12%) [US].
    - **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15–27%), Cough increase (10–18%), Dyspnea (3–11%), Rhinitis (6–11%) [US].
    - **Neurologic:** Dizziness (10–18%) [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** 
    - Asthenia, back pain, fever, tachycardia, palpitations, anorexia, constipation, dyspepsia, anemia, peripheral edema, weight changes, hypercalcemia, hyperkalemia, depression, and paraesthesia [US].
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - Seizures (Neurologic) [US].
    - Anaphylaxis and laryngeal edema (Immune system) [US].
- **Specific Clinical Note:** A transient "body odor" has been reported with levocarnitine use [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concomitantly. It is recommended to monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not assigned a letter grade in the current source, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. In dairy cows, exogenous carnitine increases milk concentrations. Risks to the infant from excess intake should be weighed against the benefits of maternal treatment [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was seen in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is consistent with adult weight-based dosing (mg/kg) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in provided sources; clinical trials focused on ESRD and metabolic populations regardless of age [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Used as a treatment for ESRD-related deficiency. However, caution is advised with *oral* dosage forms in severe renal impairment due to metabolite accumulation [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities resulting from toxic organic acid accumulation and corrects biochemical carnitine deficiency (defined as <20 µmol/L at one week post-term) [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg doses three times weekly post-dialysis increased mean pre-dialysis trough concentrations from 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While carnitine levels increased, effects on specific clinical outcomes in this population were not determined in the referenced trials [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature when mixed in PVC bags with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison/Legal Classification:** Rx-only [US].
