# Editorial Product Information: Tirzepatide

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (Therapeutic Goods Administration - TGA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of tirzepatide, synthesised for use by healthcare professionals.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and reference purposes. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority's current approved PI before prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tirzepatide (a long-acting glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist).
- **Brand Name(s):** **Mounjaro** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** A10BX16.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):**
    - **Single-dose vial and pre-filled pen (autoinjector):** 2.5 mg/0.5 mL, 5 mg/0.5 mL, 7.5 mg/0.5 mL, 10 mg/0.5 mL, 12.5 mg/0.5 mL, 15 mg/0.5 mL.
    - **Multiple-dose pre-filled pen (KwikPen):** Available in the same dosage strengths (2.5 mg to 15 mg) delivered in a 0.6 mL volume per dose. Contains preservatives (metacresol and benzyl alcohol) [Australian].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Subcutaneous injection.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Eli Lilly Australia Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM):** Treatment of adults with insufficiently controlled T2DM as an adjunct to diet and exercise. Approved as monotherapy (when metformin is contraindicated or not tolerated) or as an add-on to other glucose-lowering medicinal products.
- **Chronic Weight Management:** Adjunct to a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity for weight loss and maintenance in adults with an initial Body Mass Index (BMI) of:
    - ≥30 kg/m² (obesity), or
    - ≥27 kg/m² to <30 kg/m² (overweight) in the presence of at least one weight-related comorbidity (e.g., hypertension, dyslipidaemia, OSA, cardiovascular disease, prediabetes, or T2DM).
- **Obstructive Sleep Apnoea (OSA):** Treatment of moderate to severe OSA in adults with obesity.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Initial Dosing:** The starting dose is 2.5 mg once weekly. This dose is for treatment initiation and is not a maintenance dose.
- **Titration Schedule:**
    - After 4 weeks, increase to 5 mg once weekly.
    - If further glycaemic or weight control is required, increases may be made in 2.5 mg increments after a minimum of 4 weeks on the current dose.
- **Maintenance Dosing:** Recommended maintenance doses are 5 mg, 10 mg, or 15 mg weekly. The maximum dose is 15 mg weekly.
- **Administration:** Subcutaneous injection in the abdomen or thigh. Rotate injection sites with each dose. May be administered at any time of day, independent of meals.
- **Missed Dose:** 
    - Administer as soon as possible if within 4 days (96 hours) of the missed dose.
    - If more than 4 days have passed, skip the missed dose and resume on the next regularly scheduled day. Do not administer two doses within 3 days (72 hours) of each other.
- **Switching Schedule:** The day of weekly administration can be changed provided the interval between two doses is at least 3 days (72 hours).

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to **tirzepatide** or any of the excipients (including sodium chloride, dibasic sodium phosphate heptahydrate, and in the KwikPen, benzyl alcohol, metacresol, and phenol) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Acute Pancreatitis:** Has been reported in clinical trials. Patients should be informed of characteristic symptoms (persistent, severe abdominal pain). If suspected, discontinue tirzepatide. Do not restart if pancreatitis is confirmed. Use with caution in patients with a history of pancreatitis.
- **Severe Gastrointestinal Disease:** Tirzepatide is associated with GI adverse reactions (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea) and has not been studied in patients with severe gastroparesis. Use with caution.
- **Hypoglycaemia:** Risk increases when used in combination with insulin secretagogues (e.g., sulfonylureas) or insulin. Consider reducing the dose of the secretagogue or insulin to mitigate risk.
- **Dehydration and Renal Effects:** GI adverse events can lead to dehydration, potentially causing acute renal failure. Monitor renal function in patients reporting severe GI effects.
- **Diabetic Retinopathy:** Rapid improvement in glucose control has been associated with temporary worsening of diabetic retinopathy. Use with caution/appropriate monitoring.
- **Suicidal Ideation/Psychiatric Disorders:** Reported with other GLP-1 receptor agonists. Monitor for mood changes or suicidal thoughts.
- **Pulmonary Aspiration:** Delayed gastric emptying may increase the risk of pulmonary aspiration during general anaesthesia or deep sedation.
- **KwikPen Specific (Benzyl Alcohol):** Contains benzyl alcohol. Use with caution in patients with hepatic or renal impairment due to potential accumulation and risk of metabolic acidosis [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are primarily gastrointestinal and occur most frequently during the titration phase.

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction Term |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common** (≥10%) | Nausea, Diarrhoea, Abdominal pain, Vomiting, Constipation, Decreased appetite (T2DM), Hypoglycaemia (when used with insulin/sulfonylurea). |
| **Common** (1–10%) | Dyspepsia, Abdominal distension, Eructation, Flatulence, GERD, Fatigue, Injection site reactions, Dizziness, Hair loss (Weight/OSA indications), Hypotension (Weight/OSA indications). |
| **Uncommon** (0.1–1%) | Cholelithiasis, Acute pancreatitis, Cholecystitis, Dysaesthesia. |
| **Post-marketing** | Anaphylactic reaction, Angioedema, Intestinal obstruction (ileus), NAION (Non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy). |

- **Immunogenicity:** 51–65% of patients developed anti-drug antibodies (ADA), though no significant impact on pharmacokinetics or efficacy was observed.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Delayed Gastric Emptying:** Tirzepatide delays gastric emptying, which may reduce the rate and extent of absorption of concomitantly administered oral medications, particularly at treatment initiation or dose escalation.
- **Narrow Therapeutic Index (NTI) Drugs:** Monitor patients taking oral NTI drugs (e.g., warfarin, digoxin) closely during initiation/titration.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Concomitant use may reduce the efficacy of oral hormonal contraceptives. Advise patients using oral contraceptives to switch to a non-oral method or add a barrier method for 4 weeks after treatment initiation and for 4 weeks after each dose escalation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Category D** [Australian]. Tirzepatide should not be used during pregnancy. Animal studies have shown reproductive toxicity (embryofetal mortality and structural anomalies) at subclinical exposures. Use effective contraception during treatment. Discontinue if pregnancy is planned or occurs.
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety has not been established. A risk to newborns/infants cannot be excluded. Tirzepatide should only be used if the potential benefit to the mother justifies the risk to the infant.
- **Fertility:** Human data are unavailable. No effects on male fertility were seen in rats. In female rats, prolonged oestrus cycles and decreased corpora lutea were observed at subclinical exposures.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children and adolescents <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly (≥65 years):** No dose adjustment required based on age. Limited data available for patients ≥85 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required, including end-stage renal disease (ESRD). Caution is advised in severe renal impairment/ESRD due to limited clinical experience.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment required. Caution is advised in severe hepatic impairment due to limited clinical experience.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A 39-amino acid peptide that acts as a dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist. It enhances first- and second-phase insulin secretion, increases insulin sensitivity, reduces glucagon levels, and delays gastric emptying. It acts on the brain to regulate appetite and reduce food intake.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Absolute bioavailability is ~80%. Tmax is 8 to 72 hours.
    - **Distribution:** Highly bound to plasma albumin (99%).
    - **Metabolism:** Proteolytic cleavage, beta-oxidation of the C20 fatty diacid moiety, and amide hydrolysis.
    - **Elimination:** Half-life is approximately 5 days. Excreted via urine and faeces as metabolites; intact drug is not found in excreta.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **SURPASS Program (T2DM):** Five global Phase 3 trials demonstrated that tirzepatide (5 mg, 10 mg, 15 mg) provided statistically significant and clinically meaningful reductions in HbA1c and body weight compared to placebo, semaglutide 1 mg, insulin degludec, and insulin glargine.
- **SURMOUNT Program (Weight Management):** Phase 3 trials (SURMOUNT-1 to 4) showed superior weight loss compared to placebo. In SURMOUNT-1, mean weight reduction at 72 weeks reached up to 22.5% with the 15 mg dose.
- **SURMOUNT-OSA:** Two Phase 3 trials in patients with moderate to severe OSA and obesity (with or without PAP therapy) showed a significant reduction in the Apnoea-Hypopnoea Index (AHI) compared to placebo (reductions of up to 62.8% from baseline).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Refrigerated Storage:** Store at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Keep in the original carton to protect from light.
- **Unrefrigerated Storage (In-use):**
    - **Single-dose vial/pen:** May be stored at room temperature (below 30°C) for up to 21 days.
    - **KwikPen:** May be stored at room temperature (below 30°C) for up to 30 days [Australian].
