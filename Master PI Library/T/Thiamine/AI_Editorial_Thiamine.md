## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Thiamine hydrochloride (B1), Riboflavine sodium phosphate (B2), Nicotinamide (B3), Dexpanthenol (B5), Pyridoxine hydrochloride (B6), and Cyanocobalamin (B12).
- **Brand Name:** Biological Therapies B-Dose 2 mL Injection [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (Typically A11EA for Vitamin B-complex combinations).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Injection solution in 2 mL amber glass vials. Each 2 mL contains:
    - Thiamine hydrochloride: 20 mg
    - Riboflavine sodium phosphate: 5 mg
    - Nicotinamide: 50 mg
    - Dexpanthenol: 5 mg
    - Pyridoxine hydrochloride: 10 mg
    - Cyanocobalamin: 1 mg
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection (preferred) or slow intravenous (IV) infusion [Australian].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Biological Therapies (A Division of Orthomolecular Medisearch Laboratories Pty Ltd) [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
Parenteral administration is indicated for B vitamin deficiencies that cannot be corrected by oral intake, particularly in cases of alcoholism, pernicious anaemia, malabsorption disorders, gastrectomy, gastrointestinal pathologies, and in debilitated or elderly patients [Australian].

**Specific Indications:**
- Rapid saturation of B group vitamins [Australian].
- Use when oral administration is not feasible or appropriate [Australian].
- Management of inadequate diet in alcoholic, debilitated, and elderly patients [Australian].
- Recovery from Beriberi and Wernicke’s syndrome (Note: This specific product contains insufficient thiamine for the **acute** treatment of Wernicke’s syndrome) [Australian].
- Pellagra (Vitamin B3 deficiency) [Australian].
- Peripheral neuritis resulting from B group deficiencies [Australian].
- Pernicious anaemia (Vitamin B12 deficiency) [Australian].
- Angular stomatitis and glossitis [Australian].
- General energy depletion [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Standard Dose:** One 2 mL vial administered by slow intramuscular (deep intragluteal) injection once weekly for 2 to 6 weeks, or as determined by clinical judgment [Australian].
- **Severe Cases:** One vial daily may be administered until acute symptoms subside [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** For patient comfort, the solution should be warmed to body temperature before administration [Australian].
- **IM Route:** High-dose B complex injections are preferred via the IM route to mitigate the risk of anaphylaxis [Australian].
- **IV Route:** If used intravenously, it must be administered as a slow infusion of a diluted solution (e.g., within Total Parenteral Nutrition) [Australian].
- **Visual Inspection:** The solution should be clear and browny-yellow. Discard if cloudy or containing particles [Australian].
- **Single Use:** This product is for single use only; any residue must be discarded [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to any of the active ingredients or excipients [Australian].
- Pre-existing B hyper-vitaminosis [Australian].
- **Dexpanthenol-specific:** Contraindicated in patients with haemophilia and those with ileus due to mechanical obstruction [Australian].
- **Cyanocobalamin-specific:** Must not be administered for megaloblastic anaemia caused by folate deficiency [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Anaphylaxis:** Rare cases of anaphylactic shock have been reported following parenteral thiamine. Allergic reactions have also been noted with dexpanthenol and cyanocobalamin. Immediate cessation is required if a reaction occurs [Australian].
- **Emergency Management:** If anaphylaxis occurs, adrenaline 1:1000 (1 mg/1 mL) should be administered IM (may be repeated every 5 minutes). IV corticosteroids and antihistamines may be considered [Australian].
- **Sensitivity Testing:** For patients with a known allergic disposition requiring thiamine, a supervised intradermal test dose is recommended [Australian].
- **Masking of Diagnosis:** Indiscriminate use of cyanocobalamin (doses >10 µg/day) may produce a haematological response in folate deficiency, potentially masking a precise diagnosis [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effects are generally rare following parenteral administration of B vitamins.

| Active Ingredient | Adverse Effect and Frequency |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Thiamine (B1)** | Anaphylactic shock (rare); most severe via IV route [Australian]. |
| **Nicotinamide (B3)** | Chronic high doses (3g/day) associated with heartburn, nausea, headache, hives, fatigue, sore throat, and inability to focus eyes [Australian]. |
| **Pyridoxine (B6)** | Long-term high doses (>1000 mg/day) associated with severe peripheral neuropathies [Australian]. |
| **Riboflavine (B2)** | Bright yellow discolouration of urine (common/expected), which may interfere with laboratory tests [Australian]. |
| **Dexpanthenol (B5)** | Allergic reactions (rare/unconfirmed) [Australian]. |
| **Cyanocobalamin (B12)** | Allergic hypersensitivity reactions (rare) [Australian]. |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Levodopa:** Pyridoxine (B6) reduces the therapeutic effects of levodopa. This interaction is nullified if a dopa-decarboxylase inhibitor (e.g., carbidopa) is co-administered [Australian].
- **Laboratory Tests:** Riboflavine (B2) may cause yellow urine discolouration, potentially interfering with certain laboratory assessments [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
- **Thiamine (B1):** Essential for converting pyruvate to Acetyl Coenzyme A; critical for aerobic respiration. Deficiency leads to pyruvate accumulation, Beriberi, and Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome [Australian].
- **Riboflavine (B2):** Precursor to FMN and FAD; functions as an electron carrier in redox reactions. Necessary for B3 and B6 activation [Australian].
- **Nicotinamide (B3):** Precursor to NAD and NADP; essential for cell metabolism. Deficiency causes Pellagra [Australian].
- **Vitamin B5:** Component of Coenzyme A; essential for the TCA cycle [Australian].
- **Pyridoxine (B6):** Vital for amino acid, carbohydrate, and fat metabolism, and haem production. Deficiency leads to peripheral neuritis [Australian].
- **Cyanocobalamin (B12):** Converted to mecobalamin and adenosylcobalamin. Essential for DNA synthesis, haematopoiesis, and neurological function [Australian].

### Pharmacokinetics
- Combination pharmacokinetics have not been studied; however, individual vitamins are expected to behave as they do when administered separately [Australian].
- **Cyanocobalamin:** Rapidly converted to active coenzyme forms (mecobalamin and adenosylcobalamin) in tissues [Australian].
