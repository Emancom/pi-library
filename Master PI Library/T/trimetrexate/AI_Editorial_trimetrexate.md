# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been synthesised from the following official Product Information (PI) source: United States (FDA). 
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory information into a clinically focused summary.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for reference by healthcare professionals and does not substitute for the official Product Information approved for use in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP).
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/Amino acid derivative [(R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt].
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (Note: A16AA01).
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for both acute and chronic management of patients with inborn metabolic errors resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are receiving maintenance hemodialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion. 
    - **Loading Doses:** In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose may be given, followed by equivalent doses every 3 to 4 hours (and never less than every 6 hours).
    - **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been utilised [US].
- **ESRD on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
    - **Adjustments:** Dose adjustments (e.g., downward to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma carnitine levels [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations. Weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported. These may occur within minutes of administration, particularly in ESRD patients. Treatment should be discontinued immediately if a severe reaction occurs [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in the frequency or severity of events has been observed [US].

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Renal Impairment:** The safety and efficacy of **oral** levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO), which are normally renally excreted [US]. 
- **Monitoring:** In ESRD patients, monitoring should be guided by pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting are common. Gastritis has also been reported [US].
- **General:** Body odor (a "fishy" smell) may occur [US].

### Adverse Effects in ESRD Patients (Frequency ≥5%)
Clinical trials in hemodialysis patients reported the following events regardless of causality:
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (18-21%), Hypotension (3-19%), Tachycardia (5-9%), Chest pain (6-15%).
- **Neurological:** Headache (3-37%), Dizziness (10-18%), Paresthesia (3-12%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (9-35%), Abdominal pain (5-21%), Vomiting (9-21%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (15-27%), Cough increase (10-18%), Dyspnea (3-11%).
- **Body as a Whole:** Injection site reaction (27-38%), Pain (21-35%), Infection (10-24%), Fever (5-12%) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified in source; however, the PI states the drug should be used during pregnancy only if clearly needed.] Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine levels following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefits of maternal supplementation [US].
- **Fertility:** No mutagenic effects were found in standard tests (Salmonella, Saccharomyces, Schizosaccharomyces). Long-term animal carcinogenicity studies have not been performed [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is based on mg/kg body weight (refer to Section 3). Specific age-range safety profiles are not further detailed [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment:** Dose adjustments in ESRD are based on pre-dialysis carnitine troughs. Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if oral formulations are used [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It acts as a carrier molecule facilitating the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent ATP production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces) [US].
- **Elimination:** Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine (20 mg/kg post-dialysis) increased mean plasma carnitine concentrations from 20 µmol/L (pre-dialysis) to troughs of 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Data:** A study comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses 3 times per week showed dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Conditions:** Avoid excessive heat; protect from freezing [US].
- **Stability:** Compatible and stable for up to 24 hours in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) at room temperature [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vial; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
