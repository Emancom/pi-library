# Editorial Product Information: Tranylcypromine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**. 
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, consolidating clinical guidance into a single, structured reference.
- **Note:** This document is an editorial synthesis intended for educational and reference purposes. It does not replace the official PI or local regulatory requirements for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tranylcypromine (as sulfate).
- **Chemical Class:** Non-hydrazine Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitor (MAOI).
- **Brand Names:** Parnate [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** N06AF04.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - 10 mg film-coated tablets [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Amdipharm Mercury (Australia) Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Depressive Illness:** Indicated for the management of depressive symptoms, particularly in patients who have not responded to other antidepressant classes [Australian].
- **Note on Mild Depression:** Not recommended for the treatment of mild depressive states arising from temporary situational difficulties [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** 
    - **Initial Dose:** 20 mg daily, administered as 10 mg in the morning and 10 mg in the afternoon [Australian].
    - **Titration:** If response is inadequate after two weeks, the dose may be increased by adding 10 mg at midday. This 30 mg/day dose should be maintained for at least one week [Australian].
    - **Maximum Dose:** Do not exceed 30 mg/day unless under exceptional caution [Australian].
    - **Maintenance:** Once a response is achieved, the dose should be reduced to the minimum effective level, typically 10 mg to 20 mg daily [Australian].
- **Concomitant Therapies:**
    - **Tranquillisers:** Dosing remains unaffected when used concurrently [Australian].
    - **Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT):** Recommended dose is 10 mg twice daily during the series, followed by 10 mg daily for maintenance [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:** To reduce the risk of insomnia, the final dose of the day should generally be taken no later than 3:00 PM [Australian].
- **Cessation:** Treatment must be withdrawn gradually to mitigate the risk of withdrawal symptoms [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to tranylcypromine or any excipients [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamic Contraindications (Drug Classes):**
    - **Other MAOIs:** (e.g., phenelzine, moclobemide, furazolidone). Requires a 2-week washout after stopping a previous MAOI before starting tranylcypromine. A 1-week gap is required when switching from tranylcypromine to another MAOI [Australian].
    - **Tricyclic/Tetracyclic Antidepressants:** Especially clomipramine and imipramine (requires a 3-week washout). Other dibenzazepines require a 2-week washout after stopping tranylcypromine [Australian].
    - **SSRIs and SNRIs:** Fluoxetine requires a 5-week washout before starting tranylcypromine. Tranylcypromine requires a 2-week washout before starting SSRIs/SNRIs [Australian].
    - **Sympathomimetics:** Amphetamines, ephedrine, phenylpropanolamine, and OTC cold/allergy medications containing pressor agents [Australian].
    - **Analgesics:** Pethidine and related opioids, and nefopam. A 2-week washout is required after stopping MAOIs before starting opioids [Australian].
    - **Others:** Bupropion (2-week gap), buspirone (10-day gap), carbamazepine, and dextromethorphan [Australian].
- **Dietary Contraindications:** Foods with high tyramine content (e.g., matured cheese, yeast extracts like Marmite/Vegemite, red wine, broad bean pods, sauerkraut, pickled herring) [Australian].
- **Medical Conditions:**
    - **Vascular/Cardiac:** Confirmed or suspected cerebrovascular defect, cardiovascular disease, hypertension, or history of frequent headaches [Australian].
    - **Metabolic/Endocrine:** Phaeochromocytoma, hyperthyroidism [Australian].
    - **Organ Impairment:** Impaired hepatic function or history of liver disease [Australian].
    - **Other:** Blood dyscrasias, porphyria [Australian].
- **Age:** Patients over 60 years of age (due to risk of cerebral sclerosis) [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypertensive Crisis:** The most significant risk, often associated with tyramine ingestion or drug interactions. Symptoms include severe occipital headache, neck stiffness, palpitations, and nausea. Therapy must be discontinued immediately if palpitations or frequent headaches occur [Australian].
- **Suicidality:** Increased risk in patients under 25 years. Monitor closely for clinical worsening and emergence of suicidal ideation, particularly during initial treatment or dose changes [Australian].
- **Mania/Bipolar Disorder:** Screen patients for bipolar risk; antidepressants may precipitate a manic episode [Australian].
- **Surgery:** Discontinue tranylcypromine at least 2 weeks prior to elective surgery due to potential interactions with anaesthetics (especially those containing vasoconstrictors) [Australian].
- **Diabetes:** Potential for hypoglycaemic episodes; use with caution in patients on insulin or oral hypoglycaemics [Australian].
- **Epilepsy:** May alter the convulsive threshold [Australian].
- **Angle-closure Glaucoma:** Mydriatic effects may increase intraocular pressure [Australian].
- **Excipients:** Contains sucrose; contraindicated in patients with fructose intolerance or glucose-galactose malabsorption [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Serious Adverse Effects:**
    - **Hypertensive Crisis:** May lead to intracranial bleeding; can be fatal [Australian].
    - **Serotonin Syndrome:** Reported when combined with serotonergic agents [Australian].
    - **Haematological:** Rare instances of anaemia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, and thrombocytopenia [Australian].
    - **Hepatic:** Rare instances of hepatitis and jaundice [Australian].
- **Common Adverse Effects:**
    - **Neurological:** Insomnia (most frequent), dizziness, drowsiness, weakness, tremors, myoclonic jerks [Australian].
    - **Autonomic/Gastrointestinal:** Dry mouth, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, constipation [Australian].
    - **Cardiovascular:** Postural hypotension, palpitations, tachycardia, oedema [Australian].
- **Other:** Blurred vision, impotence, retarded ejaculation, SIADH, skin rash, and potential for dependency development [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Antihypertensives:** Action of guanethidine may be antagonised. Reserpine may cause hyperactivity. Additive hypotensive effects with other antihypertensives [Australian].
- **CNS Depressants:** Barbiturates and other hypnotics may have prolonged or potentiated actions [Australian].
- **Anticholinergics:** Effects of antimuscarinic agents and antihistamines are increased [Australian].
- **Anti-Parkinsonism Agents:** Potential for profuse sweating and hyperpyrexia [Australian].
- **Anticoagulants:** Potential for increased anticoagulant effect [Australian].
- **Metrizamide:** Avoid use as it may lower the seizure threshold [Australian].
- **Dietary/Lifestyle:** Avoid excessive caffeine and alcohol [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Category B2** [Australian]. 
    - Adequate human data are unavailable. Use only if clearly essential. Animal studies show the drug crosses the placental barrier. Potential risk of congenital abnormalities and neonatal withdrawal symptoms (if used in the third trimester) [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Tranylcypromine is secreted into breast milk. Safety has not been fully evaluated; use only if the benefits outweigh the risks [Australian].
- **Fertility:** No data available [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Not indicated for patients under 18 years of age [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Contraindicated in patients over 60 years due to the risk of existing cerebral sclerosis [Australian].
- **Renal Impairment:** Potential for cumulative effects; use with caution [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Contraindicated [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Irreversible, non-hydrazine MAOI. It increases the synaptic concentrations of epinephrine, norepinephrine, and serotonin by inhibiting their degradation [Australian].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Rapid onset of action. After withdrawal, MAO activity typically recovers within 3 to 5 days [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted within 24 hours [Australian].
    - **Distribution:** Crosses the placental barrier (observed in rats) [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- Not specified in provided source [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** PVC-PVDC/Aluminium blister packs of 50 tablets [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Refer to expiry date on packaging [Australian].
