# Editorial Product Information: Triamterene and Hydrochlorothiazide

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA) 
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of the combination product triamterene and hydrochlorothiazide for healthcare professionals.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference only. It is not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in specific jurisdictions. Clinicians should consult the current local PI for full prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Triamterene (pteridine class) and Hydrochlorothiazide (benzothiadiazide class).
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in source (Generic: Northwind Health Company/Rubicon Research).
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available source.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Tablets: 37.5 mg triamterene / 25 mg hydrochlorothiazide [US]
    - Tablets: 75 mg triamterene / 50 mg hydrochlorothiazide [US]
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Manufactured by Rubicon Research Ltd. (India); Distributed by Advagen Pharma Ltd. (US) and Northwind Health Company, LLC (US).

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hypertension:** For the treatment of hypertension in patients who develop hypokalemia on hydrochlorothiazide monotherapy, or in whom the development of hypokalemia cannot be risked (e.g., patients on digitalis or with cardiac arrhythmias) [US].
- **Edema:** For the treatment of edema in patients who develop hypokalemia on hydrochlorothiazide monotherapy, or in whom the development of hypokalemia cannot be risked [US].
- **Note on Initial Therapy:** This fixed combination is generally **not** indicated for the initial therapy of edema or hypertension, except for individuals where the risk of hypokalemia is clinically unacceptable [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):**
    - **37.5 mg/25 mg Formulation:** Usual dose is one or two tablets daily, administered as a single dose [US].
    - **75 mg/50 mg Formulation:** Usual dose is one tablet daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Can be taken with or without food; food does not significantly influence absorption [US].
- **Dose Transfer:** Patients receiving 50 mg of hydrochlorothiazide monotherapy who become hypokalemic may be transferred directly to the 75 mg/50 mg tablet. Patients on 25 mg hydrochlorothiazide may be transferred to the 37.5 mg/25 mg tablet [US].
- **Maximum Recommended Dose:** There is no clinical experience with doses exceeding one 75 mg/50 mg tablet or two 37.5 mg/25 mg tablets daily [US].
- **Monitoring:** Frequent monitoring of serum potassium is required, especially during initiation or dose changes.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Hyperkalemia:** Elevated serum potassium levels (≥5.5 mEq/L) [US].
- **Antikaliuretic Therapy:** Concomitant use of other potassium-sparing agents (e.g., spironolactone, amiloride, or other triamterene-containing products) [US].
- **Potassium Supplementation:** Use of potassium supplements, potassium-containing salt substitutes, or potassium-enriched diets [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Anuria, acute or chronic renal insufficiency, or significant renal impairment [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to triamterene, hydrochlorothiazide, or other sulfonamide-derived drugs [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings:** None specified in provided source.
- **Hyperkalemia:** A potentially fatal elevation in serum potassium can occur. Risk is increased in patients with renal impairment, diabetes mellitus, the elderly, or the severely ill. Monitor serum electrolytes frequently [US].
- **Acute Myopia and Secondary Angle-Closure Glaucoma:** Hydrochlorothiazide can trigger an idiosyncratic reaction resulting in acute transient myopia and acute angle-closure glaucoma. Discontinue immediately if symptoms (decreased visual acuity, ocular pain) occur [US].
- **Electrolyte Imbalance:** Monitor for hyponatremia, hypochloremic alkalosis, hypomagnesemia, and hypokalemia (triamterene may not fully compensate for hydrochlorothiazide's potassium-wasting effect) [US].
- **Azotemia:** May increase BUN and creatinine levels. Discontinue if azotemia increases [US].
- **Hepatic Coma:** Use with caution in patients with impaired hepatic function; minor electrolyte shifts may precipitate hepatic coma [US].
- **Renal Stones:** Triamterene has been found in renal stones; use with caution in patients with a history of lithiasis [US].
- **Folic Acid Deficiency:** Triamterene is a weak folic acid antagonist; monitor for megaloblastosis in patients with low folate stores [US].
- **Metabolic Effects:** Thiazides may impair glucose tolerance (manifesting latent diabetes) and cause hyperuricemia or precipitate gout [US].
- **Non-melanoma Skin Cancer:** Hydrochlorothiazide is associated with an increased risk of squamous cell carcinoma and basal cell carcinoma. Patients should protect skin from sun exposure [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Frequencies are not specifically quantified in the source text; however, the following effects have been reported:
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, jaundice (intrahepatic cholestatic), pancreatitis, appetite disturbance [US].
- **Central Nervous System:** Dizziness, headache, fatigue, insomnia, drowsiness, depression, anxiety, paresthesias [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, chest pain, orthostatic hypotension (potentiated by alcohol/barbiturates) [US].
- **Renal:** Acute renal failure, interstitial nephritis, renal stones, urine discoloration [US].
- **Hematologic:** Leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, hemolytic anemia, megaloblastosis [US].
- **Ophthalmic:** Transient blurred vision, xanthopsia [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Anaphylaxis, rash, photosensitivity, urticaria, purpura, necrotizing angiitis, respiratory distress [US].
- **Metabolic:** Hyperkalemia, hyponatremia, hyperglycemia, hyperuricemia [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **ACE Inhibitors:** Greatly increased risk of hyperkalemia. Use with extreme caution, if at all [US].
- **Lithium:** Generally should not be given with diuretics as renal clearance of lithium is reduced, increasing toxicity risk [US].
- **NSAIDs (e.g., Indomethacin):** Concomitant use with triamterene may trigger acute renal failure. Use caution [US].
- **Potassium-Sparing Agents/Supplements:** Contraindicated due to severe hyperkalemia risk [US].
- **Antihypertensive Agents:** Thiazides may potentiate the action of other antihypertensives; dosage adjustments may be necessary [US].
- **Digitalis:** Hypokalemia (if it occurs) can sensitize the heart to digitalis toxicity [US].
- **Norepinephrine:** Thiazides may decrease arterial responsiveness to norepinephrine [US].
- **Tubocurarine:** Thiazides may increase the muscle relaxant effect [US].
- **Quinidine:** Triamterene may interfere with the fluorescent measurement of quinidine [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified by letter, but the text states use only if the potential benefit justifies the risk to the fetus [US].
    - **Clinical Considerations:** Thiazides and triamterene cross the placental barrier. Hazards include fetal/neonatal jaundice, thrombocytopenia, and pancreatitis. Diuretics do not prevent toxemia of pregnancy and are generally inappropriate for edema caused by normal physiologic changes [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Thiazides are excreted in human milk; triamterene is excreted in animal milk and likely in humans. If the drug is essential, nursing should be discontinued [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies for the combination have not been conducted. Hydrochlorothiazide monotherapy showed no adverse effects on fertility in mice/rats [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk of hyperkalemia and elevated plasma levels of drug metabolites due to age-related decline in renal function. Frequent monitoring is required [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Contraindicated in significant renal impairment or anuria. Renal clearance of metabolites is reduced in patients with renal dysfunction [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Use with caution; electrolyte imbalances may precipitate hepatic encephalopathy [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Hydrochlorothiazide:** Inhibits renal tubular reabsorption of sodium and chloride, leading to natriuresis and diuresis.
    - **Triamterene:** Exerts a diuretic effect on the distal renal tubule, inhibiting sodium reabsorption in exchange for potassium and hydrogen. It is not an aldosterone antagonist.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Both components are well-absorbed. Triamterene peak levels occur within 1 hour; hydrochlorothiazide peak levels occur within 2 hours [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Triamterene is rapidly metabolized to the active sulfate conjugate of hydroxytriamterene [US].
    - **Elimination:** Hydrochlorothiazide is excreted unchanged in the urine. Triamterene metabolites are excreted in the urine [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Onset of diuresis for both occurs within 2 hours; peak action at 4 hours; duration of 6–12 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Dose-Response Evaluations:** Steady-state evaluations determined that 75 mg of triamterene was the optimal dose to maximize potassium conservation when combined with 50 mg of hydrochlorothiazide. For the 25 mg hydrochlorothiazide dose, 37.5 mg of triamterene was found to be the effective potassium-conserving dose [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C and 30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from light. Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].
