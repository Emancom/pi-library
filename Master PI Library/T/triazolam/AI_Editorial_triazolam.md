# Editorial Product Information: Triazolam

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States** (Food and Drug Administration).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and must not be used as a substitute for the official, current Product Information in the specific jurisdiction where a clinician is practicing or prescribing.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Triazolam
- **Chemical Class:** Triazolobenzodiazepine
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - **Tablets:** 0.125 mg (white) and 0.25 mg (blue, scored) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Mylan Pharmaceuticals Inc. (a Viatris Company) [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Short-term treatment of insomnia:** Indicated for the management of sleep onset difficulties, generally for a duration of 7 to 10 days [US]. 

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults):** The recommended starting dose is 0.25 mg once daily, administered immediately before bedtime. In patients with low body weight, 0.125 mg may be sufficient.
- **Maximum Dose:** 0.5 mg once daily; this dose should be reserved for patients who do not respond to a trial of a lower dose [US].
- **Duration of Therapy:** Use should be limited to 7–10 days. If treatment exceeds 3 weeks, a thorough evaluation for primary psychiatric or medical disorders is required [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** Take immediately before retiring to bed. Do not administer with grapefruit juice.
- **Discontinuation:** To mitigate the risk of withdrawal, a gradual downward titration is recommended. If withdrawal symptoms occur during tapering, consider pausing or returning to the previous dose before proceeding with a slower reduction [US].
- **Prescribing Limit:** Should not be prescribed in quantities exceeding a 1-month supply [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to triazolam, other benzodiazepines, or any component of the formulation (including reactions such as angioedema) [US].
- Concomitant administration with strong Cytochrome P450 3A (CYP3A) inhibitors, including but not limited to:
    - Ketoconazole, itraconazole, and nefazodone [US].
    - HIV protease inhibitors (e.g., ritonavir, lopinavir, indinavir) [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warning [US]:**
    - **Risks from Concomitant Use with Opioids:** May result in profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, and death. Reserve co-prescribing for cases where alternative treatments are inadequate.
    - **Abuse, Misuse, and Addiction:** Use of triazolam exposes patients to the risk of abuse and addiction, which can lead to overdose or death.
    - **Dependence and Withdrawal:** Abrupt discontinuation or rapid dose reduction can precipitate life-threatening withdrawal reactions.
- **Complex Sleep Behaviors:** "Sleep-driving," preparing food, or making phone calls while not fully awake have been reported. Discontinue immediately if such an event occurs [US].
- **CNS Manifestations:** May cause increased daytime anxiety, abnormal thinking, and behavioral changes (e.g., aggressiveness, hallucinations, depersonalization). Anterograde amnesia has been reported at higher rates than with other benzodiazepine hypnotics [US].
- **Depression:** Use with caution in patients with depression; benzodiazepines may intensify depressive symptoms or suicidal ideation [US].
- **Respiratory Function:** Caution is required in patients with compromised respiratory function (e.g., sleep apnea, COPD) as respiratory depression and apnea have been reported [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally dose-related. Data from US clinical trials (N=1003) identify the following:

| Event | Frequency (Triazolam %) | Frequency (Placebo %) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Drowsiness | 14.0 | 6.4 |
| Headache | 9.7 | 8.4 |
| Dizziness | 7.8 | 3.1 |
| Nervousness | 5.2 | 4.5 |
| Light-headedness | 4.9 | 0.9 |
| Coordination disorders/ataxia | 4.6 | 0.8 |
| Nausea/Vomiting | 4.6 | 3.7 |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** Paradoxical reactions, angioedema, suicidal ideation, and severe respiratory depression (especially when combined with other CNS depressants) [US].
- **Postmarketing Reports:** Jaundice, syncope, dystonia, and somnambulism [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions (CYP3A4):**
    - **Strong Inhibitors:** Contraindicated (see Section 4).
    - **Moderate/Weak Inhibitors:** Erythromycin increases triazolam Cmax by 46% and half-life by 35%. Cimetidine increases triazolam Cmax by 51% and half-life by 68%. Other interactants include clarithromycin, isoniazid, and oral contraceptives [US]. 
    - **Inducers:** Strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., rifampin) can significantly decrease triazolam plasma concentrations and efficacy [US].
    - **Grapefruit Juice:** Increases triazolam AUC by approximately 48% and should be avoided [US].
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Opioids:** Increased risk of fatal respiratory depression.
    - **CNS Depressants:** Additive effects with alcohol, barbiturates, and other sedatives [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not specified by letter, but follows PLLR format]. Use late in pregnancy can cause neonatal sedation (hypotonia, lethargy) and withdrawal (irritability, tremors). Animal data show skeletal variations at doses ≥200 times the MRHD [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Triazolam is likely present in human milk. It is recommended that lactating women pump and discard breast milk during treatment and for 28 hours (approx. 5 half-lives) after the last dose to minimize infant exposure [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal studies at doses 100 times the MRHD [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Higher plasma concentrations are observed due to reduced clearance. The starting dose must be reduced to 0.125 mg, and the maximum dose should not exceed 0.25 mg [US].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific eGFR/Child-Pugh dosing not provided; however, a case of death from hepatic failure was reported in a patient also receiving diuretics [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A benzodiazepine that binds to the benzodiazepine site of GABA-A receptors, enhancing GABA-mediated synaptic inhibition in the CNS [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Peak plasma levels (1–6 ng/mL) reached within 2 hours of oral administration.
    - **Distribution:** Does not displace bilirubin from albumin in vitro.
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily via CYP3A-mediated hydroxylation to 1-hydroxytriazolam and 4-hydroxytriazolam (subsequently glucuronidated).
    - **Elimination:** Mean plasma half-life is 1.5 to 5.5 hours. Excreted primarily in urine (approx. 80%) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Insomnia Trials:** Clinical trials involved 1003 subjects for durations of 1 to 42 days. Effectiveness was demonstrated for the short-term (7–10 days) management of insomnia. Data showed that concentrations achieved are dose-proportional, and there is no evidence of accumulation after 7 days of dosing at four times the recommended dose [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage Temperature:** Store at controlled room temperature, 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Handling:** Keep out of reach of children.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as a Schedule IV (CIV) controlled substance under the Controlled Substances Act due to the risk of abuse and dependence [US].
