# Editorial Product Information: Topiramate

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information (PI) document provided by the following regulatory authority: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **topiramate** for use by healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data, dosing, and safety information.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing in a particular region. Clinicians should consult their local regulatory authority for the most current and legally binding prescribing information.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** **Topiramate**; a sulfamate-substituted monosaccharide.
- **Brand Names:** APO-Topiramate [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:**
    - **Film-coated tablets:** 25 mg, 50 mg, 100 mg, and 200 mg [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Epilepsy (Adults and children ≥2 years):**
    - Monotherapy in patients with newly diagnosed epilepsy.
    - Conversion to monotherapy.
    - Add-on therapy for partial-onset seizures (with or without secondary generalised seizures), primary generalised tonic-clonic seizures, or drop attacks associated with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome.
- **Migraine (Adults only):**
    - Prophylaxis of migraine headache. Note: **Topiramate** is not indicated for the acute treatment of migraine.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing Principles
- Tablets should be swallowed whole and can be taken without regard to meals.
- Therapy should be initiated at a low dose followed by slow titration to the effective dose.
- Daily doses ≥50 mg should be taken in two divided doses.

### Epilepsy Monotherapy (Adults)
- **Initiation:** 25 mg nightly for one week.
- **Titration:** Increase by 25–50 mg/day at weekly or longer intervals.
- **Target Dose:** 100 mg/day.
- **Maximum Dose:** 500 mg/day (some refractory patients have tolerated 1,000 mg/day).

### Epilepsy Monotherapy (Children ≥2 years)
- **Initiation:** 0.5 to 1 mg/kg nightly for the first week.
- **Titration:** Increase by 0.5 to 1 mg/kg/day at weekly or longer intervals.
- **Target Dose:** 3 to 6 mg/kg/day.

### Epilepsy Add-On Therapy (Adults)
- **Initiation:** 25–50 mg nightly for one week.
- **Titration:** Increase by 25–100 mg/day at weekly or longer intervals.
- **Target Dose:** 200–400 mg/day.
- **Maximum Dose:** 1,000 mg/day.

### Epilepsy Add-On Therapy (Children ≥2 years)
- **Initiation:** 1 to 3 mg/kg/day (up to 25 mg/day) nightly for the first week.
- **Titration:** Increase by 1 to 3 mg/kg/day at weekly or longer intervals.
- **Target Dose:** 5 to 9 mg/kg/day.

### Migraine Prophylaxis (Adults)
- **Initiation:** 25 mg nightly for one week.
- **Titration:** Increase by 25 mg/day at weekly intervals.
- **Target Dose:** 100 mg/day (divided into two doses). Some patients benefit from 50 mg/day.

### Discontinuation
- Antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) must be withdrawn gradually to minimise the risk of increased seizure frequency. In clinical trials, doses were decreased by 50–100 mg weekly for epilepsy and 25–50 mg weekly for migraine.

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **topiramate** or any excipients.
- **Migraine prophylaxis specific contraindication:** Pregnancy and women of childbearing potential not using a highly effective method of contraception [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Suicidal Behaviour and Ideation:** AEDs increase the risk of suicidal thoughts. Monitor patients for depression, mood changes, or suicidal tendencies.
- **Ophthalmologic Effects:**
    - **Acute Myopia and Secondary Angle Closure Glaucoma:** Typically occurs within one month of initiation. Symptoms include acute onset of decreased visual acuity and ocular pain. Requires immediate discontinuation and treatment to reduce intraocular pressure.
    - **Visual Field Defects:** Reported independently of elevated intraocular pressure; usually reversible upon drug discontinuation.
- **Metabolic Acidosis:** **Topiramate** causes hyperchloremic, non-anion gap metabolic acidosis due to renal carbonic anhydrase inhibition. Chronic acidosis increases the risk of nephrolithiasis, osteomalacia, and reduced growth rates in paediatric patients.
- **Nephrolithiasis:** Increased risk of kidney stone formation, particularly in males and those with a prior history or predisposing factors (e.g., metabolic acidosis, ketogenic diet). Adequate hydration is essential.
- **Hyperammonemia and Encephalopathy:** Reported with and without concomitant valproic acid. Risk is dose-related. Monitor ammonia levels in patients with unexplained lethargy or mental status changes.
- **Oligohydrosis and Hyperthermia:** Primarily reported in children, especially in warm environments. Monitor for decreased sweating and increased body temperature.
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS) and Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN) have been reported. Discontinue if suspected.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally dose-related and most common during the titration phase.

### Very Common (>10%)
- **Adults (Epilepsy/Migraine):** Somnolence, dizziness, fatigue, paresthesia, nausea, weight decrease, nasopharyngitis [post-marketing].
- **Paediatric (Epilepsy):** Fatigue, somnolence, decreased appetite, irritability, disturbance in attention.

### Common (1–10%)
- **Nervous System:** Memory impairment, speech disorder, psychomotor slowing, nystagmus, tremor, ataxia, coordination abnormal.
- **Psychiatric:** Depression, anxiety, confusional state, insomnia, expressive language disorder.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea, dyspepsia, dry mouth, constipation, abdominal pain.
- **Other:** Diplopia, vision blurred, epistaxis, rash, alopecia, myalgia, muscle spasms.

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Stevens-Johnson Syndrome/Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis.
- Secondary angle-closure glaucoma.
- Severe metabolic acidosis.
- Suicidal ideation/behaviour.
- Hyperammonemic encephalopathy.
- Increased risk of bleeding (ranging from mild epistaxis to life-threatening haemorrhage).

## 7. Drug Interactions
### Pharmacokinetic Interactions
- **Enzyme Inducers (Phenytoin, Carbamazepine):** Decrease **topiramate** plasma concentrations by ~40-50%. Dosing adjustments may be required.
- **Phenytoin:** **Topiramate** may occasionally increase phenytoin concentrations via CYP2C19 inhibition.
- **Digoxin:** AUC decreased by 12%; monitor serum digoxin levels.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Dose-dependent decrease in ethinylestradiol exposure (18–30% at doses ≥200 mg/day). Contraceptive efficacy may be compromised.
- **Metformin and Pioglitazone:** Potential for altered exposure; monitor for diabetic control.
- **Hydrochlorothiazide:** Increases **topiramate** Cmax by 27% and AUC by 29%.

### Pharmacodynamic Interactions
- **Valproic Acid:** Concomitant use is associated with hyperammonemia (with or without encephalopathy) and hypothermia.
- **CNS Depressants:** **Topiramate** should not be used concomitantly with alcohol or other CNS depressant drugs.
- **Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors:** Use with other agents (e.g., acetazolamide) may increase the risk of metabolic acidosis and nephrolithiasis.
- **Vitamin K-antagonists:** Decreased PT/INR responses reported; monitor INR closely.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D.** 
    - Increased risk of major congenital malformations (e.g., cleft lip/palate, hypospadias) and neurodevelopmental disorders (ADHD, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorders). 
    - Risk of "small for gestational age" (SGA) infants and low birth weight.
    - Folic acid supplementation (5 mg) is recommended starting 4 weeks before conception.
- **Breastfeeding:** Extensive excretion in breast milk (based on limited human data). Diarrhoea and somnolence reported in infants. Breastfeeding is not advised during treatment.
- **Fertility:** No effect on fertility or reproduction in rat studies at human-equivalent doses.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for epilepsy (≥2 years). Not approved for migraine prophylaxis in children. Risk of oligohydrosis and metabolic acidosis is higher in this population.
- **Elderly Use:** Clearance is unchanged unless renal impairment is present. Titration should be cautious.
- **Renal Impairment:** Plasma and renal clearance are decreased in moderate/severe impairment (CrCl <70 mL/min). **Initial and maintenance doses should be reduced by 50%.** Steady-state takes longer to achieve (10–15 days).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Clearance may be decreased; administer with caution.
- **Haemodialysis:** **Topiramate** is effectively removed by dialysis. A supplemental dose (approx. half the daily dose) may be required on dialysis days.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
**Topiramate** exerts anticonvulsant and migraine-prophylactic activity through four properties:
1. Blockade of voltage-sensitive sodium channels (state-dependent).
2. Enhancement of GABA activity at specific GABAA receptors (benzodiazepine-insensitive site).
3. Antagonism of the kainate/AMPA subtype of glutamate receptors.
4. Weak inhibition of carbonic anhydrase isoenzymes (specifically II and IV).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapid; bioavailability ~81%. Not affected by food. Tmax ~2–3 hours.
- **Distribution:** 13–17% protein bound. Low capacity binding to erythrocytes.
- **Metabolism:** ~20% metabolised in healthy volunteers; up to 50% in patients on enzyme inducers.
- **Elimination:** Primarily renal (81% as unchanged drug and metabolites). Elimination half-life is ~21 hours.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Epilepsy Monotherapy:** Trials (YI, EPMN-104) demonstrated superior efficacy of high-dose (400–500 mg/day) over low-dose (25–50 mg/day) regimens in achieving seizure-free status.
- **Migraine Prophylaxis:** Two pivotal trials demonstrated a 51% and 49% median reduction in monthly migraine rates for 100 mg and 200 mg/day respectively, compared to 21% for placebo. Efficacy was comparable to propranolol 160 mg/day.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- Store below 25°C.
- Keep in original packaging to protect from moisture.
- **Shelf life:** Refer to the expiry date on the packaging [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
