# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction:
    - **United States**: Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical and regulatory data into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis and clinical summary; it is not a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific Product Information required for prescribing. For jurisdictions not listed above (e.g., Australia, Europe, Canada, Japan), please consult local regulatory authorities as information was not specified in the available sources.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class**: Carrier molecule for mitochondrial fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01 (Not specified in available sources; retrieved from external pharmacological classification).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**:
    - **Intravenous Injection**: 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Metabolic Disorders**: Acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with end-stage renal disease who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### 3.1 Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (over 2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Severe Metabolic Crisis**: A loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency**: Administer every 3 or 4 hours; never less than every 6 hours [US].
- **Maximum Dose**: 300 mg/kg/day [US].
- **Monitoring**: Obtain baseline plasma carnitine concentrations. Weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

### 3.2 ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration**: Administer as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration**: Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### 3.3 Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and initiate medical management [US].
- **Seizures**: Levocarnitine may increase seizure frequency or severity in patients with pre-existing seizure activity. Seizures have also been reported in patients without a prior history [US].
- **Accumulation of Toxic Metabolites**: Chronic high-dose oral administration in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US]. Note: This is primarily associated with oral formulations but warrants caution in renal impairment.
- **Monitoring**: Monitor INR in patients receiving concomitant warfarin therapy [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### 6.1 Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Population)
Adverse events reported with a frequency ≥5% in controlled trials, regardless of causality [US]:

| Body System | Very Common (>10%) | Common (1–10%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Chest pain, Flu syndrome, Headache, Infection, Injection site reaction, Pain | Abdominal pain, Accidental injury, Allergic reaction, Asthenia, Back pain, Fever |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension, Hypotension | Arrhythmia, Atrial fibrillation, Tachycardia, Palpitations |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea, Vomiting | Anorexia, Constipation, Dyspepsia, Nausea |
| **Metabolic** | Hypercalcemia (at 10mg dose) | Hyperkalemia, Hypervolemia, Peripheral edema, Weight change |
| **Nervous** | Dizziness, Paresthesia | Depression, Insomnia, Vertigo |
| **Respiratory** | Cough, Dyspnea, Pharyngitis, Rhinitis | Bronchitis, Sinusitis |
| **Other** | Pruritus | Rash, Amblyopia, Taste perversion |

### 6.2 Postmarketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal**: Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis.
- **Neurologic**: Seizures (with or without pre-existing history) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity**: Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports of increased International Normalized Ratio (INR) have occurred when used concurrently with levocarnitine. Monitor INR levels upon initiation or dose adjustment [US].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions**: Levocarnitine is not bound to plasma protein or albumin; therefore, displacement interactions are unlikely [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: [US: Category not specified in source; Animal studies in rats/rabbits at 3.8x human dose showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm]. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Levocarnitine is naturally present in human milk. Supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations following administration. Risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against maternal benefit [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing for metabolic disorders is based on mg/kg body weight (see Section 3.1) [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment**: Safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. In ESRD patients, IV levocarnitine is used post-dialysis to replace losses [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action**: Facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics**:
    - **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism**: Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (feces) [US].
    - **Elimination**: Terminal half-life is approximately 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics**: Increases plasma levocarnitine concentrations; in ESRD, replaces carnitine lost during dialysis and helps correct the acylcarnitine/carnitine ratio [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies**: In trials of ESRD patients undergoing hemodialysis for at least 6 months, IV administration of 20 mg/kg three times weekly resulted in pre-dialysis (trough) carnitine levels of 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks, compared to a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Data**: Trough concentrations increased proportionally with doses of 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Special Conditions**: Avoid excessive heat. Protect from freezing [US].
- **Compatibility**: Stable for 24 hours in PVC bags when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentration range 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
