# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- The information contained in this editorial synthesis is derived from the official Product Information provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **levocarnitine** injection for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and clinical resource; it is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. 

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (USP); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Carrier molecule/amino acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Levocarnitine Injection (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly stated in source but standard for levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Indicated for the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that lead to secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often given, followed by an equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours, and no less frequently than every 6 hours, either by infusion or intravenous injection [US].
- **Maintenance:** Subsequent daily doses are typically 50 mg/kg, though higher doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered if clinically required [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration:** Administered as a slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment:** Adjustments should be guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Monitoring:** It is recommended to obtain plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) prior to and during therapy (weekly/monthly) alongside blood chemistries and vital signs [US].
- **Inspection:** Visually inspect for particulate matter and discoloration before use [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None Known:** There are no known contraindications listed in the available source [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizure Risk:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **General Renal Precaution:** The safety and efficacy of oral levocarnitine have not been evaluated in patients with renal insufficiency. High oral doses in patients with severely compromised renal function or ESRD may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common Adverse Effects (≥5%)
Based on clinical trials in ESRD patients, the following events occurred at a frequency ≥5% regardless of causality [US]:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (21%), headache (37%), injection site reaction (38%), pain (35%), fever (12%), flu syndrome (29%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (21%), hypotension (19%), tachycardia (9%), chest pain (15%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (35%), vomiting (21%), nausea (12%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (18%), paresthesia (12%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (27%), cough increase (18%), dyspnea (11%).

### Other Reported Effects
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor and gastritis occur less frequently [US].
- **Neurologic:** Seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. Monitoring of INR is recommended after initiating treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category C (language equivalent)]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) revealed no evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm. However, there are no adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Specific studies in nursing mothers have not been conducted. While studies in dairy cows show increased carnitine in milk after administration, the risk of excess intake in a human infant must be weighed against the benefit of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug may be considered [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility was found in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Dosing is weight-based (50 mg/kg for metabolic disorders); see Section 3 [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the source document.
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on hemodialysis. Monitor for metabolite accumulation if oral forms are used in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US]. 
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites include trimethylamine N-oxide (urine) and $\gamma$-butyrobetaine (faeces) [US].
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), the mean terminal half-life is 17.4 hours. Approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours (uncorrected for endogenous carnitine) [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg three times weekly), mean pre-dialysis levocarnitine concentrations increased from ~20 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks. While efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is demonstrated, the impact on clinical outcomes in the ESRD population has not been determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F).
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative [US].
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL) [US].
