# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** United States (FDA)
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical, regulatory, and pharmacological data to facilitate safe and effective prescribing.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information (PI) or Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of this review, only the United States (FDA) product information was provided for analysis.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative (quaternary ammonium compound).
- **Brand Names:** Not specified in available sources [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile injection, 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of patients with a confirmed inborn error of metabolism resulting in secondary carnitine deficiency [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD undergoing maintenance hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult and Paediatric)
- **Metabolic Disorders:** 
    - The recommended dose is 50 mg/kg, administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
    - **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often administered, followed by the equivalent dose divided over the subsequent 24 hours.
    - **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours and never less than every 6 hours.
    - **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered in clinical practice [US].
- **ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis:** 
    - **Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) administered as a slow 2- to 3-minute bolus into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
    - **Dose Adjustments:** Reductions (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations.

### Administration and Monitoring
- **Inspection:** Parenteral products must be inspected visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Stability:** Stable when diluted in 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer’s at concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8.0 mg/mL. Use within 24 hours if stored at room temperature in PVC bags [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Discontinue immediately if severe reactions occur [US].
- **Seizure Activity:** Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure disorders. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity may occur [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Caution):** While this PI refers to the IV form, it is noted that high doses of *oral* levocarnitine in patients with severely compromised renal function may lead to the accumulation of potentially toxic metabolites (trimethylamine [TMA] and trimethylamine-N-oxide [TMAO]) [US].
- **Monitoring:** INR should be monitored in patients receiving concurrent warfarin therapy (see Section 7).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse event profiles vary significantly based on the underlying pathology (ESRD vs. metabolic disorders).

### Clinical Trial Data (ESRD Patients on Dialysis)
Common adverse effects (≥5%) reported in placebo-controlled trials [US]:
- **General:** Injection site reaction (33%), pain (30%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), flu syndrome (25%), headache (22%), asthenia (9%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhea (16%), vomiting (15%), nausea (8%), abdominal pain (9%), anorexia (5%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%), rhinitis (9%).
- **Nervous System:** Dizziness (13%), insomnia (4%), paresthesia (5%).
- **Metabolic/Nutritional:** Hypercalcemia (9%), hyperkalemia (6%), hypervolemia (5%).

### Postmarketing Experience
- **Gastrointestinal:** Transient nausea and vomiting; body odor.
- **Neurological:** Seizures.
- **Immune System:** Anaphylaxis.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in the International Normalised Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is used concurrently with warfarin. 
    - **Management:** Monitor INR levels at the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients on warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** No evidence of impaired fertility or foetal harm was observed in rat and rabbit studies at doses up to 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area). 
    - **Recommendation:** Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed. No adequate, well-controlled studies in pregnant women exist [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - **Data:** Levocarnitine is a normal component of human milk. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk concentrations following exogenous administration.
    - **Recommendation:** Risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother. Discontinuation of nursing or the drug should be considered [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy for metabolic disorders are established; dosing follows the mg/kg weight-based protocol (see Section 3) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically addressed in the available source; general dosing principles for ESRD apply where relevant.
- **Renal Impairment:** IV levocarnitine is specifically indicated for ESRD. It is removed by dialysis [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In ESRD, it alleviates metabolic abnormalities associated with low carnitine levels, though effects on clinical outcomes (e.g., muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy) have not been definitively determined in this population [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine.
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Terminal elimination half-life is approximately 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Efficacy:** A 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients receiving 20 mg/kg three times weekly post-dialysis showed trough levocarnitine levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** ESRD patients receiving 10, 20, or 40 mg/kg post-dialysis demonstrated dose-proportional increases in trough carnitine levels at 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C.
- **Protection:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Handling:** Single-dose vials; discard any unused portion as the formulation contains no preservative.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine).
