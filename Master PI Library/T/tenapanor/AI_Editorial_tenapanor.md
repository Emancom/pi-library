# Editorial Product Information: Tenapanor

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising regulatory data into a clinically relevant format.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not intended as a substitute for the official PI required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult the local approved PI for complete prescribing details.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tenapanor hydrochloride (a sodium/hydrogen exchanger 3 [NHE3] inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **XPHOZAH** [US].
- **ATC Code:** A02X (Other drugs for acid-related disorders - Note: Tenapanor for IBS-C is often A06AX08; a specific code for hyperphosphatemia may vary).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Film-coated tablets: 10 mg, 20 mg, and 30 mg [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Ardelyx, Inc. [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Hyperphosphatemia in Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD):** Indicated to reduce serum phosphorus in adults with CKD on dialysis. It is specifically approved as add-on therapy for patients who have had an inadequate response to phosphate binders or who are intolerant of any dose of phosphate binder therapy [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adult):** The recommended dosage is 30 mg taken orally twice daily [US].
- **Administration Instructions:** 
    - Tablets should be taken immediately prior to the first and last meals of the day [US].
    - On dialysis days: Patients should not take a dose immediately before a haemodialysis session. Instead, the dose should be taken just before the next meal following dialysis [US].
- **Titration:** Serum phosphorus levels and gastrointestinal tolerability should be monitored, with dosage adjusted as necessary to achieve target levels and manage side effects [US].
- **Missed Dose Instructions:** If a dose is missed, patients should skip the missed dose and resume the next dose at the regular time. Two doses should not be taken simultaneously [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Paediatric Population:** Contraindicated in patients under 6 years of age due to the risk of severe diarrhea and life-threatening dehydration [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Obstruction:** Contraindicated in patients with known or suspected mechanical gastrointestinal obstruction [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Diarrhea:** This is the most common adverse reaction. It may be severe, leading to dehydration and hyponatremia. In clinical trials, severe diarrhea was reported in 5% of patients. Treatment should be discontinued if severe diarrhea develops [US].
- **Hydration Monitoring:** Patients should be advised to maintain adequate fluid intake and report signs of dehydration [US].
- **Gastrointestinal Tolerability:** Dosage reduction or interruption may be required to manage gastrointestinal side effects [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
- **Very Common (>10%):** Diarrhea (reported in 43% to 53% of patients). Diarrhea typically occurs early in treatment but can occur at any time [US].
- **Common (1–10%):** Severe diarrhea (5%) [US].
- **Less than 1%:** Dehydration and hyponatremia associated with severe diarrhea [US].
- **Postmarketing Experience:** 
    - Hypersensitivity reactions (pruritus, rash, and urticaria) [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **OATP2B1 Substrates (e.g., Enalapril):** Tenapanor is an inhibitor of the intestinal uptake transporter OATP2B1. Co-administration can significantly reduce the systemic exposure of OATP2B1 substrates. For example, enalapril AUC was reduced by 50–65%. While a lower starting dose of enalapril is generally not required for CKD patients on dialysis (due to inherently higher exposures in this population), clinicians should monitor for loss of efficacy of the substrate drug and adjust dosages as needed [US].
- **Sodium Polystyrene Sulfonate (SPS):** Administer tenapanor at least 3 hours before or 3 hours after SPS to avoid potential binding interactions [US].
- **Phosphate Binders:** No clinically significant pharmacodynamic interactions were observed with sevelamer carbonate [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not formally assigned under current labeling rules, but data from a small number of pregnant women have not identified drug-associated risks. 
    - **Risk Summary:** Tenapanor is minimally absorbed systemically; maternal use is not expected to result in fetal exposure. Animal studies showed no adverse developmental effects at doses significantly higher than the human equivalent [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - Tenapanor and its metabolite M1 were not detected in the milk of lactating women following oral administration. Minimal systemic absorption suggests no exposure to the breastfed infant is expected [US].
- **Fertility:** 
    - Animal studies in rats and mice showed no effect on fertility or reproductive function [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness have not been established in patients <18 years. It is strictly contraindicated in children <6 years [US].
- **Elderly Use:** In clinical trials, 28% of participants were 65 years or older. No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between these patients and younger patients [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** The drug is specifically indicated for patients with CKD on dialysis. Pharmacokinetics of the metabolite M1 in patients with an eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 m² were similar to those in healthy subjects [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Minimal systemic bioavailability was observed in patients with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B). No dosage adjustment is explicitly required based on current data [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tenapanor is a locally acting inhibitor of the sodium/hydrogen exchanger 3 (NHE3). By inhibiting NHE3 on the apical surface of the small intestine and colon, it reduces sodium absorption. This, in turn, decreases phosphate absorption by reducing paracellular phosphate permeability [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** 
    - **Food Effect:** Taking the drug 5–10 minutes before a meal increases 24-hour stool phosphorus excretion compared to the fed or fasting state [US].
    - **Cardiac Electrophysiology:** No clinically relevant effects on the QTc interval were observed at 3 times the maximum exposure of the M1 metabolite [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:** 
    - **Absorption:** Minimally absorbed; plasma concentrations are typically below the limit of quantification (<0.5 ng/mL) [US].
    - **Distribution:** Approximately 99% plasma protein bound [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by CYP3A4/5. The major metabolite, M1, is pharmacologically inactive [US].
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted in feces (approx. 70% within 120 hours). Approximately 9% is recovered in urine, mainly as metabolites [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Monotherapy (Studies TEN-02-201 & TEN-02-301):** Evaluated tenapanor in CKD patients on dialysis with serum phosphorus between 6.0 and 10.0 mg/dL. In Study 301, phosphorus levels rose by 0.7 mg/dL in the placebo group during the withdrawal phase compared to those remaining on tenapanor (p=0.002) [US].
- **Add-on Therapy (Study TEN-02-202):** Evaluated tenapanor as an add-on to stable phosphate binder therapy (phosphorus ≥5.5 mg/dL). Serum phosphorus decreased by 0.7 mg/dL in the tenapanor group compared to placebo over 4 weeks (p=0.0004) [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted between 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Keep the bottle tightly closed and protect from moisture. Do not remove the silica gel desiccant from the bottle [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription Only Medicine) [US].
