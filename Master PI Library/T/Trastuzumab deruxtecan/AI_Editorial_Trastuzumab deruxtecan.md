# Editorial Product Information: Trastuzumab deruxtecan

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following provided jurisdiction: **Australian** (Therapeutic Goods Administration).
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data into a single, coherent reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing within a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trastuzumab deruxtecan.
- **Chemical Class:** Antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) comprising a humanised anti-HER2 IgG1 monoclonal antibody covalently linked to deruxtecan (a topoisomerase I inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **ENHERTU** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01FD04.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Lyophilised powder for injection (100 mg single-use vial). After reconstitution with 5 mL of sterile water for injection, the resulting solution contains 20 mg/mL [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** AstraZeneca Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**ENHERTU** is indicated as monotherapy for the following adult populations [Australian]:

- **Metastatic Breast Cancer (HER2-positive):** Treatment of patients with unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive breast cancer who have received prior trastuzumab and a taxane for metastatic disease, or developed recurrence during or within six months of completing (neo)adjuvant therapy.
- **Metastatic Breast Cancer (HER2-low):** Treatment of patients with unresectable or metastatic HER2-low (IHC 1+ or IHC 2+/ISH-negative) breast cancer who have received prior chemotherapy in the metastatic setting or developed recurrence during or within six months of completing adjuvant chemotherapy. HR+ patients should be ineligible for further endocrine therapy.
- **Metastatic Breast Cancer (HER2-low and HER2-ultralow):** Treatment of HR+ patients with HER2-low or HER2-ultralow (IHC 0 with membrane staining) breast cancer who have received at least one endocrine therapy in the metastatic setting and are unsuitable for further endocrine treatment.
- **Gastric Cancer (HER2-positive):** Treatment of locally advanced or metastatic HER2-positive gastric or gastroesophageal junction (GEJ) adenocarcinoma in patients who have received a prior anti-HER2-based regimen (Provisional Approval) [Australian].
- **HER2-positive Solid Tumours:** Treatment of unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive (IHC 3+) solid tumours in patients who have received prior systemic treatment and have no satisfactory alternative options (Provisional Approval) [Australian].
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):** Treatment of unresectable or metastatic NSCLC in patients whose tumours have activating HER2 (ERBB2) mutations and who have received prior platinum-based chemotherapy (Provisional Approval) [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing:** Administered once every 3 weeks (21-day cycle).
- **Indication-Specific Dosing:**
    - **Breast Cancer, NSCLC, and Solid Tumours:** 5.4 mg/kg.
    - **Gastric Cancer:** 6.4 mg/kg.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - **Initial Dose:** 90-minute intravenous infusion.
    - **Subsequent Doses:** 30-minute infusion if the initial dose was well-tolerated.
    - **Do NOT** administer as an IV push or bolus.
    - **Premedication:** **ENHERTU** is emetogenic; patients should be premedicated with a two- or three-drug combination (e.g., dexamethasone, 5-HT3 antagonist, and/or NK1 antagonist) for nausea/vomiting prophylaxis.
- **Dose Modifications:** Required for Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD), neutropenia, febrile neutropenia, and LVEF decrease. Dose reductions are permanent; do not re-escalate. 
    - **Reduction Levels (5.4 mg/kg starting dose):** 4.4 mg/kg, then 3.2 mg/kg.
    - **Reduction Levels (6.4 mg/kg starting dose):** 5.4 mg/kg, then 4.4 mg/kg.
- **Missed Dose:** Administer as soon as possible; do not wait until the next planned cycle. Maintain a 3-week interval between doses.

## 4. Contraindications
- None specified in available sources [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD) / Pneumonitis:** Cases can be fatal. Monitor for cough, dyspnoea, and fever.
    - **Asymptomatic (Grade 1):** Withhold dose and consider corticosteroids.
    - **Symptomatic (Grade 2+):** Permanently discontinue and initiate systemic corticosteroids.
- **Neutropenia:** Grade 3 or 4 neutropenia is common. Monitor CBC prior to each dose.
- **Left Ventricular Dysfunction:** LVEF decrease has been observed. Assess LVEF prior to initiation and at regular intervals. Permanently discontinue if LVEF is <40% or if absolute decrease from baseline is >20% (confirmed).
- **Embryo-fetal Toxicity:** Can cause fetal harm. Verify pregnancy status before starting.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse effect frequencies are based on pooled safety populations (5.4 mg/kg and 6.4 mg/kg) [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Reactions |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Nausea, fatigue, alopecia, vomiting, neutropenia, anaemia, constipation, decreased appetite, diarrhoea, transaminases increased, musculoskeletal pain, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, abdominal pain, stomatitis, headache, cough, and upper respiratory tract infection. |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Hypokalaemia, weight decreased, pyrexia, dizziness, dysgeusia, dyspepsia, rash, pruritus, dry eye, and blurred vision. |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Febrile neutropenia, infusion-related reactions. |

**Serious Adverse Effects:**
- **ILD/Pneumonitis:** The most common serious reaction and most frequent cause of permanent discontinuation.
- **Fatalities:** Most commonly attributed to ILD/pneumonitis (1% at 5.4 mg/kg; 2.3% at 6.4 mg/kg) [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** 
    - The released topoisomerase I inhibitor is a substrate for **P-gp**, **OATP1B1**, **OATP1B3**, **MATE2K**, **MRP1**, and **BCRP**. 
    - Coadministration with ritonavir (OATP1B/CYP3A inhibitor) or itraconazole (strong CYP3A inhibitor) slightly increases exposure of the ADC and the released payload, but no dose adjustment is required.
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** No clinically meaningful drug-drug interactions are expected with substrates of OAT1 or OATP1B1.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [Australian Category D]. There are no human data, but the topoisomerase I inhibitor is genotoxic and can cause embryo-fetal harm. Advise effective contraception for females (during and for 7 months after last dose) and males with pregnant partners or partners of childbearing potential (during and for 4 months after last dose).
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if the drug is excreted in human milk. Discontinue breastfeeding prior to treatment; it may be resumed 7 months after the final dose.
- **Fertility:** Animal studies suggest **ENHERTU** may impair male reproductive function and fertility.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in children <18 years have not been established.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients ≥65 years.
- **Renal Impairment:** No adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Data for severe impairment (CLcr <30 mL/min) are limited, and no recommended dosage has been established.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No adjustment for mild impairment. No data for moderate/severe impairment; monitor closely.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** A HER2-targeted ADC. The antibody binds to HER2 on tumour cells, is internalised, and undergoes lysosomal cleavage. The released topoisomerase I inhibitor (an exatecan derivative) causes DNA damage and apoptosis. The payload is membrane-permeable, allowing for a "bystander effect" on neighbouring cells.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Volume of distribution of central compartment (Vc) is 2.68 L.
    - **Metabolism:** mAb is catabolised into small peptides; the payload is metabolised mainly by **CYP3A4**.
    - **Excretion:** Payload is primarily excreted in faeces via the biliary route. Median elimination half-life is 5.4–5.7 days.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** No clinically meaningful effect on QTc interval at 6.4 mg/kg.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **DESTINY-Breast01/02/03:** Demonstrated efficacy in HER2-positive mBC. In **DESTINY-Breast03**, **ENHERTU** showed superior PFS (28.8 vs 6.8 months) compared to trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1).
- **DESTINY-Breast04/06:** Demonstrated efficacy in HER2-low mBC. **DESTINY-Breast04** showed improved OS (23.4 vs 16.8 months) vs chemotherapy.
- **DESTINY-Gastric01:** Showed improved ORR (40.5% vs 11.3%) and OS (12.5 vs 8.4 months) compared to physician's choice of chemotherapy in gastric cancer.
- **DESTINY-Lung02:** Established the 5.4 mg/kg dose for HER2-mutant NSCLC based on a more favourable benefit-risk profile compared to 6.4 mg/kg.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vials:** Store in a refrigerator (2°C to 8°C). Do not freeze. Store in original carton to protect from light.
- **Reconstituted Solution:** Use immediately; if necessary, store refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) for up to 24 hours.
- **Diluted Solution (in 5% Dextrose):** Use immediately. Can be stored at room temperature for up to 4 hours or refrigerated for up to 24 hours. **Do not use sodium chloride solution.**
