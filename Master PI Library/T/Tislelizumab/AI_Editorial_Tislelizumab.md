# Editorial Product Information: Tislelizumab

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of **tislelizumab** for use by healthcare professionals. It synthesises clinical data, regulatory warnings, and administration guidelines into a single reference.
- **Disclaimer:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official PI for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. Clinicians should consult local regulatory documents before initiating therapy.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tislelizumab (rch).
- **Chemical Class:** Humanized immunoglobulin G4 (IgG4) variant monoclonal antibody; Antineoplastic agent (PD-1 inhibitor).
- **Brand Name:** **Tevimbra** [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** L01FF09.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Concentrate for solution for infusion: 100 mg of tislelizumab in 10 mL (10 mg/mL) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous (IV) infusion only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** BeOne Medicines AUS Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
**Tislelizumab** is indicated for the following [Australian-approved]:

- **Oesophageal Squamous Cell Carcinoma (OSCC):**
    - First-line treatment in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy for patients with unresectable, locally advanced, or metastatic OSCC with PD-L1 expression $\ge$ 1%.
    - Monotherapy for adult patients with unresectable, recurrent, locally advanced, or metastatic OSCC after prior chemotherapy.
- **Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC):**
    - First-line treatment (in combination with pemetrexed and platinum) for locally advanced or metastatic non-squamous NSCLC with PD-L1 expression $\ge$ 50%, provided no EGFR or ALK genomic tumour aberrations are present.
    - First-line treatment (in combination with carboplatin and either paclitaxel or nab-paclitaxel) for locally advanced or metastatic squamous NSCLC.
    - Monotherapy for locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC after prior chemotherapy.
- **Gastric/Gastro-oesophageal Junction (G/GOJ) Adenocarcinoma:**
    - First-line treatment (in combination with platinum and fluoropyrimidine-based chemotherapy) for HER-2 negative locally advanced unresectable or metastatic G/GOJ adenocarcinoma with PD-L1 expression $\ge$ 1%.
- **Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma (NPC):**
    - First-line treatment (in combination with gemcitabine and cisplatin) for recurrent or metastatic NPC.

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Monotherapy (Second-line OSCC):** 150 mg every 2 weeks, 200 mg every 3 weeks, or 300 mg every 4 weeks [Australian].
- **Monotherapy (Second-line NSCLC):** 200 mg every 3 weeks [Australian].
- **Combination Therapy:** Dosing depends on the specific regimen, typically 200 mg every 3 weeks (or matched to the chemotherapy cycle) [Australian].

### Administration Instructions
- **Preparation:** Dilute in 0.9% sodium chloride to a final concentration of 2 to 5 mg/mL. Do not shake.
- **Infusion Rate:** 
    - **150 mg / 200 mg:** First infusion over 60 minutes; if tolerated, subsequent infusions over 30 minutes.
    - **300 mg:** First infusion over 90 minutes; second over 60 minutes; subsequent over 30 minutes.
- **Sequence:** When used in combination, administer **tislelizumab** prior to chemotherapy on the same day.
- **Filter:** Use a sterile, non-pyrogenic, low-protein binding 0.2 or 0.22-micron in-line or add-on filter.

### Treatment Modifications
Dose reductions are not recommended. Treatment should be withheld or permanently discontinued based on the severity of immune-related adverse reactions (irADRs) (see section 5).

## 4. Contraindications
- Hypersensitivity to **tislelizumab** or any of the excipients (sodium citrate dihydrate, citric acid monohydrate, histidine, trehalose dihydrate, polysorbate-20).

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### General Warnings
- **Immune-Related Adverse Reactions (irADRs):** Severe and fatal cases of pneumonitis and hepatitis have occurred. Reactions can affect any organ system and may occur after the last dose. 
- **Monitoring:** Perform liver function tests (LFTs), thyroid function tests, and renal function (serum creatinine) at baseline and periodically during treatment.
- **Patient Card:** All patients must be provided with a Patient Card and counselled on the risks of irADRs.

### Specific Clinical Precautions
- **Pneumonitis:** Monitor for cough, dyspnoea, or hypoxia. Rule out infectious aetiologies.
- **Hepatitis:** Monitor for jaundice and LFT elevations.
- **Endocrinopathies:** Includes hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, thyroiditis, adrenal insufficiency, hypophysitis, and Type 1 diabetes mellitus.
- **Severe Cutaneous Adverse Reactions (SCARs):** Including SJS/TEN. Permanently discontinue for confirmed SCARs.
- **Other irADRs:** Cases of myocarditis, nephritis, colitis, pancreatitis, myositis, and haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) have been reported.
- **Solid Organ Transplant Rejection:** PD-1 inhibitors may increase the risk of rejection.
- **Infusion-Related Reactions:** Monitor for signs of anaphylaxis. For Grade 3 or 4 reactions, permanently discontinue.

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency Summary (Pooled Data)
- **Very Common (>10%):** Fatigue, increased AST, increased ALT, anaemia, decreased appetite, nausea, diarrhoea, rash, pruritus, hypothyroidism, cough, and pyrexia.
- **Common (1–10%):** Dyspnoea, pneumonitis, vomiting, stomatitis, weight decreased, hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, and hyperthyroidism.

### Serious and Fatal Reactions
- Fatalities have been reported due to pneumonitis (0.1–0.3%), hepatitis (0.1%), myocarditis (0.2%), and dyspnoea [Australian].
- Serious adverse events occurred in 41–48% of patients in pivotal combination trials [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:** No formal studies conducted. As a monoclonal antibody, it is not metabolised by CYP450 enzymes; interactions with small-molecule drugs are not anticipated.
- **Systemic Corticosteroids:** Avoid use at baseline (prednisone >10 mg/day or equivalent) as it may interfere with pharmacodynamic activity. However, corticosteroids can be used to manage irADRs after treatment initiation.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** **Australian Category D.** **Tislelizumab** can cause fetal harm. Animal models show PD-1 blockade disrupts fetal tolerance. Use is not recommended unless the clinical benefit outweighs the potential risk.
- **Contraception:** Females of reproductive potential must use effective contraception during and for at least 4 months after the final dose.
- **Breastfeeding:** Excretion in human milk is unknown; however, IgG4 is known to be present. Discontinue breastfeeding during and for 4 months post-treatment.
- **Fertility:** No clinical or non-clinical data available.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy not established in patients < 18 years.
- **Elderly Use:** No dose adjustment required for patients $\ge$ 65 years. Data for patients $\ge$ 75 years are limited.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. Data for severe impairment are too limited for conclusions.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. The effect of severe hepatic impairment is unknown.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** **Tislelizumab** binds the extracellular domain of human PD-1 with high affinity ($K_D = 0.15$ nM). It blocks the interaction with PD-L1 and PD-L2, enhancing T-cell functional activity. It is engineered to minimize binding to Fc$\gamma$Rs, reducing antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC).
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Steady-state volume of distribution (Vd) is 6.42 L.
    - **Metabolism:** Degraded into small peptides/amino acids via catabolic pathways.
    - **Elimination:** Clearance is 0.153 L/day; terminal half-life is approximately 23.8 days.
    - **Steady State:** Reached at approximately 12 weeks.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **OSCC:** 
    - **RATIONALE-306:** First-line combination therapy showed significantly improved OS (16.8 months vs 9.6 months in PD-L1 $\ge$ 1%). 
    - **RATIONALE-302:** Second-line monotherapy showed OS benefit (8.6 months vs 6.3 months).
- **NSCLC:** 
    - **RATIONALE-304:** First-line non-squamous (T+chemo) showed improved PFS (9.7 vs 7.6 months).
    - **RATIONALE-307:** First-line squamous (T+chemo) showed significantly improved PFS (7.7 vs 5.5 months).
    - **RATIONALE-303:** Second-line monotherapy showed OS benefit (17.2 vs 11.9 months).
- **G/GOJ:** **RATIONALE-305** showed OS benefit in ITT and PD-L1 $\ge$ 5% populations.
- **NPC:** **RATIONALE-309** showed improved PFS (9.2 vs 7.4 months).

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Vial:** Store refrigerated at 2°C to 8°C. Do not freeze. Protect from light.
- **In-use Stability:** Once diluted, the solution is stable for up to 10 days at 2°C to 8°C. This period includes the time for the solution to return to room temperature and the 4-hour window required to complete the infusion.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Prescription Only Medicine (Schedule 4) [Australian].
