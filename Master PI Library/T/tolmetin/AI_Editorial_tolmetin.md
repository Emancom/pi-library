# Editorial Product Information: Tolmetin

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonized editorial overview of tolmetin product information for use by healthcare professionals.
- Note: This is an editorial synthesis intended for reference and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Tolmetin sodium (as dihydrate).
- **Chemical Class:** Nonselective non-steroidal anti-inflammatory agent (NSAID); pyrrole-acetic acid derivative.
- **Brand Name(s):** Tolmetin Sodium [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources (M01AB03).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 
    - Oral Tablet: 200 mg (equivalent to 246 mg tolmetin sodium dihydrate) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Lifsa Drugs LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Relief of signs and symptoms, including treatment of acute flares and long-term management of chronic disease [US].
- **Osteoarthritis:** Relief of signs and symptoms, including treatment of acute flares and long-term management of chronic disease [US].
- **Juvenile Rheumatoid Arthritis (JRA):** Treatment of signs and symptoms in pediatric patients aged 2 years and older [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Adult Population
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis and Osteoarthritis:** 
    - **Initial Dose:** 400 mg three times daily (1200 mg/day). It is recommended to include one dose upon arising and one at bedtime [US].
    - **Maintenance Dose:** Adjusted based on patient response, typically ranging from 600 mg to 1800 mg daily in divided doses (usually three times daily).
    - **Maximum Dose:** 1800 mg/day; doses exceeding this limit have not been studied and are not recommended [US].

### Pediatric Population (2 years and older)
- **Juvenile Rheumatoid Arthritis:**
    - **Initial Dose:** 20 mg/kg/day in divided doses (3 or 4 times daily).
    - **Maintenance Dose:** Usually ranges from 15 to 30 mg/kg/day.
    - **Maximum Dose:** 30 mg/kg/day; doses exceeding this limit have not been studied [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Clinical response is typically expected within a few days to one week.
- If gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms occur, tolmetin may be administered with antacids (excluding sodium bicarbonate).
- Bioavailability is reduced when taken with food or milk [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- Known hypersensitivity to tolmetin sodium [US].
- History of asthma, urticaria, or allergic-type reactions after taking aspirin or other NSAIDs (severe, rarely fatal anaphylactic-like reactions have occurred) [US].
- In the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery [US].
- History of serious skin reactions to NSAIDs [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
### Boxed Warnings [US]
- **Cardiovascular (CV) Thrombotic Events:** NSAIDs cause an increased risk of serious CV thrombotic events, including myocardial infarction and stroke, which can be fatal. This risk may increase with duration of use and dose.
- **Gastrointestinal (GI) Risk:** NSAIDs increase the risk of serious GI adverse events including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation of the stomach or intestines, which can be fatal. These may occur at any time without warning. Elderly patients are at greater risk.

### General Warnings
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Borderline elevations of liver tests occur in up to 15% of patients. Severe reactions, including jaundice and fatal fulminant hepatitis, have been reported. Discontinue if liver disease clinical signs develop [US].
- **Hypertension:** NSAIDs can lead to the onset of new hypertension or worsening of pre-existing hypertension. Monitor blood pressure closely during therapy [US].
- **Heart Failure and Edema:** Fluid retention and edema have been observed. Use with caution in patients with severe heart failure [US].
- **Renal Effects:** Long-term use may result in renal papillary necrosis. Acute interstitial nephritis with hematuria and proteinuria has been reported. Tolmetin is not recommended in patients with advanced renal disease [US].
- **Anaphylactoid Reactions:** May occur in patients without known prior exposure, particularly those in the "aspirin triad" [US].
- **Serious Skin Reactions:** Can cause exfoliative dermatitis, Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), and Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS). Discontinue at the first appearance of skin rash [US].

### Monitoring Requirements
- Periodically check CBC and chemistry profiles in patients on long-term therapy [US].
- Monitor renal function in patients with heart failure, liver dysfunction, or those taking diuretics/ACE inhibitors [US].
- Ophthalmologic evaluation is recommended if visual disturbances occur [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are typical of the NSAID class. In clinical trials, approximately 10% of patients discontinued therapy due to adverse effects, primarily GI related.

| **Very Common (>10%)** | Gastrointestinal | Nausea (11%) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Gastrointestinal | Dyspepsia, abdominal pain, diarrhea, flatulence, vomiting, constipation, gastritis |

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Fatal GI bleeding and perforation.
- Fatal MI and stroke.
- Fulminant hepatitis.
- Life-threatening skin reactions (SJS/TEN/DRESS).

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **ACE Inhibitors/ARBs:** NSAIDs may diminish the antihypertensive effect; risk of renal impairment [US].
- **Aspirin:** Concomitant use is not recommended due to increased risk of GI bleeding and lack of additional benefit [US].
- **Diuretics:** Tolmetin may reduce the natriuretic effect of furosemide and thiazides [US].
- **Lithium:** NSAIDs increase plasma lithium levels (approx. 15% increase) and reduce renal clearance; monitor for lithium toxicity [US].
- **Methotrexate:** NSAIDs may enhance methotrexate toxicity by inhibiting renal accumulation [US].
- **Warfarin/Anticoagulants:** Synergistic risk of GI bleeding; monitor prothrombin time/INR [US].
- **Corticosteroids:** Increased risk of GI ulceration/bleeding [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:**
    - **General:** NSAIDs should be avoided at 20 weeks gestation or later. Use between 20–30 weeks should be limited to the lowest dose for the shortest duration.
    - **Fetal Toxicity:** Use at 30 weeks gestation and later is associated with premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus. Use at 20 weeks or later may cause fetal renal dysfunction leading to oligohydramnios [US].
- **Labor and Delivery:** Effects in humans are unknown; in animal studies, NSAIDs increased dystocia and delayed parturition [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Tolmetin is secreted in human milk. A decision should be made to discontinue nursing or the drug, considering the importance of the drug to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** Animal studies showed no impairment of fertility [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness not established in children under 2 years of age [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Increased risk for GI bleeding and renal complications. Use with caution and consider lower effective doses [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not recommended in advanced renal disease. Monitor renal function closely if therapy is necessary [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Monitor for signs of worsening liver function; discontinue if clinical symptoms of liver disease develop [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Tolmetin is a nonselective NSAID with anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antipyretic activities. It inhibits prostaglandin synthetase and lowers plasma levels of prostaglandin E [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Rapid and almost complete; peak plasma levels reached within 30–60 minutes. Bioavailability is decreased by 16% when taken with food/milk [US].
    - **Distribution:** Highly protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolized into inactive oxidative metabolites or conjugates.
    - **Elimination:** Biphasic; rapid phase (half-life 1–2 hours) followed by a slower phase (half-life ~5 hours). Excreted almost entirely in urine within 24 hours [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Does not appear to alter the course of the underlying rheumatoid disease [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Rheumatoid Arthritis/Osteoarthritis:** Controlled studies demonstrated tolmetin is as effective as aspirin and indomethacin. Tolmetin showed a lower frequency of tinnitus and mild GI effects compared to aspirin, and fewer CNS effects than indomethacin [US].
- **Juvenile Rheumatoid Arthritis:** Comparative trials showed equivalence to aspirin in controlling disease activity with a similar incidence of adverse reactions [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Protect from light and moisture. Dispense in a tight, light-resistant container with a child-resistant closure [US].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **United States:** Classified as Rx-only (Prescription drug) [US].
