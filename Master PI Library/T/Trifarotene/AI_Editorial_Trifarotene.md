# Editorial Product Information: Trifarotene

## Editorial Note
- **Contributing Jurisdictions:** Australian (TGA). [Note: Information from other jurisdictions (e.g., US, European, Canadian, Japanese) was not provided in the source documents and is therefore listed as "Not specified in available sources" where applicable.]
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of official Product Information intended for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is for informational purposes and does not replace the official, legally approved Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trifarotene
- **Chemical Class:** Terphenyl acid derivative; selective Retinoic Acid Receptor gamma (RAR$\gamma$) agonist.
- **Brand Name(s):** **AKLIEF** [Australian]
- **ATC Code:** D10AD06
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Cream containing 50 micrograms/g (0.005% w/w) trifarotene. [Australian]
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Topical
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Galderma Australia Pty Ltd [Australian]

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Acne Vulgaris:** For the topical treatment of acne vulgaris affecting the face and/or the trunk in patients 12 years of age and older, specifically when numerous comedones, papules, and/or pustules are present. [Australian]

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Dosing (Adults and Paediatrics $\ge$ 12 years):** Apply a thin layer to the affected areas once daily in the evening.
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - Apply to clean, dry skin.
    - **Face:** One pump actuation typically covers the entire face (forehead, nose, cheeks, and chin).
    - **Trunk:** Two pump actuations typically cover the upper trunk (chest, shoulders, and reachable upper back). An additional actuation may be used for the middle and lower back if needed.
    - Patients must wash their hands after application.
    - Avoid contact with eyes, eyelids, lips, and mucous membranes. In case of accidental contact, rinse thoroughly with lukewarm water.
- **Titration and Monitoring:**
    - It is recommended that clinicians assess the patient’s progress after three months of continuous treatment. [Australian]
    - If severe local irritation occurs, the frequency of application should be reduced or treatment suspended temporarily.
- **Pump Priming:** Before the first use, the pump should be primed by pressing down until a small amount of cream is dispensed (up to 10 times).

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pregnancy:** Absolutely contraindicated during pregnancy. [Australian]
- **Women Planning Pregnancy:** Contraindicated in women intending to become pregnant. [Australian]
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to trifarotene or any excipients (including propylene glycol and ethanol). [Australian]

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Local Cutaneous Reactions:** Erythema, scaling, dryness, and stinging/burning are common, particularly during the first few weeks of therapy.
- **Sun Exposure:** Patients should avoid excessive exposure to sunlight, sunlamps, or phototherapy. The use of a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen (SPF 30 or higher) and protective clothing is recommended.
- **Skin Barrier Integrity:** Do not apply to cuts, abrasions, eczematous skin, or sunburned skin.
- **Concomitant Topicals:** Exercise caution when using medicated or abrasive soaps, cleansers, or cosmetics with strong drying effects (e.g., those containing high concentrations of alcohol or astringents) as they may cause additive irritant effects.
- **Depilation:** Avoid "waxing" as a hair removal method on skin treated with trifarotene. [Australian]
- **Excipient Cautions:** Contains **propylene glycol**, which may cause skin irritation, and **ethanol** (5% w/w), which may cause a burning sensation on compromised skin. [Australian]

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Frequency of Adverse Reactions
| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Local cutaneous reactions (erythema, scaling, dryness, stinging/burning) |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Application site irritation, application site pruritus, sunburn |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Application site pain, dryness, discoloration, erosion, rash, swelling; skin irritation, acne, allergic dermatitis, erythema |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Application site erythema, urticaria, vesicles; eyelid oedema/exfoliation; cheilitis; flushing; eczema asteatotic; seborrheic dermatitis; skin fissures/hyperpigmentation |

- **Serious Adverse Effects:** While systemic serious effects are rare due to low absorption, severe local skin irritation may require treatment discontinuation.

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **Systemic Drugs:** Trifarotene has low systemic exposure. In vitro studies indicate no inhibition of CYP450 isoenzymes (1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 3A4) and no induction of CYP1A2, 2B6, or 3A4 at clinical concentrations. It does not inhibit major uptake or efflux transporters (MATE, OATP, OAT, OCT, BCRP, P-gp, BSEP, MRP). [Australian]
    - **Hormonal Contraceptives:** Topical trifarotene does not affect the efficacy or circulating levels of oral ethinylestradiol and levonorgestrel. [Australian]
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Potential additive irritant effects when used with other desquamative or drying acne medications.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category D:** Retinoids are associated with congenital abnormalities. While systemic exposure from topical use is low, the risk cannot be ruled out, especially with a damaged skin barrier or excessive use. 
    - Treatment must be discontinued immediately if pregnancy occurs.
- **Breastfeeding:** It is unknown if trifarotene or its metabolites are excreted in human milk. A risk to the nursing infant cannot be excluded. To minimize infant exposure, nursing women should not apply the cream to the chest or breast area. [Australian]
- **Fertility:** No human data. Animal studies showed germ cell degeneration in dogs at high oral doses, with incomplete recovery after 8 weeks. [Australian]

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Approved for patients 12 years and older. Safety and efficacy in children under 12 have not been established. [Australian]
- **Elderly Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients aged 65 years and above have not been established. [Australian]
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Not specifically studied in these populations; however, systemic exposure is minimal. [Australian]

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Trifarotene is a selective agonist of Retinoic Acid Receptor gamma (RAR$\gamma$). RAR$\gamma$ is the most common RAR isoform found in the skin. Trifarotene modulates retinoid-targeted genes involved in cell differentiation and inflammatory processes.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Systemic levels are very low. In adults, after 4 weeks of maximal use, many subjects had non-quantifiable levels (<5 pg/mL).
    - **Distribution:** Highly plasma protein bound (>99.9%).
    - **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolised by CYP2C9; to a lesser extent by CYP3A4, CYP2C8, and CYP2B6.
    - **Elimination:** Primarily excreted via faeces. The terminal half-life ranges from 2.4 to 9.1 hours. [Australian]

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Pivotal Studies (18251 and 18252):** Two identical 12-week, Phase 3, vehicle-controlled trials in 2,420 patients (aged $\ge$ 9 years). Results showed significant improvement in IGA (Investigator’s Global Assessment) and PGA (Physician’s Global Assessment) success rates for both facial and truncal acne compared to vehicle. 
- **Long-term Efficacy (Study 3):** A one-year open-label study showed continued improvement, with IGA success rates increasing from 26.3% (Week 12) to 65.9% (Week 52) on the face. [Australian]
- **START Study:** Investigated atrophic acne scarring over 24 weeks. Trifarotene showed a statistically significant reduction in total atrophic acne scar counts compared to vehicle (-5.9 vs -2.7). [Australian]

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store below 25°C. [Australian]
- **Shelf Life:** 2 years from manufacture. Use within 6 months after the first opening. [Australian]

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
