# Editorial Product Information: Teriflunomide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the following official Product Information (PI) source:
    - **Australian (TGA)**: Product Information for APO-Teriflunomide (Date of Revision: 23 April 2026).
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals (HCPs) by integrating clinical data into a concise, scannable format.
- **Disclaimer:** This document is an editorial synthesis and does not substitute for the official PI or local regulatory guidance required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Teriflunomide (Chemical class: *de novo* pyrimidine synthesis inhibitor).
- **Brand Names:** APO-Teriflunomide [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** 
    - 14 mg film-coated tablets: Blue, pentagonal, biconvex; debossed with “14” and “T” [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Oral.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** 
    - Dr Reddy’s Laboratories (Australia) Pty Ltd; Distributed by Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Relapsing Multiple Sclerosis (RMS):** For use in patients with relapsing forms of Multiple Sclerosis to decrease the frequency of clinical relapses and delay the progression of physical disability [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **Standard Adult Dose:** 14 mg orally once daily.
- **Administration:** May be taken with or without food.
- **Switching Therapies:**
    - **From Interferon beta or Glatiramer acetate:** No washout/waiting period required [Australian].
    - **From Natalizumab:** Due to long half-life, caution is required; concomitant immune effects may persist for 2–3 months post-discontinuation.
    - **From Fingolimod:** A 6-week interval without therapy is typically required for clearance and lymphocyte recovery to avoid additive immune effects.
- **Missed Dose:** Not specified in available sources.

## 4. Contraindications
**Teriflunomide** is contraindicated in:
- Patients with hypersensitivity to teriflunomide, leflunomide, or any tablet excipients [Australian].
- **Pregnancy:** Pregnant women or women of childbearing potential not using reliable contraception [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated during lactation [Australian].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Patients with severe impairment of liver function [Australian].
- **Immunodeficiency/Infection:** Severe immunodeficiency (e.g., AIDS), significantly impaired bone marrow function, or severe/uncontrolled infections [Australian].
- **Severe Hypoproteinaemia.**
- **History of Severe Cutaneous Reactions:** Patients who have experienced Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), or DRESS [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Clinically significant and potentially life-threatening liver injury has occurred. Most cases occur within the first 6 months.
    - **Monitoring:** Check ALT, AST, and bilirubin within 6 months prior to initiation. Monitor ALT/AST monthly for the first 6 months, then every 6–8 weeks [Australian].
    - **Discontinuation:** If ALT/AST exceeds 3x Upper Limit of Normal (ULN), teriflunomide must be stopped and an accelerated elimination procedure considered.
- **Infections:** Teriflunomide has immunomodulatory effects. Screen for latent tuberculosis (TB) before starting therapy. Delay initiation in patients with active acute or chronic infections until resolved [Australian].
- **Blood Pressure:** Mean increases in SBP (2.7 mmHg) and DBP (1.3 mmHg) have been observed. Monitor BP at baseline and periodically during treatment.
- **Respiratory Effects:** Interstitial lung disease (ILD) and rare cases of pulmonary hypertension have been reported. New-onset or worsening pulmonary symptoms (cough/dyspnoea) require investigation and potential drug discontinuation.
- **Haematologic Effects:** Expect a mean decrease in WBC count (<15%). Perform a Complete Blood Count (CBC) at baseline, monthly for the first 6 months, then every 6–8 weeks.
- **Peripheral Neuropathy:** Reported in 1.9% of patients on 14 mg vs 0% on placebo [Australian]. If symptoms of neuropathy occur, consider discontinuation and accelerated elimination.
- **Skin Reactions:** Discontinue immediately if severe skin or mucosal reactions (e.g., stomatitis) suggestive of SJS or TEN occur.
- **Accelerated Elimination Procedure:** Due to a long half-life (~19 days), teriflunomide may take up to 2 years to reach plasma levels <0.02 mg/L naturally. If rapid removal is required (e.g., toxicity, pregnancy), use:
    - **Cholestyramine:** 8 g three times daily (t.i.d.) for 11 days (or 4 g t.i.d. if poorly tolerated).
    - **Activated Charcoal:** 50 g every 12 hours for 11 days.

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions (incidence ≥10% and ≥2% higher than placebo) include diarrhoea, nausea, alopecia, and increased ALT [Australian].

| Frequency | Adverse Reaction |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | ALT increased, Diarrhoea, Nausea, Alopecia |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Influenza, Sinusitis, Gastroenteritis, Neutropenia, Paraesthesia, Hypertension, Upper abdominal pain, Rash, Musculoskeletal pain, Menorrhagia, Weight decrease, GGT/AST increased |
| **Uncommon (0.1–1%)** | Lymphopenia, Mild thrombocytopenia, Dysgeusia, Peripheral neuropathy, Polyneuropathy, Neuralgia, Pruritus, Blood creatinine increased |
| **Rare (<0.1%)** | Interstitial lung disease (based on leflunomide data) |
| **Post-marketing** | Anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN/DRESS, Pustular psoriasis, Pancreatitis, Colitis, Drug-induced liver injury (DILI) |

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Rifampicin and Inducers:** Co-administration with rifampicin or other potent CYP/transporter inducers (e.g., carbamazepine, St John’s Wort) decreases teriflunomide exposure by ~40% [Australian].
- **CYP2C8 Substrates:** Teriflunomide increases exposure to drugs like **repaglinide**, paclitaxel, and pioglitazone.
- **Oral Contraceptives:** Increases exposure to ethinyloestradiol and levonorgestrel; however, contraceptive efficacy is not expected to be adversely affected.
- **CYP1A2 Substrates:** Teriflunomide is a weak inducer; may decrease efficacy of **duloxetine**, theophylline, and tizanidine.
- **Warfarin:** Teriflunomide can decrease INR by ~25%. Close INR monitoring is essential [Australian].
- **Transporter Substrates (OAT3/BCRP/OATP):** 
    - **Rosuvastatin:** Dose should not exceed 10 mg/day as exposure is increased 2.5-fold.
    - **Others:** Increased exposure to cefaclor, methotrexate, penicillin G, and ciprofloxacin.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy (Australian Category X):** Teratogenic in animal studies. Use is strictly contraindicated in pregnant women. Pregnancy must be excluded before starting.
- **Contraception:** Women of childbearing potential must use reliable contraception. If pregnancy occurs, the drug must be stopped immediately and the **accelerated elimination procedure** initiated to reach plasma levels <0.02 mg/L.
- **Breastfeeding:** Contraindicated. Teriflunomide is excreted in milk [Australian].
- **Use in Males:** To minimise risk of male-mediated foetal toxicity, men wishing to father a child should discontinue treatment and undergo accelerated elimination or wait 2 years until plasma levels are <0.02 mg/L on two tests [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and efficacy in patients <18 years have not been established [Australian].
- **Elderly Use (>65 years):** Limited data available; use with caution.
- **Renal Impairment:** No dose adjustment required for severe impairment, though caution is advised [Australian]. Not dialysable.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** No dose adjustment for mild or moderate impairment. **Contraindicated** in severe impairment [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Selectively and reversibly inhibits the mitochondrial enzyme **dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHO-DH)**. This blocks *de novo* pyrimidine synthesis, thereby inhibiting the proliferation of activated lymphocytes (B and T cells) while sparing resting cells that use the salvage pathway.
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Reduces lymphocyte counts (mean reduction ~0.3 x 10⁹/L). No potential for significant QTcF prolongation observed.
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Bioavailability:** ~100%.
    - **Protein Binding:** >99% (primarily albumin).
    - **Metabolism:** Hydrolysis (primary); oxidation (minor).
    - **Elimination:** Excreted in faeces (37.5%) and urine (22.6%).
    - **Half-life:** ~19 days [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **TEMSO Study:** A 108-week trial of 1,088 patients with RMS. 14 mg dose significantly reduced the Annualised Relapse Rate (ARR) vs placebo (0.369 vs 0.539) and reduced the risk of disability progression sustained for 12 weeks (20.2% vs 27.3%) [Australian].
- **TOWER Study:** Confirmed efficacy in 1,169 patients; 14 mg dose significantly reduced ARR (0.319 vs 0.501) and disability progression risk [Australian].
- **TOPIC Study:** Evaluated patients with early MS (first clinical episode). 14 mg significantly reduced the risk of a second clinical episode compared to placebo [Australian].
- **TENERE Study:** Compared teriflunomide to interferon beta-1a; teriflunomide 14 mg was not statistically superior to interferon on the primary endpoint of treatment failure [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Storage:** Store below 25°C [Australian].
- **Packaging:** Aluminium/Aluminium blisters.

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
