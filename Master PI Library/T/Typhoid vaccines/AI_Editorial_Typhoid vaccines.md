# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) and labeling documentation from the following jurisdiction:
    - **United States (FDA)**: Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation).
- **Purpose**: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, integrating regulatory data into a single clinical reference. 
- **Disclaimer**: This is an editorial synthesis prepared by an expert clinical pharmacist and is not a substitute for the official PI or summary of product characteristics (SmPC) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. Always consult the current local regulatory documentation before prescribing or administering this medication.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient**: Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt. 
- **Chemical Class**: Amino acid derivative; carrier molecule for fatty acid transport.
- **Brand Name(s)**: Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code**: A16AA01.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s)**: Sterile solution for injection containing 1 g of levocarnitine per 5 mL vial (200 mg/mL) [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration**: Intravenous (IV) use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor**: Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Secondary Carnitine Deficiency in Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: Approved for the acute and chronic management of patients with metabolic defects that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)**: Indicated for the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing maintenance dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose**: 50 mg/kg administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose**: In cases of severe metabolic crisis, a loading dose is often administered, followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency**: Dosing should occur every 3 to 4 hours (q3h or q4h), and must not be less frequent than every 6 hours (q6h) [US].
- **Maximum Dose**: The highest recorded dose administered is 300 mg/kg [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Starting Dose**: 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg dry body weight [US].
- **Administration Timing**: Administered as a slow bolus (2 to 3 minutes) into the venous return line following each dialysis session [US].
- **Dose Adjustment**: Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy, guided by pre-dialysis (trough) plasma carnitine concentrations [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Visual Inspection**: Parenteral products must be inspected for particulate matter and discoloration prior to use [US].
- **Monitoring**: It is recommended to obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels. Regular weekly and monthly monitoring should include blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35 to 60 µmol/L) [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Known Hypersensitivity**: None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Boxed Warnings**: None specified in available sources.
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions**: Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in dialysis patients. Some reactions occurred within minutes of IV administration. If a severe reaction occurs, the drug should be discontinued and appropriate medical treatment initiated [US].
- **Seizure Risk**: Seizures have been reported in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Renal Impairment (Oral Formulation Note)**: The safety/efficacy of oral levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal insufficiency. Chronic high-dose oral administration in ESRD patients may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites, trimethylamine (TMA) and trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency of ≥5% in double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (regardless of causality) include:
- **Body as a Whole**: Abdominal pain (5–21%), headache (3–37%), infection (10–24%), injection site reaction (27–38%), pain (21–35%), fever (5–12%) [US].
- **Cardiovascular**: Hypertension (18–21%), hypotension (3–19%), tachycardia (5–9%), arrhythmia (3%) [US].
- **Digestive**: Diarrhea (9–35%), vomiting (9–21%), nausea (5–12%) [US].
- **Respiratory**: Pharyngitis (15–27%), cough increase (10–18%), dyspnea (3–11%) [US].
- **Metabolic**: Hypercalcemia (6–15%), hypervolemia (3–12%) [US].
- **Nervous**: Dizziness (10–18%), paresthesia (3–12%) [US].

### Post-marketing Experience
- **Neurologic**: Seizures [US].
- **General**: Body odor, nausea, and gastritis (less frequent) [US].
- **Hypersensitivity**: Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin**: Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when levocarnitine is co-administered with warfarin. It is recommended to monitor INR levels at the initiation of treatment or following dose adjustments [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy**: 
    - **US Category**: Not specifically assigned a letter, but reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding**: Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows indicate increased milk carnitine concentrations after administration. The risk of excess carnitine intake in the infant must be weighed against the benefit to the mother [US].
- **Fertility**: No evidence of impaired fertility was observed in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use**: Dosing for metabolic disorders and ESRD is weight-based (mg/kg), but specific age-related safety profiles beyond general dosing were not detailed [US].
- **Elderly Use**: Not specified in available sources.
- **Renal Impairment**: IV levocarnitine is indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis. Dose adjustments are guided by trough plasma levels [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment**: Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
### Mechanism of Action
Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for mammalian energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane, providing substrate for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Distribution**: Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
- **Metabolism**: Major metabolites (oral) include trimethylamine N-oxide and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
- **Elimination**: Following a single IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. The mean apparent terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

### Pharmacodynamics
In patients with inborn errors of metabolism, levocarnitine facilitates the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters, which are rapidly cleared [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Study**: In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients, 20 mg/kg IV three times weekly after dialysis significantly increased plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Trough levels increased from a baseline of 21.3 ± 7.7 µmol/L to 190 ± 55 µmol/L after 8 weeks of therapy [US].
- **Dose-Ranging Study**: Studies using 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses showed dose-proportional increases in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature**: Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C (59°F to 86°F) [US].
- **Protection**: Protect from freezing and excessive heat [US].
- **Compatibility**: Compatible with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s in concentrations of 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL. Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags [US].
- **Handling**: Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].
