# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document provides a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals, synthesising clinical data and regulatory guidance into a single reference.
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and is not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction. At the time of writing, only the United States PI was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); a carrier molecule for long-chain fatty acid transport.
- **Chemical Class:** (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection [US].
- **ATC Code:** A16AA01 (Not explicitly in source, but standard for Levocarnitine).
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) sterile solution in a single-dose vial [US].
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** For the acute and chronic treatment of patients with inborn errors of metabolism that result in secondary carnitine deficiency [US].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** For the prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients with ESRD who are undergoing dialysis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection or by infusion [US].
- **Loading Dose:** Often administered in severe metabolic crises, followed by an equivalent dose over the next 24 hours [US].
- **Frequency:** Administered every 3 or 4 hours (never less than every 6 hours) via infusion or IV injection.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Subsequent daily doses are typically in the range of 50 mg/kg or as clinically required.
- **Maximum Dose:** Doses up to 300 mg/kg have been administered [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight) as a slow 2 to 3-minute bolus injection into the venous return line after each dialysis session [US].
- **Titration:** Initiation may be prompted by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine levels <40 to 50 µmol/L.
- **Adjustment:** Dose adjustments should be guided by trough plasma concentrations. Downward adjustments (e.g., to 5 mg/kg) may be made as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to administration.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **None known** [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients on dialysis. These may occur within minutes of administration. If a severe reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately and monitor for reoccurrence if re-administration is attempted [US].
- **Seizures:** Seizures have been reported in patients with and without pre-existing seizure activity. In patients with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been noted [US].
- **Monitoring:** For metabolic disorders, weekly and monthly monitoring is recommended, including blood chemistries, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation):** The safety of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal failure; chronic high doses may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
Adverse events reported at a frequency ≥5% in chronic hemodialysis patients, regardless of causality, include:

| System Organ Class | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General** | Injection site reaction (33%), Pain (30%), Flu syndrome (25%), Chest pain (12%), Headache (22%), Fever (7%), Abdominal pain (9%) |
| **Cardiovascular** | Hypertension (20%), Hypotension (14%), Tachycardia (6%), Arrhythmia (2%) |
| **Digestive** | Diarrhea (16%), Vomiting (15%), Nausea (8%), Anorexia (5%), Constipation (3%) |
| **Respiratory** | Pharyngitis (23%), Cough increase (9%), Rhinitis (9%), Dyspnea (7%) |
| **Neurological** | Dizziness (13%), Paresthesia (5%), Insomnia (4%) |
| **Metabolic/Nutritional** | Hypercalcemia (9%), Hyperkalemia (6%), Hypervolemia (5%) |

### Other Adverse Effects
- **Common:** Body odor, gastritis, and transient nausea/vomiting have been observed [US].
- **Serious:** Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, bronchospasm, and seizures [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports of increased International Normalised Ratio (INR) have occurred when levocarnitine is used concomitantly with warfarin. **Management:** Monitor INR levels after initiating levocarnitine or following dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin therapy [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US: Category not assigned]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (surface area basis) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed, as animal studies are not always predictive of human response [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk carnitine concentrations following exogenous administration. Risks to the infant from excess carnitine intake must be weighed against the benefits of supplementation to the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No evidence of impaired fertility in animal studies [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Use in children is addressed in the Dosage and Administration section (dosing is mg/kg based) [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Not specifically detailed in provided source.
- **Renal Impairment:** Primarily used in ESRD patients on dialysis. Caution is advised regarding the accumulation of metabolites if using oral formulations in this population [US].
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Facilitates the entry of long-chain fatty acids into cellular mitochondria, providing substrate for oxidation and energy production. It also promotes the excretion of excess organic or fatty acids by forming acylcarnitine esters [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not bound to plasma proteins or albumin [US].
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (TMAO) and γ-butyrobetaine [US].
    - **Elimination:** Approximately 76% of an IV dose is excreted in the urine within 24 hours. Mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg 3x/week post-dialysis), trough levocarnitine concentrations increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to 190 µmol/L at 8 weeks. Efficacy in increasing carnitine concentrations is established, though effects on clinical outcomes in ESRD have not been definitively determined [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C–30°C [US].
- **Conditions:** Protect from freezing and excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Compatible and stable for 24 hours at room temperature (25°C) in PVC bags when mixed with Sodium Chloride 0.9% or Lactated Ringer’s (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].
