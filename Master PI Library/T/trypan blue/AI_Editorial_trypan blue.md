# Editorial Product Information: Trypan Blue

## Editorial Note
- The information in this editorial has been derived from the official Product Information provided by the following regulatory jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- **Purpose:** This document is a harmonised editorial synthesis of available product data designed for use by healthcare professionals. 
- **Disclaimer:** This editorial is for informational purposes and does not replace the official Product Information required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trypan blue (an acid di-azo group dye).
- **Brand Name(s):** VisionBlue [United States].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form(s) and Strength(s):** Ophthalmic solution 0.06% (0.6 mg/mL).
- **Route(s) of Administration:** Intraocular ophthalmic use.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** D.O.R.C. Dutch Ophthalmic Research Center (International) B.V. [United States].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Surgical Aid:** Indicated for use as a diagnostic dye to assist in ophthalmic surgery by staining the anterior capsule of the lens [United States].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Dosing (Adult)
- **Preparation:** The product is supplied in a 2.25 mL single-use syringe pre-filled to a volume of 0.5 mL. A blunt cannula must be attached prior to use.
- **Procedure:**
    1. Open the eye and inject an air bubble into the anterior chamber to minimize dilution of the dye by the aqueous humor.
    2. Carefully apply the 0.06% solution onto the lens capsule using the blunt cannula.
    3. Staining is achieved immediately upon contact with the capsule.
    4. Thoroughly irrigate the anterior chamber with a balanced salt solution to remove all excess dye.
    5. Proceed with anterior capsulotomy.

## 4. Contraindications
- **Intraocular Lenses (IOL):** Contraindicated when the planned procedure involves the insertion of a non-hydrated (dry-state), hydrophilic acrylic IOL. The dye may be absorbed and cause permanent staining of the lens [United States].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Excessive Staining:** To prevent unwanted staining of adjacent tissues, any excess dye must be immediately removed from the eye by thorough irrigation of the anterior chamber after the capsule has been stained [United States].
- **IOL Interaction:** Clinicians should advise patients that the use of this dye is incompatible with certain high-water-content hydrogen or hydrophilic acrylic IOLs (see Contraindications).

## 6. Adverse Effects
Adverse reactions are generally related to the staining of non-target tissues:
- **Inadvertent Staining:** Staining of the posterior lens capsule and the vitreous face has been reported.
- **Duration:** Staining of the vitreous face or posterior capsule is typically self-limiting and resolves within one week [United States].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacokinetic/Pharmacodynamic Interactions:** Not specified in available sources.

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Risk Summary:** No clinical data are available regarding use in pregnant women. Systemic absorption is expected to be negligible following the surgical procedure and removal of excess dye. 
    - **Animal Data:** Trypan blue is teratogenic in multiple species (rats, rabbits, dogs, etc.) when administered systemically at doses 800-fold to 3,200-fold the human dose. Observed anomalies included neural tube, cardiovascular, and eye defects. However, due to negligible human exposure during ophthalmic surgery, the risk of fetal exposure or teratogenic effects is considered unlikely [United States].
- **Breastfeeding:** 
    - Data on the presence of the dye in human milk or effects on milk production are unavailable. 
    - Breastfeeding is not expected to result in infant exposure due to the negligible systemic exposure of the mother following the completion of the surgical procedure [United States].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** The safety and efficacy of trypan blue have been established in paediatric patients, supported by evidence from adequate and well-controlled clinical studies [United States].
- **Elderly Use:** No overall differences in safety or effectiveness have been observed between geriatric and younger patient populations [United States].
- **Renal Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Trypan blue selectively stains connective tissue structures, specifically the anterior lens capsule of the human crystalline lens. 
- **Pharmacodynamics:** The dye does not penetrate the capsule, allowing for clear visualization of the stained anterior capsule in contrast to the non-stained lens cortex and inner material.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Not specified in available sources; however, systemic exposure is considered negligible following irrigation and removal of the dye post-application.

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Paediatric Efficacy:** The use of the product in the paediatric population is supported by data from a well-controlled clinical trial [United States]. Detailed trial parameters were not provided in the primary source document.

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 15°C and 25°C (59°F–77°F).
- **Light:** Protect the solution from direct sunlight.
- **Packaging:** Supplied in a sterile, single-patient-use Luer Lok glass syringe within a peel pouch.

- **Legal Classification:**
