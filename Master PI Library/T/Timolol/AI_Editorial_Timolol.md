# Editorial Product Information: Latanoprost and Timolol

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on the official Product Information (PI) from the following jurisdiction: **Australian (TGA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and not a substitute for the official Product Information required for prescribing in any specific jurisdiction. As only one jurisdictional source was provided for this synthesis, clinical recommendations primarily reflect **Australian** regulatory standards.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredients:** Latanoprost (a prostaglandin $F_{2\alpha}$ analogue) and Timolol maleate (a non-selective beta-adrenergic receptor blocking agent).
- **Brand Name(s):** APO-Latanoprost/Timolol [Australian].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile, isotonic ophthalmic solution containing Latanoprost 50 micrograms/mL and Timolol 5 mg/mL (as 6.83 mg/mL timolol maleate) [Australian].
- **Route of Administration:** Topical ophthalmic.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Arrotex Pharmaceuticals Pty Ltd [Australian].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Reduction of elevated intraocular pressure (IOP):** Indicated for patients with open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension who demonstrate an insufficient response to topical beta-blockers, prostaglandins, or other IOP-lowering medications [Australian].
- **Note on Initiation:** This fixed-dose combination should not be used to initiate therapy [Australian].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
- **General Adult Dosing (including elderly):** One eye drop in the affected eye(s) once daily [Australian].
- **Frequency Limit:** Administration must not exceed once daily, as more frequent dosing of the latanoprost component reduces its pressure-lowering efficacy [Australian].
- **Administration Instructions:**
    - To minimize systemic absorption, apply pressure to the tear duct (nasolacrimal occlusion) immediately after instillation [Australian].
    - If using multiple topical ophthalmic drugs, administer them at least 5 minutes apart [Australian].
    - **Contact Lenses:** Remove lenses before instillation; they may be reinserted after 15 minutes [Australian].
- **Missed Dose:** If a dose is missed, treatment should proceed with the next scheduled dose; do not double-dose [Australian].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Respiratory Disorders:** Reactive airway disease, including bronchial asthma (current or historical) and severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) [Australian].
- **Cardiovascular Disorders:** Sinus bradycardia, sick sinus syndrome, sino-atrial block, second or third-degree atrioventricular (AV) block, overt cardiac failure, or cardiogenic shock [Australian].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to latanoprost, timolol maleate, or any excipient in the formulation [Australian].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Systemic Absorption:** Like other topical ophthalmic agents, this combination is absorbed systemically. The timolol component may cause adverse cardiovascular and respiratory reactions similar to systemic beta-blockers, including bradycardia, hypotension, and aggravation of Prinzmetal’s angina or peripheral circulatory disorders [Australian].
- **Cardiac and Respiratory Failure:** Cardiac failure should be adequately controlled before starting treatment. Respiratory and cardiac reactions, including death due to bronchospasm or cardiac failure, have been reported [Australian].
- **Ocular Effects:**
    - **Iris Pigmentation:** Latanoprost may gradually increase brown pigment in the iris, particularly in patients with mixed-colored irides. This change is typically permanent and may result in heterochromia if only one eye is treated [Australian].
    - **Eyelash Changes:** Changes include increased length, thickness, pigmentation, and number of lashes; these are generally reversible upon discontinuation [Australian].
    - **Macular Oedema:** Use with caution in aphakic patients, pseudophakic patients with a torn posterior lens capsule, or those with known risk factors for macular oedema [Australian].
- **Diabetes and Hypoglycaemia:** Beta-blockers may mask signs of acute hypoglycaemia and increase the risk of severe hypoglycaemia when used with sulfonylureas [Australian].
- **Hyperthyroidism:** May mask clinical signs of hyperthyroidism; abrupt withdrawal may worsen symptoms [Australian].
- **Surgical Anesthesia:** Inform the anaesthesiologist if the patient is using timolol, as it may impair the heart's ability to respond to reflex stimuli [Australian].
- **Herpetic Keratitis:** Use with caution in patients with a history of herpetic keratitis; avoid in active herpes simplex keratitis [Australian].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Common and Very Common Ocular Effects
| Frequency | Adverse Effect |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Very Common (>10%)** | Eye irritation (including burning, stinging, itching) [Australian] |
| **Common (1–10%)** | Eye hyperemia, blepharitis, cataract, conjunctivitis, corneal disorder, eye pain, vision abnormal, iris hyperpigmentation [Australian] |

### Systemic Adverse Effects (Occurring in >1% of patients)
- **Nervous System:** Headache (2.3%), dizziness [Australian].
- **Infections:** Upper respiratory tract infection (6.1%), sinusitis, urinary tract infection [Australian].
- **Vascular:** Hypertension (3.8%) [Australian].
- **General:** Influenza-like symptoms, chest pain [Australian].

### Serious Adverse Effects
- Bronchospasm, cardiac failure, bradycardia, and choroidal detachment (following filtration surgery) have been associated with the timolol component [Australian].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Pharmacodynamic Interactions:**
    - **Additive Effects:** Potential for hypotension and/or marked bradycardia when used with oral calcium channel blockers, beta-blockers, antiarrhythmics (e.g., amiodarone, quinidine), or digitalis [Australian].
    - **Prostaglandins:** Paradoxical elevations in IOP may occur if two or more local prostaglandin analogues are used concurrently [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetic Interactions:**
    - **CYP2D6 Inhibitors:** Potentiated systemic beta-blockade (e.g., decreased heart rate) may occur with quinidine, fluoxetine, or paroxetine [Australian].
- **Specific Management:**
    - **Thiomersal:** Contact between benzalkonium chloride (the preservative) and thiomersal causes precipitation. Separate administration by at least 5 minutes [Australian].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **Australian Category C:** Latanoprost has shown embryofetal toxicity (increased resorption, reduced fetal weight) in animal studies. Timolol was not teratogenic but showed toxicity at high oral doses. There are no adequate studies in pregnant women; therefore, use is not recommended during pregnancy [Australian].
- **Breastfeeding:** Latanoprost/metabolites may pass into milk. Timolol is detected in human milk. Due to the potential for serious adverse reactions in infants, a decision must be made to either discontinue nursing or the drug [Australian].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** Safety and effectiveness in children have not been established; use is not recommended [Australian].
- **Elderly Use:** Dosing is the same as for younger adults [Australian].
- **Renal/Hepatic Impairment:** Specific dosing adjustments are not specified in available sources, though both components are extensively metabolised in the liver [Australian].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** 
    - **Latanoprost:** A selective prostanoid $F_{2\alpha}$ receptor agonist that reduces IOP primarily by increasing uveoscleral outflow [Australian].
    - **Timolol:** A non-selective beta-blocker that reduces IOP by decreasing aqueous humour formation in the ciliary epithelium [Australian].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Latanoprost:** Absorbed through the cornea and hydrolysed to the active acid. Peak concentration in aqueous humour at 2 hours. Systemic bioavailability is 45%; plasma half-life is 17 minutes [Australian].
    - **Timolol:** Peak concentration in aqueous humour at 1 hour. Plasma half-life is approximately 6 hours. Both drugs are metabolised in the liver and excreted primarily in the urine [Australian].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **Comparative Efficacy:** Two 6-month, randomised, double-blind trials compared the fixed combination (once daily) to latanoprost (once daily) and timolol (twice daily) monotherapies. The combination therapy resulted in a significantly greater mean diurnal IOP reduction (by 1 to 3 mmHg) compared to either monotherapy [Australian].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Unopened Bottles:** Store at 2°C to 8°C (Refrigerate. Do not freeze). Protect from light [Australian].
- **Opened Bottles:** Store below 25°C. Protect from light. Store in the outer carton [Australian].
- **Shelf Life:** Discard 4 weeks after opening [Australian].

## 13. Regulatory Information by Jurisdiction
- **Poison Schedule:** Schedule 4 (Prescription Only Medicine) [Australian].
