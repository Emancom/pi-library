# Editorial Product Information: Trimethobenzamide

## Editorial Note
- This editorial synthesis is based on official Product Information (PI) provided by the following jurisdiction: **United States (FDA)**.
- The purpose of this document is to provide a harmonised editorial overview for healthcare professionals. 
- **Note:** This is an editorial synthesis and does not serve as a substitute for the official, jurisdiction-specific PI required for prescribing in any particular region. At the time of compilation, only United States regulatory data was provided for review.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Trimethobenzamide hydrochloride.
- **Chemical Class:** Antiemetic; benzamide derivative.
- **Brand Names:** Trimethobenzamide Hydrochloride Injection, USP [US].
- **ATC Code:** A04AD01 (Not explicitly stated in source, provided for clinical reference).
- **Dosage Forms and Strengths:** 
    - Sterile injection: 100 mg/mL in 2 mL single-dose vials (200 mg total) [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intramuscular (IM) injection only [US].
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** BPI Labs, LLC [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Postoperative Nausea and Vomiting (PONV):** Treatment of nausea and vomiting occurring in the recovery period after surgery [US].
- **Gastroenteritis-Associated Nausea:** Management of nausea associated with acute gastroenteritis [US].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### General Adult Dosing
- **Usual Dosage:** 200 mg (2 mL) administered three (t.i.d.) or four (q.i.d.) times daily [US].
- **Administration Route:** For intramuscular (IM) use only. Intravenous (IV) administration is not recommended [US].

### Administration Instructions
- **Site of Injection:** Administer via deep injection into the upper outer quadrant of the gluteal region [US].
- **Local Reaction Mitigation:** To minimise pain, stinging, burning, or redness at the site, ensure deep gluteal placement and avoid leakage of the solution along the needle track [US].
- **Disposal:** Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial [US].

### Dose Adjustment
- Dosage should be tailored based on the patient's symptomatic severity, clinical response, and the specific indication for therapy [US].

## 4. Contraindications
- **Pediatric Patients:** Use is contraindicated in the pediatric population [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Known hypersensitivity to trimethobenzamide or any component of the formulation [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **CNS Depression:** Trimethobenzamide may cause significant drowsiness. Patients must be cautioned against operating motor vehicles or dangerous machinery until their response to the medication is established [US].
- **Acute Febrile Illness/Encephalopathy:** In the presence of acute febrile illness, encephalitides, gastroenteritis, or electrolyte imbalance (especially in children/elderly), there have been reports of severe CNS reactions, including convulsions, coma, opisthotonos, and extrapyramidal symptoms. Use with extreme caution in these settings [US].
- **Masking of Underlying Conditions:** The antiemetic effect may obscure signs of drug toxicity (overdosage) or complicate the diagnosis of conditions such as appendicitis [US].
- **Hydration Management:** In patients with vomiting and dehydration, primary focus should be on fluid and electrolyte restoration. Avoid overhydration, as it may contribute to cerebral edema [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
*Note: Frequencies of adverse events are not specified in available sources.*

- **Neurological/Psychiatric:** Drowsiness, dizziness, headache, disorientation, depression of mood, coma, convulsions, and Parkinson-like symptoms [US].
- **Gastrointestinal/Hepatic:** Diarrhea, jaundice [US].
- **Musculoskeletal:** Muscle cramps, opisthotonos [US].
- **Hypersensitivity:** Allergic-type skin reactions (discontinue at first sign of sensitization), general hypersensitivity reactions [US].
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypotension (specifically reported in surgical patients following parenteral administration) [US].
- **Hematologic:** Blood dyscrasias [US].
- **Special Senses:** Blurring of vision [US].
- **Local Reactions:** Pain, stinging, burning, redness, and swelling at the IM injection site [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Alcohol:** Concomitant use may result in adverse drug interactions and enhanced CNS depression [US].
- **CNS-Acting Agents:** Caution is advised when administering trimethobenzamide to patients who have recently received other CNS depressants, including phenothiazines, barbiturates, or belladonna derivatives [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** 
    - **US Category:** Not specified in provided source (Animal data: Reproduction studies in rats and rabbits showed no teratogenicity, but high doses [20–100 mg/kg] were associated with increased embryonic resorptions or stillborns) [US].
    - **Clinical Recommendation:** Safety in human pregnancy has not been established; use only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Safety in nursing mothers has not been established [US].
- **Fertility:** Data not specified in available sources.

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** **Contraindicated** [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical trials did not include sufficient numbers of patients $\ge$ 65 years to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects. Due to the higher frequency of renal, hepatic, or cardiac impairment in this population, dosing should be cautious, typically starting at the lower end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Trimethobenzamide is substantially excreted by the kidneys. 
    - **Dose Adjustment:** In patients with $CrCl \le 70$ mL/min/1.73m², clinicians should consider reducing the total dose per administration or increasing the dosing interval [US].

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** The exact mechanism is not fully defined but is believed to involve the chemoreceptor trigger zone (CTZ) in the medulla oblongata. It appears to inhibit emetic impulses conveyed to the vomiting center but does not directly inhibit the vomiting center itself [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** Animal studies show inhibition of apomorphine-induced emesis, but little protection against copper sulfate-induced emesis [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Absorption:** Following a 200 mg IM injection, $T_{max}$ is approximately 30 minutes. Bioavailability of the capsule form is 100% relative to the solution [US].
    - **Distribution:** Not specified in available sources.
    - **Metabolism:** Metabolic disposition in humans is unknown [US].
    - **Elimination:** Mean elimination half-life is 7 to 9 hours. 30–50% of a dose is excreted unchanged in the urine within 48 to 72 hours [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store between 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F) [US].
- **Handling:** Sterile single-dose vial; discard unused portions [US].
