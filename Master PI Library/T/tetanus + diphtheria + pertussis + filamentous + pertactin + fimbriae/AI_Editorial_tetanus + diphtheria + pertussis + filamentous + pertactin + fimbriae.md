# Editorial Product Information: tetanus + diphtheria + pertussis + filamentous + pertactin + fimbriae

## 1. Product Identification
- Active ingredient (INN/generic name): **tetanus + diphtheria + pertussis + filamentous + pertactin + fimbriae**
- Chemical class: Combined toxoid (tetanus and diphtheria) and acellular pertussis vaccine (containing pertussis toxoid, filamentous hemagglutinin, pertactin, and fimbrial antigens)
- ATC code: Not specified in available sources
- Route(s) of administration: Intramuscular injection only
- DO NOT include brand or trade names, dosage form physical descriptions, or scheduling classifications

## 2. Regulatory History
- First U.S. approval: 2005 [US]
- Date of last revision/update of provided PI: April 2026 [US]
- Withdrawals, suspensions, or restrictions: Not specified in available sources
- Other jurisdictions' registration dates or revision histories: Not specified in available sources

## 3. Approved Indications
- Active booster immunization against tetanus, diphtheria and pertussis in individuals 10 through 64 years of age. [US]
- Immunization during the third trimester of pregnancy to reduce pertussis risk in infants younger than 2 months of age. [US]
- Use for tetanus prophylaxis in wound management (a booster dose may be administered for a tetanus-prone wound if at least 5 years have elapsed since prior receipt of a tetanus toxoid–containing vaccine). [US]
- Indications in other jurisdictions: Not specified in available sources

## 4. Dosage and Administration
### General dosing (adult)
- Single intramuscular dose: 0.5 mL administered intramuscularly.
- Approved age range for primary booster use: 10 through 64 years. [US]

### Dosing by indication
- Routine booster vaccination:
  - A first dose may be given if at least 5 years have elapsed since the last dose of a DTaP series or since a Td dose. [US]
  - A second dose may be administered if at least 8 years have elapsed since a prior Tdap dose. [US]
- Pregnancy:
  - Administer one 0.5 mL dose during the third trimester to reduce pertussis risk in infants younger than 2 months. [US]
- Wound management:
  - For tetanus-prone wounds, administer a booster dose if at least 5 years have elapsed since the previous tetanus-toxoid–containing vaccine. [US]

### Administration instructions
- For intramuscular injection only.
- Shake the vial or syringe immediately prior to use until a uniform, white, cloudy suspension is obtained.
- Inspect visually for particulate matter and discoloration prior to administration; do not administer if either is present.
- Do not mix this vaccine with any other vaccine in the same syringe or vial. Discard unused portion in single-dose vial. [US]

### Titration schedules

## 5. Contraindications
- Severe allergic reaction (e.g., anaphylaxis) after a previous dose of any tetanus toxoid, diphtheria toxoid, pertussis-containing vaccine, or to any component of this vaccine. (All jurisdictions that list this contraindication in provided sources)  
- Encephalopathy (including coma, prolonged seizures, or decreased level of consciousness) occurring within 7 days of administration of a previous pertussis-containing vaccine, not attributable to another identifiable cause, is a contraindication to further receipt of any pertussis-containing vaccine. (All jurisdictions that list this contraindication in provided sources)

## 6. Warnings and Precautions
- **Management of acute allergic reactions:** Epinephrine (1:1,000) and other appropriate medical treatment must be immediately available to manage anaphylaxis or other severe hypersensitivity reactions. (All jurisdictions that list this precaution in provided sources)
- **Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS) and brachial neuritis:** If GBS occurred within 6 weeks of receipt of a prior tetanus-toxoid–containing vaccine, risk of recurrence may be increased following a subsequent dose of this vaccine. A causal association between tetanus toxoid and brachial neuritis has been reported. (All jurisdictions that list this precaution in provided sources)
- **Progressive or unstable neurologic disorders:** Defer vaccination in persons with progressive or unstable neurologic conditions; vaccination may confound assessment of neurologic status and prognosis. (All jurisdictions that list this precaution in provided sources)
- **Arthus-type hypersensitivity:** Individuals who experienced an Arthus-type reaction after a prior dose of a tetanus-toxoid–containing vaccine should not receive this vaccine unless at least 10 years have elapsed since the last tetanus-toxoid–containing vaccine. (All jurisdictions that list this precaution in provided sources)
- **Altered immunocompetence:** Immunocompromised persons, including those receiving immunosuppressive therapy, may have a diminished immune response. Consider the possibility of reduced vaccine effectiveness. (All jurisdictions that list this precaution in provided sources)
- **Syncope:** Fainting can occur following administration of injectable vaccines. Observe vaccine recipients for syncope and have procedures to prevent injury from falls and to manage syncopal reactions. (All jurisdictions that list this precaution in provided sources)
- **Monitoring requirements:** Observe recipients for immediate adverse reactions post-vaccination as per routine immunization practice; ensure capability to manage anaphylaxis. Specific laboratory monitoring is not specified in available sources.
- **Other specific clinical precautions:** Not specified beyond those listed above in available sources.

## 8. Drug Interactions
- Concomitant administration with trivalent inactivated influenza vaccine (TIV) in adults 19–64 years was associated with a lower antibody response to the pertactin antigen compared with administration of the combined vaccine alone. Clinical significance and recommendations for coadministration: increased caution; see clinical judgment. [US]
- Concomitant administration with hepatitis B vaccine was studied; injection site and systemic adverse events were collected in trials, but specific interaction effects on immunogenicity or safety beyond the general statement of coadministration study exist in the full PI. Detailed interaction effects: Not specified in available sources.

- Contains tetanus and diphtheria toxoid antigens (formaldehyde-inactivated) and acellular pertussis antigens (pertussis toxoid, filamentous hemagglutinin, pertactin, fimbrial antigens). ## 12. Clinical Trials
### Pivotal and key trials described in provided sources
- Clinical study Td506 (first vaccination; randomized, observer-blind, active-controlled):
  - Population: Adolescents 11–17 years and adults 18–64 years who had not received tetanus- or diphtheria-containing vaccines within the previous 5 years.
  - Total evaluated for first vaccination safety: 7,143 individuals aged 10–64 (4,695 adolescents; 2,448 adults).
  - Safety assessments included solicited local and systemic reactions for 14 days, unsolicited adverse events through 28 days, and monitoring for unexpected medical visits, serious illness, and hospitalizations through 6 months.
  - Key safety findings: Injection site pain was the most frequent reaction (62.9–77.8% depending on age/group). Fever ≥38°C was uncommon but statistically higher in adolescent recipients compared with comparator vaccine. The vaccine did not meet the non-inferiority criterion for rates of ‘Any’ pain in adolescents compared with the comparator in that trial (upper CI exceeded pre-specified margin).
  - Demographics: Predominantly Caucasian (86%); minority representation included Black 8%, Hispanic 3%, Asian 1%.

- Adult re-vaccination study NCT01439165 (second vaccination; randomized, observer-blind, active-controlled):
  - Population: Adults 18–64 years who had received a first dose 8–12 years previously.
  - Safety set: Adacel group N ≈ 999; Td comparators N ≈ 328.
  - Solicited adverse reactions collected for 7 days post-vaccination; unsolicited AEs for ~28 days; SAEs monitored up to 6 months.
  - Key safety findings: High rates of injection site pain (≈87%) and myalgia (≈58%); systemic reactions (headache, malaise) common.

- Adult open-label post-marketing study NCT00347958 (readministration ~5 years after prior dose):
  - Population: Adults 16–69 years who previously received a first dose in earlier studies.
  - N = 545; solicited reactions for 14 days; SAEs monitored for 6 months.
  - Key safety findings: Solicited injection site reactions occurred in ~90% (pain 87.6%, erythema 28.6%, swelling 25.6%); ~77% had at least one solicited systemic reaction (myalgia 61%, headache 53.2%, malaise 38.2%, fever 6.5%).
