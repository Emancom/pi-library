# Editorial Product Information: Levocarnitine

## Editorial Note
- Contributing Jurisdictions: United States (FDA)
- Purpose: This document provides a harmonised editorial overview of levocarnitine injection, synthesised for healthcare professionals. 
- Disclaimer: This is an editorial synthesis intended for clinical reference and does not substitute for the official Product Information (PI) required for prescribing in a specific jurisdiction.

## 1. Product Identification
- **Active Ingredient:** Levocarnitine (INN); (R)-3-Carboxy-2-hydroxy-N,N,N-trimethyl-1-propanaminium, inner salt.
- **Chemical Class:** Amino acid derivative; fatty acid carrier molecule.
- **Brand Names:** Levocarnitine Injection, USP (Baxter Healthcare Corporation) [US].
- **ATC Code:** Not specified in available sources.
- **Dosage Form and Strength:** Sterile solution for injection; 1 g per 5 mL (200 mg/mL) in single-dose vials [US].
- **Route of Administration:** Intravenous use only.
- **Manufacturer/Sponsor:** Baxter Healthcare Corporation [US].

## 2. Approved Indications
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism:** Acute and chronic treatment of secondary carnitine deficiency resulting from genetic metabolic disorders [US-approved].
- **End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD):** Prevention and treatment of carnitine deficiency in patients undergoing chronic hemodialysis [US-approved].

## 3. Dosage and Administration
### Metabolic Disorders
- **Recommended Dose:** 50 mg/kg.
- **Method:** Administered as a slow bolus injection (2 to 3 minutes) or by infusion.
- **Loading Dose:** In severe metabolic crises, a loading dose is often followed by an equivalent dose over the subsequent 24 hours.
- **Frequency:** Dosing should occur every 3 or 4 hours, and never less than every 6 hours.
- **Maximum Dose:** Up to 300 mg/kg has been administered [US].

### ESRD Patients on Hemodialysis
- **Recommended Starting Dose:** 10 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg (dry body weight).
- **Administration:** Slow 2 to 3 minute bolus injection into the venous return line following each dialysis session.
- **Dose Adjustment:** Guided by trough (pre-dialysis) plasma levocarnitine concentrations. Adjustments (e.g., down to 5 mg/kg) may be initiated as early as the third or fourth week of therapy [US].

### Administration Instructions
- Inspect visually for particulate matter or discoloration prior to use.
- Discard any unused portion of the single-dose vial; the formulation contains no preservatives.

## 4. Contraindications
- None known [US].

## 5. Warnings and Precautions
- **Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Serious reactions, including anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, and bronchospasm, have been reported, primarily in ESRD patients. Reactions may occur within minutes of administration. If a reaction occurs, discontinue treatment immediately [US].
- **Seizures:** Levocarnitine may trigger seizures in patients with or without pre-existing seizure activity. In those with a history of seizures, an increase in frequency or severity has been observed [US].
- **Monitoring:** 
    - Obtain baseline plasma carnitine levels before parenteral therapy.
    - Conduct weekly and monthly monitoring of blood chemistry, vital signs, and plasma carnitine concentrations (target free carnitine levels: 35–60 µmol/L) [US].
- **Renal Insufficiency (Oral Formulation Note):** The safety/efficacy of *oral* levocarnitine has not been evaluated in renal failure. High-dose oral administration in ESRD may lead to the accumulation of toxic metabolites (TMA and TMAO) normally excreted in urine [US].

## 6. Adverse Effects
### Serious Adverse Effects
- Anaphylaxis and severe hypersensitivity.
- Seizures.

### Clinical Trial Experience (ESRD Patients)
In placebo-controlled trials, the following effects occurred at a frequency ≥5%:
- **Body as a Whole:** Abdominal pain (9%), headache (22%), injection site reaction (33%), chest pain (12%), fever (7%), infection (15%).
- **Cardiovascular:** Hypertension (20%), hypotension (14%), tachycardia (6%).
- **Digestive:** Diarrhea (16%), nausea (8%), vomiting (15%).
- **Respiratory:** Pharyngitis (23%), cough increase (9%), dyspnea (7%).
- **Other:** Body odor and gastritis have been reported less frequently [US].

## 7. Drug Interactions
- **Warfarin:** Reports indicate an increase in International Normalized Ratio (INR) when used concomitantly with levocarnitine.
- **Management:** Monitor INR levels following the initiation of levocarnitine or after dose adjustments in patients receiving warfarin [US].

## 8. Use in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding
- **Pregnancy:** [US Category: Not specified in letter format]. Reproductive studies in rats and rabbits at 3.8 times the human dose (based on surface area) showed no evidence of impaired fertility or fetal harm. Use during pregnancy only if clearly needed [US].
- **Breastfeeding:** Levocarnitine supplementation in nursing mothers has not been specifically studied. Studies in dairy cows show increased milk concentrations after administration. Weigh the risks of excess carnitine intake in the infant against the benefits of supplementation for the mother [US].
- **Fertility:** No impairment of fertility was observed in animal models [US].

## 9. Use in Special Populations
- **Paediatric Use:** See Section 3 (Dosage and Administration) for weight-based dosing; safety and efficacy are established for the indicated metabolic disorders [US].
- **Elderly Use:** Clinical studies did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65+ to determine if they respond differently than younger subjects. Dose selection should be cautious, starting at the low end of the range [US].
- **Renal Impairment:** Specifically indicated for use in ESRD patients on dialysis (see Section 3).
- **Hepatic Impairment:** Not specified in available sources.

## 10. Pharmacology
- **Mechanism of Action:** Levocarnitine is a naturally occurring substance required for energy metabolism. It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for oxidation and subsequent energy production [US].
- **Pharmacodynamics:** In patients with inborn errors of metabolism or ESRD, levocarnitine clears accumulated toxic acyl-CoA compounds by forming acylcarnitine, which is rapidly excreted [US].
- **Pharmacokinetics:**
    - **Distribution:** Not protein-bound.
    - **Metabolism:** Major metabolites are trimethylamine N-oxide (primarily in urine) and γ-butyrobetaine (primarily in feces).
    - **Elimination:** Following IV bolus (20 mg/kg), approximately 76% of the dose is excreted in urine within 24 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life is 17.4 hours [US].

## 11. Clinical Trials
- **ESRD Studies:** In a 9-week study of 12 ESRD patients (20 mg/kg thrice weekly post-dialysis), trough levels increased from a baseline of ~21 µmol/L to ~190 µmol/L at 8 weeks.
- **Dose-Ranging Study:** Studies comparing 10, 20, and 40 mg/kg doses confirmed a dose-dependent increase in trough levocarnitine concentrations over 12 and 24 weeks [US].

## 12. Storage Conditions
- **Temperature:** Store at 20°C to 25°C (68°F to 77°F); excursions permitted to 15°C to 30°C.
- **Handling:** Protect from freezing. Avoid excessive heat.
- **Compatibility:** Stable for up to 24 hours at room temperature in PVC bags when mixed with 0.9% Sodium Chloride or Lactated Ringer's (concentrations 0.5 mg/mL to 8 mg/mL) [US].

- **United States:** Rx-only [US].
- **Poison Schedule:** Not specified in available sources.
